# This style has limited availability!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-11**
## Executive summary

- The subject line — "This style has limited availability!" — promises scarcity urgency, but the email that opens is a plain browse-abandonment nudge: "HOW ABOUT A SECOND LOOK? SEEN THIS BEFORE? WE THOUGHT SO." There is no stock count, no "selling fast," no countdown anywhere in the body. This is the third time this exact subject has hit this inbox (2026-06-30, 2026-07-16), but unlike the 7/16 send, this one doesn't even try to reinforce the scarcity claim visually — it's the weakest execution of the pattern yet, and the gap between subject and body is now the whole story.
- The hero product (a men's GO WALK Workout Walker running shoe) is clean and well-photographed, and the "more options" grid gives a reasonable secondary path to purchase. But the 25%-off Back-to-School kids sale banner lower down has nothing to do with the men's shoe up top — two unrelated campaigns bolted into one email with no bridge between them.
- Net: another subject/body mismatch from a brand that has now run this exact bait three times. Skechers is trading credibility for open rate here — subscribers who noticed the mismatch on 6/30 or 7/16 will notice it again.

## What's working

- Hero product shot is large, sharp, and unobstructed — no render bugs.
- "SHOP NOW" CTA button is high-contrast and impossible to miss.
- The "more options" 5-up grid gives shoppers a real alternative if the hero isn't their size/style.
- Back-to-School 25% off kids banner is a legitimate, time-bounded offer (valid thru 8/13) with clear terms.

## What's weak

- Subject promises "limited availability" but the body never mentions stock, scarcity, or urgency at all — the biggest miss in the email.
- Hero copy ("SEEN THIS BEFORE? WE THOUGHT SO.") is generic browse-abandonment filler, not tied to the subject's urgency premise.
- Kids' 25%-off sale banner is jammed in below a men's running shoe with zero visual or copy transition — reads like two separate emails stitched together.
- Third occurrence of this identical subject line in six weeks (6/30, 7/16, now 8/11) with no new creative to justify the repeat.

## Recommendations

- 1. Either make the body match the subject (add a stock indicator, "few left in your size," or a countdown) or stop reusing this subject line — right now it's a bait-and-switch that trains subscribers to distrust the scarcity claim.
- 2. Split the kids' Back-to-School sale into its own send, or at minimum add a section header/divider that frames it as a separate offer rather than a tacked-on banner.
- 3. Replace the generic "SEEN THIS BEFORE?" hero copy with something that reflects the actual product being resurfaced (e.g., name the shoe or its key feature).
- 4. Retire "This style has limited availability!" for a few cycles — three uses in six weeks with unrelated creative each time is burning the subject line's credibility.
