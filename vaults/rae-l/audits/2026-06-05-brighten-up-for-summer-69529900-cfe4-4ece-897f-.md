# Brighten Up for Summer ✨
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-05**
## Executive summary

- Sally Beauty returns to summer hair territory 48 hours after "Your Summer Hair Survival Kit" — and the creative tension between those two sends is the defining problem here. The Wella Lightener Bundle at $15.99 is a genuinely sharp offer: specific, affordable, editorial-forward with the "no salon required" hook. The body execution earns its keep. But this email is trading on the permission and goodwill the June 3 kit email built, without doing any new persuasion work to justify the 48-hour follow-on.
- The biggest missed conversion is at the top of the funnel: a $15.99 bundle price sits inside the email body while inbox browsers see only "Brighten Up for Summer ✨" — an amorphous seasonal theme that leaves the open entirely on the brand's equity, not the offer's merit. That's a free lift left on the table.
- Net judgment: solid mid-funnel execution with a subject/preheader gap that suppresses opens, and a cadence question the marketing calendar should be asking.

## What's working

- **Bundle price spelled out in the hero**: "$15.99 Wella Lightener Bundle" is legible at a glance — specific, non-discounted framing sidesteps the endless-sale exhaustion Sally has earned over the past two weeks.
- **"No salon required" editorial hook**: answers the objection before it's raised; relevant to a DIY buyer nervous about at-home lightening.
- **Bundle contents visible in hero copy**: Powder Lightener + Cream Developer + Post Color & Lightening Treatment named inline — reduces first-time buyer friction.
- **Licensed Colorist on Demand callout**: a genuine service differentiator with low friction ("Get started") that adds real value for hesitant DIYers.
- **Sally Rewards section positioned immediately after the product tiles**: contextually relevant reinforcement, not buried in the footer.

## What's weak

- **Subject line hides the offer entirely**: "Brighten Up for Summer ✨" gives inbox browsers a seasonal vibe but zero reason to open; the $15.99 price is invisible.
- **No time pressure or scarcity signal anywhere**: the bundle has no expiry, no "limited availability" — the click impulse can always be deferred.
- **"Shop now" repeated on every CTA button**: same copy regardless of context — the product tiles could carry specific action language but don't.
- **48-hour cadence after a thematically identical send**: June 3's Summer Hair Survival Kit already primed this territory; following up two days later compresses the window before either send's CTAs go cold.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the bundle price in the subject line** — this is the single change that moves the needle most. $15.99 is the hook; it belongs in the inbox, not behind the open.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$15.99 Wella Lightener Bundle — No Salon Required`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Lighten at Home for $15.99 This Summer`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Powder lightener + developer + treatment in one kit. Free shipping on $49+.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `3-product at-home lightening kit, $15.99. No appointment needed.`
- 2. **Replace every "Shop now" CTA with action-specific copy**: "Get the bundle" on the hero button, "Shop lightener" / "Add developer" on the product tiles — trivial builder lift, measurable CTR improvement.
- 3. **Add a deadline or supply signal**: even a soft one ("Ships before the weekend" or "Stock is limited") creates a reason not to defer. There is no time urgency signal visible anywhere in the body.
- 4. **Acknowledge the June 3 send or create spacing**: if the Summer Hair Survival Kit was the editorial and this is the conversion follow-on, use that structure explicitly — a callback like "Still building your summer hair kit?" signals purpose and reduces perceived spam pressure for a subscriber who already engaged 48 hours ago.

## 1. Overview

Sally Beauty returns to summer hair territory 48 hours after "Your Summer Hair Survival Kit" — and the creative tension between those two sends is the defining problem here. ### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Missing `@font-face` for 'Founders Grotesk'**
The global anchor rule declares:
```css
a { font-family: 'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }
```
No `@font-face` block for Founders Grotesk exists anywhere in the stylesheet — only Open Sans variants are loaded. Founders Grotesk is not a system font. Every `<a>` element will silently fall back to Helvetica/sans-serif in all clients.

**[LOW] `white-space: pre` on `img` elements**
```css
img { -ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic; white-space: pre; }
```
`white-space: pre` on `<img>` is non-standard and has no effect on replaced elements, but it is noise that could interact unexpectedly with inline-block image layouts in older Outlook versions. Should be removed.

**[LOW] Stale Google Fonts version**
All six `@font-face` declarations reference Open Sans `v17` (e.g., `opensans/v17/`). Current Google Fonts serves v36+. While functional, stale version pinning means missing hinting and subsetting improvements. Not a breakage risk but worth updating.
