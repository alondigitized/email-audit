# For your eyes only
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-24**
## Executive summary

- Five days after Glossier ran three emails in a single day screaming "FINAL HOURS" to close the Friends of Glossier Sale, this email arrives with — surprise — essentially the same offer (20% off sitewide, 25% off $100+). The subject line "For your eyes only" is a James Bond-style promise of exclusivity and personalization. The email inside delivers a standard six-product grid with a broadcast discount code. That gap between the subject's promise and the email's delivery is the entire story here.
- The creative execution is clean: tight grid, strong product photography, legible hierarchy, no render bugs. Glossier's visual language is intact. But the strategic framing is self-defeating. If the June 19 "Final Hours: Up to 25% off" email was real, this email is admitting it wasn't. Subscribers who held off because the sale was "over" are either annoyed they missed nothing, or trained to ignore future urgency cues.
- The "HOT FOR YOU" label and "for your eyes only" framing try to dress this up as a segmented, personalized send. Nothing in the rendered email supports that — it's a full product catalog browse, not a curated pick based on any observable signal about the recipient.

## What's working

- **Offer is immediately legible.** "20% off sitewide or 25% off $100+*" is in the hero, above the fold, no hunting required.
- **Product grid is well-executed.** Six products, clean photography, consistent "Shop now" links — easy to scan.
- **Glossier's aesthetic is fully intact.** Pastel palette, skin-tone diversity in the bottom-right closeup, no visual dissonance.
- **Subject line length is perfect.** 18 characters, renders fully on every mobile client with plenty of preview room.
- **"HOT FOR YOU" tag creates mild intrigue.** It's lightweight personalization language that at least signals intent, even if not delivered.

## What's weak

- **The subject over-promises exclusivity that the body doesn't deliver.** "For your eyes only" implies a curated or personalized send; a six-product mass-catalog grid is the opposite of that.
- **This offer identical to the sale that just "ended" destroys urgency credibility.** After "Final Hours" + three June 19 emails, reissuing 20-25% off five days later teaches subscribers that every deadline is fake.
- **No deadline visible.** The offer has no expiry, which makes "for your eyes only" read as "this is just always available if you ask."
- **Asterisk on 25% off is visible but the fine print isn't.** The * flags exclusions, but they're not legible in the hero — a trust friction point.
- **"Shop now" is repeated six times and says nothing.** Every CTA is identical generic copy; none connects to the specific product below it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make it actually exclusive or kill the exclusivity framing entirely.** Either show genuinely personalized product picks ("Based on your Cloud Paint order, you might love…") or drop "for your eyes only" and just own the re-offer directly ("We extended the sale — you earned it").
- 2. **Add a real deadline.** Without one, the offer reads as a permanent markdown. "Offer ends Sunday" costs nothing and restores urgency the June 19 arc burned down.
- 3. **Swap generic "Shop now" CTAs for product-specific copy.** "Try Boy Brow," "Get Cloud Paint," "Find your shade" — anything that reflects the product underneath.
- 4. **Address the elephant in the room.** A brief one-liner like "Yes, we extended it — just for some of you" would actually build trust rather than pretending the June 19 close didn't happen.
- 5. **Surface the code explicitly if one is required.** The asterisk without visible redemption mechanics creates uncertainty at the moment of decision.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your exclusive: 25% off ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `We saved the sale for you`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `20% off sitewide or 25% off $100+ — for you, not everyone`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your personalized access expires Sunday night`

### 1. Technical Summary

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Invalid tracking beacon element**
The Klaviyo open-tracking beacon is injected as:
```html
<span rel="stylesheet" href="https://ctrk.klclick.com/l/01KVVEWQ7X3ZP1A3A8MFMYAXN5_0"></span>
```
`rel` and `href` are not valid attributes on `<span>`. This is a Klaviyo-generated workaround to load the beacon outside Outlook conditional blocks (`<!--<![endif]-->`). Browsers and email clients ignore the attributes, so no functional breakage occurs — but it is malformed HTML per XHTML 1.0 Transitional (the declared DOCTYPE), and may trigger strict HTML validators or spam-filter heuristics.

**Click-tracking domain:** `ctrk.klclick.com` — Klaviyo shared domain. No custom tracking domain (e.g., `click.glossier.com`) is in use, which reduces sender brand trust signals and makes DMARC alignment dependent on Klaviyo's infrastructure rather than Glossier's own domain.

**Preheader padding technique:** The hidden preheader span uses U+034F (COMBINING GRAPHEME JOINER) characters — `͏` — to push inbox preview text length. This is intentional and widely used, but some aggressive spam filters flag dense CGJ sequences as obfuscation. Soft risk only.

**Image `alt` attributes:** Cannot confirm from truncated source. If images are present without `alt` text, they will render as empty boxes in image-blocking clients (Outlook default, corporate Gmail).

**Minified class names** (`.n`, `.k`, `.l`, `.bc`, `.ba`, etc.) — Klaviyo-generated. No functional issue; noted for debuggability.

**MSO conditionals:** Correctly structured for Outlook 2007–2019 (`[if mso]`, `[if gte mso 9]`, `[if (mso 16)]`). `o:OfficeDocumentSettings` and `w:WordDocument` XML blobs are present and properly wrapped in `<noscript>`.
