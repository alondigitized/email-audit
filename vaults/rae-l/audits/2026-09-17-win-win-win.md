# Win-win-win
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-17**
## Executive summary

- This is ILIA leaning on its "2026 Allure Best of Beauty" wins as the hook, and it's the most editorially confident send in the streak — the maroon Polaroid-style product shots (concerecaler tube fanned on dominoes, brush leaning on a snapshot) give it a tactile, styled-shoot feel that beats the flatlay grids from 09-15 and 09-16. "win-win-win" as hero copy is clever but vague on first glance; it takes the VIP badge + subhead to clarify this is award news wrapped around a 20% VIP sale.
- The email does a lot of work: awards credibility, two hero products with real bullet-point substance (fit, wear time, materials), then a "more winning formulas" grid of six additional Allure-badge SKUs, then two cart-upgrade GWPs. That's thorough, arguably too thorough for one send — by the time you hit the third pass at "shop now," fatigue sets in.
- Coming right after 09-16's "The Art of the Eye" (7/10, single hero + tight focus) and the four days of straight VIP-sale emails before that, this is ILIA's sixth VIP-sale-flavored send in a row. The Allure-award angle is a genuinely fresh narrative hook for the fatigue, and execution here is solid — but the offer itself (20% off, VIP-gated) is identical to what's been repeated since 09-12.

## What's working

- Allure Best of Beauty badge in the header and on six product tiles gives a real, verifiable credibility signal, not just marketing claims.
- Styled Polaroid/domino photography for the two hero products feels premium and distinct from ILIA's recent flatlay-heavy sends.
- Product bullets under the hero items are specific and substantive (12-hour wear, no-powder-needed, FSC-certified wood, vegan/cruelty-free bristles).
- Two visible cart-upgrade incentives (free multi-stick on all orders, free velvet bag on $100+) add tiered value beyond the flat discount.
- Clean, consistent maroon/cream palette carries through the whole email without breaking.

## What's weak

- "Win-win-win" as the hero headline doesn't parse as an offer until you read the small VIP subhead below it — weak scannability on open.
- Six additional products in a flat grid after the two heroes dilutes focus; there's no clear single next action.
- This is the sixth consecutive sale-flavored email in under a week (09-12 through 09-17), and VIP-gating the third day of one nothing has really escalated.
- Footer text "*Click HERE for T+Cs Promotion Details" is oddly capitalized/punctuated and reads like a template artifact rather than finished copy.
- No urgency/deadline anywhere in the email — the sale could end today or in three weeks, nothing tells the reader to act now.

## Recommendations

- 1. Rewrite the hero headline to state the offer directly instead of relying on the wordplay — something like "2 award wins. 1 sale for you." — so the value prop reads without needing the subhead.
- 2. Add a deadline ("Sale ends Sunday") near the top CTA — this send has zero urgency signal, unlike genuinely time-boxed sales.
- 3. Cut the six-product "more winning formulas" grid down to 3 items or move it behind a "shop all winners" link — two heroes plus a tight secondary row would keep the eye from wandering.
- 4. Fix the footer T&Cs line copy/formatting — small thing, but it's the kind of typo that undercuts an otherwise polished, award-forward email.
- 5. Vary the sale mechanic from the last five sends (e.g., tiered discount by spend, or bundle pricing) rather than repeating flat 20% VIP-off for a sixth time.
