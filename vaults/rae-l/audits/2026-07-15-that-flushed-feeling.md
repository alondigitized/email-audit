# That flushed feeling
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- "That flushed feeling" is ILIA's most ambitious single-category franchise play since the mini tote launch — an end-to-end blush editorial that moves from hero photography through product discovery to technique content ("the art of layering") and closes with the now-familiar mini tote GWP. The creative direction is genuinely strong: the warm, flushed photography is cohesive start to finish, and the shade-name copy ("glimmer," "in the mood") gives the subscriber's eye real places to land.
- The problem is conversion mechanics. "SHOP NOW" is the weakest possible CTA for an email this long and this carefully shot. There's no discount, the GWP terms are buried in small print at the bottom, and the subject line "That flushed feeling" is pure mood — evocative, short, but it names no product and carries no hook for the subscriber deciding in inbox whether to open. Compare that to July 14's "NEW—brown mascara, made mini," which told you everything in five words.
- The mini tote is now in its fourth appearance since July 6. It still functions as a transactional anchor here, but the urgency signal is fading fast with no deadline and no scarcity copy. Gorgeous email; leaves conversion points on the table.

## What's working

- "We blush easily" hero line and warm coral editorial photography are immediately on-brand and visually cohesive
- "That summer flushed feeling" headline plays the double-meaning cleanly without straining
- Shade personality names ("glimmer," "in the mood") give the product grid genuine browsability — subscribers can identify with a mood, not just a shade number
- "The art of layering" editorial section earns the scroll with a rich model close-up that demonstrates technique rather than just selling
- Mini tote GWP at the bottom provides a transactional incentive for the subscriber who needs a reason to commit

## What's weak

- "SHOP NOW" is the weakest allowable CTA — no product name, no category, no verb that signals what you're shopping
- Subject "That flushed feeling" is mood-only; a subscriber scanning 80+ emails in inbox has nothing concrete to act on
- Mini tote GWP is now four appearances in nine days (July 6, July 9, and implied through the run) with no visible countdown or quantity signal — urgency is effectively dead
- No discount or price reduction anywhere in the email means the only lever is aspiration, which is a high bar for a browse-to-buy
- Body copy beneath the headline appears to be small and potentially unreadable at mobile width — if it's carrying the GWP threshold or offer terms, those terms are invisible to most subscribers

## Recommendations

- 1. **Replace "SHOP NOW" with a category-specific CTA** — "Shop the Blush Edit" or "Find Your Summer Shade" orients the click and outperforms in any test
- 2. **Give the mini tote a deadline or quantity signal** — "offer ends Sunday" or "while supplies last" costs nothing to write and reactivates urgency that 10 days of exposure has eroded
- 3. **Pull "the art of layering" above the second product grid** — it's the most differentiated content in the email and it's buried below two rows of product; swap the order to reward the scroller before asking them to buy again
- 4. **Inject one concrete size or format detail into the hero** — noting that the blush range includes both compact and liquid/tint formats adds dimension and gives the subscriber a decision to make, which drives clicks
- 5. **Increase the sub-headline type size** — the body copy beneath the hero headline reads as fine print at mobile; if it's carrying any offer context, it's being missed by the majority of opens

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` in `<style>` block — moderate risk:**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```
`@import` is stripped by Gmail (webmail), Yahoo Mail, and several others. The fallback `"Helvetica", sans-serif` is correctly defined and will render cleanly, but the custom Klaviyo font and Montserrat will not load in these clients. This is expected behavior for email but should be verified against design intent.

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title> </title>
```
A whitespace-only title. Some screen readers announce this. Prefer omitting or setting a meaningful value (e.g., the subject line).

**Link color contrast — unverifiable without full DOM**, but `#5f4b3c` on `#faf7f4` background yields approximately 5.4:1 contrast ratio, which passes WCAG AA. However, since `text-decoration:none` is enforced on all states including `:hover`, links rely solely on color differentiation — fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.1 (use of color alone).

**No visible focus styles** in the provided CSS.
