# Aloe, is it me you’re looking for? 🌿
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-24**
## Executive summary

- This is an ingredient-spotlight editorial email built around aloe vera — the same format Lush ran for the June 17 Super Milk send. The pun chain runs wall-to-wall: "Aloe, is it me you're looking for?" in the subject pays off as "ALOE AND BEHOLD" in the hero, and that's genuinely charming. The hero renders cleanly, the seasonal fit is strong (aloe/cooling in late June), and the brand voice is intact.
- The execution problem is focus. The email opens with logical aloe-forward anchors — Grease Lightning, Breath of Fresh Air — then quietly drifts to Argan facial oil, Posh Chocolate, and a bottom section ("A SOLID CHOICE") devoted to soap bars. The ingredient narrative evaporates into a general catalog browse by the third scroll.
- The structural problem is conversion. There is no promotional offer anywhere, the hero carries two competing CTAs ("Shop Now" and "Learn More"), and seven Lush emails have landed in the past 13 days. Engaged subscribers will still open on brand goodwill, but the click case is weak.

## What's working

- **The pun chain is airtight.** Subject → hero headline → editorial concept all pull the same Lionel Richie thread without overexplaining it.
- **Grease Lightning and Breath of Fresh Air are the right anchors.** Both are genuinely aloe-forward hero products that validate the editorial premise.
- **Summer seasonality earns the concept.** Aloe/cooling in late June maps naturally to heat and soothing skin — the timing makes the send feel intentional, not filler.
- **The hero renders cleanly** — strong image, readable large headline, visible green CTA button, no layout breaks.
- **Brand voice is consistent** with the ingredient-educated, playful tone Lush subscribers expect.

## What's weak

- **The product selection loses the thread.** Argan, Posh Chocolate, and the soap bars below don't clearly connect to aloe — the editorial concept starts as an ingredient deep-dive and ends as a catch-all SKU dump.
- **No promotional offer anywhere.** Same weakness as the June 17 milk email — editorial intent without a pricing hook is a hard sell in a crowded summer inbox.
- **Dual CTAs compete.** "Shop Now" and "Learn More" sit side-by-side in the hero, splitting attention and softening conversion pressure.
- **"A Solid Choice" soap bar section feels bolted on.** Tonally disconnected from the aloe story; reads like a modular content block appended without editorial review.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** This is the 7th Lush email in 13 days — even loyal subscribers will start reflexively archiving.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the soap bar section or make it explicitly aloe-tied.** If Honey I Washed The Kids contains aloe, call that out. If not, it doesn't belong in this email.
- 2. **One CTA in the hero.** Drop "Learn More." Let "Shop Now" own the above-the-fold moment with no split.
- 3. **Add a reason to buy *today*.** A free aloe sample with qualifying orders, a bundle, or even a spotlight on a limited-run aloe SKU would provide a click trigger the email currently lacks.
- 4. **Tighten the grid to 4 explicitly aloe-forward products**, and name the specific aloe benefit for each in the subtitle — turns a browse into an education that earns the click.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Soothe your summer skin with aloe 🌿`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The superhero ingredient hiding in 6 of your favorites`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Grease Lightning, Skin Drink, Breath of Fresh Air — all powered by aloe vera`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Cooling. Calming. Hydrating. Meet aloe vera, doing the most in your routine right now.`
