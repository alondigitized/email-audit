# Our current favorite blush looks
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-15**
## Executive summary

- This is Glossier's third discount send in five days, and it's the strongest execution yet of the "Cheek Week" arc — 8/11's flat 20%-off blush promo, then 8/13's "Score 20% off all blushes" campaign wrapper, and now this: the same 20% mechanic dressed up as a three-look editorial with real models, real quotes, and named products per look. It reads less like a blast and more like a lookbook, which is a smart way to keep the discount fresh without just repeating the banner.
- The execution is clean — no render bugs, consistent product swatches, three distinct model looks with named shades, and a persistent 20% offer stated right in the hero. The weakness is structural, not creative: this is the third "20% off blush" email this week, and a reader who opened 8/11 or 8/13 will recognize the mechanic instantly even if the wrapper is new. It's a well-made email riding a discount that's starting to feel less like news and more like the default price.
- Judgement: good creative, cadence risk. This will perform on click-through for anyone who hasn't already converted, but open rates should be expected to soften as the offer stops feeling urgent.

## What's working

- Hero clearly states the offer ("20% off Cloud Paint + Cloud Paint Plush Blush") with a visible "Shop now" CTA above the fold.
- Three named looks ("tropical vacation," "riding my bike," "have a crush on you") each pair a real model photo with a specific shade recommendation and a "Get the look" CTA — strong visual hierarchy per section.
- Product swatches are shown large and labeled with plain-English shade descriptions ("A warm rose," "A cool baby pink") — genuinely useful for a shade-matching decision.
- Diverse model casting across the three looks (varied skin tones) is a real signal of intentional creative work, not a stock template.
- Every "Shop now" button ties to a specific product/shade, not a generic homepage link.

## What's weak

- This is the third 20%-off-blush send in five days (8/11, 8/13, now 8/15) — no new urgency, no deadline visible anywhere in the render, so a reader who's already seen this offer twice has no reason to act today versus next week.
- Subject "Our current favorite blush looks" undersells the discount — the 20% off doesn't appear until the body, so a scanning inbox reader gets zero price signal from the subject line itself.
- No countdown or "ends [date]" language anywhere in the visible creative — for a third repeat of the same mechanic, urgency is exactly what's missing to justify opening again.
- Section labels ("SUNKISSED SHADES," "BARELY-THERE BLUSH," "CRUSHING PINKS") are stylish but generic — they don't reinforce the 20% offer visually at all past the hero.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a real deadline to the hero and subject.** Three sends on the same mechanic with no expiration is the single biggest miss — "Cheek Week ends Sunday" or similar would convert repeat-exposure fatigue into last-chance urgency.
- 2. **Put the discount in the subject line.** "Our favorite blush looks" is soft; a subscriber who's seen 8/11 and 8/13 needs the number up front to justify a third open.
- 3. **Differentiate this send from 8/13 explicitly** — if this is still "Cheek Week," say so in the preheader so it reads as continuity/finale rather than an unrelated fourth promo.
- 4. Consider trimming to two looks instead of three for a shorter scroll — the mechanic and shade-matching value land within the first look; the third feels like padding on a repeat-offer email.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Cheek Week: 20% off ends soon`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your blush shade, 20% off (3 looks)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Last call on 20% off Cloud Paint + Plush Blush.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Three ways to wear this week's blush drop.`
