# Chase the sun(screen)
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-11**
## Executive summary

- "Chase the sun(screen)" is a product-launch email for the new Sun Serum Mineral Sunscreen SPF 30, structured around three UGC review cards from real customers — which is the strongest editorial move ILIA has made in this run. The hero concept (lifestyle imagery, parenthetical wordplay in the headline) is on-brand, and the seasonal timing is right. But the email undermines itself with a passive, generic hero CTA ("MEET OUR BRAND") on what is explicitly a conversion-intent send. There is no price point, no urgency, and no concrete offer visible in the body.
- The UGC section redeems the back half. Stephanie, Tamerri, and Andrea each appear with personal photos, star ratings, and individual review copy — three real humans vouching for a new product carries more weight than any discount callout would. ILIA clearly understands that a new SPF launch needs credibility first, clicks second.
- The cross-sell at the bottom ("Stay Sharp") is a category hop — from sun serum into sharpened eye/lip tools. It's not wrong, but it dilutes the single-product focus this launch deserved. Overall: a structurally sound launch email held back by a hero CTA that belongs in a brand-awareness campaign, not a product drop.

## What's working

- **UGC is the star.** Three named reviewers with personal photos and star ratings directly beneath the product launch copy is a credibility-first strategy that suits a new SKU.
- **Parenthetical subject wordplay** — "(screen)" is a clean semantic trick; it catches the eye and sets a playful editorial tone consistent with ILIA's recent voice.
- **"NEW" product badge** on the serum image is a low-effort, high-signal differentiator that correctly frames this as a launch, not a restock.
- **"SHOP SUN SERUM" CTA** in the product section is specific and action-clear — verb + product noun, no ambiguity.
- **Seasonal alignment** is tight: a sun-protection launch in peak summer is timed exactly right.

## What's weak

- **"MEET OUR BRAND" is the wrong CTA for this email.** It reads like a welcome series button, not a product launch CTA. The hero is about SPF tips from Team ILIA — the button should go somewhere specific.
- **No price point anywhere in the body.** "NEW" is a hook but without a $X anchor, the email can't close anyone sitting on the fence.
- **Top banner offer is illegible.** A promotional strip runs across the top but is too small to read clearly — a GWP or site-wide offer that could have been a body-level trust builder instead disappears into fine print.
- **Cross-sell category hop.** "Stay Sharp" (eye/lip tools) after a sun-protection launch breaks the editorial thread right at the moment the reader has warmed up to SPF.
- **"SHOP TO COLLECTION"** in the Stay Sharp CTA reads like a typo ("SHOP THE COLLECTION") — minor but visibly off on a brand that usually sweats copy.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Replace "MEET OUR BRAND" with a CTA that matches the content.** "READ THE TIPS" or "GET THE SERUM" would convert more than a passive brand-awareness button on a launch email.
- 2. **Anchor a price in the hero.** Even a one-line "$48 — shop now" near the body copy would remove the friction of navigating to PDP to answer the basic question.
- 3. **Promote the top banner offer into the hero body.** If the GWP from the mini tote run (July 6–9) is still live, surface it explicitly. It was the 9/10 email in this run — don't bury the offer that's already earning trust.
- 4. **Cut or reorder the cross-sell.** Replace "Stay Sharp" with a complementary SPF-adjacent product (lip SPF, prep mist, primer) to keep the category clean through the close.
- 5. **Fix "SHOP TO COLLECTION" → "SHOP THE COLLECTION."**

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` font loading will silently fail in major clients.**
Two `@import` rules are used inside a `<style>` block:

```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```

Gmail (web and Android), Outlook (all versions), and Apple Mail on iOS strip or block `@import`. The fallback at the bottom of the stylesheet (`"Montserrat", "Helvetica", sans-serif`) is correct and will engage, so this is a font-rendering degradation, not a layout-breaking failure.

**Responsive breakpoint is 480px, not 600px.** Mobile media queries fire at `max-width: 480px`. This is narrower than the de facto 600px standard and will leave columns un-stacked on tablets and large phones (e.g., iPhone Pro Max at 430px logical width is just inside; iPad mini at 744px is not).

**Image alt text unverifiable** — no `<img>` tags visible in the truncated source.

**MSO/Outlook compatibility: no issues found.** Conditional comments, `o:AllowPNG`, `PixelsPerInch`, `mso-table-lspace/rspace` resets, and `.mj-outlook-group-fix` are all correctly implemented.

**Preheader: correctly implemented.** Hidden div with `max-height:0; overflow:hidden` and Unicode filler characters (`͏`) to prevent body text bleed-through.
