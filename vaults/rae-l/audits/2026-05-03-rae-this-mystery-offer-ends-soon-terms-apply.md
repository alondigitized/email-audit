# Rae: This Mystery Offer ends soon 👀 Terms apply
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-03**
## Executive summary

- A solid loyalty-retention email from Ulta Beauty that pairs a concrete discount hook ("$10 off $35") with an extensive Rewards point showcase. The mystery framing creates curiosity, and the personal name in the subject line adds a nice touch. The body, however, becomes a dense product-and-points grid that dilutes focus — there are too many modules competing for attention below the fold. The offer is real and legible; the execution is workmanlike rather than exceptional.

## What's working

- **Mystery + urgency combo** works in the subject ("ends soon," "before it's gone") and the hero headline builds on it cleanly.
- **$10 off $35 badge** is visually distinct (yellow on white) and scannable in under a second.
- **"Claim Your Offer" CTA** is well-placed directly below the hero — no scrolling required.
- **Rewards points grid** is a clever way to reinforce loyalty value: showing real point counts on aspirational products (Dyson-style tool, fragrance) signals that the program delivers.
- **Birthday love section** adds warm, member-specific personalization that Ulta executes better than most beauty retailers.

## What's weak

- **"Terms apply" in the subject line** signals exclusions before the email is even opened — it's defensive and undermines the mystery offer's appeal.
- **Product grid is overwhelming.** Ten-plus products with point values crammed in two columns at small size creates visual noise. Points alone don't drive a click — there's no clear "add to cart" path or urgency attached to any specific product.
- **Mystery framing is inherently vague.** "Mystery Offer" postpones the payoff; combining it with "Terms apply" makes the offer feel riskier than a straight "$10 off $35."
- **No seasonal or trend anchor.** The email doesn't signal why now — no mention of a specific collection, season, or event beyond the offer window.
- **"Don't miss out on the rewards" section** is a loyalty acquisition pitch dropped into what should be a retention email — it reads slightly off-target if this is going to current members.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Drop "Terms apply" from the subject.** Move it to fine print in the footer where it belongs. It costs open rate with zero legal benefit in the subject slot.
- 2. **Feature one hero product in the offer.** Instead of a generic $10 off $35, tie it to a trending or seasonal SKU — "Save $10 on your next skincare haul." That makes the email shoppable immediately.
- 3. **Trim the product grid to 4-6 products.** Curate by highest point value or by category alignment. A tighter grid with a per-product "Shop + earn" CTA converts better than a catalog dump.
- 4. **Add a specific offer deadline.** "Ends tonight" or "Offer expires Sunday" converts meaningfully better than "ends soon" — especially for loyalty members who are already motivated.
- 5. **Move the birthday section earlier** if it's seasonally relevant to this recipient — it's the highest-personalization signal in the email and it's buried.

**[HIGH] External Google Fonts stylesheet will be stripped by major clients**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=DM+Sans:...&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
Gmail, Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo strip `<link>` tags from `<head>`. DM Sans will not load in these clients. If body copy relies on this font without an inline fallback stack, text will fall back to the client's default serif — confirm that inline styles specify `font-family: 'DM Sans', Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif` on all text elements.
