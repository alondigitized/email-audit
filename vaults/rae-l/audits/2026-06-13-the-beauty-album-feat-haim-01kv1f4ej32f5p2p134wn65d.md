# The Beauty Album feat. HAIM
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-13**
## Executive summary

- "The Beauty Album" is the most conceptually inventive creative in this HAIM arc — framing each sister's beauty routine as liner notes in a music album is genuinely clever and brand-native in a way that the prior "clinical testimonial" and "get ready" sends weren't. Each sister gets her own portrait section, a candid personal quote, and enough editorial space to feel like a real story rather than a billboard. Danielle's teal-eyeshadow quote is the most product-specific thing any HAIM send has produced.
- The commercial problem is the same one that's plagued this arc since it stretched past two emails: there is no product CTA anywhere visible. No "Shop Este's Picks," no product link, no price reduction. The only actionable offer is a giveaway buried at the bottom — signed vinyl and $50 — which is a smart engagement hook but not a conversion vehicle. After four HAIM emails in roughly a week, a subscriber who hasn't bought yet needs a reason to buy, not another story.
- Clear judgment: the best editorial send in this arc and the weakest commercial one. The concept deserved a product shelf; instead it got a sweepstakes.

## What's working

- The "beauty album" / liner-notes concept is the single most original creative idea in this entire HAIM arc — it earns the collab rather than just repeating it.
- All three sisters get genuinely personal quotes; Danielle's ("I genuinely felt like a princess… teal eyeshadow") is specific enough to feel believable, not PR-scripted.
- Visual hierarchy reads top-to-bottom without confusion: branding → editorial profiles → giveaway → footer. Clean.
- The signed vinyl giveaway is an elegant reward that ties HAIM's music identity to the prize — it's the most brand-coherent incentive this arc has used.
- No visible render bugs; layout is clean at the screenshot resolution.

## What's weak

- Zero product CTAs in the body — after four emails on the same collaboration, the arc desperately needs a "Shop the HAIM x ILIA edit" button and it doesn't have one.
- Este's and Alana's quote text appears very small in the portrait sections — unreadable at mobile scale, which defeats the main editorial purpose of the email.
- The giveaway section is buried at the bottom with no visible deadline, making it easy to scroll past without acting.
- "The Beauty Album feat. HAIM" subject is conceptually interesting but gives no purchase or urgency signal to compete in an inbox — it reads like a Spotify album title.
- This is the fourth-plus HAIM send in under ten days; no new hook (discount, limited stock, deadline) is introduced to offset inbox fatigue from the prior arc.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a product shelf after each sister's profile** — "Shop Este's picks" with two or three product images tied to whatever she references. The profiles are already there; the product link is the missing step.
- 2. **Give the giveaway a hard deadline and move it up** — "Enter by [date]" turns a soft engagement play into a time-bounded action worth clicking. If the deadline is real, lead with it.
- 3. **Increase the quote font size** — the editorial copy is the reason this email exists; it needs to be readable without pinch-zooming.
- 4. **Retire or reset the arc after this send** — the brand history shows six ILIA emails in eleven days; the next send should either be a commercial close (deep discount, bundle) or a topic break.

- **Web fonts will silently fail in most clients** — Two `@import` calls load external fonts:
  - `https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css`
  - `https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap`

  Gmail (all variants), Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo strip `@import` and external font references. The final rule `p, h1, h2, h3 { font-family: "Montserrat", "Helvetica", sans-serif }` provides the correct fallback chain — rendering will degrade to Helvetica/Arial gracefully. No fix required, but the custom Klaviyo font (`SguDsz`) will never render outside Apple Mail/iOS, so its value should be confirmed intentional.

  No underline, no hover state differentiation. In email clients that render hover CSS (Apple Mail, iOS, some Outlook), users get no affordance that text is clickable. WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.1 requires non-color distinction for links within body text.
