# ⭐️ STACKING WEEKEND IS HERE ⭐️
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- Sephora kicks off the July 4th weekend with "Stacking Weekend" — a smart reset after the Major Summer Deals arc closed June 23 and the June 29 "something new" send began the cooldown. The timing is right, the mechanic is genuinely appealing (stack 30% off with 2X Beauty Insider points), and the hero delivers the goods: a bold coral 30% circle with immediate visual impact. This is a credible new campaign arc, not a recycled discount email.
- The problem is everything that follows the first fold. After the hero, the email tries to run seven campaigns at once — credit card perks, a GWP, BNPL, app download, a Dolce&Gabbana launch, and a bonus offer — all stacked at roughly equal visual weight. The structure stops being a campaign and becomes a promotional bulletin board. The credit card exclusivity callout appearing directly below the hero is the worst offender: non-cardholders get a "this isn't for you" message as their second impression.
- Subject line buries the lead. "Stacking Weekend is Here" tells subscribers an event exists; it never mentions the 30% that makes it worth opening. That number should be in the subject.

## What's working

- **30% hero circle is unmissable.** Dominant coral shape lands first; the eye has nowhere else to go on the first fold.
- **"Stacking Weekend" is a strong campaign frame.** Named event vocabulary signals insider status to loyal subscribers and clearly differentiates from the June sale arc.
- **Beauty Insider points woven throughout.** The "See What Your Points Can Do" section plus the per-tier points-earn callouts make loyalty feel rewarded at every level.
- **Timing aligns with a real shopping moment.** July 4th weekend is credible urgency — this doesn't feel like an invented occasion.
- **Dolce&Gabbana "Just Dropped" module.** Pairing discovery with discount is classic Sephora voice; it elevates the email beyond pure clearance.

## What's weak

- **Subject line hides the 30%.** The single most compelling number in this email is absent from the subject; subscribers see "Stacking Weekend" without knowing what they're stacking *toward*.
- **Post-hero structure completely collapses.** Seven competing modules after the hero with no visual hierarchy or breathing room — credit card, GWP, BNPL, app, points explainer, Dolce drop, bonus.
- **Credit card block lands too early.** Non-cardholders hit an exclusion message as their second impression; it should live at or below the footer.
- **No explicit end-date anywhere visible.** "Weekend" is implied but "Ends Sunday July 6 at midnight" is absent — urgency stays abstract rather than triggering.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA doesn't reinforce the campaign.** A stacking-themed CTA ("Start Stacking," "Stack Your Savings") would have connected the button to the concept instead of disappearing into generic.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the 30% in the subject.** "Stacking Weekend" alone is insiders-only copy; non-devotees need the number to justify the open.
- **Subject Alt A:** `30% off + 2X points — Stacking Weekend starts now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Stack 30% off with your Insider points this weekend`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stack your savings: 30% off + double points. Ends Sunday only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus a free Growth Bag with any $25 purchase — this weekend only.`
- 2. **Cut to three post-hero modules.** Hero → GWP (one concrete gift) → Dolce&Gabbana launch → single "Shop Stacking Weekend" CTA. Retire the BNPL, app download, and credit card blocks into the footer or a separate transactional send.
- 3. **Add a hard end-date in the hero sub-copy.** "Ends Sunday, July 6" transforms "weekend" from vague to clock-ticking.
- 4. **Move the credit card callout to the footer.** Cardholders will find it; non-cardholders won't lose momentum from the hero to a benefit they can't use.
- 5. **Rewrite the primary CTA to match the campaign name.** "Stack Your Savings" or "Start Stacking" is specific, reinforces brand terminology, and adds zero friction over "SHOP NOW."

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta — confirmed issue**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Should be removed or replaced with `user-scalable=yes`.

**Global suppression of `text-size-adjust` — confirmed issue**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  -moz-text-size-adjust: none;
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```
Applied to essentially all elements. Overrides user-configured accessibility font scaling in mobile browsers. Acceptable on `body` only; blanket application on every element is excessive.

**Mobile breakpoint too narrow — confirmed issue**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 480px) { ... }
```
Current flagship widths (iPhone 15 Pro: 393px, Pixel 9: 412px) fall under this threshold in portrait, but the breakpoint will not fire for any landscape-oriented mobile view or small tablet. Standard practice is `max-width: 600px` as the primary responsive breakpoint.

**`robots` meta value has invalid space — confirmed typo**
```html
<meta name="robots" content="no index" />
```
`no index` (with space) is not a valid directive; correct value is `noindex`. Email clients don't crawl, but this matters if the email is web-mirrored or archived at a public URL.
