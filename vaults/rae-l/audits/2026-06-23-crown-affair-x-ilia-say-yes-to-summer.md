# Crown Affair x ILIA—say yes to summer
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-23**
## Executive summary

- This is ILIA's Crown Affair collab launch — a hair-meets-skin limited-edition collection email built around a model in a pink towel, a five-step ritual product line, and a giveaway hook. The creative is clean and editorial in ILIA's signature style: muted pinks and creams, confident hero photography, and a tight product story. It's legitimately the most differentiated send in the last week — after a dense stretch of GWPs, sharpeners, and HAIM celebrity beats, a named third-party collab with Crown Affair actually cuts through.
- The structural weakness is obvious: there's no price incentive anywhere. ILIA trained subscribers on GWP mechanics across June 14–20 (free mini, free sharpener), then pivots here to a pure editorial/collection launch with only a giveaway as a conversion hook. That giveaway is smart, but it's not a purchase motivator — it's a lead-gen mechanic. The subscriber who just got three gift-with-purchase offers in a row will feel the absence of a deal.
- Clear verdict: strong creative, genuinely newsworthy collab premise, competent layout — but the email asks for full-price purchase energy without giving any reason to buy now over next week.

## What's working

- **Hero photography is the best in recent ILIA sends** — the pink-towel model is warm, aspirational, and directly product-adjacent (a hair towel you can actually buy).
- **The Pink Towel gets its own dedicated section** with its own CTA ("SHOP THE TOWEL"), which turns a piece of packaging into a covetable product.
- **The Five Step Ritual structure** gives the email a narrative arc — intro → hero product → ritual → win the whole thing — that pulls the reader down the page.
- **Giveaway section at the bottom** ("Win the Whole Routine") is a smart re-engagement hook for price-sensitive subscribers who won't buy at full price today.
- **Collab naming in the subject** ("Crown Affair x ILIA") is one of the better subjects in this batch — brand fans of either house will open.

## What's weak

- **No offer, no urgency, no deadline** — "limited edition" appears in the banner but nowhere in the body copy or subject, so there's no credible reason to click today vs. next week.
- **"Say yes to summer" headline is the weakest line in the email** — generic summer language, especially right after the June 22 "Skincare Mode: Summer" send used nearly the same seasonal framing.
- **The Five Step Ritual product grid is hard to parse at mobile size** — product descriptions appear in very small type and the grid reads as a wall of white packaging.
- **Giveaway has no visible deadline** — "Win the Whole Routine" has no end date or entry mechanic shown, which undermines urgency.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a launch-week purchase incentive** — a GWP (gift-with-purchase) or a "complimentary deluxe sample with any collab purchase" tied to a deadline. ILIA just conditioned subscribers to expect a gift; cold-launching at full price without one is a structural miss.
- 2. **Put "limited edition" in the subject line** — "Crown Affair x ILIA" is the collab label, but nothing in the subject communicates scarcity or purchase urgency. Limited edition is the actual offer here; it belongs in the subject.
- 3. **Give the giveaway a deadline and visible entry step** — "Enter by [date]" or a visible button with "ENTER NOW" drives urgency. As-is it reads like a passive scroll reward, not an action call.
- 4. **Replace "say yes to summer" with a ritual-centric headline** — lean into the Five Step Ritual framing, which is original and product-specific, rather than the same seasonal phrasing from the June 22 send.
- 5. **Increase the Five Step Ritual product card size** — at current scale, the products are unidentifiable; one large hero SKU with its own CTA would outperform the small-grid layout.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` for web fonts (confirmed issue)**
```css
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
```
`@import` inside `<style>` is stripped by Gmail (both web and Android) and ignored by Outlook. Montserrat will silently fall back to `Helvetica, sans-serif` for a significant portion of recipients. This is expected Klaviyo behavior but worth flagging if brand font fidelity matters.

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title> </title>
```
Screen readers and some preview panes surface the `<title>`. A whitespace-only value degrades accessibility. Should be "ILIA × Crown Affair — Say Yes to Summer" or similar.

**No mobile font-size reduction for headings**
```css
h1 { font-size: 40px }
@media (max-width: 480px) { h1 { font-size: 40px !important } }
```
The mobile override explicitly re-asserts 40px — same as desktop. At 320–375px viewport widths this can cause overflow or forced scaling. h2 (32px) and h3 (24px) have the same pattern.

**All anchor states share identical color, including `:hover`**
```css
a:hover { color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none }
```
No visual hover feedback. Minor; common in email but worth noting for webview/browser rendering.
