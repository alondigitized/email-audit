# Buy 3, save 20%—limited time only
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-10**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, on-brand bundle send with one clear offer: buy 3, save 20% with code BUNDLEUP. The "beauty math / rule of threes" hero with the orange "buy 3 get 20% off" burst puts the offer first. The maroon announcement bar repeats it. It's the second straight bundle-style push after "Add 3, subtract 20%" on 10-09, and it follows the 10-07 duo send. The offer is the same one, only reworded.
- The layout is tidy, but the body works against the promise. A "buy any three" offer needs help picking three. The "prime picks" grid shows four singles with no prices. The "multiples" section shows three pre-built trios and sets, also with no prices. The email never shows what 20% off actually saves. The deadline is only in the fine print at the bottom (sale ends 10/13), so "limited time only" has no real urgency. There are three CTA styles (a filled maroon button, outlined "shop now" buttons, and a second "use code" button), which dilutes the click.
- Judgement: solid and honest, but repetitive. The subscriber got this offer yesterday. It would work better with prices, a visible deadline, and one lead product.

## What's working

- The hero ties the offer to the creative. "Rule of threes" with a trio of products and a "buy 3 get 20% off" burst makes the offer hard to miss.
- The code BUNDLEUP is in the top bar, the body copy and the closing button, so there is no hunting for it.
- The "multiples" section answers "what three?" with ready-made trios: Eye Stylus Trio, Art of the Eye Trio, The Hero Set.
- The product photography is clean and consistent, and the serif section heads (prime picks, multiples) match the brand voice.
- The footer trust block is strong: free shipping over $50, Leaping Bunny, 1% for the Planet, ILIA VIP, auto-replenish.

## What's weak

- No prices appear anywhere, not even a "from $X" or a "bundle price." The 20% is abstract.
- The deadline only appears in the footer fine print ("Sale ends 11:59pm 10/13/26"). The hero says "limited time only" with no date.
- The CTA treatments are inconsistent: a filled "ADD 3 TO CART" button, outlined "shop now" and "SHOP NOW" buttons, and a repeated "USE CODE" button. "Shop now" is generic. "ADD 3 TO CART" suggests a cart action that probably just goes to a landing page.
- The four prime picks have no color, shade or review cues. There is no social proof anywhere, such as ratings or "bestseller" labels.
- The "multiples" blocks zigzag, so the buttons don't line up and the eye jumps around. The sets don't show what's inside or what you save.
- The offer is a near-repeat of 10-09's "Add 3, subtract 20%." Cadence fatigue is a real risk.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add prices and the savings to every product, for example "$34 → $27.20 with BUNDLEUP." This is the biggest lift, because it makes the 20% concrete and gives people a reason to build a basket.
- 2. Put the deadline in the hero ("Ends Monday 10/13") and drop the vague "limited time only."
- 3. Standardize the CTAs to one style with specific copy, such as "Build your trio" or "Shop the Eye Trio."
- 4. Add a rating or "bestseller" tag to each of the prime picks.
- 5. Show one hero bundle with the three items named and the price math, instead of two sections of products.
- 6. Space promos out. Don't send the same offer on back-to-back days without new creative or a new angle.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Any 3 ILIA picks, 20% off through Monday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your 3-product bundle, 20% off (code inside)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Skin tint, lash mascara, eye stylus: mix any three. Ends 10/13.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Code BUNDLEUP takes 20% off at checkout. Trios and bestsellers inside.`
