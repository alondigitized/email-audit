# Clean Beauty 101
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-04**
## Executive summary

- This is ILIA swapping its recent tight, single-product storytelling (the Thru Line eyeliner ingredient arc that ran 07-22 through 08-01, peaking at 9/10) for a scattershot back-to-school bundle. "Clean Beauty 101" leans on a clever pencil-case visual metaphor — makeup brushes styled as pencils in a ceramic pencil holder — but then stacks four separate products, a repackaged mention of the still-fresh Thru Line eyeliner ("New Pencils"), and a totally different mechanic (student/teacher discount) into one email.
- The hero concept is genuinely fun and on-brand for August timing. But where "NEW Thru Line Waterproof Eyeliner" (07-28) and "It's Over(glaze) tonight" (07-26) worked because they had one clear hero and one clear ask, this send has six competing CTAs and two different offers (free gift + 15% edu discount) fighting for the same real estate. It reads like a monthly roundup newsletter, not a targeted push.
- Solid execution, unfocused strategy. Given this is the sixth ILIA send in nine days per the brand history, subscribers are more likely to skim past a multi-product catalog email than they were to click a single hero reveal.

## What's working

- The hero image cleverly reframes makeup brushes and a product organizer as pencils in a pencil cup — a genuinely on-theme back-to-school visual, not just stock photography with text slapped on.
- Each of the four featured products gets a one-line, benefit-forward description (SPF 40, 24-hour wear, 12-hour wear) instead of generic copy.
- Reusing the Thru Line eyeliner as "New Pencils" keeps that launch's momentum alive without needing a whole new dedicated send.
- The "ilia edu" student/teacher 15% discount is a genuinely new acquisition angle not seen in any of the last six sends.
- Footer trust badges (1% for the Planet, Leaping Bunny, Pact Collective, ILIA VIP) render cleanly and reinforce brand credibility.

## What's weak

- There's no single primary CTA — "Turn the Page," four "Shop Now" buttons, and "Sign Up" all compete, so the eye has nowhere obvious to land after the hero.
- The top banner offer reads "FREE MINI EYE STYLUS W/ $75+*" — "stylus" is an odd, unclear word choice for what's presumably a mini eyeliner or pencil; it undersells its own gift-with-purchase.
- The subject line "Clean Beauty 101" gives no hint of either live offer (free gift or 15% student discount) sitting in the body.
- Both offers carry an asterisk requiring a trip to the site for details — mildly buried fine print stacked twice in one email.
- Bundling a general product roundup with a niche student/teacher discount dilutes both — most subscribers aren't students/teachers, and the "edu" block sits below four unrelated Shop Now buttons where it's easy to miss.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one hero instead of four: lead with either the free-gift banner offer or the ilia edu discount, and cut the four-product carousel down to the single item tied to that offer — this is the single biggest lift, since it turns six competing CTAs into one clear ask.
- 2. Clarify the gift copy — name the actual product ("Free Mini Eyeliner") instead of the vague "stylus," which reads like a typo or placeholder.
- 3. Give the subject line a concrete hook tied to one of the two live offers rather than a generic "101" framing.
- 4. Add a real deadline to at least one offer ("free gift ends 8/8") — nothing in the render signals urgency.
- 5. Spin "ilia edu" into its own targeted send to a student/teacher segment rather than appending it to a general routine-refresh roundup, where it's currently competing for attention it won't get.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Students + teachers: 15% off clean beauty`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus new-semester picks for your everyday routine`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your fresh routine, four picks, one free gift`
