# Did you forget something?
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- Skechers is running "Did you forget something?" again — the same subject line last sent on 5/19, confirmed in the June 6 audit. Six weeks later, with zero creative refresh, it lands like a copy-paste from the archive. The brand has now recycled three subject lines back-to-back ("This is perfect for you!" on 5/26 and 6/29, "This style has limited availability!" on 5/27 and 6/30, and now this one), which is a systemic problem, not a one-off slip. Any engaged subscriber paying even marginal attention will clock the repetition.
- The email body itself is structurally competent — three specific products above the fold, two clean CTAs, and a 20% off offer buried mid-scroll. But the discount has a critical disclosure problem: the banner reads "20% OFF A SINGLE PAIR" without signaling that it's a Skechers Plus exclusive. The restriction only surfaces in fine-print footer copy. A non-member who clicks expecting 20% off and finds out they need a membership will feel misled.
- On net: the creative bones are fine, the offer is real, and the product selection is specific enough to suggest genuine browse data. But the recycled subject and membership-gated discount with no upfront disclosure drag it from serviceable to frustrating.

## What's working

- Three specific named products shown (Glide-Step Pro, BOBS Sport Solid – Airy Stride, Mommy & Me Glide-Step Pro) — this reads as actual browse/cart data, not generic inventory push.
- "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" is clear, action-oriented, and placed twice — above and below the hero products.
- 20% off is a meaningful incentive that not every email in this sequence has included.
- No render bugs: images load, layout is clean, text is legible throughout.
- Skechers Plus mention is present (even if buried) — loyalty members can anchor to a real benefit.

## What's weak

- "Did you forget something?" is a direct recycled subject from 5/19 — this is not A/B testing, this is autopilot. Against a subscriber who got the 5/19 version, it reads as broken automation.
- The 20% off banner doesn't disclose the Plus-only restriction — it only appears in fine-print footer. That's a setup for disappointment.
- No deadline on the offer. "20% off a single pair" with no expiry kills urgency and removes any reason to act now versus later (or never).
- The secondary product grid (six thumbnails below the main trio) dilutes focus and pushes the discount banner further down the scroll.
- No seasonal or contextual hook — it's July 1 and this could have been sent on any day in the last three months.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Retire this subject line permanently.** It ran 5/19. Resending is automation failure, not strategy. The next send needs product-specific copy or the offer amount in the subject.
- 2. **Surface the 20% off in the hero, not just a mid-scroll banner.** "READY TO CHECK OUT? — Take 20% off your saved shoes" collapses the offer and the CTA into the first thing the eye hits.
- 3. **Disclose the Plus requirement on the banner itself** — "20% OFF for Plus Members" is one line that eliminates the bait-and-switch perception. Non-members seeing the uncaveated banner and clicking into a wall is a loyalty destroyer.
- 4. **Add a hard deadline.** "Offer expires July 3" is enough to trigger the send — without it, the discount is decorative.
- 5. **Cut the six-thumbnail grid.** It adds visual weight without adding clarity. Two CTAs (COMPLETE YOUR ORDER × 2) plus three hero products is already enough; the grid just scrolls the 20% banner further from view.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your saved shoes: 20% off through July 3`
- **Subject Alt B:** `[Glide-Step Pro] is waiting — 20% off today only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus Members: your exclusive discount expires Thursday`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Three styles you checked out — now 20% off with your Plus account`
