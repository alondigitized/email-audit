# Your Shortcut to Beach Waves 💞
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-11**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, competent send from Sally Beauty promoting beach waves styling tools with a "Buy two, get one free" offer on select hair care through May 22. The visual hierarchy is solid — the hero headline is legible, the model shot is aspirational and on-brand, and the BOGO offer is the clear anchor. It reads as a spring/summer seasonal campaign timed well.
- The problem is that it's built for a beauty enthusiast, not a neutral observer. The product grid below the fold is dense and assumes you already know what a "Ceramic Adjustable Deep Waver" or "Purple Conditioner for Blonde" is. There's no single breakout product with enough detail to compel a click — it's a catalog dump. The email gets you to the offer but doesn't close the deal.
- Honest verdict: a decent mid-tier promotional email. Competent execution, missed opportunity in the lower half.

## What's working

- **Hero copy is tight and on-brief** — "Skip the salon. Keep the beach waves." lands fast and supports the summer seasonal angle.
- **BOGO offer is clearly stated and deadline-stamped** — "Buy two, get one free — select hair care — Now through May 22" is visible and specific.
- **Model photography is high quality** — the long, wavy blonde hair is aspirationally on-theme and contextualizes the tools immediately.
- **Top nav is functional** — Hair Color / Hair Care / Textured Hair Care / Nails categories are all visible, reducing bounce for users who came for something else.
- **"Shop now" CTA is unambiguous** — primary button is above the fold, clear label, no mystery.

## What's weak

- **Product grid is shallow** — tools are shown as small thumbnail + price + "Shop now" with no detail (barrel size, heat settings, key benefits). Nothing convinces you which tool is right for your hair.
- **BOGO applies to hair care, not the styling tools** — the hero promotes tools, but the main offer is on a different category. This disconnect is confusing and buries the real deal.
- **No social proof anywhere** — no star ratings, no "best seller" badges, no UGC. For styling tools at $44–$49, that's a gap.
- **Lower product grid is generic** — four hair care products (conditioner, argan oil, dark brown hair building fibers, shimmer toning mask) have no connective copy explaining why they belong together.
- **App download promo competes for attention at the bottom** — placed too high relative to the email's purpose; it dilutes the closing CTA.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Clarify what the BOGO actually covers** — if the tools aren't included, say so explicitly in the hero section so users don't feel bait-switched when they click. One line under the CTA: *"BOGO offer applies to hair care products."*
- 2. **Add one hero product callout with specs** — pick the Ceramic Deep Waver or the Curling Iron and give it two lines of copy (barrel size, heat range, hair type). That's the detail that converts browsers.
- 3. **Inject star ratings on the two featured tools** — even "4.7★ (312 reviews)" next to the price dramatically increases click confidence.
- 4. **Give the BOGO product grid a theme** — tie the four hair care products to beach waves maintenance ("keep your waves looking good"). Right now they feel random.
- 5. **Move the app download to post-footer** — it's distracting in the email body and dilutes the purchase funnel.
- **Subject Alt A:** `BOGO on hair care + your beach wave tools are here`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Get beach waves at home — tools + BOGO deal inside`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Buy 2 hair care, get 1 free through May 22 — plus shop curlers & wavers`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The right tools + a BOGO deal — beach waves don't require a salon appointment`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — `img { white-space: pre; }` (global selector)**
```css
img { -ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic; white-space: pre; }
```
`white-space: pre` on a replaced element (`<img>`) has no defined effect in the CSS spec but is non-standard and can trigger unexpected whitespace gaps in Gecko-based renderers. Should be removed.

**Issue 2 — Missing `@font-face` for `Founders Grotesk`**
```css
a { font-family: 'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }
```
Six `@font-face` declarations load Open Sans variants, but `Founders Grotesk` is never declared. Every anchor tag silently falls back to Helvetica. Either add the `@font-face` rule or correct the font stack.

**Issue 3 — External Google Fonts CDN dependency**
Font files are fetched from `fonts.gstatic.com` at render time. Corporate mail gateways and some privacy-hardened clients (Hey, ProtonMail, certain Outlook configurations) block external resource loads, stripping the font entirely with no in-email fallback stack declared on the `@font-face` rules themselves.
