# Shop deals up to 40% off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-23**
## Executive summary

- This is Sally Beauty's seventh email in roughly fifteen days, and the cadence is now the biggest liability before the first pixel loads. The hero delivers: "20% off sitewide" reads cleanly at the fold, the styling-tools imagery is sharp, and the June 26 deadline is surfaced in the body. But the subject ("Shop deals up to 40% off") and the hero are immediately in tension — you open expecting 40% off everything, you land on 20% sitewide, and the 40% deal is the first card *below* the fold. That's not bait-and-switch, but it creates a micro-disappointment at exactly the wrong moment.
- Below the hero, four separate deal types stack with no priority among them — 40% styling, 40% ion hair care, 20% fragrance, BOGO 60% hair care — and the strongest mechanic (BOGO 60%) is the *last* card before the app promo. The June 15 "More Beauty, Less Spend" and June 22 "Summer's Brightest Finds" emails made the same structural mistake. Three sprawling digests in eight days is a pattern, not a coincidence.
- Verdict: Technically competent but strategically diffuse. The deals are real, the render is clean, and the deadline is credible — but the email buries its best offer, mismatches its subject to its hero, and shows up on the wrong day of an exhausting cadence.

## What's working

- **Clean hero hierarchy** — "20% off sitewide" dominates the fold; eye lands on the offer without hunting.
- **Credible deadline in the body** — "Ends June 26" is prominent and not buried in fine print.
- **Four distinct deal types** — styling, hair care, fragrance, and BOGO give different interest segments a reason to act.
- **Free 2-hour delivery module** — a logistics benefit that closes the loop for in-market shoppers already browsing alternatives.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, overlapping text, or layout bugs visible.

## What's weak

- **Subject-hero mismatch** — subject leads with "up to 40% off" but the hero serves 20% sitewide; the more generous number is buried.
- **Best offer last** — BOGO 60% on hair care is the fourth and final deal card; most readers won't scroll that far.
- **No deadline in the subject** — the June 26 deadline never appears above the fold in the inbox; free open-rate left on the table.
- **Cadence fatigue** — seventh email in ~15 days; loyal subscribers are beginning to auto-archive this sender.
- **Generic sitewide CTA** — "Shop Deals" routes to the homepage; each deal module should link directly to its category.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move BOGO 60% to the hero** — it's the strongest deal mechanic and it's currently the last card. Lead with the best offer; the 20% sitewide becomes the floor, not the ceiling.
- 2. **Add the deadline to the subject** — appending "Ends June 26" to the current subject alone would lift opens from deadline-motivated subscribers.
- 3. **Make each deal card link directly** — "40% off styling deals" should go to the styling category, not sitewide. Decision paralysis kills the click the deal earns.
- 4. **Break the digest cadence** — three sprawling multi-deal emails in eight days trains subscribers to skim and skip. The June 20 focused It's a 10 drop outperformed this format; use this digest format no more than twice a month.
- 5. **Reorder the deal stack even if the hero stays** — even without a hero change, leading the modules with BOGO 60% then 40% off would surface the best deal before scroll fatigue sets in.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Ends June 26: BOGO 60% hair care + 40% off styling`
- **Subject Alt B:** `20% off sitewide + our biggest hair deal of summer — ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Hair care, styling tools, fragrances — all on sale through June 26`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus free 2-hour delivery on $50+ orders. Don't wait.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — Undeclared font family on all anchor elements (confirmed)**

```css
a {
  font-family: 'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif;
}
```

`Founders Grotesk` has no corresponding `@font-face` declaration and no external stylesheet link anywhere in the `<head>`. Every `<a>` in the email will render in Helvetica/system sans-serif fallback on all clients — including desktop Outlook, Gmail, and Apple Mail. This is likely unintended and affects all CTA buttons that use `<a>` tags.

**Issue — `white-space: pre` on `img` elements**

```css
img { -ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic; white-space: pre; }
```

`white-space: pre` is a non-standard property for `<img>` elements (it applies to text nodes). It has no practical rendering effect on images but is dead code that may indicate a template copy/paste error. Low severity.

**Issue — Stale Google Fonts API path**

All six `@font-face` declarations reference `fonts.gstatic.com/s/opensans/v17/` — the v17 path from the 2020-era Google Fonts API. These URLs are still served but point to older font file versions. Not a deliverability or rendering blocker, but worth updating if this template is reused.

**Issue — Mobile breakpoint at 499px (non-standard)**

```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 499px) { ... }
```

The industry standard breakpoint is 600px. Devices with screen widths between 500–599px (common on Android) will receive the desktop layout, which may not be designed to reflow at that width. Assess whether this is intentional.

**Positive — Defensive meta tags present**

- `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` — prevents iOS from auto-resizing text
- `X-UA-Compatible: IE=edge` inside MSO conditional comment — correct pattern
- Gmail dark-mode workaround present: `u ~ div .pattern { min-width: 100vw !important; }`
