# What's that you smell?
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-14**
## Executive summary

- This is a catalog-style fragrance showcase from Lush — six distinct scent lines (Good Common Scents, Rose Jam, Dirty, Super Milk, Sticky Dates, Seize the Spray) each getting their own color-coded section, plus a "29 High Street" closing module. The design is clean and on-brand: each section pairs a lifestyle model shot with two product images and a shop button. Lush's visual identity is intact throughout.
- The core problem is the same problem every catalog email has: no reason to act now. There is no offer, no discount, no launch urgency — just six product lines competing for the same finite attention span. By the time you reach Sticky Dates, you've forgotten Rose Jam. The email reads as a brand refresher, not a conversion driver.
- The subject "What's that you smell?" is appropriately playful for Lush but tells you nothing about what's inside and creates zero urgency. This is a pleasant email to receive from a brand you already love; it would struggle to pull in anyone lukewarm.

## What's working

- **Consistent visual identity** — color blocking per fragrance line (pink for Rose Jam, green for Dirty, yellow for Super Milk) makes the email scannable and distinctly Lush.
- **Lifestyle + product pairing** — every section shows a model using the product alongside a clean product shot, which grounds the fragrance in real use.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no layout collapse, no overlapping text visible anywhere.
- **Subject line length** — 22 characters is well under the mobile truncation threshold; it will display fully in every inbox.
- **Multiple entry points** — six product lines means six different audiences can each find something relevant; good for broad list hygiene.

## What's weak

- **No offer anywhere** — not a single dollar off, no free shipping threshold, no GWP. Pure browse bait with no hook to convert.
- **Decision paralysis** — six named product lines in one send dilutes every section. None gets enough real estate to build desire.
- **Subject is curiosity-only, not useful** — "What's that you smell?" is charming but completely non-specific. It doesn't tell the reader whether this is a new launch, a sale, or just a Tuesday email.
- **CTAs are generic** — the shop buttons in each section appear to say some variant of "Shop Now" — no product-specific copy to create pull.
- **29 High Street module is unclear** — the dark blue closing section is visually distinct but the purpose (store? product? brand story?) isn't legible in the render.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one hero fragrance and build the email around it.** Rose Jam or Dirty — whichever is the current top-seller — gets the full email. Catalog sends should be quarterly; this frequency with this depth of catalog trains subscribers to scroll-and-delete.
- 2. **Add a reason to act now.** New formula? Seasonal bath bomb pairing? Limited shade? Even "free shipping on orders over $35" in the header bar would lift click rate.
- 3. **Rewrite the CTA buttons to be product-specific.** "Shop Rose Jam" beats "Shop Now" every time.
- 4. **Clarify the 29 High Street section.** If it's a store, link to the locator. If it's a product, name it in the headline.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Rose Jam season starts now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your next shower obsession is here`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Six scents. One for every mood — shop the full lineup.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New to Dirty? Addicted to Rose Jam? We've got both.`
