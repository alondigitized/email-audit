# Going back to school?
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-20**
## Executive summary

- This is Glossier pivoting hard away from the "Cheek Week" discount arc into a "back to school" editorial bundle — three "essentials" (Boy Brow, Lash Slick, Stretch Balm Concealer) framed as a routine, with no discount on any of them. The "Shop + save" hero CTA promises savings but the copy never states a number, and the products shown below aren't bundled or priced — it's a bait-and-switch on the one word that would drive urgency. Compare to 8/11-8/18's Cheek Week run, which led with a concrete 20%-off mechanic every single time; this send trades that clarity for vague seasonal vibes.
- The "back to school" hook itself is a stretch for a beauty brand's actual audience (adult skincare/makeup buyers, not students), and nothing in the visuals (all close-up beauty shots, no backpacks/dorm/first-day framing beyond the headline) reinforces the theme. The two "even more sets" at the bottom do carry a real "Save 20%" — but that's buried after five screens of scrolling, undercutting the hero's "+ save" promise instead of delivering it up front.
- Judgement: this is a step down from the Cheek Week closeout (8/18, 8.0/10) — no visible discount where the hero implies one, a weak seasonal conceit, and the actual offer hidden below the fold.

## What's working

- Clean, consistent product photography with the recognizable Glossier white/pink aesthetic throughout.
- Each "essential" section has a clear single CTA button ("Choose your shade," "Get yours") tied to one specific product.
- The bottom "Even more sets" module does show a concrete "Save 20%" offer on two bundles.
- No rendering bugs — images load, text doesn't overlap, layout is clean and legible.

## What's weak

- Hero CTA says "Shop + save" but no discount, price, or percentage appears anywhere near the hero or the three essentials — the "save" promise is unfulfilled until deep in the scroll.
- "Going back to school?" is a weak persona fit for a beauty brand's core buyer; the visuals never reinforce the school theme (no backpack, planner, dorm, or student styling cues).
- The real offer (20% off skincare/body sets) is buried at the very bottom, after three unrelated single-item pushes — most readers won't scroll that far.
- Compared to the immediately preceding Cheek Week arc, this is a regression in offer clarity: 8/11 through 8/18 all led with a stated percentage; this one leads with an implied one that never materializes.

## Recommendations

- 1. Either put an actual number on the hero ("20% off your school-year set") or drop "+ save" from the CTA entirely — a vague savings promise with no follow-through erodes trust after four straight discount-led sends.
- 2. Move the 20%-off bundles from the bottom to right under the hero, since that's the only real offer in this email — don't make it the ninth screen.
- 3. Replace "Going back to school?" with a hook this audience actually responds to (routine-building, "transition to fall," or just keep leaning into the proven discount language from Cheek Week).
- 4. Consider bundling the three essentials into one priced set with a single savings figure instead of three separate individually-priced CTAs — reinforces "kit" framing and gives the save claim something to point to.
