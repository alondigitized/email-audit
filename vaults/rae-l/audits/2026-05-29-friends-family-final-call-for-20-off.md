# Friends + Family, final call for 20% off
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-29**
## Executive summary

- This email lands in a folder dated May 29 with a P.S. that reads "Sale ends at 11:59pm 5/28/26" — meaning a subscriber who clicks any product CTA after midnight is met with full-priced items or a dead code. That's the headline problem. Whether this is a timezone artifact or a genuine scheduling miss, it transforms an otherwise strong send into a trust-eroding dead end.
- Set the expired timing aside for a moment and this is genuinely good creative. The behavioral preheader ("we see you've been browsing") is the best personalization signal ILIA has deployed all campaign. The "Focal Points" product grid with before/after pricing ($46 → $38.40, etc.) is clear and specific. The structure — personal note up top, product grid below — avoids the cluttered multi-collection layouts that clogged the earlier sends.
- The real verdict: the campaign framework is sound, but after 18 prior emails — including two other "final call" sends already on May 28 ("Finishing touches—20% off ends tonight" and "20% off ends in a few hours") — ILIA has burned every urgency signal it had. Subscribers who are still paying attention are exhausted; those who aren't have already unsubscribed. This email needed to not exist.

## What's working

- Behavioral preheader ("we see you've been browsing our Friends + Family sale") earns the open — it's the sharpest personalization signal in the entire campaign.
- "The Focal Points" product grid with specific before/after pricing removes the math friction and creates clear decision moments.
- Four distinct product CTAs (each with "shop now →") give multiple entry points instead of forcing everything through a single hero CTA.
- SPF 40 callout on the Super Serum Skin Tint is product-specific, not just price-pushing.
- VIP callout in the header banner ("20% OFF FOR VIPS*") adds an exclusivity layer that doesn't undercut the sitewide offer.

## What's weak

- Sale deadline (11:59pm 5/28) appears to have passed before this email was delivered — clicking any CTA leads to an expired offer.
- This is the third "final call" email on the same day (alongside "Finishing touches—20% off ends tonight" and "20% off ends in a few hours" on 5/28); the urgency signal is completely burned.
- No social proof anywhere — the product grid shows prices but zero star ratings or review counts to justify the click.
- The "SHOP NOW →" body CTA sits in a fairly low-contrast dark box that could be missed between the personal note and the product grid.
- Sale end date buried in the P.S. in small type — subscriber won't see the 5/28 deadline until after they've been sold on clicking.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the send-time automation.** If this was a browse-abandonment retargeting trigger, it needed to fire by 8pm on 5/28, not after midnight. Audit the trigger logic immediately — sending expired-offer emails destroys trust faster than any creative win can recover.
- 2. **Consolidate the final-day emails.** "Finishing touches—20% off ends tonight" was already doing this job at a 9/10. The third "last chance" email on the same day cannibalizes credibility without adding lift.
- 3. **Surface the end date in the hero, not the P.S.** "Offer ends tonight at 11:59pm" belongs in the banner next to "LAST CHANCE," not in footnote text.
- 4. **Add one social proof line under the hero product.** "★★★★★ 4.8k reviews" under the Super Serum Skin Tint SPF 40 would justify the click far better than the price drop alone.
- 5. **Make the body CTA button higher contrast.** The current dark-box "SHOP NOW →" blends into the page — use the brand's signature coral/pink to make it pop.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Tonight only: 20% off ends at midnight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your cart is still waiting — 20% off until midnight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `You browsed the Super Serum Tint. Here's your last shot at 20% off.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free mini gift with any order — while supplies last tonight`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title> </title>
```
Contains only whitespace. Screen readers announce this as the document title; some clients (Apple Mail tabs, Samsung Mail) surface it directly to users. Should contain a human-readable subject or brand name.

**Google Fonts `@import` blocked by Gmail**
```css
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```
Gmail strips `@import` rules inside `<style>` blocks. Montserrat will not load for Gmail users (the majority of consumer email opens). The stack `"Montserrat", "Helvetica", sans-serif` correctly falls back, so rendering degrades gracefully, but the brand font is absent for most recipients.

**Klaviyo custom font import — external dependency**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
```
Same Gmail-strip issue applies. If this custom font is load-bearing for brand rendering, it will also silently fail in Gmail.

**Global link `text-decoration: none` on all states**
```css
a:not([name]) { color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none }
a:link { … text-decoration:none }
a:visited { … text-decoration:none }
a:hover { … text-decoration:none }
```
No visual affordance distinguishes links from body text in hover or visited states. WCAG 1.4.1 (Use of Color) requires links to be distinguishable from non-link text by more than color alone when inline with body copy. If any CTAs rely on underline for context, this suppresses them globally.

**Link color contrast — unverified**
`#5f4b3c` on `#FAF7F4` (body background) yields a contrast ratio of approximately 5.0:1 — passes WCAG AA for normal text but should be spot-checked against any lighter section backgrounds used deeper in the email.

**Preheader Unicode padding — minor spam signal**
```html
<div style="display:none;…">This is the finishing touch ͏ ͏ ͏ ͏ …</div>
```
Uses Unicode combining characters (U+034F) for preview text padding. This is a standard Klaviyo pattern, but certain content filters (Barracuda, Proofpoint) flag high-density runs of non-printable Unicode. Not a critical issue; worth monitoring deliverability.
