# How to find your shade
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-27**
## Executive summary

- This is a useful, well-structured shade-finding email. The "Match Game" hero is a strong editorial image: a model in red holding the Super Serum Skin Tint against a blue sky. The body then does the work. Four complexion products sit in a repeating module (image, "First Impressions / Best Qualities / Long Term", Shop Now), followed by a "Step 2: Find Your Shade" block with a quiz and a selfie tool. It is a clean education-to-conversion flow.
- The commercial offer is the weak part. The only offer is a free mini mascara with a $75+ order, which has nothing to do with shade matching. It shows up in a thin top banner and again as a late block. After the 10-19 "Before the bag disappears" send and the other VIP-gift emails, this is another gift-with-purchase. The product modules also have no prices and no shade swatches beyond one or two. The email is long, and the quiz, which is the most useful tool for the subject, sits about two-thirds of the way down.
- Judgement: a good, honest utility email that should perform near the brand's average. The quiz is buried and the product blocks have no pricing, so it doesn't reach the top of the range.

## What's working

- The subject "How to find your shade" is plain and promises a clear benefit.
- The "Match Game" hero is high quality and puts the hero product front and center.
- Each of the four product modules uses the same three-line framing (first impressions, best qualities, long term), so they are easy to compare.
- The "Step 1 / Step 2" structure gives the email a logical path from coverage type to shade.
- The quiz and selfie options in the peach block serve different comfort levels, and the "Compare Shades" link covers existing favorites.
- Trust badges at the bottom (Leaping Bunny, 1% for the Planet, VIP) and the free-shipping line at $50+ reduce purchase friction.

## What's weak

- The hero text "The Match Game" is thin and sits over a busy orange-red background, which makes it hard to read at small size.
- The quiz and selfie tool come after all four product modules, so the most useful action is far below the first screen.
- The four product blocks have no prices, ratings or shade ranges. They also have repetitive "Shop Now" buttons, so there is no signal to choose one product over another.
- The mini mascara gift has nothing to do with the shade-finding theme, and its fine print ("See site for details") is small.
- The email repeats the gift-with-purchase offer again after the 10-16, 10-17 and 10-19 VIP gift sends, which adds to cadence fatigue.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the quiz and selfie block to just under the hero and make "Take the Quiz" the primary CTA. That is the strongest feature for this subject.
- 2. Add prices, star ratings and the number of shades to each product module, and change "Shop Now" to a specific label such as "Shop Skin Tint" or "Shop Concealer."
- 3. Tie the gift to the theme, or drop it from the top banner. A shade-match-guarantee line would fit better than a mascara.
- 4. Put the hero text in a solid-color band or a larger, bolder weight.
- 5. Add one review or testimonial per product, such as "Found my match in 2 minutes."

# Technical Audit: ILIA, "How to find your shade"

## 1. Technical Summary
Klaviyo-built HTML with a standard hidden preheader and a tracked-image pixel. The only confirmed code-level issues are a missing `lang` attribute, an empty `<title>`, and two images without `alt`. The header-based compliance and authentication checks could not be verified from the captured data.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 12 material links were probed and 16 were skipped, as deprioritized footer and social links. The QA output reports no failed probes, so no broken links are confirmed.
- The HTML source was truncated, so I could not inspect individual `href` values. I am not flagging redirect chains or malformed URLs.
- Tracking uses the first-party domain `trk.send.iliabeauty.com`, which is consistent with Klaviyo click and open tracking. No issues found.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing `lang` attribute:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" ...>` has no `lang`, which screen readers need to choose pronunciation.
- **Empty title:** `<title> </title>` contains only whitespace.
- **Missing alt text, content image:** `https://d3k81ch9hvuctc.cloudfront.net/company/SguDsz/images/c9cefca7-6b18-443a-896b-9e98ec5d842a.jpeg`. If the image carries content, it needs descriptive `alt`. If it is decorative, it needs `alt=""`.
- **Missing alt text, tracking pixel:** `https://trk.send.iliabeauty.com/o/01M5YX7HF2Q4RRMNWHWF9T7H96` is the open-tracking pixel. It should carry `alt=""` so it isn't announced.
- **`@import` web fonts:** `@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/.../custom_fonts.css)` and `@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat...)` are not supported in Gmail and several other clients. The declared fallbacks are `"Montserrat", "Helvetica", sans-serif`, so degradation is graceful. This is low severity.
- The viewport meta tag, MSO conditional comments, and the `#outlook a` and `-webkit-text-size-adjust` resets are present and correct.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. The visible source contains no unrendered tokens such as `{{ }}`, `{% %}`, or `*|...|*`. The truncation limits how far this can be confirmed.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` not found:** the QA check says the relay may not capture it. This is unverified, not confirmed missing. Check the raw message at the sending ESP.
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` not found:** RFC 8058 one-click support is unverified. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require it, so confirm it at the source.
- **`Authentication-Results` not found:** SPF, DKIM, and DMARC status is unknown. The cause is likely the AgentMail relay stripping the header, so this is a tooling gap rather than a sender defect.
- An unsubscribe link was included in the link probing. The QA output reports no failure for it, but I could not confirm the footer postal address from the truncated source.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Cannot be assessed. The truncated HTML exposes no destination URLs or UTM parameters, and no landing-page probe results were reported. I am not flagging an issue.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add `lang="en"` to `<html>` and a meaningful `<title>`, for example "How to find your shade".
2. Set descriptive `alt` text on the CloudFront image, or `alt=""` if it is decorative. Add `alt=""` to the tracking pixel.
3. Verify `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` on the raw message from the ESP, and verify SPF, DKIM, and DMARC alignment for `iliabeauty.com` outside the relay.
4. Re-run the audit with untruncated HTML so UTM parameters, `href` validity, and footer address compliance can be checked.
5. Optional: swap `@import` fonts for `<link>` tags, or accept the system-font fallback in Gmail.
