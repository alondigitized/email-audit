# NEW seasonal sets are here
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-27**
## Executive summary

- This is ILIA's most tightly art-directed send of the streak, and it's a clear step up from anything since "In fall tones" (09-26). "Perennial Beauty" is a full holiday-set launch dressed in the brand's California-flora aesthetic — sun-bleached sky, orange trumpet-flower branch, warm sand — and it holds that visual thread consistently through six product sections. It's photo-rich, on-brand, and never once collapses into the plain-text format that plagued 09-14/09-17/09-20.
- The problem is structural, not visual: this is a six-section product catalog crammed into one email with no single hero offer, no price anchor, and no urgency. "NEW seasonal sets are here" promises novelty, not savings — there's no visible %/$ off anywhere in the render, which is a meaningful departure from ILIA's usual VIP-sale playbook (09-21, 09-22, 09-23 all led with "20% off"). It reads more like a lookbook than a conversion email.
- Solid production, weak conversion mechanics. This lands mid-pack — better crafted than the brand's recent duds, but missing the offer clarity that made 09-19 and 09-22 the standouts of the streak.

## What's working

- Consistent seasonal art direction (sky, orange blossoms, sand, coastal rock) ties all six product sections together into one coherent story.
- Clear visual hierarchy at the top: hero banner → headline → single red CTA button ("SHOP THE COLLECTION") before any product clutter.
- Good use of section labels ("The Palettes," "The Mascara Sets," "The Lip Sets," "The Eye Sets") with short, benefit-forward copy under each.
- "1% for the Planet" cause-marketing block adds credibility and breaks up the product grid visually.
- No render bugs — images, text, and layout all display cleanly with no overlap or broken assets.

## What's weak

- No concrete offer anywhere in the visible email — no %/$ off, no bundle price, no "starting at $X." For a brand that just ran three straight 20%-off VIP pushes, this is a jarring gap.
- Six near-identical product sections in a row (palettes, mascara, lip, eye) with no single hero product creates a flat, scroll-heavy experience — nothing to look at first.
- Only one CTA button appears (top of email); everything below it is images and text with no repeated buy button until the reader is deep into the scroll.
- Top banner promo ("FREE MINI EYE STYLUS IN BEAM W/ $75+") is the only real incentive in the email but is buried in tiny text above the hero image — easy to miss on mobile.
- Subject line "NEW seasonal sets are here" is generic — doesn't name a single product, price, or reason to open now.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with one hero set and its price/bundle value instead of six flat sections — pick the best-selling palette or a "starter set" and give it the full hero treatment, then link out to "see all sets" for the rest.
- 2. Surface the $75+ free-gift offer from the tiny top banner into the hero copy block itself — that's the only real incentive in this email and it's currently invisible on first glance.
- 3. Add a second CTA button lower in the email (after "The Lip Sets" or "The Eye Sets") so scrollers don't have to travel back to the top to buy.
- 4. Add urgency or scarcity language somewhere — "limited edition" appears once in body copy but never in subject/hero; last year's palettes reportedly sold out, which is a compelling reason to act now and isn't being used.
- 5. Tighten the subject line to name the incentive or a specific product.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free mini stylus + new holiday sets, just landed`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The sold-out palette is back (limited edition)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free mini eye stylus with $75+ — plus 5 new sets inspired by SoCal`
