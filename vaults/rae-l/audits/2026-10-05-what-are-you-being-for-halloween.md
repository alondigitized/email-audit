# What are you being for Halloween?
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-05**
## Executive summary

- This is Glossier's best idea in weeks. It's a Halloween costume-makeup guide with three looks: Elizabeth Bennet, Porcelain Doll and Goddess Athena. Each look has a short how-to and three shoppable products. It solves a real seasonal problem ("what am I being?") and doesn't just push a single SKU. It's also the first send in this run with no gift-with-purchase and no award news hook, so it reads as content rather than promotion.
- The execution is clean but low-energy. The hero is a close-up of an open mouth with a flat "Halloween's creeping up" headline. It's pretty but not clearly Halloween. There's no offer, no urgency and no loyalty hook, and the hero CTA is a generic "Shop now". Every one of the nine products has the same "Shop now" button, so nothing stands out. The looks are also very natural, which makes them hard to read as costumes. Elizabeth Bennet is basically "no makeup", and Athena is "keep the rest simple".
- Verdict: a solid, useful editorial send that will get opens on the subject line, but it's weak on conversion mechanics. It beats the 10/2 teaser and the 10/3 "Your fall face awaits" catalog on relevance and timing. It still lacks the concrete offer that made the 9/22 fragrance GWP send land.

## What's working

- The subject line asks a question, and the body answers it with three named costume looks.
- The three-look structure (Costume 01/02/03, each with a label, headline, short how-to and products) is easy to scan.
- The products are specific and sized to the look, such as Cloud Paint in Haze for the deep fuchsia and No. 1 Pencil in Canvas for the white liner.
- Each product has a short descriptor ("A deep fuchsia", "Tubing mascara") that gives a reason to click.
- The imagery is crisp and varied, and the models are diverse. There are no broken images or layout bugs.

## What's weak

- The hero is a tight crop of an open mouth with no clear Halloween cue. The headline is small and sits in a lot of white space.
- There's no offer, deadline or incentive anywhere. After the 9/28 "Last call! Free Mini Glossier You Wardrobe" send, a plain full-price email feels thin.
- All nine products use the same "Shop now" button and the hero uses another, so there's no verb-plus-noun CTA and no single clear next step.
- The looks are subtle, so they don't deliver the "costume" promise. There's no step-by-step, no look photo and no "get the full look" bundle.
- Boy Brow appears again. It was the hero of three sends in mid-September, so it feels repetitive.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a "Shop the whole look" CTA under each costume that links to a bundle or a pre-filled cart. A small bundle discount or a free-shipping threshold would give it the offer it's missing.
- 2. Put a finished-look photo for each costume next to its headline, so the reader sees the costume payoff before the products.
- 3. Rework the hero. Show a clear Halloween cue, such as the porcelain doll look, and use a headline that names the payoff. Add an urgency line like "Order by 10/24 for delivery before Halloween".
- 4. Replace the generic buttons with specific CTAs, such as "Get the Bennet look", "Get the Doll look" and "Get the Athena look". Keep one button per look instead of nine.
- 5. Lead with one bolder costume and rotate Boy Brow out of the lineup.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Elizabeth Bennet, Porcelain Doll and Goddess Athena, with the exact products`
