# Summer skin staples
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-16**
## Executive summary

- "Summer Skin Staples" is ILIA's pivot back into editorial mode after a dense two-week run: the HAIM celebrity arc, then back-to-back GWP closes on June 14-15. The creative is confident — a bisected light/shadow close-up hero does the seasonal skincare work cleanly, and the three-product grid below is tight and benefit-led. A new GWP (free mini multi-stick, $75+) threads through as the preheader offer, maintaining the offer cadence without making it the entire email.
- The problem is execution. The preheader banner reads "FREE MINI MULTI-STICK IN AT LAST 8) ANY $75+ ORDER" — "8)" is a broken emoji surviving right into the very first character a subscriber sees, both in the inbox and at the top of the opened email. For a brand that ran visually flawless HAIM creative across five sends, this is a QA failure at the worst possible location. Compounding it: this appears to be ILIA's second send on June 16 (after "BFF + SPF"), which means the brand is hitting some subscribers seven or more times in six days.
- The email earns its keep — the Super Serum SPF 40 + summer framing is perfectly timed and the HOT TIP on Base Face Milk is a nice editorial layer — but the broken banner and fatigue-inducing cadence both hold it back.

## What's working

- Split-face hero (lit/shadow bisect on a close-up complexion shot) is visually arresting and immediately signals "summer SPF" without needing copy to explain it.
- Three-product grid is tight and benefit-led — each line does real selling work ("seamlessly under makeup," "mineral sun protection," "hint of lip color") without padding.
- The HOT TIP callout under Base Face Milk ("Store in the fridge for extra cooling + soothing") adds editorial utility and differentiates from a plain product push.
- Seasonal timing is exact — SPF, cooling, and "sun, heat, travel" framing lands perfectly in mid-June.
- Footer trust stack (Leaping Bunny, Pact collective, 1VIP, auto-replenish) reinforces clean-beauty positioning without cluttering the body.

## What's weak

- The banner renders "8)" — a broken emoji — as the first visible character in the email. It survives into the inbox preheader and the opened header simultaneously.
- No product pricing anywhere: without a price anchor, subscribers can't assess whether $75 GWP threshold is one product or three, which depresses click confidence.
- "SHOP SKINCARE" CTA sends to a category page, not a curated summer kit landing — the product edit earns a more specific destination.
- Subject line "Summer skin staples" is pleasantly descriptive but generates no curiosity, urgency, or offer hook; the stronger GWP pull is hidden in the preheader.
- Cadence is at maximum strain — June 11 through June 16 has been near-daily sending; a second email on June 16 risks unsubscribes among even loyal ILIA subscribers.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader emoji before anything else.** Replace "IN AT LAST 8) ANY" with clean copy — "FREE MINI MULTI-STICK WITH ANY $75+ ORDER." A broken character in the very first inbox-visible line is the single highest-priority fix.
- 2. **Move the GWP offer into the subject line.** The June 15 send "A free mini moment" scored 8/10 partly because the offer anchored the subject. "Summer skin staples" buries the hook.
- 3. **Add price anchors to the product grid** — even "From $38" under each card gives readers the mental math to know they're $75-away from the free mini.
- 4. **Link the primary CTA to a curated summer kit page**, not the generic skincare category — "Shop the Summer Kit" is more intent-matched than "Shop Skincare."
- 5. **Rest the cadence** — if "BFF + SPF" already went on June 16, this email should have been held to June 17 as the standalone send.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free mini inside — summer skin, covered`
- **Subject Alt B:** `SPF, tint, balm: your summer 3-step`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free mini multi-stick with any $75+ order. Shop the summer edit.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Cooling milk, SPF tint, and a hydrating lip balm — your summer essentials.`

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
`<title> </title>` — whitespace only. Some clients (Outlook Web, Apple Mail) surface this as the window/tab title. Should be a descriptive string or omitted.

**Google Fonts loaded via `@import` (will silently fail in Gmail)**
```css
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```
Gmail, Yahoo, and most webmail clients strip `@import` at parse time. Montserrat will not render for the majority of recipients. The declared fallback chain (`"Montserrat", "Helvetica", sans-serif`) is correct — fallbacks will fire — but this should be noted as expected behavior, not a bug.

**Klaviyo custom font `@import` has the same stripping risk**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
```
Same failure mode. Fallback fonts will render instead.

**Links are visually indistinguishable from body text (WCAG 1.4.1)**
```css
a:not([name]) { color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none }
a:link       { color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none }
a:visited    { color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none }
a:active     { color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none }
a:hover      { color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none }
```
Every link state removes underline and uses the same color as body text. WCAG 1.4.1 requires links to be distinguishable from surrounding text by more than color alone when underlines are removed. This fails that criterion. Hover state (`:hover`) also has no differentiation, which is irrelevant in most email clients but indicates the template was not designed with accessibility in mind.
