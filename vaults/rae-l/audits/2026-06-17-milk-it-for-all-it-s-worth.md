# Milk it for all it's worth 🥛
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-17**
## Executive summary

- This is Lush's Super Milk range launch email — an editorial product showcase built around the dairy pun ("Milk It") and what reads as a hair-care hero. The visual execution follows the same playbook as the banana email from June 4: bold custom display typography in the hero, clean editorial photography, and a tight grid of product pods below. It's well-produced and on-brand.
- The problem is it's a catalog masquerading as a story. The June 8 ingredient editorial (9/10) told a focused four-ingredient narrative with each raw material mapped to one hero product — you left knowing *why* each thing existed. Here, the grid dumps eight-plus Super Milk SKUs with only brief copy per card. The "why this range, why now" question never fully gets answered. The "Super Daily Perfume" block near the footer is the hardest cut: it undercuts the focused Super Milk story and feels like a late tack-on.
- No sale, no urgency, no loyalty hook — which is consistent with Lush's brand positioning, but it means this email lives or dies on its editorial hook alone, and that hook isn't as sharp as it could be.

## What's working

- **Hero type is immediately legible.** "MILK IT" in large display lettering communicates the campaign theme the instant the email loads — no confusion about what this send is about.
- **Product grid is clean and scannable.** The Super Milk pods are consistently formatted — product shot, name, short descriptor — which makes browsing effortless.
- **Brand voice is consistent.** The dairy wordplay in the subject ("Milk it for all it's worth") maps cleanly to the "Milk It" hero copy; Lush's playful tone is intact throughout.
- **"Shop Super Milk" CTA is specific.** It names the product line rather than defaulting to "Shop Now," which adds category signal and reduces cognitive load.
- **No render bugs.** Images load, text blocks are clean, layout holds — nothing visually broken in the render.

## What's weak

- **Eight-plus SKUs without a clear hero pick.** The grid gives equal weight to every variant; there's no editorial vote on which product to try first, which diffuses purchase intent.
- **"Super Daily Perfume" block near the footer is off-message.** It breaks the Super Milk throughline mid-scroll and reads like a cross-sell that didn't have a better home — it earned its own email.
- **No urgency or reason to act today.** No limited stock language, no seasonal hook, no "new launch window." Full-price, no deadline = easy to save for later and forget.
- **Hero copy doesn't answer "what does Super Milk actually do?"** The editorial intro sets tone but doesn't give a concrete benefit (repair, hydration, heat protection) that a new-to-range shopper can hang a decision on.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with one Super Milk product as the editorial hero.** Pick the strongest-selling or most-buzzed SKU, give it a full-width feature with a specific claim (e.g., "heat protection without the crunch"), then surface the rest of the range below. This is how the June 8 ingredient email hit 9/10 — four deliberate picks, not eight equivalent ones.
- 2. **Cut or defer the Super Daily Perfume block.** It belongs in its own send, ideally following up within a day or two. Keeping it here divides attention and dilutes the Super Milk story.
- 3. **Add one concrete benefit line per product pod.** Even four words ("detangles without weighing down") gives the grid functional depth and helps shoppers self-select.
- 4. **Inject a soft launch signal.** "New to Lush" or "Just launched" near the hero buys urgency without a discount — which fits Lush's no-sale-culture constraint.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New: Super Milk just landed 🥛`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your hair's new obsession has dairy vibes`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Heat protection, deep hydration — no crunchy residue.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Meet the Super Milk range — and why your hair will thank you.`
