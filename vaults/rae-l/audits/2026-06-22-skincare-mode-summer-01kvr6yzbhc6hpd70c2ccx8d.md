# Skincare Mode: Summer
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- This is ILIA's "Skincare Mode: Summer" email — a multi-product editorial send that reads more like a lookbook than a campaign. The hero is a confident beauty shot (close-up of a model with luminous skin) under the "summer" wordmark, and the body walks through four use-case categories: Skincare, SPF, Shine, and a GWP tie-in at the bottom for the Lip Sketch Sharpener. The structure is logical and the photography is strong, but the email is long and product-dense — there's no single hero offer anchoring it, which dilutes urgency.
- The SPF section is the most seasonally on-point and the most commercially useful. Everything else feels like a catalog drop rather than a prompt to act. The GWP at the bottom ("stay sharp") is a callback to the June 20 Lip Sketch Sharpener send — but landing it at the very bottom, below three sections of skincare, means most readers won't reach it.
- Verdict: solid editorial execution, weak conversion architecture. This email reinforces brand equity but doesn't ask for the order clearly enough.

## What's working

- Hero image is visually arresting — clean close-up skin photography communicates the product benefit (luminous, glowing skin) without a word.
- Seasonal framing ("summer") lands exactly when it should — mid-June, pre-holiday weekend, fits the cadence perfectly.
- SPF section ("Wear SPF Wear SPF Wear SPF") is the standout block — repetition creates emphasis, feels intentional and branded, not lazy.
- Four-category structure (Skincare → SPF → Shine → GWP) gives the email a coherent arc rather than a random product dump.
- "Stay sharp" GWP callback connects to the June 20 send, rewarding subscribers who saw that email.

## What's weak

- No single concrete offer anywhere in the email — no % off, no dollar threshold, no deadline — making the CTA "Shop Now" generic and low-urgency.
- CTA buttons are repeated per section but all resolve to the same vague "Shop Now" / "Learn More" copy; none is product-specific enough to create click intent.
- Email is very long for a scroll-heavy mobile context; the GWP hook buried at the bottom will be missed by most recipients.
- Product names and prices are not legible at render size — the copy blocks between images are too small to read comfortably, reducing their persuasive value.
- The "Soften Shine" section feels visually thinner than the others — the product image appears isolated without a compelling hook.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a single anchoring offer to the hero** — even "Free shipping on SPF orders this week" would give the email a reason to act now rather than later.
- 2. **Make the CTA buttons product-specific** — replace "Shop Now" with "Shop Super Serum Tint," "Shop SPF," etc. Specific CTAs consistently outperform generic ones.
- 3. **Move the GWP ("stay sharp") above the fold or into the hero** — it's the most transactional hook in the email; don't bury it after four product blocks.
- 4. **Tighten the email length** — pick two hero categories (SPF + GWP) and cut Skincare + Shine to secondary mentions or a single row. Less scroll = more clicks.
- 5. **Subject line is vague** — "Skincare Mode: Summer" communicates category but not benefit or urgency.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your summer SPF routine, sorted`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Glow all summer — our 4 go-tos`

**CSS `@import` for fonts will fail in most email clients:**
