# Memorial Day Glow-Up: Up to 50% Off ✨
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- This is Sally's Memorial Day mega-sale send — the broadest, deepest promotional email the brand has run this month. The hero is clean: large model shot, bold "Up to 50% off" headline, single "Shop Now" button. That part works. Below it, the email packs six or seven distinct deal modules — $5 nail lacquer, 2-for-$15 hair care, 40% off conditioning masks, a $49.99 brushing kit, Shimmer Lights, and 50% off OAK Luxe cordless tools — stacked back to back with almost no breathing room. By module three, you've forgotten what you came to buy.
- The dual-offer structure is the email's biggest own-goal: a 20% off sitewide flash sale banner runs across the top, while the hero hero promises "up to 50% off." Two separate value propositions, zero guidance on which applies where, and no code visible in the hero itself. The shopper has to do mental math before they even scroll.
- Compare this to the May 15 SAVE20 send (the 9/10 this month): one mechanic, one code, one deadline, and it was clean. This email has more firepower but dilutes it. It's a solid Memorial Day blast — just not as tight as Sally's best work this month.

## What's working

- Hero module lands well above the fold: large model, bold discount headline, single CTA — the first scroll-stop is strong.
- Memorial Day framing is timely; "Glow-Up" is brand-voice-consistent and more interesting than "Sale."
- Deal variety is genuinely broad — $5 nail lacquer to $49.99 styling kits, something for every price point signals a real event.
- OAK Luxe cordless tools at 50% off is a high-ticket anchor that makes this feel like more than a routine promo.
- The 20% off sitewide flash banner at the very top is a strong scanner hook — even skim readers register a sitewide deal.

## What's weak

- Dual-offer structure (20% sitewide code vs. "up to 50% off" on select items) creates immediate confusion — which deal is better and where does each apply?
- Six-plus deal modules in the product grid share identical "Shop Now" CTA copy; zero differentiation means zero pull toward any specific category.
- No social proof anywhere — not a single "bestseller" badge, customer rating, or editorial callout in the entire scroll.
- Deadline is implied by Memorial Day but never stated explicitly; "ends Monday" would convert ambiguity into urgency.
- After May 15 SAVE20, May 14 Hot Tools, and the May 11 BOGO — promo cadence is heavy. No member/loyalty pricing callout to give subscribers a reason to open this one over the last three.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse the dual offer.** Either it's "20% off sitewide with MEMDAY20" or it's "up to 50% off select categories." Stacking both without a clear priority order fragments the value prop. Pick the stronger hero and make everything subordinate to it.
- 2. **Write specific CTA copy for each module.** "Shop Hair Masks," "Shop Styling Tools," "Shop $5 Nails" — this is the single highest-leverage CTR fix in the email. The same button copy used seven times trains the eye to skip it.
- 3. **Add a hard deadline.** "Ends Monday, May 26" somewhere in the hero — not just implied by the holiday name. A concrete date converts passive interest into clicks.
- 4. **Inject one social proof signal.** A "Customer Favorite" badge or a review star on the highest-margin deal tile would break the pure price-grid monotony.
- 5. **Add a members-only hook.** A "Sally Beauty Members, you get an extra 10%" tier would differentiate this send from the prior blasts for subscribers who already got SAVE20.
- **Subject Alt A:** `50% Off Ends Monday — Memorial Day Sale`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Glow-Up Weekend: Deals End Monday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Hair care, tools, and nails — all on sale through Memorial Day.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `20% off everything + up to 50% off select faves. Ends Monday.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`img { white-space: pre; }` (head `<style>`)** — Anomalous. `white-space` is not a valid property for `<img>` elements and has no effect, but its presence suggests a template copy-paste artifact. Low risk; should be removed.

**Missing `@font-face` for Founders Grotesk** — The global `a {}` rule declares `font-family: 'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif`, but no `@font-face` block for Founders Grotesk exists in the source. Every link in the email silently falls back to Helvetica. If brand rendering of Founders Grotesk is intended, the webfont must be declared or the fallback stack changed intentionally.

**Open Sans loaded at version 17** (`fonts.gstatic.com/.../opensans/v17/`) — Current Google Fonts serves v35+. The v17 endpoint is still served by Google's CDN so it won't break, but the `local()` hints reference legacy PostScript names (`OpenSans-Italic`, `OpenSans-SemiBold`, etc.) that don't match system font names on macOS 13+ or Windows 11, defeating the local-cache optimization.

**External font CDN blocked by major clients** — Gmail (web), Gmail iOS, Outlook 2016–2024, and Samsung Mail all block `@font-face` from external URLs. Open Sans will not render in these clients; Helvetica/Arial fallback applies. This is expected email behavior but confirm the fallback stack is visually acceptable.

**`box-shadow` on `.boxShadow` class** — Not supported in Outlook, Gmail, or Yahoo Mail. Will silently drop. No functional issue, but the visual design relies on a shadow that 60–70% of recipients will not see.

**Mobile breakpoint at `max-width: 499px`** — Non-standard. Most email frameworks use 600px. Devices with viewport widths between 500–599px (many Android phones in landscape) will render the desktop layout without mobile overrides.
