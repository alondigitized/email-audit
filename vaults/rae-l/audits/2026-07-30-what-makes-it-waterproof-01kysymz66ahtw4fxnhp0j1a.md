# What makes it waterproof?
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-30**
## Executive summary

- This is ILIA's "ingredient deep-dive" follow-up to the Thru Line launch (2026-07-28, 9/10), and it's a smart sequencing move — hook with the reveal, then educate on why it works. The eye/lip macro photography is gorgeous and on-brand, the "here to stay / smooth operators / a fine line" ingredient breakdown is genuinely differentiated content, not just another discount blast, and the swatch shot doing double duty as shade range + texture proof is a nice touch.
- Where it falls down is structure: this is a long, single-column scroll with two identical "SHOP THRU LINE" CTAs and a wall of macro photography before you get any concrete claims. The waterproof/sweatproof/24-hour/sensitive-eye bullets — the actual payoff the subject line promises — are buried near the bottom of the email, after four screens of ingredient photography. A reader skimming on mobile has to work to get the answer to "what makes it waterproof."
- Solid content, weak pacing. It should have led with the claims block, then let ingredients do supporting work.

## What's working

- Hero image and headline copy directly answer the subject line — "thru line waterproof eyeliner / What Makes It Waterproof?" is a clean, literal match.
- 4X VIP points banner up top gives loyalty members an immediate incentive layer.
- Ingredient sections (film formers, avocado oil pigments, omega fatty acids) are specific and educational, not generic beauty copy.
- Swatch photo shows actual shade range and pigment payoff, useful pre-purchase detail.
- Closing claims block (24-hour wear, waterproof + sweatproof, safe for sensitive eyes/waterline) is exactly the proof a skeptical buyer needs — just placed too late.

## What's weak

- The concrete product claims are buried below three ingredient photo blocks — that's a lot of scroll before the "why should I believe this is waterproof" payoff arrives.
- Two identical "SHOP THRU LINE →" CTAs with no offer or price attached — no discount, no bundle, nothing beyond VIP points, so click motivation rests entirely on curiosity.
- No visible price, no reviews/social proof, no "why waterproof matters" framed around a use-case (workout, humidity, tears) — the claims read as a spec sheet rather than a benefit.
- All-text final section (the four claim lines) has no visual treatment — after four rich photography blocks it reads as an afterthought instead of the hero payoff.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the four claims bullets (24-hour wear, waterproof + sweatproof, safe for sensitive eyes, safe for the waterline) up directly under the hero CTA, before the ingredient deep-dive — lead with the answer, then let ingredients back it up.
- 2. Add a price or shade-count callout near the top CTA ("10 shades, $28") — right now there's no purchase-relevant info until deep in the scroll.
- 3. Turn the final claims block into a styled icon/badge row instead of plain italicized text — it deserves visual weight given it's the email's whole thesis.
- 4. Add one social-proof element (star rating or a one-line review quote) near the swatch image to convert claims into trust.

- **Subject Alt B:** `The 3 ingredients behind Thru Line's hold`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Waterproof, sweatproof, and safe for your waterline — the ingredient story.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `10 shades. 3 hero ingredients. 24-hour wear.`
