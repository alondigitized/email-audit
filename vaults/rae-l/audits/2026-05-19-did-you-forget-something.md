# Did you forget something?
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- This is a textbook cart abandonment trigger — "Did you forget something?" subject, "READY TO CHECK OUT?" hero, "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" repeated twice, three specific named products in the body (Glide-Step Pro, BOBS Sport Solid, Glide Step Pro Rosy Glow). The mechanic is correctly deployed and the visual execution is clean. But the email leaves serious money on the table: there is **no financial incentive to recover the cart**. No countdown, no "here's 10% off to finish your order," no inventory scarcity signal. For a brand that spent May 4–11 hammering BOGO 50% off across six consecutive sends, the shopper's reference price is almost certainly the BOGO rate — and this recovery email arrives asking them to pay full price, silently.
- The $5 reward on $75+ is a standing members perk shown in every Skechers send; it is not a recovery incentive. The "Mommy & Me" product in the hero is an odd inclusion — it reads as a Mother's Day holdover and muddies who this email is for. Cart abandonment emails need one job: close the sale. This one asks without offering anything new, which is why the conversion ceiling is lower than it should be.
- Clean execution, blunt instrument. Good enough to catch the casual forgetter; won't move the considered abandoner.

## What's working

- "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" CTA is unambiguous — verb + direct object, repeated at the right scroll depth.
- Three named products shown with clean photography — the visual recall of what you left behind is the entire mechanism, and it works.
- "$5 REWARD ON $75+" loyalty nudge adds a thin financial frame even if it's not a recovery offer.
- No render bugs — layout holds, images load, text is legible.
- Cart abandonment as a triggered send breaks the promo-blast cadence from May 4–11; the inbox context change alone improves open odds.

## What's weak

- No cart recovery incentive — the email asks the shopper to complete a purchase with no new reason to do so, while their frame of reference is BOGO 50% off from earlier this month.
- No urgency or scarcity signal — no "items in your cart expire in 24 hours," no "only 3 left in your size," nothing.
- "Mommy & Me: Glide Step Pro — Rosy Glow" in the hero muddies the demographic — it implies a specific buyer type (mothers with young kids) that may not match the actual cart owner.
- Preview text is not visible in this render; the top of the email shows only a tracking code ("SMS:85385"), which means the preheader slot may be wasted or leaking junk.
- No social proof anywhere — for a considered purchase where someone already walked away once, a single star rating or review count would do real work.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a cart recovery incentive** — even "Free shipping on your order, today only" is enough. A time-limited BOGO callback ("The BOGO deal you were shopping is still on — finish your order by midnight") would directly exploit the promotional context from May 4–11.
- 2. **Add urgency with a specific deadline** — "Your cart expires in 24 hours" or "Only 2 left in your size" removes the "I'll do it later" exit.
- 3. **Remove the Mommy & Me product** unless it is confirmed to be in the actual abandoned cart — it introduces demographic noise into a personalized trigger.
- 4. **Surface one star rating** under each product — copy like "★★★★★ 4,200 reviews" beside the Glide-Step Pro is a one-line addition with high trust payoff.
- 5. **Fix the preheader** — if the tracking code at the top is leaking into the preview slot, that is the highest-priority fix before the next send.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your cart's getting cold — and so is the deal`
- **Subject Alt B:** `3 pairs waiting. They won't wait forever.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free shipping if you finish your order in the next 24 hours.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The BOGO deal is still live — your cart, your call.`

## Technical Audit — Skechers "Did you forget something?" (Cart Abandonment)

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard Attentive-served cart abandonment email with MSO/VML Outlook conditionals and responsive breakpoints. The truncated source reveals one confirmed protocol issue and several items worth flagging before sending.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**HTTP tracking redirect (high)**
The click-tracking wrapper uses `http://` not `https://`:
```
href="http://skechers.attentivemail.com/ls/click?upn=u001.LNc6…"
```
Modern browsers and spam filters penalize plain-HTTP redirect hops even when the final destination is HTTPS. Attentive supports HTTPS tracking — this should be the CNAME/SSL-enabled endpoint (`https://skechers.attentivemail.com/…`).

**Web version link also HTTP** — same domain, same issue. Both the "web version" anchor and all CTA links in the visible source share the `http://` prefix.

**No visible UTM parameters in source** — Attentive may append UTMs post-redirect, but they are not embedded in the raw `href`. See Section 6.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Screen readers and some webmail clients (Yahoo, AOL) surface the `<title>` as the document label. Should be set to the email subject or brand name.

**Preheader padding technique**
The preheader uses a mix of Unicode variation selectors (`U+034F ͏`) and soft hyphens (`U+00AD ­`) to push whitespace:
```
͏ ͏ ͏ … ­ ­ ­ …
```
This is common but inconsistent across clients. Gmail occasionally renders stray characters. A single `&zwnj;` sequence is cleaner and less likely to surface.

**`role="presentation"` on layout tables** — correctly applied; no issues.

**MSO conditionals** — present and correctly structured (`<!--[if mso]>`, `PixelsPerInch`, `AllowPNG`). No issues.

**Mobile breakpoint** — `@media (max-width:620px)` with explicit `row-20`/`row-21`/`row-23` column overrides. Font-size overrides (`19px`, `18px`) present. Cannot audit image alt text or button contrast from truncated source.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No merge tokens (first name, cart product name, price, thumbnail) are visible in the provided HTML. For a cart abandonment trigger, the absence of a product name or customer name in the subject (`"Did you forget something?"`) and visible preheader (`"You left something in your cart"`) reduces relevance signal. Cannot confirm whether tokens appear lower in the truncated body — **verify that at minimum `{{product_name}}` or equivalent renders correctly and has a fallback value if the cart session is stale.**

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM / Unsubscribe / Authentication)

**Cannot confirm from truncated source:**
- Physical mailing address (required by CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5)) — typically in footer; not visible in provided excerpt.
- Unsubscribe link and one-click List-Unsubscribe header — must verify footer and `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers.

**Sending domain:** `hello@msgs.skechers.com`
- `msgs.skechers.com` should be covered by Skechers' SPF record and a DKIM selector provisioned by Attentive.
- DMARC alignment requires the `d=` in the DKIM signature to match `skechers.com` (relaxed) or `msgs.skechers.com` (strict). Verify with:
  ```
  dig TXT msgs.skechers.com   # SPF
  nslookup -type=TXT _dmarc.skechers.com
  ```

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM / Landing Page)

**UTM parameters not embedded in source links.** If Attentive appends `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` post-redirect, verify they arrive at the Skechers landing page by curl-following the redirect chain:
```bash
curl -sIL "http://skechers.attentivemail.com/ls/click?upn=u001.LNc6…" | grep -i location
```
Expected final URL should include at minimum `utm_source=attentive&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=cart_abandonment` (or equivalent). If UTMs are missing, sessions land as `(direct)` in GA4/analytics, making cart recovery attribution invisible.

**Landing page alignment** — cannot audit without full link list, but cart abandonment emails must deep-link to the specific cart or product page, not the homepage. Confirm the resolved destination is `/cart` or a product PDP, not `skechers.com/`.
