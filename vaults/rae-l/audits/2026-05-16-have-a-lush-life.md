# Have a Lush life 🏖️
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- This is a summer editorial email from Lush — a vibes-first catalog anchored by a bright yellow hero ("ENDLESS SUMMER VIBES") and a multi-product grid covering Big, Passion, Jasmine Dream, Happy Hippy, The Kiss, and Super Milk. It closes with a playful "We're Bananas for Bananas" product section that's the most specific moment in the entire email. The visual execution is clean and on-brand, but structurally this is the same formula as the May 14 "What's that you smell?" send (6/10) — a color-coded product parade with no price hook anywhere in the scroll.
- The summer theming earns it a leg up on that May 14 send: the hero is bolder, the seasonal peg is timely, and "Bananas for Bananas" gives the email a distinct personality moment. But without a concrete offer — no dollar amount, no free sample threshold like the May 9 citrus email used to justify an 8 — this remains a browsing trigger, not a buying trigger. The audience gets visual inspiration; they don't get a reason to transact today.
- Verdict: a competent, pretty editorial email that will do fine for brand equity and won't drive meaningful conversion. Same problem Lush keeps hitting when they go pure catalog.

## What's working

- **Hero command**: The full-bleed yellow hero with "ENDLESS SUMMER VIBES" dominates above the fold — eye lands there immediately, zero ambiguity about the campaign theme.
- **Seasonal lock-in**: Beach emoji in subject, summer language in hero, May timing — all aligned. Doesn't feel stale or out-of-season.
- **"Bananas for Bananas" section**: Best moment in the email. Specific product theme, fun copy, distinctive visual — this is the kind of section that earns a scroll and a click.
- **Product grid variety**: Six named product lines give different readers an entry point without the email feeling chaotic.
- **Clean render**: No broken images, no overlapping text, no layout breaks visible anywhere in the scroll.

## What's weak

- **No offer anywhere**: No discount, no free-shipping threshold, no GWP — nothing to create transactional pull. Compare to the May 9 citrus email where "$80+ free sample" gave subscribers a concrete reason to act.
- **"Shop Now" CTA is generic**: A summer catalog email with six product lines needs product-specific CTAs ("Shop Big," "Explore Jasmine Dream"), not one catch-all button.
- **Subject is a pun, not a pitch**: "Have a Lush life 🏖️" is charming wordplay but communicates nothing about what's inside — no product, no offer, no urgency.
- **Cadence fatigue risk**: This is the fifth Lush email in roughly two weeks. Without a fresh hook (price, exclusive access, limited edition), another editorial drops into a crowded inbox with no differentiator.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a concrete offer to the hero** — a GWP threshold ("Spend $65, get a Banana sample free") or a single-day flash discount would transform this from a catalog into a campaign. The May 9 email proved this works.
- 2. **Replace the single "Shop Now" CTA with per-section CTAs** — each product family should link directly to its own page, not to the homepage. "Shop Jasmine Dream →" is more clickable than "Shop Now."
- 3. **Rewrite the subject to surface the Bananas section** — that's the most distinctive thing in the email; use it.
- 4. **Add a deadline** — even a soft one ("New summer drops, here now") creates forward motion. Nothing in this email implies any action needs to happen today vs. next week.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some clients (notably Outlook on Windows) surface the `<title>` in the window bar or tab. Should contain the email subject or a descriptive fallback.

**`text-size-adjust: none` on `<body>`**
```css
-webkit-text-size-adjust:none;text-size-adjust:none
```
Disabling text-size-adjust blocks iOS Mail's automatic font scaling and also prevents users with browser zoom preferences from scaling text. This is an accessibility regression (WCAG 1.4.4). The conventional safe value is `100%`, not `none`.

**Link underline suppression via `#MessageViewBody`**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none }
```
This removes underlines from all links in Gmail's web client. Undecorated links on non-contrasting backgrounds fail WCAG 1.4.1 (use of color). Acceptable only if link color contrast vs. surrounding text is ≥ 3:1 everywhere — cannot confirm without full body source.
