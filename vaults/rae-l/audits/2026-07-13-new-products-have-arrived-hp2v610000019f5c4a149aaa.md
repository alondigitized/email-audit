# 🎉 New products have arrived
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-13**
## Executive summary

- This is Sephora's fourth email since Stacking Weekend closed on July 7, and it marks a tentative pivot back toward commerce — a "Buy 2 Minis, Get Both 50% Off" deal banner sits at the top, and a free Rare Beauty gift appears mid-body. But the hero is still editorial: a soft "Take your PICK" new-arrivals layout, not a headline offer. The result is an email that tries to have it both ways — keep the summer editorial tone while sneaking commerce back in — and only partially succeeds. It's visually clean and technically sound. It is not memorable.
- After three consecutive editorial sends since July 8 ("Summer's Hottest," "These picks are everywhere," "Obsessed? Same."), this needed a stronger commercial re-entry. Instead, "New products have arrived" is the softest possible subject line after an already-soft streak. No product name, no deal in the subject, no urgency. The 50% minis offer — which should be the star — is confined to a small top banner while the hero real estate does brand-building.
- Judgment: competent execution, wrong creative strategy for this moment in the send cadence. Sephora needed to either go full editorial (a single great product story) or go full commerce (make the deal the hero). This email does neither cleanly.

## What's working

- The "Buy 2 Minis, Get Both 50% Off" top banner provides a real, concrete deal for shoppers who scan headers
- "Take your PICK" hero is visually clean — large type, warm palette, unambiguous SHOP NOW button
- New product grid gives browsers multiple entry points with visible brand names and prices
- Free Rare Beauty gift mid-email adds a second offer layer that increases click surface area without clutter
- Beauty INSIDER loyalty section appears at bottom, reinforcing member identity

## What's weak

- "New products have arrived" is the most generic subject line a beauty retailer can send — no brand, no category, no hook for any persona
- The 50% minis deal (the strongest commercial signal in the email) is buried in a small banner; the hero communicates curation, not savings
- This is the fourth email since July 8 without a strong headline discount — cadence fatigue is a real unsubscribe risk after the Stacking Weekend flood preceding it
- No single product hero: the grid diffuses urgency across too many simultaneous choices
- No deadline or scarcity on any of the three separate offers (minis deal, free gift, new arrivals)

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the deal in the subject.** "New products" arrive at Sephora daily — it's table stakes. The subject needs to be the 50% minis or the free gift.
- **Subject Alt A:** `50% off minis — pick your new summer faves`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New drops + free Rare Beauty gift inside`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Buy 2 minis, get both 50% off. Free gift too — today only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The Benefit Benetint Pocket Pal just landed. Plus: your free gift.`
- 2. **Pick one hero and own it.** The 50% minis, the free Rare Beauty gift, and the Benefit launch all compete for first-read attention. Elevate one as the clear lead; demote the others to supporting callouts.
- 3. **Add a deadline.** Even "This weekend only" or "While supplies last" on the free gift converts browsing intent into urgency-motivated clicks. None of the offers in this email have one.
- 4. **Give the Benefit Benetint Pocket Pal a real feature moment.** A specific new-product story ("The cult classic just got pocket-sized") beats a generic arrivals grid every time for driving a single focused click.
