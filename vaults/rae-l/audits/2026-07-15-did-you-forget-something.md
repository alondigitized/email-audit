# Did you forget something?
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- This is the third Skechers "Did you forget something?" email in less than two months — the same subject line hit on May 19, then again on July 1 (fourteen days ago), and now July 15. The July 1 audit already called it a dead creative with zero refresh; this send is that email's clone. A subscriber paying attention isn't reminded of their cart — they're reminded that Skechers runs the same drip on autopilot.
- Inside the body, the execution is solid. The hero is clean, three featured shoes display without render issues, the 20% off Skechers Plus offer is legible with a code (SUMMER), and the dual "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" CTAs bookend the product zone cleanly. The technical bones are fine. The strategic problem is that the cart-abandonment products shown — including a "Mommy & Me" co-branded slip-in — feel like a generic best-sellers dump rather than a reflection of what the recipient actually browsed.
- The email passes the functional test but fails the freshness test entirely. At this cadence, open rates on this subject line have almost certainly collapsed with engaged subscribers, which is the only audience worth optimizing for.

## What's working

- **Clean, scannable layout** — no render bugs, no broken images, no overlapping text; three hero products read clearly at a glance.
- **Dual CTA placement** — "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" appears above the product block and again after it, giving two conversion touchpoints without feeling pushy.
- **20% off with a visible code** — CODE: SUMMER is legible, associated with Skechers Plus, and gives a member a concrete reason to click even if they weren't planning to.
- **SHOP ALL BEST SELLERS dark bar** — provides a clear escape valve for anyone who doesn't recognize the hero products as their own browsed items.
- **Seasonal signal** — CODE: SUMMER + "Summer Favorite Sale" fine print at the bottom anchors this in the current moment, even if barely.

## What's weak

- **Exact same subject line as July 1** — fourteen days is too short a reuse window for an identical subject line; subscribers who got both are now pattern-matching this as filler.
- **Hero products don't read as personalized** — showing a "Mommy & Me" style alongside slip-ins for a solo adult cart-abandonment email signals batch-send, not behavioral trigger.
- **The 20% off offer is buried below the fold** — the discount only appears after the product grid and a "SHOP ALL BEST SELLERS" bar; a quick-scroll reader misses it entirely.
- **No deadline or scarcity** — the offer has no urgency signal in the body, and the hero carries no expiry copy; paired with the vague subject, there's no pressure to act now.
- **Preview text unavailable** — no preheader text is visible in the render, which means the inbox preview defaults to whatever first crawlable text the client pulls — often footer junk.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rotate the subject line** — this one is burned. Any specific product, category, or offer beats a third spin of "Did you forget something?" in 57 days.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your Slip-Ins are still waiting — 20% off today`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Take 20% off before SUMMER expires`
- 2. **Surface the offer in the hero** — move the 20% off / CODE: SUMMER block into the banner. A subscriber shouldn't need to scroll past three shoes and a best-sellers rail to find the discount.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Skechers Plus members: 20% off regular-priced styles with code SUMMER`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `20% off your cart — use code SUMMER before it's gone`
- 3. **Show the abandoned product(s) explicitly** — if behavioral data is available, replace the generic three-product hero with the one or two SKUs the recipient actually viewed. "Mommy & Me" has no place in a solo adult's abandonment trigger.
- 4. **Add a deadline** — even a soft one ("Offer ends Sunday") converts the 20% off from a standing promotion into a reason to act on this email specifically.
- 5. **Suppress this send for anyone who received the July 1 version without clicking** — they already demonstrated they won't convert on this creative; hitting them again burns sender reputation and confirmed-unengaged frequency simultaneously.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag** — `<title></title>` is blank. Some email clients (notably Gmail web) surface the `<title>` value in tab labels and accessibility trees. A descriptive title (e.g., `"Your Skechers cart is waiting"`) would improve screen reader context.

**Preheader padding technique** — The preheader div uses two distinct invisible-character types for length padding:
- U+034F COMBINING GRAPHEME JOINER (`͏`) — 150 repetitions
- U+00AD SOFT HYPHEN (`­`) — ~200 repetitions

Soft hyphens (`­`) can render as visible hyphens in some obscure clients and webmail renderers. The safer, universally compatible approach is to use only `&zwnj;` (`&#8204;`) or `&nbsp;` sequences. Not a critical failure but worth standardizing.

**`#MessageViewBody a` rule** (`color:inherit; text-decoration:none`) — This CSS targets Outlook Web App's message container and suppresses all link underlines globally within that scope. If any links lack explicit color styling in their inline attributes, they will inherit the surrounding text color and become visually invisible as links in OWA. Verify that all CTA links have explicit `color` set inline.

**`a[x-apple-data-detectors]` rule** — Present and correct. Prevents iOS Mail from auto-linking phone numbers and addresses found in body copy.

**MSO conditional comments** — Present and well-formed (`<!--[if mso]>`, `<!--[if !mso]><!-->`, `<!--<![endif]-->`). VML namespace and `PixelsPerInch` / `AllowPNG` Office document settings are included — correct for Outlook rendering fidelity.

**Responsive breakpoint** — `@media (max-width:620px)` with `.stack .column { width:100%; display:block }`. Mobile stacking logic present. Row-level padding overrides for rows 16, 17, 19 and font-size overrides confirm mobile-specific layout tuning is in place.
