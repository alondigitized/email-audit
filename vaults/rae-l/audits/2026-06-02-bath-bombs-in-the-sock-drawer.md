# Bath bombs in the sock drawer?
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-02**
## Executive summary

- Lush is back in editorial mode — this is a "tips you didn't ask for but actually needed" concept send, structured around the playful premise of stashing bath products in unexpected everyday places (the subject's "sock drawer" and the body's "work drawer" callback). The hero framing — "No Gatekeeping Here" — positions it as insider knowledge sharing, which is a smart angle that earns goodwill without requiring a discount.
- The structure is solid: hero editorial → two "fresh idea" product tiles → centered feature product (bath bombs/work drawer) → two more tiles → new product launch (Super Fairy Perfume) → loyalty footer. That's a logical escalation from habit-forming tips to new product excitement. The Super Fairy Perfume section is the clearest "action moment" in the email, but it's buried after four editorial tiles, which dilutes its impact.
- The real weakness here is the curiosity gap. "Bath bombs in the sock drawer?" creates a compelling hook that opens well, then the email disperses into a multi-product editorial instead of delivering a tight payoff. Compare this to the May 20th Dream Cream send — that one picked a lane and stayed in it. This email is trying to do five things at once.

## What's working

- **Subject line curiosity hook is genuinely strong** — 31 chars, quirky premise, earns the open without feeling gimmicky.
- **"No Gatekeeping Here" hero copy** reframes product promotion as a favor to the reader; that's a reliable tone win for Lush.
- **New Super Fairy Perfume section has clear visual differentiation** — the pink background breaks the grid and signals something new, functioning as a de facto second hero.
- **Cadence reads fresh** — after Karma, Dream Cream, floral guide, and shower themes, a "use-it-everywhere" concept email is a genuine angle change.
- **No render bugs** — clean layout, images load, no overlapping text visible.

## What's weak

- **No offer anywhere** — no discount, no free-shipping threshold, no BOGO. For a multi-product email this long, there's no transactional hook to convert browsers.
- **"Try These" CTA is too vague** — a verb + a pronoun is not a verb + a noun. What are "these"? The hero button doesn't tell you.
- **The work drawer section is the named concept but reads as one tile among five** — it should be the hero, full-bleed, not a mid-scroll module.
- **Super Fairy Perfume launch is buried** — a new product deserves to lead, not close. If this is a launch moment, it needed its own send (see: May 20th Dream Cream treatment).
- **No urgency or deadline visible anywhere** — the email has no reason to act today vs. next week.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Split this into two sends.** The "sock drawer tips" editorial and the Super Fairy Perfume launch are two different emails competing for the same render. The Dream Cream send on May 20th proved single-product focus outperforms multi-product grids for Lush's list.
- 2. **Make the "sock drawer" concept the full-bleed hero.** Right now it's sandwiched between a generic banner hero and subsequent tiles. Lead with the quirky premise and deliver on it immediately.
- 3. **Replace "Try These" with a specific CTA** — "Shop Bath Bombs" or "Grab Yours" is more specific and clickable.
- 4. **Add a light urgency layer** — even "New this week" or "Limited scent" on the Super Fairy Perfume is enough. Right now there's zero reason to click today.
- 5. **Reheader Alt options:**
- **Subject Alt A:** `Bath bombs don't belong in the bathroom`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Keep this in your desk drawer`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New Super Fairy Perfume + the products worth stashing everywhere`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Tips Lush fans actually use — plus something brand new`
