# Coming 06.02—our *softest* launch yet
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-29**
## Executive summary

- After 18+ sends hammering Friends + Family 20% off into the ground, ILIA drops a full stop — no offer, no urgency, just a powdery textured hero and a date: 06.02.26. The creative pivot is sharp and earned. "Shift your focus" works precisely because subscribers have been conditioned to expect discount emails; landing a clean teaser on the same calendar day as the final-call campaign email is a smart palette cleanser.
- The weakness is that "softest launch yet" stays a complete secret. No product category, no ingredient hint, no visual of the actual product — just atmosphere and a waitlist button. A loyal subscriber curious about what's next will click. A subscriber fatigued by 18 promo emails and reading this back-to-back with "final call for 20% off" may not even register the shift in tone.
- Clear verdict: strong awareness and brand-health send, not a conversion send. It earns its score on creative discipline, not on offer mechanics.

## What's working

- Creative pivot from discount noise to a clean teaser is perfectly timed — same day as the Friends+Family final-call, it resets the brand register in one send
- "Shift your focus" works double duty: invites subscribers out of sale mode and sparks curiosity about what's coming
- 06.02.26 appears in both hero and body copy, anchoring anticipation to a specific date rather than vague "coming soon" language
- "JOIN THE WAITLIST >" is a single, unambiguous CTA — no competing links, no confusion about the ask
- Render is clean: hero image, headline, body, button, and footer all sit correctly with no broken assets or overlap

## What's weak

- Zero product visibility — not even a category. "Softest" is a texture adjective with no referent; it could be a lip balm or a foundation or an SPF
- Sending a coming-soon teaser the same day as a final-call promo email creates tone whiplash for anyone who reads both (and many will)
- Body copy ("We're putting the finishing touches on our newest launch—arriving next week") adds no new information beyond what the subject and hero already say — a missed opportunity to build intrigue
- No social proof, no ingredient or skin-type hook, no "why this is different" signal — the email leans entirely on brand trust, which caps its ceiling for lapsed or newer subscribers

## Recommendations

- 1. **Name the category, not the product**: Don't reveal the product — but "our softest *skin* launch yet" or "our softest *foundation* yet" narrows the mystery from "anything" to something the reader can picture. That single category word could meaningfully lift click rate.
- 2. **Add a micro-teaser image**: A detail shot — a texture swatch, a shade blur, a single tube cap — gives the eye something to anchor desire on without blowing the reveal.
- 3. **Body copy should earn its place**: "One year in the making. Formulated for bare-skin days." or "Built around a single ingredient — you'll see why on 06.02." These add insider-access energy that justifies the tease.
- 4. **Rewrite the preheader**: "sweeping in on 06.02.26" repeats the date the subject already contains and adds nothing new. Replace it with a curiosity loop.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Coming 06.02 — our softest skin launch yet`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Meet your new skin-first foundation. 06.02.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `One year in the making. Join the waitlist first.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Formulated for bare-skin days. First look inside.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<title>` tag is empty:**
```html
<title> </title>
```
Some screen readers (NVDA + Thunderbird) announce the document title. A space is better than nothing but still fails accessibility expectations.

**Duplicate `img` CSS rule:**
The `<style>` block declares `img { border: 0; line-height: 100%; ... }` twice — once in the standard reset block and once in the Klaviyo-specific block. The second declaration adds `max-width: 100%` which is intentional, but the duplication is unnecessary dead weight.

**Google Fonts loaded via `@import` — unreliable in email clients:**
```css
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
```
`@import` inside `<style>` is blocked by Gmail (web and iOS), Apple Mail (some versions), and all Outlook versions. Montserrat will silently fall back to Helvetica/Arial in the majority of opens. The `display=swap` parameter is a web browser hint with no effect in email rendering. Fallbacks (`"Montserrat", "Helvetica", sans-serif`) are present and will apply — this is a rendering fidelity issue, not a breakage.

**All anchor states locked to identical style:**
```css
a:not([name]) {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
a:link    {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
a:visited {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
a:active  {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
a:hover   {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
```
Visited links are visually indistinguishable from unvisited. Hover state provides no affordance. This is a common email pattern to suppress blue underlines, but it removes all interactive feedback for sighted users.

**No `alt` text visible on images** in the truncated source. Cannot confirm — must audit full render.
