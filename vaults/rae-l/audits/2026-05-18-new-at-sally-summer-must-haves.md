# New at Sally: Summer Must-Haves ☀️
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- This is a new-arrivals editorial from Sally Beauty — a "look what just landed" send spanning summer hair tools, lip care, and styling products. The layout is clean, the seasonal framing is on-point, and the model imagery is warm and well-chosen. But compared to the "$20 Off Is Calling" flash sale from May 15, this email hands back almost all of its commercial leverage. There is no discount, no deadline, and no single hero product to anchor a purchase decision — just a wide catalog sweep across three loosely related categories.
- The "summer routine, covered" positioning works conceptually, but lip plumpers, heat tools, and dry shampoo in the same scroll feels like a quarterly newsfeed rather than a curated editorial. The three separate section headers ("waves," "lips," "style + protect") add structure but dilute focus — nothing rises to the level of a hero.
- This is a content email doing the job of a campaign send. It belongs in a proper editorial cadence, not as a standalone drop three days after a high-performing promo.

## What's working

- Summer framing is timely; the hero model shot leads with lifestyle warmth, not a cold product flat-lay.
- Three-section structure (waves → lips → style/protect) gives the catalog browse a navigable flow.
- Product grid modules are clean — individual product names and brief copy are legible.
- No render bugs, broken images, or layout failures visible.
- Brand recognition is instant — the Sally wordmark and familiar color palette anchor the send.

## What's weak

- Zero price signal anywhere — no %, no $, no BOGO — makes this a pure awareness email with no conversion hook.
- Three product categories competing for attention means no single product is memorable after you close the email.
- The primary CTA reads "Summer-ready waves." — it's a lifestyle phrase, not a button; there's no action verb.
- No urgency signal anywhere: no expiry date, no "just landed," no stock scarcity.
- Arriving three days after the May 15 $20-off promo, this delivers no step-up in value — it's a downshift.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Layer in a thin offer.** Even "free shipping on all new arrivals this week" converts this from a browse-and-forget to a browse-and-buy. A discovery email with zero price signal almost never closes.
- 2. **Collapse to one hero product.** Pick the most giftable or trending new arrival — the heat brush, the lip plumper, whatever — and let the product grid be supporting cast. Right now the hero model and four product rows compete at equal weight.
- 3. **Rewrite the CTA.** "Shop new arrivals" or "Shop summer must-haves" is a verb + noun and tells you exactly what happens when you click. "Summer-ready waves." does not.
- 4. **Add one urgency line.** "Just landed — shop before they sell out" or "New this week" in the hero sub-copy is enough to create a reason to act today.

No `@font-face` block for `Founders Grotesk` exists in the `<head>`. All anchor text silently falls back to Helvetica. If brand consistency relies on this font, a web-safe fallback or hosted `@font-face` declaration is required.

**Open Sans pinned to stale version (`v17`)**
All six `@font-face` declarations load from `fonts.gstatic.com/s/opensans/v17/`. Current Open Sans on Google Fonts is v34+. Google maintains historical versioned paths, but these URLs are a fragile long-term dependency — any CDN path deprecation breaks font rendering silently.

**`img { white-space: pre; }` — incorrect property**
```css
img { -ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic; white-space: pre; }
```
`white-space` is not a valid property for `<img>` elements. It has no effect on images and should be removed to avoid parser noise.

**Mobile breakpoint at `max-width: 499px`**
The responsive breakpoint targets devices ≤499px wide. The industry standard is ≤600px. Devices in the 500–600px range (common on Android) will receive the desktop layout regardless of actual viewport width.
