# What makes us smell so good?
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-08**
## Executive summary

- This is Lush's ingredient transparency editorial — a "what's inside" story built around four signature raw materials (Lavender Oil, Patchouli Oil, Balsam Fir Resin, Rose Absolute), each mapped to a hero product (Sleepy, Karma, Skinny Dip, Rose Jam). The format is a close cousin of the May 27 floral category guide and the June 4 banana send — it's a well-worn Lush playbook, and the execution is clean. Color-coded sections, clear product pairings, zero render issues.
- What keeps this from being a standout send is that it's pure brand education with no commercial path. Every CTA says "Read More" — four identical buttons across four sections, none pointing to a product page. The June 6 fragrance layering email had the same problem. Subscribers who'd buy Sleepy or Karma right now have no way to do that from this email.
- The Father's Day module at the bottom is the biggest misstep. The ingredient narrative builds genuine momentum through four sections, then the email pivots cold to blue waves and dad gifting. Those are two separate briefs that should have been two separate sends.

## What's working

- Bold color-coded sections (purple, near-black, forest green, coral) give each ingredient its own identity and make the email scannable on mobile.
- Product-to-ingredient mapping is immediately legible — a Lush fan knows Sleepy is lavender before reading a word of copy.
- The hero "WHAT'S INSIDE COUNTS" headline delivers directly on the subject's promise, which is rarer than it should be.
- No render issues — images load clean, text legible throughout, layout intact.
- The Father's Day tie-in at least nods to the calendar; the May 29 grooming guide set that expectation well, and this keeps the seasonal thread alive.

## What's weak

- No commercial offer anywhere — four "Read More" CTAs lead to editorial, not buy paths. This is the third consecutive send (June 2, June 6, now June 8) with no transactional hook.
- Father's Day module appended to an ingredient editorial feels like a stapled-on brief — it breaks the conceptual flow and dilutes both messages.
- "Read More" × 4 is generic and indistinguishable — zero opportunity for specificity, personalization, or urgency.
- Subject line ("What makes us smell so good?") is a question with no payoff hint. The June 4 "A-peeling and nourishing" pun and May 27 "Stop and smell the flowers" both signaled more about what was inside.
- This is the 4th email in 7 days. Even well-targeted subscribers notice cadence fatigue at that clip.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Replace every "Read More" with a product-specific CTA** — "Shop Sleepy," "Explore Karma," "Try Skinny Dip," "Get Rose Jam." This is the single highest-lift change: same layout, four times the click specificity.
- 2. **Decouple the Father's Day block** — make it a standalone send the following day, or lead with it as a full editorial with its own hero. Never append it to a conceptually complete email.
- 3. **Add at least one transactional hook per ingredient section** — a small "Add to Bag" or price anchor. The ingredient story earns the consideration; let subscribers act on it in the same session.
- 4. **Sharpen the subject** — the current one is a question that could belong to any fragrance brand. Make it Lush-specific:
- **Subject Alt A:** `Lavender, patchouli, fir, rose — the ingredients behind your favourites`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The 4 raw ingredients behind Sleepy, Karma and Rose Jam`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Meet the materials that make our most-loved scents work.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Four ingredients. Four icons. Here's what's actually inside.`
