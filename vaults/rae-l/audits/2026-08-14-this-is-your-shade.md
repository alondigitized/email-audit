# This is your shade
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-14**
## Executive summary

- This is ILIA's smartest send in weeks — it takes the eye color personalization idea and actually executes it as a shoppable color-matching guide, not a gimmick banner. The hero locks onto "For your Green eyes," then the "Color theory" module below breaks out shadow stick + eyeliner + mascara picks for all four eye colors (Blue, Brown, Green, Hazel), so even if this render is templated by segment, the structure reads as genuine 1:1 personalization rather than a generic seasonal dump like 08-05's "Unlock your summer glow" (9/10, but for different reasons).
- Where it loses points against the top performers in this arc (08-06 "Art of the Eye Trio" at 9/10, 08-10 "Do you line, smudge or wing it" at 8/10) is focus: this email tries to do color-matching AND the eyeliner/brush/sharpener essentials set AND the mascara bestseller, all in one long scroll, so by the time you hit "just the essentials" the personalization hook has faded and it's back to generic multi-CTA catalog mode. The top banner GWP offer ("free mini eye stylus w/ $75+") is also disconnected from the rest of the email — it's never referenced again.
- Net: strong concept, good execution on the hero, but it dilutes into three separate asks by the bottom. Call it a 7/10 — good, not great.

## What's working

- The hero directly names the recipient's eye color ("For your Green eyes") over a close-up eye photo — immediate, legible personalization.
- The "Color theory" grid is genuinely useful product content: swatches, shade names, and paired mascara for all four eye colors, not just a sales pitch.
- Clean, consistent ILIA visual language throughout (serif italics, cream/blush palette, consistent product photography).
- Trust bar at the footer (1% for the Planet, Leaping Bunny, PACT Recycle, VIP, Auto-Replenish) is visible and reinforces brand credibility.
- Clear CTA buttons throughout ("Shop Thru Line," "Save 15%," "Shop the Bestseller") — no ambiguous "Discover" copy.

## What's weak

- Top banner promo ("Choose a free mini eye stylus w/ $75+") is never tied back to any of the products shown below — feels bolted on.
- Three competing CTAs (Shop Thru Line / Save 15% essentials / Shop the Bestseller mascara) blur the primary action — a reader can't tell what they're "supposed" to click.
- The color-matching payoff (the reason for the personalized subject) ends at "Shop Thru Line" — the back half of the email (essentials set, mascara) has nothing to do with eye color and undercuts the hook.
- No visible price or specific discount amount for "Save 15%" — the number is there but no sense of what the eyeliner set actually costs.
- Very long single-column scroll with four repeated color blocks (Blue/Brown/Green/Hazel) — most readers only need their own one, so the rest is dead scroll weight for a recipient already matched to Green.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email down to one narrative: show only the recipient's own eye-color block big and prominent (not buried among all four), then a single follow-on CTA. If ILIA wants to reference the other eye colors, do it as a small "shop by eye color" nav row, not four full repeated modules.
- 2. Tie the top banner GWP offer to the actual shade match — e.g. "Free mini stylus in your Green shade" — so it isn't a generic disconnected banner.
- 3. Pick one primary CTA. If the essentials set is the real push this week, lead with it after the color match; drop the third "Shop the Bestseller" mascara CTA or fold it into the essentials set.
- 4. Add the actual price/discount amount next to "Save 15%" so the offer feels concrete rather than abstract.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your Green eyes, decoded`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The shadow + liner made for Green eyes`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plums, bronzes, pinks, and browns — matched to you`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free mini eye stylus with $75+, plus your shade match`
