# Double the gifts
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-24**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, well-built Black Friday gift-with-purchase send. The hero reads "the free gift edit" over a six-tile product collage, and the logic is simple: shop the 20% sitewide sale and get free gifts. After a week of plain-text recaps (Nov 23 "Ready to claim it all?", Nov 24 "This week in beauty: 20% off"), a visual, gift-led send is a welcome change of pace.
- The two gift tiers are clear: a free complexion stick with any order, and a free mini of choice at $100+. Each tier has its own header, product image and button. The weak spots are repetition and vague copy. All three buttons say "GET YOUR GIFT →", and the hero line "make that two" is a pun that needs a second read. The "*" is also never explained in the body, only in a tiny footer link.
- Overall it's a solid 6-7 send. It's skimmable and on-brand, but it adds no new offer to what the subscriber has heard all week.

## What's working

- The "free complexion stick with any order" tier is a low-friction, concrete hook, and the italic serif headline makes it easy to find.
- The tiered structure (any order, then $100+) gives a reason to add to the cart.
- The product images are clean, and the handwritten callouts ("firming + smoothing", "weightless coverage") add a useful product detail.
- The hero is on-brand: maroon and blush with the serif mix, and the top bar carries the "25% off sitewide, join VIP" message.
- The footer trust row (free shipping over $50, Leaping Bunny, shade match) reassures without cluttering.

## What's weak

- The offer is muddled. The top bar says 25% off for VIP and the hero says 20% off, with no clear explanation of the difference.
- All three CTAs read "GET YOUR GIFT →". They don't name the product, so they're generic and repetitive.
- The subject "Double the gifts" doesn't match the hero's "free gift edit". "Double" is never explained, and "make that two" is only a small line of body text.
- The asterisked terms are buried in a tiny footer link, which makes the "free" claims feel less honest.
- It's the fourth or fifth sale send in about four days, so the cadence feels heavy.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the hero explicit: "20% off + a free gift with every order", with the exact thresholds and end date shown in one line under the headline.
- 2. Give each CTA a specific label, such as "CLAIM YOUR FREE COMPLEXION STICK →" and "UNLOCK YOUR FREE MINI →".
- 3. Explain the "double" in the body, and put a one-line summary of the terms near the buttons.
- 4. Reconcile the 25% VIP and 20% public offers in a single sentence, with VIP as an upsell.
- 5. Add shade or product names to the complexion tier, plus a review snippet.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free complexion stick with any order`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your free gift: pick a mini at $100+`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `20% off sitewide, plus a free Multi-Stick with any order. Ends Cyber Monday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Spend $100 and choose a free mini: mascara, gloss or shadow stick.`

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Incomplete link verification:** QA probed 3 of 12 material links and skipped 9. The result reads "0 issues", but it only covers the 3 probed links. The remaining links are unverified, not confirmed good.
- **Open-tracking pixel without alt:** `https://trk.send.iliabeauty.com/o/01M873V6FASGED855TA8M72QZ7` is flagged for missing alt text. This is the open-tracking pixel. It should carry `alt=""` so screen readers skip it. It is low severity.
- **Tracking domain:** The branded `trk.send.iliabeauty.com` domain is used, which is good practice. The truncated source doesn't let me inspect the other `href` values or their redirect chains.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text:** `https://d3k81ch9hvuctc.cloudfront.net/company/SguDsz/images/c9cefca7-6b18-443a-896b-9e98ec5d842a.jpeg` has no `alt` attribute. If this is content or a CTA image, it is invisible with images blocked and to screen readers.
- **Empty title:** `<title> </title>` contains only whitespace.
- **No language attribute:** The root element is `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" xmlns:o=... xmlns:v=...>` with no `lang`. Screen readers can't pick the right pronunciation rules.
- **Web font via `@import`:** The `<style>` block uses `@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat...)` and `@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css)`. Gmail and several other clients strip or ignore these. The stack falls back to `"Montserrat","Helvetica",sans-serif`, so degradation is acceptable.
- **Headings:** `h1`, `h2`, `h3` and `h4` default to Arial, and the `p, h1, h2, h3` rule overrides this to Montserrat. This is not a defect.
- **Responsive and Outlook:** The viewport meta is present, there are `max-width: 480px` media queries, and MSO conditional comments and `OfficeDocumentSettings` are in place. No issues found.

No issues found. The visible source has no unrendered tokens such as `{{ }}`, `{%`, or `*|…|*`. Because the HTML is truncated, I can't check the footer or the rest of the body.
