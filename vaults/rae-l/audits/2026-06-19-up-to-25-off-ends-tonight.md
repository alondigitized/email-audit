# Up to 25% off ends tonight!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- This is Glossier's second send on June 19 — the hard-close "ends tonight" push following the earlier "ends tomorrow" email from the same day. By this point the Friends of Glossier Sale arc has produced eleven emails in eight days, which is the inescapable context for everything. The creative team made the right call: they recycled "All good things must come to an end" from the earlier send (which the brand history flagged as the strongest headline of the arc), added real product depth with the fragrance cards and the Black Cherries seasonal hook, and closed with a subscribe-and-save section that captures fence-sitters on a recurring discount. That's a structurally smart final-day email.
- The core tension is that a two-tier offer in the hero ("20% off sitewide OR 25% off $100+") requires a micro-decision from someone you're trying to move fast. The asterisk does real work, but the split dilutes the punch of a final-hours message. "Ends tonight" does the heavy lifting in the subject line; inside the email the offer logic softens the urgency the subject promised.
- Verdict: well-assembled execution for day eleven of an eleven-day sale. The fragrance product cards with named SKUs and prices are genuinely unusually specific for Glossier. But the generic "Shop now" CTAs used five times across the email, the buried subscription section, and the two-tier hero are all leaving lift on the table at the moment of peak intent.

## What's working

- "All good things must come to an end. Treat yourself before it's too late." is emotionally resonant final-day copy — warmer than the percentage-announcement pattern that opened most of this arc.
- Fragrance product cards show specific names (Glossier You 100ml, Glossier Deux 100ml), category (Eau de parfum), and a price point ($87) — rare SKU-level specificity for Glossier.
- "Black Cherries are in season" extends the email into lifestyle storytelling; the visual contrast of the dark cherry photograph against the pastel fragrance section gives the scroll a beat.
- Subscribe-and-save closer is a smart final-conversion play: captures subscribers who've resisted the arc by offering a permanent 25% lock-in.
- Render is clean — no broken images, no overlapping text, consistent orange CTA buttons throughout.

## What's weak

- Two-tier hero offer forces a mental calculation at the worst possible moment: "Am I spending $100+?" slows the click.
- "Shop now" appears five times with no variation — every section could carry a more specific verb/noun that drives higher CTR (and tells Glossier what the subscriber clicked through to).
- Discounted price is not visibly surfaced on the fragrance cards; the $87 badge appears to be the original price with the discount applying at checkout. The saved-dollar amount would accelerate the click.
- Eleven emails in eight days means even engaged subscribers are likely muting or deprioritizing opens by this point — this send absorbs that fatigue.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse the two-tier hero to one number:** Lead with "Save 25% — spend $100+ — sale ends tonight." Hide the 20% tier in a footnote. Higher anchor wins.
- 2. **Section-specific CTAs in place of five "Shop now" buttons:** "Explore our fragrances," "Shop Balm Dotcom," "Lock in 25% forever" — each drives intent signal data and feels less templated.
- 3. **Promote the subscribe section above the fragrance product cards.** It's the most distinctive offer in the email; burying it below the fold wastes it on the already-decided.
- 4. **Surface the discounted dollar amount on the fragrance cards.** "Was $87 → $65" (or equivalent) is a far stronger click trigger than just showing the original price.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Sale closes tonight — 25% off at $100+`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Final hours: up to 25% off Glossier`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `All good things must end. Treat yourself before it's too late.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `20% off sitewide, 25% off $100+ — ends at midnight.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is built on Klaviyo (klclick.com tracking domain), using XHTML 1.0 Transitional with standard MSO conditionals. One invalid HTML construct is present in the open-tracking mechanism; the remainder of the infrastructure appears standard but the truncated source prevents full footer/compliance verification.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Invalid open-tracking element**
```html
<span rel="stylesheet" href="https://ctrk.klclick.com/l/01KVFW84NYK85QQKMJ4V8THYPV_0"></span>
```
`rel` and `href` are not valid attributes on `<span>`. This is Klaviyo's open-pixel injected as a link inside a span — syntactically malformed. Strict-parsing clients (some corporate gateways, certain Outlook versions) may strip or log it as suspicious. Should be an `<img>` pixel (`width="1" height="1"`) or the ESP's standard tracking tag.

**Unbranded click-tracking domain**
All links route through `ctrk.klclick.com` (Klaviyo's default shared domain) rather than a branded subdomain like `click.glossier.com`. Shared tracking domains score lower on reputation lookups and expose Klaviyo ESP fingerprint to filters. Klaviyo supports custom tracking domains — this should be configured.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`format-detection` meta incomplete**
```html
<meta content="telephone=no" name="format-detection">
```
Only suppresses phone auto-linking. iOS/macOS will still auto-link dates, addresses, and emails. Full suppression: `content="telephone=no, date=no, address=no, email=no"`.

**Hover-state CSS coverage**
The stylesheet contains 12+ `.bd:hover` / `.rollover:hover` rules. These render only in Apple Mail, Samsung Mail, and Outlook.com web — all other major clients (Gmail app, iOS Mail < 16, Android Gmail) silently ignore them. No functional issue, but these rules add ~1 KB with limited reach.
