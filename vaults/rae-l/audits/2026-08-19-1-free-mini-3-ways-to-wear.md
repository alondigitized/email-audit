# 1 free mini, 3 ways to wear
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-19**
## Executive summary

- This is ILIA back on its Thru Line eyeliner arc after briefly detouring into "One, two, free" and the manifesto/editorial sends — and it's a genuinely well-built execution. The hero locks onto a single clear offer ("free mini Eye Stylus w/ $75+"), the "3 Ways to Wear" section is real product education with tight macro eye photography, and the "in safe hands" clean-beauty banner closes the loop with brand values instead of another discount ask. It reads like the eyeliner-launch machine finally firing on all cylinders.
- Where it loses points is redundancy and thresholds: the free-mini GWP with a $75 minimum is a lower-value, higher-bar offer than the "One, two, free" $50/$65-trio send from 08-12, and this is now at least the fourth email in the Thru Line arc — cadence fatigue is real. The "mini reviews" social-proof block is a nice touch but oddly styled like a screenshot rather than editorial copy. Overall this is a strong mid-arc send: focused, well-photographed, on-brand — just not a top-tier email because the offer itself is modest and the arc is getting long in the tooth.

## What's working

- Hero banner states the exact offer up top: "FREE MINI EYE STYLUD W/ $75+" (sticky bar) reinforced immediately below the hero image with shade options (Beam or Adorn).
- "3 Ways to Wear" is genuine, specific product education — three named techniques (Cover With Color, Line + Define, Light It Up) each with its own macro close-up photo.
- Two clear, high-contrast CTA buttons ("SWIPE IT" and "CLAIM YOUR GIFT") both point at the same conversion goal, reducing ambiguity.
- Demographic representation is strong — multiple skin tones shown across the eye close-ups, consistent with a broad beauty audience.
- Clean-beauty trust signals (Leaping Bunny, 1% for the Planet, Pact Collective, personalized shade match, VIP, auto-replenish) are all visible in the footer icon row, reinforcing the "Clean at ILIA, cruelty-free" banner copy just above it.

## What's weak

- "Ending soon" urgency has no visible date/time or countdown — it's vague urgency dressed as time-bound urgency.
- The gift threshold ($75+) is a step down in generosity from the 08-12 "One, two, free" $50 GWP trio, which risks feeling like a downgrade to anyone who saw that send.
- "mini reviews" testimonial card looks like a low-effort screenshot mockup (product art pasted onto a flat color block) rather than a polished editorial module — it breaks the visual quality of the rest of the email.
- This is at minimum the fourth Thru Line-arc email since 07-22; there's no acknowledgment of prior sends (no "still haven't tried it?" framing), so it risks reading as more of the same to anyone who already opened 08-12 or 08-14.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a real countdown or explicit end date to "Steal a glance before it's over" — vague urgency doesn't convert as well as "Ends 8/21."
- 2. Restyle the "mini reviews" block to match the email's photography quality — a real customer photo or a cleaner pull-quote treatment instead of the flat screenshot-style card.
- 3. Consider testing a lower or tiered threshold ($50 like 08-12) since stepping the free-gift bar up to $75 while shrinking the reward (mini vs. trio) may suppress conversion versus that recent precedent.
- 4. Add one line of copy acknowledging repeat exposure for engaged subscribers, e.g. "Still haven't tried the Eye Stylus?" to differentiate this from prior Thru Line sends.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your free mini eye stylus is waiting`
- **Subject Alt B:** `3 looks, 1 stylus, ends soon`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free with $75+: shade Beam or Adorn, your call`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `See it applied 3 ways before the gift's gone`
