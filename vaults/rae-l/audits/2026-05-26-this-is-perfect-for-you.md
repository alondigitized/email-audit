# This is perfect for you!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-26**
## Executive summary

- This is the third cart abandonment email from Skechers in six days — following "Did you forget something?" on 5/19 and "Your Items Are Waiting!" on 5/20. The sequence has now stretched long enough that the cart recovery angle feels tired, and Skechers appears to know it: "STILL THINKING ABOUT IT?" is softer than the prior two direct pushes, pivoting toward a product-recommendation frame with six "you'll love" suggestions alongside the hero item.
- The bigger structural problem is the offer. The 20% off single pair banner undersells the full promotion — the fine print at the bottom clearly states "Buy One, Get One 50% Off or 20% Off 1 Item." This is the same BOGO 50% that anchored four of the last five Skechers emails, and it's buried here below a 6-tile product grid. Any recipient who saw the 5/11 or 5/8 sends will feel like the deal got worse, when it didn't.
- The subject line "This is perfect for you!" is doing the email no favors. It's a generic personalization claim with no concrete hook, no offer, no urgency. This is a 4th-tier subject for a brand that ran "BOGO 50% Off" copy in the subject just two weeks ago.

## What's working

- **Hero product rendering is clean.** The Skechers Garage shoe is sharp, centered, and well-lit — no broken images or alignment issues.
- **"STILL THINKING ABOUT IT?" + "YOU'VE GOT GREAT TASTE!"** is a more flattering cart abandonment hook than the blunt prior two, softening the re-engagement.
- **Six-tile recommendation grid** fills out the email body usefully — gives the recipient an off-ramp even if the carted item no longer interests them.
- **20% off banner is visually prominent** — high contrast, clear copy, legible CTA button.
- **Utility footer (Afterpay/Klarna, app, curbside)** is well-organized and adds real transactional support without clutter.

## What's weak

- **The banner buries the better offer.** "20% OFF A SINGLE PAIR" is the headline but the fine print confirms BOGO 50% is active — leading with the weaker option is a self-own.
- **Subject line is non-specific.** "This is perfect for you!" communicates nothing about what's inside, no offer signal, no product category.
- **Third cart abandonment email in 6 days** — cadence has crossed into friction territory; this send dilutes the urgency the earlier two built.
- **Recommendation grid mixes genders without clear logic** — men's and women's Slip-ins alternate without segmentation, undermining the "perfect for you" personalization claim.
- **No deadline or countdown** — 20% off floats without urgency anchoring, making it easy to defer again.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the 20% banner with the BOGO instead.** "BOGO 50% Off — or 20% Off a Single Pair" gives the recipient the better framing and matches what the fine print actually says. Every prior send led with BOGO; don't downgrade the perceived value now.
- 2. **Rewrite the subject to include the offer.** "This is perfect for you!" will get skipped by anyone in inbox triage. Put the incentive or the product hook front and center.
- 3. **Add a deadline.** A "Offer ends Sunday" line anywhere in the body (under the hero, under the banner) converts the passive browse-recovery into an actual timed decision.
- 4. **Segment the recommendation grid by gender** — if the carted item was men's, surface men's Slip-ins, not a mixed grid. The personalization claim rings hollow when the recs visibly include the wrong gender.
- 5. **Consider resting the cart sequence.** At day 6, a 3rd recovery email is usually negative ROI. A 4-5 day window with 2 emails is the norm; this third touch should at minimum escalate the offer rather than hold flat or appear to soften.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Skechers Garage pick + 20% off — last chance`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your carted item is waiting — plus our best offer of the season`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Buy one, get one 50% off. Or 20% off a single pair. Pick your deal.`
