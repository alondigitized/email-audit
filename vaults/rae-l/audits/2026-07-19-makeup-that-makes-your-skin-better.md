# Makeup that makes your skin better™
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-19**
## Executive summary

- This is a "Made for All Types" skin-compatibility editorial — ILIA presenting its formula as a universal fit across different skin tones and types (Sunkissed Glow, Blonde Brow, STRATUM, Flushed Face). The concept has real legs: it's an inclusivity pitch dressed as a product selector, and the photography is strong. But the execution scatters across too many mini-editorials without a hero product or a single driving offer.
- The subject line — "Makeup that makes your skin better™" — is the brand tagline. Running it as an inbox subject is a cardinal sin: subscribers have seen this line on every ad, on the website header, on packaging. It earns zero curiosity in the inbox. The July 15 "That flushed feeling" send (9/10) proved that a single, focused skin-tone editorial works. This email attempts to do ALL tones in one email and the individual sections compress into thumbnails, each fighting for attention without winning it.
- The mini tote reappears at the bottom — the third consecutive send it has been appended to. What was a fun re-launch teaser has curdled into a boilerplate module that signals the brand is running out of editorial fuel.

## What's working

- Hero photography is beautiful — the glowing close-up skin shot immediately communicates "skin first" without text.
- "Made for All Types" is an inclusive, differentiated brand angle that fits ILIA's positioning.
- The skin type segmentation (Sunkissed Glow, Blonde Brow, Flushed Face) is a smart navigation device — it invites self-identification.
- The "A Million" social proof module gives the email a credibility anchor.
- Clean layout — no visible render bugs or broken images.

## What's weak

- Subject is the brand tagline verbatim with a ™ symbol — offers no reason to open, no curiosity, no product hook.
- No concrete offer anywhere — no % off, no free shipping, no threshold promotion — leaves the email with no commercial urgency.
- Four mini-editorials in one email (Sunkissed, Blonde Brow, STRATUM, Flushed) dilutes focus; each section is too small to do the work of a hero.
- The mini tote module appears again at the bottom for at least the third send in a row — it reads as filler now, not excitement.
- STRATUM sits mid-email as a product/ingredient section but is visually indistinct from the skin-type sections — the hierarchy breaks down in the middle of the scroll.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the tagline subject, write a real one.** "Makeup that makes your skin better™" is brand copy, not inbox copy. Pick the ONE skin type with the most visual drama (Sunkissed Glow is the hero candidate) and write a subject for that.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Made for your skin tone — not against it`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Find your ILIA shade match`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sunkissed, blonde brow, flushed — which one are you?`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your skin type, your formula. Shop the match.`
- 2. **Pick one skin type per send, execute it like "That flushed feeling."** The July 15 blush editorial proved the format at a 9/10. A four-skin-type send compresses each section to a thumbnail; four single-skin-type sends would be four clean 8+ emails.
- 3. **Add a soft commercial layer.** Even "free shipping on your first shade match" or a bundle offer (two skin-match products) gives the reader a reason to click now rather than "maybe later." The July 11 Sun Serum send (8/10) shows product-launch emails with UGC drive clicks; editorial-only sends rarely do.
- 4. **Cut or defer the mini tote module.** It has now appeared in the July 17, 18, and 19 sends. Either give it its own dedicated email (which it deserves as a relaunch) or pull it entirely from general editorial sends.
- 5. **Clarify STRATUM's placement.** As-rendered, STRATUM sits in the middle of the skin-type narrative and doesn't read as a distinct section — rename the header or give it a visual separator so it doesn't feel like a layout mistake.

## Full review
## 1. Overview

This is a "Made for All Types" skin-compatibility editorial — ILIA presenting its formula as a universal fit across different skin tones and types (Sunkissed Glow, Blonde Brow, STRATUM, Flushed Face). The concept has real legs: it's an inclusivity pitch dressed as a product selector, and the photography is strong. But the execution scatters across too many mini-editorials without a hero product or a single driving offer.

The subject line — "Makeup that makes your skin better™" — is the brand tagline. Running it as an inbox subject is a cardinal sin: subscribers have seen this line on every ad, on the website header, on packaging. It earns zero curiosity in the inbox. The July 15 "That flushed feeling" send (9/10) proved that a single, focused skin-tone editorial works. This email attempts to do ALL tones in one email and the individual sections compress into thumbnails, each fighting for attention without winning it.

The mini tote reappears at the bottom — the third consecutive send it has been appended to. What was a fun re-launch teaser has curdled into a boilerplate module that signals the brand is running out of editorial fuel.

## 2. What worked

- Hero photography is beautiful — the glowing close-up skin shot immediately communicates "skin first" without text.
- "Made for All Types" is an inclusive, differentiated brand angle that fits ILIA's positioning.
- The skin type segmentation (Sunkissed Glow, Blonde Brow, Flushed Face) is a smart navigation device — it invites self-identification.
- The "A Million" social proof module gives the email a credibility anchor.
- Clean layout — no visible render bugs or broken images.

## 3. What didn't

- Subject is the brand tagline verbatim with a ™ symbol — offers no reason to open, no curiosity, no product hook.
- No concrete offer anywhere — no % off, no free shipping, no threshold promotion — leaves the email with no commercial urgency.
- Four mini-editorials in one email (Sunkissed, Blonde Brow, STRATUM, Flushed) dilutes focus; each section is too small to do the work of a hero.
- The mini tote module appears again at the bottom for at least the third send in a row — it reads as filler now, not excitement.
- STRATUM sits mid-email as a product/ingredient section but is visually indistinct from the skin-type sections — the hierarchy breaks down in the middle of the scroll.

## 4. What I'd change

1. **Kill the tagline subject, write a real one.** "Makeup that makes your skin better™" is brand copy, not inbox copy. Pick the ONE skin type with the most visual drama (Sunkissed Glow is the hero candidate) and write a subject for that.
   - **Subject Alt A:** `Made for your skin tone — not against it`
   - **Subject Alt B:** `Find your ILIA shade match`
   - **Preheader Alt A:** `Sunkissed, blonde brow, flushed — which one are you?`
   - **Preheader Alt B:** `Your skin type, your formula. Shop the match.`

2. **Pick one skin type per send, execute it like "That flushed feeling."** The July 15 blush editorial proved the format at a 9/10. A four-skin-type send compresses each section to a thumbnail; four single-skin-type sends would be four clean 8+ emails.

3. **Add a soft commercial layer.** Even "free shipping on your first shade match" or a bundle offer (two skin-match products) gives the reader a reason to click now rather than "maybe later." The July 11 Sun Serum send (8/10) shows product-launch emails with UGC drive clicks; editorial-only sends rarely do.

4. **Cut or defer the mini tote module.** It has now appeared in the July 17, 18, and 19 sends. Either give it its own dedicated email (which it deserves as a relaunch) or pull it entirely from general editorial sends.

5. **Clarify STRATUM's placement.** As-rendered, STRATUM sits in the middle of the skin-type narrative and doesn't read as a distinct section — rename the header or give it a visual separator so it doesn't feel like a layout mistake.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — `@import` font loading will fail in Gmail:**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```
Gmail strips `@import` rules at parse time. Montserrat and the custom Klaviyo font will not load for the majority of subscribers. The fallback stack `"Montserrat", "Helvetica", sans-serif` (applied to `p, h1, h2, h3`) will kick in correctly, but verify this is visually acceptable.

**Issue — Links are visually indistinguishable from body text:**
```css
a:not([name]) {color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none}
a:link    {color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none}
a:visited {color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none}
a:active  {color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none}
a:hover   {color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none}
```
All five link pseudo-states are identical — same color as the brand text, no underline, no hover change. If any inline links appear in body copy (not just styled buttons), they will be invisible as links. Sighted keyboard users also lose hover feedback entirely.

**Minor — empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title> </title>
```
Some webmail clients (Yahoo, AOL) render the title in the browser tab. Not critical, but a missed touchpoint.

**Cannot audit**: Image `alt` attributes — not visible in truncated source. All `<img>` tags must have non-empty `alt` text for accessibility and images-off rendering.
