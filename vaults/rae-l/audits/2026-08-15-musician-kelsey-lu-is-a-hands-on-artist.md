# Musician Kelsey Lu is a hands-on artist
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-15**
## Executive summary

- This is ILIA pivoting from campaign-arc marketing into pure editorial: a "musician profile" piece built around Kelsey Lu, framed entirely through the lens of the Thru Line eyeliner launch that's been running since 07-22. It's a content-marketing move rather than a promo — long-form quotes about hands, instruments, and "feeling" makeup, interspersed with product shots and two CTAs buried deep in a very long scroll. It reads more like a magazine feature than a marketing email, and that's both the appeal and the problem.
- Compared to 08-10's "Do you line, smudge, or wing it?" (8/10, tutorial-driven and product-first) or 08-06's "The Art of the Eye Trio" (9/10, concept-tight bundle), this send trades conversion clarity for brand storytelling. It's a legitimate lane for a beauty brand to work — artist partnerships build long-term equity — but as a promotional email it's slow, offer-free, and asks a lot of scroll before any CTA appears.
- Judgement: a well-produced brand piece, poorly optimized as an email. Fine as an occasional trust-building send in the rotation, but it shouldn't be back-to-back with other non-offer sends, and the first CTA needs to move up dramatically.

## What's working

- Strong, consistent portrait photography — moody, artist-forward, on-brand with ILIA's minimal aesthetic.
- "4X VIP POINTS — NEW THRU LINE EYELINER" eyebrow at the very top does give one concrete, scannable incentive before the fold.
- The editorial quotes tie makeup application directly to Kelsey Lu's tactile relationship with instruments — a genuinely coherent creative concept, not just a celebrity slap-on.
- Product callout ("thru line waterproof eyeliner in portrait + flourish, limitless lash mascara in after midnight") is specific and links shade names to the story.
- Clean closing footer block with loyalty program, returns, and shipping info all visible.

## What's weak

- No visible price, discount, or bundle offer anywhere in the body — the only "deal" is 4x points, and it's not repeated near either CTA.
- First CTA ("STEP INTO THE STUDIO") doesn't appear until well past several screens of scroll — mobile readers will bail before reaching it.
- Two CTAs ("Step Into the Studio" and "Shop Thru Line") point to different destinations with no clear signal which one is the "buy" action — ambiguous primary action.
- This is a heavily story-first layout for a promotional email list; there's no urgency, no deadline, nothing time-bound.
- Coming one day after 08-14's "This is your shade" and in a dense multi-day Thru Line cadence, this risks fatigue — a fifth-plus touch on the same launch with diminishing offer strength each time.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move a CTA button above the fold, directly under the hero image — don't make people scroll past three quote blocks to act.
- 2. Give the 4x VIP points line more visual weight (bigger, colored banner) since it's the only concrete incentive in the whole email — right now it's a tiny gray eyebrow easy to miss.
- 3. Collapse to one consistent CTA action/destination repeated 2-3 times ("Shop Thru Line") rather than splitting attention between "Step Into the Studio" and "Shop Thru Line."
- 4. Trim the body — three separate quote blocks plus four photos is a lot of scroll distance for a marketing email; cut to two beats max.
- 5. Given the brand history, slot this kind of story-first send less frequently — space it further from other non-discount sends to avoid fatigue.
