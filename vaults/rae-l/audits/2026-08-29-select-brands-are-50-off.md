# Select brands are 50% off 🤑
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-29**
## Executive summary

- This is a bait-and-switch send. The subject promises "50% off select beauty" and the hero delivers a big pink "50% off Select Beauty" banner with a lipstick image — but everything below that hero pivots hard into "Real Luxury," a full-price prestige catalog scroll (YSL, Caudalie, U Beauty, Jo Malone, Kerastase, Oribe) with zero discount language. The 50% off deal is confined to a tiny fine-print line ("Too Faced, Drunk Elephant, and MAC Cosmetics... Today Only") that's easy to miss against luxury-brand imagery that has nothing to do with the deal.
- Compared to yesterday's 2026-08-28 "50% off select beauty" send (6.00/10), which at least kept the deal front-and-center with a full daily-deals calendar, this one buries the actual offer under five straight sections of undiscounted luxury merchandising. It reads like two unrelated emails stapled together — a deals email and a prestige-brand catalog — and the deal email lost.
- Judgement: weak send. The subject/hero promise and the body content diverge so much that anyone who opens for the 50% off will feel misled by the time they scroll past the second section.

## What's working

- The pink hero banner is bold, legible, and states a real, specific discount (50% off) with named brands (Too Faced, Drunk Elephant, MAC).
- "TODAY ONLY" urgency badge is visually distinct and easy to spot in the hero.
- Section dividers ("Real Luxury," "Next-Level Skincare," "Feel-Good Fragrance," "Hype-Worthy Hair Care") are clean, well-typeset, and give the scroll a coherent editorial feel.
- Product photography is high quality and consistent in styling (warm backdrops, tight product groupings) throughout.
- "NEW" and "CLEAN + PLANET AWARE" and "ONLY AT SEPHORA" badges add helpful differentiation signals for shoppers scanning quickly.

## What's weak

- The subject line's 50% off offer applies to none of the products actually shown below the hero — total disconnect between promise and content.
- No product-level pricing or discount badges anywhere in the luxury catalog section, so the whole mid-body reads as a full-price ad, not a deal email.
- "Real Luxury" framing directly contradicts a "50% off" subject — luxury and discount messaging clash and dilute both.
- The Klarna/Afterpay/PayPal payment-plan block and "New from rhode" section feel bolted on, unrelated to either the deal or the luxury story, adding scroll fatigue.
- No visible countdown or explicit end time for "Today Only," so the urgency is a label, not a mechanic.

## Recommendations

- 1. Rebuild the body to match the subject: lead with the actual 50% off Too Faced/Drunk Elephant/MAC products immediately after the hero, with prices and struck-through comparisons, before any luxury catalog content.
- 2. If Sephora wants to run both a deal and a luxury catalog, split them into two separate sends — this hybrid dilutes the open-driving promise and makes the luxury section look like filler.
- 3. Add explicit sale pricing (e.g., "$24 (was $48)") to at least the hero products so the discount is provable, not just asserted in fine print.
- 4. Move the "Exclusions/terms apply" fine print out of the hero graphic and into readable text below it so shoppers know what's actually included before they scroll.
- 5. Cut or relocate the Klarna/rhode blocks to the footer area — they compete with the core deal message for attention.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Today only: 50% off Too Faced, Drunk Elephant & MAC`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your 50% off pick is waiting (ends tonight)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stack the savings before this one's gone at midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Too Faced, Drunk Elephant, MAC — half off, today only.`
