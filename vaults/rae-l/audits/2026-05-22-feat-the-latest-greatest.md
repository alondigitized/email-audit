# Feat. the latest + greatest
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- This is ILIA's eighth email in six days — and it's the one where the content strategy actually makes sense but the inbox strategy completely breaks down. "Feat. the latest + greatest" is doing something smart: using the Friends+Family sale halo to spotlight genuinely new launches (Sun Serum Mineral Sunscreen SPF 50, Overglaze Lip Gloss, Skin Blur Serum Concealer, Soft Focus Blurring Blush). New product context inside an active sale is a legitimate reason to send. The problem is that the subject line throws away the hook entirely — "Feat. the latest + greatest" could run any week of any year, and after seven prior ILIA emails in six days, it does zero work to earn the open.
- Once you're inside, the email is clean and well-executed. Product photography is sharp, descriptions are benefit-forward, the 2×2 grid is scannable, and the "Cart Deco" free-gift-with-every-order section is the freshest incentive ILIA has deployed this entire sale run. But no deadline is visible anywhere, no social proof, and the "Cart Deco" header is too clever to communicate "free gift" on a fast scroll.
- The email earns its place in the sale cadence; the subject line does not earn the open.

## What's working

- **"Cart Deco" free-mini-with-every-order** is a new incentive not present in any prior send this run — it gives a second reason to click beyond the 20% discount
- **Product descriptions are specific and benefit-forward** — "mineral SPF 50," "12 long-wearing shades," "24-hour hydration" give the reader real information fast
- **Dual-tier discount banner** (20% sitewide / 25% VIP) maintains sale continuity and reminds VIPs of their extra benefit without a separate section
- **Individual "SHOP NOW >" CTAs on each product card** reduce friction — no hunting for a way in
- **Product photography is consistent** with ILIA's clean, soft-editorial aesthetic

## What's weak

- **Subject "Feat. the latest + greatest" is completely generic** — after seven consecutive sale emails, it provides no specific reason to open; the new-launch angle is buried
- **No urgency or deadline visible anywhere in the email** — the sale has been running since at least May 17 with no end date in sight, and this email doesn't add one
- **Zero social proof** — the May 17 "award winners" send led with press validation from ELLE, allure, and Vogue; this send has none of that, and it would land well on new product launches
- **"Cart Deco" headline is too cute** — the actual offer ("free mini with every order—no minimum") lives in the subhead; the headline should be the offer, not a wordplay on "Décor"
- **Cadence has reached inbox-fatigue territory** — this is email eight in six days on the same sale, and the subject line does nothing to differentiate it

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject to lead with "new launches"** — the four products shown are genuinely new arrivals, and that's the reason to send; "20% off new drops" is more compelling than any wordplay
- 2. **Add a sale end date** — even a single line reading "Sale ends Sunday" in the body or as a banner update would add credibility and urgency that's entirely absent
- 3. **Move a social proof signal onto the hero product** — a star rating or a single press stamp ("allure Best of Beauty") would borrow equity from the May 17 send and anchor the new launch
- 4. **Rewrite "Cart Deco" headline to lead with the offer** — "Free mini with every order, no minimum" as the H2, then a flavor line beneath
- **Subject Alt A:** `New drops, 20% off: 4 just-launched picks`
- **Subject Alt B:** `4 new arrivals — your 20% off applies`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `SPF, Lip Gloss, Concealer, Blush — all new, all in the Friends + Family sale. Plus a free mini with every order.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New arrivals landed in the sale. Free mini with every order — no minimum required.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**`@import` for external fonts will be stripped by major clients.**
Both font imports are inside a `<style>` block:

```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```

Gmail, Outlook.com, and Samsung Mail strip `<style>` block contents or block external `@import` calls. Any Montserrat or Klaviyo custom font rendering depends on fallback stacks (`"Helvetica", sans-serif`), which appear to be set — but this should be verified visually in those clients.

**Body links not auditable** — HTML is truncated before product/CTA links appear. UTM coverage and redirect chain health cannot be confirmed from this source alone.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title> </title>
```
Screen readers and some accessibility tooling use `<title>` as the document label. Should be set to the email subject or a descriptive string.

**All link states suppress hover affordance:**
```css
a:hover { color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration: none }
```
Hover state is identical to default — no visual feedback. Not a compliance issue, but breaks expected interactive behavior in web-based clients (Gmail, Outlook.com).

**`<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" content="IE=edge">` is inside a conditional comment:**
```html
<!--[if !mso]><!--><meta content="IE=edge" ...><!--<![endif]-->
```
The comment syntax here is malformed — the closing `<!--<![endif]-->` should be `<![endif]-->`. This is a known MJML quirk; functionally harmless in modern clients but technically invalid.

**No issues found** with MSO/VML shims, viewport meta, or responsive breakpoints (`max-width: 480px` / `min-width: 480px`).
