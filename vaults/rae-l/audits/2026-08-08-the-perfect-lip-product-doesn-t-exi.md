# The perfect lip product doesn’t exi-
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-08**
## Executive summary

- This is Glossier pivoting again — from the "Leo season" editorial hook on 8/7 straight into a full Lip Line product deep-dive, no discount, no GWP, no urgency. It's the most information-dense send in this thread's recent history: hero application shot, packaging stack, an "our shades are nipple-matched" cheeky education beat, a macro lip-texture close-up, an eight-shade arm swatch chart, and a closing bundle upsell. It reads like a proper category launch/relaunch push rather than a quick promo blast.
- The subject line is doing the heavy lifting here — "The perfect lip product doesn't exi-" is a meme format cut mid-sentence, which is a genuinely fun open-driver, but it's vague about what's actually inside. Once opened, the email delivers on substance: real shade names, real skin-tone range in the swatch photo, and a formula callout (smudge/transfer/water-resistant) that gives an actual reason to click beyond "new colors."
- Judgement: this is one of the stronger execution efforts in the recent cadence — clean, well-photographed, informative — but it's an evergreen product-education email wearing a viral-meme subject line, and there's no offer or urgency anywhere to convert browsing into buying today.

## What's working

- The arm-swatch chart with eight named shades (Hold, Grip, Graze, Buff, Bit, Nip, Flush, Bare) on multiple skin tones is genuinely useful product information, not just decoration.
- The macro lip-texture shot under "A smudge, transfer and water-resistant essential" ties a real functional claim to a striking visual.
- Consistent "Shop now" CTA buttons appear at every section break, so there's never a dead zone without a next action.
- The closing "In A New York Minute Set" bundle gives a second conversion path (cross-sell) for anyone who scrolled past the primary Lip Line pitch.
- Clean render throughout — no overlapping text, no broken images, no lorem-ipsum.

## What's weak

- Zero offer anywhere in the email — no %, no bundle discount, no free shipping threshold — despite ending on a "Save with sets" section that never states what's actually saved.
- The subject line's meme hook ("...doesn't exi-") never pays off in the body; nothing inside references or completes the joke, so the curiosity gap opens the email but doesn't land.
- "Shop now" is repeated four times verbatim — it's clear, but it's not specific, and it does nothing to differentiate the liner from the lipstick from the set.
- The "nipple-matched shades" copy is a bold, risky line for a mainstream retail email — cute for some readers, off-putting or TMI for others, and it's buried under "THE HISTORY" with no visual payoff to back the claim.
- No social proof (review count, star rating, "bestseller" badge) anywhere despite this being framed as an established, beloved product ("Our beloved lip liner").

## Recommendations

- 1. Pair the meme subject with an actual payoff in the hero — either complete the joke in the H1 ("...it exists. Meet Lip Line.") or drop the meme format for something that states the product outright, since right now the curiosity hook and the body content don't connect.
- 2. Add one concrete mechanic — even a modest GWP or bundle discount on the "New York Minute Set" — since this brand's history shows GWP mechanics (Applebaby Keychain, Mini Beauty Bag) consistently outperform pure editorial sends.
- 3. Swap at least one "Shop now" for shade-specific or product-specific CTA copy (e.g., "Shop Lip Line," "Find your shade") to make the click action match the specific content just shown.
- 4. Move or soften the nipple-matching claim — either give it a visual proof point (e.g., a diversity-of-tones callout tied directly to the swatch chart above it) or cut it if it's not core to the pitch.
- 5. Add a light social-proof line under the hero or formula section ("Glossier's #1 lip liner" / review stars) since the copy already claims it's "beloved" but shows no evidence.
