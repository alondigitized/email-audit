# Over 800+ responses later …
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-25**
## Executive summary

- This is a community-driven product launch email for Lush's shower gummies — a new jelly-format bath product whose scents were voted on by subscribers. The creative leans hard into the "Get a Wiggle On" concept, and the visual execution is peak Lush: vivid yellow background, colorful 3D-rendered gummy shapes, punchy headline. The structural flow works — hero, product explainer with an inventor quote, embedded video reveal, and a loyalty nudge — it's a full funnel in a single scroll.
- The body is the strongest thing here; the subject is the weakest. "Over 800+ responses later …" is a teaser nod to community participation but it names no product, no category, and creates no urgency. Compare that to Lush's June 20 "Shower in self-care 🚿" send, which put the content zone in the subject immediately — this one buries the lede entirely. A casual subscriber scanning a crowded inbox has every reason to skip it.
- Once opened, the email earns its click. The product is novel enough to hold attention, the inventor quote adds a human anchor to an abstract product format, and the video section extends the story. The two competing CTAs ("Shop Drops" vs. "Watch Now") are the main conversion-layer friction.

## What's working

- The yellow hero with 3D colorful gummy shapes is immediately eye-catching and communicates "new, fun, different" without needing a word of copy.
- "Get a Wiggle On" headline lands — it's on-brand Lush wordplay and physically describes how the product works.
- Gary Shears inventor quote adds credibility and a human face to a novel, unfamiliar product format.
- The "Choices, Choices" video section extends dwell time and explains the product far better than copy alone could.
- Lush Club rewards reminder is well-placed at the bottom — soft loyalty hook without breaking the product story.

## What's weak

- Subject names no product, no category, and no benefit — "Over 800+ responses later …" is curiosity bait that a distracted inbox scanner will skip.
- "Limited-edition" is buried in a long hero subtitle; it should be a visible badge or callout, not prose.
- Two prominent CTAs ("Shop Drops" and "Watch Now") dilute click intent — there's no clear primary action.
- "Shop Drops" is insider slang; a subscriber unfamiliar with drop culture gets no signal about what they're shopping.
- No price point anywhere — for a brand-new product format a subscriber has never bought, a "from $X" anchor would reduce friction.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Name the product in the subject** — the community angle is charming, but "shower gummies" or even just a scent hint needs to appear somewhere in subject or preheader. Curiosity alone doesn't carry a new product launch.
- 2. **Make "limited-edition" a visual badge** — stamp or ribbon on the hero product image, not buried text. Scarcity should be scannable.
- 3. **Sharpen the primary CTA** — "Shop Shower Gummies" > "Shop Drops" for anyone outside Lush's core superfan base.
- 4. **Pick one CTA per section** — demote "Watch Now" to a text link so "Shop Drops" reads as the clear primary action above the fold.
- 5. **Add a price anchor** — even "from $12" in the hero subtitle reduces hesitation for a product nobody has held yet.
- **Subject Alt A:** `You voted. Meet your shower gummies 🍬`
- **Subject Alt B:** `800 Lush fans chose these 5 scents — now dropping`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New limited-edition shower gummies. Community-voted scents, now live.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Wiggle them on your shower wall. Five fan-picked scents dropping now.`

## Technical Audit — Lush USA "Over 800+ responses later…"

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard Beefree/BEE-generated table-based email with MSO conditional blocks and a 500px fixed container. No catastrophic rendering failures are visible; issues are concentrated in the empty `<title>`, double preheader implementation, and unverifiable link destinations due to opaque redirect encoding.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Click-tracking domain:** All links route through `clicks.hello.us.lush.com` using long encoded redirect URLs (e.g. `https://clicks.hello.us.lush.com/f/a/oEuORMJfR34k68fVdow6IQ~~/...`).

- **UTM parameters unverifiable:** The redirect URLs are opaque base64-encoded blobs. Final destination URLs and any UTM parameters cannot be confirmed from source alone. Decoding one sample redirect is required to verify campaign tracking is intact.
- **No bare/untracked links detected** in the visible portion — all hrefs pass through the click domain. Confirm this holds for the unsubscribe and social links in the footer (commonly missed).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- **Empty `<title>` tag:** `<title></title>` renders as a blank tab title. Some email clients (Outlook on Windows, Yahoo web) surface the `<title>` value in the UI or as the notification text. Should be set to the subject line or a descriptive string.
- **Preheader duplication:** Two consecutive `display:none` preheader divs are present — one with the visible preview text ("Something bouncy was born") and one containing Unicode spacer characters (`͏ ‌ ͏ ‌ …`). The spacer block is a deliberate padding technique to suppress body text from leaking into the preview, which is fine, but confirm the spacer div comes *after* the text div (correct order is visible here — text first, spacers second). Swap would suppress the intended preview.
- **Fixed 500px container:** `width:500px` on the row-content table is narrower than the 600px industry standard. This is a deliberate Lush choice but may clip slightly in some Outlook preview panes. No breakage, just a flag.
- **`lang="en"` present** on `<html>` — correct.
- **`role="presentation"` on all tables** — correct.
- **Alt text:** Cannot confirm from truncated source. Images must have `alt=""` (decorative) or descriptive alt text for accessibility and images-off rendering.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No merge tokens (e.g. `{{first_name}}`, `*|FNAME|*`) are visible in the truncated source. Cannot confirm whether the footer or body contains personalization — no unrendered tokens detected in the visible segment.

---

### 5. Compliance

- **Physical mailing address:** Not present in the visible HTML. CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address. Confirm it appears in the footer (outside the truncated range).
- **Unsubscribe mechanism:** Not visible in truncated source. CAN-SPAM and CASL require a functional unsubscribe link. Confirm the footer contains one and that it links through the click-tracker correctly (tracked unsubscribe clicks can interfere with list-unsubscribe processing if misconfigured).
- **List-Unsubscribe header:** Cannot verify from HTML alone — must be confirmed at the SMTP/header level. RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe (`List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`) is required for bulk senders under Gmail and Yahoo's 2024 sender requirements. Verify this header is present on the raw message.
- **Sending domain:** `hello@hello.us.lush.com` — subdomain of `lush.com`. Confirm SPF, DKIM (signed on `hello.us.lush.com`), and DMARC (`_dmarc.lush.com`) are all passing. A `p=reject` or `p=quarantine` DMARC policy on the parent domain with a misconfigured subdomain alignment is a common deliverability failure point.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

- **Cannot verify UTM coverage** without decoding a sample redirect URL from `clicks.hello.us.lush.com`. Decode at least the primary CTA link and confirm `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` are appended to the final destination URL.
- **Landing page alignment:** Subject and preheader reference a product launch ("Something bouncy was born", "Over 800+ responses later"). Confirm the CTA destination is the product page for the new item, not the Lush homepage or a generic category page.
