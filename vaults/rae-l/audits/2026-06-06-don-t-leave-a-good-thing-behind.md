# Don't leave a good thing behind.
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-06**
## Executive summary

- This is the sixth Skechers abandonment-flavored email in roughly eighteen days — following "Did you forget something?" (5/19), "Your Items Are Waiting!" (5/20), "This is perfect for you!" (5/26), "Your cart expires soon!" (5/26), and "This style has limited availability!" (5/27). "Don't leave a good thing behind" extends a sequence that has already exhausted its credibility. The honest diagnosis: this is a catalog browse email dressed up in abandonment subject-line clothing. There is no discount, no countdown, no inventory number, no personalized product name — nothing that would move a subscriber who already ignored five prior recovery attempts.
- The "SELLING FAST..." hero is the weakest urgency play in the whole series. It doesn't say *what* is selling fast, doesn't show a stock level, and doesn't include a deadline. The secondary "you might also love" grid is fine as padding but it's not doing any persuasion work. The email is visually clean and the CTA is readable, but clean execution of a weak idea is still a weak email.
- Judgement: this send should not have gone out without a concrete incentive attached. At send six with no offer, the only measurable outcome is unsubscribes.

## What's working

- Clean visual hierarchy — hero product image, "SELLING FAST" header, and the navy SHOP NOW button are immediately legible without zooming.
- The hero is tied to a named product ("Skechers' Garage") rather than a faceless catalog shot.
- Secondary product grid gives five alternatives for subscribers who aren't interested in the hero, adding browse surface without cluttering the above-fold.
- Afterpay and Klarna callout in the footer is a genuine utility signal for price-sensitive shoppers.
- No render bugs — layout is clean, images load, no overlapping text.

## What's weak

- Zero offer — no percentage off, no free shipping, no BOGO — at send six in a sequence, "SELLING FAST" alone won't close anyone.
- "SELLING FAST" with no unit count, no deadline, and no restock disclaimer is hollow urgency; it reads as boilerplate after this many sends.
- Subject line "Don't leave a good thing behind" names no product, no category, and no offer — it's the softest subject in the entire abandonment sequence.
- No personalization visible anywhere — no first name, no cart product name, no "you were looking at X."
- The cadence itself is the biggest problem: six near-identical abandonment touches in eighteen days trains subscribers to ignore, not to click.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a concrete offer.** At this stage in the sequence, a subscriber has already ignored five sends. 15% off or free shipping is the only thing that changes the calculus. No offer = no reason to send.
- 2. **Name the product in the hero copy.** "Your Skechers Garage sneaker is almost out" is five times more compelling than generic "SELLING FAST."
- 3. **Set a hard deadline.** "Available through Sunday, then it's gone" is credible. Indefinite scarcity is not.
- 4. **Cap this sequence at four sends maximum.** A subscriber who didn't convert on sends one through five is either gone or waiting for a deal — flooding them only accelerates unsubscribes.
- 5. **Write a real preheader.** The inbox preview is likely leaking structural junk or repeating the subject; a dedicated preheader line with the offer would lift open rate immediately.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your Skechers Garage is selling out — here's 15% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last chance on the shoe you kept looking at`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stock is thin. Grab yours before Sunday and save 15%.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `We're holding it — but not for long. Here's a nudge.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
`<title></title>` — empty. Screen readers and some email clients surface this as the document label. Should be set to something meaningful (e.g., `"SKECHERS — Don't leave a good thing behind"`).

**`#MessageViewBody a { text-decoration: none }` global rule**
This CSS block strips underlines from all links in Gmail's web client. Links that rely solely on color for differentiation from body text will fail WCAG 1.4.1 (use of color). If any body copy contains inline links (not CTA buttons), they will be visually indistinguishable.

**Preheader padding — soft hyphens (`­`, U+00AD)**
The preheader filler block mixes COMBINING GRAPHEME JOINER (`͏`, U+034F) with SOFT HYPHEN (`­`, U+00AD). Soft hyphens are rendered as visible dashes in some assistive technologies and older email clients. Recommend replacing all U+00AD characters with zero-width non-breaking spaces (`&#8203;`) or additional CGJ characters.

**Image alt text**
The HTML is truncated before the image blocks render. If `alt=""` is omitted on product/hero images (not explicitly set to empty string), clients with images blocked will show broken image icons instead of blank space. Verify all `<img>` tags carry explicit `alt` attributes (empty string acceptable for decorative images).

**MSO/VML and mobile breakpoints**
VML namespace declarations and `[if mso]` conditional comments are present and correctly structured. Mobile breakpoints fire at `max-width:620px` against a 600px content width — correct. No issues found here.
