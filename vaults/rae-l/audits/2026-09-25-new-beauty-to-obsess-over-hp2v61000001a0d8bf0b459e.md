# New beauty to obsess over 😍
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-25**
## Executive summary

- This is Sephora's "new arrivals" grid again, and it's a weaker execution of the same idea they ran on 9/20 ("Your fall beauty routine is coming together," 5.00/10). There's no single hero offer — instead it's a top banner (up to 50% off Korean beauty + free hair-fave sample) followed by a long, undifferentiated scroll of ten-plus new product launches (Dior, Innisfree, M·A·C, Oribe, Sol de Janeiro, Unite, Eadem) each with its own tiny "Shop Now." Nothing anchors the eye; every module looks equally important, which means none of them are.
- The top banner is actually the most compelling thing in this email — a real discount ("up to 50% off") plus a free gift with code — but it's visually the smallest, least-designed element, competing for attention with a wall of new-launch cards below it. A subscriber scanning on their phone will bounce off the banner text, not register an offer, and scroll past a dozen product logos without a reason to stop.
- Compared to the fragrance-points send (9/22, 7.00/10) or the Hair Daily Deals send (9/23, 7.00/10), which each had one clear, category-specific offer, this is Sephora's least focused creative of the week. Judgment: a scattershot new-arrivals dump masquerading as a curated "obsess over" moment — 4/10 territory.

## What's working

- Top banner does contain a real, specific offer: "Up to 50% off Korean Beauty" plus a free hair-fave sample with code, both time-boxed ("Today Only").
- Clean/cruelty-free badges are visible on several products (Obart, Innisfree, Lys Beauty), which signals values-based shopping for a segment that cares.
- Section headers ("Hot & New Beauty," "New and Iconic EADEM," "Try It, Love It") do break the page into visually distinct color-blocked zones, so it doesn't feel like one undifferentiated wall.
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, text isn't overlapping, layout holds together end to end.

## What's weak

- No single hero product or offer — visual hierarchy is flat, every product card gets equal weight, so the eye has nowhere to land.
- The strongest offer (50% off + free gift) is buried at the very top in small text, easy to skim past before the real message registers.
- Subject "New beauty to obsess over 😍" promises excitement/curation but the body delivers an undifferentiated product catalog — a mismatch between promise and payoff.
- Too many near-identical "NEW · ONLY AT SEPHORA" callouts (Dior, Innisfree, M·A·C, Oribe, Lys, Sol de Janeiro, Unite) dilute each other — nothing reads as the one thing to check out.
- Fatigue risk: this is the second "new arrivals" grid dressed as a story in six days (9/20 was the first), and neither leads with pricing/promo the way Sephora's flash-sale and points emails do.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one hero launch (Dior or Oribe, the two most premium/recognizable) and give it the full top-of-email real estate with a large lifestyle image and single CTA — cut the rest to a "more new arrivals" carousel below.
- 2. Promote the 50% off Korean Beauty banner to a full-width, high-contrast module immediately under the header — right now it reads like fine print, not the lead offer.
- 3. Cut the product count in half. Ten-plus "new and only at Sephora" cards in one send trains subscribers to skim past all of them.
- 4. Make the free hair-fave gift its own line item with the code visible, not folded into the top banner's small text.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `50% off Korean beauty, today only`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `Dior's new blur stick just dropped`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus: a free hair fave with any order, today only`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `10+ new launches, one 50%-off flash deal`
