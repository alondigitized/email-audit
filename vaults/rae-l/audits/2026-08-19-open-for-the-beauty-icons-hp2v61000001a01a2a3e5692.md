# Open for the beauty icons
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-19**
## Executive summary

- "Open for the beauty icons" leads with genuine star power — a striking editorial hero shot of a model in a black dress cradling the new CHANEL Coco Mademoiselle Crush Absolu, framed by "BEAUTY'S MOST WANTED / Prepare to be starstruck." That's a real hook, more magazine-cover than promo-blast, and a nice change of pace after two weeks of Sephora catalog scrolls.
- But it collapses the same way every recent Sephora send has: after the fragrance hero, it's back to an unstructured eight-item grid (shampoo, eye cream, hand sanitizer, blush) with no organizing logic, followed by three more disconnected promo tiles (Tower 28 app-exclusive, Beauty Insider event, Touchland "Latest Lineup"). The trial-size offer at the very top is the only concrete discount-adjacent thing in the whole email, and it's buried above a hero that doesn't even mention it.
- Compared to 2026-08-17's "App Fest" send, which at least had a calendar structure to justify its length, this one has a strong opening beat and then no throughline. Judgment: a genuinely good hero, undercut by the same catalog-dump pattern that's been dragging every Sephora email this cycle down to mid-range scores.

## What's working

- The CHANEL hero image is high-production, on-brand, and visually distinct from Sephora's usual product-tile aesthetic.
- "BEAUTY'S MOST WANTED / Prepare to be starstruck" is a confident, curiosity-driving headline that doesn't oversell.
- The trial-size offer (NARS lip balm or FENTY BEAUTY lip gloss with $30 purchase) is a legible, specific incentive placed right at the top.
- "ONLY AT SEPHORA" badges on Kérastase and Juliette Has a Gun items add credible exclusivity signal.
- Render is clean — no overlapping text, broken images, or layout breaks anywhere in the scroll.

## What's weak

- The fragrance hero has zero visible CTA tied to it beyond a small "Buy online, pick up in store..." line — no obvious button under the product name/price.
- After the hero, the email reverts to the exact same eight-item, no-category grid pattern seen in six of the last six Sephora sends (shampoo → eye cream → toner → hand sanitizer → blush, no logic connecting them).
- Subject "Open for the beauty icons" is vague and doesn't reflect either the trial-size offer or the CHANEL launch — a reader has no idea what's inside.
- Three unrelated bottom promos (Tower 28 app exclusive, Beauty Insider event finder, Touchland lineup) are crammed in with no transition, diluting the fragrance-launch focus established up top.
- No visible price/urgency framing on the trial-size offer beyond fine print ("Exclusions/terms apply") — feels like standard boilerplate rather than a real deadline.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the CHANEL fragrance the entire email's spine — cut the eight-item unrelated grid, or move it to a genuinely separate "New Arrivals" module. The hero image is strong enough to close on its own with a clear "Shop Coco Mademoiselle Crush Absolu" button directly under the price.
- 2. Rewrite the subject to reference the actual hero product, not a vague tagline — specificity drove engagement in the 08/14 rhode send ("rhode's NEW Spotwear has arrived," 7/10).
- 3. Add a real CTA button directly beneath the fragrance name/price block — right now the eye has to hunt for how to buy it.
- 4. Give the trial-size offer its own clear value: "free with $30" is good, but pair it visually with the fragrance section since it likely applies to the same purchase.
- 5. Consolidate the three bottom promo tiles into one "This Week at Sephora" block instead of three competing CTAs.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Meet CHANEL's new obsession: Coco Mademoiselle Crush Absolu`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your free trial size + the fragrance everyone's talking about`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus: a free NARS or FENTY mini with any $30 purchase`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New from CHANEL, Kérastase, and more — shop before it sells out`
