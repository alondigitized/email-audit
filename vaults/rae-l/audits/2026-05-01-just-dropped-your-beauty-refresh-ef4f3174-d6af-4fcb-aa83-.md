# Just Dropped: Your Beauty Refresh ✨
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-01**
## Executive summary

- This is a deals-roundup email from Sally Beauty with a thin "new arrivals" framing layered over what is plainly a promotions dump. Four distinct offer structures — a loyalty discount, a Mother's Day gift guide, a bundle deal, two BOGO/buy-more promotions, and a Sauce Beauty cross-sell — compete for attention in a single send. The hero visual is strong (vibrant model, on-brand), but the editorial logic breaks down quickly below it. The loyalty tier callout (Level 3) is the most meaningful personalization in the email, and it's buried in a gray bar at the top. Nothing here feels genuinely "just dropped."
- Functional, but the lack of hierarchy means the email is doing too many jobs at once — and likely winning none of them cleanly.

## What's working

- **Hero image** is high-quality and immediately communicates the hair color category; the model's styled hair does the selling.
- **Manic Panic bundle with explicit pricing ($21.99)** is the sharpest single offer in the email — concrete discount, specific SKU combination, no mental math required.
- **Loyalty tier indicator** (Level 3) adds a personalization signal that most competitors don't bother with; it sets a tone of "this is for you specifically."
- **BOGO Ineiro section** shows actual product swatches (purple, deep blue), which is smart for a hair color audience that shops by shade.
- **Breadth of delivery options** (in-store, free shipping, BOPIS) is neatly communicated through icons near the footer.

## What's weak

- **Four competing offer structures with no hierarchy.** BOGO 50%, buy-two-get-one-free, flat bundle price, and a percent-off loyalty discount all appear in the same email. There is no lead offer — every module screams equally.
- **Mother's Day and "Just Dropped" are two different campaigns in one body.** "Gifts for Mom under $25" and "New Deals Just Dropped" are incompatible narratives; neither gets the space it needs.
- **Generic CTAs.** Every module uses "Shop now" or "Shop all deals." No CTA differentiates by offer type, category, or urgency.
- **Product thumbnails in the BOGO section are undersized.** The Ineiro color bottles are the most visually interesting product in the email and they're rendered small — a wasted conversion opportunity for a brand where shade selection is the key decision.
- **"Your Beauty Refresh" never lands.** The subject promises a refresh, but the email is a deals sheet. The concept is abandoned immediately.
- **Sauce Beauty module lacks context.** Buy one, get one 50% off Sauce Beauty — but what is Sauce Beauty? No brand context or product description is visible; it reads as an afterthought.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Choose one hero offer per email.** If it's BOGO hair color, commit the entire email to it. If it's the Manic Panic bundle, make that the editorial spine.
- 2. **Separate the Mother's Day campaign.** It deserves its own send with gift-angle creative. Stapling it here dilutes both the deals message and the gifting message.
- 3. **Make the BOGO Ineiro section visually bigger.** The shade swatches (purple, deep blue) are exactly what hair color shoppers respond to — double the image size, add shade names prominently.
- 4. **Write offer-specific CTAs.** "Shop BOGO Colors," "Build Your Bundle," "Find a Gift Under $25" — each module should tell you what you're clicking into.
- 5. **Activate the Level 3 signal.** Tie the loyalty tier directly to the offer: "Unlocked for Level 3 members" on at least one module converts a generic promo into a members-only moment.
- 6. **Give Sauce Beauty a one-line descriptor.** Unknown brands need 10 words of context to earn a click.

6. **Give Sauce Beauty a one-line descriptor.** Unknown brands need 10 words of context to earn a click.

### 6. Bottom Line

Sally Beauty has genuine deal value in this email, but a failure of editorial discipline means the reader has to do too much work to extract it. The Manic Panic bundle and BOGO Ineiro section are the two strongest assets — either one could anchor a focused, higher-converting send. As assembled, the email reads like a weekly flyer, not a curated drop.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>`:**
```html
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" xmlns:v="..." xmlns:o="...">
```
No `lang="en"` attribute. Screen readers cannot infer document language; WCAG 2.1 SC 3.1.1 failure.

**Issue 2 — `white-space:pre` on `img` elements:**
```css
img { -ms-interpolation-mode:bicubic; white-space:pre; }
```
`white-space:pre` on `<img>` is non-standard and has no valid effect on replaced elements. In some WebKit-based clients it can introduce unexpected whitespace spacing between stacked images. Should be removed.

**Issue 3 — Undeclared font `'Founders Grotesk'`:**
```css
a { font-family:'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }
```
No `@font-face` declaration for `Founders Grotesk` appears in the visible CSS block (only Open Sans is declared). If it's not loaded via an external `<link>` tag in a portion of the `<head>` not included in the truncated source, all link text will silently fall back to Helvetica. Confirm the font is either declared or loaded elsewhere.

**Issue 4 — Stale Open Sans version (`v17`):**
```
url(https://fonts.gstatic.com/s/opensans/v17/...)
```
Current Google Fonts Open Sans is `v36+`. The v17 paths are still served by Google but represent a ~4-year-old subset. External web fonts are also blocked outright in Gmail (renders system fallback) and Outlook desktop. Low risk, but the version mismatch is a hygiene flag.
