# “I match 40-80 people a day.”
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-08**
## Executive summary

- This is ILIA's longest, most editorial send in the recent history — a "meet the matchmakers" profile of four real shade-matching specialists (Gabi, Janna, Kortnii, Briana), each with a quote, match count, and their favorite combo. It's a genuine departure from the GWP-deadline arc (08-19–08-31) and the product-launch beat (09-01, 09-03) — closer in spirit to the 08-29 plain-text recap but far more polished, with real photography and a clear editorial hook.
- The concept is smart for a foundation/concealer brand where shade-matching anxiety is the #1 purchase blocker — putting a face and a number ("40-80 people a day") behind the recommendation borrows social proof from in-store BA culture. But structurally it's four nearly-identical bio blocks stacked vertically with a single "MATCH ME" CTA up top, and the reader has to scroll past ~4 full profiles before hitting the actual shopping mechanism (The Match Game / quiz / selfie tool). For a mobile inbox, that's a lot of scroll before value.
- Verdict: strong concept, weak execution on hierarchy — the offer and the CTA are buried under editorial weight that a promotional email can't really afford.

## What's working

- The subject line quote ("I match 40-80 people a day") is a genuinely different hook than anything in the recent history — curiosity-driven, human, on-brand for a complexion-match campaign.
- Real staff photography (not stock models) builds trust for a shade-match trust problem.
- "The Match Game" section clearly explains two paths (quiz vs. selfie) with distinct CTAs — good UX once you reach it.
- Each profile includes a concrete favorite product combo, which doubles as soft product merchandising without feeling like a hard sell.
- Consistent visual template across all four bios keeps it scannable despite the length.

## What's weak

- The single "MATCH ME" button sits right under the intro and before any of the four profiles — by the time a reader finishes scrolling through Gabi/Janna/Kortnii/Briana, that CTA is long gone from view.
- No visible discount, offer, or urgency anywhere until "Customized Coverage" near the bottom, and even that badge ("Save 25%!") is small and easy to miss against the layout.
- Four consecutive, visually identical bio blocks create scroll fatigue — nothing differentiates them enough to make a reader stop at profile #2, #3, #4 individually.
- The hero doesn't state a clear action — "Meet the Matchmakers" is a soft, branding-style headline, not a directive.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move a CTA (or repeat the "MATCH ME" button) after every 1-2 profiles, not just once at the top — don't make the reader scroll back up.
- 2. Pull "The Match Game" (quiz/selfie tool) up near the top, right after the hero, and trim the bios to 2 instead of 4 — let the tool be the hero action, with staff quotes as supporting proof underneath.
- 3. Surface the 25% off "Customized Coverage" offer higher and bigger — right now it's the only concrete discount in the whole email and it's buried near the footer.
- 4. Give the hero a directive subhead under "Meet the Matchmakers," e.g. "Take our 60-second quiz or snap a selfie — free shade match, guaranteed."
