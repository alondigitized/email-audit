# BFF + SPF
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-16**
## Executive summary

- ILIA has swapped the celebrity-collab playbook for a real-person friendship story — two women, Aissa and Emily, with noticeably different skin tones who use the same product (the Super Serum Skin Tint) and trade quotes about why it works for them. The creative hook is genuinely warm and the "same but different" headline earns its pun. SPF in mid-June is textbook seasonal relevance, and after a run of HAIM-branded emails this feels like a deliberate reset to everyday relatability.
- The problem is that this email is entirely editorial. There is no offer, no CTA button, no link to a specific product page, and no discount or GWP mechanic — just a scrolling series of testimonial quotes and product close-ups. After the June 14 and June 15 GWP emails that trained readers to expect a reward for opening, this send delivers no payoff. It reads like a brand awareness insert rather than a marketing email.
- ILIA's editorial instincts are strong, but this format is underutilized without a single shop-able element. The creative deserves a destination.

## What's working

- **Seasonal timing is perfect.** "BFF + SPF" in June, with SPF/summer-skin copy, is genuinely on-calendar in a way the HAIM arc wasn't tied to a moment.
- **The friendship framing is differentiated.** After the HAIM arc, real-person testimonials from diverse women feel grounded and more credible than celebrity quotes.
- **"Same but different" headline lands.** It encapsulates the diversity-of-skin-types message without being preachy and makes the pun in the subject payoff.
- **Quotes are specific and natural.** "It doesn't break me out" and "great coverage and still feel like me" are actual reasons someone buys a product, not PR copy.
- **Render is clean.** No broken images, overlapping text, or layout issues visible.

## What's weak

- **No CTA button anywhere in the email.** There is no visible "Shop Now," "Try the Tint," or even a text link to click. An editorial email with zero exit ramp is a missed conversion opportunity.
- **No product name called out boldly.** The Super Serum Skin Tint (presumably the hero) is referenced in fine-print body copy but never given a headline moment — someone who skims will miss it entirely.
- **No offer or incentive.** Two consecutive GWP emails set an expectation; this send delivers nothing actionable, which risks a lower-engagement signal on this list segment.
- **Quote attribution is repeated and cluttered.** "AISSA" and "EMILY" appear multiple times each, making the scroll feel long without building to anything. The email needs a structural anchor (a product tile or a button) to give the quotes context.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a single CTA button mid-email and at the bottom.** "Find Your Shade" or "Shop the Tint" — something that turns the scroll into a funnel. This is the single highest-leverage change.
- 2. **Bold the product name in the first text block.** "Super Serum Skin Tint with SPF" should be typeset as a proper headline, not buried in narrative copy.
- 3. **Consider a light GWP or loyalty hook.** Even "free shipping today only" would justify the open after the June 14–15 GWP conditioning. Without it, the sequence teaches subscribers to skip non-offer sends.
- 4. **Tighten the quote cascade.** Pick two quotes total (one per person), put them in visually distinct callout blocks, and use the saved vertical space for a product shot with a linked button.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<title>` tag is empty:**
```html

```
Some assistive technologies and tab labels surface the title. Should be set to the email subject or brand name.

**CSS `@import` for web fonts — cross-client failure risk:**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```
Gmail (both web and app) strips `@import` entirely. Montserrat will silently fall back to `Helvetica`/`sans-serif` for the majority of recipients. This is expected behavior for email, but worth knowing if pixel-perfect font rendering is a goal. No fix needed unless brand requires it — just document the fallback.
