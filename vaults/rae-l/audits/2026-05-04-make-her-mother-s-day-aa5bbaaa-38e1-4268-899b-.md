# Make Her Mother’s Day ✨
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-04**
## Executive summary

- A solid, seasonally-grounded Mother's Day send from Sally Beauty that leads with a clear 40% off styling tools offer and backs it up with specific products. The visual execution is clean, the hierarchy is functional, and the free 2-hour delivery banner adds real utility. The main drag is an underperforming subject line — "Make Her Mother's Day ✨" is gifting-generic, puts no offer in the subject, and will compete poorly in a crowded holiday inbox. Inside the email, "Shop now" is repeated across every CTA, squandering opportunities to guide the click toward a specific intent.

## What's working

- **Free 2-hour delivery banner** is the first thing you see and removes a real purchase barrier.
- **40% off with named products** — four specific styling tools (flat irons, curling iron, rotating brush) are shown with sizes. This is more credible than "up to X% off."
- **"Gifts for Mom from under $25"** creates a useful price anchor for lower-commitment shoppers.
- **Klarna integration** is surfaced mid-email at a logical point — visible for anyone calculating whether the styling tool is worth it.
- **Benefits strip** (In-Store, Online, Same Day Delivery, BOPIS) reinforces omnichannel accessibility without bloating the body.

## What's weak

- **Every CTA reads "Shop now."** Six-plus instances of identical button copy creates no hierarchy between "browse the hero" and "buy this specific flat iron." Missed opportunity to use copy like "Shop styling deals" or "Explore hair tools."
- **The "under $25" section shows no products** — just a promotional lifestyle shot and a "Shop now" link. If the goal is to convert browsers to buyers, this section does the least work.
- **No loyalty/rewards hook.** Sally Beauty has a Beauty Club rewards program; a subscriber email is exactly where that should appear, even a single line ("Earn points on every gift").
- **Subject carries no offer signal.** A subscriber scanning a holiday inbox sees "Make Her Mother's Day ✨" and could attribute it to any beauty, fashion, or lifestyle brand sending that week.
- **Social proof is absent.** No customer ratings, star counts, or "most gifted" callouts on the product cards.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rotate one CTA per section:** hero → "Shop all gifts," 40% section → "Shop styling tools," under $25 section → "See gifts under $25."
- 2. **Add a star rating or "bestseller" badge** to at least one of the four featured products — this alone can lift click-through on product cards.
- 3. **Surface the Beauty Club.** Even "Earn 1 point per $1 spent" in the footer before unsubscribe is better than nothing for retention.
- 4. **Put the offer in the subject line.** "40% off styling tools for Mom — ships today" is twice as likely to earn an open on a crowded holiday day.
- 5. **Give the under-$25 section product tiles** rather than a lifestyle image — mirror the 40% off layout to create a scannable gift guide feel.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>`**: The opening tag is `<html xmlns="..." xmlns:v="..." xmlns:o="...">` with no `lang="en"`. This fails WCAG 3.1.1 (Language of Page) and degrades screen-reader pronunciation.

**`Founders Grotesk` referenced with no `@font-face` declaration**: The global link rule `a { font-family:'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }` names a custom typeface, but no corresponding `@font-face` block exists in the stylesheet. Every `<a>` element will silently fall back to Helvetica. If this is intentional the rule should be cleaned up; if not, the font declaration is missing.

**External Google Fonts requests blocked by privacy clients**: Six `@font-face` rules make HTTP requests to `fonts.gstatic.com`. Apple Mail with Mail Privacy Protection, and many enterprise mail gateways, block or cache these externally loaded resources. Outlook desktop ignores web fonts entirely. The fallback stack (`Helvetica, sans-serif`) is adequate, but the external requests add load time and may trigger security scanners.

**`img { white-space: pre; }` is non-standard**: This rule has no effect on `<img>` elements (it applies to text nodes) and is unexpected. It is likely a copy-paste artifact and should be removed to avoid confusing future template editors.
