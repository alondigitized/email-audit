# *poof* the results are in
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-06**
## Executive summary

- This is the fourth email in a tightly sequenced product arc ILIA has been running all week: launch (June 2), ingredient story (June 3), bundle offer (June 5), and now the social proof close (June 6). The sequencing is smart — launch a product, tell the science story, pitch the bundle, then validate with real consumer data. "*poof* the results are in" is a payoff beat that earns its place. The subject-line pun ("*poof*" = proof + powder poof) is clever, and the "proof is in the powder" hero headline lands the same gag with more elegance. The three-stat format (95% / 95% / 93%) is clean and confidence-building.
- The problem is that this email has no offer. It's a pure proof play — which would be fine as a second email in a post-launch arc, but this is the fourth send in five days, and there is nothing here that closes a wavering buyer. A subscriber who already saw the June 5 duo CTA and didn't act needs a reason to reconsider — not more validation. ILIA is doing the sequencing right but stopping one beat short of conversion.
- **Verdict:** A well-crafted proof email that does its job — but runs the risk of being the email that finishes warming up a buyer and then doesn't ask for the sale.

## What's working

- **"Proof is in the powder" / "*poof*"** — the dual pun across subject and hero copy is the best creative line in this whole arc; it's earned, not forced.
- **Three-stat format** — 95% / 95% / 93% presented as clean stacked callouts reads fast and builds credibility without feeling clinical.
- **"Beaming reviews"** — "beaming" does double duty (glowing skin + pride); sharp copy that reads on-brand.
- **Visual hierarchy** — product hero → stats → reviews → CTA follows a logical proof sequence the eye tracks naturally.
- **No render bugs** — clean layout, no broken images or overlapping elements.

## What's weak

- **No offer** — after three preceding emails (one of which was a direct 20%-off bundle pitch), this send has no discount, no free gift, no urgency hook to move a fence-sitter.
- **Subject is arc-dependent** — "*poof* the results are in" only lands if you followed June 2–5; cold or disengaged subscribers have no idea what product is being proven.
- **Cadence fatigue** — four sends in five days on the same product family is getting heavy; the "cadence feels right" criterion does not pass here.
- **Top banner text is unreadable** at screenshot resolution — if there's a shipping offer up there, it's doing no work visually.
- **CTA is shop-only** — no price anchoring, no urgency, no member exclusive — just a shop button on a proof email with no call to act now.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a time-limited close** — pair the proof stats with a 48-hour free-shipping offer or bundle price. The receipts are in; now give people a reason to act on them today, not tomorrow.
- 2. **Rewrite the subject to name the product** — "95% saw results. The proof is in the powder." or similar. Subscribers who missed June 2–5 should know what they're clicking into without prior context.
- 3. **Anchor the CTA to the offer** — "Shop Soft Focus — Free Shipping, 48 Hours" beats a naked "Shop" button on a proof email.
- 4. **Make the top banner legible** — if that's a shipping threshold or offer, it should be readable; if it's boilerplate, remove it so it stops competing with the hero.
- **Subject Alt A:** `95% saw a difference. Proof is in the powder.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The Soft Focus study results are in`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Consumer perception data from real wearers — plus free shipping for 48 hours.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Real results, real reviews. Here's what testers said about the Soft Focus Finishing Powder.`
