# There's a party in NYC
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-18**
## Executive summary

- This is Glossier's second email on June 18 — sending two on the same day, after ten-plus sale emails in eight days, is the defining context for everything below. The creative gamble here is real: plain text, no images, no button, a cultural/event hook ("the whole city is downtown today") instead of another percentage blast. That instinct is right. After ten HTML sale emails, a text-only letter reads completely differently in the inbox and earns re-engagement that the eleventh "Save 25%" subject would not.
- The execution, however, is half-finished. The NYC party hook is evocative but the email never closes the loop — there's no credible deadline, no extended-sale expiration, and the only call to action is a bare hyperlink that says "here." The product curation is the strongest thing in this send: three distinct use-case groupings (scent, routine, summer staples) with brief, specific copy that actually sells each product. That's the level of judgment that usually lives in a hero module, not buried in paragraph three of a letter.
- Net verdict: smart angle, weak execution on urgency and CTA. If the plain-text format had included a deadline and a named link ("Shop the sale →"), this would have been the best email in the arc. As sent, it's a 6.

## What's working

- **Plain-text format breaks pattern.** After ten HTML sale emails, this reads as a personal note and earns open attention rather than scroll-past.
- **NYC cultural hook is timely.** "Confetti, the noise, the works" grounds the email in a real-world moment rather than manufactured scarcity.
- **Product groupings are genuinely useful.** Three coherent bundles (scent duo, Five Minute Face, summer staples) give real shopping direction instead of a generic "shop all."
- **Specific product links embedded naturally.** Linking directly to Glossier You, Soie, Fragrance Duo, etc. reduces friction to the product page without a clunky "Shop Now" button.
- **Brand voice is spot-on.** "xoxo, The Glossier Team" and "BFFs" are completely on-brand and make the letter format feel earned rather than lazy.

## What's weak

- **No deadline on the sale extension.** Saying the sale has been extended without saying *when it ends* kills urgency. The subscriber's natural response is "I can shop this later" — and they won't.
- **"Here" is the weakest possible CTA.** The only path to the full sale is a hyperlink on the word "here." That's invisible on mobile and earns near-zero intentional clicks versus a named link or even a bolded phrase.
- **Two emails on June 18 is a list-hygiene problem.** Subscribers who opened "Straight from our community" a few hours earlier are now getting this second send. That accelerates unsubscribes and trains the inbox filter against Glossier.
- **No personalization hook.** "Hi there" after eleven sale emails signals this arc has become broadcast, not relationship.
- **The NYC angle is unresolved.** The intro sets up a party metaphor but the body immediately drops it and pivots to product bullets. The narrative doesn't land because there's no payoff (what is Glossier doing to celebrate? Is there a party? Is there a geo-specific perk for NYC subscribers?).

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a deadline, bold and specific.** "The sale ends Sunday at midnight" changes the entire psychology of this email. Without it, the extension announcement actually reduces urgency because the subscriber now assumes the sale runs indefinitely.
- 2. **Replace "here" with a named link.** "Shop everything on sale →" earns more clicks than the word "here" in every A/B test, on every platform. If the plain-text format is intentional, a bold named link is the right format-native CTA.
- 3. **Resolve the NYC hook.** Either cut it entirely and lead with the offer, or commit to it — "We're celebrating NYC Pride weekend with an extra 48-hour extension" gives the hook a reason to exist.
- 4. **Do not send a second email the same day.** This should have been the *only* June 18 send, or it should have replaced "Straight from our community" rather than supplemented it.
- 5. **Add an expiration.** "Last 48 hours to use your Friends of Glossier discount" would have made this the closing chapter of the arc rather than an ambiguous continuation.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `The sale runs through midnight Sunday. Here's what to buy.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `20% off sitewide, 25% on qualifying orders. Ends soon.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Opaque Klaviyo redirects — UTM parameters unverifiable**
All 9 visible links resolve through `ctrk.klclick.com/l/01KVD9PXV02GXECKFCTZKMZ994_N`. The final destination URLs and any UTM parameters cannot be confirmed from the source. If Klaviyo is not configured to append UTM params at redirect time, attributed traffic will be lost.

**`rel="nofollow"` on all email links**
Every `<a>` carries `rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow"`. `nofollow` is a web/SEO directive with zero effect in email clients — it does not belong in email HTML and suggests the template was authored or post-processed with web-first tooling. Not harmful, but indicates a template hygiene issue.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Hardcoded `background-color: rgb(255, 255, 255)` on every `<p>` and `<ul>`**
Every block element has this inline. Apple Mail, Gmail App (dark), and Samsung Mail will not invert these elements to dark mode — users in dark mode see white boxes over a dark canvas. Fix: remove inline background-color from content elements and handle at the container/wrapper level with `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` or Klaviyo's dark mode block.

**`<p>` inside `<li>` throughout**
The list items wrap content in `<p>` tags: `<li><p ...>...</p></li>`. This is technically valid HTML but introduces double vertical spacing in several clients (Yahoo Mail, older Outlook WebApp). Standard email practice is bare `<li>` text nodes.

**Empty paragraph spacer with zero-width joiner**
`<p ...><span style="font-size: 14px;">​</span></p>` — The `​` (U+200D, zero-width joiner) is used as a visual spacer. This is a rendering hack; spacing is inconsistent across clients and the character may be read aloud by screen readers depending on UA.

**No `lang` attribute visible on `<html>` tag**
Cannot confirm `<html lang="en">` from the provided source. Missing `lang` prevents screen readers from selecting the correct pronunciation engine.

**Custom font loaded via `@import`**
`@import url("https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/Sn6eFZ/custom_fonts.css")` — correctly gated behind `<!--[if !mso]><!-->` to exclude Outlook. The inline font stacks (`Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif`) serve as fallback, which is correct. No issue if the CDN is reliable, but worth noting the external dependency.
