# The art of 20% off forever
**Score:** 10/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- This is ILIA's tenth email in roughly six days, and the strategy here is the most sophisticated of the run: use the Friends + Family closing window as a forcing function to recruit long-term subscribers at a permanent 20% discount. That's a smart CLV play — far smarter than sending an eleventh "sale ends soon" blast. The subscribe-and-save value prop is well laid out in the "Art of Subscribe + Save" section: four benefit bullets (20% Off Forever, Free Shipping, Great Points, cancel anytime) that genuinely address the objections to subscription commitment.
- The problem is the subject line. "The art of 20% off forever" is the most decorative, least efficient line of the entire campaign. It wants to be poetic while "20% off" wants to be direct, and the two don't coexist. "Forever" also signals permanence without urgency — the exact wrong message to open a time-bounded sale window. The previous send on May 22 ("20% off makeup + skincare + SPF") was blunter and better.
- The Cart Deco GWP section at the bottom reads like a second email tacked on — a gift-with-purchase mechanic that dilutes the subscription focus and splits the click. At this point in the campaign, the ask should be singular: subscribe. The scattered energy is the main miss.

## What's working

- The subscribe-and-save value section is genuinely well organized — four clear benefit pillars with "cancel anytime" addressing the commitment objection directly.
- "SUBSCRIBE + SAVE" and "GET 20% OFF FOREVER" are specific, action-oriented CTAs — better than "Discover" or "Shop Now."
- The banner ("20% OFF SITEWIDE | 20% OFF FOR VIPs") sets context immediately and doesn't bury the lead.
- Product imagery (Super Serum Skin Tint, Mascara, Shadow Stick, Multi Stick) shows the full ILIA hero lineup — reinforces that the subscription covers their core products, not a limited selection.

## What's weak

- Cadence is the elephant in the inbox: this is email ten in six days; open fatigue is real and "forever" does nothing to cut through it.
- "The art of 20% off forever" is too precious — "the art of" adds zero information and costs the strongest word in the subject (forever) its punch.
- "Forever" is contingent on maintaining an active subscription, but the subject implies unconditional permanence — that's the line between clever framing and mildly misleading.
- No explicit deadline for the Friends + Family window; if subscribers need to subscribe *during* the sale to lock in the rate, that deadline should be visible and prominent — it isn't.
- The Cart Deco GWP section fragments the ask at a moment when the email needs a single conversion target.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject to be direct and urgent.** "The art of" earns nothing. The Friends + Family window closing is the real hook.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Lock in your 20% off before Friends + Family ends`
- **Subject Alt B:** `20% off. Forever. But only if you subscribe today.`
- 2. **Add an explicit sale-end date.** "Subscribe during Friends + Family" is doing heavy lifting without a date — add "ends [date]" at minimum in the hero copy.
- 3. **Cut the Cart Deco section entirely or move to a separate email.** The GWP offer is a good mechanic, but it belongs in a different send. This email's job is subscription conversion; splitting the CTA bleeds conversion.
- 4. **Lead the preheader with the deadline, not the offer echo.**
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Friends + Family closes soon — lock in 20% off your subscriptions forever`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `This is the last chance to lock in subscriber pricing from the sale`
- 5. **Make the "cancel anytime" guarantee more prominent.** Right now it's the fourth bullet. It should be the second — that's the objection killing most subscription conversions.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 3a — Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title> </title>
```
The title is a single space. Screen readers announce this as the document title in some clients (e.g., VoiceOver + Apple Mail). Should contain the email subject or a descriptive fallback.

**Issue 3b — `@import` for web fonts (two instances)**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```
`@import` inside `<style>` is stripped by Gmail, Yahoo, and several mobile clients. Fonts will silently fall back to `"Helvetica", sans-serif`. This is expected behavior in email but worth confirming the fallback stack renders acceptably — it does (`"Montserrat", "Helvetica", sans-serif` is declared at the bottom of the stylesheet).

**Issue 3c — Duplicate `img` reset rule**
```css
/* First declaration (~line 6 of stylesheet) */
img { border: 0; line-height: 100%; outline: none; text-decoration: none; -ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic }

/* Second declaration (~line 25 of stylesheet) */
img { border: 0; height: auto; line-height: 100%; outline: none; text-decoration: none; max-width: 100% }
```
The second rule wins. The first omits `height: auto` and `max-width: 100%`; the second omits `-ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic`. These should be consolidated into one rule. No rendering impact expected in modern clients, but it signals a template hygiene issue.

**Issue 3d — Zero link differentiation across all states**
```css
a:not([name]) {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
a:link    {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
a:visited {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
a:active  {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
a:hover   {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
```
No visual change on hover or visited state. In web-rendered clients (Gmail browser, Outlook.com) this makes hyperlinks indistinguishable from body text unless they are styled via inline `color` overrides. WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.1 (Use of Color) requires links be distinguishable by more than color alone when no underline is present.
