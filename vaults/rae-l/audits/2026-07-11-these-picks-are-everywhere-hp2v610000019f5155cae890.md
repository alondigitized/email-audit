# These picks are everywhere
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-11**
## Executive summary

- This is Sephora's second consecutive editorial email since the Stacking Weekend arc closed — and like the July 8 "Summer's Hottest Beauty" send, it leans entirely on curation over commerce. The "Hot on Social" hero is a solid concept: lean into TikTok/IG-driven discovery and let social proof carry the click. The Patrick Starrr / One Size launch gives it a genuine news hook. But the email rapidly fragments into a parade of unrelated modules — 4X points, Bundled Beauty, New from Rhode, Insider membership — with no connective tissue. Each block competes with the others instead of building toward a single point.
- The cadence concern is real. After five emails in seven days (July 1–7 Stacking Weekend) followed by "Summer's Hottest" on July 8, this lands as send number seven in eleven days. The subject "These picks are everywhere" is the vaguest of the bunch — less directional than "Summer's hottest beauty ☀️" which itself was a 6/10. There's a ceiling on engagement when you're this deep in an inbox run.
- The honest verdict: visually competent, structurally scattered. A clear editorial thesis (social picks) is buried under loyalty nudges, brand launches, and membership housekeeping. This email would have been stronger as just the Hot on Social curated moment, or just the Rhode launch — not both and three other things.

## What's working

- **"Hot on Social / You're skin-fluenced" hero** is genuinely clever copy and aligns with how this audience actually discovers products (TikTok, Reels).
- **Patrick Starrr / One Size collab** is a recognizable name drop with a specific news hook ("available in three swift shades") — gives the editorial a real anchor.
- **4X Points module** is cleanly blocked and easy to parse — loyalty members will notice it instantly.
- **"New from rhode"** is well-timed brand news (Hailey Bieber's brand is a genuine cultural moment) and uses a strong model shot.
- **No render bugs** visible — layout holds, images loaded, text is legible throughout.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is completely inert** — "These picks are everywhere" names no product, no offer, no category, no urgency. Coming after "🚨 ENDS TODAY" and "Ends 7/7," this lands as a shrug.
- **Six competing modules with no hierarchy** — Hot on Social → product grid → 4X Points → Bundled Beauty → Rhode → Insider is five pivots too many. The eye doesn't know where to land after the hero.
- **No deadline or offer anchor in the hero** — "Hot on Social" is a mood, not a reason to act today. Nothing pulls the trigger.
- **"SHOP NOW" on repeat** — every module's CTA is generic "Shop Now." No specificity anywhere below the hero.
- **Cadence fatigue risk** — this is send 7 in 11 days from Sephora. Even strong editorial can erode open rates when subscribers feel over-messaged.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse to one editorial thesis** — pick the Patrick Starrr launch OR the Rhode launch OR the Hot on Social roundup. Running all three dilutes each.
- 2. **Give the hero a click reason** — add "trending this week on TikTok" with a date stamp, or tie the Hot on Social picks to the 4X points window ("Earn 4X on today's trending picks").
- 3. **Rewrite the "Shop Now" CTAs** — "Shop the Drops," "Get the Rhode Look," "Earn 4X Now" each do real work; "Shop Now" does not.
- 4. **Surface the 4X points offer higher** — right now it's buried below the product grid. Loyalty members respond to it; don't make them scroll.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `Patrick Starrr just dropped — and it's already everywhere`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `TikTok made them do it (these are the picks)`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `Earn 4X points on the drops everyone's talking about this week`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `One Size by Patrick Starrr. New from Rhode. Your For You page, but shoppable.`

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport meta** — confirmed in source:
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
This blocks pinch-to-zoom on mobile, which is an accessibility failure (WCAG 1.4.4). Should be removed or replaced with `maximum-scale=5`.

**`text-size-adjust: none` applied to every element** — confirmed:
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  -moz-text-size-adjust: none;
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```
This overrides the OS/browser text-size accessibility setting for all elements. Acceptable on `body` to prevent iOS auto-inflate, but applying it globally suppresses user-initiated zoom adjustments throughout the email.

**`<title>Sephora</title>`** — non-descriptive. Screen readers read the `<title>` when the email opens in a browser; a subject-aligned value (e.g., "These picks are everywhere — Sephora") improves accessibility.
