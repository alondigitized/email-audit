# NEW Soft Focus Finishing Powder
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-02**
## Executive summary

- This is the payoff send ILIA telegraphed on May 29 — "Coming 06.02—our *softest* launch yet." It delivers exactly what that teaser promised: a clean, editorial product reveal built around a single SKU with strong photography, precise benefit language, and a notable absence of any discount. After 18+ Friends + Family emails hammering 20% off, that restraint is correct and earned. The email earns its open because the anticipation was already set.
- The structure is tight — name, tagline, feature stack, ingredient story, shade range, sustainability story, VIP section — and the visual hierarchy is clear throughout. This is one of ILIA's better product-launch executions: the product does the talking, the copy stays out of its way.
- The weak point is the subject line. "NEW Soft Focus Finishing Powder" is a catalog header, not a subject line — it throws away all the atmospheric equity built in the May 29 teaser ("our *softest* launch yet"). A subscriber who opened that teaser with genuine anticipation deserves a subject that rewards them, not one that reads like a product listing.

## What's working

- **Perfect cadence timing.** After the May 31 brand-story cooldown and the May 29 teaser, landing the launch on June 2 exactly as promised builds credibility.
- **Single-product focus with genuine editorial depth.** Featherweight → Blurring → Buildable → 12-hour shine control + hydration is a clean benefit ladder — each word earns its space.
- **Soft Focus Complex ingredient story.** Naming the proprietary complex and tying it to the product name is strong brand-building; it's not just a powder, it's a formula.
- **Sustainability callout (Reusable Mirror + Recyclable Compact)** lands naturally mid-scroll rather than buried in footer fine print.
- **VIP section at the bottom** rewards members without interrupting the product story — correct placement.

## What's weak

- **Subject line wastes the teaser equity.** "NEW Soft Focus Finishing Powder" is mechanical where "our *softest* launch yet" was evocative — the switchback in tone is jarring.
- **No price anywhere in the email.** For a new product with no discount anchor, omitting the price entirely creates friction when intent converts; the reader has to go to the site to know if it's $38 or $68.
- **Only three translucent shades** (light, medium, deep) — this is narrow for a finishing powder and it reads as a potential miss for deeper skin tones. The email doesn't address it.
- **CTA buttons appear small and hard to differentiate** at a glance — at this scroll depth there appear to be multiple CTAs competing rather than one dominant action.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject line.** It's the single highest-leverage change. The May 29 teaser set the tone — carry it through.
- **Subject Alt A:** `The softest thing we've ever made is here`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Soft Focus is here. Your new filter.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Featherweight, blurring, buildable — and it's finally June 2.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Three translucent shades. Up to 12-hour wear. Meet Soft Focus Powder.`
- 2. **Add the price.** Even a single line — "Shop Soft Focus Finishing Powder · $XX" — removes a purchase-friction step and anchors perceived value without requiring a promo.
- 3. **Address the shade range directly.** Three shades sounds thin on paper. A one-liner like "Three universally translucent shades — finish-perfecting on every skin tone" reframes scarcity as design intent.
- 4. **Consolidate CTAs to one primary action.** The product story builds well; let a single clear button ("Shop Soft Focus Powder") be the release valve rather than multiple smaller links.
