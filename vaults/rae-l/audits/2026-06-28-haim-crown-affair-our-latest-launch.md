# HAIM, Crown Affair + our latest launch
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-28**
## Executive summary

- This is a catch-all digest that tries to do three things at once — announce the Soft Focus Finishing Powder (Skin Tint "new head"), reprise the Lip Sketch Sharpener GWP, and remind you the Crown Affair collab exists — and ends up doing none of them well. The format is essentially a plain-text newsletter with two small right-rail images and zero CTA buttons. The subject teases "HAIM" as the lead hook, but the email body never visibly delivers HAIM; it's buried in the image thumbnails if it's there at all. That's a subject-line bait the body can't cash.
- The brand cadence problem is acute here. Crown Affair appeared in the June 23 launch email and again in the June 27 "Don't overthink it" follow-up. This is now the third email in five days touching that collab, but this one also tries to fold in two other campaigns. The result is diluted attention and subscriber fatigue. The Soft Focus Finishing Powder — what could be a strong standalone launch — gets buried as a bullet point in a digest.
- Verdict: a newsletter that should have been three separate emails, or confidently leaned on one hero. The plain-text digest format drains urgency from every offer it carries.

## What's working

- Summer lifestyle opening copy is warm and on-brand — the "juice farmers market hauls / lazy picnics" framing is charming and matches ILIA's casual voice.
- Subject line is under 40 characters and avoids spam triggers — it'll render cleanly in mobile inboxes.
- The GWP mechanic (free sharpener with Lip Sketch) is a real, specific offer — not vague percent-off language.
- Crown Affair collab description is factual and benefit-led ("clean haircare + skincare + makeup essentials / simple art of getting ready").

## What's weak

- HAIM is the lead in the subject line but is invisible in the email body text — if the images contain HAIM, they're thumbnails too small to do the heavy lifting.
- No CTA buttons anywhere — a text digest with zero clickable buttons is asking subscribers to do all the work.
- Three campaigns ≠ one email: Soft Focus Powder, Lip Sketch GWP, and Crown Affair collab each deserve their own moment; stacked like this, none has urgency.
- Crown Affair appears here for the third send in five days — cadence fatigue is real, and this isn't adding new information to justify the repeat.
- "A new head for Skin Tint" is confusing product-internal jargon; a new subscriber has no idea what that means.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make this email about ONE thing.** The Soft Focus Finishing Powder is new — that's the send. Drop the Lip Sketch reprise (it had its moment on June 20) and move Crown Affair to a standalone close-out send later in the week.
- 2. **Add at least one CTA button per section.** Even a simple "Shop Now" tied to an anchor URL would lift click-through dramatically from near-zero.
- 3. **Put HAIM in the hero, not just the subject.** If HAIM is the creative hook, lead with a full-width HAIM image before the body copy. The subject promise needs to be visible above the fold.
- 4. **Replace "A new head for Skin Tint"** with consumer-facing language: "We added a finishing powder to the Skin Tint family."
- 5. **Add a deadline to the GWP.** "Free sharpener with Lip Sketch, today through Sunday" creates urgency the current copy lacks.

**Root cause pattern:** The `=3D` occurrences in the unsubscribe URL and the trailing `),` in product URLs strongly suggest quoted-printable encoding was applied to the HTML body but not fully decoded before link extraction — either in the sending pipeline or the QA checker. **Tracking pixel:** `https://trk.send.iliabeauty.com/o/01KW837P8XAASCZR1SE63YRV6Y` is present (open-tracking pixel). No issues with the tracker URL itself, but the `<img>` has no `alt=""` attribute (see §3).

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- **Missing alt text on two images:**
  - `2aa0e67a-8e4a-49f9-900e-e2378e86b5d1.jpeg` — main content image, `alt=""` attribute is present in the tag but the value is empty string. For a decorative image this is technically acceptable, but if this image carries product/campaign content, it should have descriptive alt text for screen readers and image-blocking clients.
  - Tracking pixel `trk.send.iliabeauty.com/o/01KW837P8XAASCZR1SE63YRV6Y` — tracking pixels should explicitly carry `alt=""` (empty, not absent) to suppress broken-image icons in blocking clients. Verify the attribute is present, not just omitted.

- **Font import via `@import` inside `<style>`:** `@import url("https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css")` is wrapped in an MSO conditional comment (`<!--[if !mso]>`), which is correct practice. No issue.

- **Inline `<p>` inside `<li>`:** The HTML nests `<p>` elements directly inside `<li>` elements. This is valid HTML5 but can cause unexpected spacing in older email clients (Outlook 2016/2019 in particular adds extra margin). Low severity but worth noting.

- **Mixed font-size units:** Body copy uses `font-size: 15px` on `<span>` elements, while `<li>` elements declare `font-size: 11pt`. These are not equivalent (11pt ≈ 14.67px). This can produce inconsistent rendering across clients.

### 5. Compliance

- **Physical mailing address not detected** by QA scanner. The truncated HTML provided does not include a footer — this may exist below the truncation point, but the QA check reports it absent. If the address is genuinely missing from the footer, this is a CAN-SPAM violation.
- **Unsubscribe link is broken:** `https://manage.kmail-lists.com/subscriptions/unsubscribe?a=3DSguDsz&=` — the `=3D` encoding artifact means the link will 404 when clicked. A non-functional unsubscribe mechanism is a direct CAN-SPAM violation (15 U.S.C. § 7704(a)(3)).

**RFC 8058 / One-click unsubscribe:**

- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not detected. Google and Yahoo's 2024 bulk sender requirements mandate `List-Unsubscribe-Post` for senders above 5k/day. If ILIA meets that threshold, this is a deliverability risk.
- These headers may simply not be captured by the relay — confirm by inspecting raw message source directly.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
