# Rae, get $10 off your $50 purchase⁺ or $20 off your $100 purchase⁺! Terms & excl. apply
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- Ulta Beauty is running a tiered Memorial Day discount — $10 off $50 or $20 off $100 — and the email executes the mechanic cleanly. The "Summer savings:ON" hero dominates above the fold with the two thresholds side by side in large type, the barcode for in-store redemption is visible, and the loyalty kicker (5X points) and free insulated tote GWP add two more reasons to act. The overall structure is competent and legible.
- What holds this back is the subject line, which is doing too much work. The dual-threshold offer is naturally a two-clause sentence, and tacking "Terms & excl. apply" onto it turns the subject into a run-on that exhausts before the inbox has a chance to hook. Compare this to the May 3rd "Rae: This Mystery Offer ends soon 👀" send — that one led with intrigue and the discount was the reveal. Here the whole offer is front-loaded with none of the urgency that made the mystery-offer sends more compelling.
- Cadence is also a mild concern: this lands the day after the Benefit Cosmetics catalog send (May 20). Two sends in two days without escalating stakes risks inbox fatigue, and this email doesn't signal why today specifically.

## What's working

- **Tiered discount is hero-first.** The $10/$20 split is visually dominant and scannable without any reading effort.
- **Personalization lands twice.** "Rae" in the subject and "Hi, Rae" inside the email both fire — rare in a promotional send.
- **Loyalty layer is well-placed.** The 5X points block is positioned mid-email as a second reason to act, reinforcing value beyond the raw discount.
- **In-store redemption is supported.** The barcode signals omnichannel utility — not just an online-only play.
- **Free insulated tote is a strong secondary hook.** A tangible GWP with a seasonal item (insulated tote → summer) adds perceived value beyond the percentage math.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is too long and buries itself.** At ~87 characters it will truncate on every mobile client; "Terms & excl. apply" in the subject is unusual and erodes trust on first glance.
- **No visible deadline.** Neither the subject, hero, nor any in-email copy I can see states when the offer expires. The May 3rd mystery-offer send had explicit urgency ("ends soon") — this one doesn't.
- **The minimum spend increased quietly.** Earlier May sends were $10 off $35 (May 1, May 3). This one is $10 off $50 — 43% higher floor, with no "upgrade" framing to soften it.
- **CTA copy is generic.** "Shop now" appears alongside every other Ulta email; given two distinct offer tiers, two separate CTAs ("Save $10" / "Save $20") would sharpen the choice.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Shorten and sharpen the subject.** The dual-threshold structure forces a long subject; pick the hero threshold or lead with the season, not both.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Rae, save $20 on $100 this Memorial Day⁺`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Summer savings are ON — $10 or $20 off inside`
- 2. **Add a deadline.** Even "Offer ends Monday" visible in the hero would make the discount feel time-sensitive and justify the send timing relative to the May 20 email.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Ends Memorial Day — use in-store or online`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop the summer sale before it's gone — offer ends soon`
- 3. **Split the CTAs by tier.** Replace a single "Shop now" with "Save $10 →" and "Save $20 →" buttons under their respective offer tiles. Reduces decision paralysis and tracks conversion by tier.
- 4. **Frame the threshold increase.** The previous mystery-offer sends used $10 off $35. Acknowledge the bigger reward explicitly — "Our biggest discount this season" or "More off, more to love" — so the higher floor reads as upgrade, not restriction.

## 1. Overview
