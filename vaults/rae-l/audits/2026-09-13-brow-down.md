# Brow down
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-13**
## Executive summary

- This is a hard pivot away from the Labor Day discount run — "Brow down" is a single-SKU, no-discount social-proof send for Boy Brow, and it's a clean break from the five-plus promo emails between 9/3 and 9/8. There's no offer here at all: no %, no free-gift threshold, no urgency. It's pure product-marketing, leaning entirely on "thousands of 5-star reviews" and three real customer testimonials with photos.
- The execution is tight and on-brand — clean hero shot, clear single CTA, genuine-feeling review grid with named customers and star ratings. But after a run of discount emails trained this subscriber to expect a deal, a zero-offer send is a lower-urgency, lower-click proposition by design. It reads more like a brand/retention play than a conversion play, and it'll score accordingly on click-through even though the craft is solid.

## What's working

- Single, unambiguous CTA ("Best brows ever") tied to one clear hero product — no split attention.
- Hero image is authentic and product-in-use rather than a stock beauty shot, which fits Glossier's voice.
- The "SO, WHAT ARE THEY SAYING?" review grid uses named customers, star ratings, and real-sounding quotes — strong social proof.
- Diverse, real-looking models in the review section (not just one demographic), which reads as inclusive and credible.
- No render bugs — no overlapping text, broken images, or layout breakage visible.

## What's weak

- No offer of any kind — after a discount-heavy run, this could read as a letdown or simply get skipped by deal-trained subscribers.
- Subject "Brow down" is vague/punny and doesn't say what's inside (no product name, no reviews hook, no price).
- CTA copy "Best brows ever" is a brand-voice flex, not an action-oriented instruction — doesn't tell the reader what happens next.
- Bottom-right review card image (third product shot) is cut off at the right edge of the frame.
- No visible price or "shop now"-style urgency anywhere in the email, so there's little reason to click today vs. later.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a price or "shop Boy Brow, $18" somewhere near the hero CTA — right now there's zero purchase-decision info visible before clicking.
- 2. Rewrite the CTA to be concrete: "Shop Boy Brow" beats "Best brows ever," which is a slogan, not an instruction.
- 3. Fix the subject to actually reference the product/social-proof angle instead of a vague pun — "Brow down" doesn't convey what's in the email.
- 4. Add a preheader — none appears designed into the render, which wastes prime inbox real estate.
- 5. Consider layering a small loyalty or bundle nudge (e.g., "add to your usual Glossier order") since there's no discount to lean on this time.
