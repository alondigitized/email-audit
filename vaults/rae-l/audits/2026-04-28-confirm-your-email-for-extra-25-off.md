# CONFIRM your email for extra 25% off!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-04-28**
## Executive summary

- This is a single-purpose email confirmation trigger — Macy's asking a new subscriber to verify their email in exchange for an extra 25% off. The offer is clear and the CTA is visible, but the execution is stripped-down to the point of feeling unpolished. The hero does its job; everything below it either dilutes focus or disappears into a sea of empty canvas. The email is functional, not compelling.
- It converts subscribers who are already motivated, but leaves engagement on the table for anyone who wasn't fully committed at open. Low persuasive investment for a high-value action (email confirmation drives deliverability and list quality).

## What's working

- **Offer clarity.** "Extra 25% off" is front and center — the value exchange for confirming is immediately legible.
- **Single CTA.** "I'M IN!" is the only primary action in the hero. No competing buttons above the fold.
- **Dark hero contrast.** White text on dark background makes the core message readable and visually distinct from inbox chrome.
- **Navigation bar.** The top nav (Women, Men, Home, Handbags, Shoes, Beauty, Brands) gives a quick brand-credibility signal and secondary escape valve for browsers.

## What's weak

- **Hero copy is clunky.** "You'll be the first to know about all of our best sales & special events plus, we'll send you a special for extra 25% off. Act fast!" is run-on and grammatically awkward ("send you a special"). It undercuts the premium brand perception Macy's works to maintain.
- **CTA label is ambiguous.** "I'M IN!" doesn't tell the user *what* they're confirming. "Confirm My Email" or "Claim My 25% Off" would be more action-explicit and reduce hesitation.
- **Massive empty canvas below content.** The actual email content occupies roughly the top 15–20% of the visible canvas. Everything below is blank grey space — a visible rendering issue that makes the email feel broken or incomplete.
- **"More to explore" section is misplaced.** Buy online / Find a store / Free shipping are utility features, not motivators. Placing them between the primary CTA and the footer creates unnecessary scroll noise on a confirmation email.
- **SMS upsell is a focus tax.** Immediately after asking for email confirmation, there's a "Sign up for texts, get 25% off" strip. Two asks in one email — especially when the first ask hasn't been acted on yet — splits attention and may reduce completion of either.
- **No personalization.** No name, no reference to what the subscriber browsed or expressed interest in. Generic.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Tighten the hero copy.** Something like: "One quick step — confirm your email and we'll send you an exclusive 25% off code. It's yours the moment you tap below." Eliminates the grammar stumble and adds forward momentum.
- 2. **Rename the CTA.** Change "I'M IN!" to "Confirm & Get 25% Off" — ties the action directly to the reward.
- 3. **Fix the empty canvas.** Whether this is a template artifact or a rendering issue, the massive blank space below the footer must be eliminated. It signals broken email to recipients.
- 4. **Move the SMS strip to a post-confirmation thank-you page or a follow-up email** — don't ask for two commitments in the same send.
- 5. **Cut the "More to explore" module entirely** from this specific trigger. Save utility features for a welcome series email. Confirmation emails should have one job.

**Triplicate `<style data-name="grid">` blocks**

CSS content is near-identical. This is redundant parse weight and can cause specificity ambiguity in strict-mode renderers.

**Dark-mode display inconsistency**
- Base style: `.acr-dark-img { display: none }`
- Default media override: `display: inline-block !important`
- AJO fragment override (`ajo:0a04f8db`): `display: block !important`

Mixing `inline-block` and `block` for the same selector across injected fragments can cause image alignment differences in dark-mode-capable clients (Apple Mail, iOS Mail) depending on cascade order.

`<html lang="en" ... class style>` — bare `class` and `style` attributes with no values. Technically invalid HTML 4.0 and XHTML; harmless in practice but signals a template authoring issue.
