# Discover top shelf favorites
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- This is ILIA's third consecutive day of emailing — and the first one that buries the lead. The "Cult Classics for A Reason" editorial frame is well-executed: rich product storytelling, inclusive casting, layered benefit claims, and a clean scroll rhythm. But the 20% Friends + Family offer that anchored the first two sends has been demoted to a promo banner near the footer, while the subject line "Discover top shelf favorites" waves off any urgency entirely. That's a structural mis-match — three days of promo pressure and then a soft editorial subject.
- The body is genuinely strong. Foundation → Mascara → Serum, each with a quote, specific claims, and a CTA. The product detail density (30 shades, SPF, flake-resistant, smudge-resistant, 17,000+ five-star reviews) gives subscribers real reasons to click. The issue is that none of that muscle is flexed at the inbox level, where subject + preview decide whether this gets opened at all.
- Net: a well-built product editorial that earns clicks from anyone who opens it, but leaves open-rate on the table by hiding the sale and going vague in the subject.

## What's working

- **Editorial structure earns the scroll** — foundation with skin-tone model, mascara with lash close-up, serum with texture shot; each block has a quote + benefit ladder + CTA, which creates a satisfying rhythm.
- **Social proof is specific and stacked** — "17,000+ five-star reviews" and "it's a game changer" / "the holy grail of mascara" are visible and credible.
- **Inclusive casting** — the Black model for the skin-tint section is a meaningful signal for a 30-shade foundation range.
- **Product claims are granular** — SPF, 30 flexible shades, flake-resistant, smudge-resistant, safe for sensitive eyes are all visible without clicking anything.
- **20% off sitewide is still present** — the Friends + Family promo survives into this send even if deprioritized.

## What's weak

- **Subject abandons the sale hook** — "Discover top shelf favorites" is soft editorial copy; the 20% offer that's been the spine of this sequence doesn't appear until well down the scroll.
- **20% off banner is bottom-of-email placement** — new subscribers who came in on that sale (see the 5/16 welcome email) will wonder if it's still live; burying it suggests it isn't.
- **All CTAs read "SHOP NOW"** — three distinct products, three identical buttons; each one is a missed opportunity to get specific and personal.
- **No urgency signal anywhere** — neither in the subject, hero, nor the 20% banner; the sale could end today or in a month for all the reader knows.
- **Third email in three days** — cadence is aggressive; a softer subject at this frequency increases skip/ignore risk with less-engaged subscribers.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the sale in the subject.** This is still the Friends + Family window — say so. A subject like "Cult classics. 20% off. Still on." is three facts that each earn the open. Right now the subject and the sale are strangers.
- 2. **Move the 20% banner to directly below the hero** — the promo scroll should be: hero offer → product 1 → product 2 → product 3 → footer. Not: product 1 → product 2 → product 3 → oh, also sale.
- 3. **Add a deadline to the 20% banner** — even "ends Sunday" turns a passive banner into a conversion lever. Without it, the offer has no gravity.
- 4. **Differentiate the CTAs** — "Shop Super Serum Skin Tint", "Shop Limitless Lash", "Shop [Serum Name]" costs nothing and signals you know what you're promoting.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Cult Classics — 20% off, still on`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your top shelf, now 20% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Friends + Family ends soon — shop the award winners while the discount lasts`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `17,000 five-star reviews. 20% off sitewide. This is the send.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` font calls will silently fail in most clients:**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```
Gmail (web + Android), Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo strip or ignore `@import` rules. Montserrat will not load in these clients — fallback chain (`"Helvetica", sans-serif`) will render instead. This is survivable but should be noted if brand font consistency is required. Mitigation: inline critical font weights via `<link>` in `<head>` (partially respected by Apple Mail/iOS) or accept system font fallback.

**All anchor states neutralized:**
```css
a:not([name]) { color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none }
a:link, a:visited, a:active, a:hover { color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none }
```
Hover state has no visual differentiation from static text. Not a blocker (email CTAs typically use button containers), but confirm all interactive links are wrapped in styled button elements rather than bare inline anchors.

**Empty `<title>` element:**
```html
<title> </title>
```
A whitespace-only title provides no context to screen readers that expose the document title. Should be `<title>ILIA – Discover top shelf favorites</title>` or similar.

**Alt text on images:** Cannot verify — `<img>` tags not present in truncated source. Require non-empty `alt` on all product images and decorative `alt=""` on spacers.

**Mobile breakpoint:** 480px threshold is correctly applied across all responsive rules. Column stacking logic (`div.kl-row.colstack`) is present and standard.

**MSO/VML conditionals:** Correctly structured for Outlook rendering. `<o:OfficeDocumentSettings>` block with `AllowPNG` and 96 DPI is present.
