# OPI polish for just $8! 💅
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-10**
## Executive summary

- This one actually delivers on its subject line — a rarity for this brand's recent sends. "OPI polish for just $8!" leads straight into "Shop $8 OPI Nail Lacquer. Ends September 13," with eight named OPI shades laid out in a clean grid, each with its own swatch photo and "Shop now" link. No app-download bait-and-switch in the hero this time, unlike 8/29's "Spend $35, Save 15%" or 9/7's "Flawless nails, for less" — both of which buried their own subject-line promise behind an app pitch.
- The weak spot is the top: before you reach the $8 offer, there's a smiling model with red nails and generic "15-minute mani. No salon required" copy with its own "Shop now" CTA — a second, competing offer that isn't the $8 deal and could pull clicks toward a vaguer destination. It's also a wall of scroll (roughly 8 product tiles) before you hit the skincare cross-sell and the now-inevitable app pitch further down, this time at least positioned honestly as a secondary ask rather than the headline.
- Solid, on-brand execution with a real, specific, dated offer up front — a clear step up from the brand's last several sends, though the hero mixed-messaging costs it a point.

## What's working

- Subject line's exact offer ($8 OPI polish) is visible in the hero within the first screen, unlike recent sends that buried their subject's promise.
- Clear expiration date ("Ends September 13") creates real, specific urgency.
- Eight distinct OPI shades shown with individual product photography and names — feels like real inventory, not a generic promo.
- App download pitch is present but demoted to mid-body, not competing with the hero for attention.
- 30% off skincare cross-sell is clearly separated with its own end date, not conflated with the nail offer.

## What's weak

- The hero ("15-minute mani. No salon required.") is a second, vaguer offer competing with the actual $8 deal — two CTAs before the reader even sees the discount.
- No price/value framing on the $8 tiles themselves (no strikethrough "was $X" to show savings) — reader has to infer this is a deal from copy alone.
- Long unbroken product grid (8 tiles) with no visual breaks or secondary CTA until skincare — heavy scroll for mobile.
- "Beauty in a Dash" and "Free 2 Hour Delivery" utility banners at the very top eat prime real estate before any offer appears.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead the hero with the $8 OPI offer itself — merge the "15-minute mani" emotional hook into the same hero as the price and end date, so there's one CTA, not two, before the fold.
- 2. Add a "reg. $X" strikethrough price on at least the first couple of product tiles to make the $8 savings concrete rather than implied.
- 3. Trim the grid to 4-6 shades or break it into two rows with a mid-scroll CTA banner to reduce mobile scroll fatigue.
- 4. Move the utility banners (curbside, delivery) below the hero, not above it — they're delaying the actual subject-line payoff.
