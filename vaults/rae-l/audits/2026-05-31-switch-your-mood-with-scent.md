# Switch your mood with scent ✨
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-31**
## Executive summary

- This is Lush's mood-by-scent editorial — three curated product groupings (Energy Boost, Reclaim Your Calm, Pure Indulgence) anchored by a gradient hero and closed out with a "Build Your Scent Story" video segment featuring a Lush fragrance expert. The architecture is smart: emotional benefit first, products second, which is the right order for fragrance. The editorial logic holds throughout.
- The tension here is that this is Lush's second fragrance-focused editorial send in five days — May 27's "Stop and smell the flowers" already walked through floral scent families. There's overlap in premise even if the angle differs (category guide vs. mood guide). Subscribers paying attention will notice, and that risks making this feel like a content production loop rather than a curated edit.
- Biggest missed opportunity: there's no purchase hook. No discount, no urgency, no loyalty callout. The entire email asks you to "explore" — which is fine for a content nurture send, but paired with aggressive send cadence, it reads as filler rather than strategy.

## What's working

- The three-section mood taxonomy (Energy / Calm / Indulgence) is genuinely useful framing — it teaches subscribers how to shop fragrance, not just what to buy.
- Color-coded sections map to mood register — cooler purples for energy, earthy darks for calm — a visual design choice that reinforces the copy without being heavy-handed.
- "Build Your Scent Story" with a named Lush expert (Joy) and a "Watch Now" video CTA is differentiated content that no other mass-market beauty brand is doing at email scale.
- Free shipping threshold banner is prominent at the top — small nudge but it removes an objection.
- Clean render: no broken images, no layout bleed, no orphaned text visible.

## What's weak

- Second fragrance editorial in five days — the May 27 "Stop and smell the flowers" floral guide already covered scent by category; this one risks subscriber fatigue on the same theme.
- No incentive to buy now — no sale, no time limit, no bundle price. The entire email is browse-only, which wastes the product grid's potential.
- "Explore Fragrances" CTA sends to a category page, not to any of the nine specific products shown — the editorial does the work of building desire and then deposits you at the front door of a 200-product category.
- Hero headline "Let Your Senses Guide You" is evocative but vague — doesn't make a promise a subscriber can act on.
- Send cadence: six emails in eleven days from Lush — this one risks being skipped on volume alone.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a product-specific CTA for each mood section.** Each of the three sections (Energy Boost / Calm / Indulgence) should have its own "Shop [Mood]" button linking to a filtered collection, not a blanket "Explore Fragrances" drop-off.
- 2. **Give the hero a deliverable.** Replace "Let Your Senses Guide You" with something that makes a specific promise, e.g. "Find the scent that fits your mood" or — even better — link to a short scent quiz.
- 3. **Tie the "Watch Now" video CTA to a product outcome.** Right now it just says "Watch Now" — add "then shop Joy's picks" or link the video directly to a curated collection to convert the viewing intent.
- 4. **Rest the fragrance theme for a send or two.** The May 27 and May 31 sends are too adjacent in concept. Route subscribers to bath/body or a seasonal summer angle before returning to fragrance.
- 5. **Surface a limited-time offer**, even if modest: free sample with fragrance orders, or a "Scent Discovery" trial set at a fixed price.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Find your mood in a bottle ✨`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `3 moods. 9 products. One quiz to match you to your scent.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Energy boost or total indulgence — explore your fragrance match.`
