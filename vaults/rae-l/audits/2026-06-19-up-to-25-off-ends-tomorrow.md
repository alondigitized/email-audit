# Up to 25% off ends tomorrow
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- This is the final-day send of the Friends of Glossier Sale — and it opens with the strongest headline this arc has produced since the June 16 UGC email (9/10). "All good things must come to an end" is emotionally honest rather than mechanically promotional, and it earns the urgency that follows. As a closing statement, the creative instinct is right.
- But context is everything. This is at minimum the eleventh email Glossier has sent in nine days about the same 25% off offer. No amount of good headline writing rescues a list that has been trained to scroll past this brand for over a week. The subscribers who were going to buy have already bought. What remains is the fence-sitters — and a multi-product grid of anonymous "Shop now" buttons doesn't convert them.
- The most interesting thing in this email is the subscription upsell at the bottom, and it's completely buried. A subscriber who ignored ten sale emails might still say yes to "lock in 25% off forever" — but Glossier put it where almost nobody scrolls.

## What's working

- **"All good things must come to an end" headline** is a confident, human departure from nine days of percentage-first subject lines.
- **Price overlays on product thumbnails** make the grid tangible — "$27" and "$22" visible on shots add specificity that body copy alone doesn't.
- **Subscription module** at the bottom is a smart evergreen hedge: it extends the relationship beyond a single sale event for subscribers not ready to buy today.
- **Visual consistency** holds — no render breaks, Glossier's clean aesthetic is intact throughout.
- **Subject line is mobile-clean** at 27 characters; "ends tomorrow" is the right urgency signal for a final-day send.

## What's weak

- **Five identical "Shop now" CTAs** across the product grid with no product names legible at thumbnail size — a subscriber can't decide what to click when every option looks the same.
- **Hero body copy restates the same formula** (up to 25% off makeup, 20% off skin) that Glossier has been sending for nine days; the headline earned freshness that the body copy doesn't sustain.
- **Subscription module is buried** below the entire product grid — the one genuinely different call to action in this email is placed where most readers never reach.
- **No hard end-time** — "ends tomorrow" is softer than "tonight at midnight PT"; on a final day, a specific cutoff converts better than a relative date.
- **Fatigue from cadence** is the unignorable shadow — eleven emails in nine days means the "ends tomorrow" line reads like the fourth-quarter sale tactics that erode brand trust over time.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the product grid to a single hero SKU.** Pick the best-seller, show its name, its full price, its sale price, and one CTA. Undecided subscribers need a recommendation, not a browse.
- 2. **Move the subscription module to second position**, immediately after the hero. The subscribers who've ignored ten sale emails are exactly the audience for "never pay full price again" — put that offer where they'll see it.
- 3. **Add a hard close time** to both the hero copy and the subject: "tonight at 11:59 PM ET" is a more actionable deadline than "tomorrow."
- 4. **Match body copy tone to the headline** — "All good things must come to an end, so here's one last thing worth trying" hands off cleanly to the subscription pitch without restating the same sale breakdown one more time.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last call: 25% off ends tonight at midnight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your last shot at the Friends sale — or save 25% forever`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sale closes tonight. Or lock in member pricing for good.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `One last day — then it's gone until next year.`
