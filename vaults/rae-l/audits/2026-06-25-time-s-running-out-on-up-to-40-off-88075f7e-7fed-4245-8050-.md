# Time's running out on up to 40% off ⏰
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-25**
## Executive summary

- This is effectively the same email as the June 23 "Shop deals up to 40% off" send, re-dated two days later with a clock emoji and "Time's running out" swapped onto the headline. The hero does its job — "Up to 40% Off Online & In Store" with the free 2-hour delivery callout reads cleanly above the fold, and the product shot is large and uncluttered. That's where the discipline ends.
- Below the fold, the email collapses into the same multi-module kitchen-sink layout that's now appeared in at least four of Sally Beauty's last eight sends. Styling tools, fragrances, BOGO offers, an "Online Only" subsection — each competing with the others, none winning. The best individual offer visible in this email (the $59.99 off Infrared Styler in the Online Only row) is buried so far down and rendered so small it will get zero clicks from anyone who doesn't scroll deliberately.
- The urgency is the biggest miss here. "Time's running out" with a clock emoji is a claim that demands a deadline to back it up. There's no visible date, no end time, no "tonight" or "Sunday midnight." After eight promo emails in fifteen days, subscribers are conditioned to know the next deal is already drafted. The urgency reads as theater.

## What's working

- Hero is clean and fast to parse — "Up to 40% Off" is unambiguous, the product visual is strong, and "free 2-hour delivery" adds a credible secondary hook
- "Big Deals rewards & savings" section ties discounts to the loyalty program, giving members a behavioral reason to act vs. waiting
- Multiple deal tiers (40% off, BOGO 50%) give different intent-levels a route in
- The $59.99 dollar-amount anchor on the Infrared Styler is the most compelling specific offer in the email — dollar-off beats percent-off for perceived value

## What's weak

- "Time's running out" urgency is hollow without a visible deadline — no date, no end time, just emoji and vibes
- This send is nearly identical to the June 23 email in offer framing and headline copy; any subscriber who opened both will feel déjà vu before they scroll
- The Online Only section is typographically too small to read without zooming — deals worth clicking are invisible at normal reading distance
- Ten-plus modules shatter click intent; nothing feels like the one thing this email wants you to do

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor the hero on the dollar-off deal, not the percent-off range.** "$59.99 off the Infrared Hair Styler — today only" is a specific, high-value hook. "Up to 40% off" could mean 10% on most of the site. Lead with the best specific deal.
- 2. **Give the urgency a real deadline.** "Sale ends Sunday at 11:59pm" converts; "Time's running out" doesn't. This is a one-field copy change with measurable lift.
- 3. **Trim to three modules max.** Keep styling tools, BOGO, and the hero product. Cut fragrances and the cluttered Online Only grid, or make them a separate send to a fragrance-segment.
- 4. **Break the every-two-days cadence.** The June 23 and June 25 sends are the same email. Send the second one only to non-openers of the first.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Ends tonight: $59.99 off the Infrared Styler`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your 40%-off window closes Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus BOGO deals on tools and free 2-hour delivery.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Styling tools, fragrances, and more — this weekend only.`

**`Founders Grotesk` referenced but never loaded**

There is no `@font-face` declaration for `Founders Grotesk` in the provided `<head>`. It will silently fall back to Helvetica everywhere. Verify whether this is intentional or a missing embed.

**`img { white-space: pre; }` — unusual rule**
`white-space` is a non-inherited, non-applicable property on replaced elements (`<img>`). It has no visual effect but adds noise and could interfere with some rendering engines' style resolution. No standard email template guidance recommends this.

Google Fonts is currently at v40+. The versioned URL still resolves (Google maintains permanent URLs), but it loads an outdated subset. Low-severity unless specific glyph coverage is needed.
