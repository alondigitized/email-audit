# Friend, these gifts have your name on them
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-17**
## Executive summary

- This is a second helping of the 10-16 "The (2) gifts of VIP" send. The offer is the same: a free mini mascara and a velvet makeup bag. The "Double Take" hero and the red "2 FREE GIFTS" badge keep the gift message at the top. The red announcement bar and the red "JOIN FREE + SHOP →" button make the primary action unambiguous.
- The body then dilutes the message. After the gift pitch there are product tiles, a "More The Merrier" 15% off sets block, a "perennial beauty" editorial, an "In safe hands" clean-beauty banner and a second "SHOP ALL" CTA. A reader gets the gift offer, a sets discount and a brand-values story in one scroll. The thresholds also disagree: the announcement bar and body copy say $100+, while the mini mascara tile says "any $75+ order." That undercuts the "honest offer" read.
- Overall it's competent and on-brand, but it's a near-repeat of yesterday's send with a "Friend," subject that adds little. Expect a middling result, around 6.

## What's working

- The "Double Take" hero, with its "2 FREE GIFTS" badge, ties the pair of gifts to the creative.
- The red announcement bar and the full-width "JOIN FREE + SHOP →" button are easy to spot and easy to read.
- The product tiles for the mini mascara and velvet bag carry "FREE GIFT" and "A VIP EXCLUSIVE" tags, so the gifts are easy to identify.
- The layout is clean, with consistent tile spacing and no visible broken images.
- The footer trust badges (free shipping over $50, Pact, Leaping Bunny) add credibility.

## What's weak

- The thresholds conflict: the bag requires $100+, while the mascara tile says "$75+ order" and the hero says $100+. That reads as confusing fine print.
- The email carries three competing messages: free gifts, 15% off sets, and the editorial and clean-beauty banners. Hardly any of it supports the gift hook.
- The "shop now" buttons under the products are low-contrast outlined boxes, much weaker than the red CTAs.
- It repeats the 10-16 gift email almost exactly, so the cadence feels stale.
- There's no price, shade, or review detail on the product tiles, and there is no social proof anywhere.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email to a single message. Keep the gift hero, the two gifts and one CTA, and move the sets and editorial content to another send.
- 2. Reconcile the thresholds into a single clearly stated spend, such as "$100+ gets both gifts," and fix the mascara tile.
- 3. Put the free-gift tiles directly under the hero, and drop the "Eyeing These?" tiles that don't carry a gift.
- 4. Make the product CTAs solid-filled buttons with specific copy, such as "Add mascara + bag."
- 5. Add a credible deadline, such as "Gifts end Sunday," and a review count or star rating near the mascara.
