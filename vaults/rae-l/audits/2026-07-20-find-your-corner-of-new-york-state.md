# Find your corner of New York State
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- This is a flavor-catalog send — four Balm Dotcom SKUs (Black Cherry, Strawberry, Mango, I ❤️ NY) each get a dedicated visual tile, loosely mapped to different corners of New York State. The premise is clever in a creative brief but it arrives as the **ninth send in the I ❤️ NY arc in roughly 13 days**, and the tile-per-flavor format nearly repeats the July 17 "your next summer flavor" send. The creative team is executing cleanly; the email strategy is running the arc two sends too long without escalating the offer.
- The subject line is the biggest single problem. "Find your corner of New York State" is evocative geography that tells you nothing about what you're buying or why you'd open it now. By send #9, subscribers need more than NY poetry — they need a reason. The free Big Apple Sticker Pack buried at the bottom is the most compelling hook in the email and it never makes it anywhere near the subject line.
- Judgment: solid brand execution, flat inbox positioning, fatiguing cadence. Worth sending; not worth sending *this way*.

## What's working

- Glossier's visual identity holds throughout — red logotype, clean white tiles, product-forward close-ups, fully on-brand.
- Flavor-to-region editorial conceit is a real idea that could differentiate this send from prior catalog formats.
- The free sticker pack ("Claim yours") is a genuine value-add that gives subscribers a reason to engage beyond browsing.
- The four-tile layout gives multiple entry points — different flavors will click for different people.

## What's weak

- "Find your corner of New York State" names no product, no offer, and creates no urgency — it's the weakest subject in the entire arc.
- "Did you know?" opener delays the products; subscribers have to scroll before seeing anything to buy.
- Campaign cadence has crossed into fatigue territory — nine NY-themed sends since July 7 with no visible end or escalation.
- No price, no discount, no deadline anywhere in the email — nothing converts a browser into a buyer.
- The sticker pack (the strongest concrete offer) is the last section before the footer, not the first thing subscribers see.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the sticker pack into the subject line** — "free" is the word most likely to earn an open from a subscriber who's seen eight prior NY sends. This is the single highest-leverage change.
- 2. **Cut "Did you know?"** and open directly with the flavor grid. Subscribers know Glossier; they don't need an educational intro before they see the product.
- 3. **Give each flavor a single geographic label in large type** — if "Black Cherry = Hudson Valley," make that the tile headline, not body copy. The conceit needs to scan at a thumb-scroll pace.
- 4. **Signal the arc's end.** Something like "last call for the full I ❤️ NY collection" — even if the products stay up — would reduce scroll-past behavior from fatigued subscribers.
- 5. **Surface a price or bundle.** Showing a "grab all 4 for $X" bundle would make this feel like a sendable reason rather than a browse invitation.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Black Cherry, Strawberry or Mango — which NY are you?`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Each flavor maps to a different corner of New York. Find yours.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Grab a free sticker pack while supplies last.`
