# The wait is almost over…
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-13**
## Executive summary

- This is Glossier's reveal email for the "I ❤ NY Balm Dotcom" — an apple-flavored, NYC-themed Balm Dotcom launching tomorrow (July 14). The creative is clean and distinctly Glossier: wordmark, editorial NYC photography, a tight product shot against apples, and punchy copy ("Take a bite out of the Big Apple"). The narrative arc is working — the July 7 "Something new is coming" teaser withheld the name, and this email delivers the payoff. The sequence is textbook launch cadence done right.
- The single weak point is the subject line. "The wait is almost over…" is the laziest version of a reveal subject — it tells you nothing and leans entirely on existing subscriber curiosity. After a week-long tease, subscribers who are already engaged didn't need the mystery extended; they needed the product name in the subject so they could feel the reward of the reveal landing in their inbox. The body does everything right. The subject undersells it.
- The CTA is also slightly misdirected — "Sign up" to be notified when the product drops puts friction between an already-warmed subscriber and actual conversion. If it launches tomorrow, a countdown or a clear "Shop tomorrow" moment would be more satisfying than asking people to opt into another notification.

## What's working

- **NYC editorial photography + product shot pairing** is cohesive and visually earned — the street scene and the apple/tube juxtaposition communicate concept without needing copy to explain it.
- **"COMING TOMORROW" label** above the hero sets immediate, credible urgency.
- **"Take a bite out of the Big Apple"** is genuinely good copy — specific, playful, on-brand, and product-descriptive all at once.
- **Narrative continuity** from the July 7 teaser is satisfied — this email delivers the product name + flavor, which is what a subscriber who opened the teaser was waiting for.
- **Clean visual hierarchy** — logo → hero headline → product shot → CTA flows without clutter.

## What's weak

- **Subject "The wait is almost over…" reveals nothing** — product name, flavor, even "Balm Dotcom" are all withheld from the inbox view, which is a miss when the body has a genuinely exciting reveal.
- **"Sign up" CTA is friction for a warm audience** — a subscriber who opened the teaser a week ago doesn't need another notification gate; they want to know what time it drops or a direct pre-launch link.
- **No price anchor visible** — even a "starting at $XX" or "same price as classic Balm Dotcom" would reduce pre-launch hesitation.
- **Footer emoji cluster (smiley face + waving hand)** reads oddly small and disconnected from the premium editorial tone of the rest of the email.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the product name in the subject.** This is the single highest-leverage change. The reveal is the whole point of this email — burying it in the body while the subject says "the wait is almost over" leaves conversion on the table for everyone who didn't open.
- 2. **Replace "Sign up" with a time-specific CTA.** Something like "Shop July 14 at 9AM ET" or even just "Remind me" with a concrete time. The vagueness of "sign up" after a reveal email feels like a let-down.
- 3. **Add a price line.** One line: "Balm Dotcom, $XX. Ships free." Removes the last pre-launch objection.
- 4. **Cut the emoji footer row** or integrate it more intentionally — it currently looks like stray assets that didn't get cleaned up.

## Technical Audit — Glossier "The wait is almost over…"

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is built on Klaviyo (sending domain `eml.glossier.com`, click tracking via `ctrk.klclick.com`) using what appears to be a Stripo-compatible template with full Outlook MSO conditional block coverage. The HTML is structurally sound but contains one non-standard open-tracking implementation and several rendering concerns worth flagging.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Non-standard open-tracking implementation.**
The open pixel is injected as:
```html
<span rel="stylesheet" href="https://ctrk.klclick.com/l/01KXERY89H2C3W2NKBMNFWXGJF_0"></span>
```
`rel="stylesheet"` is not a valid attribute on `<span>` — browsers and email clients ignore it. This request will not fire in most email clients; it appears to be an attempt to load a "stylesheet" URL to trigger a server hit, but `<span>` elements do not fetch `href`. If this is intended as the open-tracking mechanism, it will under-report opens. Standard implementation uses an `<img>` with `height="1" width="1"` and `alt=""`. Klaviyo's own pixel SDK typically injects a `<img>` tag — this `<span>` construction is anomalous and likely a template error.

**Cannot audit destination URLs** — the HTML is truncated before body CTAs are visible. Verify all `ctrk.klclick.com` redirects resolve to live pages and carry UTM parameters.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Hover-based rollover images will fail silently in most clients.**
The template includes:
```css
.rollover:hover .rollover-first { max-height:0 !important; display:none !important }
.rollover:hover .rollover-second { max-height:none !important; display:inline-block !important }
```
CSS `:hover` pseudo-class is supported only in Apple Mail, Outlook.com (webmail), and a handful of others. In Gmail (Android/iOS), Outlook desktop, and Samsung Mail, only the `.rollover-first` image will ever display. If the rollover-second image carries the primary CTA or product shot, those recipients never see it. Confirm the first-state image is always the complete/meaningful content.

**Preheader padding technique is correct** — uses U+034F (combining grapheme joiner `͏`) and U+00AD (soft hyphen `­`) to prevent inbox clients from pulling body text into the preview. No issue, but worth noting for audit trail.

**Alt text on images** — cannot confirm from truncated source. Must verify all `<img>` tags carry descriptive `alt=""` attributes; images-off rendering in Outlook is common.

**`lang="en"` and `dir="ltr"`** are correctly set on the `<html>` element.

**MSO conditionals** — present and complete (`mso 16`, `gte mso 9`, `[if mso]` for VML background, Word typography suppression). Outlook rendering baseline is covered.
