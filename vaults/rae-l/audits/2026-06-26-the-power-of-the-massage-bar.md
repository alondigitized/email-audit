# The power of the massage bar
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-26**
## Executive summary

- This is a full-catalog editorial send for Lush's massage bar range — product showcase meets ingredient storytelling. The hero lands the "KNOT A WANT BUT A KNEAD" pun cleanly, and the email walks you through the range (six individual bars shown with names and brief copy), then detours into a cocoa butter origin story before cross-selling the Sanrio collab that first appeared on June 18. It's thorough and well-designed, but it's trying to do four things at once: introduce the range, educate on ingredients, catalog products, and cross-sell a collab. That breadth works against focus.
- The email follows Lush's consistent playbook — pun headline, editorial product grid, rich color sections — but this one lacks the sharp hook that made the June 25 shower gummies send land at 9. There's no offer, no limited availability, no urgency of any kind. This reads like a "browse the range" send, not a "buy today" send. That's a valid content strategy, but it should be scored honestly: the email will inform, not convert.
- The Sanrio cross-sell in the footer feels recycled — it's been two full sends since the collab announcement and this is the third appearance. Subscribers who want it have already clicked.

## What's working

- **Hero pun executes perfectly.** "KNOT A WANT BUT A KNEAD" is classic Lush — the visual of hands kneading a bar reinforces it immediately; no explanation needed.
- **Product grid is scannable.** Six named products with distinct packaging photography give you a quick visual menu of the range — easy to compare.
- **Cocoa butter section breaks up the scroll.** The warm gold/amber "COCOA BUTTER FROM BEAN TO BAR" block adds a brand credibility beat without feeling like a hard sell.
- **Visual hierarchy holds throughout.** Each section has a clear focal point and the eye flows top to bottom without confusion or dead zones.
- **No render bugs visible.** Images load, text doesn't overlap, layouts are clean across the full scroll.

## What's weak

- **No offer, no reason to act today.** Zero percent-off, no free shipping, no bundle deal — the "Shop Now" CTA has nothing behind it except general brand goodwill.
- **Subject is flat.** "The power of the massage bar" is descriptive but generates zero curiosity or urgency; it reads like a category page title.
- **Sanrio cross-sell is stale.** This is at minimum the third Sanrio mention — subscribers who are interested have already been prompted twice. It dilutes the massage bar focus.
- **"Learn More" CTA for the cocoa butter section is low-intent.** If the goal is to sell the bars, that section should end in a product link, not a content link.
- **No social proof anywhere.** No reviews, no star ratings, no user-generated imagery — for a tactile, sensory product like a massage bar, this is a real miss.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hook or reason to act.** Even "Buy 2, get a travel size free" would turn this from a browse email into a buy email. This is the highest-leverage single change.
- 2. **Rewrite the subject to create curiosity or urgency.** The current subject is a statement, not a pull.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your muscles called. They want this.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Knot what you think — meet the massage bar`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `6 bars. One ritual. Find your match.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Cocoa butter, shea, and the best hour of your week.`
- 3. **Cut the Sanrio footer.** It's diluting the massage bar story and fatiguing the collab. If Sanrio inventory needs moving, give it its own dedicated send.
- 4. **Add at least one star rating or short review quote** under the hero product — "4.8 stars, 2,400 reviews" would do more than the current blank white space below the product names.
- 5. **Replace "Learn More" in the cocoa butter section with a product link.** Route directly to the bars that contain it, not to a content page.
