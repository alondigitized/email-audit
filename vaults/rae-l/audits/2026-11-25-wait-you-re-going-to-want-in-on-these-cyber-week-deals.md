# Wait, you’re going to want in on these Cyber Week deals 😅
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-25**
## Executive summary

- This is the fourth Cyber Week send in five days, after 11/21, 11/22, 11/23 and 11/24 ("Cyber Week is really bringing it ON 🤩"). It repeats the same purple sequin template with huge "30% OFF" numerals. Four brand blocks stack down the page: Eilish, Lancôme, a "Living proof." block and Viktor&Rolf. The numerals are easy to read and the SHOP NOW buttons are clear, so the offer lands fast.
- The email is overloaded. A free sample bag promo with code GRABMYBAG sits at the very top, followed by a 4X points banner, the 30% hero stack and a product grid. Below that come a fragrance block, a "Big Savings Are Here!" block, category buttons and a barcode. That is at least six competing offers. The top of the email has no single hero, and the sample bag gets first position even though the subject promises "deals."
- The subject is vague. "Wait, you're going to want in on these" names no brand, discount or category. A subscriber who has seen four near-identical sends this week has little reason to treat this one as new. The creative is competent but fatigued. I'd call it solid on execution and weak on freshness and focus.

## What's working

- The "30% OFF" numerals are huge, on purple sequins. The offer is unmistakable and the eye lands on it.
- Each brand block has its own SHOP NOW button, with the brand logo beside it (Eilish, Lancôme, Viktor&Rolf), so the click target is clear.
- The "24 hours-only" language in the Cyber Week Deal header gives credible urgency.
- The product grid carries price, brand and "Only at Sephora" tags, which gives concrete detail to click on.
- Footer extras (free shipping, Credit Card offer, in-store barcode) cover several shopper intents.

## What's weak

- The top stacks two unrelated offers: the free sample bag with promo code, then 4X points. Both come before the Cyber Week deal the subject promised.
- Four near-identical 30% blocks run on for most of the scroll, with little differentiation or hierarchy.
- The "Living proof." block shows the brand name only as small text, so it reads as less branded than the logo-led blocks around it.
- The tail is crowded: the product grid, the fragrance and 4X block, the "Big Savings" block and the category buttons. Fine print and the barcode add more length.
- The length (about 7,300 px) buries any single message and repeats the 11/24 structure almost unchanged.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one hero. Lead with the 24-hour 30% deal and drop the sample bag and 4X banners from the top, or move them to a slim strip.
- 2. Cut the 30% blocks from four to two, and put the strongest brand first.
- 3. Make the subject concrete. Name the brand or the discount, and keep the emoji out of it if cadence is the problem.
- 4. Vary the creative across sends. A fourth identical sequin template trains subscribers to skip it.
- 5. Trim the tail to one grid and one CTA row.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible HTML source is a table-based, 700px fixed-width layout. It has two confirmed code-level defects: a missing plain-text part and a fixed 700px layout width. The truncated source and relay-stripped headers mean most compliance and link checks can't be confirmed either way.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Not assessable.** The source is cut off before any `<a href>` appears. The QA run probed 12 links and skipped 41. It reported no per-link status codes, redirect chains or failures, so I'm not flagging any broken links.
- **Open-tracking pixels (informational):**
  - `https://mi.sephora.com/p/up/f6716d0a0f52104e/o.gif?mi_u=4958433775694848&mi_ecmp=20261125_Cyber_Week_Daily_Brand_Deals_Day_3` is a 1x1 pixel with a per-recipient `mi_u` ID.
  - A second hidden pixel loads from `https://app.sephora.com/O/AQEAAZcF2g…` (`display:none; mso-hide: all`).
  - Neither is a defect. Both are standard ESP tracking.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Fixed width, no mobile fallback.** The wrapper is `<table width="700" style="min-width:700px; width:700px">`. `min-width:700px` overrides any narrower viewport, so mobile clients that don't scale down will scroll horizontally. The `@media (max-width:480px)` block only resizes individual classes (`.mobile`, `.mobileHeaderLogo`, etc.). It doesn't collapse the outer table.
- **Malformed CSS in the media query.** `<!--[if mso]> img.mso-size {…}<![endif]-->` sits inside the `@media` block in a `<style>` tag. A browser CSS parser would likely treat `<!--[if mso]> img.mso-size` and `<![endif]--> .mobileHeaderTierRouge` as invalid selectors and drop those rules. That would break the mobile sizing for `.mso-size` and `.mobileHeaderTierRouge`. This is a parse-level inference, not a confirmed render failure.
- **Zoom disabled.** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Invalid robots value.** `<meta name="robots" content="no index">` is not valid. The correct token is `noindex`. This is harmless in email.
- **Generic `<title>`.** It reads `Sephora`.
- **Alt-text findings are overstated.**
  - The QA tool flagged `o.gif`, but the source shows `alt="" aria-hidden="true"`. That is correct for a decorative pixel.
  - The `app.sephora.com` pixel has no `alt` attribute, but it's `display:none`, so there's no practical accessibility impact.
  - Alt text on the visible content images can't be checked in the truncated source.
- **Plain-text fallback missing.** QA reports `[FAIL] Text version is 0 chars`. This is the only hard FAIL. It could be an AgentMail capture artifact, so I'd verify it against the raw MIME before treating it as a sender defect. If it is real, it hurts deliverability and text-only clients.
- **Structured data is malformed.** The `application/ld+json` block declares `"discountCode": ""` (empty) and a `PromotionCard` with no properties. Gmail ignores promotion markup for senders it hasn't approved, so this has no practical effect.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **Not confirmed as defects:**
  - `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) are not found.
  - `Authentication-Results` is not found, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown.
  - QA itself says these headers may not be captured by the AgentMail relay. They need checking against raw headers before they count as sender violations.
- **Footer content unknown.** The source is truncated before the footer, so the physical address and the in-body unsubscribe link couldn't be inspected. QA did say an unsubscribe link was included in the probed set, but it gave no result for it.
- **From and subject.** The From address `shop@beauty.sephora.com` is on a sending subdomain. Nothing is wrong with the subject header.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not assessable from the data provided. No destination URLs are visible in the truncated source, and QA reported no landing-page results. The only campaign identifier visible is `mi_ecmp=20261125_Cyber_Week_Daily_Brand_Deals_Day_3` in the tracking pixel. I can't say whether click URLs carry UTMs or matching campaign tags.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Confirm the plain-text FAIL** against the raw MIME. If the text part is really absent, add a `text/plain` alternative.
2. **Fix the layout width.** Replace `min-width:700px; width:700px` with `width:100%; max-width:700px`, and add a fluid or hybrid fallback for clients that ignore media queries.
3. **Move the `<!--[if mso]>` block** out of the `@media` rule, or remove it, so the mobile rules that follow it parse correctly.
4. **Drop `maximum-scale=1`** from the viewport meta tag.
5. **Fix the minor markup:** `noindex` instead of `no index`, a descriptive `<title>`, and remove or complete the empty JSON-LD `DiscountOffer`.
6. **Re-run QA on untruncated HTML and raw headers** to check the footer, UTM parameters, `List-Unsubscribe`, and SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment.
