# This style has limited availability!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- This is Skechers recycling the exact same subject line — "This style has limited availability!" — that landed on June 30, just 16 days ago. That June 30 send already scored a 6/10 here and called the creative out for urgency fatigue; nothing has changed. The hero product is the BOBS Misty Kiss Summer Stroll, the same sandal from the July 15 "This is perfect for you!" browse-abandon send yesterday. So the subscriber receiving this has now seen the same product telegraphed twice in 24 hours, under the same urgency claim they already chose to ignore once this month.
- The render itself is legitimately clean: strong visual hierarchy, a real promo code (SUMMER, 20% off for Plus members), no broken images, no overlapping text. "HOW ABOUT A SECOND LOOK? SEEN THIS BEFORE? WE THOUGHT SO." is actually a slightly more self-aware headline than the usual "STILL THINKING ABOUT IT?" — it acknowledges the repetition with a wink, which is the right creative instinct. But the subject line completely undermines it before the subscriber ever opens.
- The email would be a competent 8/10 if it were a first contact. As the third Skechers send in 48 hours on the same product, under a subject Skechers already fired 16 days ago, it reads like a broken automation rule.

## What's working

- **"Second Look" headline is a genuine improvement** over the tired "STILL THINKING ABOUT IT?" — the self-aware framing acknowledges the abandon loop without being heavy-handed.
- **Real offer, real code**: 20% off for Plus members with "SHOP NOW CODE: SUMMER" is specific and actionable, not a vague "save more" tease.
- **Hero product is unambiguous**: BOBS Misty Kiss Summer Stroll is front and center with a clean product image and an immediately visible blue CTA.
- **No render bugs**: Layout is clean, all images load, no overlapping or truncated elements anywhere.

## What's weak

- **Exact subject line repeat from June 30**: "This style has limited availability!" is word-for-word identical to the June 30 send audited here — 16 days, zero creative refresh.
- **The urgency claim has no proof inside the email**: The subject says "limited availability" but the email body shows no stock count, no size warning, no low-inventory badge. The claim hangs unsupported.
- **Preheader appears to be leaking junk**: The top of the render shows what looks like an internal product code or tracking string ("505.203.00" or similar) rather than intentional preview copy — that's what inbox clients surface next to the subject.
- **20% off is Plus-gated with no non-member path**: A subscriber who isn't enrolled in Skechers Plus hits the offer block and has nowhere to go — no "join free" micro-CTA, no fallback incentive.
- **Three sends in 48 hours**: July 15 ("This is perfect for you!"), July 15 ("Did you forget something?"), July 16 (this one). The cadence has crossed from triggered automation into saturation.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Retire this subject line** — it's been used, it was already flagged, it landed again without change. Write a subject that names the product or a size-specific claim instead.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your BOBS Misty Kiss is almost gone`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Running low on your size — Summer Stroll sandal`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus members save 20% with code SUMMER — grab it before it's gone`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Still on the fence? 20% off for Plus members ends soon`
- 2. **Substantiate the urgency in the email body**: If availability is genuinely limited, show it — an "Only 3 left in your size" badge on the hero, or a "Selling fast" module. Otherwise drop the urgency claim entirely; an unsubstantiated scarcity signal burns trust faster than no signal at all.
- 3. **Add a non-member path to the offer block**: The 20% off section should include a "Not a Plus member? Join free →" one-liner directly under the promo code, or surface a non-member incentive (free shipping, first-order discount) alongside the Plus benefit.
- 4. **Throttle the trigger**: Enforce a minimum 3–4 day suppression window between abandon sends for the same subscriber on the same product. Two triggers in 24 hours is the automation equivalent of a doorbell rung twice per minute.
- 5. **Fix the preheader**: Whatever is appearing at the top of the email body before the logo — product code, tracking param, or an empty tag — needs to be replaced with intentional preview copy before the next send.
