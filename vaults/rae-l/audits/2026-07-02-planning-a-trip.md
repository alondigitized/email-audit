# 🏖️ Planning a trip?
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-02**
## Executive summary

- This is Lush's summer travel editorial — "Travel Light, Smell Great" — a category-organized guide mapping their product range to different travel scenarios: carry-on cosmetics, foot care for walking days, backpack basics, wheelchair/accessibility travel, and festival freshness. The concept is smart and seasonally right for early July. The five-section framework gives the email genuine editorial utility, not just a product dump.
- The execution, however, is cramped and dark on mobile. Product names and descriptors are rendered in small, low-contrast italic text, making them nearly illegible at this resolution. The hero image is evocative but crops tight enough that the person and product blur together. Critically, the email closes with what appears to be a recycled Lush × Sanrio Characters module — the same collab block audited on June 30 — which undercuts the travel theme and feels tacked on rather than integrated.
- Overall: a well-organized concept with a clear seasonal occasion, hobbled by type legibility issues and a mismatched tail section.

## What's working

- **Travel-occasion sectioning is genuinely useful.** Carry-on, foot care, backpack basics, wheelchair, festival — each bucket is distinct and the headers ("Carry-On Cosmetics," "Best Foot Forward," etc.) communicate clearly at a glance.
- **"TRAVEL LIGHT, SMELL GREAT" hero copy is punchy and on-brand.** Gets the concept across in under five words.
- **Seasonal timing is perfect.** July 2nd, peak pre-travel planning week — this send has real relevance right now.
- **Section headers use large display type** that survives mobile rendering and creates a readable skim path even without reading body copy.
- **Accessibility/wheelchair section ("Wheelie Fresh")** is a differentiated inclusion that signals brand values beyond the obvious travel tropes.

## What's weak

- **Product names and descriptions are tiny and low-contrast.** At mobile rendering size, the italic body text beneath each product image is nearly unreadable — "Montalbano," "Sea Bomb," "1 For Toes," "Ultrablanc" all get lost.
- **The Sanrio collab module at the bottom is off-theme.** A "Lush × Sanrio Characters" promotional block appended to a travel email is jarring — it reads like a footer newsletter block stitched onto the wrong send.
- **No concrete offer or discount.** Every send reviewed recently has been editorial/price-free, and this is another one — there is no percentage off, no bundle deal, no free-shipping threshold. The CTAs are purely navigational.
- **Hero image is murky on mobile.** The person with their arms up is rendered small enough that detail is lost — the product being featured isn't identifiable.
- **CTA copy is generic.** "Shop Travel Essentials" and section CTAs appear to use similar phrasing throughout, rather than leading with specific product names or offers.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Drop or fully integrate the Sanrio tail module.** A Lush × Sanrio Characters block in a travel email confuses the send's identity. Either cut it, or reframe it as "festival packing essentials" with Sanrio products mapped to the festival section.
- 2. **Increase body type size and contrast on product callouts.** "Montalbano," "Sea Bomb," etc. need to be legible at 14px+ minimum — right now they're decorative at best on mobile.
- 3. **Add one concrete offer to anchor the editorial.** A "free travel-size with orders over $X" or "10% off carry-on picks" would give readers a tangible reason to click, not just browse. This brand has run four+ straight sends with zero pricing signals.
- 4. **Write section-specific CTAs.** "Shop Foot Essentials" or "Grab a Sea Bomb" beats a generic button — specificity lifts click rates on editorial-format sends.
- 5. **Sharpen the hero image crop** so the product (not just the person) reads clearly at 375px width.
