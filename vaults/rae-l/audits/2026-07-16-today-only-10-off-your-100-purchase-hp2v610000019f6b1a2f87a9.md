# Today only ‼️ 10% off your $100+ purchase.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- Sephora shifts back to discount mode the day after "Just Your Type" — a jarring gear change that makes the cadence feel like two different marketing teams working the same list. The subject is clear and specific ("Today only ‼️ 10% off your $100+ purchase."), but subscribers who open expecting a discount land on a "Free Hair Sample Set" GWP hero — not the 10% they clicked for. The discount badge is there at the top, but the GWP eats the hero real estate. That's a subject/hero mismatch that quietly erodes trust.
- The body tries to do four things at once: push the 10% discount, sell the GWP, surface new loyalty point rewards, and showcase a product grid too small to engage with on mobile. 10% off a $100 minimum is Sephora's thinnest offer in this arc — a subscriber who passed on stacking 30% during Stacking Weekend two weeks ago isn't rushing back for $10 of value. The "New 300+ Point Rewards Are Here!" buried in section four is the most genuinely differentiated hook, and it's getting zero airtime.
- Execution is clean and on-brand, but the creative is doing too many jobs to land any one with force.

## What's working

- Subject line is unambiguous: specific percentage, specific threshold, specific deadline — no guessing required
- The circular "10% off" badge is visually immediate and legible above the fold
- Beauty Insider loyalty tier display at the bottom reinforces membership value
- "New 300+ Point Rewards Are Here!" is a legitimately new hook that doesn't feel like recycled discount theater
- Clean render — no broken images, no layout gaps visible

## What's weak

- Subject promises 10% off; the hero delivers a GWP — the two don't reinforce each other and the opener can feel misled
- Four competing messages (10% off, free hair sample set, point rewards, product grid) dilute every single one of them
- 10% off $100 delivers $10 of value — after 30% Stacking Weekend, this feels paltry rather than timely
- "Shop To Receive" is an ambiguous CTA — receive what, exactly?
- The ‼️ double exclamation reads spam-adjacent even as emoji; it undercuts Sephora's usual editorial confidence

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one hero and commit.** The subject already set the 10% discount expectation — let that own the hero with "Spend $100, save 10%" CTA copy. Move the GWP to a supporting callout below, not the lead visual.
- 2. **Fix the CTA copy.** "Shop To Receive" confuses two offers. "Get 10% Off Now" or "Shop $100+ to Claim" is unambiguous.
- 3. **Lose the double exclamation.** "Today only: 10% off your $100+ purchase." is stronger — confidence beats urgency theater.
- 4. **Spin the point rewards off into their own send.** It's a genuinely differentiated hook that deserves to lead, not trail as a buried 4th section.
- 5. **Consider raising the floor or sweetening the rate.** "10% off sitewide, no minimum" or "15% off $75+" would feel less deflating after the Stacking Weekend high-water mark.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus a free hair sample set — while supplies last`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Spend $100, save $10 + new 300-point rewards just dropped`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — Pinch-to-zoom disabled (confirmed)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom on iOS Safari. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, AA) violation. Fix: remove `maximum-scale=1` or raise it to `maximum-scale=5`.

**Issue 2 — System text-size-adjust suppressed globally (confirmed)**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  -moz-text-size-adjust: none;
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```
Applied to nearly every element type. Overrides OS-level accessibility font scaling for users who rely on it. Standard practice is `body { -webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%; }` — not `none` across all elements.

**Issue 3 — `<title>` is non-descriptive (confirmed)**
```html
<title>Sephora</title>
```
When recipients open in a browser tab or forward the email, the tab title carries no context. Minor, but best practice is `<title>Sephora | Today only: 10% off $100+</title>`.
