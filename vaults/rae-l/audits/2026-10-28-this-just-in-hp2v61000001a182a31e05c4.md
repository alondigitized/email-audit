# 🌟 THIS JUST IN 🌟
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-28**
## Executive summary

- This is a new-arrivals catalog send wearing a "THIS JUST IN" subject, with a lot of promo stacked on top of it. The strongest part is the product grid: eight "New / Only at Sephora" items with names, sizes and prices ($24 to $198). The Get a Fragrance Mini offer (Carolina Herrera Good Girl Jasmine, with a $40 purchase) is also a real hook. Sephora is a trusted sender, so the email gets credit for the brand and the Insider framing.
- The top of the email is cluttered. It has a Savings Event banner (30% / 10% tiers), a "Coming soon, learn more" block, a fragrance-mini offer, and then a separate "Something new is here" hero. Three messages compete before the eye reaches any product. The subject also promises news but gives no product, category or offer, and it reads as another daily send after the 10/27 "We picked this beauty just for you" and 10/26 "These gifts = 🌶️🌶️🌶️" sends.
- Verdict: solid content, weak packaging. A good catalog email with a throwaway subject and a muddy top third.

## What's working

- The product grid is clear. Each item has a badge ("NEW · ONLY AT SEPHORA"), brand, product name, size and price.
- The Carolina Herrera hero shows a specific fragrance with two price points ($37 travel spray, $172 full size).
- The Fragrance Mini offer with a code is a concrete reason to act.
- The "Spot it. Shop it." and "Holiday Gift Hits" blocks tie the email to the gifting season.
- The Insider, VIB and Rouge tier strip at the bottom reinforces loyalty.

## What's weak

- Three stacked promos (Savings Event, "Coming Soon", Fragrance Mini) bury the "something new" hero.
- The subject, "THIS JUST IN", names no product, category or offer, and the emoji bookends add noise.
- The tiny, dense legal text at the bottom is a wall of fine print. Combined with "exclusions apply" language up top, the offers feel less honest.
- Many different "Shop Now" CTAs go to different places, and none says what you get.
- The hero is a fragrance and the grid is a scattershot of unrelated categories (lashes, nail polish, shampoo, hair mask), so there is no clear theme.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the top to one message. Lead with the fragrance-mini offer, or the Savings Event, and push the rest below the grid.
- 2. Make the subject concrete: name the hero product or the offer.
- 3. Rename the generic CTAs to a verb plus a noun, such as "Shop new fragrance" or "Get my free mini".
- 4. Group the grid by category (fragrance, hair, nails) with one header and one CTA each.
- 5. Shorten the legal footer, or collapse it behind a "See details" link.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus new fragrance, hair and body picks you can't get anywhere else.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML is a table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional email with VML/Outlook support and a mobile media-query block. QA found one hard failure (no plain-text part) and several alt-text gaps. The source I received is truncated, so body links, footer and UTM parameters could not be checked directly.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- QA probed 12 of 66 links, skipping 54. It reported no broken links among the probed set, so the other 54 are unverified.
- The first image missing alt text is `https://app.sephora.com/O/AQEAAZcF2g…`. This looks like an open-tracking pixel served from the sender's own click/open domain, with images on `images.harmony.epsilon.com` (Epsilon's content handler). A missing alt on a 1x1 pixel is low risk, but it should have `alt=""`, `width="1"` and `height="1"`.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text (QA, 4 images):**
  - `2021_Navs_Header_06.jpg`: nav header image, so the navigation is unreadable with images blocked or in a screen reader.
  - `20261028_CS_Just_Arrived_hed_05.jpg`: headline image.
  - `20261028_CS_Just_Arrived_fix_06.jpg`: fixed content image.
  - The tracking pixel above.

  If the headline image carries the message text, the email has no readable message when images are off. The filenames suggest the header images carry meaningful content, so they need descriptive alt text.
- **`<meta name="robots" content="no index" />`:** the standard value is `noindex`, with no space. This is harmless in email but is invalid markup.
- **`<meta name="viewport" … maximum-scale=1 />`:** this blocks pinch-zoom on some clients and is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **`<title>Sephora</title>`:** it is generic and does not match the subject line. This is minor.
- **Leftover template comment:** `<!-- US shell snippet -->` is harmless but shows an unscrubbed template.
- **Mobile CSS:** it relies on attribute selectors like `*[class=hide]` alongside class selectors. This is a legacy pattern that can be stripped by some clients, but nothing here is confirmed broken.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found in the visible source. No unresolved tokens (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`, `[FIRSTNAME]`) appear in the truncated HTML. The body and footer were not available to check.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not verifiable. No destination URLs or UTM parameters appear in the truncated HTML, and QA reported no landing-page results. Tracking appears to go through the `app.sephora.com` redirect domain, so UTMs may be applied server-side and be invisible in the source.

1. **Add a `text/plain` part.** This is the only confirmed hard failure.
2. **Add descriptive `alt` text to the three content images,** and `alt=""` to the tracking pixel (`app.sephora.com/O/…`).

4. **Confirm the `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers** against the raw message at the source, bypassing the relay, before treating them as defects. Do the same for SPF/DKIM/DMARC.
