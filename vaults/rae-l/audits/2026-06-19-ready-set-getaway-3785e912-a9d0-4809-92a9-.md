# Ready, Set, Getaway ☀️
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- "Ready, Set, Getaway" arrives eight days after the BOGO 50% hair send and escalates the deal slightly to BOGO 60%, which should feel like a reward for loyal subscribers — except anyone who bought on June 11 is now looking at a sharper discount they missed. The "getaway checklist" frame is the most coherent editorial hook this brand has used since "Your Summer Hair Survival Kit" on June 3, giving six disparate modules a shared travel rationale. The problem is that a checklist with six items isn't a checklist — it's a catalog, and the metaphor collapses under its own weight.
- The hero executes cleanly: blonde model, yellow palette, hard BOGO 60% offer, a deadline baked right into the CTA copy. That part earns its keep. But then comes a gel nail deal with a different mechanic, a nail travel must-haves grid, a GiGi wax bundle, a vivid hair color BOGO with a *different* deadline (June 23 vs. June 20 for hair care), and finally an app clippers/trimmers module that belongs in a different email entirely. By module four the checklist metaphor is memory.
- The kitchen-sink structure is a recurring Sally Beauty pattern — "More Beauty, Less Spend" on June 15 ran seven modules and scored 8/10 for execution quality. This one is tighter at the top but loses the thread faster. It's a 7 email trying to be an 8.

## What's working

- **Hero offer is specific and deadline-anchored.** BOGO 60% off hair care with "Ends June 20" is concrete, and putting the deadline directly in the CTA copy ("BUY NOW BEFORE JUNE 20") is smart execution — no digging required.
- **Getaway checklist framing earns a few modules.** Hair care + nail + wax waxing kit maps logically onto a travel beauty bag; the concept works for the first three modules.
- **BOGO escalation from June 11 creates perceived urgency.** Going from 50% to 60% off signals a better deal, and the shorter deadline (June 20 vs. open-ended) sharpens it.
- **Sally Rewards callout is well-placed.** Positioned between the hair hero and the nail section — it intercepts consideration without interrupting purchase flow.
- **Yellow CTA button reads clearly.** High contrast, legible copy, no visual competition from surrounding elements in the hero section.

## What's weak

- **Six modules destroy the checklist metaphor.** The clippers/trimmers app module at the bottom is the most damaging — it's masculine-skewing in a feminine beauty travel email and signals the template got filled rather than curated.
- **Two competing deadlines create cognitive friction.** Hair care ends June 20, hair color ends June 23 — a subscriber who reads linearly has to track two expiry dates for what presents as a single campaign.
- **June 11 BOGO 50% buyers feel like chumps.** Escalating to 60% eight days later in the same category with no loyalty acknowledgment actively penalizes engaged subscribers.
- **No preheader / leaking header text.** The email appears to open directly into the nav bar — inbox clients will likely pull navigation link text as preview copy, wasting a prime persuasion slot.
- **Nail must-haves grid is ungrouped filler.** The "more nail must-haves to pop in your travel tote" section is four products with no offer mechanic attached — it reads as a missed upsell, not a checklist item.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut to three modules max and build the checklist out properly.** Hero hair BOGO + gel nail deal + one bonus item (wax kit or hair color). Drop the unanchored grid and the clippers module entirely. A real checklist has ≤5 items; email attention spans support ≤3 offers.
- 2. **Consolidate to one deadline.** Align hair care and hair color BOGO end dates. If you must stagger, don't surface both deadlines in the same send.
- 3. **Add a loyalty acknowledgment for recent buyers.** "Missed the June 11 deal? This one's better" — even implicit framing like "our biggest summer BOGO yet" defuses buyer regret.
- 4. **Write a real preheader.** Currently no visible preheader — inbox clients will leak nav text. One line that extends the subject line's promise is enough.
- 5. **Subject should surface the deal escalation.** "Ready, Set, Getaway" is playful but gives nothing to a skimmer. Name the offer.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Getaway Ready: BOGO 60% Off Hair Care Ends June 20`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Summer Beauty Checklist — 60% Off Second Item`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Buy one, get one 60% off select hair care + buy 2 get 1 free gel polish. Ends June 20.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your travel tote, sorted: BOGO 60% hair care, free gel polish with 2, and more. Ends soon.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue: `Founders Grotesk` declared with no `@font-face` rule**
```css
a { font-family: 'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }
```
Six `@font-face` blocks load Open Sans only. `Founders Grotesk` is never declared, so all anchor text silently falls back to Helvetica. If Founders Grotesk is intentional, it requires a hosted `@font-face` declaration pointing to a CDN-hosted file (not Google Fonts, which doesn't carry it).

**Issue: Open Sans loaded at version `v17` (2019)**
```
https://fonts.gstatic.com/s/opensans/v17/...
```
Google Fonts currently serves v40+. v17 still resolves, but hardcoded version URLs bypass gstatic's automatic update path. If Google retires v17 assets, the font silently disappears. Prefer the Google Fonts CSS API URL or self-host.

**Issue: `font-display: swap` in email context**
All six `@font-face` blocks declare `font-display: swap`. Email clients do not implement the CSS Font Loading API — this property is ignored in every tested client and adds dead bytes.

**Issue: `img { white-space: pre; }`**
`white-space` is not a valid property on replaced elements (`<img>`). It has no rendering effect but is noise. Not harmful, but indicates template cruft.

**Positive signals:**
- `[x-apple-data-detectors]` reset present — prevents iOS from auto-linking phone numbers/dates inside anchor text.
- `u ~ div .pattern { min-width: 100vw !important; }` — correct Gmail clipping/dark-mode hack.
- VML namespaces (`xmlns:v`, `xmlns:o`) present for Outlook VML background image support.
- `X-UA-Compatible: IE=edge` inside conditional comment — correct Outlook rendering target.

**Cannot assess from truncated source:** `role="presentation"` on layout tables, `alt` attributes on images, color contrast ratios.
