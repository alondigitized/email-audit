# Deals of the Month Are Here ✨
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-03**
## Executive summary

- This is Sally Beauty's monthly deals roundup, dressed up in COLORfest branding lifted straight from the 7/30 teaser ("Your first look at Sally COLORfest"). Where that email was a pure campaign tease, this one cashes it in: a COLORfest hero gives way to three stacked offers ($5 off $20 hair care, buy-two-get-one-free Wella color, up to 40% off styling tools) plus a rewards push, all in one long scroll. It's a lot of email, but the execution is clean — no render bugs, specific SKUs with shade names, and a hero image that actually looks like real Sally merchandising rather than stock photography.
- The subject line is the weak link. "Deals of the Month Are Here ✨" is vague where the body is specific — it doesn't tell you which deal, and coming five sends after "Don't miss these summer deals 🤩" (7/25) and six after the COLORfest teaser, it reads as one more entry in a cadence that's already sending near-daily. This brand's creative execution keeps earning strong scores; its subject-line discipline and send frequency are what's dragging down open behavior.
- Net: strong body, generic subject, cadence fatigue. Solid but not best-in-class for this sender.

## What's working

- Clean visual hierarchy: COLORfest hero → lifestyle photo → three clearly sectioned offers, each with its own product imagery.
- Specific SKU-level detail (shade names like "Lightest Ash Blonde," "Medium Blonde," product names like Sauce Beauty Guacamole Whip Deep Moisture Mask) gives shoppers enough to act without clicking through.
- Three distinct, genuinely different offer mechanics (fixed-$ discount, BOGO, percentage-off) stacked without feeling repetitive.
- Loyalty tie-in ("Shop Twice in August, Earn a $5 Reward in September") and Klarna financing both visible, giving multiple reasons to convert beyond the base discount.
- No layout breakage, broken images, or overlapping text anywhere in the render.

## What's weak

- Hero section leads with campaign mood copy ("Welcome to your best color era") instead of the actual discount — a shopper has to scroll past the hero before hitting a dollar figure.
- None of the two lead offers ($5/$20, BOGO Wella) carry a visible expiration date, unlike this brand's recent flash-sale sends (e.g., 7/31's "ends 08/02," 7/24's "ending July 25") — no urgency to act today.
- No star ratings or review counts on any featured product, despite this being a considered-purchase category (hair color, especially).
- Subject line is generic relative to the specificity in the body — "Deals of the Month" undersells three concrete, nameable offers that were available to headline instead.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a dollar figure in the subject line — this brand has three concrete offers to choose from and picked none of them for the subject. Lead with the strongest one.
- 2. Add an explicit end date to the $5/$20 and BOGO Wella callouts — every high-performing send in this brand's recent history (7/31, 7/25, 7/24) anchored urgency with a hard date; this one doesn't.
- 3. Move a discount above the hero fold, or fold it into the hero headline itself, so mobile readers see a number before they scroll.
- 4. Add a review star rating under at least the Wella and Sauce Beauty products to build purchase confidence on a category where fit/shade risk is high.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$5 off $20 + BOGO hair color — deals of the month`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Buy 2, get 1 free Wella color (ends soon)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus up to 40% off styling tools and a $5 reward when you shop twice in August.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Save on Wella, Sauce Beauty, and ION — no code needed.`
