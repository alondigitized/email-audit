# They’re always SO good 👑
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-16**
## Executive summary

- This is the same catalog-scroll structure Sephora has run at least six times in the last two weeks, and once again the subject line makes a promise the body doesn't deliver — "They're always SO good" implies a curated, high-confidence pick, but what follows is a Dior perfume hero into an unstructured eight-item grid (skincare, haircare, fragrance, hand sanitizer, cleansing balm) with zero connective tissue explaining why these items are "always so good." The trial-size offer at the very top ("Choose a Trial Size" with Medik8/AESTOTA/Ultra Violette, $30 minimum) is actually the most concrete offer in the whole email, but it's crammed above the fold before the hero even loads, so it reads like a banner ad rather than the lead story.
- The "$5 off with 250 points" Beauty Insider offer near the bottom is buried under two more promo tiles (Makeup's Latest Lineup, Celebration Events) — by the time a reader scrolls that far, they've already been asked to context-switch four times. Nothing here is broken or ugly, but nothing is prioritized either; it's the fifth iteration of "hero + undifferentiated grid" this cycle, and the fatigue is starting to show relative to 8/14's rhode Spotwear send, which at least had a clear single-brand focus.
- Judgement: a middling, low-effort send that leans on brand recognition rather than any real curation or personalization — consistent with the 6.00 average this brand has posted all week.

## What's working

- Clean, legible product grid with consistent pricing display for every item.
- The Medik8/AESTOTA/Ultra Violette trial-size banner has a genuine, specific offer (free with $30 purchase) and a clear CTA button.
- "ONLY AT SEPHORA" and "Allure Best of Beauty" badges on Kérastase, Farmacy, and Touchland add credible social proof.
- The Beauty Insider tier bar (Insider / VIB / Rouge) at the footer reinforces loyalty segmentation visually.
- No render bugs — images, prices, and layout all display cleanly.

## What's weak

- Subject promises a "so good" pick but the body is an unranked eight-SKU grid spanning fragrance, hair, skin, and hand sanitizer — no editorial thread ties it together.
- The "$5 Off" Beauty Insider offer, the single most actionable line-item CTA, is stranded near the bottom under two other promo banners.
- "The Drop-Everything Edit" script header and Dior hero disconnect from the trial-size banner above it — two different offers competing for the same top-of-email real estate.
- Same catalog-scroll pattern as 8/13's "Beauty you can take from day to night" and 8/11's "Not a contest" — this is now the sixth near-identical structure in two weeks.
- No personalization signal anywhere (no name, no "based on your purchases," no category targeting) despite Sephora having rich loyalty/purchase data.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the grid to 3-4 items and give each a one-line "why it's a cult-fave" reason — turns a generic scroll into the curated pick the subject promises.
- 2. Move the "$5 Off / 250 points" Beauty Insider offer up near the top — it's the most concrete, member-specific incentive in the email and it's currently buried.
- 3. Resolve the competing headers — either lead with the trial-size offer or "The Drop-Everything Edit," not both stacked at the top.
- 4. Add real personalization (e.g., "since you loved [X]" or filter the grid by a stated category) — six sends in two weeks with an identical unstructured grid is showing fatigue.
