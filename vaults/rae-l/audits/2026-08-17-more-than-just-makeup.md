# More than just makeup
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-17**
## Executive summary

- This is a full pivot away from the Thru Line eyeliner arc that's dominated ILIA's calendar since 07-22 — "More than just makeup" is a brand-manifesto send, not a product launch. It leads with "Makeup That Makes Your Skin Better," then runs three hero products (Super Serum Skin Tint, Overglaze Lip Gloss, Skin Blur Concealer) each backed by clinical-claim bullets and a "Safe for sensitive skin" tag, closing on a "Clean at ILIA. Sensitive Skin-friendly. Cruelty-free." brand statement.
- It reads more like a catalog/brand-values page than a promotional email — there's no discount, no urgency, no single CTA to rally around. Three "Shop" buttons compete (Shop All, Shop All Formulas, Shop Now), and none of them point to a specific offer. Compared to 08-13's stripped-down educator/student note (4/10) or 08-12's clean GWP hook (7/10), this one has strong visual production but weak commercial pull — it's a well-designed pamphlet, not an email built to convert on open.
- Judgement: handsome execution, thin on the "why click today" logic. This works as a quarterly brand-values refresher but shouldn't be run back-to-back with anything else this positioning-heavy.

## What's working

- Clean, consistent product-card layout — each of the three products gets the same clinical-proof-point + "Safe for sensitive skin" treatment, which builds a repeatable trust pattern down the scroll.
- Hero image and copy are on-brand and legible: "Makeup That Makes Your Skin Better" lands clearly against the model close-up.
- Clinical stats ("100% said it smooths skin texture," "Clinically proven... after 28 days") give concrete, specific proof points rather than vague beauty-marketing fluff.
- Footer trust icons (1% for the Planet, Leaping Bunny, Recycle With Pact, Personalized Shade Match, ILIA VIP, Auto-Replenish) reinforce brand values compactly without extra clutter.
- No render bugs — images, text, and buttons all display cleanly with proper spacing.

## What's weak

- No offer anywhere in the email — no %/$ off, no bundle, no free shipping callout beyond the standard "$50+ free shipping" footer line. Nothing time-bound to justify opening today vs. next week.
- Three separate CTAs (Shop All, Shop All Formulas, Shop Now) dilute the primary action — a reader has no obvious single next step.
- Visual hierarchy sprawls: three near-identical product blocks in a row with no lead product means the eye doesn't land on one hero offer.
- Subject "More than just makeup" is abstract brand language, not a concrete hook — no product name, benefit, or urgency signal.
- This is thin on personalization or segmentation cues — nothing that signals it knows the reader's shade, skin type, or past purchases despite the "Personalized Shade Match" footer icon sitting right there unused.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one hero product (Super Serum Skin Tint SPF 40 is the strongest candidate — it's the newest/most differentiated) and lead the email with it; demote the other two to a "complete your routine" strip below the fold.
- 2. Collapse to one CTA. Three "Shop" buttons with slightly different copy reads as indecisive — standardize on a single action tied to the hero product.
- 3. Add a concrete incentive — even a modest one (free mini with purchase, bundle price) would turn this from a brand refresher into something worth clicking now.
- 4. Put a specific benefit or product name in the subject line instead of an abstract tagline.
- 5. Activate the "Personalized Shade Match" hook in the footer as a real CTA earlier in the email — this is a distinct enough feature to be a curiosity-driver, not a footer afterthought.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Skincare-powered makeup, 100% skin-tested`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New Super Serum Skin Tint: makeup that treats skin`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Clinically proven to improve skin barrier function in 7 days`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Find your shade + your new everyday skin tint`
