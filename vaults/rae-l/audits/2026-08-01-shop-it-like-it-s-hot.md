# Shop it like it’s hot
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-01**
## Executive summary

- This is a seasonal "beat the heat" tips listicle dressed up with a Snoop Dogg pun subject line ("Shop it like it's hot") that promises more than the email delivers — there's no actual sale here, just four cooling-product tips and a thin $5-off-the-app banner riding along at the top. Compared to July 30's Scrubee spotlight (7.0), which built real conviction around one hero product with 1,800+ reviews, this send spreads thin across four different items (shower jelly, toner, foot lotion, "cooling crew" products) plus a bolt-on Sleepy module, so nothing gets the depth to actually convert.
- Visually it's clean and on-brand — teal "Keep Your Cool" hero, consistent numbered-tip layout, decent product photography — but the CTA is a vague "Hit Refresh" that doesn't tell you what happens next, and there's zero social proof anywhere in the body. The subject's "shop" framing sets up an expectation of a deal or urgency that the content never pays off.
- Net: competent execution of a familiar Lush format, but a subject/body mismatch and a vague CTA cap this well below the brand's best sends (Naked, Advent calendar) at 7/10.

## What's working

- Clean 4-step numbered tip format with alternating image/text blocks makes the email easy to scan quickly.
- The teal "Keep Your Cool" hero has clear visual hierarchy with a single obvious button.
- Each tip ties directly to a specific product with a cooling ingredient callout (mint, arnica, lavender), giving the copy some texture.
- Lush Club loyalty module and "Bring It Back" recycling CTA reinforce brand values and membership without cluttering the main flow.
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, no overlapping text or layout breaks.

## What's weak

- Subject promises "shop"/deal energy but the body is an editorial tips list — no sale, no discount tied to the theme itself.
- Primary CTA "Hit Refresh" is a pun, not an instruction — doesn't say whether it leads to a shop page, a collection, or an article.
- Zero social proof (no star ratings, no review counts, no customer quotes) anywhere, a step back from July 30's Scrubee email which led with 1,800+ five-star reviews.
- The only concrete offer ($5 off with the app) is buried in a thin top banner, easy to miss on a scroll-past.
- No visible urgency or deadline — the promo terms exist only in tiny footer fine print.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the subject/body mismatch first — either build a real "shop it like it's hot" moment (a heat-wave flash discount) or drop the "shop" framing entirely and lead with the cooling-tips hook, which is the actual content.
- 2. Swap "Hit Refresh" for a concrete CTA per module ("Shop Shower Jelly," "Shop Foot Lotion") so each tip has its own clear next step instead of one ambiguous hero button.
- 3. Add a proof point to at least one hero product (rating stars or a review count) — the format has room for it and it's proven to lift this brand's scores (Scrubee, 7.0).
- 4. Give the $5-off-app banner more visual weight or move a version of it into the hero section, since it's currently the only real "shop" payoff in the whole email.

- **Subject Alt B:** `4 ways to cool down this week`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shower jellies, foot lotion & more to beat the sweat`
