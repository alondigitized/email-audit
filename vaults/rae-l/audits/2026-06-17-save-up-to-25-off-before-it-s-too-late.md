# Save up to 25% off before it’s too late
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-17**
## Executive summary

- This is Glossier's second send on June 17 — and the ninth Friends of Glossier Sale email in seven days. The creative angle here is the right call: rather than another mechanical percentage announcement, this email leans on UGC community recommendations to justify a final-push "ENDS SOON" frame. The structure is sound and the "ENDS SOON" badge at the top translates subject-line urgency into a visual anchor cleanly.
- But the execution stumbles on the format it chose. The UGC review blurbs are too small and truncated to do any persuasion work — if you're going to stake the email's credibility on community voice, those quotes need to be readable. The second product tile has a label artifact ("Home texte Last Stop in Brown") that reads like a metadata bleed, not a product name. And the subscription pitch at the bottom — "lock in 25% off forever" — directly undercuts the urgency arc the rest of the email built.
- The concept is the strongest creative angle in the entire nine-email arc. The implementation is the weakest execution of it. A better-rendered version of this email would have earned an 8; as built, the UGC promise is undermined by unreadable copy and a confused CTA architecture.

## What's working

- **"ENDS SOON" banner at the very top**: anchors urgency immediately without repeating the subject verbatim — the eye lands here before anything else.
- **Community UGC framing**: after eight percentage-off subject lines, leading with "Recommendations from You" is the freshest angle Glossier has tried in this arc.
- **Three named products, not a generic sale page**: Glossier Trio, Last Stop in Brown, and Balm Dotcom give subscribers a specific reason to click rather than a blank "shop now" ramp to the homepage.
- **Subscription lock-in pitch**: converting a fence-sitter into recurring 25% savings at the tail of a sale is a smart last-mile play — good idea, wrong placement.
- **Visual palette holds up**: pink/cream tones, polished photography, no hero breakage.

## What's weak

- **UGC quotes are too small and truncated to read**: reviewer text trails off mid-sentence; social proof that can't be read is worse than no social proof.
- **"Home texte Last Stop in Brown" product label** looks like a data or formatting artifact — one brief trust wobble in an otherwise clean layout.
- **Three identical "Shop now" CTAs in a row**: no differentiation by product, no hierarchy — creates noise instead of directing intent.
- **Subscription block fights the urgency arc**: "ENDS SOON" followed by "lock in forever" sends contradictory signals about what the email is actually asking you to do.
- **Day nine of nine sends**: the subscriber fatigue ceiling is real; "before it's too late" loses its edge when it's been "too late" every day for a week.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the UGC quotes legible.** Two full readable sentences per reviewer, reviewer name prominent. Small truncated blurbs signal filler; readable quotes signal social proof. This is the single highest-leverage fix.
- 2. **Label each CTA with the product, not "Shop now."** "Shop Lash Slick," "Shop Last Stop in Brown," "Shop Balm Dotcom" — turns three identical buttons into three targeted entries.
- 3. **Cut the subscription block from this email.** It belongs in a post-sale standalone, not mid-urgency-arc. The ENDS SOON frame and the forever-savings frame cancel each other.
- 4. **Fix or replace the "Home texte" label** — it should render as the clean product name.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your community's top picks — 25% off ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last chance: the Glossier products your people swear by`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Glossier Trio, Balm Dotcom, and more — see what made the list`

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is built on a Klaviyo-delivered XHTML 1.0 Transitional template with standard MSO conditionals. One structurally invalid element introduces a silent failure risk; everything else in the visible source is well-formed.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Issue: `<span>` used as stylesheet/resource loader with `href` and `rel` attributes**

```html
<span rel="stylesheet" href="https://ctrk.klclick.com/l/01KVB5CK3DBKCRPKT1P0VV07VX_0"></span>
```

`rel` and `href` are not valid attributes on `<span>`. This element will be silently ignored by all conforming parsers — it will not load a stylesheet, fire a request, or register as a click. If this is intended as a stylesheet injection or a Klaviyo open-tracking hook, it is broken. The correct element for a stylesheet load is `<link rel="stylesheet" href="...">` in `<head>`. If it is intended as an open-track pixel, it should be a 1×1 `<img>` with `src`.

- **Domain confirmed:** `ctrk.klclick.com` is Klaviyo's click/tracking CDN — sending domain alignment is correct.
- CTA links in the body are not visible in the truncated source; cannot audit UTM passthrough on those.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Preheader padding uses mixed zero-width characters**

The preheader filler block mixes U+034F COMBINING GRAPHEME JOINER (`͏`) and HTML soft-hyphen entities (`­` / U+00AD):

```html
<span style="display:none ...">Explore product picks from our community ͏ ͏ ͏ ... ­ ­ ­ ...</span>
```

Soft hyphens (`­`) can render as a visible hyphen glyph in Outlook 2016+ and some Samsung Mail versions. The U+034F block alone is sufficient to suppress preview text leakage; soft hyphens should be removed.

**Preheader/subject mismatch**

Hidden preheader: *"Explore product picks from our community"*
Subject: *"Save up to 25% off before it's too late"*

These are thematically unrelated. Email clients that surface the preheader alongside the subject (Gmail, Apple Mail, Outlook mobile) will show a confusing secondary message. This is a functional rendering concern, not just a content one.

**DOCTYPE**

XHTML 1.0 Transitional is in use. Functionally acceptable, but `<!DOCTYPE html>` (HTML5) is now the safer default — it prevents Outlook's quirks-mode box model on newer builds and is what Klaviyo, Litmus, and MJML all emit by default.

**No image `alt` text confirmable** — source is truncated before `<img>` tags appear. Flag for QA pass on full source.
