# How to survive your graduation
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- This is a "Dear Glossier" Q&A-format email — a user named "Anxious Grad" asks how to survive graduation day, and Glossier responds with product picks: Boy Brow, Futuredew Solid, and Lash Slick. The editorial hook is genuinely clever. Framing mascara as "cry-proof, hug-proof, dependable" for graduation is exactly the kind of copy that earns product trust, and the conversational letter format makes recommendations feel like advice from a friend rather than a push. That's Glossier doing what Glossier does well.
- The problem is context. Glossier already sent "Meet The Grad Look!" on May 8th — a clean kit-focused campaign that scored an 8/10. This email is hitting the same graduating subscriber with the same products, the same "The Grad Look" bundle label, no new offer, and no discount. For anyone who saw that email and didn't buy, this doesn't move the needle. For anyone who did buy, it's redundant. The Q&A angle is fresher creative, but it's arriving too late in the graduation cycle with nothing new to offer.
- No offer is the key miss. The May 4th Spring Fever Sale at 30-50% off set a high-value anchor; returning to full-price editorial with no incentive to act now is a significant drop in momentum.

## What's working

- **Q&A editorial frame is the right creative format** — "Dear Anxious Grad" earns attention and makes product recs feel organic rather than pushed.
- **"Cry-proof, hug-proof, dependable" copy** is occasion-specific and memorable — connects product benefit directly to the life event.
- **Subject line "How to survive your graduation"** is clean, short (30 chars), question-hook format, no spam signals — above average for subject craft.
- **Product specificity** — Boy Brow, Futuredew Solid, and Lash Slick are named, not just gestured at. The "The Grad Look" bundle label keeps the kit cohesive.
- **"Get in touch" bottom section** with a "Message" CTA is a low-friction brand touchpoint that fits the conversational tone of the whole email.

## What's weak

- **No offer** — no discount, no urgency, no free gift with purchase. The May 4th sale set a high anchor; this email gives the subscriber no reason to buy today versus any other day.
- **Graduation fatigue** — "Meet The Grad Look!" already shipped May 8th with the same kit. This is the second full graduation send in 10 days with no new hook for non-converters.
- **Products are below the fold** on mobile — the letter text runs long before the product grid appears, so anyone scanning quickly may bounce before reaching the "Shop now" CTAs.
- **No hero visual above the fold** — the email opens as dense text; a single strong product or lifestyle image would anchor attention faster before the copy does its work.
- **"The Grad Look" bundle label is recycled** — same name, same kit as the May 8th send. A non-converter who remembers the first email gets zero new reason to reconsider.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a win-back incentive for non-converters.** A free travel-size add-on or a "grad gift" bundle pricing (even 10-15% off) turns this from pure editorial into a send that drives action. The Q&A creative is strong enough to carry it without feeling discount-only.
- 2. **Open with a product image, not text.** A single lifestyle image of the three products (or a model in graduation regalia) before the letter copy would halt the scroll faster on mobile.
- 3. **Reframe the bundle away from "The Grad Look"** — they've used that label twice now. "The Cry-Proof Kit" or "Grad Essentials" gives the same products a fresher name for anyone who ignored the May 8th send.
- 4. **Cut the letter opening by half.** The "First off, congratulations" preamble delays the product recommendations. Get to the "stick to the essentials" line in two sentences, not five.
- 5. **Add a deadline hook.** Even "Ships in time for your ceremony if you order by [date]" creates a conversion reason without requiring a discount.

- **Preheader Alt B:** `Because smudge-proof is non-negotiable on grad day`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Non-standard open/tracking beacon implementation**
```html
<span rel="stylesheet" href="https://ctrk.klclick.com/l/01KRW6629PSS04VCBAG9Z7Y80T_0"></span>
```
`<span>` does not accept `rel` or `href` attributes in any HTML spec. The browser/email renderer will silently ignore both attributes, so this element does nothing. If this was intended as a Klaviyo open-tracking pixel, it must be an `<img>` with `width="1" height="1"`, not a `<span>`. If it was intended as a tracked redirect link, it must be an `<a>`. **This beacon is dead and produces no tracking signal.**

**Click-tracking domain confirmed:** `ctrk.klclick.com` — Klaviyo's standard redirect domain. No anomaly there.

**Source comments expose design studio:**
```html
<!-- EMAIL CREATED BY GLOSSIER x SORRY STUDIO SORRY.LAND -->
```
Not a security vulnerability, but third-party studio attribution is visible in source to anyone who inspects the email.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS `:hover` rollover effects are non-functional in primary clients**
```css
.rollover:hover .rollover-first { max-height:0 !important; display:none !important }
.rollover:hover .rollover-second { max-height:none !important; display:block !important }
```
Hover states are suppressed in Gmail (all surfaces), Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo Mail. The `.rollover-second` content will never surface for the majority of recipients. If this is swap imagery or a CTA variant, the fallback (`.rollover-first`) must stand alone.

**`lang="en"` and `dir="ltr"` present** — correct, no issue.

**Preheader padding technique** uses U+034F (Combining Grapheme Joiner `͏`) and soft hyphens (`­`). This is a known valid technique, though some aggressive spam filters penalize high concentrations of combining characters.
