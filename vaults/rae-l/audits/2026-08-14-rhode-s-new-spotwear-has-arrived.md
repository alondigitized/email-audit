# rhode’s NEW Spotwear has arrived
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-14**
## Executive summary

- This is Sephora's strongest structural move in two weeks: instead of opening cold into the usual eight-item catalog grid, it leads with a genuine single-brand hero — "JUST DROPPED," rhode's new Spotwear hydrocolloid patches, exclusive-to-Sephora badge, and a real product shot. That's the same "departure from the scroll" instinct that made the 8/9 K18 send the best-performing email in this brand's recent history (8/10). Unfortunately this one doesn't commit to the bit — after the rhode hero, it folds right back into the identical unstructured eight-item grid (Westman Atelier, Emi Jay, Yepoda, Saie, Dior, BeautyBlender, Kenra, Rare Beauty) that's driven six-out-of-ten scores all month.
- There's also a structural conflict at the very top: before you even reach the rhode hero, there's a competing offer banner for a free rhode Highlight Milk trial size (code RHODETREAT, $30+ purchase). Two rhode offers stacked back to back, in different formats, before the "headline" product even loads — muddies what the email actually wants you to do.
- Net: better than the median Sephora send because of the real hero moment and a genuine, code-based offer, but it squanders the momentum by reverting to catalog-scroll for 70% of the email. Call it solid-not-exceptional.

## What's working

- Real single-product hero: "JUST DROPPED" + "NEW FROM RHODE" with an actual product shot of the Spotwear patches and a clear "SHOP NOW" button.
- Concrete, redeemable offer: RHODETREAT code for a free rhode Highlight Milk trial size with $30+ purchase — a specific promo, not vague "new arrivals" language.
- "ONLY AT SEPHORA" exclusivity badge on the Spotwear launch adds a real reason to buy here vs. elsewhere.
- Loyalty context is visible throughout (Insider point count at top, Insider/VIB/Rouge tier bar in the footer).
- Clean render — no overlapping text, broken images, or layout breaks anywhere in the scroll.

## What's weak

- The email opens with the Highlight Milk trial-size banner, not the rhode Spotwear hero the subject line promises — the first thing your eye hits isn't what got you to open.
- After the hero, it collapses straight back into the same unstructured eight-item grid (skincare, fragrance, makeup, haircare all mixed with no logic) that's run in nearly every recent Sephora send — this is the exact pattern criticized in the 8/10, 8/11, 8/13 sends.
- No models or lifestyle imagery anywhere — every product shot is a flat-lay, so there's nothing to signal who this is "for."
- No urgency or deadline on the RHODETREAT offer beyond fine print ("*Exclusions/items apply") — feels like it could run indefinitely.
- Two rhode CTAs above the fold (the trial-size banner and the Spotwear hero) compete for the same click, diluting the single clear ask.

## Recommendations

- 1. Kill the top Highlight Milk banner or fold it into the rhode hero block as a secondary line ("+ free Highlight Milk trial with $30 purchase") — right now it steals the first-impression slot from the actual news.
- 2. Cut the eight-item catalog grid entirely, or replace it with 2-3 curated "pairs well with" items directly tied to rhode/skincare — the K18 send proved a focused story outperforms the scroll.
- 3. Add a real deadline to the RHODETREAT code ("through Sunday") — turns a soft perk into a reason to act today.
- 4. Put one lifestyle/model shot near the Spotwear hero so the "who's this for" question isn't left entirely to flat-lay product photography.

- **Subject Alt B:** `rhode dropped something new (+ a free gift)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Strawberry-shaped hydrocolloid patches, $16 — plus a free Highlight Milk trial with $30`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Get in before it sells out — new rhode, exclusively here`
