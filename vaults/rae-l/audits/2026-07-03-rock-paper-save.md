# Rock, paper, save
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-03**
## Executive summary

- "Rock, Paper, Save" is the third consecutive email in ILIA's Buy 3, Save 20% promotional run — following the July 1 launch send and yesterday's "Third one's the charm" eye-look editorial. The creative earns its keep: the rock-paper-scissors riff maps neatly onto the "buy three" mechanic, and organizing the body into named Skincare / Complexion / Color trifectas is the smartest merchandising decision this campaign has made. Someone on the team thought about this one.
- The trouble is the cadence. Three emails in three consecutive days for the same offer, even with the creative rotating, starts to feel like nagging. The offer itself hasn't changed — BUNDLE20, 20% off, buy 3 — so subscribers who didn't bite on day one or day two are now getting a third nudge with diminishing returns. There's no visible deadline, which removes the one lever that would justify the frequency.
- Execution is clean: no render bugs, clear visual hierarchy, hero CTA above the fold. If ILIA had mailed this on day one instead of the opener, it would be the best send of the trio. As the closer, it risks being the one that triggers unsubscribes.

## What's working

- The rock-paper-scissors wordplay maps cleanly onto the "buy 3" mechanic — pun earns its place.
- Product trifectas organized by category (Skincare, Complexion, Color) give subscribers a clear decision path rather than a firehose of SKUs.
- "BUILD YOUR TRIO" CTA copy is specific and action-oriented — one of the stronger button labels in this campaign.
- Top banner + hero + body all consistently reinforce BUNDLE20 — no confusion about the offer.
- Clean render, no overlapping text or broken images; ILIA's minimalist aesthetic holds at every scroll depth.

## What's weak

- No deadline anywhere in the visible email — three sends in three days with no end date makes the urgency feel manufactured, not real.
- The Color trifecta CTA breaks the pattern: "SHOP OUR BESTSELLERS" instead of "BUNDLE UP: COLOR" — signals this category wasn't curated as a bundle, which undercuts the trifecta framing.
- Offer is identical to the July 1 launch send; subscribers who already opened that one see nothing new to act on.
- The hero subhead ("Give 20% loves everything" or similar) reads a little garbled — possibly an artifact of the copy trying to cover "everyone loves 20%" — the exact phrasing is awkward.
- No product counts or inventory signal to manufacture scarcity as a substitute for the missing deadline.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard deadline.** "Offer ends Sunday" or "Last 48 hours" would justify the third send and give the cadence a purpose. This is the single change that lifts this email most.
- 2. **Fix the Color CTA.** Change "SHOP OUR BESTSELLERS" to "BUNDLE UP: COLOR PICKS" so it matches the trifecta architecture the rest of the email established.
- 3. **Surface the code in the hero.** BUNDLE20 is in the banner but not in the hero block itself — put it next to the CTA so it's one-tap copy.
- 4. **Reframe the hero subhead** away from awkward "Give 20% loves everything" construction toward something like "Pick any 3, save 20% — no rules."
- 5. **Consider stopping at 2 sends next time.** The July 2 editorial + this trifecta format would have been a cleaner two-email arc; three consecutive sends without a deadline wears out the offer.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` fonts stripped in Gmail:**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```
Gmail removes `@import` inside `<style>`. Montserrat and any Klaviyo custom typeface will not render; fallback chain `"Montserrat", "Helvetica", sans-serif` kicks in. Acceptable degradation, but intentional brand font is absent for ~40%+ of consumer opens.

**Conflicting `<p>` margin rules:**
```css
/* Rule 1 (reset block) */
p { display: block; margin: 13px 0 }

/* Rule 2 (later in same <style>) */
p { margin-left: 0; margin-right: 0; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 0; padding-bottom: 1em }
```
Rule 2 overrides rule 1's `margin: 13px 0` to zero. In clients that strip `padding` on block elements (older Outlook, some Yahoo), the `padding-bottom: 1em` fallback also fails, collapsing paragraph spacing entirely.

**Hover/visited state color lock:**
```css
a:visited {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
a:hover   {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
```
Visited links are visually indistinguishable from unvisited. Hover provides no feedback. Minor accessibility concern (WCAG 2.4.4 link purpose / 1.4.1 use of color); not a blocker for email delivery.

**`<title>` is empty:** `<title> </title>` — no screen reader label for the document. Low severity in email context.
