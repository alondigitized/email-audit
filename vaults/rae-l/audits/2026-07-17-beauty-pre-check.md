# Beauty pre-check
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-17**
## Executive summary

- "Beauty pre-check" is a travel-concept editorial that reframes ILIA's summer lineup as an in-flight packing checklist — the "now boarding" hero with a passport and blush product is the strongest executional flourish. The conceit is clever and topical, and it earns ILIA credit for not retreating to a straightforward discount email. But the concept buckles under its own ambition: five separate product modules stacked vertically with individual "Shop Now" CTAs turns this into a catalog scroll rather than a focused send. No single product wins.
- The mini tote GWP reappears at the bottom — it was the centerpiece of the July 6 "PSA—mini totes are back" (9/10) and the July 9 "A mini mentality" (8/10) follow-up. Hitting the same GWP a third time in 11 days is a cadence problem; this module now reads like filler, not a reason to click.
- Net judgement: a conceptually interesting email that commits the most common ILIA mistake — trying to do too much. The travel conceit is strong enough to anchor a one- or two-product send; spread across five modules it loses all urgency and gives the subscriber no clear action to take.

## What's working

- **"Now boarding" travel hero** is visually distinct and gives the multi-product structure a legitimate editorial framing.
- **Product pairings** (SPF + Makeup Air, Protect + Blur Duo) are smart merchandising — they tell a mini routine story rather than dumping SKUs.
- **Rating badges** (4.1★, 4.4★, 4.6★) visible on at least three modules add social proof without cluttering the layout.
- **Visual consistency** — the muted rose/taupe palette holds across all modules; no render bugs visible.

## What's weak

- **Five CTAs with identical "Shop Now" copy** — no hierarchy, no reason to choose; the subscriber's attention is spread across the whole screen.
- **Mini Tote GWP is the third appearance in 11 days** (July 6, July 9, July 17) — by now it reads as padding rather than a bonus offer.
- **No price, no discount, no urgency signal anywhere** — the email asks for the click with no financial or time-bounded hook.
- **Hero module text is tiny** — "Beauty pre-check. Pack these ready summer essentials" is barely legible at mobile scale, undercutting the concept.
- **Brown Mascara module** was already the star of the July 14 send (8/10 score); restacking it here without a new angle dilutes that launch's momentum.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse to one hero product.** Pick the SPF-and-Go kit (strongest seasonal hook) and build the whole email around it. Move remaining products to a "also in your carry-on" secondary rail — smaller, lower visual weight.
- 2. **Add an urgency or price signal.** Even "Free mini tote with any $65+ order — this weekend only" in the hero block gives the subscriber a reason to act today versus tomorrow.
- 3. **Rewrite the five identical CTAs.** Match copy to the product: "Shop the SPF Kit," "Add the Duo," "Shop Brown Mascara." Differentiation drives click confidence.
- 4. **Retire the mini tote module or reframe it.** If it must appear, lead with the condition ("spend $65") rather than the asset — make it feel like a reward, not a leftover.
- 5. **Increase hero text size.** The "now boarding" lockup works; the supporting copy beneath it is unreadable on mobile.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title> </title>
```
Value is a single space. Some email clients (Apple Mail, Outlook desktop) surface the title; screen readers may announce it as blank. Should reflect the subject line or campaign name.

**Google Fonts loaded via `@import` — will not render in Gmail or Outlook**
```css
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
```
Gmail strips `@import` rules entirely. Outlook ignores them. Montserrat will **not render** in either client. The fallback stack (`"Montserrat", "Helvetica", sans-serif`) is functional, but the intended brand font is effectively absent for the majority of recipients.

**All anchor states are identical — no hover feedback**
```css
a:not([name]), a:link, a:visited, a:active, a:hover {
  color: #5f4b3c; text-decoration: none;
}
```
`a:hover` is identical to resting state. Users get zero visual affordance when hovering over links. Accessibility concern (WCAG 2.4.4 – Link Purpose).
