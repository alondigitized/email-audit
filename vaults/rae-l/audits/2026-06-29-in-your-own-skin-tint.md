# In your own skin (tint)
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-29**
## Executive summary

- This is ILIA's full-spotlight send for the Super Serum Skin Tint SPF 40+, a product that got a brief mention as the "new head" in last Sunday's crowded June 28 digest. The bet here is UGC-as-proof: four real community members with photos and testimonials do the selling instead of a discount or urgency hook. That instinct is right for this product — "comfortable in your own skin" is precisely where social proof lands hardest, and the subject line wordplay ("in your own skin (tint)") is the smartest copy ILIA has shipped in this recent streak.
- The problem is the preheader. That top banner — a Lip Sketch GWP cross-sell — almost certainly bleeds into inbox preview text, so the first thing a subscriber sees alongside the Skin Tint subject is an offer for a completely different product. That's a self-inflicted dilution of an otherwise coherent send. The sustainability kicker ("Rooted In Change, 1 million trees planted") is well-placed and on-brand, but it's doing work that a price hook or shade-range call-out could do more immediately.
- Net judgement: a well-designed editorial send weakened by preheader misalignment and zero urgency. It earns opens on subject-line cleverness; it earns clicks on social proof alone — but it leaves conversion on the table.

## What's working

- Subject-line wordplay ("in your own skin (tint)") is genuinely clever — plays on the idiom, telegraphs the product, does both in 24 characters.
- Four diverse community members with photos and named testimonials is the right strategy for a skin-tone-matching product; the representation across ages and complexions is visible and intentional.
- "your best skin—bottled" subheadline is punchy and product-specific — one of ILIA's better body-copy lines recently.
- "Rooted In Change" sustainability block with a concrete number (1 million trees) adds brand depth without being preachy.
- Visual hierarchy is clean: hero → community grid → product bottle → mission. The eye travels naturally.

## What's weak

- The Lip Sketch GWP cross-sell banner sits at the very top of the email, almost certainly becoming the inbox preview — a different product's offer as the preheader for a Skin Tint send is a messaging collision.
- No offer, no urgency, no shade-range specificity on the primary product. "Shop now" is doing all the heavy lifting without a price reason or "find your shade" hook to drive action.
- Testimonial copy is rendered at small enough size that the actual quote content is unreadable at mobile scan speed — the faces convince, the words don't land.
- Cadence risk: ILIA has been in the inbox nearly every other day since June 18; this is the sixth send in roughly ten days, and subscriber fatigue is real even when the creative shifts.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the preheader first.** Kill or move the Lip Sketch GWP banner so the Skin Tint message is unbroken from subject line through preview text. A preheader that reads "12 shades, SPF 40+, worn by real people" would close the loop the subject opens.
- 2. **Pull one testimonial quote into the hero.** Instead of a product-only hero image, overlay a real quote in large type ("I stopped wearing foundation" or similar) — the UGC grid below earns more clicks when the hero sets the frame.
- 3. **Add a shade-finder CTA secondary to the main shop button.** "Not sure your shade? Take the quiz" reduces friction for the undecided and is the most likely reason someone reads this email without clicking.
- 4. **Give the Lip Sketch GWP its own dedicated moment** rather than using it as a top-banner afterthought. The June 20 and June 18 sends already burned a full email on that product; either retire it or make it a PS, not a header.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Find your skin tint match`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your shade is in here`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `12 shades, SPF 40+, worn by real people — find yours`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Community-tested. Real results. See their skin.`
