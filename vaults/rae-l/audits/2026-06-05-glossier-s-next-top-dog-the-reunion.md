# Glossier’s Next Top Dog: The Reunion
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-05**
## Executive summary

- This is Glossier's most purely editorial send in the Pink Capsule Collection arc — a mock reality-TV reunion framing the campaign dogs as "contestants" from *America's Next Top Model*, complete with interview quotes, neighborhood tags, and the kind of dry, self-aware humor that is unmistakably Glossier's voice. It's a content play, not a promotional one: there's no discount, no urgency, no membership hook. The product grid at the bottom (Collar, Leash, Hoodie, Errand Bag) is ancillary to the storytelling.
- The creative is well-executed and the dog profiles are genuinely funny — Runkle's "My parents haven't approved to me since I was eliminated. Thanks so." lands. But stripped of any offer, this email asks subscribers to click purely on brand affection. For shoppers on the fence about a $30 leash, there's nothing to tip them.
- Coming the day after the 6/4 fragrance pivot, returning to the dog universe feels like backtracking rather than building. The Pink Capsule arc has been running since 5/28 — this reunion may read as charming to superfans and as noise to everyone else.

## What's working

- Brand voice is the strongest in the arc — the dog "interview quotes" are specific, dry, and laugh-out-loud for anyone who's been following the campaign.
- Editorial visual hierarchy is clean: big dog portrait → neighborhood/age tag → quote, repeated four times without confusion.
- The subject line ("Glossier's Next Top Dog: The Reunion") earns attention on pun recognition alone — short (37 chars), no spam signals, memorable.
- Each product in the bottom grid has its own "Shop now" link, so shoppers can route directly rather than landing on the homepage.

## What's weak

- No price signal anywhere — not even a "starting at $X" or "free shipping" nudge. The email has no promotional hook.
- Product section is buried below four full dog profile blocks — most mobile readers won't scroll to the Collar/Leash/Hoodie grid.
- The "Shop now" CTA copy is passive and generic across all four products — zero differentiation.
- Returning to the Pink Capsule dog content one day after the fragrance pivot (6/4) breaks cadence; it reads like a leftover send rather than a planned arc beat.
- No urgency or scarcity signal anywhere — for a product drop from 5/28, there's no "still available" or "selling out" frame that would create a reason to click now.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one price anchor or free-shipping line** to the product grid. Even "Ships free on orders $30+" converts some browsing-mode readers into buyers without killing the editorial tone.
- 2. **Move the product grid above at least two of the dog profiles** — or introduce a floating "Shop the Collection" sticky CTA — so mobile readers encounter it before abandoning the scroll.
- 3. **Replace "Shop now" with product-specific CTA copy**: "Get the Collar" / "Grab the Leash" — costs nothing and increases click specificity.
- 4. **Add a send-timing rationale** that the 6/5 send date feels disconnected from the 6/4 fragrance pivot; if this send is part of a planned "reunion week," the subject or preheader should signal that.
