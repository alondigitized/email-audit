# Set your sights here 👀
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-04**
## Executive summary

- This email is Glossier's clean editorial pivot after a punishing sale arc — the Friends of Glossier Sale generated near-daily discount emails through most of June, and this is the first send that doesn't mention a percentage off. Three "NEW" product sets (Boy Brow Duo, a No-Makeup Makeup Look bundle, The Face Kit) anchor the scroll, with a "Coming Soon" NYC collection tease at the bottom. The creative is classic Glossier — white space, spare product photography, consistent badge-and-CTA structure throughout.
- The subject line is the central failure. "Set your sights here 👀" tells the subscriber nothing — no product, no offer, no reason to tap — and lands the day after July 4th on a list that has been conditioned by weeks of explicit "25% off" hooks. Open rate will bleed. The second problem: "Shop + save" appears six times, but no actual savings amount is shown anywhere in the email. After an arc that trained the list to expect specific discount numbers, a vague "save" promise with no dollar or percent figure attached will feel hollow or deceptive.
- The sets narrative itself is well-constructed and timely. If this email had a subject line that named what was inside, it would punch significantly harder.

## What's working

- **Pivot timing is correct.** After the Friends of Glossier Sale exhausted itself across eleven-plus emails, the list needed editorial content — new products are the right thing to lead with.
- **Three distinct set narratives** (brow, face, skin) give a real reason to scroll and speak to different buyer intents within the same session.
- **"Lightweight base perfect for summer"** in The Face Kit copy lands on July 3 with genuine seasonal relevance.
- **Consistent "NEW" badge + short description + CTA structure** is easy to parse — the eye moves through the email without friction.
- **Model casting in The Face Kit** is natural and age-appropriate; the hold-up-the-products pose feels like a real person, not a lookbook shoot.

## What's weak

- **"Set your sights here 👀" is a throwaway subject.** It signals nothing about sets, newness, or savings — maximum curiosity gap, minimum information, and the emoji doesn't make up for it.
- **Six "Shop + save" CTAs with no visible savings amount.** Every time that phrase appears without a number, it erodes the claim. Either show the bundle savings or change the CTA copy.
- **No price anchor anywhere in the email.** Not a single dollar figure, not a "save $X vs. buying separately" line, nothing to justify the savings framing.
- **The NYC "Coming Soon" block is structurally lost.** Buried below two product sections and an "Even More Sets" grid, a vague sign-up for an unspecified NYC collection drives neither curiosity nor conversion — it just stops the email cold.
- **No social proof.** Boy Brow is Glossier's most-reviewed product and the lead item in two of the three sets — a review count or a single quote would have done real work here.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject to name the product or the sets angle.** This is the single highest-leverage change — the body is solid; the door just doesn't open.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New sets: Boy Brow, Face Kit + more`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your favorites just got a plus-one`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Three new ways to bundle your routine — and actually save.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Boy Brow Duo. The No-Makeup Look. The Face Kit. All new.`
- 2. **Show the "save" amount.** If buying the Boy Brow Duo saves $7 versus singles, say so. One line of pricing math transforms "Shop + save" from vague to credible.
- 3. **Cut or defer the NYC tease.** A half-formed "Coming Soon" block at the bottom of a product sets email serves neither the sets story nor the NYC launch. Ship it as a standalone email when there's something real to show.
- 4. **Add one proof point to Boy Brow.** "The eyebrow product with over [X] reviews — now in a duo." is five words and would meaningfully lift the Boy Brow Duo section, which currently has no credibility signal beyond the Glossier name.

## Full review
## 1. Overview

This email is Glossier's clean editorial pivot after a punishing sale arc — the Friends of Glossier Sale generated near-daily discount emails through most of June, and this is the first send that doesn't mention a percentage off. Three "NEW" product sets (Boy Brow Duo, a No-Makeup Makeup Look bundle, The Face Kit) anchor the scroll, with a "Coming Soon" NYC collection tease at the bottom. The creative is classic Glossier — white space, spare product photography, consistent badge-and-CTA structure throughout.

The subject line is the central failure. "Set your sights here 👀" tells the subscriber nothing — no product, no offer, no reason to tap — and lands the day after July 4th on a list that has been conditioned by weeks of explicit "25% off" hooks. Open rate will bleed. The second problem: "Shop + save" appears six times, but no actual savings amount is shown anywhere in the email. After an arc that trained the list to expect specific discount numbers, a vague "save" promise with no dollar or percent figure attached will feel hollow or deceptive.

The sets narrative itself is well-constructed and timely. If this email had a subject line that named what was inside, it would punch significantly harder.

## 2. What worked

- **Pivot timing is correct.** After the Friends of Glossier Sale exhausted itself across eleven-plus emails, the list needed editorial content — new products are the right thing to lead with.
- **Three distinct set narratives** (brow, face, skin) give a real reason to scroll and speak to different buyer intents within the same session.
- **"Lightweight base perfect for summer"** in The Face Kit copy lands on July 3 with genuine seasonal relevance.
- **Consistent "NEW" badge + short description + CTA structure** is easy to parse — the eye moves through the email without friction.
- **Model casting in The Face Kit** is natural and age-appropriate; the hold-up-the-products pose feels like a real person, not a lookbook shoot.

## 3. What didn't

- **"Set your sights here 👀" is a throwaway subject.** It signals nothing about sets, newness, or savings — maximum curiosity gap, minimum information, and the emoji doesn't make up for it.
- **Six "Shop + save" CTAs with no visible savings amount.** Every time that phrase appears without a number, it erodes the claim. Either show the bundle savings or change the CTA copy.
- **No price anchor anywhere in the email.** Not a single dollar figure, not a "save $X vs. buying separately" line, nothing to justify the savings framing.
- **The NYC "Coming Soon" block is structurally lost.** Buried below two product sections and an "Even More Sets" grid, a vague sign-up for an unspecified NYC collection drives neither curiosity nor conversion — it just stops the email cold.
- **No social proof.** Boy Brow is Glossier's most-reviewed product and the lead item in two of the three sets — a review count or a single quote would have done real work here.

## 4. What I'd change

1. **Rewrite the subject to name the product or the sets angle.** This is the single highest-leverage change — the body is solid; the door just doesn't open.
   - **Subject Alt A:** `New sets: Boy Brow, Face Kit + more`
   - **Subject Alt B:** `Your favorites just got a plus-one`
   - **Preheader Alt A:** `Three new ways to bundle your routine — and actually save.`
   - **Preheader Alt B:** `Boy Brow Duo. The No-Makeup Look. The Face Kit. All new.`

2. **Show the "save" amount.** If buying the Boy Brow Duo saves $7 versus singles, say so. One line of pricing math transforms "Shop + save" from vague to credible.

3. **Cut or defer the NYC tease.** A half-formed "Coming Soon" block at the bottom of a product sets email serves neither the sets story nor the NYC launch. Ship it as a standalone email when there's something real to show.

4. **Add one proof point to Boy Brow.** "The eyebrow product with over [X] reviews — now in a duo." is five words and would meaningfully lift the Boy Brow Duo section, which currently has no credibility signal beyond the Glossier name.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Malformed tracking injection:**
```html
<span rel="stylesheet" href="https://ctrk.klclick.com/l/01KWN6D5H0G3GDFE2WBCCDKAWR_0"></span>
```
A `<span>` element does not support `rel` or `href` attributes — this is invalid HTML. This is a Klaviyo open-tracking mechanism injected as a stylesheet-mimicking span. While common in Klaviyo sends, it can trigger spam-filter heuristics on stricter MTA rules and will fail HTML validation. A standard 1×1 tracking pixel (`<img>`) is the correct vehicle for open tracking.

**Tracking domain confirmed:** `ctrk.klclick.com` — Klaviyo's CDN. No issues with the domain itself.

**Remaining links not auditable** — HTML is truncated before body links appear.
