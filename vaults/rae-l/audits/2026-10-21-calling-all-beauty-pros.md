# Calling all beauty pros
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-21**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, well-built announcement for ILIA's new Artist Collective, a trade program for makeup artists, skin experts and students. The structure is easy to follow: hero, one-line pitch, a "Who's It For?" tier list, "How To Apply" in three steps, and a repeated CTA. The dark "JOIN THE PROGRAM →" and "APPLY NOW →" buttons are unmistakable.
- It's a segmentation problem, though. The email goes to a general list, and most recipients can't qualify. Eligibility needs proof of professional status or accredited enrollment. For a consumer on this list, the email is a closed door with a pretty frame. Coming right after the 10-16 to 10-19 run of three near-identical VIP gift sends and the 10-20 "New techniques" tutorial, it is at least a fresh beat. The "know a pro? Forward this email" block is the right fallback for non-pros, but it sits near the bottom.
- My judgement is a solid execution of a niche message. It will do well with pros and students and mean little to everyone else.

## What's working

- The three tiers carry concrete perks: 40% for MUAs, 30% for skin experts and 20% for students, each with early launches and events.
- The CTA is clear and repeated twice, with a dark button, a verb and an arrow, so it gets attention against the cream background.
- The "How To Apply" steps (submit, review, account setup) lower the effort of applying.
- The hero's pencil-and-palette still life, the backstage photo and the eye close-up all signal a working artist. The imagery fits the audience.
- The forward-to-a-pro prompt gives non-eligible readers something to do.
- The render looks clean: no broken images, overlaps or odd gaps.

## What's weak

- The subject and hero never state the headline offer. The 40% discount is buried in the third section.
- The accordion-style tier rows have radio-like circle icons that look interactive but aren't. They hint at a tap target that doesn't exist.
- The "Who's It For?" copy is small and gray, so the discount percentages are easy to miss.
- It's a long scroll before the second CTA, and the three-step block gives the most generic copy the most space.
- The "Click HERE for ILIA Promotion Details" fine print is tiny, and the proof-of-eligibility requirement sits mid-email rather than up top.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the discount in the hero and the subject, for example "Up to 40% off for beauty pros". The best offer should be visible before any scroll.
- 2. Bold the percentages in the tier rows, or make each tier a card with a large "40%" numeral. Remove the fake radio icons or make the tiers link to the application.
- 3. Add a one-line eligibility note under the first CTA ("Requires proof of license or enrollment") so non-pros opt out early.
- 4. Move the "know a pro?" forward block up and add a share link. Segment sends so non-pro subscribers get a lighter version.
- 5. Tighten the apply steps to one line each or a single row of three.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Pros get 40% off ILIA`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Artist Collective: 20–40% off for pros`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `MUAs, estheticians + students: apply for early launches and up to 40% off.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Apply in 3 steps. Know a pro? Forward this.`
