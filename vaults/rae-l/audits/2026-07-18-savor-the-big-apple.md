# Savor the Big Apple 🍎
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- "Savor the Big Apple" is Glossier's seventh I ❤️ NY send in roughly ten days — and it shows. The creative is clean (Glossier always delivers on presentation), but this email is trying to accomplish three things at once: sell the I ❤️ NY Balm Dotcom via an editorial section, move limited-edition merch (keychain + tee), and hand out free stickers as a GWP. None of the three gets enough runway to convert. Compare this to the July 14 launch email — which owned a single story, a single hero, and a single CTA, and earned its 8/10 — and the structural problem is obvious.
- The editorial "Take a bite" CTA is clever copy, but it's functionally ambiguous — it reads like flavor marketing, not a purchase prompt. The stickers GWP is the most universally compelling incentive in the whole email, and it's buried below two full sections at the bottom. That's a layout decision that actively suppresses clicks.
- The verdict: well-dressed and on-brand, but overextended. At send seven in the same arc, this needed either a harder offer or a clean editorial pivot — not a three-way split.

## What's working

- Hero section lands immediately — "Savor the Big Apple" + product imagery + I ❤️ NY badge establishes context without effort
- "MERCH IN THE CITY" dual-tile layout is efficient: two products, two "Shop now" CTAs, visually balanced side by side
- "Limited edition" label on both merch items provides mild scarcity without being heavy-handed
- "PSST." label on the stickers section is warm Glossier voice — when a subscriber does scroll that far, it rewards them
- No render bugs; color palette and typography are crisp throughout

## What's weak

- Three competing sections (Balm Dotcom editorial → merch → stickers) dilute attention and give the subscriber no clear dominant action
- "Take a bite" is charming writing but a weak CTA — it describes the product experience rather than directing a purchase
- Merch tiles show no prices; the July 14 launch email priced everything inline, and that context made the decision easier — this email removed it
- Free stickers (the most compelling offer) is buried at the bottom after two full sections, where scroll depth is lowest
- Zero urgency signal across the entire email — no deadline, no "this week only," nothing to close the loop on a subscriber who's seen six near-identical emails already

## Recommendations

- 1. **Surface the GWP in the hero** — A single line under the headline, "Free NYC sticker sheet with any order," would be the highest-lift change in this email. The sticker offer is real value; hiding it wastes it.
- 2. **Pick one primary action and make it product-specific** — The Balm Dotcom is the anchor of this entire arc. Kill the editorial copy block and replace "Take a bite" with "Shop I ❤️ NY Balm Dotcom." Let the product do its job.
- 3. **Put prices back on the merch tiles** — $18 and $32 are impulse-buy prices. Show them. The July 14 email did this; this one regressed.
- 4. **Add a credible deadline to the stickers section** — "Free stickers through Sunday, while supplies last" beats the current vague "while supplies last" copy.
- 5. **Cut to two sections maximum** — Either Balm Dotcom + stickers, or merch + stickers. Three sections is one too many for a send this late in the campaign arc.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free NYC stickers with every order 🍎`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last chance: I ❤️ NY Balm Dotcom + a little gift`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `A sticker sheet so good you'll want to send one.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Invalid tracking element — `<span rel="stylesheet">`**

```html
<span rel="stylesheet" href="https://ctrk.klclick.com/l/01KXS8D17FT31KQN6ABSDD4WEC_0"></span>
```

`rel` and `href` are not valid attributes on `<span>`. This appears to be a Klaviyo open-tracking pixel inserted as a span rather than a proper `<img>` or `<link>` tag. The construct is semantically invalid per HTML/XHTML spec. Some clients will silently ignore it; others may surface it as a broken resource request or strip it entirely, breaking open-rate tracking.

**Expected form:** a 1×1 `<img>` tag, or nothing — Klaviyo's own templates use `<img>` for pixel tracking.

**UTM parameters on destination URLs:** Cannot confirm — the link list is not visible in the truncated source. Verify that all `https://glossier.com/...` destination URLs carry `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` parameters after Klaviyo redirect unwrapping.
