# The Art of the Eye—20% off
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-16**
## Executive summary

- This is the strongest ILIA send in the streak — "for your eyes only," a full styled hero shot with mascara wands fanned on a jewelry-tray backdrop, and a clean maroon/blush palette that finally looks like a real campaign rather than a placeholder. It's still the same VIP-sale mechanic ILIA has run since 09-12, now on its sixth consecutive day, but this time the creative earns it: the "art of the eye" 3-step routine (Shadow Stick → Eyeliner → Mascara) gives the eye something to do beyond "20% off" repeated four times.
- The weak point is the offer structure itself, not the visuals — the 20% is VIP-gated (join VIP to unlock), which adds a click of friction right when the subject promises a flat discount. The gifts-at-checkout module (free multi-stick, free bag at $100+) is a nice incremental hook but is buried two scrolls down, well past where a mobile reader typically stops.
- Verdict: solid execution, real design effort, but sixth-day sale fatigue and the VIP-gate friction cap this below the top tier.

## What's working

- Hero image is genuinely styled — mascara wands on a mirrored tray with jewelry, reinforcing "art of the eye" visually, not just in copy.
- Clear step-by-step product story (Shadow Stick, Eyeliner, Mascara) gives the email a narrative instead of a flat grid.
- "NEW" badge on the Thru Line Eyeliner calls out news within the sale, which helps against promo fatigue.
- Multiple CTA buttons (Join VIP + Shop, Shop 20% Off Eye, Shop the Sale) keep intent-to-click low-friction throughout the scroll.
- VIP perks band (free shipping, points, early access) is compact and legible, reinforcing loyalty value beyond this one sale.

## What's weak

- The 20% off requires VIP membership — subject line reads like a straight discount but the first CTA is "Join VIP + Shop," adding a step.
- This is day six of essentially the same VIP-sale premise (09-12 through today); the subject variety is masking a very repetitive cadence.
- Gift-with-purchase offers (free multi-stick, free bag) are visually interesting (Polaroid-style) but sit far down the email, likely below the fold on mobile.
- Fine print disclaimer block ("offer valid on iliabeauty.com...") is dense and gray, standard but adds visual clutter right before the closing brand block.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the discount mechanic honestly — either say "20% off for VIPs" in the subject, or move VIP enrollment out of the primary path so non-members aren't surprised by a gate after they've already clicked in.
- 2. Pull the gift-with-purchase module up above the 3-step product story — free gifts convert better than routine education and shouldn't require four scrolls.
- 3. Vary the sale framing to fight fatigue — six straight VIP-sale sends needs a different angle (e.g., "last day," a specific product spotlight) rather than another generic art/eye theme.
- 4. Add a countdown or explicit end date near the hero, not just buried in the disclaimer ("Ends 09.22.26") — urgency should be visible without reading fine print.
