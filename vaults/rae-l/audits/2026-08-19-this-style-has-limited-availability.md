# This style has limited availability!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-19**
## Executive summary

- This is a verbatim repeat of the 2026-08-11 send — same subject line ("This style has limited availability!"), same "HOW ABOUT A SECOND LOOK? SEEN THIS BEFORE? WE THOUGHT SO." browse-abandonment header, same hero product (Skechers Garage), same 40% off banner. Nothing has changed in eight days except the calendar date in the fine print (offer now reads 8/14–8/19/26). That's a real problem: a subscriber who opened the 8/11 email either bought the shoe or didn't want it, and this send gives them zero new reason to reconsider.
- The recommendation carousel is also mismatched to the hero: "Skechers Garage" is a boys'/kids toy-car-themed sneaker, but the six "more great options" are almost entirely Care Bears and BOBS women's styles. That's not a "great options you'll love" list tailored to someone who looked at Skechers Garage — it reads like generic fallback inventory, which undercuts the personalization pitch of the whole email.
- Judgment: this is a weak, recycled send propped up by a real 40%-off sitewide offer. The discount alone will get a few clicks, but the browse-abandonment premise is stale and the subject line still overpromises "limited availability" for a product not actually shown as scarce anywhere in the body.

## What's working

- Clean, on-brand layout — logo, blue/black color scheme, and typography are consistent and legible.
- "UP TO 40% OFF SUMMER STYLES" banner is the strongest, clearest visual element on the page — bold numerals, unmissable.
- Utility footer (app download, curbside pickup, buy-now-pay-later, store locator, social) is well organized and functional-looking.
- No render bugs — images load, text doesn't overlap, buttons are crisp.

## What's weak

- Subject line claims "limited availability" but nothing in the email shows stock levels, a countdown, or scarcity messaging — it's a bait for urgency that isn't paid off.
- Recommendation grid doesn't match the hero product's audience (boys' Skechers Garage vs. six Care Bears/BOBS women's/girls' items) — this is the same broken-personalization issue as the 8/11 send.
- This is a byte-for-byte repeat of the "This style has limited availability!" email from 8/11/26, just eight days later — no new creative, no new copy.
- Single hero CTA ("SHOP NOW") for the Skechers Garage item competes with the 40% off "SHOP THE SALE" CTA lower down, splitting attention between two different offers.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the subject/body mismatch first — either add real scarcity signaling (low-stock badge, "only a few left") to the hero product, or drop "limited availability" from the subject entirely and lead with the 40% off, which is the actual offer being made.
- 2. Fix the recommendation logic so the six "more great options" are actually adjacent to the hero (other men's/boys' athletic/graphic styles), not Care Bears and women's BOBS.
- 3. Don't resend the identical creative eight days apart — rotate hero product or at minimum refresh the header copy so repeat openers don't get deja vu.
- 4. Move the 40% off banner above the single-product hero, or merge them, so there's one primary offer instead of two competing CTAs.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 40% off summer styles — today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Still eyeing the Skechers Garage? Here's 40% off.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sitewide summer savings end soon — shop before sizes sell out.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The shoe you checked out, plus 40% off everything else.`
