# More Beauty, Less Spend 💕
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- "More Beauty, Less Spend" is a sprawling summer deals digest — seven distinct modules stacked under one hero, covering leave-in treatments, hair care BOGO, gel polish, a wax warmer bundle, hair color, color-maintenance products, and clippers/trimmers. Structurally it resembles the June 12 "Fresh Finds for Summer" kitchen-sink send that scored a 6, but this version earns more credit: each module has a concrete discount mechanic (20% off, BOGO 50%, buy-two-get-one-free) and the summer throughline holds across categories. The hero image lands cleanly and the Sally+ rewards nudge is well-placed mid-scroll.
- The fatal flaw is the subject line. "More Beauty, Less Spend" is a store-sign platitude with no offer specificity whatsoever — no percentage, no product, no category. Contrast that with June 11's "Hair Care Favorites: Buy One, Get One 50% Off ✨" which front-loaded the mechanic and scored an open-worthy 8. This subject abandons everything that made that send work. A subscriber who has already received six Sally Beauty emails in the past two weeks has no signal here to override inbox fatigue.
- The "all month long" framing in the body is a quiet click-killer — it telegraphs zero urgency and trains the subscriber to wait. The email does a lot of things reasonably well in execution; the strategy is where it undersells itself.

## What's working

- **Hero image and copy are cohesive** — the woman/summer palette matches the "summer looks you love for less" headline, and the red "Shop now" button is prominent.
- **Every deal module has a named mechanic** — 20% off, BOGO 50%, buy 2 get 1 free, and a specific dollar anchor ($39 bundle). This is a meaningful step up from the June 12 editorial send.
- **Sally+ rewards badge mid-scroll** — surfacing the $5/$50 reward threshold inside the promotional flow is smart; it nudges loyalty-tier behavior without a dedicated section.
- **Category breadth is actually reasonable here** — hair care + nail + clippers shares more DNA than June 12's nail + eyeliner + Pride Month mashup.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, footer links intact.

## What's weak

- **Subject is a generic retail cliché** — "More Beauty, Less Spend" names no offer, no category, no mechanic. It reads like the brand went on vacation and left the auto-fill on.
- **"All month long" kills urgency dead** — a subscriber who reads this subhead has zero reason to click today vs. in two weeks.
- **Seven deal modules create decision paralysis** — after the fifth module, eye-tracking drops off and the clippers/trimmers section (a very different customer intent) lands in dead air.
- **"Shop now" CTA repeated verbatim across all modules** — with six distinct categories, generic copy misses a lift opportunity on each individual deal.
- **Cadence context is working against this send** — four Sally Beauty emails in 12 days means the "all month" framing especially compounds fatigue rather than offering relief.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor the subject to the single strongest offer.** The BOGO 50% hair care or the buy-two-get-one gel polish mechanic is far more compelling than "More Beauty, Less Spend." Pick one and lead with it — exactly what June 11 did to earn its 8.
- **Subject Alt A:** `BOGO 50% off hair care — all summer long 💕`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Buy 2 get 1 free gel polish (+ more deals inside)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Hair lighteners, nail colors & more — enjoy these deals all month.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Save on the summer look: hair care, nail, color & more.`
- 2. **Replace "all month long" with a soft mid-month deadline.** "Through June 30" or "while supplies last" restores at least implied urgency without being deceptive.
- 3. **Differentiate CTA copy per module.** "Shop It's a 10" / "Get the BOGO" / "Grab the deal" — three words that tell the subscriber exactly what they're clicking into.
- 4. **Cut or consolidate the clippers/trimmers module.** It's a different buyer intent; it dilutes the summer beauty story and almost certainly drives the lowest CTR of any module. Move it to a dedicated segment or a standalone footer block.
- 5. **Flag the Sally+ earning context inside deal modules**, not just in the standalone badge — "Earn rewards on this BOGO" converts better than a passive mid-scroll reminder.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Font fallback gap — confirmed issue:**
```css
a {
  font-family: 'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif;
}
```
`Founders Grotesk` is referenced as the primary link font, but **no `@font-face` declaration for it exists in the `<head>`**. Only Open Sans variants are declared. In clients that don't load external fonts (Gmail, Outlook, most mobile), this silently falls back to Helvetica — acceptable, but the intent and the implementation are mismatched. If Founders Grotesk is expected to render, it needs either an `@font-face` block or a hosted woff2 URL.

**Outdated Google Fonts version — minor:**
All Open Sans `@font-face` rules reference `opensans/v17/`. Current CDN version is v29+. v17 assets are still served by Google but the `local()` fallback hints (`local('OpenSans-Regular')` etc.) use old PostScript names that no longer match the system font on macOS 13+ / Windows 11. Result: the `local()` short-circuit will miss on modern OS installs, forcing a CDN fetch that could be avoided.

**Mobile breakpoint — minor:**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 499px)
```
Industry standard for email is `600px`. Devices with viewport widths 500–599px (common on small Android phones) will receive the desktop layout. This affects stacking, image scaling, and font-size overrides.

**`img` CSS anomaly:**
```css
img { -ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic; white-space: pre; }
```
`white-space: pre` on `<img>` is a no-op (images are replaced elements) but is technically invalid for this element type. Not a rendering risk, but a template hygiene flag.

**Alt text:** Cannot evaluate without full source — verify all `<img>` tags have non-empty `alt` attributes, especially hero and product images, for screen reader and images-off rendering.
