# Buy 3, Save 20% starts now
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- ILIA pivots hard from last Tuesday's wildfire-resilience brand-values send and Sunday's UGC-driven Super Serum spotlight directly into a transactional bundle promo. The offer itself is mechanically clear — Buy 3, get 20% off with code BUNDLEUP — and the email executes the one-thing-only discipline that the June 28 digest badly lacked. Single CTA, three-step mechanic, clean layout.
- The problem is what's missing: no hero product to anchor the "pick your trio" decision, no social proof, and no specific deadline beyond "limited time only." This ask puts all the discovery work on the subscriber. After ILIA set a high creative bar with the Crown Affair collab and the UGC Super Serum send, a generic bundle promo with no product direction reads like an ops send, not an editorial one. The "the power of ⭐ sale" headline also reads oddly — that phrasing with the star feels like a formatting artifact, not intentional brand voice.
- This is a competent promo email, but it's working against its own cadence. Seven sends in nine days is aggressive, and this one doesn't deliver enough pull to justify its slot.

## What's working

- **Single-offer focus** — the email commits to one mechanic and doesn't clutter it with secondary products the way the June 28 digest did.
- **Code name is memorable** — "BUNDLEUP" is easy to recall and has a playful, phonetic quality.
- **Three-step breakdown** — numbered instructions reduce friction for first-time bundle buyers who might not know the mechanic.
- **High-contrast CTA button** — the red "USE CODE: BUNDLEUP" button is the clearest visual anchor in the email.
- **Perks row at bottom** — 1% for the Planet, Leaping Bunny, and 2VIP loyalty visibility all reinforce trust without requiring explanation.

## What's weak

- **No hero product anchor** — "pick any three items" makes sense as flexibility but offers no starting point; a subscriber has to leave the email to figure out what to buy.
- **Headline copy is awkward** — "the power of ⭐ sale" reads like truncated or improperly formatted text, not brand voice.
- **Asterisk disclaimer** — "*See note for details" below the CTA creates uncertainty about exclusions right at the point of decision.
- **"Limited time only" is non-urgency** — there's no end date, no countdown, no hard boundary; "starts now" is launch framing, not closing urgency.
- **Cadence fatigue** — this is the seventh ILIA email in nine days; even a well-executed promo is going to get scrolled past at this frequency.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Feature 3 curated "starter trios"** — show subscribers three pre-built combos (e.g., a skin-tint trio, a lip trio, a complexion-essentials trio) as tappable shortcuts. Reduce decision work entirely; let them swap if they want. This is the single highest-lift change.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline** — "Offer ends July 6" or "This weekend only" converts "starts now" into real urgency. The June 27 Crown Affair send ("Don't overthink it") scored 9/10 partly on urgency — this needs the same.
- 3. **Rewrite the hero headline** — "The power of ⭐ sale" should be something like "Build your trio. Save 20%." — direct, clean, no emoji artifact.
- 4. **Replace the asterisk with inline clarity** — tell subscribers the one exclusion (if any) right in the body copy. "Excludes sets" is better than an asterisk pointing nowhere visible.
- 5. **Write a real preheader** — the top banner text repeats the subject; an inbox viewer sees "Buy 3, Save 20% starts now / BUY 3, SAVE 20% WITH CODE BUNDLEUP" — that's two lines of the same information.
- **Subject Alt A:** `BUNDLEUP: your 20% off trio, starting now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Build your ILIA trio — 20% off, this week only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Pick any 3 favorites and save — here's your code`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The Skin Tint. The Balm. The third is yours — 20% off all three`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` font loading will fail in Gmail and Outlook**

```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```

Gmail strips `@import` from `<style>` blocks. Outlook ignores them entirely. The fallback stack `"Montserrat", "Helvetica", sans-serif` is correctly defined, so visual degradation is minimal — but custom brand fonts will not render in the two largest desktop/webmail clients.

**Google Fonts API endpoint is outdated**

`fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat` is the legacy v1 endpoint. The current endpoint is `css2` (`fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Montserrat:wght@400&display=swap`). The legacy endpoint still works but may be deprecated without notice.

**Hover state suppressed for all anchor elements**

```css
a:hover { color: #5f4b3c; text-decoration: none }
```

This disables any visual feedback on hover in the browser/web-view render of the email. Not a deliverability issue, but reduces interactivity signal for web-view and HTML preview panes.

**Empty `<title>` tag**

```html
<title> </title>
```

Only a whitespace character. Renders as a blank tab title in the web-view version. Should be set to the subject line or brand name.
