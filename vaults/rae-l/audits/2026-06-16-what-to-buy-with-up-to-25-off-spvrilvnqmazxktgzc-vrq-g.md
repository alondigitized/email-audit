# What to buy with up to 25% off
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-16**
## Executive summary

- This is Glossier's seventh Friends of Glossier Sale email in six days — and the creative team has made the right call by shifting angles from discount-announcement to UGC curation. Instead of the mechanical "Save 25% off" subjects that ran June 11–15, this send asks "What to buy?" and answers with three community members' actual product picks, complete with specific shade names. That's a meaningful editorial pivot. After six consecutive discount banners, it reads like something worth opening.
- The execution is undermined by the format itself: three influencer sections, three "Shop now" buttons pointing to three different product sets, and no single hero product with enough weight to create a pull-toward-cart moment. The subscription close at the bottom ("Start a subscription and lock in 25% off") is a smart conversion angle — but it's buried below a lot of content, and most skimmers will never reach it.
- The single largest gap in this email — and in every email this arc has sent — is the absence of any urgency signal. No end date, no countdown, no "last chance" language. A subscriber who has skipped six previous sends has no reason to click today instead of tomorrow.

## What's working

- **UGC curation format refreshes the arc** — after six consecutive discount banners, community member picks add editorial credibility and break the visual monotony.
- **Shade and variant specificity** — product names like "Skin Tint in G3," "Cloud Paint in Soar," and "Boy Brow in Brown" give the lists real texture that generic product names don't.
- **Diverse casting** — three models with visibly different skin tones, consistent with Glossier's brand identity and inclusive beauty positioning.
- **Subject breaks the "Save X%" pattern** — the question format ("What to buy with up to 25% off") is the first departure from the discount-forward subject this arc has used since June 11.
- **Subscription lock-in offer** — surfacing "Start a subscription and lock in 25% off" is a smart long-term retention play timed well at the tail of a sale.

## What's weak

- **Zero urgency anywhere** — the sale has run for six days with no visible end date, countdown, or "final hours" language. Nothing in this email answers "why click today?"
- **Three competing CTAs fragment intent** — each influencer section has its own "Shop now" button; the reader must self-select before clicking, which adds friction and dilutes conversion focus.
- **No single hero product** — with 12+ SKUs spread across three lists, there's no "this is THE thing to buy" moment that anchors a wavering shopper.
- **Tiny product imagery** — the three images are portrait-style lifestyle shots showing the person, not the products. Visually identifying what you're buying requires reading the text lists.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — seven emails in six days means this send is arriving in inboxes where subscribers have already made a deliberate choice not to open the previous six.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add an end date** — this is the single change that would lift this email the most. "Sale ends Sunday" or "48 hours left" in the subject and hero copy gives fence-sitters a reason to click now vs. never.
- 2. **Collapse to one influencer set** — pick the strongest one and make it the full email. One curated list with a tight "Shop her picks" CTA outperforms three lists with three generic buttons.
- 3. **Make each CTA specific** — "Shop @glossierlovers' routine," "Try her picks" — anything more specific than "Shop now" × 3.
- 4. **Elevate the subscription CTA** — "Lock in 25% off forever" is a differentiated value proposition worth featuring mid-email, not just at the bottom.
- 5. **Add a mini urgency line** — even without a hard date, "These are going fast — sale quantities are limited" creates movement pressure.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Sale ends Sunday — see what our BFFs actually bought`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Their 25%-off picks, your cart (before it's gone)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Three community members. Three routines. All up to 25% off — for 48 more hours.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `We asked our BFFs what to buy. Here's the list.`

## Technical Audit — Glossier "Up to 25% off" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is ESP-sent via Klaviyo (`ctrk.klclick.com`) with standard MSO conditional-comment scaffolding and a responsive CSS media query stack. One non-standard tracking element is present that warrants investigation; the truncated source limits full compliance and link auditing.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Non-standard tracking element — high priority**

```html
<span rel="stylesheet" href="https://ctrk.klclick.com/l/01KV6Z44HR4FTGNAR6P8EZJWBF_0"></span>
```

`rel` and `href` are not valid attributes on a `<span>`. A `<span>` does not trigger network requests, so this element does not function as a stylesheet loader or open-tracking pixel in any compliant email client. The conventional open-tracking mechanism is `<img src="..." width="1" height="1" />`. If this is intended as a tracking trigger, it is silently failing. If it is a rendering artifact from the ESP template system, it is dead weight in the DOM.

**ESP click-tracking domain confirmed:** `ctrk.klclick.com` (Klaviyo). All click links should route through this domain — verify no bare/untracked destination URLs are present in the untruncated source.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Positive findings:**
- Correct XHTML 1.0 Transitional DOCTYPE for email
- `lang="en"` on `<html>` ✓
- `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` present ✓
- MSO conditional comments for Outlook 2007–2019 ✓
- `<o:OfficeDocumentSettings>` with `AllowPNG` and 96dpi ✓
- `format-detection telephone=no` ✓
- Gmail fix class (`.gmail-fix`) present ✓
- Responsive breakpoints at 600px and 384px ✓

**Hover/rollover effects (minor):**

```css
.rollover:hover .rollover-first { display:none !important }
.rollover:hover .rollover-second { display:block !important }
```

`:hover` CSS works only in webmail (Gmail web, Outlook.com). It is non-functional in Outlook desktop, Apple Mail, and most mobile apps. The `rollover-first` state should be the safe fallback visible in all clients — confirm the default (non-hover) state is the intended experience for ~60–70% of opens.

**Alt text:** Not auditable from truncated source — verify all `<img>` tags carry non-empty `alt` attributes, particularly product shots used as CTA drivers.

**Single-letter class names** (`.q`, `.o`, `.n`, etc.) are minified/obfuscated. This is common from ESP builders but makes QA tooling harder to run against specific elements.
