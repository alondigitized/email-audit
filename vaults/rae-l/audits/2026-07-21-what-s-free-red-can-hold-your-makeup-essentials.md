# What’s free, red, + can hold your makeup essentials?
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- This is Glossier's GWP (gift-with-purchase) pivot after eight consecutive days of I ❤️ NY Balm Dotcom sends. The mechanic — free red Mini Beauty Bag with a $75+ order — is the strongest offer Glossier has dropped in this run, and the riddle subject line ("What's free, red, + can hold your makeup essentials?") is clever enough to earn the open. The red bag hero image is clean and immediately answers the riddle, which makes the reveal satisfying rather than frustrating.
- The structural problem is the email tries to do two things at once: sell the GWP mechanic AND push four summer-favorites SKUs below the fold. The GWP has no threshold-building logic attached — no "here's how to hit $75 fast" bundle nudge, no starred products that count toward the minimum. The summer favorites grid (I ❤️ NY Balm Dotcom, Greater You EDP, Invisible Shield SPF, Boy Brow) reads as a re-run of the July 17 "summer flavor catalog" send rather than a curated path to the $75 threshold.
- The GWP mechanic is genuinely useful and the creative is clean. But eight sends in eight days is aggressive, and the grid-at-bottom formula is now familiar enough that loyal subscribers may start skimming past it. This is a solid 7 — the offer saves it from the fatigue of the surrounding cadence.

## What's working

- **The riddle subject delivers on reveal.** Opening the email immediately shows the red bag, making the riddle payoff satisfying and frictionless.
- **Hero GWP offer is above the fold and specific.** "Free Red Mini Beauty with $75+ order" — threshold, product, and CTA all visible without scrolling.
- **The red bag product shot is bold and on-brand.** High contrast against white background; eye goes directly to it.
- **I ❤️ NY Balm Dotcom appearing in the summer grid** is a smart anchor — it's the most-hyped Glossier SKU right now, and it gives subscribers a familiar hook to build a cart toward $75.
- **No render bugs.** Clean grid layout, all images loading, no layout breaks visible.

## What's weak

- **No threshold-building logic.** The email tells you to spend $75+ but never shows you what to put in the cart. A curated "reach $75" bundle or "add these three" recommendation would close that gap.
- **"Shop now" is repeated six times verbatim.** Every CTA in the email uses identical copy — no product-specific verb, no offer hook. It flattens the hierarchy between the hero GWP CTA and the grid SKU links.
- **"Limited time" urgency is vague.** No end date, no countdown, no "only X left." The July 16 merch drop had the same issue; at least that one had implied scarcity from a product launch.
- **The Mini Beauty Bag product callout section below the hero is redundant.** It re-describes the same bag in a second content block with its own "Shop now" — this reads as padding that dilutes the hero's punch.
- **Eight emails in eight days is starting to show.** The summer-favorites grid format appeared in nearly identical form on July 17 and July 18. Returning subscribers will feel the repetition.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Replace the second "Mini Beauty Bag" content block with a $75 threshold builder.** Show three or four specific product combinations that hit exactly $75 — lip balm + mascara + SPF, for example — with a single "Build your bag" CTA. This removes redundancy and turns the GWP offer into a guided conversion path.
- 2. **Differentiate the CTA copy per product.** Hero: "Get the free bag" or "Claim your free bag." Grid SKUs: "Shop Balm Dotcom," "Shop Boy Brow," etc. Specificity lifts click rates; "Shop now" × 6 does not.
- 3. **Add a credible end date to the GWP.** Even "offer ends Sunday" is more clickable than "for a limited time."
- 4. **Consider retiring or rotating the four-product grid format** — it's appeared in three of the last five sends. A single hero SKU with a strong story would break the pattern usefully.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your free red bag is waiting (just spend $75)`
- **Subject Alt B:** `We're giving something away — and it's red`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The Mini Beauty Bag is free with your $75 order. Limited time.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Claim yours before it's gone — free with $75+`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Confirmed issue — non-standard tracking element:**
```html
<span rel="stylesheet" href="https://ctrk.klclick.com/l/01KY3B4V4AAJ8W7MBJ5K980MHZ_0"></span>
```
`<span>` does not support `rel` or `href` attributes in any HTML/XHTML spec. This is a Klaviyo open-tracking pixel injected via an invalid element. It functions in most clients through permissive parsing, but strict XHTML renderers (some older corporate clients) may drop it silently, causing open-tracking loss. Standard practice is a 1×1 `<img>` tracking pixel instead.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issue — preheader character stuffing:**
```html
<span style="display:none !important;...">Open to find out ͏ ͏ ͏ ... ­ ­ ­ ...</span>
```
The preheader uses a mix of U+034F (Combining Grapheme Joiner) and U+00AD (soft hyphen) as spacer characters. Some spam filters (SpamAssassin, Proofpoint) assign negative scores to high densities of these Unicode control characters. The soft-hyphen run is particularly aggressive. A safer padding technique is `&zwnj;` (U+200C) or `&nbsp;` only.

**Confirmed — proper Outlook mitigations present:**
- MSO `PixelsPerInch`, `AllowPNG`, `DontUseAdvancedTypographyReadingMail` all present.
