# Your fall palette
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-16**
## Executive summary

- This is Glossier finally doing straightforward seasonal merchandising — no discount hook, no urgency, just a clean "here's the fall lip/blush lineup" catalog send organized into Sultry Reds and Cozy Browns categories. Compared to the brand-history run of Labor Day repeats (9/3–9/8) and the two single-SKU no-discount pushes (Boy Brow 9/13, You Doux fragrance 9/15), this is a broader, more useful send — it's an actual assortment story with eight products, tight macro-beauty photography, and a real seasonal narrative ("sultry reds," "cozy browns").
- The execution is strong on craft — consistent close-up lip/eye macro shots, one clear hero model up top, a "Get them all" CTA — but it's a pure product-and-price-absent catalog with zero offer, zero urgency, and no differentiation from what any beauty brand sends every September. For someone already primed by three straight no-discount sends this week (Boy Brow, You Doux, and now this), fatigue on "no deal here" emails is a real risk even though the visual execution is the best of the bunch.
- Judgement: well-crafted seasonal merchandising, but with no offer, no urgency, and a cadence that's now three non-discount sends in four days, it will underperform on clicks relative to its production quality.

## What's working

- Clear visual hierarchy: hero banner up top with one model, headline, subhead, and single CTA button before any scrolling clutter.
- Consistent, high-quality macro photography throughout — every lip/eye shot is sharp, well-lit, and on-brand.
- Logical categorization (Sultry Reds / Cozy Browns) makes the eight-product grid easy to scan rather than an undifferentiated wall of products.
- Each product tile pairs a clear name, a one-line shade description ("A deep burgundy," "Roasted coffee + mocha tint"), and its own "Shop now" button — no ambiguity about what to click.
- Diverse model casting across the tiles (varied skin tones, hair textures) feels authentic rather than tokenistic.

## What's weak

- No offer anywhere in the email — no %, no bundle price, no free-gift threshold — a pure full-price catalog dump.
- Headline "Your fall palette" is generic seasonal copy that could belong to any beauty brand's September send.
- No urgency or scarcity signal at all, and given this is the third no-discount, no-urgency Glossier send in four days (Boy Brow 9/13, You Doux 9/15), open fatigue is a real risk.
- Eight products with eight identical "Shop now" buttons dilutes the primary CTA — there's no single hero product to click after the top banner.
- No social proof, ratings, or bestseller callouts anywhere in the grid to help someone choose among 8 near-identical shade options.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a hero anchor offer or bundle price (e.g., "Buy 2, get 15% off the Fall Palette") — right now there's nothing pulling a full-price shopper toward checkout after three straight non-discount sends this week.
- 2. Pick one flagship SKU (e.g., Ultralip in Cranberry, which is already featured on the model) and make it the singular CTA in the hero, then let the grid below serve as secondary "shop the edit" — reduces the eight-competing-buttons problem.
- 3. Add a "Bestseller" or "New" badge on at least one or two tiles to help differentiation and drive social proof — right now every product reads with equal weight.
- 4. Tighten the subject/headline to be more concrete than "fall palette" — name the shade story directly.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New: Ultralip in Cranberry + 7 more fall shades`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Sultry reds, cozy browns — fall lips are here`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `8 new shades, from crimson-berry to mocha brown.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Fidan's wearing Cranberry. See the full lineup.`
