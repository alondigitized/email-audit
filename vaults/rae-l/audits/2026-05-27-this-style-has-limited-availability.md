# This style has limited availability!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- This is a browse/cart abandonment email wearing an urgency costume. The subject line — "This style has limited availability!" — promises scarcity messaging about a specific product, but the body delivers a completely generic "HOW ABOUT A SECOND LOOK? SEEN THIS BEFORE? WE THOUGHT SO." browse-abandonment template. There is no copy in the body that names a style, quantifies availability, or backs up the subject's claim. The hero product is labeled "Skechers Garage" — a product line, not a scarce individual SKU — and the immediately-following grid of six additional recommendations actively contradicts the limited-stock premise.
- Context makes this worse: this is the **fifth Skechers email in nine days** and the **third in two days**, following two cart-abandonment sends on 5/26 alone. The cadence has long since crossed into subscriber fatigue territory. The offer has also regressed — 20% off a single pair, down from the BOGO 50% that ran on 5/11 — which, for anyone tracking the sequence, signals "wait for a better deal."
- The bones of the template are clean, the CTA is visible, and the layout renders properly. But the subject-body mismatch is a trust issue, not just a copywriting miss.

## What's working

- **Render is clean** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no layout bugs; the email displays crisply.
- **20% off offer is clearly stated** in a prominent banner module — the value prop is unambiguous.
- **SHOP NOW CTA is visible twice** (hero and offer banner) — no hunting required.
- **Hero visual hierarchy holds** — the product image and headline land first before the utility modules below.

## What's weak

- **Subject-body mismatch destroys trust** — "This style has limited availability!" is backed by zero scarcity copy in the body; the hero says "HOW ABOUT A SECOND LOOK?" with no urgency reinforcement.
- **No product named as the scarce style** — the subject implies one SKU is running out; the body shows "Skechers Garage" (a category) plus six alternatives. The signals are incoherent.
- **Cadence is broken** — this is send #5 in 9 days, #3 in 24 hours; a subscriber paying attention would already have tuned out or unsubscribed.
- **Offer is weaker than recent sends** — 20% off one pair vs. BOGO 50% off on 5/11; a savvy shopper reads the sequence and waits.
- **Top-of-email leaking junk** — "685-97285" is visible as the first text element in the render, suggesting a tracking/customer ID is bleeding into the preheader slot rather than real copy.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Earn the urgency or drop it.** Either name the specific SKU running low (with actual stock count: "Only 3 left in your size") or retire the "limited availability" hook entirely. Bait-and-switch subjects erode the brand's sender reputation with real subscribers more than any deliverability metric.
- 2. **Bring the body copy into alignment with the subject.** If this is a browse-abandonment trigger, say so cleanly: "You looked. We noticed. Here's 20% off before it sells out." Don't bury the cart-connection under vague "second look" framing.
- 3. **Surface the specific browsed product prominently** — name it, show it large, link directly to the PDP. Six equally-sized recommendations dilute the relevance signal.
- 4. **Suppress this send entirely after two same-day sends.** No suppression logic firing after yesterday's double-send on 5/26 is a list-hygiene and trust problem.
- 5. **Fix the preheader** — "685-97285" should never be subscriber-facing.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Only a few pairs left — grab yours`
- **Subject Alt B:** `[Product name]: almost gone`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `20% off before your size disappears`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `You viewed it. Here's a reason to come back.`
