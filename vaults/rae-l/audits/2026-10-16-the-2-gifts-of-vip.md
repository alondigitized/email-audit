# The (2) gifts of VIP
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-16**
## Executive summary

- This is a gift-with-purchase email wrapped around a VIP loyalty push. The hero is strong: a velvet makeup bag and a mini mascara against a blue sky and palm fronds, with a clean "The Gifts of VIP" headline. The red announcement bar and the "From: us. To: you." line read like ILIA. The first CTA, "JOIN VIP + CLAIM GIFTS →", is a full-width red button that is easy to spot.
- The offer is confusing, though. The top bar and the "a free lift" block say a free mini mascara comes with any $75+ order. The body copy says a mini mascara and the velvet bag come with any $100+ VIP order. Both thresholds are asterisked, with "See site for details" below. A subscriber can't tell what they have to spend. The email also contradicts its own subject line: the subject says "(2) gifts", while the bottom half only promotes one gift, the mascara.
- After the hero, the email becomes a generic six-product grid with soft "shop now" outline buttons. That's a lot of scrolling for a send with a single promotion. After the 10-10 "Buy 3, save 20%" send (7/10) and the 10-13 "20% off ends in 3, 2, 1..." send (7/10), this one feels more muddled. It is a better-looking email than the 10-11 plain-text nudge, but the offer is less clear than the BUNDLEUP sends were.

## What's working

- The hero image is polished and shows both gifts, the mascara and the velvet bag, so the incentive is visible immediately.
- The red announcement bar carries the offer and a "SHOP NOW" link at the very top.
- "From: us. To: you." is a short, warm line that fits ILIA's voice.
- The two full-width red CTAs are high-contrast and easy to find.
- The VIP badge in the hero and the VIP icon in the footer tie the email to the loyalty program.

## What's weak

- Two offers conflict: $75+ for a mini mascara in the bar and the bottom block, versus $100+ for the mascara plus bag in the body.
- The subject promises "(2) gifts", but the second half of the email only shows the mascara gift. The "Mini Mascara Gift" badge reinforces the single-gift story.
- The six-product grid has no prices, shades or reviews, so the "shop now" buttons carry no information. Their outline style is also much weaker than the red CTAs.
- The footer is crowded with sustainability badges, an auto-replenish icon and a second VIP block. These dilute the one message.
- There is no deadline or end date, so nothing makes the reader act today. The terms are asterisked and sent to the site.

## Recommendations

- 1. Use one threshold and one clear offer. For example, "Free velvet bag + mini mascara with $100+ for VIPs", with the same line in the bar, the hero and the footnote. If the gift is really two-tier, show it as two tiers.
- 2. Add a real deadline, such as "Ends Sunday", in the bar and under the primary CTA.
- 3. Cut the grid from six products to two or three. Show prices and a star rating, and link the mascara tile straight to the gift product page.
- 4. Delete the repeated "a free lift" block, or fold it into the hero so the mascara isn't promoted twice.
- 5. Move the VIP join explanation near the top, with one line on what VIP costs and gives. "Join VIP" is a commitment, and the email never says what it is.
- 6. Make the shop-now buttons filled or underlined, so the product links look as clickable as the hero CTAs.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your VIP gifts end Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Spend $100, get a velvet makeup bag and a mini Limitless Lash Mascara free.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Two free gifts for VIPs, plus a mini mascara for everyone at $75.`
