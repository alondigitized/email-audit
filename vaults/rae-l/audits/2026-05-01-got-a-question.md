# Got a question?
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-01**
## Executive summary

- This is a service-nurture email, not a promotional blast. Glossier is positioning its gTEAM support function as a human, high-touch shopping companion rather than a ticket queue. The design is clean, on-brand, and well-structured — but the subject line undersells the actual value proposition, and the two-column layout creates a slight tension between "shop now" and "talk to us" without clearly prioritizing either path.
- Solid brand reinforcement, low conversion pressure. Good for retention and reducing purchase anxiety among new or hesitant buyers — but unlikely to drive immediate revenue on its own.

## What's working

- **Brand coherence.** Minimal layout, muted pink/nude palette, playful emoji icons (smiley, wave) in the footer — completely on-brand Glossier. Nothing feels out of place.
- **Dual-purpose structure makes sense.** Pairing "We're here to help" (discovery/shopping) with "Not your average support team" (gTEAM contact) creates a logical 1-2: browse or ask. The two CTA buttons ("Shop now" / "Contact us") are differentiated and unambiguous.
- **Product image carries weight.** The hero product shot (pink bottle + secondary products) is well-composed and reinforces the tangible, sensory quality Glossier is known for.
- **gTEAM copy is effective.** "Not your average support team" is actually a stronger hook than the subject line. Calling out "product tips, shade matches, and dream routine lineups" makes the support offering feel genuinely useful rather than defensive.

## What's weak

- **Subject line does nothing.** "Got a question?" is generic enough to belong to any brand. It doesn't signal Glossier, doesn't tease the offer, and doesn't earn the open on its own merits.
- **Top banner is partially cut off** in the render, suggesting a discount code offer (reads "Enjoy 10% off your first order with c…") — but the truncation means the full value prop is invisible without scrolling or resizing. This is a real friction point.
- **No personalization visible.** No first name, no reference to past purchases or browsing behavior. For a brand that talks about meeting you where you are, the content feels generic.
- **CTA hierarchy is flat.** Both "Shop now" and "Contact us" get equal visual weight. The email doesn't signal which action is primary — this diffuses intent.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject line** to tease the gTEAM value prop specifically — something like "Your personal Glossier guide is waiting" or "We'll build your routine for you."
- 2. **Fix the top banner truncation.** If there's a first-order discount code visible, that's actually the most compelling hook in the email — lead with it, don't bury it in a clipped banner.
- 3. **Elevate gTEAM as the hero.** The right-column copy ("Not your average support team") is the most differentiated message here. Consider leading the email with that, and letting "Shop now" be secondary.
- 4. **Add one specific example** of what gTEAM does — e.g., "Not sure which serum is right for your skin type? We'll tell you." Specificity converts.
