# Your Sally favorites, now 20% off
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-04**
## Executive summary

- This is a solid, clean Labor Day sitewide sale email — but the subject line oversells it. "Your Sally favorites, now 20% off" promises personalization; the actual hero is a generic "20% off sitewide" with code LABORDAY20, and the product grid below (ion Perfection Booster, Wella T18 toner, tint brush) reads like generic best-sellers, not "your" history. That's a bait-and-switch on expectations, even if a small one.
- On execution, it's clean: no render bugs, a clear headline offer, a real code and deadline (Ends September 7), a warm on-brand hero photo, and a strong secondary hook (Sally Rewards multiplier tiers) further down. Compared to 9/3's "September deals are here!" (7/10, seasonal reset with a warm hero) this is a similar tier of craft, but the false-personalization subject line drags it down versus that send's more honest framing.
- Verdict: a competently built, generic sitewide promo wearing a personalized subject line it doesn't earn — solid mid-tier send.

## What's working

- Hero is unambiguous: "20% off sitewide," bold code LABORDAY20, and a hard deadline ("Ends September 7") all visible above the fold.
- Clear black CTA button ("Shop now") with good contrast against the cream background.
- Diverse, well-lit lifestyle photo (two women, Sally-branded shopping bags) reinforces brand identity without feeling stock-generic.
- Fine print under the hero ("Cannot combine with other discounts. Excludes clearance.") is visible and honest, not buried in footer legalese.
- Rewards multiplier block (2x/3x/4x points) gives loyalty members a second, escalating reason to shop.

## What's weak

- Subject line promises personalized "favorites" but the email delivers a generic sitewide sale — no dynamic product recommendations tied to purchase history are visible.
- The product grid (ion, Wella items) has no visible connection to "your" past purchases — captions are just generic product names, undermining the subject's personalization claim.
- Two competing header bars ("Buy online, pick up in-store" / "Free 2 hour delivery") above the hero dilute the primary 20%-off message before the reader even reaches it.
- No urgency signal reinforced near the product grid or rewards block — the September 7 deadline appears once and isn't repeated.

## Recommendations

- 1. Rewrite the subject to match the actual offer — drop the false personalization since the body doesn't deliver it: either make the subject match the real hero ("20% off sitewide for Labor Day") or actually personalize the grid with the recipient's purchase history to earn the "favorites" framing.
- 2. Trim the two promo bars above the hero (BOPIS / 2-hour delivery) or move them below the fold — they compete with the main 20%-off message for the first glance.
- 3. Repeat the September 7 deadline near the rewards block to keep urgency present as the reader scrolls.
- 4. Add a one-line "based on your recent purchases" tag over the product grid if this send is meant to feel personalized — otherwise relabel that section "Bestsellers" to stop implying personalization it doesn't have.
- **Subject Alt A:** `20% off sitewide: Labor Day starts now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `LABORDAY20: 20% off everything, ends 9/7`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Use code LABORDAY20 at checkout — sitewide, through September 7.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus: earn up to 4x Rewards points during the sale.`
