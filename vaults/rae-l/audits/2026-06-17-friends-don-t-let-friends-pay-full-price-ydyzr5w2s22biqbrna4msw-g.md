# Friends don't let friends pay full price
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-17**
## Executive summary

- This is day eight of the Friends of Glossier Sale arc — and Glossier finally broke the percentage-in-subject-line pattern that has defined every send since June 11. "Friends don't let friends pay full price" is the creative high point of this arc's subject lines: cultural reference, 40 characters, zero spam signals, and a genuine departure from six consecutive sends with explicit discount callouts. That alone earns the open.
- Inside, the email is doing three things at once: a "STILL ON" urgency hero, a UGC-led social proof section ("BELIEVE THE HYPE"), and a new subscription lock-in angle at the close. The dollar-value anchor ("SAVE $87" on the Glossier You 150ml) is the most concrete offer the arc has surfaced since launch — more compelling than abstract percentages. The subscription pitch ("Subscribe to your favorites and lock in 25%") introduces a genuine loyalty hook that no prior send in this arc used.
- The weak point is persistent: every CTA in this email reads "Shop now." After eight days, subscribers who haven't converted need a more specific reason and a harder close. "Not for long" and "very, very limited time" have appeared in every send this arc and now feel like boilerplate. A real end date would convert more.

## What's working

- Subject line breaks the pattern — "Friends don't let friends pay full price" is the most creative departure in the entire FOG Sale arc and will stand out after a week of percentage-heavy subjects.
- "SAVE $87" dollar-value callout on the Glossier You 150ml is the sharpest, most concrete offer signal in any email this arc.
- "BELIEVE THE HYPE" section with star ratings and real review excerpts gives fence-sitters the social proof push they've been missing.
- Subscription lock-in ("Subscribe to your favorites and lock in 25%") is a brand-new value prop not used in any prior arc send — a smart close.
- Clean render throughout; no broken images, no layout drift, consistent Glossier aesthetic.

## What's weak

- Every CTA reads "Shop now" — five or six repetitions of the same generic copy, with zero specificity to the product or offer beneath it.
- Vague urgency language ("not for long," "very, very limited time") without a specific end date has appeared in every send this arc and now reads like a template artifact, not a real deadline.
- The email is long: subscribers must scroll past hero, UGC, savings grid, and subscription pitch. On mobile, the close risks getting skipped.
- After eight days of back-to-back Glossier sale emails, cadence fatigue is real; this should have landed harder as a definitive "last chance" frame.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a specific sale end date** — "Ends Sunday at midnight" is more credible and more urgent than any amount of "not for long" copy. One line in the hero would lift conversion.
- 2. **Differentiate the CTA copy** — Minimum: rename the subscription CTA "Lock In 25%" and the savings-section CTA "Shop Glossier You." "Shop now" five times is a missed conversion lever.
- 3. **Trim to three sections** — Hero offer → One hero product savings callout → Subscription close. Cutting the UGC grid shortens scroll depth without sacrificing social proof (move one or two star ratings into the hero).
- 4. **Frame this as a last-chance send** — After eight days, the creative should signal arc closure: "Last day," "Final hours," or "This is the last time" would be honest and more motivating.
- **Subject Alt A:** `25% off ends Sunday — save before it's gone`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last call: friends don't pay full price`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Lock in 25% on every future order before this sale closes.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Sale ends Sunday — grab Glossier You at the biggest savings of the year.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Invalid tracking element in `<body>`:**
```html
<span rel="stylesheet" href="https://ctrk.klclick.com/l/01KV9E2S3FM8ZWZFEZ7FMAN8RA_0"></span>
```
`rel` and `href` are not valid attributes on `<span>`. No email client will fetch this as a stylesheet, and no standard rendering engine will treat it as a click beacon. If Klaviyo intends this as a stylesheet injection (e.g., web fonts), it will silently fail in all major clients. If it's a tracking ping, it will only fire in webmail environments where Klaviyo's own JavaScript is executing — which is non-standard and unreliable. This element should be a `<link>` in `<head>` if it's a stylesheet, or removed if it's vestigial.

**Tracking domain:** All outbound links route through `ctrk.klclick.com` (Klaviyo standard). Consistent — no mixed tracking domains detected in visible source.
