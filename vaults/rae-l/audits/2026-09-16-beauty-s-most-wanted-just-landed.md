# Beauty’s most wanted just landed 🤩
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-16**
## Executive summary

- Same bait-and-switch structure the brand history has flagged repeatedly (2026-09-14 "K18 serum" send, 6/10; 2026-09-14 "BIG NEWS" send, 6/10): the subject "Beauty's most wanted just landed 🤩" promises a specific new-arrival drop, but the very first thing that loads is yet another "50% off Select Beauty" flash-sale banner (First Aid Beauty, Sephora Collection, Fenty Beauty). The actual "most wanted" content — a Charlotte Tilbury setting powder launch and a "Beauty's Biggest Hits" bestsellers grid — doesn't show up until well into the scroll.
- Once you get past the top banner, this is actually a decent editorial send: a clean hero ("Beauty's Biggest Hits"), a genuinely new-product spotlight (Charlotte Tilbury Airbrush Flawless Blur powder, "ONLY AT SEPHORA"), and a well-organized bestseller grid with prices visible. But it's buried under a sale banner that has nothing to do with the subject line, plus two more offers stacked below (15% off shampoo/conditioner, 5x points, Kérastase 24-hour sets) — this is the fourth or fifth distinct promo mechanism competing for attention in one email.
- Net: content quality is fine, sequencing is not. This is a stitched-together digest wearing a curiosity-driven subject line, and subscribers who've gotten three of these this week will recognize the pattern.

## What's working

- Clean, high-production hero image and copy for "Beauty's Biggest Hits" — clear headline, clear CTA button.
- The Charlotte Tilbury feature reads like real editorial content: product name, benefit copy, price, and exclusivity ("ONLY AT SEPHORA") all visible.
- Bestseller grid is well laid out — product photo, brand, name, price for all 8 items, no broken images.
- No render bugs — images, prices, and buttons all display correctly throughout the long scroll.

## What's weak

- Subject promises "most wanted just landed" (new arrivals) but hero banner is a generic 50% off sale — mismatch repeats the exact pattern flagged in the 2026-09-14 K18 send.
- Too many competing offers in one email: 50% off sale, Biggest Hits grid, Charlotte Tilbury launch, 15% off subscribe, 5x points, Kérastase sets — no single clear priority.
- The thing the subject actually references (new arrivals / Charlotte Tilbury) is three scrolls deep, past the unrelated sale banner.
- "Spot it. Shop it." section header is vague filler copy that adds nothing.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the Charlotte Tilbury / new-arrivals content to the top of the email so the hero matches the subject line — this is the single biggest fix, since right now the open converts into a bait-and-switch.
- 2. Cut the email down to one primary offer. If the 50% off sale is the real business priority, put it in the subject line instead ("50% off select beauty is back"); if new arrivals is the priority, drop the sale banner or move it below the fold.
- 3. Trim the number of separate promo mechanisms — 6 different offers in one send trains subscribers to skim past all of them.
- 4. Replace "Spot it. Shop it." with copy that actually names what's in the grid.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New from Charlotte Tilbury: the setting powder everyone wants`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Beauty's biggest hits, just restocked`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus: 50% off Select Beauty, today only`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The Charlotte Tilbury powder we can't stop talking about`
