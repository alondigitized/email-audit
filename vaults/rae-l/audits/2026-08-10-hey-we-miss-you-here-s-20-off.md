# Hey, we miss you. Here's 20% off.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-10**
## Executive summary

- This is a genuine win-back email, and it's a departure from Skechers' recent playbook — instead of another manufactured cart-abandonment/urgency send, this one honestly labels itself "Hello, Stranger!" and leads with a real, unambiguous incentive: 20% off the entire order with a visible code. That's a meaningfully different beat than the last six sends in this brand history, all of which leaned on fake scarcity ("limited availability," "selling fast," "cart expires").
- The offer is clear and the CTA is repeated twice, but the hero image is a collage of product close-ups (a pant cuff, a sneaker, shoe soles) with no clear single hero product, and the copy above the fold ("we've updated our collections with new looks and even more comfort") is generic filler that doesn't earn a click on its own — the 20% off is what will actually drive action, and it doesn't appear until a full scroll past the hero.
- Overall this is a solid, honest re-engagement email that breaks the urgency-fatigue pattern flagged in prior audits, but it buries its best asset (the discount) below an underwhelming hero.

## What's working

- Real, quantifiable offer (20% off entire order) with a visible, legible promo code — no bait-and-switch.
- "Hello, Stranger!" framing honestly signals this is a win-back email rather than dressing up a browse-nudge as urgency, which is a break from the pattern flagged in the 7/25 and 7/21 audits.
- Two clear "SHOP NOW" CTAs (hero + offer block), both high-contrast navy buttons.
- Clean secondary navigation (Women/Men/Kids/Clothing/New Arrivals/Sale) gives multiple entry points if the main offer doesn't land.
- No render bugs — no overlapping text, broken images, or layout breakage visible.

## What's weak

- The hero collage doesn't feature one clear hero product — it's a pant leg, a sneaker in profile, and two shoe soles, none of which pop or tell a styling story.
- The 20% off headline offer is stacked below the hero fold, so on mobile a scroller sees "Hello, Stranger" filler copy before the actual reason to open the email.
- "GIVE US ANOTHER TRY WITH THIS EXCLUSIVE OFFER" is a slightly apologetic, low-energy line for a 20%-off headline moment.
- No product recommendations or "here's what's new" imagery tied to the win-back message — it's generic collage + code, no personalization signal.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the 20% off code into the hero itself, replacing or overlaying the product collage, so the offer is visible without scrolling — this is the single biggest lever here since the current hero spends prime real estate on mood shots instead of the discount.
- 2. Swap the collage for one or two clear hero products (e.g. a bestseller or new-arrival silhouette) so there's a concrete visual anchor, not just fragments.
- 3. Tighten the eyebrow copy — "GIVE US ANOTHER TRY" reads apologetic; something like "WELCOME BACK — HERE'S 20% OFF" is more confident and matches the code that follows.
- 4. Add a countdown or explicit expiration date near the code (the fine print says "valid through September 24, 2026" but that's buried in 8pt legal text) — surfacing a real date near the CTA would add credible urgency without resorting to the fake-scarcity language flagged in past audits.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
Standard SFMC-served promotional/win-back email with functional tracking pixels and links, but it fails plain-text MIME requirements and lacks verifiable unsubscribe/authentication headers, plus nearly all embedded images load over unencrypted HTTP.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- Open-tracking pixel present and correctly formed: `https://click.emails.skechers.com/open.aspx?XEMWE4U2GYYEZCF33AWX3XONUI.100227&d=100227&bmt=0`.
- Multiple third-party tracking pixels fire on open: Krux/Salesforce DMP user-match pixels (`beacon.krxd.net/1x1_usermatch.gif`, x2), a Krux ad-impression pixel, a Return Path/Validity pixel (`pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif` — note: **missing `http://`/`https://` scheme**, rendering as a relative/invalid URL in most clients), and a MovableInk personalization pixel (`ink1000.com/p/up/...`).
- The Return Path pixel `src="pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?r=..."` has no protocol prefix — this will not resolve in standard email clients and should be `https://pixel.app.returnpath.net/...`.
- 29 outbound tracking/click-redirect links detected in the body (skipped for HTTP probing per QA policy — expected behavior for `click.emails.skechers.com` redirect domains, not a defect itself).

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 10 of 12 body/footer images missing `alt` text (e.g., `o.gif`, `49468f73-...png`, `00100b23-...png`, `fc08601a-...png`), impacting screen-reader users and broken-image fallback legibility.
- Dark-mode media query (`@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)`) contains a malformed comment: `/*Insert all your dark mode classes here, as you would insert mobile responsive classes./` — the closing `*/` is missing before the next comment opens, using a stray `/` instead. This is inside a `<style>` block and will either break the dark-mode rule or, depending on parser, swallow subsequent CSS until the next legitimate `*/` is found. Recommend auditing rendered output in a dark-mode client (e.g., Apple Mail dark mode) to confirm no downstream style corruption.
- Responsive breakpoints (`@media only screen and (max-width: 640px)`) and Outlook conditional comments (`<!--[if gte mso 9]>`) are present and structured correctly.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No visible unresolved merge/personalization tags (e.g., `%%FirstName%%`, `{{...}}`) in the truncated source — no issues found.
- MovableInk dynamic-content pixel is present (`mi_u=8108776175&mi_ecmp=TRG_US_EN_WINBACKENGAGE_1_06302026_A`), indicating personalized/dynamic image content elsewhere in the (truncated) body that wasn't fully visible for review — recommend confirming fallback creative renders correctly if MovableInk fails to resolve.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **[WARN]** `List-Unsubscribe` header not detected — could be a true absence or an artifact of the AgentMail relay not surfacing it; needs confirmation against raw SMTP headers at the source MTA.
- **[WARN]** `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe) not detected — same caveat; if genuinely absent, this is a Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender requirement gap as of their 2024 sender guidelines.
- **[WARN]** `Authentication-Results` header (SPF/DKIM/DMARC) not present in the captured headers — cannot confirm authentication passed; likely a relay-visibility gap rather than a sending-domain issue, but should be verified directly against `emails.skechers.com` DNS/DKIM records.
- No visible unsubscribe link or physical-address footer in the truncated HTML — cannot confirm CAN-SPAM footer compliance from the available source; flagged as unverified rather than failing, since content was truncated.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Campaign identifiers are consistent across tracking systems: `TRG_US_EN_WINBACKENGAGE_1_06302026_A` appears in both the Krux ad-impression pixel and MovableInk pixel, indicating consistent campaign tagging.
- Actual click-through/CTA destination URLs were not visible in the truncated source (all 29 links were redirect-domain and skipped by the probe) — cannot confirm UTM parameters or landing-page alignment without the untruncated HTML or a resolved redirect target.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Fix the malformed CSS comment in the `prefers-color-scheme: dark` block (`.../* Remember to use...` → ensure `*/` terminates the first comment before the next `/*` opens).
2. Migrate all image and pixel assets from `http://` to `https://` — 9 distinct assets currently load over plain HTTP (`image.emails.skechers.com`, `image.emails.nl.skechers.com`, `www.ink1000.com`), risking client-side blocking (Outlook/Gmail flag mixed content) and broken images.
3. Fix the protocol-less Return Path pixel URL (`pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif...` → prepend `https://`).
4. Add `alt` attributes to all 10 flagged images, particularly the App Store/Google Play badges and social icons, for accessibility and image-blocked fallback.
5. Add a non-empty plain-text MIME part — current plain-text fallback is 0 chars, which hurts deliverability/spam scoring and violates basic multipart-alternative best practice.
6. Verify `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results` (SPF/DKIM/DMARC) directly against raw headers at the sending MTA, since the QA tool's relay may not surface them even if present — do not treat as confirmed failures without that check.
7. Provide untruncated HTML in a future audit pass to verify CTA URLs carry correct UTM parameters and resolve to the intended landing page.
