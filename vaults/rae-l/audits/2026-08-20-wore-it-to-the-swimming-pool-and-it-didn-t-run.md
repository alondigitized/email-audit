- This is ILIA leaning fully into its "prove it" playbook for Thru Line — no manifesto, no editorial profile, just four stat cards (100%, 98%, 96%, 90%) stacked against skin-tone close-ups of the eyeliner in use, capped with a handwritten-style testimonial ("no tugging and lasts all day"). It's the cleanest, most disciplined execution in this arc since the 08-14 "This is your shade" send — single product, single claim (waterproof), repeated with escalating proof points instead of scattered messaging.
- The gap between subject and body is the real issue. "Wore it to the swimming pool and it didn't run" promises a pool-specific anecdote or testimonial hero — the render delivers stat cards and a generic "24-hour wear / waterproof + sweatproof" claim instead. There's no pool imagery, no swim context anywhere in the body. It's a bait-and-switch of tone: punchy, personal subject line married to a clinical proof-point layout.

## What's working

- Proof stacking (100%/98%/96%/90%) with footnoted methodology gives the waterproof claim real credibility instead of just asserting it.
- Close-up product photography on real skin at each stat card ties the number directly to a visible use-case (application, blend, all-day wear, no smudge).
- The closing testimonial callout ("no tugging and lasts all day") in a handwritten-style font adds a human voice after four data-heavy screens.
- Clear CTA ladder — "SHOP THRU LINE" up top, "SHOP THE EYELINER" mid-scroll, "PAINT, SKETCH, FRAME" for the cross-sell — gives multiple exit points without cluttering any single screen.
- 4X VIP points banner at the very top gives loyalty members an immediate reason to stay.

## What's weak

- Zero pool/swim imagery or anecdote despite the subject line explicitly promising one — the strongest hook in the send is abandoned in the body.
- No discrete offer (%, $, BOGO) anywhere in the visible body; the only near-offer is the boilerplate "free shipping over $50" buried in the footer.
- The email is long and repetitive — four near-identical stat-card screens before you reach the testimonial or second product section, which risks fatigue on mobile scroll.
- "The Bottom Line" header is a pun that doesn't clarify what's coming (proof points vs. a summary/offer) — weaker orientation than a direct headline would give.

## Recommendations

- 1. Open with an actual pool visual or short quote card matching the subject line — a wet-look eye shot with the testimonial text overlaid would pay off the hook immediately instead of four screens later.
- 2. Move the testimonial ("no tugging and lasts all day") to the top, right under the hero, then use the stat cards as supporting evidence beneath it — testimonial-first performs better as an opening hook than stat-first.
- 3. Cut two of the four stat cards — 100%/98%/96%/90% is overkill; two strong proof points plus the testimonial say the same thing faster.
- 4. Add one concrete incentive line near the primary CTA (even a modest "+ double VIP points this week" callout) since the current loyalty mention is passive, not active.
