# Last chance to buy more & save more 🔥
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-21**
## Executive summary

- This one actually delivers on its subject. "Last chance to buy more & save more 🔥" leads straight into "Final days to buy more, save more" with a clean tiered discount ladder — $10 off $40 / $20 off $80 / $30 off $100 — and a single unambiguous "Shop in app" button over a bright, on-brand hero. No bait-and-switch, no scavenger hunt to find the offer.
- The catch is the offer only applies "in-app," which the hero and CTA make clear, but that narrows the audience to app users before anyone clicks. It's also a hair-care hero (Texture ID styling products) on a subject that's category-agnostic, so nails/color shoppers get a slightly off-target visual even though the discount tier itself applies storewide.
- Structurally this is one of the stronger sends in the recent history — similar to 2026-09-10's "$8 OPI polish" send (8/10) in that subject and hero actually match, unlike the 09-11/09-14 Appiversary sends that promised a deal and delivered an app pitch instead.

## What's working

- Subject promise ("buy more & save more") and hero headline ("buy more, save more") match exactly — no bait-and-switch.
- Discount tiers are concrete and scannable: $10/$20/$30 off at three price points, no vague "save big" language.
- Single primary CTA ("Shop in app") is visually dominant against the hero image, easy to spot.
- "LAST CHANCE" ticker banner and the fine print's explicit 9/30 expiration give the urgency real teeth.
- Utility bar up top (BOPIS, 2-hour delivery) and Klarna financing add credible secondary value without cluttering the hero.

## What's weak

- Offer is app-exclusive but that restriction isn't obvious until you're already reading the CTA — a subscriber on desktop/web has no browse-online path for this specific deal.
- Hero model/product (hair styling foam/mousse) doesn't represent the full "entire in-app purchase" scope of the discount — reads as a hair-care ad, not a storewide sale.
- Immediately below the hero, a red block pivots to "We're closing out our Appiversary... check back tomorrow" — this buries the actual offer under a second, competing message before the eye even leaves the fold.
- "Don't have the app?" download pitch stacks a third competing CTA in the same scroll, diluting the single clear action the hero set up.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the "in-app only" restriction visible in the hero copy or a small badge near the price tiers, not just inferred from the button — sets expectations before the click and avoids frustrating desktop-first subscribers.
- 2. Cut or move the "closing out our Appiversary, check back tomorrow" block — it competes with and undercuts the last-chance urgency of the hero directly above it.
- 3. Swap the hair-care hero image for a more category-neutral storewide shot (or rotate hero images by segment) so the visual matches "entire purchase."
- 4. Move the app-download pitch further down, after the value props, so it doesn't compete with the hero CTA.
