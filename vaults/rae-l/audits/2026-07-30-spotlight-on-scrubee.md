# Spotlight on: Scrubee
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-30**
## Executive summary

- This is a single-SKU spotlight, not a campaign push — Lush is deep-diving on "Scrubee," a seven-year-old body butter, and building the whole email around social proof (1,800+ five-star reviews, a customer quote, three real-use photos) rather than a discount. Compared to the brand's recent run — the Moomin collab (7/23, 7.0), the "Naked" low-waste push (7/26, 8.0), the scalp editorial (7/19, 7.0) — this send trades campaign energy for product education. The bee pun ("The Humble Bumble," "Join The Hive," "bee-n") is cute and on-brand, and the "What's Inside Counts" ingredient breakdown is a genuinely useful addition that none of the recent sends had.
- The problem is there's no reason to act *now*. There's no discount tied to Scrubee itself — the only offer in the whole email is the generic "$5 off with the Lush app" banner at the very top, which reads like evergreen boilerplate rather than something built for this send. The CTA "Join The Hive" is charming but tells the reader nothing about what happens when they click.
- Solid execution on trust-building, weak on urgency and conversion clarity. This lands in the middle of the pack for this brand — better than the flat "Pick of the Bunch" fruit send (5.0), but a notch below the collab and Naked campaigns that had a real hook.

## What's working

- The review carousel (three real product photos + a five-star customer quote) is strong, specific social proof — more concrete than most Lush sends.
- "What's Inside Counts" ingredient breakdown (cocoa butter, coconut shell, honey) gives a real reason to trust the product beyond "moisturizing" marketing-speak.
- The bee wordplay is consistent and doesn't feel forced — "Humble Bumble," "Join The Hive," "cross-pollinating" all tie back to the honey ingredient story.
- Clean render throughout — no overlapping text, no broken images, ingredient icons and product photos all display correctly.
- The "Ethical Bee Keeping in Brazil" module adds a sourcing story that reinforces brand values without feeling like padding.

## What's weak

- No discount, bundle, or time-bound offer tied to Scrubee itself — the only $ hook is the generic app-download banner at the top, which is disconnected from the featured product.
- Primary CTA "Join The Hive" doesn't say what action the reader is taking (buy? subscribe? explore?) — it's thematic but not actionable.
- Subject line "Spotlight on: Scrubee" has zero urgency or curiosity hook — it reads like an internal content label, not a reason to open.
- Footer legal copy references a promotion window of "June 1–July 21, 2026," which has already lapsed as of this July 30 send — stale boilerplate that undercuts trust in the fine print.
- No visible pricing anywhere in the body — a reader interested enough to scroll through ingredients and reviews still doesn't know what Scrubee costs.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add an actual incentive to the hero, not just the top banner.** A "20% off Scrubee this week" or "Buy 1 Get 1 Half Off" directly under the headline would give the strong review/ingredient content something to convert against.
- 2. **Rewrite the CTA to a literal verb+noun** — "Shop Scrubee" or "Try It Risk-Free" instead of "Join The Hive," which requires the reader to already understand the pun.
- 3. **Fix the stale footer promo dates** — a June–July window sitting in a July 30 send is either dead copy or a compliance risk; refresh or remove it.
- 4. **Surface price near the hero image** — reviews and ingredients build desire, but nothing tells the reader what they're committing to.
- 5. **Tighten the subject to hint at the payoff** (reviews/loyalty), not just the product name:
- **Subject Alt A:** `1,800+ five-star reviews can't be wrong: meet Scrubee`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The body butter we can't stop restocking`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `7 years, one cult favorite — see why it's still selling out`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Cocoa butter, coconut, honey — nothing else`
