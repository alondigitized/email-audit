# One match. $2,000 of ILIA.
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-10**
## Executive summary

- This is a short, two-offer ILIA send. The top half pushes a shade-match quiz with a $2,000 sweepstakes. The bottom half pushes a free mini Eye Stylus Shadow Stick with a $75+ order. The sweepstakes hero is on-brand and calm, and the dark "GET SHADE MATCHED" button is the clearest element in the email. The "$2,000 of ILIA" overlay on a scatter of products reads instantly.
- The email splits its attention. A quiz-plus-sweepstakes ask and a purchase-gated gift each want a different action, and the free mini gets its own large image, headline and button. The shade-match quiz also appeared in the 11-09 "Gift ideas loading…" send, whose top banner read "GET SHADE MATCHED + WIN $2,000 OF ILIA*". This is the second day in a row on the same sweepstakes, so the creative feels familiar.
- Overall it is clean and competent but not sharp. The sweepstakes is a mild lead-gen hook rather than a reason to buy. The free-mini offer is the real revenue lever and sits below the fold. It's a middling send.

## What's working

- The hero states the prize clearly: "ENTER TO WIN $2,000 of ILIA" in large type over a product flat-lay.
- One dark, full-width "GET SHADE MATCHED →" button gives the top section an unmistakable action.
- The body copy is short: "All it takes is one match."
- The eye close-up with a "FREE MINI" badge is a strong product visual, and the offer is concrete: free mini Eye Stylus in Beam with a $75+ order.
- Footer trust signals (free shipping over $50, 1% for the Planet, Leaping Bunny, VIP, Auto-Replenish) add credibility without clutter.

## What's weak

- Two different asks (take a quiz, spend $75) compete, and the two buttons are styled differently with no clear priority.
- The mid-email "ILIA — Makeup That Makes Your Skin Better" banner is a large, dead block. It has no product and no link cue.
- The sweepstakes asterisk and "*See site for details" push the fine print into a tiny link at the bottom, which weakens the sense of an honest offer.
- No product pricing, reviews or shade visuals appear. Shade matching is the pitch, yet no skin tones or models are shown.
- The hero image is mostly empty space, and the "$2,000" text sits low-contrast over the light flat-lay.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with one action. Either make the free-mini gift the hero, since it drives orders, or tie the quiz to the gift ("Get matched, get a free mini").
- 2. Cut the brand banner and replace it with 2-3 shade-range swatches or a product row with a "Shop foundation" link.
- 3. Add urgency with a real sweepstakes end date, and put the key terms in the body instead of a footer link.
- 4. Add social proof such as a star rating or a "most-loved shade" call-out near the gift.
- 5. Darken the hero text or add a subtle scrim so "$2,000" reads on the busy background.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Free mini + a shot at $2,000`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Take the 1-minute quiz, enter to win, and unlock a free mini with $75+.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your perfect shade match is one quiz away. Sweepstakes ends soon.`
