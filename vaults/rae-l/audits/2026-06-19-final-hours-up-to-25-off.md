# Final Hours: Up to 25% off
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- This is Glossier's **third email on June 19 alone** — following "Up to 25% off ends tomorrow" and then "Up to 25% off ends tonight!" (which the brand history already flagged as the "hard-close" push). At this point the sale arc is north of twelve emails in nine days, and subscribers have now been told the sale ends "tomorrow," "tonight," and now "in final hours" across a single calendar day. The credibility of every urgency signal in this email is spent before it's read.
- Inside the email, the creative makes the right call: "Okay, this is really it" is self-aware and honest, tacitly acknowledging fatigue while making a genuine last plea. The product grid is clean, the CTA buttons are visible, and the subscription upsell at the bottom is a smart pivot — it offers a way to capture discount-seekers who won't buy today. But none of that overcomes the fundamental problem: a third same-day send has trained the most engaged subscribers that Glossier's deadlines are negotiable. The word "final" is doing no work here.
- Clear judgement: the execution inside the email is adequate, but the send itself is a mistake. Glossier would have been better served letting the "ends tonight" email (8/10, already the second send that day) stand as the closer.

## What's working

- **"Okay, this is really it"** — the self-aware hero copy is the best line in the entire arc; it earns a tiny bit of credibility by not pretending the fatigue doesn't exist.
- **Product grid is well-merchandised** — mascara, lip, liner, and the glitter cup all show distinct product types, which gives a reason to scroll regardless of which category you're shopping.
- **Subscription upsell at the bottom is well-placed** — locking in 20% off long-term is a legitimate offer for fence-sitters, and it doesn't cannibalize the primary sale.
- **Orange CTA buttons are consistent and visible** — no ambiguity about what to tap; button color is on-brand and passes contrast.

## What's weak

- **Third email on one day destroys urgency credibility** — subscribers were already told "ends tonight" in the previous send. "Final hours" is a direct contradiction of a promise made hours earlier.
- **Subject line is a near-clone of the previous two** — "Final Hours: Up to 25% off" vs. "Up to 25% off ends tonight!" vs. "Up to 25% off ends tomorrow" is barely distinguishable in an inbox thread view.
- **"Up to 25% off" hedge is doing compounding damage** — after twelve emails, subscribers still don't know what actually qualifies. The ambiguity reads as bait-and-switch by this point.
- **Preheader appears to leak structural copy** ("Sale ends tonight! | View Online") — if that top-bar text is bleeding into the inbox preview, it repeats the subject and adds zero pull.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Don't send this email at all.** The "ends tonight" send should have been the last. If a third email was mandated, it should have gone out at 10–11 PM with a drastically different creative angle, not mid-afternoon as a near-duplicate.
- 2. **Make the offer specific, not "up to."** "25% off everything" or "20% off orders over $100 — no exceptions" would land harder at this stage of the arc than a perpetual hedge.
- 3. **Lead with the subscription offer, not the sale.** At this point in the arc, the marginal sale conversion is low; the subscription lock-in is the most interesting offer. Flip the hierarchy.
- 4. **Rewrite the subject to acknowledge the absurdity** — the brand voice earned the right to be cheeky:
- **Subject Alt A:** `Okay, we really mean it this time`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last call (we promise)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `25% off ends at midnight — this is the actual last email`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Or lock in 20% forever with a subscription`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Gmail head CSS stripping (confirmed issue)**
All styles are declared in `<head>` `<style>` blocks. Gmail web strips `<head>` CSS entirely. The entire hover/rollover interaction system (`.rollover:hover .rollover-first`, `.bj:hover`, etc.) will silently fail in Gmail — a significant rendering gap given Gmail's ~27% market share. Only inline styles survive Gmail.

**Hover states are non-functional in most clients**
The CSS defines extensive `:hover` button states across ~25 class selectors (`.bj-9168:hover`, `.bj-6784:hover`, etc.). These work only in Apple Mail and a subset of desktop clients. Outlook, Gmail (web + Android), and most mobile clients ignore `:hover` entirely.

**Vendor comment leaks internal metadata**
```html

```
