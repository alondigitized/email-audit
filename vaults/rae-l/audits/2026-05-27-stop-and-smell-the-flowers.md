# Stop and smell the flowers 💐
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- Lush has assembled a clean, structured floral category guide — three scent families (Geraniums, Lavender, Roses), each with a nature photo, product shot, name, and brief copy. The editorial logic is coherent and the seasonal timing is strong: late May is peak "flowers are everywhere" energy. Compared to the dense fragrance catalogs from May 12 and May 14, this feels more curated — three lanes instead of six.
- The problem is that this is the seventh Lush email in fifteen days, and the format is immediately familiar. Subscribers have seen this exact section-per-scent-family structure before. There's no offer, no urgency, and the single hero CTA is the perennial "Explore Now." The email is pleasant but gives a returning subscriber no new reason to click today versus any other day.
- Strong execution on a format that's been used one too many times this month without a discount or time hook to differentiate it.

## What's working

- **Seasonal anchoring is perfect.** "Stop and smell the flowers" in late May lands naturally; the floral photography reinforces the moment without being forced.
- **Three-section structure is digestible.** Each category (Geraniums → Lavender → Roses) follows the same visual rhythm — hero crop, product shot, name, copy — so scanning is effortless.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean.** The hero loads first, the section headers are color-coded to their scent family (pink/olive, purple, rose), and the eye moves down without confusion.
- **No render bugs.** Layout is solid across the visible render — no broken images, overlapping text, or layout collapse.
- **Lush Cares section grounds the brand.** The values footer reinforces the ethical positioning without cluttering the product content.

## What's weak

- **Zero offer.** No discount, no gift-with-purchase, no free shipping threshold — nothing to motivate a click today. The May 20 Dream Cream send scored higher precisely because it gave subscribers something to act on.
- **"Explore Now" CTA is the weakest possible verb.** It commits to nothing — no destination, no category, no product. After three sections of product storytelling, landing on "Explore Now" is a momentum killer.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** This is email seven in fifteen days. The floral theme doesn't differentiate enough from the May 14 fragrance-category send or the May 22 Karma showcase; a subscriber could reasonably wonder if they already opened this one.
- **No product detail visible.** Scent descriptions are present but sizing, format (gel vs. soap vs. cream), and pricing are absent — the three things a buyer needs to know before clicking.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a threshold offer or gift.** "Spend $40, get a free floral sample" or "10% off floral picks this week" transforms a browse email into a buy email. No other change matters as much.
- 2. **Replace "Explore Now" with section-level CTAs.** "Shop Geraniums," "Shop Lavender," "Shop Roses" — one per section. This is how the May 12 vibe email should have worked and where this format leaves clicks on the table.
- 3. **Surface one price or format detail per product.** Even "from $13" or "available in 3 sizes" next to each product name reduces the trip to the PDP and increases conversion.
- 4. **Push to every-other-day cadence.** Cutting from 7-in-15 to ~4-in-15 would restore open rates meaningfully; the brand's quality is being diluted by volume.
