# Flowers, powders, and kelp
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-03**
## Executive summary

- ILIA is treating this as a day-two "formula in focus" editorial — an ingredient deep-dive on the Soft Focus Finishing Powder they dropped June 2 (scored 8/10 in brand history). That sequencing is smart: launch day gets the product reveal, day two gets the science. The ingredient storytelling is genuinely differentiated for a powder email — edelweiss squalane, winged kelp extract, plant-based squalane, soft focus complex — each with its own panel and short rationale. If you were on the fence after the June 2 launch, this is the email that closes the loop.
- The visual execution is consistent with ILIA's editorial DNA: warm sand tones, clean type, close-up skin photography that shows the finish rather than sells it. No discount, no urgency, no promo code — and that's a deliberate choice. After eighteen Friends + Family emails pounding 20% off through late May, ILIA is clearly in a "earn back trust with content" mode.
- The weak spot is the subject line. "Flowers, powders, and kelp" is poetic but opaque — a subscriber skimming their inbox at 7am has no obvious signal that this is the follow-up to a new product launch they might actually want. The open hinges entirely on brand recognition, not copy pull.

## What's working

- **Ingredient panels are specific and credible** — each ingredient (edelweiss squalane, winged kelp, plant-based squalane) gets a named benefit, which is rare for a powder email and earns trust.
- **"SHOP SOFT FOCUS →" CTA is product-specific**, not a generic homepage redirect — verb + noun, clear destination.
- **"the feel + finish" section** — "featherweight, blurring, illuminating" lands as a concise sensory pitch just before the second CTA.
- **Model photography shows skin**, not just the compact — the close-up demonstrating finish is the best single reason to click.
- **No render bugs** — layout holds cleanly, warm palette is consistent top to bottom.

## What's weak

- **No offer** — after the May promo fatigue, that's defensible, but a first-purchase incentive (even free sample with order) would convert fence-sitters reading ingredient copy.
- **Subject line is too poetic for cold inbox skimming** — "Flowers, powders, and kelp" doesn't surface the product name or any action signal; it relies entirely on the subscriber already being warm.
- **"Zooming in" section header is weak** — it's a filler transition; "What's inside" or "The formula" would be more direct and searchable.
- **No reviews or social proof** — for a brand-new SKU that just launched yesterday, even a pull-quote ("I can't go back to anything else") would add conviction.
- **Top banner text is illegible at mobile scale** — whatever offer or navigation lives there is lost.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Surface the product name in the subject** — "Flowers, powders, and kelp" is too abstract; tell the subscriber what they're clicking into.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Inside the formula: Soft Focus Finishing Powder`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Kelp in a powder? Here's why it works`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Edelweiss, winged kelp, and plant-based squalane — the ingredients doing the work.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Everything in the new Soft Focus Finishing Powder, explained.`
- 2. **Add one first-buy hook** — even "New launch: complimentary sample with first order" in the top banner converts ingredient readers into buyers without undermining the editorial tone.
- 3. **Replace "Zooming in — Let's take a closer look at key ingredients"** with a single strong header like "The Formula" — saves vertical space and respects the reader's time.
- 4. **Drop a single review pull-quote** inside the ingredient section — ILIA likely has internal tester feedback or early UGC from the launch day. One sentence from a real person breaks the monotony of brand-only copy.
- 5. **Make the top banner legible** — if there's an offer there (free shipping, gift with purchase), it needs to render at a size humans can read on mobile.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<title>` tag is empty**
```html
<title> </title>
```
Several email clients (Apple Mail, Samsung Mail) display the `<title>` value in tab labels or accessibility trees. A blank title is a missed label.

**No hover affordance on links**
```css
a:hover { color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none }
```
Hover state is identical to default state — no underline, no color shift. Fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.1 (use of color) for link identification without relying solely on color.

**Google Fonts `@import` will not load in Gmail**
```css
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```
Gmail strips `<style>` block `@import` rules. Montserrat will silently fall back to Helvetica for Gmail recipients. This is expected behavior with external fonts in email, but worth documenting. Also uses the older CSS1 API path — not a functional issue since `display=swap` still works, but the CSS2 API (`family=Montserrat:ital,wght@...`) is more expressive if weights/italics are needed.

**Klaviyo custom font import**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
```
Second external font dependency with the same client-support caveat. If this is serving the same Montserrat, it is redundant alongside the Google Fonts import.
