# 🍂 Your fall beauty routine starts here 🍂
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-19**
## Executive summary

- Sally Beauty pivots away from the COLORfest hair-color push into a "fall routine" seasonal roundup — the same generic slot 8/17's "More beauty. More savings." occupied, and it inherits the same weakness: four product blocks split across two unrelated categories (hair care, nails) with no single offer carrying the send. The hero is all mood, no mechanic — "Your routine, ready for fall" and a bare "Shop now" button, with the actual discounts ($5 off $20, BOGO nails) buried two scrolls down in small type under product tiles.
- Calling this "fall" on August 19th is early, and after six-plus COLORfest sends in under two weeks this reads as a category reset rather than a strong follow-up — closer in energy to 8/14's "Keep your color going strong" (5/10, four-way grid) than to 8/16's tightly-focused "$10/$20 off" hero (8/10).
- It's clean and on-brand visually, but there's no reason to click any one thing over another. Middle of the pack, held back by split focus rather than by execution quality.

## What's working

- Hero image is strong — warm, in-focus model shot with genuine texture (curly hair, red lip) that fits a fall palette.
- Confetti-dot brand banding at top and bottom gives visual consistency with prior COLORfest sends, so the brand is instantly recognizable.
- Sally Rewards banner ("Shop Twice in August, Earn a $5 Reward in September") is a clear, low-friction loyalty hook.
- Klarna financing callout and the five-icon service row (in-store, online, free shipping, 2-hr delivery, BOPIS) add real utility signals without clutter.
- No layout bugs — every tile, price, and button renders cleanly.

## What's weak

- The hero has no offer at all — just "Shop now" — so the strongest visual real estate in the email sells nothing concrete.
- Four disconnected product blocks (shampoo, mask, nails, gel polish) with no single throughline; the eye has nowhere obvious to land after the hero.
- Every CTA reads "Shop now" — six identical buttons with zero differentiation in copy or visual weight.
- The only two actual discounts ($5 off $20, buy-two-get-one nails) are small-type callouts under product photos, not promoted anywhere near the top.
- "Fall routine" in mid-August, right after a two-week hair-color campaign, feels like an abrupt category switch rather than a natural next beat.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one hero offer and put it in the hero block — either the $5-off-$20 hair care deal or the BOGO nails deal, not both buried below. A generic "shop now" hero is the single biggest miss here.
- 2. Cut to one category per send. Splitting hair care and nails halves the impact of both; run this as two focused sends instead.
- 3. Vary CTA copy by section ("Shop hair care," "Shop the mani kit") so the buttons aren't interchangeable.
- 4. Move the Sally Rewards "$5 Reward in September" banner up near the hero — it's the most concrete, time-relevant hook in the email and it's currently below four product grids.
- 5. Hold "fall routine" framing until closer to September, or anchor it to something dated now ("Labor Day" / "back-to-routine") so it doesn't read premature.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$5 off hair care + BOGO nails, this week only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Fall prep: refresh your hair, refresh your mani`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `$5 off $20 in hair care, plus buy 2 get 1 free nails — shop before it's gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Earn a $5 Reward when you shop twice this month.`
