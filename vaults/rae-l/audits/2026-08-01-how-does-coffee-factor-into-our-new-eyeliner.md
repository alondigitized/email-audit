# How does coffee factor into our new eyeliner?
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-01**
## Executive summary

- This is ILIA's third "ingredient story" send in the Thru Line launch arc — after the teaser (07-22), the reveal (07-28), and the waterproof-chemistry deep dive (07-30) — and now it pivots to the packaging material: coffee chaff. The Q&A format with Packaging Development Manager Kristin HooSang is a smart trust-building device that the brand hasn't used yet in this sequence, and it gives the send a distinct identity instead of just repeating the "hero macro shot" playbook.
- Where it slips is pacing: this is the fourth Thru Line email in under two weeks, and two of the last three have been ingredient explainers. The story is well told, but a subscriber who already read "What makes it waterproof?" two days ago may feel like ILIA is stretching one product launch further than the excitement can carry. There's also no real promotional lever here — just a 4X VIP points line — so this send lives or dies on curiosity about coffee chaff, not on a deal.
- Net: well-crafted, on-brand, good photography, but a cadence and cadence-fatigue risk drags it down from the 9s this campaign hit at launch.

## What's working

- The Q&A format with a real headshot of Kristin HooSang is a fresh structural choice that adds credibility this campaign hasn't used before.
- Clean narrative arc — waste-reduction hook, ingredient explainer, sourcing Q&A, shop CTA, product benefit list — is easy to follow scrolling.
- Coffee bean and swatch macro photography is well lit and reinforces the "coffee chaff" story visually.
- The closing beauty shot (model applying eyeliner) plus the bulleted claims (24-hour wear, waterproof + sweatproof, safe for sensitive eyes, safe for the waterline) gives a clear, scannable payoff after the education section.
- Two "SHOP THRU LINE" buttons bracket the content so the CTA is never far away.

## What's weak

- No concrete promotional offer — "4X VIP points" is the only incentive, and it's a small banner, not a hero element.
- This is the third ingredient-education email tied to one launch in ten days; "coffee chaff sourcing" is a niche detail that risks reading as filler content rather than news.
- The hero section leads with abstract swirl art and a tagline ("grounds for innovation") rather than a clear product-forward image, softening the visual hierarchy at the very top.
- No social proof (star ratings, review count) anywhere in the send, despite this being deep into the launch when reviews would likely exist.
- Overall length is long for what amounts to one ingredient factoid — several screens of scrolling before the benefit payoff.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this to a single, tighter send or fold the coffee-chaff story into a "by the numbers" sidebar on a future promotional email — three straight educational sends on one launch is one too many; the next Thru Line email should carry an actual discount or bundle offer to re-engage.
- 2. Move the benefit bullets (24-hr wear, waterproof, sensitive-eye safe) higher, above the Q&A, so shoppers who don't care about sourcing details still get the sell.
- 3. Add a review snippet or star rating near the final beauty shot now that Thru Line has been live for a week — it's the one social-proof gap in an otherwise trust-building send.
- 4. Lead the hero with the product macro shot (as used further down) instead of the abstract coffee swirl, so the eye lands on "what is this" before "here's a concept."
