# Ready, set, shop—new beauty has landed
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-08**
## Executive summary

- This is Sephora's "new arrivals" digest — "Just Dropped & Only Here" — stacking seven brands (Rare Beauty, Josie Maran, Patrick Ta, Innisfree, Marc Jacobs Beauty, Burberry) into one long scroll with no single hero to anchor the eye. It's a step down from the tightly focused sends this pipeline has scored 9/10 all week — the Aug 6 body-care takeover and the Aug 5 4X-points PSA both won by narrowing to one clear story, while this one reverts to the "everything, all at once" catalog format that scored well on Jul 31 and Aug 4 only because novelty carried it.
- The strongest thing here is the Rare Beauty x Selena Gomez launch up top — a genuinely newsworthy, high-recognition drop — but it's given the same visual weight as six other brand blocks stacked below the fold, so a scrolling reader loses the signal in noise. The two footer modules (4X points, "Just-Dropped Skincare") feel bolted on and compete with the new-arrivals narrative rather than supporting it.
- Verdict: solid production quality, zero render bugs, but strategically unfocused — this reads as a merchandising list, not a story, and will underperform the brand's recent single-theme sends.

## What's working

- Clean, consistent product photography throughout — no broken images, no layout breaks across seven brand blocks.
- Rare Beauty by Selena Gomez leads with real specificity: shade/scent names, prices ($30–$78), and a Same-Day Delivery callout.
- "NEW • ONLY AT SEPHORA" badge repeats consistently, reinforcing exclusivity as the throughline.
- Clean/Planet Aware badges appear on Josie Maran and Innisfree, useful trust signals for ingredient-conscious shoppers.
- Top banner clearly states the tentpole perks (Same-Day Delivery, free shipping, rewards pickup) before the scroll even starts.

## What's weak

- No single hero or CTA — seven brand blocks of near-equal visual weight force the reader to do the prioritization work themselves.
- The primary "SHOP NOW" button under the hero image links to an undifferentiated haul, not to the Selena Gomez launch that's the actual headline.
- Fine print price ranges ("$30–$78", "$16–$36") without stating what's actually new vs. restocked make the offer feel vague.
- Footer modules (4X points promo, "Just-Dropped Skincare") are visually disconnected from the top story — they read like two different emails glued together.
- Subject line "new beauty has landed" is generic — it could describe any of the last five Sephora sends.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this to one hero: lead with Rare Beauty x Selena Gomez alone, move the other six brands to a "More New Arrivals" carousel below — this is the single highest-leverage change, since it's the only genuinely newsworthy item in the email.
- 2. Put the primary CTA button directly under the Rare Beauty block, linking to that product page, not a generic "haul" landing page.
- 3. Pull the 4X points promo out entirely — it's a loyalty mechanic, not a new-arrival, and dilutes this email's one clear job.
- 4. Add a real deadline or stock cue ("while supplies last") to at least one product to create urgency; right now nothing is time-bound.
- 5. Tighten subject/preheader to name the actual headline product instead of generic "new beauty."
