# 🟠 Karma coming your way
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- This is a full Karma product-line showcase — every section is orange, every headline says "Karma," and the visual identity is unmistakably on-brand. Lush knows their signature scent and leans into it hard: the hero immediately orients the reader, and the catalog grid below covers Karma in every format (body cream, solid perfume, fragrance, soap, etc.). The bottom yellow section appears to be a free-gift or gifting module, though the copy is too small to read without zooming.
- The trouble is this is the seventh Lush send in under three weeks, and it reads like a catalog drop rather than a reason-to-buy moment. Compared to the May 20 Dream Cream send — which gave one product the hero treatment and felt intentional — this email spreads its weight across every Karma SKU and lands without a single focal punch. There's no visible discount, no deadline, no price reduction anywhere in the scroll.
- Clear judgement: a competent on-brand catalog send with a fun subject line, but no commercial hook strong enough to trigger a purchase over and above "I already like Karma."

## What's working

- **Subject line pun lands.** "🟠 Karma coming your way" is clever, on-brand, and visually flagged with an orange circle that matches the email's palette — earns an open.
- **Visual cohesion is exceptional.** Every section is orange, amber, or earthy — the Karma color world is consistent from hero to footer with zero palette drift.
- **Hero CTA is immediate.** "Shop Karma" button appears above the fold in the hero, no scrolling required.
- **Breadth of format coverage.** Showing Karma Kream, the solid perfume, the fragrance spray, and soap in one send serves a reader who knows the scent and wants to explore adjacent SKUs.
- **No render bugs.** Layout is clean, images load, text doesn't overlap.

## What's weak

- **No offer anywhere above the fold.** There is no visible discount, free-shipping threshold, or spend incentive until the buried yellow section near the bottom — which is too small to read in render.
- **Catalog fatigue compounding.** This is the 7th Lush send since May 7. The format mirrors the May 12 "What's your vibe?" vibe-taxonomy send — a different theme but the same product-grid structure. Diminishing attention returns.
- **"Good Karma... Everybody Needs Some" section adds no purchase signal.** It reads as editorial filler — a lifestyle image with no product link or price — breaking the transactional momentum.
- **CTAs are generic throughout the grid.** Below the hero, each product tile appears to use a standard button with no specific verb-noun copy beyond the product name, reducing click intent.
- **No urgency or deadline visible anywhere.** Nothing in the email communicates why today is the right time to buy Karma versus any other day.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one concrete offer to the hero.** A single line — "Spend $50, get a free Karma sample" or "Free shipping on Karma orders today" — transforms this from a catalog into a reason to act. The May 9 "ob-zesst" citrus send used an $80+ free-sample threshold and scored 8/10 for exactly this reason; replicate that mechanic here.
- 2. **Cut the grid to 3-4 hero Karma SKUs with individual CTAs.** The May 20 Dream Cream single-product format outperforms the multi-SKU catalog for click. Either go full catalog (with prices visible) or go hero (one product, full focus). Right now it's neither.
- 3. **Kill the "editorial" middle section or make it transactional.** "Good Karma... Everybody Needs Some" needs either a price, a CTA, or a cut.
- 4. **Surface the bottom offer section at or near the hero.** If there's a free gift or spend threshold buried in the yellow block, it belongs in the first visible section, not after seven product tiles.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some desktop Outlook versions render this in the window titlebar; screen readers may announce an empty document name. Should be set to the email subject or brand name.

**Issue — global link underline suppression:**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none }
```
This strips underlines from all links in Gmail's web client. If linked text isn't otherwise visually distinguished from body copy (e.g., button treatment only), this fails WCAG 1.4.1 (use of color). Acceptable only if every link is a styled button or clearly delimited.
