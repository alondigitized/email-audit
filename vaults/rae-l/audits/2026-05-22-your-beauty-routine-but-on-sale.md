# Your Beauty Routine, But on Sale ✨
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- This is Sally's day-two Memorial Day cadence blast, arriving 24 hours after the "Memorial Day Glow-Up: Up to 50% Off ✨" anchor send (8/10, May 21). The strategic logic is defensible — a follow-up reminder catches non-openers — but the execution moves in the wrong direction. The subject is softer and more generic than yesterday's, while the body somehow contains *more* offers: a competing flash 20%-off-sitewide banner sitting above a 50%-off Memorial Day hero, followed by six separate product-level promotions spanning nails, hair care, braiding, and cordless tools. It reads like a monthly catalog, not an escalating sale reminder.
- The two competing sale mechanics at the top are the biggest conversion risk. A subscriber sees "20% off sitewide with code" in the banner and "Up to 50% off" in the hero — they don't know which offer to prioritize or whether the code stacks. That ambiguity will kill click intent faster than any single design flaw. The 60% off cordless tools buried near the bottom is the strongest offer in the email and deserves its own send.
- The day-two send should raise the temperature — urgency, deadline, highest offer in the subject. This one retreats to "but on sale," which is weaker than what it's following up on.

## What's working

- Memorial Day hero is timely and seasonally accurate — nothing is stale here.
- Concrete price points appear throughout: $5 nail lacquers, $14.99 braiding kit, 2 for $15 hair care — specific anchors that beat percentage-only pricing.
- Flash sale banner layered on top of the hero sale creates a two-tier urgency structure, which is a smart mechanic even if the execution is confusing.
- Product category breadth (nails, hair, tools) means at least one section is likely to hit for any Sally subscriber.
- The 60% off cordless styling tools section is a genuinely strong offer — highest discount in the email.
- Subject ("Your Beauty Routine, But on Sale") is generic copy-speak — no %, no product, no deadline, no reason to open now vs. later.
- Two competing sale mechanics at the very top (flash code banner + Memorial Day hero) split attention and create confusion about which offer to use.
- Six product sections make this feel like the May circular, not a focused promotional email.
- Day-two cadence with a weaker subject than day one is a tactical mistake — a reminder should escalate, not soften.
- Plum loyalty section is buried so far down it contributes nothing to conversion; most subscribers won't scroll to it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject as a deadline/urgency escalation, not a repositioning.** Day-two sends work when they raise the stakes. Lead with the strongest offer (60% off cordless tools) or put a hard deadline in the line.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 60% Off — Memorial Day Sale Ends Soon`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last Chance: Memorial Day 50% Off This Weekend`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cordless tools 60% off. $5 nail lacquers. Flash code MEMORIAL today only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The biggest Sally sale of May. Ends soon — shop before it's gone.`
- 2. **Pick one sale mechanic for the hero.** Either the flash code or the 50% off Memorial Day headline — not both. If the flash code is expiring today, make it the single hero. Competing promos above the fold kill decision-making.
- 3. **Cut from six product sections to three.** Nail, hair care, heat tools. One section headline, one CTA per block. The braiding kit and the Simmer Lights section can merge into hair care or get dropped to a secondary email.
- 4. **Move Plum up.** Put it directly below the hero or after the first product block. Loyalty members click at higher rates — don't waste the placement.
- 5. **Add a visible sale-end timestamp to the hero.** "Ends Monday May 25" or "Ends in 3 days" — anything that creates real urgency in the body, not just the flash banner.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — Missing `@font-face` for 'Founders Grotesk':**
The global anchor rule `a { font-family:'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }` references a custom font with no corresponding `@font-face` declaration in the `<style>` block. Every link in the email silently falls back to Helvetica. If this font is intentional, it needs a hosted woff2 `@font-face` entry; otherwise the declaration is dead weight.

**Issue — `img { white-space: pre; }`:**
`white-space: pre` on `img` elements is a non-standard rule with no documented email rendering benefit. It does not affect block-level images but may cause unexpected spacing on inline images in some Outlook versions. Should be removed.

**Minor — Open Sans pinned to v17:**
`@font-face` URLs reference `fonts.gstatic.com/s/opensans/v17/...`. Current version is v36. Version-pinned paths are stable until Google deprecates them, but this is 5+ major versions behind. Low immediate risk; worth updating on next template revision.

**Minor — Mobile breakpoint at 499px:**
`@media only screen and (max-width: 499px)` is non-standard. Most ESP templates use 480px or 600px. Devices between 500–599px wide receive desktop layout. Confirm this matches QA device testing matrix.

**Cannot audit — alt text:**
Image tags are not visible in truncated source. All `<img>` elements require descriptive `alt` attributes; decorative images require `alt=""`.
