# Boo! Halloween is here!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- "Ghost the Grind" is genuinely clever creative — the pun (ghosting + hustle culture + literal ghosts) is sharp, the green slime face-mask model is visually arresting, and "Get Some Treats" is a clean trick-or-treat callback. The brand voice holds up throughout. The problem is everything above the render: this is a Halloween email landing on July 16, three months before Halloween. "Halloween is here!" on a day in mid-July reads either as a scheduling error or as a brand that's lost the plot entirely.
- Compare this to the July 13 "Spotlight on: Fresh" and the July 7 "Smells like summer" sends — Lush has been running tight summer editorial for weeks. Jumping from a sun-care and honey-product cadence straight into pumpkin scrubs and fearsome fizzes hits like a costume worn to a beach barbecue. If this was an intentional "be first" strategy, it's fired 6-8 weeks too early for even the most ambitious Halloween preview window.
- The creative can ship without changes. The send date cannot.

## What's working

- **Headline pun lands cleanly.** "Ghost the Grind" ties Halloween to the self-care/rest angle without feeling forced — Lush's sweet spot.
- **CTA copy is thematically locked in.** "Get Some Treats" is a specific, fun trick-or-treat callback that earns the click copy rather than defaulting to "Shop Now."
- **Visual hierarchy is uncluttered.** Large hero text → subhead → body → single button; eye doesn't have to work.
- **Brand voice is consistent.** "Melt in peace (and quiet)," "fearsome fizzes" — distinctly Lush throughout.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no overlapping elements, no layout breaks visible.

## What's weak

- **Catastrophically off-season.** "Halloween is here!" on July 16 is not a teaser — it's a credibility crater. Zero in-store or cultural context supports it.
- **No hero product surfaced.** The body sells a vibe ("pumpkin scrubs, spicy scents, fearsome fizzes") but doesn't name a single product or show a product image above the CTA fold.
- **No price incentive in the email body.** The $5 app banner is persistent boilerplate, not a campaign-specific offer. The Halloween collection has no deal, no scarcity hook, no member exclusive.
- **No real urgency mechanism.** Without a deadline or limited-stock signal, there's nothing pushing the click beyond mild curiosity.
- **Cadence whiplash.** Five summer sends in a row followed immediately by Halloween registers as a mistake, not a strategy.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Hold until late September.** The entire creative works — the only broken element is timing. Target the Sep 29–Oct 7 window when Halloween purchase intent actually exists.
- 2. **Surface one named hero product.** Put a product name, image, and price (e.g., "Pumpkin Patch Bath Bomb — $9") between the body copy and the CTA. Sell a product, not just a collection.
- 3. **Own the "early access" framing if sending early is intentional.** "First dibs on our Halloween collection — launching October 1" turns the premature send into a subscriber benefit. Right now it's just confusing.
- 4. **Add a Lush Club member hook.** "Double points on Halloween picks this weekend" would make the app banner feel earned rather than boilerplate, and ties loyalty to campaign behavior.
- 5. **Rewrite the subject for specificity.**
- **Subject Alt A:** `Get first pick of our Halloween collection`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Pumpkin scrubs + spooky scents — your preview`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Ghost the grind with baths made for October (and beyond).`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Pumpkin scrubs. Spooky fizzes. Launching soon — shop early.`

### 1. Technical Summary

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**EU CDN serving US audience**
All images are served from `cdn.braze.eu`. For a Lush USA send, routing through a European CDN endpoint adds unnecessary latency and increases the risk of client-side image load timeouts. Confirm Braze account is configured to use the US CDN endpoint (`cdn.braze.com`).

Evidence: `src="https://cdn.braze.eu/appboy/communication/assets/image_assets/images/6a4e266f165ce1008393d37d/original.png?1783506542"`

**UTM parameters unverifiable**
All hrefs route through `clicks.hello.us.lush.com` Braze click-tracking redirects with encoded destination URLs. UTM parameters on landing pages cannot be audited from the HTML source. Recommend verifying via decoded destinations (see §6).

**No issues** with the click-tracking domain itself — `clicks.hello.us.lush.com` is a properly subdomain-isolated tracker, reducing deliverability risk.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
`<title></title>` is blank. Webmail clients (Gmail web, Yahoo) surface this as the browser tab title when a recipient opens the email in a browser tab. Should be set to match the subject line or a short descriptive label.

**Non-standard desktop width (500px)**
`row-content stack` is constrained to `width:500px`. Industry standard is 600px. At 500px, the layout has unusually wide gutters on desktop clients and may render disproportionately in wide-viewport preview panes (Outlook desktop, Apple Mail).

Evidence: `style="...width:500px;margin:0 auto" width="500"`

**Mobile breakpoint mismatch**
The media query fires at `max-width:520px` but the desktop container is 500px. This means the breakpoint activates at a width only 20px wider than the desktop column — functionally correct, but if the desktop width is ever updated to 600px, this breakpoint would need adjustment.

**Logo `alt` text is acceptable** — `alt="Logo: LUSH"` is present and non-empty. No issue.

**`role="presentation"` on all layout tables** — correct implementation. No issue.
