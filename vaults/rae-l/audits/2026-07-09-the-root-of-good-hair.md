# ✨ The root of good hair
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- This is Lush's hair care editorial — organized top-to-bottom as scalp → length → full product grid → tips content hub. The "ROOT FOR YOUR HAIR" hero lands the double pun cleanly (root as in hair root, root as in cheer on), which is quintessential Lush voice. The visual hierarchy works: cream and sage palette, a strong circular hero frame, then a logical educational descent into scalp saviors, length care, and specific products.
- What holds it back is what's always held Lush back: no offer. After the Sanrio collab and the mint cooling send ("I've got chills," scored 9/10 on June 28), this is a pure editorial send with zero price incentive. That's fine for brand-building — Lush does editorial well — but it caps click-through ceiling for anyone not already in a buying mindset. The "Top Tips" read-more module at the bottom is a nice addition as it gives an alternative conversion path for the browsing segment.
- The timing also reads slightly generic. Unlike "I've got chills" which was tightly season-locked to late June heat, hair care doesn't scream July. It could have been sent any month. The email is polished and on-brand but is coasting rather than converting.

## What's working

- **The pun stacks.** "ROOT FOR YOUR HAIR" + "✨ The root of good hair" in the subject is the kind of wordplay Lush pulls off better than almost anyone — the June massage bar ("KNOT A WANT BUT A KNEAD") set the bar and this holds it.
- **Clear section logic.** Scalp → Length → Full product catalog → Tips is a natural editorial arc; each section head ("Find Scalp Saviors," "Go to great lengths") builds on the last.
- **Star ratings on Scalp Saviors.** The Roots and Ginger product cards both show star ratings — the only social proof in the email, and it lands where the purchase decision is most likely being made.
- **The "Top Tips" dark module.** A contrasting-background CTA that pivots from product push to content value is smart; it captures the reader who's curious but not ready to buy.

## What's weak

- **No offer whatsoever.** No discount, no GWP, no free shipping threshold — for a subscriber base that's seen Lush's Sanrio drop and multiple curated editorial sends this month, "here are our hair products" isn't a strong enough hook to click through.
- **Hero CTA copy is illegible at mobile scale.** The button below the hero block is too small to read in the render; whatever it says is lost, which is a wasted primary CTA position.
- **The top banner likely bleeds into preheader.** The small-text row at the very top (appears to be a "view online" or utility link) is almost certainly what inbox clients are pulling as preheader — a recurring Lush problem visible in prior audits.
- **No urgency or time anchor.** Nothing in the email tells me why I should shop hair care on July 9 specifically; compare to "I've got chills" which had an obvious seasonal hook.
- **Demographic narrowness.** Both hero and the "Go to great lengths" models appear to have long straight or loosely wavy hair; the scalp savior section heroes a model with textured/curlier hair but it's not consistent throughout, which can make the targeting feel unfocused.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a single concrete incentive.** Even "Free shipping on orders over $35" or "Buy 2 hair products, get a free scalp treatment" turns this editorial into an action trigger. Lush's recent cadence is heavy on editorial and light on conversion mechanics; this is where it hurts.
- 2. **Fix the preheader.** Write a real 80-90 character preheader line that reinforces the scalp/hair angle — right now the inbox trio (sender, subject, preheader) is doing only two-thirds of the work.
- 3. **Make the hero CTA copy legible.** At minimum, bold the button text; better yet, A/B between "Shop Scalp Care" and "Find Your Hair Fix" to match the section language.
- 4. **Add a time anchor.** "Summer heat is rough on your scalp — here's the fix" or a simple "July routine" framing would make this feel intentional rather than evergreen.
- 5. **Lean into the Roots/Ginger star ratings.** Pull a real review snippet into the Scalp Saviors section — one sentence from a customer with a concrete result ("my itchy scalp cleared up in a week") would do more than the product card blurb.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` element**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some webmail clients (Yahoo, AOL) render `<title>` as the browser tab label. An empty value produces a blank or domain-derived tab title. Should contain a descriptive string matching the subject line.

**Mobile breakpoint at 520px (non-standard)**
The single media query fires at `max-width:520px`. The de facto email standard is 600px. Clients rendering at 521–600px viewport width will receive the desktop layout, which may overflow on mid-size screens (e.g., tablets in portrait, some Android clients).

**Link underline suppression in Gmail**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none; }
```
This strips underlines from all anchors inside Gmail's `#MessageViewBody`. Combined with body-text-colored links, this fails WCAG 1.4.1 (use of color as the only visual differentiator). If CTAs rely on button styling this is lower risk, but inline text links become indistinguishable.

**`lang="en"` present** — correct; no issue.
