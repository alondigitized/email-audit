# This style has limited availability!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-14**
## Executive summary

- This is Skechers' browse-abandonment template again, the ninth or so abandonment-style send in two weeks. It follows 11/13 "This is perfect for you!" and the two 11/12 sends. The subject promises scarcity ("limited availability"), but the body says "HOW ABOUT A SECOND LOOK? SEEN THIS BEFORE? WE THOUGHT SO." Nothing in the render supports the limited-availability claim: no low-stock badge and no count.
- The layout is clean. A single hero shoe, the Britto S-1992 Landscapes sneaker, sits over a navy SHOP NOW button. Below it is a six-shoe grid of running and walking styles, and then a navy Black Friday band: "BUY ONE, GET ONE 50% OFF or 20% OFF A SINGLE PAIR." The hero is a colorful retro court sneaker, while the grid is all performance walkers and runners. That mismatch weakens the "options we think you'll love" framing.
- Judgement: competent, but fatiguing and mildly misleading. The BOGO offer is strong and well placed, but it is buried under a hero that sells nothing and a long footer-style nav stack.

## What's working

- The headline is large and navy, and the hero shoe is well isolated. The eye lands on it first.
- The Black Friday band is high contrast, with "BUY ONE, GET ONE 50% OFF" as the biggest text after the headline. The offer is concrete.
- The six-shoe grid gives variety across men's and women's, with product names under each shoe.
- "Discount auto-applied at cart" removes the friction of a promo code.
- The render shows no broken images or overlapping text.

## What's weak

- The subject says "limited availability," but the body shows no stock or scarcity signal. The hero copy ("Second look?") doesn't match it.
- The hero shoe has no price, no sale price and no sizing or availability detail.
- The grid shoes are tiny and have no prices, and the product text is very small. The grid mixes athletic styles with a fashion hero.
- The offer is below the fold and secondary. The "SHOP NOW" inside the banner is a small text link, not a button.
- The nav stack (Women/Men/Kids/Clothing/Gift Guide/Sale), app badges, Afterpay/Klarna and the SMS block make the email feel long and generic.
- The fine print is dense, and the offer is limited to select regular-priced shoes. Sale styles are excluded, which sits oddly with a SALE nav link.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the BOGO offer into the hero, with the abandoned shoe pictured next to it. Use "Your Britto S-1992 + BOGO 50% off" and a button that says "Get my pair."
- 2. Make the subject match the body. Use real scarcity only if inventory data supports it. Otherwise drop "limited availability."
- 3. Add price and a star rating under the hero shoe and the grid items.
- 4. Make the grid relevant. Use items related to the browsed style, not generic runners.
- 5. Cut the footer clutter (app, curbside, Afterpay, Klarna). Keep one secondary block.
- 6. Space abandonment sends out. Suppress repeats within 48 hours and vary the creative.
