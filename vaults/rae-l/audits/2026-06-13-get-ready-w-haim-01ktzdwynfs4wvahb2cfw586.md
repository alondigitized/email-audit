# Get ready w/ HAIM
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-13**
## Executive summary

- This is the third HAIM x ILIA send in five days — and the most commercially complete. Where June 9 launched the collab and June 11 leaned on Este Haim's clinical testimonial, this email zooms out to all three sisters simultaneously in a "get ready with" format: each Haim gets her own module, her quote, and an ILIA product tied to her routine. The structural concept is solid and the art direction carries the brand's signature clean-beauty aesthetic cleanly.
- The giveaway at the bottom ("Win A Signed Vinyl + $500") is the most actionable thing in the email and it's buried. A signed vinyl from HAIM is a genuine fan incentive — that hook should be in the subject line, not the scroll-past footer. Instead, "Get ready w/ HAIM" is vague Instagram caption energy in a medium that rewards specificity.
- The email works as a brand editorial and a nice capstone to the arc. It does not work as a conversion email — there are no prices visible, no product CTAs with strong copy, and no discount. If the goal was clicks to product pages, this undershoots. If the goal was giveaway entries, the structure is backwards.

## What's working

- **Three-sister structure gives the email visual rhythm** — the repeating module (headshot → quote → product) makes it scannable without demanding reading.
- **Giveaway hook is genuinely compelling** — signed vinyl + $500 in ILIA is a fan-native prize, not a generic coupon.
- **Clean render, no visual bugs** — layout holds, images load, no broken gaps or overlapping elements.
- **Brand aesthetic is consistent** — muted tones, editorial photography, and ILIA's signature minimalism carry through every module.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is weak and abbreviated** — "w/" is social-media shorthand; it reads like a TikTok caption, not inbox copy that earns an open.
- **Giveaway is buried below three content modules** — the most action-driving element is the last thing a reader sees.
- **No visible product pricing or offer** — this is a pure editorial send; there's no path to purchase beyond "scroll and hope."
- **Este Haim's module echoes the June 11 send** — subscribers who opened that email are getting another Este quote without new information, which reads as filler.
- **Individual CTA buttons are too small to parse at mobile scale** — it's unclear what each sister's section links to.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the giveaway to the subject and hero** — "Win a Signed HAIM Vinyl (+ $500 in ILIA)" would be the most open- and click-driving subject this arc has sent. The editorial content can follow the giveaway CTA.
- 2. **Replace Este's module with something new** — she had the spotlight on June 11. Use this email for Alana and Danielle, who haven't had featured moments yet, and keep Este only in the giveaway framing.
- 3. **Add a shop link to each sister module with product name and price** — "Alana uses Super Serum Skin Tint in Dusk — $58" gives a click target and a reason to click.
- 4. **Sharpen the "Get ready" concept** — tie it to a moment (summer tour, live show, festival season) so the phrase lands rather than floats.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Win a Signed HAIM Vinyl + $500 in ILIA`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Alana, Este & Danielle's ILIA get-ready`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Enter now — plus see each sister's must-have product.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Their routines. Your chance to win a signed vinyl.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Font loading via `@import` in `<style>` block:**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```
Gmail (webmail) strips `<style>` blocks that contain `@import`. Montserrat and the custom Klaviyo font will silently fall back to `"Helvetica", sans-serif` in Gmail. This is a known Klaviyo limitation; fallback stack is defined, so rendering degrades gracefully rather than breaking — but verify the fallback renders acceptably at the brand level.

**No hover differentiation on links:**
```css
a:hover { color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none }
```
Hover color is identical to default. In desktop clients that render hover states, there is no visual affordance that the element is interactive. Not a blocker, but an accessibility note.

**Empty `<title>` tag:**

Some clients (notably older Outlook versions) display the title. Should contain the subject line or brand name rather than a single space.
