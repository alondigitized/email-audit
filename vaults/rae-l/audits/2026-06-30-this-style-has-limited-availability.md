# This style has limited availability!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-30**
## Executive summary

- This is Skechers' latest entry in a now deeply fatigued abandonment sequence — and it brings two damning pieces of baggage before a single line of copy lands. First: the subject line "This style has limited availability!" is a straight recycle from the May 27 send, audited 33 days ago at 6/10. Second: the hero product is the SKX NEXUS, the exact shoe Skechers pushed yesterday (June 29, "This is perfect for you!"). A subscriber who opened both emails would see the same sneaker, two days in a row, under a month-old subject line.
- The body copy tries to salvage things with "HOW ABOUT A SECOND LOOK? SEEN THIS BEFORE? WE THOUGHT SO." — self-aware, winking abandonment language that's actually decent. The render is clean, the hero image is attractive. But the subject promises scarcity and the body delivers none of it: no low-stock callout, no specific quantity, no countdown. It's bait-and-switch by omission.
- At this point in the sequence, Skechers isn't recovering a cart — it's training unsubscribes. The email needs a fundamentally different hook or a real offer, not another "SHOP NOW" against a shoe the subscriber has now seen three times.

## What's working

- Hero image is clean and appealing — the mint/teal SKX NEXUS photographs well against the white background.
- "HOW ABOUT A SECOND LOOK? SEEN THIS BEFORE? WE THOUGHT SO." is self-aware copy that at least acknowledges the abandonment dynamic without being aggressive.
- Render is fully intact — no broken images, no overlapping text, no layout breaks.
- "SHOP NOW" CTA button is immediately visible and high-contrast above the fold.
- Footer payment options (Afterpay, Klarna) are a quiet but real conversion assist for price-sensitive browsers.

## What's weak

- Subject line is a direct repeat of the May 27 send — any engaged subscriber has seen it before.
- SKX NEXUS appeared as the hero product in yesterday's email (June 29); this is now the second consecutive day featuring the identical shoe.
- "Limited availability" in the subject goes completely unsubstantiated in the body — no stock count, no urgency copy, no deadline.
- No discount or offer of any kind — after 7+ abandonment touches, there is nothing new to give the subscriber a reason to act.
- The six-thumbnail product grid below the hero is too small to be compelling and functions as visual noise rather than genuine discovery.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Introduce an actual offer.** This sequence has now run without a discount for seven-plus emails. A single 10% or 15% off coupon code — even time-gated to 48 hours — would outperform every creative tweak on this list. That's the only lever left.
- 2. **Retire this subject line.** It was filed, used in May, and is now in the audit trail. Reusing it tells any subscriber who opens consistently that the program is on autopilot.
- 3. **Rotate the hero product.** The SKX NEXUS has been the hero two days in a row. Pull a second product from the grid into the hero or surface a related style to signal variety.
- 4. **Back the scarcity claim.** If "limited availability" is true, prove it in the body: "Only 3 left in your size" or a low-stock badge on the product. If it isn't true, drop the claim entirely.
- 5. **Sharpen the CTA.** "SHOP NOW" is placeholder copy. "Get My Pair" or "Grab It Before It's Gone" would align with the subject's urgency promise.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Only a few pairs left — grab yours`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your SKX NEXUS is almost gone`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stock is running low. Here's your last real chance.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `This is the nudge. Low inventory, no restocking date.`
