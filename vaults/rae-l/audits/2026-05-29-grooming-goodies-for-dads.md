# Grooming goodies for Dads
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-29**
## Executive summary

- This is a seasonal Father's Day editorial pivot from Lush, structured as a three-step grooming guide: enzymic face wash → moisturizer → beard oil. The hero image earns it — a real bearded man in a plaid shirt, not stock photography, instantly grounding the category. The numbered product flow mirrors an actual grooming routine, which is smart editorial logic.
- What's missing is everything a gift-occasion email needs to close. No price points. No delivery deadline. No urgency. The May 27 "Stop and smell the flowers" send (9/10) used the same editorial structure but had the advantage of being a passive discovery send — you don't need a deadline to browse fragrance families. Father's Day is a hard date. This email treats it like a catalog rather than a conversion moment, and that's a real miss.
- Verdict: solid creative, weak conversion mechanics. A marketer could lift this significantly with two additions: "Order by [date]" and product prices on every card.

## What's working

- Hero model is genuinely on-brief — a real, full-bearded man validates the grooming category immediately
- Numbered three-step product flow (wash → moisturize → oil) mirrors an actual routine and makes the cross-sell feel earned, not stuffed
- Ingredient callouts per product (argan, jojoba, aloe vera, cedarwood, sandalwood) add credibility without cluttering the layout
- "Breeze Into Beard Care" headline is punchy and on-voice for Lush
- Free shipping threshold banner is visible and sets a basket-building anchor before the scroll begins

## What's weak

- Zero price points in the entire email — gift-givers need price anchoring; this send provides none
- No Father's Day delivery deadline anywhere — the most important urgency mechanic for a hard-date occasion is completely absent
- Preheader is the generic shipping banner ("Enjoy free shipping on all orders $80+"), not a Father's Day hook — a wasted first impression in the inbox
- "Gifts for Dads" bottom section uses a blue bath bomb on a blue background — low contrast, visually muddled, and "limited edition" copy doesn't specify *which* products are actually limited
- Third email in five days (May 24, 27, 29) — even a seasonal justification doesn't fully offset cadence fatigue

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add an "Order by [date]" delivery deadline** — one line above the hero CTA. "Order by June 12 for Father's Day delivery" is the single highest-leverage change in this email. Without it, urgency is zero.
- 2. **Add price points to every product card** — "$14," "from $12," whatever the tier. Gift purchases stall without price context. This alone would lift click-through materially.
- 3. **Rewrite the preheader** to extend the Father's Day narrative instead of leaking the generic shipping banner.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Gift dad great skin (and a great beard)`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Dad's new grooming routine — from Lush`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Beard oils, face wash, and moisturizer made for real dads — order by June 12`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The Father's Day grooming kit he'd actually use`
- 4. **Promote the beard oil section to #2**, swapping Cosmetic Lad to #3 — beard oil is more distinctly giftable and more specifically "dad" than a moisturizer; let it anchor the middle of the email
- 5. **Fix the Gifts for Dads section contrast** — either lighten the background or swap to the white product-card treatment used above; "limited edition" needs a badge on specific products to mean anything
