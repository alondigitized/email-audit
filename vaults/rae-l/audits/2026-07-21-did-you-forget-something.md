# Did you forget something?
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- This is the fourth "Did you forget something?" send from Skechers since May 19 — and it arrived just six days after the July 15 send, which itself was already the third. The cadence has accelerated from roughly every six weeks to now every six days, which is a flashing unsubscribe signal. The creative is clean and competent: three specific abandoned products render correctly, hierarchy is clear, and the "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" CTA is prominent. But none of that matters when the inbox is this saturated.
- The email offers no price reduction, no deadline, and no new hook to justify the re-send. The July 15 send showed the exact same abandonment template; this is structurally identical. Skechers has also sent "Your Items Are Waiting!" and "This style has limited availability!" in the four days between July 15 and today — meaning the subscriber has received five abandonment or urgency emails in six days. That is list-burning behavior.
- This send is not bad email production. It is bad send strategy. The render is clean, the products are specific, and the CTA works. What it cannot overcome is the four-send repetition of an identical subject line on a six-day cadence.

## What's working

- Three specific product images with full names render correctly — no broken assets, no lorem ipsum.
- Visual hierarchy is tight: hero → specific products → primary CTA → best sellers cascade → navigation. The eye follows the intended path.
- "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" is unambiguous — a concrete verb + noun, not "Discover" or "Shop Now."
- Summer color palette (warm golds, pink Care Bears shoes) is seasonally appropriate for July.
- Payment flexibility (Afterpay, Klarna) is surfaced in the footer — a genuine friction-reducer for a cart-complete nudge.

## What's weak

- **Fourth identical subject line in two months, sent six days after the last.** The July 15 audit already called this the third send; a sixth-day gap to a fourth is acceleration in the wrong direction.
- No offer. No discount, no free shipping threshold, no loyalty member pricing — there is no new reason to complete the order that wasn't present when the shopper left.
- The best sellers block beneath the abandoned products features men's running shoes (Men's Go Walk, Men's Slip-ins), while the abandoned items are Care Bears collab and pink Arch Fits — a demographic mismatch that undermines the personalization signal.
- No urgency mechanism is visible — no countdown, no low-stock copy, no deadline. The hero copy "READY TO CHECK OUT?" is a question, not a reason to act now.
- Preheader is not visible in this render — whatever copy (if any) is loading there is invisible in this web view, wasting the second-most-read inbox field.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Retire this subject line.** Four sends of "Did you forget something?" has trained the subscriber to ignore it. The next send must lead with the specific product, a concrete benefit, or a first-time offer — not a rhetorical question they've seen four times.
- 2. **Add a one-time incentive on the fourth touch.** A 10% or free-shipping offer on final follow-up converts the fence-sitters this sequence has failed to close. Without a new hook, send four is just noise.
- 3. **Kill the men's best sellers block** or swap it to women's/Care Bears adjacencies. Serving gender-mismatched product recommendations below a personalized Care Bears cart is a personalization fail visible to the subscriber.
- 4. **Write a real preheader.** The web render shows none — that is an inbox character slot going to waste or leaking technical junk.
- 5. **Space the sequence out.** Six days between sends three and four is aggressive to the point of being hostile. Fourteen days minimum; or suppress if the subscriber opened the July 15 send and still did not click.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Three styles you viewed — complete your order today and get free shipping`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The Brighten The Day pink is going fast — don't leave it behind`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

| Issue | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Empty `<title>` | `<title></title>` — some assistive technologies read this; should be set to the subject line |
| Link color/decoration suppression | `#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none }` — kills visual link affordance in Gmail webmail; users cannot distinguish clickable text |
| No dark mode handling | Background hardcoded as `background-color:#fff` with no `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block — white background will invert harshly in dark mode clients (Apple Mail, iOS Mail) |
| Image alt text | Cannot confirm from truncated source; `img+div { display:none }` selector suggests fallback divs exist but alt attributes on `<img>` tags are unverified |
| Preheader spacer characters | Mix of U+034F (Combining Grapheme Joiner `͏`) and U+00AD (soft hyphen `­`) used for padding — functional but soft hyphens may render as visible dashes in some edge clients |
