# $15 for your thoughts
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-04**
## Executive summary

- This is a survey-for-credit email, and it works as one. A tight face-to-face hero with "Let's Catch Up" sets a warm, personal tone. Two short paragraphs make the ask plain: take a 5-minute survey and get a $15 site credit. The offer is honest and concrete, and it's the only thing the email asks for.
- The weakness is that the email is purely transactional. It doesn't show a product, and the "$15" sits in body copy instead of the hero. The "Get Started" button doesn't say what you're starting. A second "ILIA / Makeup That Makes Your Skin Better" banner repeats the brand and adds nothing. After the last few sends (shade-finding, skin tint, blush layering), this is a sensible change of pace, but it's under-sold.
- Verdict: a clean, trustworthy, low-effort send. It will probably get opens from loyal subscribers, but the value exchange could be much clearer above the fold.

## What's working

- The hero is warm and human, and the white "Let's Catch Up" headline is legible over the skin tones.
- The offer is specific and honest: 5 minutes for a $15 credit, with no hidden conditions visible.
- There is one CTA, in a solid rose button with strong contrast against the cream background.
- The copy is short and conversational, and it explains why ILIA is asking ("deliver more of what you're into").
- The footer trust badges (Leaping Bunny, 1% for the Planet, Pact) and the free-shipping line reinforce the brand without clutter.

## What's weak

- The $15 is buried in the second paragraph, below a tall hero that has no offer in it.
- "Get Started →" is vague. It doesn't mention the survey or the credit.
- The mid-email "Makeup That Makes Your Skin Better" banner is a flat tile with no product, link cue or purpose, so it looks like filler.
- There is no deadline, no credit expiry and no survey-length reassurance beyond "5-minute". Nothing makes it feel urgent.
- The credit terms are only in a small footnote link, and nothing says whether the credit has a minimum spend.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put "$15" in the hero. Replace "Let's Catch Up" with something like "Tell us what you love, get $15", so the value is visible before the scroll.
- 2. Make the CTA specific: "Take the survey, get $15".
- 3. Replace the empty brand banner with one or two bestsellers, or a line saying what the credit can buy. This gives the credit a destination.
- 4. Add a credible deadline and brief terms, for example "Credit valid 30 days, no minimum".
- 5. Add a small social-proof or "what we'll do with your answers" line to raise completion.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Get $15 for a 5-min survey`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your $15 ILIA credit is waiting`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Answer a few questions about your skin and shade, and we'll send the credit.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `5 minutes now, $15 toward your next ILIA order.`
