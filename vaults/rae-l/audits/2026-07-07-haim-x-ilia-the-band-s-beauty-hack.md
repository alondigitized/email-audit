# HAIM x ILIA—the band’s beauty hack
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-07**
## Executive summary

- After five consecutive promotional emails in the "Buy 3, Save 20%" run (July 1–5) and yesterday's GWP mini tote reset, ILIA goes fully editorial today. This is a brand-building send, not a conversion send: no offer, no urgency, just three HAIM sisters vouching for Skin Tint through candid, personal quotes. That's a deliberate and correct cadence choice — the list got four days of discounting, so a prestige editorial pivot is exactly the antidote to discount fatigue.
- The execution is polished. "Hits every note" is a clean music pun, the three-section structure gives each sister equal real estate and creates natural scroll momentum, and the Skin Tint positioning (makeup + skincare + SPF all in one) lands in the very first sentence. The brand voice stays consistent throughout — aspirational but warm.
- The weakness is the CTA. "BAND APPROVED" functions as a badge above the fold, not a button, so a subscriber who finishes reading and wants to buy has to hunt for where to click. For a pure content send that's survivable, but one clear mid-scroll button would lift conversion without breaking the editorial tone.

## What's working

- **Celebrity real-talk copy** earns its credibility: Este's sensitive-skin admission, Alana's "I didn't grow up wearing sunscreen" SPF confession, and Danielle's "put it on my fingers" simplicity all sound unscripted.
- **"Hits every note" wordplay** (plus the music note emoji) is a clean nod to HAIM being a band — clever without being labored.
- **Three-section structure** distributes attention and creates scroll momentum; each sister gets her own headline, portrait, and testimonial.
- **Product pitch is front-loaded**: "makeup + skincare + SPF all in one" appears in the intro section, not buried at the bottom.
- **Cadence reset works**: editorial send after a promotional run is exactly the move that prevents subscriber burnout.

## What's weak

- **No offer or discount** anywhere in the visible render — this is pure content, which suppresses immediate click intent.
- **"BAND APPROVED" is a badge, not a CTA** — the primary above-fold action is ambiguous; it's not clear to a skimmer where to tap.
- **Este Haim's quote is truncated mid-sentence**: "It felt like a goddamn" cuts off abruptly — this reads either as a copy error or a hard render crop.
- **Product imagery is delayed**: the Skin Tint bottle appears partway down the email; a hero product shot earlier would anchor the editorial to a SKU for skimmers who don't read prose.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a real CTA button after the intro block** — "Shop Skin Tint →" before Este's quote so skimmers have a clear tap target without reaching the footer.
- 2. **Fix the truncated Este quote** — restore the full quote or cut cleanly before "goddamn." A garbled endorsement undercuts credibility.
- 3. **Move product imagery into the hero or intro** — first-time openers should see the Skin Tint tube before they read three paragraphs of quotes.
- 4. **Layer in a soft discovery hook** (not a discount): "See how they layer it" or "Meet the trio's daily edit" gives a click reason that fits editorial tone without requiring a promo.
