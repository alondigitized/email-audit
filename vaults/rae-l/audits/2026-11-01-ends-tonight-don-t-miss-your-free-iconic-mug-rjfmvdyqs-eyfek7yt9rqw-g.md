# Ends Tonight! Don't miss your free iconic mug ☕
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-01**
## Executive summary

- This is the last-chance send for the Three Eyed Smiley Mug gift-with-purchase. The offer is clear: "ENDS TONIGHT / Get a free Three Eyed Smiley Mug with your $75+ order*". The mug photo, with marshmallows in a pink cup with a red handle, is charming and fills the right half of the hero. The eye lands on the headline and mug first, and the "Shop now" button is directly below.
- The email leans on repetition rather than new information. Glossier has run gift-with-purchase campaigns for about nine days. The Milky Jelly Cleanser had three sends (10/22, 10/24 and the "Last call" on 10/26). The mug had "Get a free Three Eyed Smiley Mug!" on 10/30, and this is the follow-up. A subscriber has seen this pattern before and knows another gift will probably appear. That weakens the "ends tonight" urgency. The subject's "iconic" is also a stretch for a mug that has been around for days.
- The lower half is generic. "START HERE" shows three products (You Doux, Boy Brow, Black Cherry Lip Line + Balm Dotcom) with no price, no reason to pick them, and no tie to the $75 threshold. The third product name is cut off at the right edge. The email works as a reminder but does little to help someone reach $75. Verdict: competent last-call, held back by cadence fatigue and a weak lower half.

## What's working

- The hero is clear: "ENDS TONIGHT" plus a named gift and a $75+ threshold.
- The mug photo is warm and seasonal, and the marshmallows suggest cozy-season gifting.
- The copy is short and on brand: "Hurry, this (super cute) offer ends tonight!"
- The "Shop now" button sits right under the hero and is easy to find.
- The product row gives a quick way to start building toward the $75 threshold.

## What's weak

- The third product name is cut off ("Black Cherry Lip Line + Balm D…"), and the third card runs off the right edge.
- All four buttons say a generic "Shop now", so the CTA copy doesn't say what the click leads to.
- The products show no prices, so nothing tells the shopper how close they are to $75.
- This is another gift-with-purchase send in a series, which dulls the urgency.
- The "Terms apply" disclaimer is tiny grey italic text at the bottom, though the asterisk on the offer is easy to notice.

## Recommendations

- 1. Replace the generic product row with items that make $75 easy to reach, such as a "Build your $75 order" set with prices. A "Shop $75 favorites" button would give the row a purpose.
- 2. Give the main CTA specific copy, such as "Claim your free mug", and use "Add to bag" on the product cards.
- 3. Fix the clipped third card so the product name and layout fit within the frame.
- 4. Make the urgency concrete with an exact deadline, such as "Ends tonight at 11:59pm PT", rather than leaning on "tonight" alone.
- 5. Break the gift-with-purchase pattern when possible, for instance by adding a note on how many mugs are left. A real stock signal would be stronger than a repeated deadline.
