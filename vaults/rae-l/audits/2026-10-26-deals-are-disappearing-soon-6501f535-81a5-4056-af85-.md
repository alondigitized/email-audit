# Deals are disappearing soon...
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-26**
## Executive summary

- This is a Halloween-framed "last chance" deals roundup with a real offer menu. The strongest part is the code-based banner at the top: "$10 off $50 or $20 off $75 online with code SPOOKY. Ends 10/27." Below it are four clear product tiles. The weakness is the hero. "You should be scared … of missing these deals" is a pun with no offer in it, and a huge photo of blue nails with black slit pupils fills about a fifth of the email. The nails don't connect to any of the four deals below.
- As a subscriber who got "Just in time for Halloween 🎃" on 10/16 and "A gift for the guys" on 10/20, I'd read this as the clearance-style closer to the month. The urgency is credible because the code expires the next day. The email makes a reader scroll past the hero and a tall image to reach anything concrete. It's decent, but it buries its best offer.
- The tiles are clean and scannable, with specific percentages and a "Shop now" link on each. The rewards strip, Klarna banner and four-icon service row at the bottom are useful but dilute the close. Judgement: solid mid-tier promo, held back by a hero that doesn't sell anything.

## What's working

- The SPOOKY code banner names two spend tiers and a deadline, and it sits above the hero.
- The four tiles each show one product and one specific discount: 20% off men's grooming, BOGO 50% off lashes, 50% off OPI Chrome Effects, 25% off hair care.
- The alternating left/right tile layout reads cleanly and makes each deal easy to scan.
- The pale pink rewards bar ($5 off $25 for signing up) is visible but doesn't compete with the hero.
- The hero adds in-store pickup and "Buy online, pick up in-store today," which is a helpful use-case cue.

## What's weak

- The hero headline has no offer. The code and the dollar savings are in a thin banner above it.
- The nail photo is very tall and has no tie to the tiles. It pushes the first real deal about 600px down the screen.
- The subject "Deals are disappearing soon..." is vague. It names no discount, and the ellipsis reads as spammy.
- There are too many CTAs and too much clutter: the rewards bar, the code banner, four "Shop now" links, Klarna, the icon row and an app download.
- The 10/27 deadline is only in small banner text. The hero says "won't last long" with no date.
- This is the fifth push of the $5 off $25 rewards offer since early October, which adds to the repetition.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the SPOOKY offer into the hero headline, e.g. "$10 off $50 online. Code SPOOKY. Ends tomorrow." Add a "Shop the deals" button with the code visible.
- 2. Shrink the nail image to a short banner, or swap it for a collage of the four tiles' products.
- 3. Add a countdown or a plain "Ends 10/27" line next to the hero button.
- 4. Drop the rewards strip and the Klarna and app blocks from this send, since they've been hit repeatedly this month.
- 5. Lead the tiles with the biggest draw (BOGO lashes or 50% off OPI) rather than men's grooming.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Code SPOOKY: 50% off OPI, BOGO lashes`

- **Preheader Alt B:** `BOGO lashes, 50% off OPI Chrome and 25% off hair care. Ends tomorrow.`

# Technical Audit: Sally Beauty, "Deals are disappearing soon..."

## 1. Technical Summary
The 57% pass rate comes from 3 warnings and 0 hard failures. The confirmed problems are in the third-party tracking pixels: an unresolved `[CACHEBUSTER]` macro, an `http://` pixel, and an empty-`src` image. The compliance and authentication warnings are most likely artifacts of the AgentMail relay and can't be confirmed from this data.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **51 tracking links were skipped.** They use click-redirect domains, so the HTTP probe didn't run. Destination validity, redirect chains and link rot are unverified.
- **Unresolved macro in a pixel URL.** `https://t.myvisualiq.net/impression_pixel?r=[CACHEBUSTER]&et=i&ago=212...` still contains the literal `[CACHEBUSTER]`. The vendor never substituted it, so the pixel can be cached and impressions undercounted or deduplicated.
- **Empty-`src` image.** QA flagged an `<img>` with `src: ` (blank). It produces a broken request and may fire a request to the page or base URL in some clients.
- **Third-party pixel stack.** `pxl.mon-trk.com/987bfe61-.../2328305/003aZ00000rqS8GQAU`, `t.myvisualiq.net` and `login.dotomi.com/ucm/UCMController` are each an extra off-domain request.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt on 4 images.** These are the empty-src image, `003aZ00000rqS8GQAU`, `impression_pixel` and `UCMController`. The three tracking pixels are likely 1×1, so they should carry `alt=""` and `width="1" height="1"`, plus `role="presentation"` or `aria-hidden="true"` if they must stay. Without that, screen readers may announce the filename or URL.
- **Non-HTTPS image.** `http://login.dotomi.com/ucm/UCMController?...` is a mixed-content source. Gmail's image proxy and some clients may block it, so the pixel won't fire. Switch it to `https://`.
- **Template markup.** I saw nothing wrong in the visible head: it has an XHTML doctype, VML/Office namespaces, the viewport meta, `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`, and an MSO conditional. The HTML was truncated, so body structure, `<table role="presentation">` usage and font fallbacks are unverified. `a { font-family:'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif }` has no `@font-face` in the visible CSS, but it falls back correctly.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Unresolved token:** `[CACHEBUSTER]` in the `myvisualiq` URL, as in section 2.
- **Identifier in a URL.** The path segment `003aZ00000rqS8GQAU` looks like a Salesforce Contact ID (the `003` prefix). It is embedded in a third-party pixel URL, which is a privacy consideration. It is rendered, not an unresolved merge field.
- I found no unrendered `%%…%%`, `{{…}}` or `${…}` tokens in the visible source.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **No `List-Unsubscribe` or `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) header found.** QA itself says the relay may not capture them, so I can't call this a sender defect. Check the raw message headers from a direct inbox, because Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require one-click unsubscribe.
- **No `Authentication-Results` header.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment for `em.sallybeauty.com` are unknown. The relay strips this, so verify it by other means.
- **Footer not seen.** The visible HTML is truncated, so the unsubscribe link and postal address in the body are unverified.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not assessable. All 51 tracked links were skipped, so landing URLs, UTM parameters and destination alignment are unverified. This is a data gap, not a confirmed defect.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Fix the macro.** Have the ESP or ad vendor resolve `[CACHEBUSTER]` in the `myvisualiq` pixel at send time, or remove the pixel.
2. **Remove or repair the empty-`src` `<img>`.**
3. **Move `login.dotomi.com/ucm/UCMController` to `https://`**, or drop it if the vendor doesn't support it.
4. **Mark tracking pixels as decorative:** `alt=""`, `width="1" height="1"`, `style="display:block"`.
5. **Re-run QA with a direct-delivery copy** to capture `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results`. Then re-probe by resolving the 51 redirects to get final URLs and UTM values.
6. **Review the pixel stack.** Three third-party trackers plus a contact ID in a URL add weight, privacy exposure and deliverability risk. Keep only those that are in use.
