# New I ♥️ NY merch just arrived!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- This is Glossier's merch drop reveal — the fourth I ❤️ NY send in three days (July 14 launch, July 15 follow-up, July 16 this email). The creative holds together: a Big Apple Tee ($32), an Applebaby Keychain ($18), a free sticker pack hook, and a catch-all "Take a bite" shop module, all united by the NYC apple motif. The photography earns its place — the tee shot is breezy editorial, and the keychain hanging from a chain-link fence feels genuinely downtown. Glossier expanding into lifestyle merch (a physical tee, a keychain) is a brand beat worth a dedicated email.
- The problem is cadence saturation. A subscriber who opened July 14 (launch), July 15 (remix), and now this is effectively being sent the same creative world for the third consecutive day. "Just arrived" is doing real urgency work in the subject, but it competes against inbox fatigue from the arc. The free sticker hook is the smartest move here — it's a genuine pull that the prior two sends didn't have — but it's buried three modules deep.
- The email is competent and on-brand, but it's fighting its own cadence. A consolidated merch-plus-product drop, or a one-day gap, would have landed harder.

## What's working

- **Free sticker hook is genuinely compelling** — a $0 offer with a dedicated CTA ("Claim pack") is the strongest action driver in the email.
- **Keychain editorial photography** — chain-link fence setting is gritty and NYC-specific, not stock-y.
- **Apple naming system is airtight** — Big Apple Tee, Applebaby Keychain, Big Apple Sticker Pack: the naming is cohesive and memorable.
- **Clean single-column layout** — no render bugs, each product gets its own scannable module, CTAs are consistently placed.
- **Subject delivers the news crisply** — "New I ♥️ NY merch just arrived!" is 31 characters, zero ambiguity, emoji adds personality without spam signals.

## What's weak

- **Third consecutive NYC email in three days** — cadence fatigue is real; subscribers who opened July 14 and 15 are seeing the same creative world again.
- **Hero CTA copy is muddled** — the first CTA button appears to read "See a bite" or similar, which reads as a pun before it reads as a call to action.
- **Free sticker offer is buried below the fold** — the strongest pull in the email is the third module down; it should be surfaced earlier.
- **No sizing or availability signal for the tee** — apparel requires friction-reducing detail (sizes in stock, fit note) that's entirely absent.
- **No urgency or scarcity** — "just arrived" in the subject doesn't carry into the body; nothing tells the reader this is limited.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the free sticker pack to module one or call it out in the hero** — the free offer is the reason to act now; bury it and you're just showing a product catalog.
- 2. **Rewrite the hero CTA** — replace the wordplay button ("See a bite"?) with something that drives commerce: "Shop the I ♥ NY Collection" or "Get the tee."
- 3. **Add one sizing/fit line under the Big Apple Tee** — "Runs true to size, S–2XL" is all it takes to reduce click hesitation on apparel.
- 4. **Introduce scarcity or time-bound language in the body** — "Limited run" or "Get yours before it's gone" does more work than the current module copy.
- 5. **Pull back cadence one day** — this email would have hit harder on July 17, after a day without an NYC send.
