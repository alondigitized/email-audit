# Line up—something new arrives 07.28
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- This is a pure launch-teaser send — no product revealed, no discount, just "something new arrives 07.28" anchored by a dramatic black-and-white close-up of a closed eye with heavy lashes. The wordplay in the subject and the CTA ("LINE UP") is doing real work — "line" reads simultaneously as queue-up and eyeliner, and the Italian "thru" in the hero overlay skews young/TikTok in a way that feels earned rather than forced for ILIA's aesthetic. As a pattern break from six consecutive editorial/promotional sends this past week, this is exactly the right moment to go sparse.
- The mechanic — join the waitlist, enter to win a $1,000 PR mailer — is well-chosen. It builds a list, creates anticipation, and the sweepstakes prize is legitimate and on-brand. The weakness is that the giveaway is buried at the body-copy level and never becomes the visual hero it deserves to be; a subscriber scanning this in two seconds sees a pretty eye and "LINE UP" and may not register the $1,000 prize at all.
- Strong concept, well-executed for a teaser. The email does one job and does it cleanly. It does not ask for a purchase and should not — that comes 07.28.

## What's working

- **Pattern break is perfectly timed.** After six consecutive editorial/promo sends (including two on 07.20–07.21 in rapid succession), dropping a spare teaser resets subscriber attention before the launch.
- **"LINE UP →" CTA is the email's best line.** It's a tight verb, it plays on eyeliner, and it communicates scarcity/anticipation without any manufactured countdown-timer gimmick.
- **The eye hero is unambiguous category signaling.** Even without a product reveal, the lash close-up tells a beauty subscriber exactly what family of product is likely coming — eye, liner, mascara.
- **Email is appropriately short.** Nothing to scroll past; the entire message is above or just below the first screen. Right call for a teaser format.
- **The $1,000 sweepstakes prize is stated clearly** with an explicit dollar value — credible, non-vague, no asterisk.

## What's weak

- **The $1,000 sweepstakes is buried in body copy, not a visual module.** The prize is the second-most compelling reason to click and it's getting maybe 12px of text. It should be its own callout or a secondary headline.
- **"Something new" is doing too little work.** Even a category tease — "something for your eyes" — would spike curiosity without spoiling the reveal. Blank-slate vagueness doesn't build tension; a single hint does.
- **No urgency on the waitlist itself.** The date 07.28 gives a frame but there's no signal that the waitlist closes, is limited, or has any scarcity attached. "Line up before spots fill" would create actual urgency.
- **The feature-icon footer rail (1-for-1, Leaping Bunny, ILVIP, etc.) is wasted real estate.** In a teaser this short, a five-icon brand-values row reads as padding. Those modules belong in product/educational sends, not launch countdowns.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Elevate the sweepstakes prize visually.** Give the "win $1,000 in ILIA" its own bold callout line or a two-column visual break between the body copy and the CTA. This is the conversion hook — treat it like one.
- 2. **Add a single category tease.** One short line of copy — "You've been asking about our eyes" or "Your next favorite line is coming" — is enough to direct curiosity without revealing the product.
- 3. **Create waitlist scarcity.** Change the sub-CTA copy to "Waitlist closes 07.27" or "Limited early-access spots." This makes "LINE UP" feel urgent rather than aspirational.
- 4. **Drop the feature-icon footer rail.** It diffuses the email's intentional minimalism. Save it for a post-launch nurture.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `Your next ILIA obsession — first look 07.28`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `Line up before 07.28 (and you could win everything)`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `Enter to win a $1,000 ILIA PR haul — waitlist closes soon.`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `Something for your eyes. Waitlist open now — closes 07.27.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title> </title>
```
Some clients (Samsung Mail, older Android) surface this as the email subject fallback. Set a meaningful value or omit entirely.

**Issue — `lang` attribute missing on `<html>`:**
```html
<html xmlns="..." xmlns:o="..." xmlns:v="...">
```
No `lang="en"` declared. Screen readers (NVDA, JAWS, VoiceOver) cannot select the correct language model; WCAG 3.1.1 failure.

**Issue — Links visually indistinguishable from body text:**
```css
a:not([name]) {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
a:link    {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
a:visited {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
a:active  {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
a:hover   {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
```
All five link pseudo-states share the same color as body text with no underline. Any inline text links will be invisible to users who rely on visual differentiation. CTA buttons styled via `bgcolor` attributes are unaffected, but this rule applies globally.

**Issue — External `@import` font loads:**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```
Gmail (web), Apple Mail (privacy relay), and Outlook block external CSS imports. Montserrat will silently fall back to `Helvetica`. Not a breakage — fallback stack `"Montserrat","Helvetica",sans-serif` is present — but the import itself fires an external HTTP request that may leak open/render data in privacy-hardened clients. Consider inlining the font-face declarations if Montserrat fidelity matters.
