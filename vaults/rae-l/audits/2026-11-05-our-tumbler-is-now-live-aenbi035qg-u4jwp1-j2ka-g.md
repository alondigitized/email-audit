# OUR TUMBLER IS NOW LIVE! 
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-05**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean launch-day send for the Hot Take Tumbler, the product teased on 11/3 ("Our newest Tumbler is almost ready to sip… ☕"). The hero gets the job done. "NEW! Hot Take Tumbler" sits beside a pink bottle with a red cap, and the line "last year, it sold out in under 24 hours" supplies real scarcity. The "Shop Tumbler" button is clear.
- The email loses focus after the hero. The tumbler gets a second full block with a different photo and a slightly generic headline, "The ultimate meltdown recovery accessory". Then four limited-edition gift tiles each add a "Shop now" button. There's no price, no discount and no loyalty hook anywhere. The body is all product and mood, with no commercial reason to click today beyond the sell-out claim.
- Overall it's competent and on-brand, but undersold. The tumbler needs a price and a stronger reason to act now, and the gift grid dilutes the one product the subject promised.

## What's working

- The hero pairs a product shot with the "NEW! Hot Take Tumbler" headline and a "Shop Tumbler" button that names the product.
- "Last year, it sold out in under 24 hours" is a credible scarcity line that fits a teaser-to-launch sequence.
- The images are bright and consistent: pink tumbler, red cap, gingerbread-man balm. They feel seasonal and unmistakably Glossier.
- The "Limited edition" labels on all four gift tiles add light urgency without shouting.
- The layout is clean. Nothing overlaps, no images are broken, and the footer is tidy.

## What's weak

- No price appears anywhere, and there is no offer, free-shipping line or member benefit.
- The tumbler appears twice, with two images and two near-identical buttons, so the repeat feels like filler.
- "The ultimate meltdown recovery accessory" is cute but vague, and the tumbler's size and features aren't shown.
- The four gift tiles use the same generic "Shop now" button and have no prices, which dilutes the launch focus.
- There are no reviews or social proof beyond the one sell-out claim.
- The hero text block floats in a lot of white space beside the image, which makes the headline feel small.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the price and a hard urgency line in the hero, such as "$XX · Limited run, selling fast". Then cut the second tumbler block, or turn it into a feature callout (capacity, hot/cold hours, colorways).
- 2. Add a gift incentive or a "ships by Dec 15" note so the gifts section does more than list products.
- 3. Write specific button copy, like "Get the Tumbler" and "Shop the Duo", instead of repeating "Shop now".
- 4. Add one social-proof line, such as a review quote or "Sold out last year".

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a Stripo-built, Klaviyo-tracked XHTML 1.0 Transitional template. The QA run passed 57% of checks with 0 failures and 3 warnings. The findings that hold up are missing alt text on 7 images and link coverage that is too thin to call the links verified. The header warnings are probably relay artifacts.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Link verification is mostly incomplete.** Only 1 of 12 material links was probed, and 4 of those returned 429.
  - The four "Shop now" links that returned 429 were `ctrk.klclick.com/l/01M6P4RHB4QFWEZZBYKBCN8V0H_8`, `_10`, `_11` and `_12`. A 429 is rate limiting by the click tracker, not a broken link, but it means none of them was resolved to a destination.
  - Six links were never checked because the 70s time budget ran out: `_14`, `_16`, `_17`, `_18`, `_1…` (truncated in the QA output). They count as neither pass nor fail.
- **Every link goes through the Klaviyo click-tracking redirect** (`ctrk.klclick.com/l/…`). This is normal for the platform, but it hides the final destinations from static analysis.
- **Invalid markup near the top of `<body>`:** `<span rel="stylesheet" href="https://ctrk.klclick.com/l/01M6P4RHB4QFWEZZBYKBCN8V0H_0"></span>`. `rel` and `href` aren't valid attributes on `<span>`. It looks like an ESP artifact. It is harmless in most clients, but a strict parser or link scanner may treat it as a link.
