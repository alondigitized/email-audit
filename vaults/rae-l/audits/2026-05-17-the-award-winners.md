# The award winners
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- ILIA follows yesterday's welcome email with a press-clippings showcase — letting editorial validation from ELLE, Bustle, allure, and Vogue do the selling. The structure is confident: "Critical Acclaim" hero, then a scroll through award-winning SKUs each anchored by a pull-quote. It's a smart second email for a new subscriber who just entered a Friends + Family sale window.
- The problem is the email can't decide if it's an editorial lookbook or a sale driver. The "SHOP AWARD WINNERS" CTA lands right after the hero, which is good — but the 20% off offer is buried near the bottom after six product blocks. A subscriber who doesn't scroll misses the only financial hook entirely. Given the welcome email yesterday already opened with F+F pricing, this send should have led with it more aggressively.
- Net judgement: well-designed, credible, but structurally timid about converting. The editorial content earns trust; the offer positioning fails to cash it in.

## What's working

- **Social proof density is high.** Six editorial pull-quotes from named publications give every product an independent validator — this is unusually thorough for a product email.
- **"SHOP AWARD WINNERS" CTA is above the fold.** It appears immediately after the hero shot, before the scroll begins.
- **Clean visual system.** Product shots are consistent, white-space is generous, no render artifacts.
- **Product breadth makes sense together.** Serum tint, complexion stick, mascara, face mist, eye cream, lip balm — a coherent full-face story, not a random grab-bag.

## What's weak

- **The 20% off offer is buried at the bottom.** Six full product + quote blocks before the sale callout — many subscribers will never see it.
- **Subject "The award winners" is vague.** It names no brand, no product category, no discount. Curiosity is modest at best.
- **No urgency signal anywhere above the fold.** If the F+F sale has a deadline (it likely does — yesterday's welcome email confirmed that window), it's invisible until the scroll endpoint.
- **Two emails in two days on the same sale.** Yesterday's welcome opened with F+F; today repeats it without a clear "ends soon" escalation. Starts to feel like cadence filler rather than a fresh prompt to act.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Surface the 20% off in the hero block.** Move the sale callout (or even just a badge/ribbon) directly onto or below the hero product image. "20% off sitewide — Friends + Family" should live within the first scroll, not the last.
- 2. **Add a deadline to the CTA button.** Change "SHOP AWARD WINNERS" → "Shop Award Winners — 20% Off Ends Sunday." One line of urgency doubles the reason to click now.
- 3. **Rewrite the subject to carry the offer.** The editorial angle is the hook; the discount is the closer — the subject needs both.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Our most-awarded products — 20% off today`
- **Subject Alt B:** `ELLE, Vogue, allure agree. Now 20% off.`
- 4. **Restructure preheader to tee up the editorial framing.**
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The products press keeps calling out — and they're in your F+F sale.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Critical acclaim + 20% off: your inbox two-for-one.`
- 5. **Reduce to 3–4 featured SKUs.** Six products + six quotes is a long read. Curate the strongest two or three press moments and let them breathe; a tighter edit increases the chance a subscriber reaches the offer section.
