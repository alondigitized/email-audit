# The base layer: 20% off skincare
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- Seven emails in six days, and ILIA is playing its final card in the Friends + Family run: a clean skincare pivot. "The base layer" framing is clever — it positions skincare as the necessary foundation beneath all the makeup ILIA has been pitching since 5/16, giving the sale a new narrative angle rather than just resending "20% off." The "SALE EXTENDED" banner at the top does double duty as both urgency signal and implicit acknowledgment that the original deadline passed.
- The execution is solid. Five products — Base Face Milk, Barrier Build Serum, Bright Start Retinol Eye Cream, the NEW Sun Serum, Super Serum Skin Tint — each with a short benefit line and individual SHOP NOW CTA. The Cart Deco free gift with no minimum anchors the bottom and gives a low-commitment reason to click even for undecideds. Visual hierarchy is clean throughout.
- The problem is cadence math. Yesterday's Marissa "personal note" email was specifically designed to cut through ILIA's own inbox noise. Today's email asks the same depleted attention a second time in 24 hours. For anyone who didn't open yesterday, this is yet another skip; for anyone who did, the plain-text intimacy is immediately undercut by a full editorial blast. This email would be a strong 8 or 9 if it were email #3 of a series — arriving as #7, it's fighting its own reputation.

## What's working

- **"The base layer" framing** is the best creative decision in this send — it reframes skincare as essential infrastructure rather than an upsell, giving the sale a fresh rationale.
- **Sale Extended banner** at the very top addresses the obvious subscriber question ("wait, I thought the sale ended") before they can bounce.
- **NEW badge on the Sun Serum** is the only product flag in the lineup and correctly draws the eye to a reason to buy something they haven't seen pitched before.
- **Cart Deco free gift section** — "no minimum needed" is clean and removes the last objection for a smaller-basket buyer.
- **Individual product CTAs** with benefit copy let scanners self-select to the product that fits them without requiring a single-hero commitment.

## What's weak

- **No sale end date visible anywhere.** "SALE EXTENDED" creates urgency but "extended until when?" is unanswered, which defuses the very urgency it tries to build.
- **Product descriptions are too small to read at mobile render size** — benefit lines like "Packed with antioxidants and hyaluronic acid that strengthens..." are largely illegible at rendered size, which means the copy work is invisible on first scan.
- **No social proof.** ILIA's 5/17 awards email was built entirely on editorial validation — none of that credibility is brought forward here. A single star rating or "Best of Allure" stamp on the hero product would close the gap between browsing and buying.
- **Cadence fatigue.** Following a deliberately personal, low-design plain-text email from Marissa with a full production blast the next morning breaks the emotional arc the 5/19 plain-text was designed to create.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard deadline to the hero.** "Sale ends Sunday" (or whatever the actual date is) in the hero copy would make the extension feel bounded, not open-ended. Right now "SALE EXTENDED" reads as the brand crying wolf.
- 2. **Pull one press quote into the hero product.** The Base Face Milk or Super Serum Skin Tint has likely been covered by ELLE or Allure — one line of editorial validation above the SHOP CTA would meaningfully lift click confidence.
- 3. **Make the product benefit lines legible.** Either increase font size or reduce copy to a punchy 5-6-word tagline per product. Currently these lines render below readable threshold on mobile.
- 4. **Give Cart Deco a visual.** The "choose a free item" section is all text — a small image of the gift options would make the offer feel more concrete and desirable.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Base Face Milk, Bright Start Retinol, Sun Serum + more — sale ends Sunday`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`@import` in `<style>` block — confirmed issue**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```
`@import` inside `<style>` is stripped by Gmail (all variants), Yahoo Mail, and some Outlook builds. Both the Klaviyo custom font and Google Fonts Montserrat will silently fail to load in these clients. The fallback stack `"Montserrat", "Helvetica", sans-serif` handles graceful degradation, but this is a delivery reliability gap for the primary typeface.

**All anchor pseudo-states locked to identical color — confirmed**
```css
a:not([name]) {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
a:link    {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
a:visited {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
a:active  {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
a:hover   {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
```
`a:hover` returns no visual change. No underline on any state. Links are indistinguishable from body text for users who rely on hover feedback or text-decoration as affordance. WCAG 1.4.1 (Use of Color) requires non-color differentiation.

The `img` rule appears twice in the `<style>` block (once in the base reset, once in the Klaviyo section). The second declaration is redundant but not harmful; it adds unnecessary payload bytes.
