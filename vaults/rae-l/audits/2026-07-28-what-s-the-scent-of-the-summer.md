# What's the scent of the summer?
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-28**
## Executive summary

- "Pick of the Bunch" is Lush leaning fully into fruit-scented summer product — no ambiguity about the theme, and the fruit-sticker badges (lemon/orange/lime, coconut/mango/banana, orange/apple/blueberries) are a genuinely nice organizing device that makes four separate product drops feel like one coherent campaign instead of a grab-bag. The four-panel hero strip (dyed hair under citrus spray, hands smashing a peach, a soap bar, a phone camera) is moody and tactile, and it sets up the "juicy" language well.
- Where it falls down is depth and offer clarity. There's no discount, no price, no "shop now for $X" — just "Enjoy $5 off with the Lush app" crammed into a thin top banner that's easy to skim past. Compare this to July 21's Advent calendar send, which had a clear pre-order CTA, or July 16's "Ghost the Grind," which paired a strong visual pun with a specific call to action ("Get Some Treats"). This email has strong visual craft and weak commercial urgency — it's a lookbook, not a sales push.
- Net: solid seasonal creative that continues Lush's pattern of confident, editorial-style sends, but it under-indexes on giving the reader a reason to click *today* versus just browsing later.

## What's working

- The fruit-sticker badge motif (Lemon/Orange/Lime, Coconut/Mango/Banana, Orange/Apple/Blueberries) visually groups each of the three product sections and reinforces the scent theme without extra copy.
- Section backgrounds shift color (orange → green → red/pink) to match each scent family, giving strong visual differentiation as you scroll.
- Each product tile has a clean product name + category label (e.g., "Orange / BODY SCRUB"), so browsing is easy even without prices.
- The closing "We Like Big Bags" banana-tote image is a fun, on-brand capper that doesn't feel like a bolted-on afterthought.
- Render is clean — no overlapping text, broken images, or layout breaks visible.

## What's weak

- No visible price or discount tied to any specific product — the only offer ("$5 off with the Lush app") is buried in a thin top strip, easy to miss on mobile.
- The primary hero CTA "Squeeze The Day" is a pun, not an action — it doesn't tell you what happens when you tap it (shop all? one collection?).
- Three separate product sections (9 products total) with no single hero product means the eye has nowhere obvious to land first — high visual variety but low hierarchy.
- No urgency signal anywhere (no "limited edition," no end date) — this reads evergreen, which is a step back from July 21's "25 days" countdown framing or July 16's Halloween timing.
- Model/skin-tone diversity is present but there's no product-in-use texture/review proof (no ratings, no "bestseller" flags) to build confidence before purchase.

## Recommendations

- 1. Give the hero a real, specific offer — swap "Enjoy $5 off with the Lush app" from the thin top banner into the hero itself (e.g., "$5 off your first fruity haul — today only"), so the click has an actual reason attached to it, not just aesthetics.
- 2. Pick one hero product from the nine shown and feature it above the fold with a price and a direct "Shop [Product]" CTA — right now all three sections compete equally and nothing gets the reader to commit.
- 3. Replace "Squeeze The Day" with an action-specific CTA like "Shop Fruity Scents" so scent-curious but time-pressed readers know exactly where the tap leads.
- 4. Add a light urgency cue (e.g., "while summer stock lasts") to at least one section — this brand has used credible urgency well before (Advent calendar countdown) and it's absent here.
- 5. Consider trimming to two product sections instead of three — nine products in one email dilutes attention that a tighter, better-merchandised set would capture.
