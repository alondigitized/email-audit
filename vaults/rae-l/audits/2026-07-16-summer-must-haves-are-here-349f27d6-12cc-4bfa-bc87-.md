# Summer Must-Haves Are Here
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- This is Sally Beauty doing its editorial catalog playbook — no discount, just curated summer hair picks organized into two logical clusters: repair/treatment and styling tools. The hero is clean, the model photography is on-brand, and the two-section structure (soft + hydrated → trending styles) tells a coherent story. Nothing breaks visually.
- The problem is the subject line. "Summer Must-Haves Are Here" arrived on July 16 — exactly seven days after "Your summer must-haves" on July 9. Subscribers who opened both will clock the repeat instantly, and this email gives them nothing new to justify the déjà vu: no offer, no urgency, no new collection name, no specific product hook. The July 9 send scored a 9 because it was a smart reset after eight days of discounting. This one just re-runs the same frame.
- The Klarna financing call-out buried near the footer is the most underutilized element here. The infrared curling iron and adjustable deep waver are the exact SKUs that benefit from installment messaging — that belongs in the styling tools section, not the payment-options footnote.

## What's working

- **Red hero with clear hierarchy** — "Keep your summer style soft, shiny & hydrated" lands first; the "Shop now" CTA is immediately visible without cognitive load.
- **Two-section content logic** — repair/treatment (serum, bond conditioner) then styling tools (silk drops, heat protection, waver, curling iron) reads as intentional, not random.
- **Model photography is on-brand** — curly-haired woman fits the product promise and the demographic; no stock-photo mismatch.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no layout shifts, no lorem ipsum leaking anywhere.
- **Klarna financing present** — even if buried, the "4 interest-free payments" call-out is the right move for a styling tools SKU at this price tier.

## What's weak

- **Subject recycles July 9's "Your summer must-haves"** — seven-day repeat on the same frame with no new hook signals cadence fatigue.
- **No offer anywhere in the body** — every prior send in this sequence had a concrete mechanic (AMERICA25, 20% off, free gift); this one has nothing except "Shop now."
- **All CTAs say "Shop now"** — six products, six identical buttons; no product name or category differentiation in the tap target.
- **Klarna is in the wrong place** — installment messaging is most persuasive next to the $50–$100 styling tools, not in the universal footer.
- **Hero subtext is weak** — "Hair care that protects and repairs" under the headline is redundant with the headline; a specific product name or a single stat ("up to 90% less breakage") would work harder.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Name a product or benefit in the subject** — "Summer Must-Haves Are Here" is indistinguishable from July 9. Pick the most search-able or trending SKU (the Infrared Curling Iron, the Bond Conditioner) and lead with it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `The infrared curling iron everyone's talking about`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Bond, protect, wave — your summer hair kit is here`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Silk drops, bond conditioner, heat tools — shop the summer lineup`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `As low as $X/month with Klarna — the styling tools worth it`
- 2. **Move Klarna messaging into the tools section** — Add a one-liner ("As low as $X/month with Klarna") under the Adjustable Deep Waver and Infrared Curling Iron. These are the exact products where price hesitation exists.
- 3. **Differentiate the CTAs** — Replace generic "Shop now" with category-specific copy: "Shop bond care" / "Shop heat tools" / "Shop wavers." One line of copy, meaningful lift on click intent.
- 4. **Add one concrete signal in the hero subtext** — swap "Hair care that protects and repairs" for a specific claim tied to a featured product (e.g., "Bond Conditioner restores strength in one wash").
- 5. **Consider a de-risk close** — The free shipping icon in the footer is selling an important benefit; surface it in or near the CTA section, not buried in the fulfillment icon strip.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — Undeclared font `Founders Grotesk`:**
The global anchor rule `a { font-family: 'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }` references a font with no `@font-face` declaration in the `<style>` block. Only Open Sans has declarations. `Founders Grotesk` will silently fall back to Helvetica for all recipients, meaning CTA button typography may not match design intent.

**Issue — Stale Google Fonts version:**
Open Sans is loaded from `fonts.gstatic.com/s/opensans/v17/` — version 17 is significantly outdated (current is v40+). While functionally harmless in most clients, it's a maintenance flag.

**Issue — `img { white-space: pre; }`:**
`white-space` has no effect on replaced elements like `<img>`. This is dead CSS, likely a template artifact. Not a rendering risk but indicates template hygiene issues.

**Positive signals:**
- `<meta name="x-apple-disable-message-reformatting">` present — correct for Apple Mail
- VML namespaces (`xmlns:v`, `xmlns:o`) present for Outlook background/button support
- `u ~ div .pattern { min-width: 100vw !important; }` Gmail mobile hack is present
- Mobile breakpoint set at `max-width: 499px` — slightly narrow (600px is the common container width; 480px is the common mobile breakpoint)
