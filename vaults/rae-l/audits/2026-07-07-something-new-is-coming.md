# Something new is coming
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-07**
## Executive summary

- This is a product-launch teaser for G NY Bake Deluxe — a limited-edition, apple-flavored Glossier product launching exclusively at Glossier NYC on July 18th and broadly on July 14th. The creative leans fully into the apple concept: "AN APPLE A DAY" tag, "Take a bite" hero headline, and a lush fruit image. The CTA asks you to join a waitlist, not to buy — which is the right call for a limited drop.
- Strategically, this is the cleanest send Glossier has produced in over a month. After the Friends of Glossier Sale arc bled out across eleven emails (three on June 19 alone), the July 4th editorial reset started to restore the brand, and this email completes the pivot. No discount. No urgency theater. A real product with a real story.
- The one swing and miss: the subject line. "Something new is coming" is the single most overused teaser line in email marketing, and it squanders the one moment where the apple hook — "Take a bite" — could have done serious work in the inbox.

## What's working

- **"Take a bite" headline is genuinely good copy** — evocative, brand-right, and earns the apple visual without spelling it out.
- **Limited-edition framing is specific**: NYC exclusive on July 18, broad launch July 14 — real dates give the teaser urgency without manufactured scarcity.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean**: large fruit image leads, headline reads immediately below, CTA is reachable without scrolling.
- **Tonal reset holds**: after a punishing sale arc, no percentage off anywhere in this email — it reads like Glossier trusts the product enough to let it stand alone.
- **Footer is on-brand and clean** — the smiley + wave emoji feels like Glossier, not filler.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is a cliché**: "Something new is coming" is generic launch-tease boilerplate that gives the inbox no reason to prioritize this over anything else arriving that day.
- **CTA is a text link, not a button**: "Get on the list" appears as inline text, not a styled clickable element — this reduces affordance and likely suppresses click-through.
- **No product category signal in the body preview**: a reader new to Glossier cannot tell whether this is fragrance, skincare, food, or a collab — the copy is artful but not informative enough for the inbox.
- **"AN APPLE A DAY" tag-line above the headline is small and easy to miss** — it carries the pun that makes the headline land; if it's skimmed, "Take a bite" floats without context.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the subject line first** — swap the generic tease for a line that carries the apple hook into the inbox. The creative team clearly cracked this concept in the body; the subject should be the sharpest version of it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Take a bite — something new from Glossier NYC`
- **Subject Alt B:** `G NY Bake Deluxe drops July 14`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `A limited-edition, apple-flavored love letter to the city that never sleeps.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Our juiciest limited edition yet — get on the list before it's gone.`
- 2. **Upgrade "Get on the list" to a styled button** — even a simple pill-button in Glossier pink would materially improve click affordance. Text links get lost on mobile.
- 3. **Move the product category one line earlier** — add a single descriptor ("a new lip treat," "a juicy new balm," etc.) before the product name so a skimming reader knows what they're signing up for.
- 4. **Consider a "quantities limited" or "NYC-first" badge on the image** — the exclusivity is there in the copy, but a visual callout on the hero would reinforce scarcity at a glance.

## Full review
## 1. Overview

This is a product-launch teaser for G NY Bake Deluxe — a limited-edition, apple-flavored Glossier product launching exclusively at Glossier NYC on July 18th and broadly on July 14th. The creative leans fully into the apple concept: "AN APPLE A DAY" tag, "Take a bite" hero headline, and a lush fruit image. The CTA asks you to join a waitlist, not to buy — which is the right call for a limited drop.

Strategically, this is the cleanest send Glossier has produced in over a month. After the Friends of Glossier Sale arc bled out across eleven emails (three on June 19 alone), the July 4th editorial reset started to restore the brand, and this email completes the pivot. No discount. No urgency theater. A real product with a real story.

The one swing and miss: the subject line. "Something new is coming" is the single most overused teaser line in email marketing, and it squanders the one moment where the apple hook — "Take a bite" — could have done serious work in the inbox.

## 2. What worked

- **"Take a bite" headline is genuinely good copy** — evocative, brand-right, and earns the apple visual without spelling it out.
- **Limited-edition framing is specific**: NYC exclusive on July 18, broad launch July 14 — real dates give the teaser urgency without manufactured scarcity.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean**: large fruit image leads, headline reads immediately below, CTA is reachable without scrolling.
- **Tonal reset holds**: after a punishing sale arc, no percentage off anywhere in this email — it reads like Glossier trusts the product enough to let it stand alone.
- **Footer is on-brand and clean** — the smiley + wave emoji feels like Glossier, not filler.

## 3. What didn't

- **Subject line is a cliché**: "Something new is coming" is generic launch-tease boilerplate that gives the inbox no reason to prioritize this over anything else arriving that day.
- **CTA is a text link, not a button**: "Get on the list" appears as inline text, not a styled clickable element — this reduces affordance and likely suppresses click-through.
- **No product category signal in the body preview**: a reader new to Glossier cannot tell whether this is fragrance, skincare, food, or a collab — the copy is artful but not informative enough for the inbox.
- **"AN APPLE A DAY" tag-line above the headline is small and easy to miss** — it carries the pun that makes the headline land; if it's skimmed, "Take a bite" floats without context.

## 4. What I'd change

1. **Fix the subject line first** — swap the generic tease for a line that carries the apple hook into the inbox. The creative team clearly cracked this concept in the body; the subject should be the sharpest version of it.
   - **Subject Alt A:** `Take a bite — something new from Glossier NYC`
   - **Subject Alt B:** `G NY Bake Deluxe drops July 14`
   - **Preheader Alt A:** `A limited-edition, apple-flavored love letter to the city that never sleeps.`
   - **Preheader Alt B:** `Our juiciest limited edition yet — get on the list before it's gone.`

2. **Upgrade "Get on the list" to a styled button** — even a simple pill-button in Glossier pink would materially improve click affordance. Text links get lost on mobile.

3. **Move the product category one line earlier** — add a single descriptor ("a new lip treat," "a juicy new balm," etc.) before the product name so a skimming reader knows what they're signing up for.

4. **Consider a "quantities limited" or "NYC-first" badge on the image** — the exclusivity is there in the copy, but a visual callout on the hero would reinforce scarcity at a glance.
