# NEW! Inner Truth Bath Melt
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-11**
## Executive summary

- This is a cause-marketing product launch email tied to Pride Month — Lush's Inner Truth Bath Melt, with 70% of the purchase price donated to organizations protecting trans athletes' access to sport. The hero is bold and visually arresting: a gradient-washed abstract image dominated by the headline "SUPPORT TRANS ATHLETES" in large, punchy type. The mission framing is the entire pitch; the product itself is nearly secondary.
- It's a sharp pivot from the editorial cadence Lush has been running — the June 8 "What makes us smell so good?" send was a deep-dive on ingredients and scent storytelling; this one buries the product to foreground the cause. That trade-off works emotionally but leaves the shopper without the sensory language that typically closes a Lush sale. There's no scent profile, no texture cue, nothing that makes you want to *buy this specific bath melt* beyond the donation mechanic.
- Honest judgement: the cause is compelling and the timing is right (Pride Month, June 11), but this email over-indexes on mission and under-delivers on product. It would do better work if the two were in better balance.

## What's working

- **Hero headline is unambiguous and emotionally resonant** — "SUPPORT TRANS ATHLETES" is legible and impactful at a glance; no guesswork about what this email stands for.
- **70% donation specificity is credibility-building** — the "minus the taxes" qualifier reads as honest, not evasive; it signals this isn't vague cause-washing.
- **Seasonal alignment is exactly right** — June 11, Pride Month, trans athlete rights as a cultural flashpoint. The timing feels intentional, not opportunistic.
- **Visual hierarchy works** — eye goes hero → headline → body copy → CTA with no competition from cluttered secondary offers.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no layout collapse, no lorem ipsum; email displays crisply end to end.

## What's weak

- **Zero sensory product details** — no scent description, no texture language, no skin benefit. Lush subscribers open emails partly to smell them with their imagination; this one is mute on that front.
- **"Shop Now" CTA is generic** — compared to the brand's stronger editorial sends, this is a step down. Nothing in the button copy tells you *what* you're buying or *why* the act of buying matters right now.
- **No price visible** — even a ballpark ("starting at $X") would reduce friction; without it, the link to checkout is the first moment of sticker surprise.
- **Limited-edition urgency lives only in body copy** — the subject says "NEW!" but doesn't signal scarcity; readers who skim the hero and hit "Shop Now" won't feel the limited-run pressure that should be accelerating the purchase.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the CTA to carry the cause** — "Shop & Donate 70%" tells the buyer exactly what their click does. "Shop Now" tells them nothing. This is the single highest-leverage change.
- 2. **Add one line of sensory copy to the body** — even a single scent anchor ("a grounding blend of [X] and [X]") keeps this feeling like a Lush email and not just a donation landing page.
- 3. **Surface the limited-edition signal earlier** — move it into the subject or at minimum into a visible badge on the hero image, so it reads at inbox-open speed rather than mid-scroll speed.
- 4. **Show a price or "starting at"** — reduces decision friction and sets expectations before checkout.
- 5. **Preheader should reinforce the cause, not leak app-promo copy** — the app offer banner at the top is showing up as likely preheader context; that undercuts the emotional momentum.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Limited edition. 70% of every purchase donated to trans sports protection orgs.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **All links wrap through `clicks.hello.us.lush.com`** (Braze click-tracking). Final destination URLs and any UTM parameters are obscured behind the redirect — cannot confirm UTM hygiene without following each redirect chain.
- **Source is truncated** (`…` at the image block `href`), so not all CTAs could be audited. At minimum the hero CTA and any footer links are unverified.
- **No bare/raw URLs detected** in visible source — tracking wrapper is consistently applied to the one confirmed link.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

| Issue | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Empty `<title>` tag | `<title></title>` — some clients (Outlook web, Gmail) surface this in tab/window labels |
| CSS `background-image` on `<table>` element | `style="…background-image:url(https://cdn.braze.eu/appboy/…original.png…)"` on `row-2` table — not rendered by Outlook (any version), Windows Mail, or older Android clients |

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No merge/personalization tokens (`{{first_name}}`, `[[EMAIL]]`, etc.) detected in the visible source. The `$5 off` banner appears hard-coded rather than dynamically populated. No issues with unrendered tokens in the auditable portion.

---

### 5. Compliance

- **Unsubscribe link**: Not present in the auditable portion. CAN-SPAM §5 requires a clear, functional opt-out mechanism. The footer is cut off — confirm it contains a working unsubscribe link and physical postal address before send.
- **Physical mailing address**: Not visible in truncated source — required by CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5). Must be present somewhere in the email body.
- **Sender authentication**: `hello@hello.us.lush.com` is a subdomain sender. SPF/DKIM alignment to `us.lush.com` (not `lush.com`) must be verified; a misconfigured subdomain is a common DMARC failure point. Cannot confirm from HTML alone — check sending domain DNS records.
- **Preheader / subject mismatch risk**: Hidden preheader reads `🏳️‍⚧️ Support trans athletes`; first visible element reads `🏳️‍⚧️ Enjoy $5 off with the Lush app!`. These are semantically different messages sharing the same emoji. In clients that leak preheader text into the visible body (some webmail), this pairing could appear contradictory.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

- **UTM parameters unverifiable** — all destination URLs are behind Braze redirect wrappers. Must confirm that the Braze link configuration appends `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, and `utm_content` before final redirect.
- **No evidence of deep-linking** to the specific product page ("Inner Truth Bath Melt") in the confirmed link — the banner CTA resolves through a generic `clicks.hello.us.lush.com` redirect. Confirm the landing page is the product page, not the homepage.
- **App deep-link**: The banner promotes a `$5 off with the Lush app` offer but links to a web URL, not a universal link or app scheme. If the offer is app-exclusive, web-click recipients will likely hit a page that cannot redeem the offer.

---

### 7. Recommendations

1. **Verify the footer is complete** — confirm CAN-SPAM unsubscribe link and physical address are present before send.
2. **Fix or replace the `background-image` on the row-2 table** — use a foreground `<img>` tag with a proper `alt` attribute so Outlook recipients see the intended visual.
3. **Confirm SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment** for `hello.us.lush.com` subdomain — run `dig TXT hello.us.lush.com` and verify `dmarc=pass` in a test send header.
4. **Audit Braze link configuration** to confirm UTM parameters are appended to all redirect destinations, especially the hero CTA.
5. **Resolve the app-offer / web-link mismatch** — if the `$5 off` is app-redeemable only, the link should fire a universal link (`https://` with `apple-app-site-association` branch) or include explicit redemption instructions on the landing page.
6. **Add `alt` text** to all image blocks and verify it is non-empty in the complete source.
7. **Migrate the background asset** to the US Braze CDN instance to eliminate EU-origin latency for US recipients.
