# 😍 Oh. My. Minis. 😍
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-23**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean holiday mini-sets send. The big blue "Mini Sets for the Holidays" hero, with the Laneige lip set at $26 ($36 value), makes the theme obvious within a second. The product grid below it is well organized: every tile has a badge, a name and a price with the "value" price beside it. It looks like Sephora and it renders without breakage.
- The send is weakened by the stack of competing messages. A "Choose a Trial Size" banner with a KBEAUTY code and a $40 purchase threshold sits above the real hero. Below the grid are Halloween beauty services, free samples with $25+, "Gifts Under $50" and the Beauty Insider block. This matches the 10/20 and 10/18 sends, which also pitched two or three offers at once. Two days ago a sample bag was the headline, so this email makes the reader choose again.
- Overall it is solid, but the subject is more fun than the body. "Oh. My. Minis." sets up one idea and the email delivers about six.

## What's working

- The hero is on theme: a saturated blue background, a clear "Mini Sets for the Holidays" headline, and a single white "SHOP NOW" button.
- Price anchoring works. "$26 ($36 value)" and similar figures appear on every tile, so the discount is visible without clicking.
- Badges like "Only at Sephora," "Limited Edition" and "Clean" help a shopper decide quickly.
- The 2-column grid of mini sets is easy to scan and the product photography is consistent.
- The Beauty Insider tier block and "You have 0 points" header put loyalty benefits on display.

## What's weak

- The "Choose a Trial Size" banner above the hero sells a different offer, with a code and a threshold, so the top of the email has two heroes.
- The lower third (Halloween services, free samples, Gifts Under $50) pulls attention away from minis and makes the email very long.
- CTAs are generic. "SHOP NOW," "SHOP TO REDEEM" and "BOOK NOW" say nothing about what's behind them.
- No deadline or stock cue appears anywhere in the body. The hero's "just-right prices" has no reason to act today.
- Prices come from several vendors, with only some marked down, so the section doesn't feel like one value story. The tiny fine print under the trial-size offer ($40 purchase) is easy to miss.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the trial-size banner, or move it to the bottom. Let the minis hero be the only thing above the fold, and give it a threshold like "Minis under $40."
- 2. Trim the lower section to one secondary module (free samples) and drop Halloween services and Gifts Under $50.
- 3. Make the CTAs specific: "Shop mini gift sets" and "Shop minis under $50."
- 4. Add a real deadline or scarcity line, such as "Limited-edition sets while supplies last."
- 5. Group the grid by price tier or by gift recipient so it reads as a curated edit and not a catalog.
