# This only happens once a year
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-20**
## Executive summary

- This is the plain-text collapse for the third time in this streak — after 09-14's "This could be your moment, friend" and 09-17's "Browsing the VIP sale?", ILIA has now sent yet another no-logo, no-hero, no-product-photo email that reads like a personal note rather than a beauty brand campaign. The subject "This only happens once a year" promises urgency and exclusivity, but the render delivers a wall of black text and blue hyperlinks with a fake-personal sign-off from "Marissa (from ILIA)."
- The offer itself is genuinely strong on paper — 20% off sitewide, a free full-size Multi-Stick, a free Velvet Makeup Bag at $100+ — but none of it is visually reinforced. There's no product image of the Multi-Stick or the bag, no color swatch, nothing that makes a beauty shopper stop scrolling. Compare this to 09-16's "The Art of the Eye" mascara-on-jewelry-tray shot, which scored 7/10 on visual merchandising alone.
- Judgement: this is a low-effort send riding on a legitimately good offer and a decent urgency subject line. It'll likely get some clicks from already-warm VIP-curious subscribers purely because the offer is clear, but it does nothing to build the brand and continues a worrying pattern of alternating full-production sends with these bare-bones ones.

## What's working

- The offer stack (20% off + free Multi-Stick + free Velvet Bag at $100+) is specific, tiered, and easy to scan as bullets.
- "Ends 09.22.26 at 11:59pm PT" gives a credible, concrete deadline in the fine print.
- The year-round VIP perks (points, early access, birthday gifts, free shipping) are clearly listed and add real value beyond the one-time sale.
- Two clear CTAs ("join ILIA VIP" and "Sign up for free + shop the sale") give the reader more than one path to convert.

## What's weak

- No logo, no hero image, no product photography anywhere in the render — this looks like a personal email, not a brand campaign, for the third time in under two weeks.
- Named product call-outs (Super Serum Skin Tint SPF 40, Limitless Lash Mascara) have zero accompanying imagery, wasting an easy visual merchandising opportunity.
- The free-gift items (Multi-Stick, Velvet Makeup Bag) are the kind of tangible, giftable products that beg for a photo — completely unshown.
- Visual hierarchy is flat top-to-bottom; nothing tells the eye where to land first, so the offer competes with perk bullets and fine print equally.

## Recommendations

- 1. Bring back the hero image treatment used on 09-16 and 09-18 — even a single styled shot of the Multi-Stick and Velvet Makeup Bag would transform this from a note into a campaign and directly showcase the free-gift incentive.
- 2. Add a real branded header/logo lockup at the top so the email is identifiable as ILIA before the reader even reads "from ILIA" in the sign-off.
- 3. Turn the offer stack into a visual badge or banner (e.g., "20% OFF + 2 FREE GIFTS") near the top instead of burying it in bullet three.
- 4. Make the primary CTA a designed button, not a blue hyperlink — "Sign up for free + shop the sale" is doing double duty as both hyperlink and closing line, which dilutes its punch.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your once-a-year VIP unlock: 20% + 2 free gifts`
- **Subject Alt B:** `3 days left: 20% off + free gifts (VIP only)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free Multi-Stick, free Velvet Bag, 20% off sitewide — ends soon`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your key to VIP: sitewide savings + gifts, before it's gone`
