# This is perfect for you!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-28**
## Executive summary

- This is a browse-abandonment send, and it's the same "STILL THINKING ABOUT IT? YOU'VE GOT GREAT TASTE!" template and the exact same subject line — "This is perfect for you!" — audited here 13 days ago on 2026-07-15, which scored an 8/10 with this same Arch Fit-style hero treatment. Production quality holds up: clean hero shot of the Slip-ins Arch Fit Arcade, a tidy 6-item "more options" grid, and a bold "2 FOR $100" banner lower in the email. Nothing is broken, nothing looks templated wrong.
- But the subject line does none of the work it should. "This is perfect for you!" is generic filler that could headline any retail email from any brand — it doesn't hint at the browse-abandonment hook, the specific shoe, or the live "2 for $100" offer sitting in the body. That's a real gap between what's inside (a concrete, dated, dollar-figure deal) and what gets shown in the inbox.
- The bigger issue is cadence: Skechers is now reusing subject lines within two weeks (this one, plus "Your Items Are Waiting!" reused June 30→July 16, and "This style has limited availability!" reused the same way). A subscriber checking their inbox has now seen "This is perfect for you!" twice inside a month. Solid creative, weak subject, fatigued repetition — net a middling send.

## What's working

- Clean, single-focus hero: one product (Arch Fit Arcade), one clear CTA button ("TAKE ANOTHER LOOK"), no clutter competing for attention.
- The "2 FOR $100" banner is high-contrast, specific, and dated ("Valid online thru 8/3"), giving the email a real, time-bound reason to act.
- Six-item recommendation grid is well-produced — real product photography, no placeholder/lorem-ipsum, no layout breakage.
- Utility footer block (BNPL via Afterpay/Klarna, curbside pickup, store finder, app download) is genuinely useful and rendered cleanly with no overlap.

## What's weak

- Subject line "This is perfect for you!" is a verbatim repeat of the 2026-07-15 send — same words, same vagueness, no new information.
- The one concrete offer in the email ("2 FOR $100") is buried well below the fold, after the hero and the 6-item grid — nothing in the subject or the top of the email signals it exists.
- All six "more great options" recommendations are men's sneakers with zero variation in category (no apparel, no accessories) despite the offer banner explicitly including "Apparel & Accessories."
- No loyalty/rewards-member messaging anywhere, despite Skechers having a rewards program — a missed personalization lever.
- "Exclusions apply. See below for details**" sits right next to the offer, visible fine-print hedging rather than a clean, confident offer.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the offer in the subject line.** "This is perfect for you!" wastes the strongest asset in the email — the dated, dollar-specific "2 for $100" deal — by hiding it from the inbox view entirely. Lead with the number.
- 2. **Move the 2-for-$100 banner above the recommendation grid**, right under the hero CTA, so mobile readers see the real incentive before they have to scroll past six unrelated product tiles.
- 3. **Rotate subject lines against send history.** This exact line was used 13 days ago; check against the last 30 days of sends before reusing copy, especially given the brand's pattern of recycling "Your Items Are Waiting!" and "This style has limited availability!" on similarly short cycles.
- 4. **Diversify the recommendation grid** to include at least one apparel or accessory item, matching what the offer banner actually covers.
- **Subject Alt A:** `2 for $100 on your favorites — thru 8/3`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Still thinking about the Arch Fit Arcade?`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Mix and match sneakers, apparel & accessories — 2 for $100, online only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The Arch Fit Arcade is still waiting. So is a 2-for-$100 deal.`
