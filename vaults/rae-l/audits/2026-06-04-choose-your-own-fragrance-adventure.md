# Choose your own fragrance adventure!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-04**
## Executive summary

- After five consecutive Pink Capsule Collection emails (pet accessories, the hoodie origin story, the summer checklist), this is the right moment to pivot — and Glossier does it cleanly. "Choose your own fragrance adventure" introduces a new customizable fragrance set concept using a "Travel Style" editorial framework: four distinct use-case scenarios (Travel Spray solo, The Trio, The Duo, Travel Spray + Balm) each with its own lifestyle rationale and a specific product recommendation. The cadence break feels earned, not random.
- The concept is smart — it flatters the subscriber by treating them as someone with a taste-defined lifestyle rather than just a buyer — and the execution is clean and consistent with Glossier's usual minimal aesthetic. Each product block has a clear CTA ("Customize yours") and enough copy to differentiate the four options without burying the reader.
- The miss is that the "choose your own adventure" promise in the subject implies some kind of interactive or quiz-style experience, but the email delivers a standard editorial scroll. That gap between expectation and format is the biggest thing holding this back. There's also no price anchor anywhere — not even a "starting at" — which makes it harder for an interested reader to self-select before clicking.

## What's working

- **"Travel Style" editorial framing** gives each product a distinct scenario identity, making four similar products feel like meaningfully different choices rather than options in a dropdown.
- **"Build your own" hero CTA** is specific and action-oriented — far stronger than "Shop now" or "Discover."
- **Clean alternating layout** (image left / copy right, then copy left / image right) keeps the scroll interesting without introducing visual clutter.
- **Fresh category pivot** from the Pink Capsule arc lands well — this doesn't feel like a fifth re-skin of the same drop.
- **No render bugs** — images load, text doesn't overlap, CTAs are visible throughout.

## What's weak

- **Subject line over-promises interactivity** — "choose your own adventure" implies a quiz, configurator, or branching experience; the email is a straightforward scroll.
- **No price anchor anywhere** — not even "sets starting at $X" — makes it hard to self-qualify before clicking.
- **Five CTAs compete for attention** — hero "Build your own" plus four "Customize yours" buttons; the hero call-to-action loses primacy.
- **"TRAVEL STYLE 01–04" labeling is cryptic** — the numbering implies a ranking or sequence that doesn't exist; these are parallel options.
- **Preview text likely leaks body copy** — no visible explicit preheader, meaning the first hero copy ("Choose your own (fragrance) adventure") repeats the subject verbatim in the inbox.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Deliver on the "adventure" promise** — add a short quiz hook ("Answer two questions → we'll pick your style") before the product grid, or at minimum open with a "Which one are you?" headline. The subject sets up an expectation the format doesn't meet.
- 2. **Add a price floor to the hero** — "Sets from $X" or "Starting at $XX" gives a subscriber a reason to self-select before clicking. No price = higher unqualified click abandon.
- 3. **Collapse to one primary CTA in the hero, then let the product grid serve as secondary** — "Build your own" should be the one destination above the fold; the four "Customize yours" buttons work fine as secondary selectors below.
- 4. **Replace "TRAVEL STYLE 01–04" with lifestyle labels** — "The Weekend Wanderer," "The Road Warrior," etc. would do the editorial work the numbers can't.
- 5. **Write an explicit preheader** that extends the subject rather than repeating it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Which fragrance type are you?`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your scent, your rules — new fragrance sets`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Travel Spray, Trio, Duo, or Balm — four styles, one quiz to find yours.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New customizable sets built for how you actually move.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Invalid tracking element — confirmed defect.**

```html
<span rel="stylesheet" href="https://ctrk.klclick.com/l/01KT7YXC0MQWSAHYWEKER6GYBE_0"></span>
```

`rel` and `href` are not valid attributes on a `<span>` element. This construct does nothing in compliant renderers. If the intent is a CSS file load, it must be `<link rel="stylesheet" href="…">` in `<head>`. If the intent is a tracking ping, a 1×1 `<img>` or a Klaviyo open-pixel tag is the correct mechanism. As written, this call is silently dropped by all major email clients — any open-tracking or style-loading tied to this URL will not fire.

**Tracking domain:** `ctrk.klclick.com` — Klaviyo's standard click-redirect subdomain. No anomaly there beyond the broken element above.
