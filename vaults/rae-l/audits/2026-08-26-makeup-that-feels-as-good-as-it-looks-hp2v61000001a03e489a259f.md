# Makeup that feels as good as it looks 🌱
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-26**
## Executive summary

- This is a "Clean at Sephora" catalog send — a sky-blue "Must-Haves" hero with a green Clean at Sephora seal, followed by a long, undifferentiated scroll of product tiles (Kosas, Milk Makeup, Aora México, Westman Atelier, Lawless, Ogee, Colorbar, Fel Beauty, Korean hair care, body washes, Summer Fridays). It's competent execution with zero render bugs, but it's the weakest kind of Sephora send: no single hero product, no offer beyond the top App Fest banner, and a clean-beauty framing that reads as a category dump rather than a curated story.
- Compared to 2026-08-24's "App Fest" send (8/10) and even 2026-08-23's Tower 28 before/after email (5/10), this one has less narrative pull — App Fest had a calendar structure and escalating daily discounts; the Tower 28 send had one distinctive proof point. Here, "Clean at Sephora Must-Haves" is a values-based claim with no visible substantiation (no ingredient callouts, no "why clean matters" copy) before dropping into ten near-identical product cards.
- Verdict: solid brand consistency, clean render, but this is a mid-pack catalog email that will underperform the brand's better structured sends. 5/10 territory.

## What's working

- Clean render throughout — no overlapping text, broken images, or layout breakage across a very long scroll.
- The green "Clean at Sephora" circular seal and sky-blue hero band create a clear, on-brand visual anchor for the campaign theme.
- Product tiles consistently show name, brand, price, and "NEW / ONLY AT SEPHORA / ONLINE ONLY" badges — good scannability for browsing shoppers.
- The top banner (25% off select brands, App Fest, free same-day delivery) gives at least one concrete, time-bound offer even though it's tangential to the "Clean" theme.
- Sub-sections ("Spot it. Shop it.", "New from Summer Fridays") break the monotony of the grid slightly with themed pairs.

## What's weak

- No single hero product or clear primary CTA — after the top banner there are at least 6 "Shop Now" buttons, diluting focus entirely.
- "Clean at Sephora Must-Haves" promises a values-driven curation but delivers a generic multi-brand catalog grid with no ingredient/clean-certification explanation visible on the page.
- The 25% off banner at the very top is disconnected from the "Clean" body content — two unrelated campaigns stitched into one email.
- Extremely long, repetitive scroll (10+ product tiles) with no visual hierarchy differentiating a "best" or "editor's pick" item.
- Subject emoji (🌱) signals ingredient/clean focus, but nothing in-body reinforces that story beyond the seal graphic.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the top 25%-off/App Fest banner entirely or move it to its own send — it competes with and undercuts the "Clean" narrative before the reader even reaches it.
- 2. Pick one hero clean product (e.g., the M.PH Lip Gloss or Kosas Talc-Free Powder) and lead with a large, single-CTA hero block instead of 10 competing tiles.
- 3. Add one line of substantiating copy under "Clean at Sephora Must-Haves" — what "clean" means here (e.g., "formulated without parabens, sulfates...") to make the seal credible, not decorative.
- 4. Trim the grid to 4-6 curated items max; move the rest to a "See all Clean picks" link.
- 5. Add a specific, time-bound clean-beauty offer (e.g., "20% off Clean picks this weekend") rather than relying on the unrelated App Fest discount.
