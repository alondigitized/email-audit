# This one’s just for you 😘
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-12**
## Executive summary

- "Found It for You" is another "personalized" recommendation email that isn't personalized at all — it's a Prada Paradoxe hero followed by an eight-item grid spanning fragrance, skincare, haircare, lipstick, and hand sanitizer. That's not "what's popular for you," that's Sephora's general merchandising catalog with a friendlier subject line slapped on top. The subject "This one's just for you 😘" promises 1:1 relevance and the body delivers the same scroll format seen on 8/11, 8/10, and 8/8 — just with a $380 fragrance as the new hero instead of a beauty roundup headline.
- The good news is execution is clean: no render bugs, a genuine hero product with a real price, and a "NEW" badge that gives a reason to look. But there's no offer anywhere in this email — no %, no $, no BOGO — which is unusual even for this brand's recent cadence (8/9's K18 send and 8/5's 4X points push both had a concrete hook). This is pure browse-inspiration, which caps how hard it can perform on clicks.
- Judgement: mid-pack execution, weak targeting claim, no offer — this lands around a 5, dragged down by the personalization/subject mismatch and the now-repetitive multi-brand-grid format.

## What's working

- Hero product (Prada Paradoxe) is large, well-lit, and unambiguous — clear single focal point above the grid.
- "NEW" tag and full product name + price are visible right under the hero image, reducing friction to purchase decision.
- Top banner clearly surfaces a concrete offer (free trial-size Kenra spray with $40 purchase) even though it's not the main CTA.
- Grid items are legible with brand, product name, and price on every tile — no missing text or broken image slots.
- "Beauty Insider Celebration" and "New in Skincare" mid-email blocks add secondary reasons to click without cluttering the top.

## What's weak

- Subject says "just for you" but the body is a generic eight-brand grid (Farmacy, Kérastase, MAC, Touchland, Armani, etc.) with zero visible personalization signal — no name, no purchase history callout, no "because you bought X."
- No discount, % off, or dollar offer anywhere in the main body — the only offer (Kenra trial) is buried in the pre-header-style top strip, easy to miss on mobile.
- This is the fourth catalog-scroll-style send in six days (8/11, 8/10, 8/8, now 8/12) — subscribers who opened those will recognize the format instantly and fatigue is a real risk.
- Grid has zero thematic coherence — a $380 fragrance, a $10 hand sanitizer, and a $170 EDT sit side by side with no organizing logic a shopper can follow.
- "Beauty Insider Celebration" mystery reward tile competes visually with "New in Skincare" for attention right after the main grid, diluting whichever CTA the brand actually wants clicked.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the subject earn its promise — either add real personalization (first name, "based on your last order") or stop claiming "just for you" on a generic catalog email; false personalization erodes trust over repeated sends.
- 2. Add one visible, concrete offer to the hero block itself (e.g., "20% off fragrance this week") instead of burying the only discount in the top utility strip.
- 3. Cut the grid from eight unrelated products to 3-4 with a shared theme (e.g., "your fragrance faves" or "skincare edit") so it reads as curation, not inventory dump.
- 4. Reorder secondary blocks — put "Beauty Insider Celebration" OR "New in Skincare" first, not both stacked, to avoid CTA competition.
- 5. Vary the format from the last three sends — try a single-category deep-dive (like 8/9's K18 send, which scored 8/10) instead of another multi-brand scroll.

- **Subject Alt B:** `New fragrance, made for you`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Paradoxe is here — plus 7 more picks curated for your routine`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Skip the scroll: your top beauty picks this week`
