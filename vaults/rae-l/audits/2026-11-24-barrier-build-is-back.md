# Barrier Build is back
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-24**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, single-hero Black Friday send. The product is Barrier Build, a restock, and it fills the top of the email. The macro shot of the cream in its jar is the strongest visual in the recent run of ILIA sends. "barrier build is back" is a real news hook, and it beats the generic sale recaps from Nov 23 ("Ready to claim it all?") and Nov 24 ("This week in beauty: 20% off").
- The structure is sensible: one hero, one button, then a second block for the free gift edit. The cost is focus. Two offers and two near-identical maroon buttons compete, and the free-gift section repeats what the earlier "Double the gifts" send did today. A subscriber has now seen the free gift edit twice in one day. The "back in stock" news is the only new idea here, and it is undersold.
- Verdict: a solid 6. The restock hook and hero image are good. The offer is buried under asterisks and a header bar that is ordinary Black Friday copy. It is the third or fourth ILIA send in about 48 hours, so it will feel like part of the same stream.

## What's working

- The hero is a tight macro shot of the Barrier Build cream. It lands the product and its texture immediately.
- "barrier build *is back*" uses the serif and italic pairing well. The restock news carries real urgency without shouting.
- The headline "hydrating + comforting + 20% off*" puts benefits and the offer in one line.
- The body copy states a concrete claim: clinically proven to hydrate and improve the skin barrier.
- The button "SHOP BARRIER BUILD" is a verb plus a noun, and it goes to one specific product.
- The free gift edit uses specific terms: a free complexion stick with any order, and a free mini of choice with $100+.

## What's weak

- Two offers and two CTAs of equal weight dilute the hero. The gift block pulls attention away from the restock.
- The fine print is heavy. There is a header asterisk ("25% OFF FOR VIPS*"), "20% off*", "Black Friday.*", a footnote reading "See site for details" and another reading "In instrumental studies of 21 people." The "21 people" sample undercuts the "clinically proven" claim.
- The 20% off is not clearly tied to the Barrier Build button. A reader can't tell whether the discount applies to this product.
- The pink "ILIA Makeup That Makes Your Skin Better" block is a large empty brand banner. It adds scroll and no information.
- There is no price, no review count and no social proof for the hero product. A buyer has no reason to choose it over any other cream.
- The free-gift collage is a repeat of today's earlier "Double the gifts" send. The 25% VIP rate is mentioned only in the top bar and not explained.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the restock the whole email. Add the price, a star rating and review count, and one line on who it is for (dry or compromised skin). Remove the gift block, or shrink it to a one-line strip.
- 2. Show the discounted price on the hero ("$XX now $XX") so the 20% is concrete. Say plainly that the 20% applies to Barrier Build.
- 3. Replace the "21 people" footnote with a stronger proof point, such as a customer quote or a count of reviews.
- 4. Delete the pink brand banner and put a second product line there (for example, a cleanser or serum to pair with it).
- 5. If both blocks stay, use different button treatments, for example a filled button for the hero and a text link for the gift.
- 6. Space out the near-duplicate gift-with-purchase sends. This is the second today.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Barrier Build is back in stock (20% off)`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Back in stock: the barrier cream, 20% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `It sold out fast. Now it's 20% off for Black Friday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Clinically shown to hydrate + repair your barrier. Limited restock.`

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text (confirmed, QA WARN):** `https://d3k81ch9hvuctc.cloudfront.net/company/SguDsz/images/c9cefca7-6b18-443a-896b-9e98ec5d842a.jpeg` is a content image served from the Klaviyo CDN. It has no alt attribute, so screen readers and image-blocked clients get no description.
- **Open pixel (minor):** The tracking pixel `trk.send.iliabeauty.com/o/01M87M0Y1B8BGDWFD7CY1SH1T5` also lacks alt. A decorative pixel should have an explicit `alt=""`.
- **No `lang` attribute:** The root element is `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" xmlns:o=… xmlns:v=…>`. This affects screen-reader pronunciation.
- **Empty title:** `<title> </title>` is blank.
- **Links look like body text:** The `<style>` block sets `a:link, a:visited, a:active, a:hover { color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none }`. That is the same colour as the heading and body text (`h1–h4 { color:#5f4b3c }`). Inline links have no colour or underline cue, which fails WCAG 1.4.1 (use of colour) for any text links.
- **External font `@import` rules:** The email imports `static-forms.klaviyo.com/.../custom_fonts.css` and `fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat`. Gmail and several other clients strip `@import`. The declared fallback is `"Montserrat","Helvetica",sans-serif`, so the failure is graceful.
- **Mobile and Outlook handling:** The email has a viewport meta tag, `max-width:100%` on images, and MSO conditionals (`OfficeDocumentSettings`, `mj-outlook-group-fix`). Nothing here is flagged.
- **Preheader:** It is correctly hidden (`display:none; max-height:0; opacity:0`). The `͏` padding characters are standard.
