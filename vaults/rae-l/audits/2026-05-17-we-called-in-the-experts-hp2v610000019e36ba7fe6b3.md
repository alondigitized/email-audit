# WE CALLED IN THE EXPERTS 🤝🏽
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- This is a content-first editorial email anchored in Sephora's "the Chat" series — a recurring format that leans on influencer expertise rather than promotional offers. The May 17 send features a slow-beauty routine walkthrough and a separate makeup tips segment from two different artists. The production is clean, the quotes are specific enough to feel credible, and the brand voice is consistent. But the email commits fully to editorial at the expense of commerce: there's no promotional offer, no discount, no price point in sight.
- Compared to the April 26 welcome email — which drove a clear action (join Beauty Insider) — this send is relationship maintenance. It's a brand equity deposit, not a revenue draw. That's a legitimate strategy for a loyalty brand, but it means the bar for engagement has to be met entirely by editorial quality, and "WE CALLED IN THE EXPERTS" in all-caps does not set that bar high from the inbox.
- The ICYMI block at the bottom competes for attention without earning it — too compressed to parse at a glance. Overall: well-executed for what it is, but "what it is" limits commercial upside.

## What's working

- Expert pull quotes are product-specific — naming "The Radiance Pear Oil" by name gives the editorial section real shoppability without hard-selling.
- "3 Makeup Tips, 1 Brilliant Artist" is cleanly scannable: numbered tips, artist photo, clear CTA.
- Beauty INSIDER loyalty tabs (POINTS / BEAUTY OFFERS / BIRTHDAY REWARD / ROUGE REWARD) persist at the bottom as a quiet membership reminder without interrupting content.
- Layout renders cleanly — no broken images, no overlapping text, two distinct editorial heroes with clear visual separation.
- The "the Chat" label establishes this as a known content series, which helps loyal subscribers contextualize the send immediately.

## What's weak

- ALL-CAPS subject line reads as a spam signal before the email is even opened — kills open likelihood before it starts.
- Zero promotional offer in the email; for a brand running this many sends, a curated "shop the routine" block with pricing would have cost nothing and added real conversion lift.
- The ICYMI section is too small and too vague to decode at a glance — what is it promoting? Unclear without close reading.
- Two competing editorial heroes ("Romanticize Your Routine" + "3 Makeup Tips") dilute focus — neither CTA gets the email's full weight behind it.
- The "BUILD YOUR ZEN ROUTINE +" CTA is abstract about destination — does it go to a routine builder, a category page, a curated collection? Unclear.

- 1. **Add a product/price anchor** — insert a "Shop the routine" rail between the two editorial blocks with the exact products mentioned in the expert quotes, with visible prices. Even three product tiles with prices transforms this from a read into a buy.
- 2. **Rewrite the subject** — the all-caps and vague framing are the biggest single drag on open rate.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Grace's slow-beauty morning, step by step`
- **Subject Alt B:** `3 expert rules for an effortless face`
- 3. **Make the preheader earn its place** — the current preheader appears to be generic Sephora evergreen copy that does nothing for this specific send.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Scalp massage, overnight repair, and the pear oil she swears by`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your new morning ritual starts here — a Sephora expert walk-through`
- 4. **Consolidate to one primary editorial hero** — either the routine walkthrough or the makeup tips, not both. Give the winner more vertical space and a stronger CTA.
- 5. **Expand or cut the ICYMI block** — as rendered it reads as filler. Either give it a dedicated section with a clear headline and offer, or remove it and tighten the email.

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta — confirmed issue**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
This blocks user-initiated pinch-to-zoom on iOS/Android. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) requires content to be resizable up to 200% without loss of function. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

No `lang="en"` (or locale-appropriate value) is present. Screen readers cannot infer language for correct pronunciation. Add `lang="en"` (or `lang="en-US"` to match the `<!-- US shell snippet -->` comment).
