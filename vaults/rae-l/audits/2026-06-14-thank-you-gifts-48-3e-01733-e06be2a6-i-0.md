# 🍎 Thank you gifts
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-14**
## Executive summary

- This is a gift occasion email timed smartly around end-of-school teacher appreciation — the apple emoji in the subject does a lot of work without spelling it out, and the warm orange hero is well-matched to the gifting occasion. Six named product collections (Super Star, Thank You, All The Best, Refresh, Garden Party, Good Karma) give a shopper a clear taxonomy rather than dumping them on a category page.
- The structural problem is the mid-email Cinnamoroll/app pivot. A reader browsing gift collections suddenly lands in a Sanrio collab app-download section — a different visual language (cream/yellow Sanrio aesthetic) and a completely different job-to-be-done. Lush demonstrated on June 11 with the Inner Truth Bath Melt launch that a single-focus email can be powerful; this one dilutes both messages. The gifting send should ship clean; the Cinnamoroll collab deserves its own moment.
- This is also Lush's seventh email in roughly two weeks. No single element here is bad enough to tank the email, but at this cadence the bar to earn an open is rising fast and "Thank you gifts" without a price anchor or deadline doesn't clear it.

## What's working

- The apple emoji + "thank you gifts" subject is a clever seasonal shorthand for end-of-school gifting — punches above its 17-character count.
- Six named gift collections give a clear browsing taxonomy rather than a single undifferentiated "shop" link.
- Orange hero is warm and gift-appropriate, visually distinct from Lush's more editorial recent sends (the June 8 ingredient deep-dive, the June 6 fragrance layering piece).
- "Shop Now" button has good contrast and sits above the fold — easy to find.
- Clean render, no layout bugs or overlapping elements.

## What's weak

- The Cinnamoroll/app section mid-email is a non-sequitur that breaks the gifting narrative and introduces a competing visual aesthetic.
- "Shop Now" is the weakest possible CTA copy — no noun, no occasion tie-in, no urgency.
- No price anchor anywhere; gift-givers need a budget signal (e.g. "from $12") to self-sort quickly.
- Seventh email in ~14 days means cadence fatigue is real — this send isn't differentiated enough to earn the inbox slot.
- No visible deadline or urgency signal despite the fact that end-of-school has a natural hard date.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the Cinnamoroll/app section.** Run it as a standalone send — the Inner Truth Bath Melt on June 11 is the exact template. One job, one send.
- 2. **Add a price floor to the hero.** "Gifts from $X" converts occasion-driven browsers faster than any headline rewrite. Put it in the subhead.
- 3. **Rewrite the CTA to be occasion-specific.** "Shop Thank You Gifts" beats "Shop Now"; better still, each collection tile should have its own verb ("Shop Super Star," "Shop Garden Party").
- 4. **Build in urgency around a real deadline.** If there's a school-year end date or shipping cutoff, name it in the hero. If there isn't, create one: "Order by [date] for on-time delivery."
- 5. **Rest the cadence.** A skipped send this week would lift open rates on the next one.
