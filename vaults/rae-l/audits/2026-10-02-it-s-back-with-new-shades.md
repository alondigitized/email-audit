# It’s back (with NEW shades)
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-02**
## Executive summary

- This is a well-built product-launch send. The hero is a tight close-up with a serif "It's Back" headline. A single focused product, the Ethereal Baked Face Palette, runs through three named colorways (Botanical Blur, Coastal Bloom, Poppy Fields). Each one gets a model portrait and a shade list with "(new)" tags. That follows the editorial standard set by "In fall tones" (09-26) and "NEW seasonal sets are here" (09-27). It's a clear step up from the plain-text VIP-sale collapses of 09-14 through 09-23.
- The weak spot is the offer. The only concrete incentive is a mini Limitless Lash Mascara with a £100+ order. It sits in a thin top banner and again near the bottom, and the "£" symbol is an odd fit for a US-addressed footer. The palette has no price and no review proof. The mascara gift also pulls attention away from the palette, so the email is really selling two things.
- Verdict: strong creative, mid-strength commercial hook. It's a good send for people who already know ILIA, and it won't convert many people who don't.

## What's working

- The hero is a confident close-up with a serif "It's Back" lockup, so the eye lands on the product and the message immediately.
- The "(But not for long.)" line gives credible scarcity, because it's tied to a last-year sold-out item.
- Each colorway pairs a model portrait with a shade list that has swatches, finish and "(new)" tags. That gives a shopper real product detail.
- The two red "SHOP THE PALETTE →" buttons are high-contrast and repeated, one after the intro and one after the shades.
- The skin-claims strip (safe for sensitive skin, non-comedogenic, no talc) answers a common objection before the shopper has to ask.
- The models are visibly diverse, which broadens who the email speaks to.

## What's weak

- Both red "SHOP THE PALETTE" buttons go to the palette as a whole, not to the colorway shown above them. That wastes the three-way shade merchandising.
- There is no price, no star rating and no review count anywhere near the palette. That's a missed chance to turn a sold-out comeback into proof.
- The mascara gift appears in the banner, the lower hero and the closing section, so it competes with the palette launch for attention.
- The £100+ threshold is unusually high for a beauty gift, and it's mentioned only in small print, so it reads as a hurdle and not a perk.
- Captions like "Fig. 03" and the small shade text are tiny and hard to read at mobile width.
- The lower hero shows the closed compact on green foliage. It's attractive, but it's the third near-identical product shot and doesn't add anything new.

## Recommendations

- 1. Link each colorway block to its own product page or shade filter. Add a short "Shop Botanical Blur" style button under each one.
- 2. Put a price and a social-proof line, such as "4.8★ · 1,200 reviews", directly under the intro paragraph.
- 3. Pick one hook. Either lead with the palette and use the mascara as a single small footer, or lead with the gift and make the threshold lower and clearer in the headline.
- 4. Make the shade lists larger, and drop the "Fig." captions or enlarge them.
- 5. Cut the closing compact image and replace it with a swatch-on-skin shot of the new shades.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Ethereal Baked Face Palette returns with 3 limited new shades.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Blur, smooth, build. Plus a free mini mascara on £100+.`
