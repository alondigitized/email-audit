# Father's Day finds
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-07**
## Executive summary

- A clean, on-brand seasonal email from Lush announcing their Father's Day gift range. The visual execution is solid — the hero is eye-catching and clearly seasonal — but the email stops short of delivering real purchase intent. No specific products are surfaced, no discount is attached to the Father's Day collection itself, and the single "Shop Now" CTA leaves all the work to the landing page. The Lush Club module adds value for members but the secondary blocks (Click & Collect, Preferences) dilute focus on what should be a single-minded gifting push.

## What's working

- **Hero visual is strong.** The "DAD" bath product lettering on a green ground is playful and unmistakably on-brand. It communicates occasion instantly.
- **Headline is punchy.** "FRESH FOR DAD" is short, confident, and consistent with Lush's irreverent tone.
- **Lush Club placement is smart.** Putting the points/rewards module directly beneath the hero is the right move — it gives members a reason to click over and above the product proposition.
- **Subject line brevity.** At 18 characters, it renders perfectly on mobile without truncation.
- **Clean layout.** No broken images, no overlapping elements, no obvious rendering issues.

## What's weak

- **No product specificity.** The entire email pushes to a category page with a single generic "Shop Now." There are no featured SKUs, no gift sets shown, no price anchoring. Gift-occasion emails convert better when at least one hero product is shown with a name and price.
- **No urgency mechanism.** There is no "order by X date," countdown, or stock signal. Father's Day has a hard deadline — the email doesn't use it.
- **$5 off is buried and conditional.** The app-download offer sits in a thin banner above the logo. It requires downloading an app, making it a weak purchase incentive for users who don't have it.
- **Secondary modules drain focus.** The Click & Collect and Preferences blocks below Lush Club add clutter to what should be a two-decision email: gift set or no gift set.
- **Copy below the headline is dense.** The subheadline block ("Zest-packed showers, mousse melting baths and beard-softening oils…") is doing too much. It lists product categories instead of painting a picture of the recipient's experience.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show at least one hero product.** Feature a flagship gift set — name, image, price. Even a single "Most-Gifted for Dad" callout will give the CTA landing page a specific hook.
- 2. **Add a deadline.** "Order by June 12 for delivery before Father's Day" is a conversion lever the email wastes entirely.
- 3. **Tighten the sub-headline.** Cut to one sentence: "Everything dad needs to smell like a human being again." Let the products do the rest.
- 4. **Collapse or cut the utility modules.** Click & Collect and Preferences belong in the footer, not as equal-weight content blocks. They dilute the gifting message.
- 5. **Surface the $5 app offer more prominently** — or swap it for a straight sitewide gift discount that doesn't require an app install.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag** — `<title></title>` is blank. Some spam filters treat this as a signal; more importantly, screen readers and some preview panes surface the title.

**CSS background image on `<table>` will not render in Outlook:**
```html
background-image:url(https://cdn.braze.eu/appboy/communication/assets/image_assets/...);
```
Applied directly to a `<table>` element via inline `style`. Outlook (all versions) ignores CSS `background-image` on table elements. If this image carries meaningful content, it is invisible to ~40% of B2B and desktop inboxes. VML fallback is not present in the visible source.

**Link underline suppressed globally:**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none }
```
This kills underline on all links inside Gmail's `#MessageViewBody`, including body-copy links that rely on underline as their only visual affordance — WCAG 1.4.1 (Use of Color) concern.

**Alt text audit incomplete** — HTML is truncated; cannot confirm all `<img>` tags carry `alt` attributes.
