# Your Items Are Waiting!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- This is Skechers' standard cart abandonment email — the exact same subject line, "Your Items Are Waiting!", that was audited here just six days ago on July 16, where it scored a 9/10. Inside the email, the creative holds up: three specific abandoned-cart products (Slip-ins Arch Fit D'Lites Star Love, Care Bears UNO Party, Care Bears Arch Fit Incaan Brighton The Day) are displayed cleanly, the "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" CTA is hard to miss, and the Skechers Plus 25% off discount ($100+ with code SAVINGS) gives members a concrete reason to act today. Production quality is genuinely solid — images load, hierarchy flows, no layout breakage.
- The problem is entirely upstream. A subscriber who got "Your Items Are Waiting!" six days ago is now looking at the identical subject line with no signal that anything changed. The "WE NOTICED YOU NOTICING US... YOUR CART IS STILL WAITING" hero is clever once; on the second hit within a week it reads as a drip sequence that simply recycled itself.
- Good email, broken sequence. Skechers is eroding a well-built abandonment template by firing it too fast with zero creative differentiation.

## What's working

- Three specific abandoned-cart products are front and center, with full product names — the subscriber knows exactly what they're being nudged back toward.
- "COMPLETE YOUR ORDER" is a clean, specific CTA on a visible blue button — no ambiguity about what to do next.
- Skechers Plus 25% off with code SAVINGS is a concrete, time-bounded incentive — real reason to act today rather than revisit later.
- "WE NOTICED YOU NOTICING US..." is playful and brand-appropriate — better than generic "You left something behind" copy.
- Clean render: images load, type is legible, layout holds across the full email.

## What's weak

- Identical subject line as the July 16 send — six days later, no creative refresh whatsoever.
- The product recommendations grid below the hero appears to serve men's athletic silhouettes while the three hero abandonment items clearly skew women's — demographic mismatch in the same email.
- No name personalization anywhere in the hero — "WE NOTICED YOU NOTICING US" could go out to every subscriber on the list.
- The 25% off promo block's fine print at the bottom contains date language that appears potentially stale — can't fully resolve from this render, but the "Apr 1" reference visible in the footer is a flag.
- There is a small alphanumeric code visible at the very top of the email (appears to be a product or order reference) that, if bleeding into the inbox preheader slot, would render as junk text rather than copy.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Retire this subject line immediately and vary the hook.** A subscriber seeing "Your Items Are Waiting!" for the second time in six days gets zero new reason to open. Shift to urgency (inventory) or a different angle (the Plus discount itself) before recycling this framing again.
- 2. **Fix the recommendations grid targeting.** Hero products are women's; the grid appears to surface men's styles. Unify the gendering or suppress the module for this segment.
- 3. **Add first-name personalization to the hero.** "We noticed YOU noticing us, [First Name]" makes the cute line an actual 1:1 moment rather than a broadcast.
- 4. **Surface the Plus discount deadline next to the CTA, not buried below the fold.** If it expires in days, that's the click driver — make it impossible to miss.
- 5. **Introduce a scarcity signal on at least one hero product.** The July 16 "This style has limited availability!" send showed Skechers can execute inventory urgency well — apply it here at the product tile level.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Only a few left — your cart is still here`
- **Subject Alt B:** `25% off ends soon, [First Name] — your picks are waiting`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus members save 25% on $100+ — your cart is ready when you are`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `These are still in stock, but not for long`
