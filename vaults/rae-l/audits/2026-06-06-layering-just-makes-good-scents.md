# 💫 Layering just makes good scents
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-06**
## Executive summary

- This is Lush's fragrance layering concept email — a tutorial-style send built around the idea that combining scents creates something more personal than wearing any single product alone. The "Better Together" hero introduces the layering premise, and the body delivers it through four mood-coded sections (Love & Confidence, Energy & Joy, Calm & Comfort, plus a New Limited Edition block), each pairing two complementary products side by side.
- Production values are high and consistent with Lush's recent editorial run. Color-coded sections, clean grid layout, and recognizable product names do the heavy lifting. But there's a hard-to-ignore déjà vu problem: the May 31 "Switch your mood with scent" email already organized Lush fragrances by emotional state — Energy Boost, Reclaim Your Calm, Pure Indulgence — in the same multi-section grid format. This email hits the same beats, just relabeling the categories and adding a layering hook. For any subscriber who opened that one, this reads as a remix, not a new idea.
- What's missing is any purchase driver: no discount, no limited-time price, no concrete urgency beyond "New, Limited Edition Scents" quietly sitting at the bottom of a long scroll. Lush leans editorial, and that works when the story is fresh — but pairing a repeated creative frame with zero commercial incentive produces a beautiful email that's easy to admire and easy to close.

## What's working

- Color-coded mood categories provide instant navigational clarity — subscribers self-sort to their section without reading every word.
- "Better Together" + layering hook is a more specific concept than a generic fragrance roundup; it gives subscribers a reason to own multiple products simultaneously.
- "Explore Fragrances" CTA is unambiguous — visible button, clear verb-noun copy, placed at the hero before any scrolling required.
- The Limited Edition section (Million Dollar, Banoffee Pie) adds one genuinely fresh element that could drive curiosity among loyal fans.
- No render bugs, no image gaps, consistent type treatment throughout — clean execution.

## What's weak

- Structurally near-identical to the May 31 scent-mood email — same category grid, same mood-label logic, slightly reworded; repeat openers will clock this immediately.
- No price, discount, or offer of any kind — full editorial with no conversion hook attached.
- "Explore Fragrances" lands on a category, not a specific product or layering guide — weak click destination for an email built around a specific concept.
- The Limited Edition section is buried at the bottom of a long scroll; if Banoffee Pie and Million Dollar are genuinely limited-run, that's the most urgent story in the email and it's the last thing you see.
- The layering concept stays abstract ("find your perfect scent by combining") and never demonstrates a specific pairing — one concrete example (e.g., "Rose Jam body lotion + Dirty perfume = unexpected warmth") would sell the concept far harder.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Promote the Limited Edition section to hero.** Scarcity is the sharpest purchase trigger Lush has in this email. Move Banoffee Pie and Million Dollar above the fold with a concrete quantity or deadline and let urgency lead.
- 2. **Show one real layering recipe in the hero.** Name two specific products, describe the combined result in one line. Abstract advice about layering is forgettable; a concrete combination is a reason to go buy both.
- 3. **Tighten the CTA to match the concept.** "Explore Fragrances" is a generic category link. "Try Layering" or "Shop Layering Picks" (if a bundle or guide page exists) closes the loop between the editorial promise and the click destination.
- 4. **Break the mood-category grid pattern.** The same structure has appeared twice in seven days. Consider a single hero product takeover, a quiz format ("What's your layering profile?"), or a before/after framing to give the email a distinct visual identity from May 31.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Try Rose Jam + Dirty — or find your own signature pairing inside.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New limited edition scents are here — and they layer beautifully.`
