# Painter Rory Gevis has stories to tell
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-03**
## Executive summary

- This is ILIA leaning fully into the "meet the artist" format — a muse profile on painter Rory Gevis, structured as three portrait-plus-quote blocks, wrapped around the ongoing Thru Line Waterproof Eyeliner launch. It's the most editorial, least salesy entry in the arc yet: closer in spirit to the Crown Affair founder-letter send (07-27, 7/10) than to the product-forward reveal (07-28, 9/10) or the ingredient deep-dives (07-30, 07-22).
- The photography and typography are consistently strong — warm, intimate studio shots, a restrained serif for pull-quotes, generous whitespace. But this is now the sixth-plus Thru Line-adjacent email in under two weeks, and this one buries the product furthest: there's no eyeliner shot, no swatch, no shade grid until the very final "SHOP THRU LINE" button. The subject line doesn't mention makeup at all.
- Verdict: beautifully executed as a brand-story piece, but weak as a conversion email — it reads like ILIA is coasting on launch momentum rather than giving subscribers a fresh reason to click, and the cadence is starting to show fatigue.

## What's working

- Clean, consistent visual hierarchy — hero photo, bold headline overlay, then a linear scroll of portrait/quote pairs with no clutter.
- "4X VIP POINTS" banner at the very top gives a concrete loyalty incentive before any scrolling.
- Two clear, high-contrast CTA buttons ("STEP INTO THE STUDIO," "SHOP THRU LINE") stand out against the cream background.
- No render bugs — images load fully, text doesn't overlap, footer icon row (1% for the Planet, Leaping Bunny, VIP, Auto-Replenish) is intact and legible.
- The mirror close-up near the bottom is a strong, human final beat before the shop CTA.

## What's weak

- Subject line gives zero indication this is a beauty/makeup email — a subscriber skimming an inbox has no reason to connect "painter" to eyeliner.
- The product itself is nearly invisible: no packaging shot, no swatch, no shade range anywhere in the body — just one small "rory wears thru line waterproof eyeliner" caption in tiny type.
- This is the fourth+ Thru Line-related send since 07-22; the artist-profile format is a nice variation but doesn't reset the fatigue of repeated exposure to the same launch.
- No urgency or deadline anywhere — nothing pushes a reader to act today versus next week.
- The VIP points offer is easy to miss (small banner text, no reinforcement elsewhere in the email).

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the subject line to actually name the product category — right now it reads as a random artist newsletter, not a makeup brand. This is the single biggest lever for open rate.
- 2. Insert one clean product shot (packaging + swatch) partway down, so a scroller who isn't drawn into the artist narrative still sees what's for sale.
- 3. Reinforce the VIP points offer a second time near the final CTA — right now it only appears once, above the fold, disconnected from "SHOP THRU LINE."
- 4. Add light urgency to the closing CTA block ("points offer ends [date]" or similar) rather than leaving it open-ended.
- 5. Space this format out — after 6+ Thru Line touches in two weeks, consider skipping this arc for the next send or pivoting to a non-Thru-Line topic to avoid fatigue.
