# Do you line, smudge, or wing it?
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-10**
## Executive summary

- This is ILIA's fourth send in the Thru Line Waterproof Eyeliner arc, and it's the one that finally does what a launch campaign should: teach the product's actual use case. "Do you line, smudge, or wing it?" reframes the eyeliner around technique rather than ingredients or muse-of-the-week, with a clean three-step tutorial (line+define, smudge+soften, wing it) that's the most useful, scroll-worthy content this brand has sent in the arc so far. It's a smart pivot after 08-05's generic catalog dip and 08-08's straightforward bundle announcement — this goes back to teaching, which is where the arc peaked (08-01, 9/10).
- The GWP hook up top (free mini stylus at $75+) is a real, concrete incentive, and the layout builds logically: hero → offer → tutorial → product hero shot → benefit bullets → mascara cross-sell → brand footer. The weak spot is the CTA strategy: three "SHOP" buttons across one email (pencil, Thru Line, bestseller mascara) dilutes the single clear action a tutorial email should be driving toward, and the mascara section at the bottom feels bolted on rather than earned.
- Overall this is a solid, on-brand execution that outperforms the recent scattershot sends but doesn't hit the tight, singular focus of the arc's best moments.

## What's working

- Tutorial format (line, smudge, wing) gives the reader a concrete reason to open and use the product beyond "buy it" — genuinely differentiated content for this arc.
- GWP banner ("free mini eye stylus w/ $75+") is a specific, scannable incentive right at the top.
- Close-up eye macro shots are clean, well-lit, and demographically consistent with prior ILIA sends.
- Benefit bullets (24-hour wear, waterproof + sweatproof, safe for sensitive eyes/waterline) are specific and answer real purchase-hesitation questions for an eyeliner.
- Allure badge adds credible social proof near the mascara section.

## What's weak

- Three separate "SHOP" CTAs (pencil, Thru Line, bestseller) split attention instead of driving one clear action.
- The mascara cross-sell at the bottom feels like a tacked-on second campaign rather than a natural extension of the eyeliner tutorial.
- Subject line doesn't mention the free GWP incentive, which is arguably the strongest hook in the email.
- No visible price or specific "up to X% off" — the offer is a free-gift threshold, not a discount, so shoppers scanning for a deal may skip past it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the mascara block or move it to a separate send — this email is strongest as a single-product tutorial; splitting focus with a second product undercuts the "learn one technique" narrative.
- 2. Collapse to one CTA style/color hierarchy — keep "SHOP THE PENCIL" as the only button until after the tutorial, then a single "SHOP THRU LINE" close; drop the third.
- 3. Pull the GWP hook into the subject line — it's a bigger differentiator than another beauty-technique question.
- 4. Add a price or "$75+" context near the top banner so the gift threshold is legible without scrolling to the footer.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Line. Smudge. Wing it. One eyeliner, three looks.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `A step-by-step guide to Thru Line's waterproof formula, plus a free gift at $75+.`

## Full review
## 1. Overview

This is ILIA's fourth send in the Thru Line Waterproof Eyeliner arc, and it's the one that finally does what a launch campaign should: teach the product's actual use case. "Do you line, smudge, or wing it?" reframes the eyeliner around technique rather than ingredients or muse-of-the-week, with a clean three-step tutorial (line+define, smudge+soften, wing it) that's the most useful, scroll-worthy content this brand has sent in the arc so far. It's a smart pivot after 08-05's generic catalog dip and 08-08's straightforward bundle announcement — this goes back to teaching, which is where the arc peaked (08-01, 9/10).

The GWP hook up top (free mini stylus at $75+) is a real, concrete incentive, and the layout builds logically: hero → offer → tutorial → product hero shot → benefit bullets → mascara cross-sell → brand footer. The weak spot is the CTA strategy: three "SHOP" buttons across one email (pencil, Thru Line, bestseller mascara) dilutes the single clear action a tutorial email should be driving toward, and the mascara section at the bottom feels bolted on rather than earned.

Overall this is a solid, on-brand execution that outperforms the recent scattershot sends but doesn't hit the tight, singular focus of the arc's best moments.

## 2. What worked

- Tutorial format (line, smudge, wing) gives the reader a concrete reason to open and use the product beyond "buy it" — genuinely differentiated content for this arc.
- GWP banner ("free mini eye stylus w/ $75+") is a specific, scannable incentive right at the top.
- Close-up eye macro shots are clean, well-lit, and demographically consistent with prior ILIA sends.
- Benefit bullets (24-hour wear, waterproof + sweatproof, safe for sensitive eyes/waterline) are specific and answer real purchase-hesitation questions for an eyeliner.
- Allure badge adds credible social proof near the mascara section.

## 3. What didn't

- Three separate "SHOP" CTAs (pencil, Thru Line, bestseller) split attention instead of driving one clear action.
- The mascara cross-sell at the bottom feels like a tacked-on second campaign rather than a natural extension of the eyeliner tutorial.
- Subject line doesn't mention the free GWP incentive, which is arguably the strongest hook in the email.
- No visible price or specific "up to X% off" — the offer is a free-gift threshold, not a discount, so shoppers scanning for a deal may skip past it.

## 4. What I'd change

1. Cut the mascara block or move it to a separate send — this email is strongest as a single-product tutorial; splitting focus with a second product undercuts the "learn one technique" narrative.
2. Collapse to one CTA style/color hierarchy — keep "SHOP THE PENCIL" as the only button until after the tutorial, then a single "SHOP THRU LINE" close; drop the third.
3. Pull the GWP hook into the subject line — it's a bigger differentiator than another beauty-technique question.
4. Add a price or "$75+" context near the top banner so the gift threshold is legible without scrolling to the footer.
