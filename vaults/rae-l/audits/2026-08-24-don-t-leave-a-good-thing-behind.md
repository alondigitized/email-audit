# Don't leave a good thing behind.
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-24**
## Executive summary

- This is the same cart-abandonment shell Skechers has run repeatedly — "SELLING FAST... ADD TO CART BEFORE THEY'RE GONE!" — and the subject line, "Don't leave a good thing behind," is a verbatim repeat of the 2026-08-16 send with the exact same header copy and layout. The hero product this time is a Skechers Garage sneaker with a chunky, toy-truck-cluster design bolted onto the side — a novelty/kids-adjacent silhouette that reads oddly for a "you left this in your cart" nudge aimed at an adult shopper, and there's no size, price, or color info anywhere near it.
- The one genuinely useful thing in this email is buried below six unrelated recommendation tiles: a 20% off Skechers Plus members offer with code SKXPLUS. That's a real, specific incentive, but it's demoted to a secondary banner instead of leading the email, and the fine print at the very bottom reveals real restrictions (must be logged into Skechers Plus, excludes sale styles/premieres/gift cards).
- Given the brand's cadence — this is at least the fourth near-identical cart/browse-abandonment send in two weeks — this lands as fatigue-inducing. Judgment: 4.5/10. The layout is clean and bug-free, but the content is a rerun with a mismatched hero product and a good offer that's poorly positioned.

## What's working

- Clean, bug-free render — no overlapping text, broken images, or layout breaks.
- Product photography is sharp and the hero sneaker is genuinely eye-catching (bold color-blocking, novelty detail).
- The 20% off Skechers Plus banner is a concrete, specific offer with a real code (SKXPLUS) and clear CTA ("SHOP THE SALE").
- Footer utility (app download, Klarna/Afterpay, store locator, texting signup) is comprehensive and well-organized.

## What's weak

- Subject line and header template are a verbatim repeat of the 2026-08-16 "Don't leave a good thing behind" send — zero creative refresh.
- Hero product (Skechers Garage novelty sneaker) doesn't match a plausible "item in your cart" for most adult recipients — it reads more like a kids' or collector shoe, undermining the abandonment narrative.
- No price, size, or urgency deadline shown for the actual hero product — "selling fast" has no supporting evidence (no stock count, no countdown).
- The best offer in the email (20% off, SKXPLUS) is pushed below six filler recommendation tiles instead of leading.
- Fine print reveals real exclusions (sale styles, gift cards, must be logged into Plus) that undercut the "sitewide" framing.

## Recommendations

- 1. Retire this cart-abandonment template for a while — it's been run at minimum 08-16, 08-11 (browse variant), and now 08-24 with the same or near-identical subject/header. Rotate in genuinely new creative or fold the SKXPLUS offer into a standalone sale email.
- 2. Move the 20% off Skechers Plus offer to the top of the email, above the hero product — it's the only concrete, current incentive here and it's currently an afterthought.
- 3. Swap the hero product for something that visually reads as a mainstream cart-abandonment item, or better, dynamically show the actual browsed/cart item with price and size.
- 4. Add a real urgency signal (stock count, hours-left countdown) if "selling fast" is going to be the header — otherwise drop the scarcity language entirely.
