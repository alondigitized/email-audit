# More hair care, more savings
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- This is Sally Beauty running a classic BOGO mechanic — "Buy two, get one free on select hair care" — with a summer scarcity close ("Ends July 29"). The offer is clean, legible, and structurally sound. But the subject line ("More hair care, more savings") is chronically vague: it promises savings without naming them, which undercuts open rates when a concrete tease ("BOGO free on hair care, ends July 29") would have been free to use.
- The product grid below the hero is doing solid work — four SKUs from recognizable brands (ion, L'Oreal Technique, Beauty Secrets) with individual "Shop now" links. Klarna is surfaced with a trust-bar that's appropriate for a mid-ticket cart. App and store locator callouts round things out sensibly.
- The email is competent but not sharp. Coming off the July 18 "Last Day for 30% Off" close and the July 13 freebie send, this BOGO is structurally a step down in urgency despite the July 29 deadline. The offer is real and the execution is clean — it just won't stop a scroll.

## What's working

- BOGO offer is front-and-center in the hero with large, readable type — offer is unmissable above the fold.
- "Ends July 29" deadline is visible in the hero subtext, giving the deal credibility without manufactured pressure.
- Product grid anchors the offer in real, shoppable SKUs rather than sending to a generic category page.
- Klarna 0-interest payment callout is a smart friction-reducer for a multi-item BOGO purchase.
- No render bugs — images load, text is clean, hierarchy is intact.

## What's weak

- Subject line is pure category filler ("More hair care, more savings") — gives zero reason to open ahead of other inbox clutter.
- The four product tiles are not personalized — if you're a color customer, you get the same grid as a nail customer.
- Model photography in the hero is solid but the red dress/lifestyle shot has no direct connection to the BOGO mechanic or any specific product.
- July 29 deadline is buried in small subtext beneath the CTA — it should be the headline's co-pilot, not a footnote.
- No loyalty / Beauty Club pricing callout visible, which is a miss given how hard Sally leans on that program.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject to name the offer.** "More savings" is what every retailer says. "BOGO free" is what stops a thumb.
- 2. **Surface the deadline in the subject or preview** — July 29 is 9 days out, which is real urgency. Use it.
- 3. **Move the deadline up in the hero** — put "Ends July 29" in the headline or immediately below the "get one free" line, not in 10px gray text.
- 4. **Add a Beauty Club / loyalty callout** in the hero subtext — "Beauty Club members, stock up now" costs nothing and rewards your best customers.
- 5. **Personalize the product grid** — even a simple color-vs-care split would make this feel curated rather than broadcast.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Buy 2, get 1 free on hair care — ends July 29`
- **Subject Alt B:** `BOGO free: stock up on select hair care`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Pick any two, the third is on us. Shop summer essentials before July 29.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `ion, L'Oreal, Beauty Secrets & more — BOGO free this week only.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`white-space: pre` on `img` elements — medium risk**
```css
img { -ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic; white-space: pre; }
```
`white-space: pre` on `<img>` is non-standard and has no useful effect, but can produce unexpected whitespace or layout collapse in some WebKit-based clients (Apple Mail, iOS Mail). Should be removed.

**`Founders Grotesk` declared but never loaded — low risk**
```css
a { font-family: 'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }
```
`Founders Grotesk` is set as the primary typeface for all links but has no `@font-face` declaration and no external stylesheet link in the provided `<head>`. Every email client will fall back to Helvetica. If intentional (web-safe fallback), the declaration is misleading. If the font should load, the `@font-face` block is missing.

**Google Fonts pinned to stale version v17 — low risk**
All six `@font-face` declarations reference `fonts.gstatic.com/s/opensans/v17/...`. Open Sans is currently at v40+. Google has historically kept old versioned URLs alive, but hardcoded version paths are a long-term fragility. More critically: virtually no email client executes external `@font-face` URLs (Gmail, Outlook strip them); the fallback stack (`Helvetica, sans-serif`) will render in the majority of clients regardless.

**`font-display: swap` — informational**
Declared in all `@font-face` blocks. `font-display` is a CSS4 property ignored by all major email clients. No functional impact, but adds dead bytes.

**Mobile breakpoint at `max-width: 499px` — low risk**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 499px) { ... }
```
Standard email practice uses `600px` or `480px`. Devices with viewport widths of 500–599px (common on mid-size Android phones and small tablets) will receive the full desktop layout with no responsive overrides applied.
