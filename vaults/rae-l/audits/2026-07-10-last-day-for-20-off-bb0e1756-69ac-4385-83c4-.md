# Last day for 20% off
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- This is the mechanical close on Sally Beauty's 20% off flash sale — the one that's been running since July 8 and that was telegraphed as expiring "July 10" in both the launch and yesterday's "Your summer must-haves" send. As deadline emails go, this one does exactly what it needs to: a clean hero, a prominent "LAST DAY" label, and the exclusions disclosed upfront in the body rather than buried in fine print. No surprises, no tricks.
- The problem is the freight it's carrying. This is email number six in nine days from Sally Beauty, and the third communication about this specific 20% off offer. Subscribers who bought on July 8 already got the deal; subscribers who didn't buy after seeing it twice have made their choice. The email that's left serving is the impulse-driven procrastinator — and this send is well-built for that narrow slice.
- Execution is competent but the creative is as minimal as it gets: no hero product, no lifestyle imagery, no reason to buy beyond the clock. A last-day email can get away with that if the offer has been set up well across the run — and it has been, barely. Solid mechanical send, weak creative send. Gets the job done.

## What's working

- **"LAST DAY" label is front and center** — no ambiguity about what this email is asking you to do.
- **Exclusions disclosed in the hero** — "Excludes hair color & clearance" appears right below the headline, not in a footnote. Honest framing builds trust.
- **Sally Rewards block adds layered value** — 2x/3x/4x multipliers for the same basket size is a credible push for loyalty members to go bigger, not just buy.
- **Klarna callout reduces purchase friction** — 4 interest-free payments for orders above $35 directly addresses hesitation on larger carts.
- **Clean layout, zero render bugs** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no layout drift. The email looks like the design intended.

## What's weak

- **No hero product** — the entire email is discount plumbing; there's nothing to want. A single product image (even a flat lay of three products) would give the eye something to land on and the click a more specific destination.
- **"Shop now" CTA is generic** — nothing in the button copy tells you what you're shopping for. "Shop Hair Care," "Shop the Sale," or "Save on Tools & Nails" would all be more clickable.
- **Cadence is fully burned** — six emails in nine days, three specifically about this 20% off window. Subscribers who are disengaged are not going to re-engage on the fourth attempt to tell them the same thing. The people who will open this already know about the offer.
- **No product urgency signal** — "stock running low" or a single "only a few left" callout would be more persuasive than a pure deadline close. Deadline + scarcity is stronger than deadline alone.
- **Preview text is wasted** — cannot confirm from the render, but given yesterday's brand behavior, it likely leans on generic filler rather than a reinforcing close hook.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one hero product image** — pick the best-selling item in the 20% off window and show it. Even a single flat lay of a shampoo, a tool, and a nail polish breaks the wall-of-text feel and gives the CTA somewhere specific to point.
- 2. **Sharpen the CTA** — replace "Shop now" with "Claim 20% Off" or "Shop the Sale — Ends Tonight." Verb + specific noun outperforms generic every time.
- 3. **Inject one scarcity signal** — a small "popular items may sell out" line or a bestseller callout near the CTA adds a second reason to act beyond the clock.
- 4. **Tighten the Rewards block** — the 2x/3x/4x structure is good but the layout buries it. Move it closer to the CTA as a "plus, earn rewards on every dollar" addendum rather than a standalone section.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last day: 20% off styling tools, nails & more`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Ends tonight — 20% off (almost) everything`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Grab it before the deal disappears — code-free, no minimums on most styles.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your cart is sitting at full price. Fix that before midnight.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — `Founders Grotesk` declared but not loaded**
```css
a {
  font-family: 'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif;
}
```
No `@font-face` block for `Founders Grotesk` exists in the `<style>` block. All anchor text silently falls back to Helvetica. If this is an intentional brand font, the declaration is missing; if it is a leftover, the dead reference should be removed.

**Issue — `white-space: pre` on `img`**
```css
img { -ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic; white-space: pre; }
```
`white-space: pre` is not a valid or meaningful property for `<img>` elements. It is a no-op on images in most clients, but it can cause whitespace artifacts around inline images in legacy rendering engines (Outlook, older Yahoo). The rule should be removed.

**Issue — `lang` attribute missing from `<html>`**
```html
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" xmlns:v="..." xmlns:o="...">
```
No `lang` attribute present. Screen readers (JAWS, NVDA) use this to select the correct voice/language profile. CAN-SPAM does not require it, but WCAG 2.1 SC 3.1.1 does. Add `lang="en"`.

**Minor — Open Sans pinned to v17**
Font URLs reference `opensans/v17/...` from `fonts.gstatic.com`. Google has released newer variable-font versions; v17 static woff2 files remain served but are on a legacy path. No confirmed 404s, but worth updating on the next template revision. More importantly, most major clients (Gmail, Outlook) block external `@font-face` loading entirely, so these declarations affect only Apple Mail, iOS Mail, and Samsung Mail.
