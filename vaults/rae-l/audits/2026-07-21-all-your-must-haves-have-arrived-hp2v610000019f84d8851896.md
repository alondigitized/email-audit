# All your must-haves have arrived
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- This is a new-arrivals catalog email dressed up with a 4X-points loyalty hook. Sephora is doing two things at once: showcasing a wave of "only here" exclusive launches across six or seven brands (Hung Vanigo Beauty, Sincerely Yours, Jo Malone London, Crown Affair, Touchland, Son by Marc Pullen, Jennifer) and riding the 4X-points weekend offer that was the lead mechanic in the July 19 send. The combination gives casual browsers a reason to click and loyalty-conscious subscribers a reason to urgency-buy. That's smart layering.
- The problem is execution at the scroll level. The hero is clean — "JUST DROPPED & ONLY HERE" reads instantly, the 4X badge is prominent, and there's a SHOP NOW CTA — but below the fold the email becomes a corridor of brand tiles that blur together. No single brand gets enough real estate to build desire. After the third tile, visual fatigue sets in.
- Compared to yesterday's bag-edit send ("Open for our bag edit," July 20), which used a tighter editorial format, this one trades depth for breadth and loses some punch as a result. The subject "All your must-haves have arrived" is generic — it neither names a brand nor mentions the 4X offer — which misses the single biggest hook in the email.

## What's working

- **Hero is above the fold and unambiguous** — "JUST DROPPED & ONLY HERE" + SHOP NOW + 4X badge communicates the offer hierarchy in one glance.
- **4X points urgency is repeated** — the loyalty multiplier appears in the hero and again at the bottom, reinforcing the time-bound mechanic without being annoying.
- **Exclusive positioning is explicit** — "Only at Sephora" banners on the brand tiles add genuine perceived value for beauty fans who track exclusives.
- **Clean layout, no visible render bugs** — no broken images, no overlapping text, consistent grid.
- **Brand variety is real breadth** — six-plus distinct launches signals an actual drop moment, not a padded filler send.

## What's weak

- **Subject line wastes the 4X hook** — "All your must-haves have arrived" doesn't mention the loyalty multiplier, a discount, or any specific brand. It reads like a shipping confirmation, not a promo.
- **Too many brands, no hero product** — attention is sliced too thin; every brand getting equal tile size means no single product builds desire.
- **CTAs are all "SHOP NOW" variants** — none of the per-brand CTAs are specific enough to pull a click from someone scanning; "Discover Crown Affair" or "Shop Jo Malone exclusives" would outperform.
- **Cadence tension** — the July 19 send already led with 4X points on hair care. This email reuses the same mechanic two days later on a broader category. Without a clear escalation, repeat subscribers may tune it out.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the 4X offer in the subject** — it's the biggest concrete reason to open and it's buried. Swap the vague "must-haves" phrasing for the actual mechanic.
- **Subject Alt A:** `4X points on new drops — this weekend only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Just landed: exclusives + 4X points til Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Jo Malone, Crown Affair, Touchland and more — only here.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New Sephora exclusives just hit. Earn 4X before they're gone.`
- 2. **Pick one hero brand and give it a full-width feature** — Crown Affair or Jo Malone would earn the anchor slot; the rest can cascade below as tiles. One product that earns desire beats six that share attention.
- 3. **Differentiate the per-brand CTAs** — "Shop [Brand]" or "See the full drop" is more clickable than a generic SHOP NOW button on every tile.
- 4. **Tighten the brand tile count** — six-plus brands is a lot for one send. Consider two sends (hair/fragrance vs. skin/tools) to improve relevance targeting and avoid scroll fatigue.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-zoom (WCAG 1.4.4 violation)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents users from zooming on mobile. Fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Should be removed or set to `maximum-scale=5`.

**`text-size-adjust: none` applied globally**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  -moz-text-size-adjust: none;
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```
Disabling text-size-adjust on all these elements overrides user accessibility settings. Acceptable for `body` as a reset; applying to every element is excessive and may break OS-level font scaling in some clients.

**Malformed robots meta value**
```html
<meta name="robots" content="no index" />
```
Value should be `noindex` (no space). Gmail and some webmail clients do cache/index email content; the space renders this directive ineffective.

**`ExternalClass` line-height declared twice**
```css
.ExternalClass * { line-height: 112%; }
.ExternalClass p, .ExternalClass span, .ExternalClass font, .ExternalClass td { line-height: 112%; }
```
Redundant — the wildcard selector already covers all descendants. No rendering defect, but dead CSS.
