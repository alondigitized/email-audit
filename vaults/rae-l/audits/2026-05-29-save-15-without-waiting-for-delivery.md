# Save 15% Without Waiting for Delivery ✨
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-29**
## Executive summary

- This is Sally Beauty's seventh promotional email in nine days, and for once the creative team pivoted the strategy: instead of hammering another Memorial Day percentage, this send is built around BOPIS — Buy Online, Pick Up In-Store, 15% off, deadline May 31. The pivot is smart in isolation. The subject line's "without waiting for delivery" framing differentiates the value prop cleanly from the prior week's sale noise.
- The execution, however, is bare-bones. The entire body is a typographic hero with no product imagery, no category tiles, no specific product to anchor the click. A subscriber who opens expecting a reason to shop leaves with nothing to browse — just a generic "Shop now" button pointed at the full site. The banner at the very top promotes free 2-hour delivery, which conceptually competes with the BOPIS pitch below it: the brand is simultaneously telling you to come to the store and to order for same-day delivery.
- At this cadence — seven emails in nine days — the credibility of urgency is exhausted. "Ends May 31" is two days away, but after "Last Call" (May 28), "Okay, Last Chance 😉" (May 25), and four others, subscribers have learned that Sally's deadlines are not deadlines.

## What's working

- **The BOPIS framing is differentiated.** "Without waiting for delivery" is a clear, honest benefit that breaks from the preceding sale cadence.
- **Offer is immediately legible.** "15% off when you Buy Online and Pick Up In-Store" is stated plainly in the hero — no decoding required.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no layout breakage.
- **Deadline is present.** "Ends May 31." is in the hero — urgency is at least stated.
- **Klarna integration is visible.** For higher-AOV buyers, the interest-free payment note adds purchase confidence.

## What's weak

- **Zero product imagery.** There is nothing to browse — no model shots, no category tiles, no hero product. The email gives a subscriber no reason to click beyond the discount itself.
- **Banner and hero contradict each other.** The top strip pushes free 2-hour delivery (online → home); the hero pushes BOPIS (online → store). Two fulfillment messages in one email split focus.
- **"Shop now" CTA is maximally generic.** After six prior promotional emails, this button needs a noun: "Shop In-Store Picks," "Browse & Reserve," anything specific.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** This is email seven in nine days; the urgency signals stopped landing several sends ago.
- **No product or category anchor.** The email doesn't tell you what to pick up — no "bestsellers available in-store," no category tiles — so the BOPIS benefit floats without a reason to act.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a product or category grid below the hero.** Three or four in-store-available items (bestsellers, trending colorists, whatever is actually stocked locally) give the subscriber something to pick. BOPIS works as a purchase driver only when it's attached to a product the person wants today.
- 2. **Kill the free-delivery banner or subordinate it clearly.** The contradiction between "pick up in store" and "free 2-hour delivery" needs resolution. If both are real offers, separate them visually and label each use case.
- 3. **Rewrite the CTA.** "Shop now" is doing no work here. Change to "Find It In-Store" or "Reserve & Pick Up" — verbs that reinforce the BOPIS mechanic.
- 4. **Rest the send cadence.** Seven emails in nine days is subscriber-trust debt. A 2–3 day gap before the next send would make the May 31 deadline feel real again.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Skip the wait — 15% off, pick up today`
- **Subject Alt B:** `15% off when you grab it in store · Ends May 31`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Buy online, pick up in 2 hours — no shipping wait, no minimum.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop the sale today and walk out with your order by tonight.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — `img { white-space: pre; }` (line ~56 in `<style>`):**
`white-space: pre` is not a valid or meaningful property on `<img>` elements and is redundant. In some rendering environments (older WebKit, Windows Mail) it can interact unexpectedly with inline image layout. Remove it.

**Issue — `'Founders Grotesk'` font referenced but not loaded:**
`a { font-family: 'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }` references a custom font, but no `@font-face` declaration for `Founders Grotesk` exists in the stylesheet — only Open Sans is loaded. All anchor text silently falls back to Helvetica. Either load the font or remove it from the stack.

**Issue — Google Fonts CDN for Open Sans (`fonts.gstatic.com`, v17):**
Web fonts loaded from `fonts.gstatic.com` are blocked by Gmail (both web and Android), Outlook.com, and iOS Mail in some configurations. The version referenced (`v17`) is also significantly outdated (current: v36). This is expected-but-notable behavior; the system font fallbacks will engage silently. If brand rendering requires Open Sans, embed base64 or accept the fallback.

**Issue — Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>`:**
`<html xmlns="..." xmlns:v="..." xmlns:o="...">` has no `lang="en"` (or equivalent). Screen readers cannot infer document language, violating WCAG 3.1.1. Add `lang="en"`.

**No issues found:** VML namespace declarations are correct for Outlook. `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` is present. Conditional comment structure (`<!--[if !mso]><!-- -->`) is valid.
