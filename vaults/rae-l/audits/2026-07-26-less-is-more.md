# 🧼 Less is more!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-26**
## Executive summary

- This is Lush's "Naked" campaign email — a low-waste, package-free haircare pitch under the "STRIP DOWN" headline. The concept has real brand equity (Lush has built its identity on solid bars and minimal packaging for decades), and the hero visual — hands passing colorful product tins over a tiled, pool-like backdrop — is a clean, on-brand open.
- Where it falls short: the subject line "Less is more!" gives away nothing about what's being sold, there's no discount on any actual haircare product (the only dollar figure is a $5 app-download incentive buried in a top banner), and the send is structurally identical to the multi-card product-grid format Lush has run in five of the last five audited emails (July 9's "root of good hair," July 19's scalp send, July 12's Honey grid). The "less packaging, more effective ingredients" angle is a genuinely different story than shampoo-bar-of-the-week, but the execution doesn't lean into that differentiation — it just slots the same grid template under a new headline.
- Compared to July 21's Advent calendar send (8/10, driven by a self-aware, urgency-free hook) and July 16's Halloween send (8/10, driven by sharp pun-based creative), this one reads as solid-but-safe. Judgement: a competent, on-brand send that undersells its best asset (the sustainability story) and repeats a creative structure the list has already seen multiple times this month.

## What's working

- The "STRIP DOWN" hero headline and hands-with-tins visual is a strong, distinctive open — better art direction than a flat product shot.
- The sustainability angle ("more effective ingredients, less fillers and little to no packaging") is a genuine differentiator versus the last several product-spotlight sends and ties directly to a haircare/low-waste persona.
- Product cards are clean and scannable — name, format (shampoo bar, co-wash, hot oil treatment), one-line benefit, and a clear arrow CTA per item.
- The "Carry-On Besties" travel-bundle module at the bottom adds a second, distinct purchase path (bundle vs. single product) without cluttering the main narrative.
- No visible render bugs — images load, text doesn't overlap, spacing is consistent throughout a long scroll.

## What's weak

- The subject line doesn't reference haircare, low-waste, or any product at all — "Less is more!" could be any brand, any category.
- No product-level discount anywhere in the body; the only offer ($5 off) is gated behind an app download and lives in a thin top banner, disconnected from the "STRIP DOWN" story.
- The promotion's actual terms (dates, reward expiry, exclusions) are pushed into dense asterisked fine print at the very bottom — a subscriber has to hunt for what the offer actually is.
- Seven near-identical product cards in a row (Seanik, American Cream, Avocado, Hennaed, Soak and Float, Jason and the Argan Oil) is the same repeating-grid structure used in the last several Lush sends audited this month — nothing here escalates or varies the format.
- "Shop Now" and "Explore Range" are both generic CTA copy — neither names a product or specifies an action tied to the sustainability hook.

## Recommendations

- 1. Rewrite the subject to name the actual hook — low-waste/package-free — since that's the one thing this send has that recent sends didn't. This is the single highest-leverage fix; right now the best asset in the email never reaches the inbox.
- 2. Move the $5 app offer (or a real product discount) into the hero itself, with the terms condensed to one visible line instead of buried fine print at the footer.
- 3. Cut the product grid from seven cards to three or four and let the extra space go to the "Handmade Hennas" and "Carry-On Besties" modules, which already have stronger visual identity than the grid.
- 4. Swap "Shop Now" for a specific CTA tied to the sustainability story, e.g. "Shop Naked" or "Go Package-Free."
- 5. Vary the format from the last few sends — this brand has run grid-heavy product spotlights repeatedly; a full-bleed lifestyle layout (like the Hennas module) applied brand-wide would break the sameness.

- **Subject Alt B:** `We stripped the packaging. Not the results.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shampoo bars, conditioner bars & more — minus the plastic`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `30 years of less packaging, more ingredients`
