# Shower in self-care 🚿
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-20**
## Executive summary

- This is Lush's shower-routine guide email — a content-led, editorial send that organizes their catalog into five sequential steps: LATHER → SCRUB → SOFTEN → FRESHEN → GLOW. The creative concept is "Stand-Up Self-Care," and the warm pink/peach/yellow gradient palette carries the summer self-care feeling well. There's no discount, no urgency, no limited offer — this is pure brand education, and it knows it.
- The five-step routine framework is the sharpest thing in the email. It imposes structure on what would otherwise be a product dump, and it earns repeat scrolling because each section is a new category. Worth noting: Super Milk appeared in the June 17 launch email and is already showing up again here in the FRESHEN block — which either signals smart cross-sell reinforcement or starts to feel redundant to an active subscriber who saw that launch three days ago.
- Verdict: solid editorial content, but it asks a lot of the reader (long scroll, no price incentive, vague top CTA) and does little to close anyone who isn't already a Lush convert.

## What's working

- The LATHER → SCRUB → SOFTEN → FRESHEN → GLOW routine structure is genuinely useful — it transforms a product grid into an instructional moment.
- Warm pastel palette (pink, peach, yellow-orange) is cohesive throughout and communicates "summer self-care" clearly.
- Star ratings on product cards provide in-email social proof without requiring a click.
- The hero section is large, colorful, and loads cleanly with the model imagery complementing the self-care theme.
- Section headers are bold and scannable — a fast scroller gets the routine concept without reading a word of body copy.

## What's weak

- "Discover More" is the weakest possible CTA copy — it tells you nothing about what you're discovering or why you should.
- No offer anywhere: no discount, no free shipping threshold, no gift-with-purchase — for a 5-section, 10-product email, there's zero conversion hook beyond "go look."
- Super Milk already headlined the June 17 email; putting it back in FRESHEN and GLOW here within three days feels like padding rather than intentional re-emphasis.
- Product cards are small at mobile width — the star ratings and product names read as visual texture rather than actionable information.
- The routine concept (LATHER through GLOW) isn't explained — a new subscriber won't know if these are meant to all be used in one shower or across a week.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Replace "Discover More" with a routine-specific CTA.** "Shop the Full Routine" or "Build Your Shower Routine" earns the scroll and signals what clicking delivers. This is the single highest-leverage change.
- 2. **Add one conversion trigger.** Even "Free shipping on any 3 routine products" or a bundle note would give browsers a reason to act now rather than save-for-later.
- 3. **Anchor Super Milk to a different section or pull it entirely.** Three days after its launch email, it shouldn't feel like filler. If it stays, add a "just launched" or "as seen in" callout so it reads intentional.
- 4. **Add a one-sentence "how to use this routine" lede.** E.g., "Every great shower has five steps. Here's how to nail each one." It makes the editorial frame explicit for new subscribers.
- 5. **Tighten the product cards on mobile.** Show one product per section row with a larger image, or collapse to a single hero product per step with a "see more" link.
