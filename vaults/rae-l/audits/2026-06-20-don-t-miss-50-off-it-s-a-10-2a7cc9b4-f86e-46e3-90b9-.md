# Don't Miss 50% Off It's a 10 💖
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-20**
## Executive summary

- This is a tightly focused brand drop — Sally Beauty spotlighting the "It's a 10" Miracle Leave-In line with a 50% off offer, framed around summer frizz control. The photography is clean, the hero is legible, and the "smooth hair / eliminate frizz / hold up in humidity" copy earns its summer placement. Four product variants give subscribers enough choice without overwhelming the layout.
- The problem is context. This send lands one day after the June 19 BOGO 60% email — meaning a subscriber who caught that deal yesterday is now staring at a lesser offer with "LAST CALL" screamed four times in a red repeating banner. That manufactured urgency undercuts trust precisely when the brand is asking for another click. It reads less like a true last call and more like a cadence filler.
- The email executes cleanly on the creative side. The recurring weakness — offer regression, no prices shown, generic "Shop now" repeated five times, Pride module bolted on without integration — keeps this from being a standout send.

## What's working

- Hero photography is strong: the model's hair demonstrates the product benefit directly, no abstraction needed.
- "Smooth hair, eliminate frizz & hold up in humidity" — the best copy in the email; concretely seasonal and benefit-led.
- Four It's a 10 product variants give subscribers a reason to click even if one SKU doesn't apply (leave-in, plus keratin, silk express, lite).
- Sally Rewards integration is well-placed between hero and product grid — it adds loyalty context without competing with the main offer.
- Render is clean: no broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds.

## What's weak

- "LAST CALL" repeated four times in a red banner directly undercuts the honesty signal — especially when yesterday's BOGO 60% was more valuable than today's 50% off select.
- No prices shown anywhere in the product grid: subscribers can't evaluate the deal without clicking, which adds unnecessary friction.
- "Shop now" appears five times with no variation — the hero CTA, four product CTAs, all identical and generic.
- The Pride Month module is visually disconnected from the hair theme above it; it reads like a compliance tack-on, not a celebration.
- "Select" qualifier on 50% off is disclosed but small — the visual contrast between the large "50% off" and the smaller "select" creates a mild bait-and-switch read.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show a price anchor in the product grid.** Even one example — "Was $18, now $9" — would make the 50% concrete and pull clicks. Subscribers currently have to click to know if the deal is meaningful.
- 2. **Replace the LAST CALL banner with a specific deadline.** "Offer ends Sunday" is more credible than four iterations of LAST CALL. The repetition reads desperate, not urgent.
- 3. **Vary the CTA copy across the product grid.** "Get the Miracle Leave-In", "Shop Keratin Formula" — one verb + one noun per product instead of five generic "Shop now" links.
- 4. **Tie Pride Month to the hair category.** "Express yourself with styles that hold" or "Pride-worthy hair, all summer" bridges the two themes instead of dropping a disconnected module.
- 5. **Set an explicit preheader** that doesn't let the delivery banner leak ("50% off is live — frizz-free hair starts now").

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`Founders Grotesk` undeclared — confirmed defect.**
The global anchor rule specifies:
```css
a { font-family: 'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }
```
No `@font-face` block for `Founders Grotesk` exists anywhere in the `<style>` block. Every link in the email renders in the fallback (Helvetica / system sans-serif) in all clients. If this is intentional, the font name should be removed to avoid the lookup cost and the misleading declaration.

**Open Sans pinned to `v17` — outdated but functional.**
Six `@font-face` rules point to `https://fonts.gstatic.com/s/opensans/v17/...`. Open Sans is currently at v29+ on Google Fonts. Google continues to serve old version paths, so no rendering breakage today, but these URLs will stop working if Google cleans up legacy paths. The version-pinned URL pattern is a maintenance liability.

**Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>`.**
```html
<html xmlns="..." xmlns:v="..." xmlns:o="...">
```
No `lang="en"` (or equivalent) is present. Screen readers (NVDA, JAWS, VoiceOver Mail) use this to select the correct pronunciation engine. Required for WCAG 2.1 SC 3.1.1.

**`img { white-space: pre }` is a no-op.**
The `white-space` property does not apply to replaced elements (`<img>`). It's harmless but is template cruft that should be removed.

**Outlook VML namespaces present** — `xmlns:v` and `xmlns:o` are declared, indicating the template has Outlook background/shape support. This is correct practice.

**`a[x-apple-data-detectors]` suppressor present** — correctly prevents iOS from auto-linking phone numbers and addresses inside anchor text.

**`x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` meta present** — correct.
