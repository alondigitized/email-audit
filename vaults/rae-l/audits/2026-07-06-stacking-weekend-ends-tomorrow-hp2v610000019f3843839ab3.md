# Stacking Weekend ends tomorrow 😳
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- This is email four of four in Sephora's Stacking Weekend arc — the true day-before close following the July 5 "Don't miss 30% off" send (rated 5/10), the July 3 midpoint (8/10), and the July 1 launch (8/10). "Ends tomorrow 😳" is exactly the subject line this send needed: short, time-stamped, low-noise. The problem is the body doesn't cash that urgency check. Rather than funneling a primed subscriber straight to the deal, Sephora serves up a full product catalog — Bold Summer Favorites, Get Up Close and Personal, Clean at Sephora, and more — that belongs in an evergreen browse email, not a closing salvo.
- The visual design is clean and on-brand. The hero circle with the offer callout reads clearly at the top. But the sheer scroll depth (five-plus product sections) works against the "you have 24 hours" energy the subject line earns. A subscriber on autopilot will scroll, feel overwhelmed, and defer — which at T-24 hours is as good as a lost conversion.
- Clear judgement: the subject does its job, the body doesn't respect the urgency it sets up. A tighter, one-offer, one-CTA structure would have lifted this from a functional close into a strong one.

## What's working

- **Subject line timing is exactly right** — "ends tomorrow" is specific, credible, and creates genuine urgency at T-24.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, consistent Sephora typography throughout.
- **Free same-day delivery callout** is present and well-placed for last-minute shoppers who missed the earlier emails.
- **Multiple category sections** give different shoppers (skincare, fragrance, clean beauty) an entry point.
- **Hero badge** with the offer is visible above the fold.

## What's weak

- **Body energy contradicts the subject's urgency** — five-plus browsable product sections feel like a Tuesday newsletter, not a campaign closer.
- **The 😳 emoji in the subject** reads more like social media shock bait than credible brand urgency; the July 3 send's 📣 was a better fit.
- **Fourth email in six days on the same campaign** — subscriber fatigue is real, and this send does nothing structurally different from July 5's send to re-earn attention.
- **No visible countdown or explicit "7/7 midnight" deadline** in the hero — "tomorrow" is vague enough that procrastinators won't feel the pinch.
- **Too many competing CTAs** — no single action is elevated above the rest.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse to one hero offer block**: giant "30% OFF ENDS TOMORROW, 7/7" headline, one CTA button, deadline timestamp. Everything else is noise at this stage of the funnel.
- 2. **Surface the stacking mechanic explicitly** — "30% off + 2X points + free same-day delivery" is the real value prop and it never appears together in the hero.
- 3. **Cut to two product sections max** — feature the two highest-converting categories and remove everything else.
- 4. **Add an explicit deadline time** ("offer ends tonight at midnight PT") — "tomorrow" is doing less work than a clock would.
- 5. Replace 😳 with a clock or calendar emoji to signal deadline rather than surprise.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last day: 30% off ALL Sephora Collection ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Stacking Weekend closes 7/7 — 30% off + 2X points`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free same-day delivery + 2X points stack on top — deal ends midnight`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your 30% off + 2X points disappear at midnight — grab them now`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — `robots` meta value malformed:**
```html
<meta name="robots" content="no index" />
```
`no index` with a space is not a valid directive. Correct value is `noindex`. Most crawlers will ignore the malformed directive and **may index the email web-view** if one exists.

**Issue — `maximum-scale=1` on viewport:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents OS-level pinch-zoom in mobile browsers. Fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Standard email practice, but flags as an accessibility risk.

**Issue — `text-size-adjust: none` applied globally:**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  -moz-text-size-adjust: none;
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```
Applied to `body` and nearly every element selector. Suppresses the browser's automatic text scaling for small-screen readers. Acceptable on narrow selectors; applying to `body` is overly broad.

**No issues found:** DOCTYPE, charset declaration (`utf-8`), VML namespace for Outlook, IE Edge compatibility meta, and media-query breakpoint structure are all standard and correct.
