# I've got chills 🧊
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-28**
## Executive summary

- This is Lush's summer cooling editorial — "MINT CONDITION" — timed for late June heat and organized entirely around mint and freshness-forward products. The subject pun ("I've got chills") lands cleanly against the hero concept, and the creative execution is consistent with Lush's recent editorial playbook: big pun headline, warm/contrasting background, grid of 8 products below, and a fresh cosmetics educational block at the foot.
- The problem is the same one that hurt the June 26 massage bar send: this is a browse email, not a buy email. There is no promotional offer, no urgency, no launch hook. Compare this to the June 25 shower gummies send (9/10) — that one had community co-creation, a specific launch event, and a palpable "this is new and you voted for it" energy. Here, Lush is asking you to scroll eight products on the hope that one of them sparks a purchase. It's a thin ask.
- The cadence is also wearing. This is the third email in four days from Lush. Without a real hook or offer differentiating it from the two sends before it, inbox fatigue is a real risk. The execution is polished, but the strategy is coasting.

## What's working

- **Hero concept is tight.** "MINT CONDITION" against "I've got chills 🧊" is one of Lush's stronger recent puns — the subject and headline speak to each other without repeating.
- **Seasonal timing is perfect.** A mint/cooling theme landing at the tail end of June is exactly right; the calendar alignment feels intentional, not accidental.
- **Product depth is real.** Eight distinct products (Pink Peppermint, Dirty, The Olive Branch, Maxx Or Magnanimity, Sports Run, Breath Of Fresh Air, Fresh As, Whoosh) give the send catalog weight and something for different sub-segments to latch onto.
- **Visual hierarchy holds.** Hero → product grid → editorial footer reads top-to-bottom without confusion; the Lush pink/orange check grid separates sections cleanly.
- **No visible render bugs.** Layout holds, images load, no overlapping text or broken gaps.

## What's weak

- **No offer means no reason to act now.** Without a discount, free shipping threshold, or launch hook, there's nothing converting browsers into buyers on this send.
- **"I've got chills" doesn't tell you what's inside.** The pun creates mild curiosity but zero specificity — anyone who doesn't open is flying blind on the category.
- **Aggressive cadence undercuts each individual send.** June 25, June 26, June 28 — three emails in four days dilutes the impact of all of them.
- **Eight products, no hierarchy.** The grid treats Pink Peppermint the same as Sports Run — a hero product or "summer's must-have" pick would give the eye somewhere to land and the send a sharper pitch.
- **Educational footer feels grafted on.** The "What Are Fresh Cosmetics?" block is useful long-form content, but after an already-long product grid, it reads as filler at this send length.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a summer exclusive or bundle offer.** Even "free shipping on orders over $35 this weekend" would give this email a reason to exist beyond "here are mint products." Without it, the send is doing catalog work at editorial cost.
- 2. **Pick one hero product.** Elevate one product — probably Pink Peppermint or Breath Of Fresh Air — as the "summer essential" with a bigger visual and first CTA. Let the grid serve as supporting cast, not co-stars.
- 3. **Subject: put the product category in the line.** "I've got chills" is charming but mystery-boxes the whole email. Specificity converts.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Mint condition: your summer cooling routine`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Cool down with Lush's mintiest hits 🧊`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Pink Peppermint, Whoosh, Breath of Fresh Air — all in one place.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `8 products to keep you cool when it's anything but.`
- 4. **Cut or condense the "What Are Fresh Cosmetics" footer block.** Move it to a dedicated editorial send or trim it to two sentences. At this scroll depth it bleeds urgency out of the send.
- 5. **Slow down the cadence.** Skip one send this week. The June 25 shower gummies launch was strong enough to give breathing room — a fourth email in six days risks training subscribers to ignore Lush.
