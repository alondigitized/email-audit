# Your gift, gone in a swipe
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- "Gone in a Swipe" opens with a tight, clever mascara-GWP concept — buy a mascara, earn a free gift — and then immediately abandons it in favor of a sprawling four-section bundle showcase with three different discount mechanics (10% off duo, 15% bundle save, 15% off SPF duo, plus a separate free mascara duo). The result is a hero that sets up one promise and a body that delivers four unrelated ones. The customer who clicked expecting to grab a free gift with a mascara purchase now has to navigate competing offers before they can act on the original hook.
- The creative fatigue is real. "Makeup that makes your skin better™" — the subhead anchoring the "Meet Our Heroes" section — was yesterday's subject line (July 19). And this is ILIA's third mascara or gift-adjacent send in six days: "NEW — brown mascara, made mini" (July 14), "Two gifts are better than one" (July 18), now this. Compared to the "That flushed feeling" blush editorial (July 15, 9/10) — which built a complete story around a single category — this send feels like four unfinished emails stitched together.
- The honest verdict: a promising subject line and a clean hero wasted on a body that has no editorial spine.

## What's working

- **"Gone in a Swipe" headline** — mascara-gesture wordplay is specific and brand-appropriate; gets a reader to the open.
- **Hero render is clean** — mascara illustration on a simple background, no layout breaks, good contrast with the button.
- **Multiple price-reduction signals** — 10% off, 15% off, "Free Mascara Duo" give value-hunters multiple entry points.
- **"Free Mascara Duo" section** — the clearest, most conversion-oriented module in the email; "SHOP TO CLAIM" is the strongest CTA copy in the whole send.
- **No visible render bugs** — images load, text doesn't overlap, layout is intact top to bottom.

## What's weak

- **GWP mechanic is never spelled out simply** — the hero body copy buries the trigger condition; a subscriber has to read carefully to know what purchase earns the gift.
- **Four competing offer mechanics create decision paralysis** — 10% off duo, 15% trio bundle, 15% skincare SPF duo, and a free mascara duo are all live simultaneously with no clear hierarchy or call to choose.
- **"Makeup that makes your skin better™" recycled from yesterday's subject line** — signals creative exhaustion and undermines the "Meet Our Heroes" frame.
- **"BUILD YOUR CART" is passive and generic** — should name the product or the gift, not the shopping behavior.
- **Top ribbon ("THE SKIN TINT + THE SERUM") is disconnected from the mascara/gift hero** — creates a misaligned first impression before the email even starts.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to one offer** — either lead the entire email on the GWP (mascara → free gift) OR lead on the duo bundles. Running both dilutes both. The free mascara duo mechanic at the bottom is the strongest hook; surface it in the hero.
- 2. **Make the GWP trigger explicit in the hero body copy** — one sentence: "Buy [product name], get [gift name] free. Today only." No ambiguity.
- 3. **Replace "BUILD YOUR CART"** with something that earns the click — "Claim Your Free Gift," "Get the Duo," or "Swipe to Claim."
- 4. **Retire "Makeup that makes your skin better™" from this send** — it was yesterday's subject line; using it as interior copy 24 hours later is lazy. Pick a fresh benefit line.
- 5. **Cap the body at two sections** — the Skin Tint Duo and the Free Mascara Duo are the strongest pairing; cut the Eye Stylus Trio and the SPF Duo from this send or save them for a separate drop.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free mascara — yours with any mascara buy`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The free duo. Today only.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Swipe once, get twice — add any Limitless Lash and claim your free gift.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Our best-selling mascara + a free second one. While it lasts.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**A — Google Fonts loaded via `@import` (high impact)**
```css
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```
`@import` inside `<style>` is blocked by Gmail (web and app), Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo Mail. Montserrat will silently fail to load for the majority of recipients. The fallback chain `"Montserrat", "Helvetica", sans-serif` on `p, h1, h2, h3` is correct, so rendering degrades gracefully — but the intended typeface is effectively unused.

**B — Klaviyo custom fonts also via `@import` (same issue)**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
```
Same blocking behavior. If a custom brand font is served here, it will not render in Gmail or Outlook.

**C — Hover state suppressed on all links**
```css
a:hover {color:#5f4b3c;text-decoration:none}
```
Hover color is identical to default state. Users in hover-capable clients (Apple Mail, Outlook desktop) get zero visual affordance that text is a link. This is a UX regression, not a rendering bug, but it's enforced at the CSS level.

**D — Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title> </title>
```
Minor: some accessibility tools and screen readers surface `<title>` as the document name. A meaningful value (`ILIA — Last call for your free mascara duo`) improves screen reader context.

**E — Alt text on images**
Cannot confirm — source truncates before `<img>` tags. Must verify all product and CTA images have descriptive `alt` attributes for screen readers and image-blocked clients.
