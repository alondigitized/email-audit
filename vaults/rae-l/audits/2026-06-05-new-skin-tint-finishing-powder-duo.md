# NEW Skin Tint + Finishing Powder Duo
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-05**
## Executive summary

- This is the commercial close of a tight three-email launch arc: June 2 introduced the Soft Focus Finishing Powder, June 3 told the ingredient story ("flowers, powders, and kelp"), and now June 5 delivers the ask — buy the Powder together with the Super Serum Skin Tint for 15% off. As launch sequences go, that pacing is textbook. The duo framing is also smart merchandising: it solves a real use-case problem (SPF base + shine control finish = a complete summer face) rather than just stacking two SKUs.
- The email is clean, well-rendered, and earns its offer. The two-step product education structure does real work — you understand *why* the bundle makes sense before you see the discount, which makes 15% feel like a reward rather than a clearance move. The stripped-back visual language is consistent with the last five sends.
- The main miss is urgency. There is no deadline on the 15% offer anywhere in the visible render, which makes it easy to table. After two editorial sends, this is exactly the moment a time-bound offer would convert best — and that lever isn't pulled.

## What's working

- **Three-email arc lands cleanly.** The duo bundle is the natural commercial payoff to the June 2 launch + June 3 deep-dive; subscribers who read all three get a coherent story.
- **"Soften shine — not your summer glow"** is a confident headline with good wordplay (strike styling on "shine") — sells the emotional benefit, not just the product.
- **Two-step education structure** makes the 15% feel earned; readers understand SPF + shine-control before they're asked to buy.
- **Ingredient callouts in Step 2** (no fragrance, no talc, non-comedogenic) speak directly to the ILIA subscriber's clean-formula expectations without lecturing.
- **VIP points banner + 2× VIP footer** captures loyalty tier and gives members a second reason to click beyond the discount.

## What's weak

- **No urgency on the offer.** "SAVE 15% ON THE DUO*" has no end date, no countdown, no "through Sunday" copy — a classic lift-lever left on the table.
- **Asterisks without visible conditions.** The VIP banner, the CTA button, and the offer copy all carry asterisks; no qualifying text is visible in the render, which erodes trust.
- **No social proof.** Neither product shows a star rating or review count — the Skin Tint is established enough to warrant it, and it would help validate the Powder for undecideds.
- **Strike headline can misread on a fast scan.** "soften shine~~—not~~" could read as "soften your summer glow" with a negation lost in motion; a cleaner construction avoids ambiguity.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a deadline to the 15% offer.** "SAVE 15% ON THE DUO — through Sunday" costs nothing to add and converts the email from "nice, I'll buy later" to "I should do this now." This is the single highest-lift change.
- 2. **Resolve the asterisks.** Inline the condition as a one-liner ("*Duo discount applied at checkout; excludes sets") directly below the CTA so trust isn't silently undermined.
- 3. **Add a review count to the Skin Tint.** Something like "★ 4.7 — 6,400 reviews" next to the product name takes one line and answers the skeptic.
- 4. **Tighten the strike headline.** Try "shine *less*, glow *more*" or "skip the shine, keep the glow" — same idea, zero ambiguity.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Build your full face — Skin Tint + Powder, now 15% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `SPF base + 12-hour shine control. Shop the duo through Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The complete summer face, now bundled — 15% off through 6/8.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**External font imports will fail silently in most clients:**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```
Gmail (web + app), Apple Mail on iOS with "Protect Mail Activity," and most corporate clients strip or block `@import`. Montserrat and the custom Klaviyo font will not load. Fallback chain `"Montserrat", "Helvetica", sans-serif` is appropriate, but the visual divergence may be significant if the layout relies on Montserrat's metrics.

**Duplicate `img` CSS rule block:**
Two separate `img { border: 0; ... }` declarations exist in the same `<style>` tag. The second overrides the first; the duplication is dead code and indicates a copy-paste error or template merge issue.

**`h4` excluded from font-family reset:**
The terminal rule `p, h1, h2, h3 { font-family: "Montserrat", "Helvetica", sans-serif }` omits `h4`, which retains `"Helvetica Neue", Arial` — a different stack than the rest of the document. Likely unintentional.

**Links are visually indistinguishable:**
```css
a:not([name]) { color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none }
a:hover { color:#5f4b3c; text-decoration:none }
```
All link states — default, visited, active, hover — are identical. No underline, no color shift. This fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.1 (use of color) and creates usability issues for screen-reader users who rely on link affordance cues.

**`<title>` element is blank:**
```html

```
Some preview panes (Outlook web, Gmail) surface the `<title>` as a fallback subject. A space character is present but semantically empty; this is low-risk but indicates incomplete template hygiene.
