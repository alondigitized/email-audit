# These go well with free gloss…
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- "These go well with free gloss…" is ILIA's pairing-concept send — the mechanic is clean and shoppable: here's what pairs well with the free mini Overglow lip gloss in Enamel you'll earn on any order. The hero lip image is arresting, the "goes with gloss" grid executes the concept efficiently (three product categories, each shown as a "+" pairing with the gloss), and the shade name "Enamel" gives just enough product specificity to feel editorial rather than generic.
- The problem, again, is the split-concept structure. The bottom half introduces "The Mini Tote is Back" — a completely separate limited-edition bag sell with its own CTA ("BAG IT"). This is the same fragmentation pattern we saw in the 7/18 "Two gifts are better than one" send and the 7/20 "Your gift, gone in a swipe" send. The gloss-pairing concept was strong enough to carry a standalone email. The tote punches a hole in the focus.
- The free gloss GWP has no deadline. "With any order*" is evergreen language — there's no reason to act today vs. next week, which is a problem in a week where ILIA has already landed in the inbox five times. The subject is decent (the curiosity gap on "these" plus the "free" hook will drive opens), but the heavier lift is retention of that click once open.

## What's working

- Free mini gloss GWP is called out early and clearly in the hero body copy — the offer lands before you scroll.
- The "goes with gloss" pairing grid is intuitive: three different entry points (blush, lip crayon, eye stick) give different shoppers a relevant on-ramp without fragmenting the gloss concept.
- Shade specificity ("Enamel") signals editorial intentionality rather than a generic dump.
- Hero lip imagery is tight, high-contrast, and on-brand — the strongest visual ILIA has used this week outside of the 7/22 launch teaser.
- Subject line's curiosity gap + "free" is an above-average open driver for the category.

## What's weak

- "The Mini Tote is Back" section competes with the gloss pairing concept for attention and click — this is its own send and doesn't belong here.
- No urgency on the free gloss offer: "with any order*" is open-ended with no cutoff date, reducing the reason to act now.
- "GET YOUR BLUSH +" CTA copy is ambiguous — the trailing "+" reads as a filter tag or icon, not a call to action.
- Fifth GWP-adjacent send this week after "The gift of glaze" (7/21) and "Your gift, gone in a swipe" (7/20) — the free-gift mechanic is becoming ambient noise in this inbox.
- "These" in the subject is a vague pronoun that requires an open to resolve — fine for curiosity, but it undersells the actual offer.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the Mini Tote section entirely.** Save it for a dedicated send. This email does one thing well — let it.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline to the free gloss offer.** "Free through Sunday" or "while supplies last — ends 7/27" creates the urgency that's completely absent right now.
- 3. **Rewrite "GET YOUR BLUSH +"** to "SHOP THE PAIRING" or "GET BLUSH + FREE GLOSS" — make the value of clicking the button legible.
- 4. **Name the gift in the subject line** rather than hiding behind "these."
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free mini gloss with any order — here's what to pair it with`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Get a free Overglow gloss + pinks that love it`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Mini Overglow in Enamel is yours free on every order this week — here's what it goes with.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Three shades built for the gloss that's already in your bag.`
- 5. **Move "Enamel" shade callout higher** — make it a visual badge on the gloss image rather than buried in prose copy.
