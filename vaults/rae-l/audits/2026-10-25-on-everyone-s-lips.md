# On everyone’s lips
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-25**
## Executive summary

- This is a well-built seasonal editorial email with a clear idea behind it: build a lip look from three steps (line, fill, gloss). The "Holiday Hues" hero is moody and on-brand. The three-step formula is repeated in the "Lip Lineup" and then shown as three "Looks" (Cool Berries, Bold Reds, Effortless Neutrals), so it teaches as it sells. After the VIP gift sends of 10-16 through 10-19, this is a softer, more inviting beat.
- The offer is thin. The only commercial hook is the small "FREE MINI LIMITLESS LASH MASCARA W/ $75+*" banner at the very top, with an asterisk. It has nothing to do with lips, and it never comes back in the body. The first CTA, "SHOP LIP", is a generic category link. The email is also very long, and the product cards, shade names and "Learn More" button don't point to anything specific.
- Verdict: good creative, soft commerce. It will earn browsing clicks but not urgency. A stronger offer and tighter CTAs would lift it.

## What's working

- The hero ("Holiday Hues") is on-season, visually rich and clearly about lips.
- The three-step Line + Fill + Gloss formula is easy to scan. The numbered product callouts match the numbered steps.
- Each look names its shades (Rhyme, Wild Aster, Gallery and so on) under a clear step label, which gives shoppers something specific to act on.
- The Limited-Edition Lip Sets section ties the email to gifting and shows two products with their own "SHOP NOW" buttons.
- The footer reinforces trust: cruelty-free, 1% for the Planet, Pact recycling, free U.S. shipping over $50 and ILIA VIP.

## What's weak

- The only offer is a tiny asterisked banner about mascara, which is off-topic for a lip email and visually buried above the hero.
- The primary CTA, "SHOP LIP →", is generic, and the three looks have no individual shop buttons that I can see.
- The email is long. The looks section repeats the same structure three times before the product sets, which are the real conversion point and sit far down the page.
- The "Clean at ILIA" block with the "LEARN MORE" button is a brand-values detour that pulls attention away from shopping. It also reuses the sensitive-skin theme from the 10-22 send.
- The footer has a lot of small, low-contrast text, and the "Click HERE for ILIA Promotion Details" link is tiny. That buries the fine print on the mascara offer.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the offer the hero. Put a lip-specific incentive (a free mini lip item, or a discount on the full trio) in the hero or just under it. Drop or move the mascara banner.
- 2. Give each look its own CTA, such as "Shop Cool Berries", so the steps lead straight to a three-product bundle.
- 3. Move the Limited-Edition Lip Sets higher, ahead of or right after the Lip Lineup. Gifting is the strongest holiday angle.
- 4. Cut the "Clean at ILIA" block, or shrink it to a footer line.
- 5. Put the offer's deadline and terms in plain view instead of behind a small link.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Line, fill, gloss: 3 holiday lip looks`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your holiday lip combo, picked for you`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cool berries, bold reds and soft neutrals, each in 3 easy steps.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus limited-edition lip sets made for gifting.`
