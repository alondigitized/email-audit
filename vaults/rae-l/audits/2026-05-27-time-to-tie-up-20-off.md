# Time to tie up 20% off
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- "Time to tie up 20% off" is ILIA's second send on May 27, landing on top of the already-audited "20% off ends tomorrow" (8/10) — making this the 17th email in the Friends + Family campaign by any honest count. The creative does something smarter than the morning send: it layers two conversion mechanics simultaneously. The 20% discount is now seven days old and generating fatigue; the Cart Deco GWP section — a free mini with any order — is the only genuinely new thing in the campaign's late stage. That combination gives fence-sitters two reasons to finally click when one alone has clearly stopped working.
- The metaphor — "frame and finish," "tie up" — is more distinctive than the morning send's blunt countdown language, and the "inspired by you" product section names specific SKUs with formula details, which is more useful than the generic bestseller grid seen in the May 24 email (6/10). Clean render throughout.
- The weakness is strategic, not creative: two emails in one inbox in one day is aggressive even for a final close, and "ENDS TOMORROW" is a deadline ILIA has now invoked for at least two consecutive days. The credibility of urgency in this campaign is nearly depleted. This email is the right play — it just would have worked better as the only play today.

## What's working

- **Cart Deco GWP section is the freshest mechanic in the entire campaign** — the first time a free-gift-with-purchase has been surfaced as a standalone section, giving a second conversion reason beyond the stale discount.
- **Dual-tier offer clearly visible in the subhead** — "20% off—or 25% off when you join VIF (it's free)*" efficiently surfaces the loyalty upsell without burying it.
- **"Inspired by you" product section names real SKUs** — Super Serum Skin Tint SPF 40, Limitless Lash Mascara, Eye Stroke Shadow Stick, Multi-Stick — product-level specificity that recent sends like May 24's "Shop 20% off sitewide" lacked.
- **Subject is short and metaphorically distinct** — 22 characters, no spam signals, "tie up" differentiates from the morning send's flat countdown copy.
- **Clean render throughout** — no broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds.

## What's weak

- **"ENDS TOMORROW" in the hero is a credibility liability** — subscribers who opened the May 26 and morning May 27 sends have seen this exact claim at least twice. The urgency trigger is crying wolf.
- **Two sends in one day cannibalizes both** — the morning "20% off ends tomorrow" (8/10) already covered the urgency angle. This email offers the Cart Deco hook as genuine differentiation, but it's buried in the second email when it should have been the headliner of a single, cleaner send.
- **Primary CTA is generic** — "FINAL HOURS TO SAVE →" is better than "Shop Now" but still flat. The Cart Deco section's own CTA — "SHOP TO CLAIM →" — is more specific and more compelling; the primary button should have borrowed from it.
- **Hero product image reads as decoration** — the compact/palette in the dark navy hero is small and underfeatured; it doesn't create desire or anchor a specific product narrative.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make Cart Deco the hero, not a supporting section** — the free gift is the only new creative argument in the campaign's final hours. Lead with it in the hero; subordinate the 20% off to supporting copy. "Get a free mini + 20% off — tonight only" is a stronger headline than the nth variation of "20% off sitewide."
- 2. **Retire "ENDS TOMORROW" and name a hard clock time** — "Ends tonight at 11:59 PM PT" is more credible than a deadline that's been "tomorrow" for two days running. If the sale is actually ending, prove it with a time.
- 3. **Upgrade the primary CTA to match the freshest offer** — "CLAIM YOUR FREE GIFT →" is specific, action-oriented, and mirrors the only new conversion mechanic. It also has an implied scarcity that "SAVE" doesn't.
- 4. **Consolidate future final-day sends to one email** — if the strategy calls for an AM and PM send, the PM send needs to be a meaningfully different email (e.g., GWP-led), not a second pass on the same discount story.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free gift + 20% off—ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your free mini is waiting (+ 20% off everything)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Pick a free mini on any order. 20% off sitewide ends at midnight tonight—VIF members get 25%.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Cart Deco is here: a free mini, yours on any order. Plus 20% off sitewide. Tonight only.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>`**
```html
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" xmlns:o="..." xmlns:v="...">
```
No `lang="en"` (or locale equivalent). Screen readers cannot infer language; some spam filters penalize this. Fix: `<html lang="en" xmlns=...>`.

**Issue — `@import` font declarations will be stripped by Gmail**
```css
@import url(https://static-forms.klaviyo.com/fonts/api/v1/SguDsz/custom_fonts.css);
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Montserrat&display=swap);
```
Gmail (web) strips `@import` rules entirely. Montserrat and the custom Klaviyo font will silently fall back to the declared stack (`"Helvetica", sans-serif`) for Gmail users — which is the majority of consumer inboxes. This is functionally acceptable only if the fallback stack is intentional and visually tested. No `<link>` tag alternative is present.

**Issue — Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title> </title>
```
A single space is the standard Klaviyo stub, but some accessibility auditing tools flag it. Low severity.

**Cannot assess — Image alt text** — Body images are in the truncated portion. Confirm all `<img>` tags carry non-empty `alt` attributes, especially for CTA buttons rendered as images.
