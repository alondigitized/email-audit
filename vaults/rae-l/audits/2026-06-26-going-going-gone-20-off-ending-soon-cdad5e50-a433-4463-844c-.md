# Going... Going... Gone! 20% Off Ending Soon
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-26**
## Executive summary

- "Going... Going... Gone!" is Sally Beauty's closing bell on what is now a 4-day deal-ending arc — the June 23 "Shop deals up to 40% off," the June 25 "Time's running out" clone, and now this. The hero does land cleanly: "20% off sitewide*" is bigger and broader than the category-specific 40% offers on the prior two sends, which is a genuine upgrade. But every subscriber who opened either of those earlier emails is now getting their third urgency email in four days for what reads as the same sale — which is the single biggest liability before the first pixel loads.
- The layout is tight. Hero is legible, sub-deals cascade logically, and the free 2-hour delivery callout is a smart lower-funnel nudge. The asterisk on "sitewide*" introduces a small cloud of doubt — is this actually everything? — but fine print is below the fold and not aggressively buried.
- The email does its job as a final push within a sale cycle. The real question is whether that cycle has already been milked dry.

## What's working

- "20% off sitewide*" hero is cleaner and more universally appealing than the category-specific 40% off framing on the prior two sends.
- Supporting deal tiles (40% styling tools, 40% ion hair care, BOGO 60% bondio) give multiple entry points for different buyer intent.
- "Ends June 26" hard deadline is visible at the hero, no hunting required.
- Free 2-hour delivery callout surfaces a conversion accelerator that the previous sends lacked.
- "Shop Deals" CTA is visible and unambiguous above the fold.

## What's weak

- This is the seventh email in eleven days and the third in four days with functionally identical "deal ending" messaging — cadence fatigue is severe by now.
- The asterisk on "sitewide*" undercuts the headline; subscribers who have been burned by "sitewide" exclusions before will hesitate.
- "Going... Going... Gone!" is an auction-house cliché that signals nothing new — same urgency trope as the June 25 clock emoji.
- No product specificity in the hero — the styling tools in the hero image are generic shot, no hero SKU or brand name called out.
- Sub-offer copy ("Shop now") is passive and interchangeable; none of the four tiles has differentiated CTA copy.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse the cadence.** Three urgency emails in four days for the same sale dilutes all three. The June 25 send should have been the final push; this one would only be justified with a genuinely new hook (an added category, a deeper discount, a cart-abandonment segment).
- 2. **Resolve the asterisk tension.** Either own it ("20% off sitewide — yes, even color") or reframe ("20% off almost everything*") so the fine print doesn't sneak up on the click.
- 3. **Give the hero a product anchor.** Feature one named product or brand in the hero image with a price comparison — it makes the 20% feel concrete rather than abstract.
- 4. **Differentiate the sub-deal CTAs.** "Shop styling tools," "Shop hair care," "Shop fragrances" beats four identical "Shop now" links.
- 5. **Lean into the 2-hour delivery hook higher.** It's buried — for same-day shoppers deciding whether to bother, that's a reason-to-buy that should appear in the subject or preheader.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last chance: 20% off sitewide ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your final 20% off — ends June 26`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus free 2-hour delivery on your order`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `40% off styling tools, BOGO hair care + more`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Cannot fully assess** — tracking links (click-wrapped URLs) are not present in the truncated source.

- Sending domain `em.sallybeauty.com` is a standard ESP subdomain (pattern consistent with Salesforce Marketing Cloud / ExactTarget). SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment cannot be confirmed from HTML alone.
- `.EID a { color: #464646 !important }` — this blanket override on an EID class could suppress link underlines/colors on any tracked anchor placed inside that container, making links visually indistinct. Verify no CTA links fall inside `.EID`.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issue — orphaned font reference:**
```css
a { font-family: 'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }
```
No `@font-face` for `Founders Grotesk` is declared anywhere in the `<style>` block. Only Open Sans variants are loaded. Every anchor in the email will silently fall back to Helvetica/sans-serif. If Founders Grotesk is intentional brand typography, it must be loaded or the rule corrected to match the loaded stack.

**Confirmed issue — stale font version:**
All Open Sans `@font-face` sources reference `v17` on `fonts.gstatic.com`:
```
url(https://fonts.gstatic.com/s/opensans/v17/...)
```
Current Google Fonts Open Sans is v23+. v17 URLs still resolve (Google preserves old paths), but this signals the template hasn't been updated since ~2020. No functional breakage today, but a latent risk if Google ever retires the path.

**Confirmed issue — anomalous `img` rule:**
```css
img { -ms-interpolation-mode: bicubic; white-space: pre; }
```
`white-space: pre` on `img` is a no-op for replaced elements, but it is semantically incorrect and could interact unexpectedly with certain email client CSS parsers. The rule should be removed.

**Cannot assess** — `alt` text on images is not visible in the truncated source. This is the highest-likelihood accessibility gap in any marketing email; must be verified against full source.
