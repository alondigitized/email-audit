# Your summer must-haves
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- Sally Beauty sends the fourth consecutive summer hair-care email in as many days — and burns it. This is the day before the 20% off offer expires (July 10, per yesterday's July 8 "Save on summer hair care essentials" send), which means today's job was a clean urgency close. Instead, the brand pivoted to an editorial "summer survival essentials" frame and shuffled the discount to a lower section beneath three product grids and a loyalty rewards block. The result is a well-produced content email that arrives at exactly the wrong moment in the campaign arc.
- The execution is genuinely clean: the use-case editorial structure (swim protection → heat defense → color care) gives the product grid a logical spine, the hero image is cohesive, and the layout holds. But editorial framing is what you use to build affinity at the start of a campaign, not 24 hours before a sitewide offer closes.
- This is the same creative mistake Sally made in reverse on July 5 — then, the brand correctly relabeled the hero "Last Day to Save" when it counted. Today's send retreats when it should have pressed. If there's a third email tomorrow acknowledging the deadline, the cadence will look frantic. This needed to be the close.

## What's working

- Use-case sectioning (swim / heat / color) earns the product grid — each block has a reason to exist beyond "stuff for sale"
- Hero image is visually clean and on-brand; the model and palette match the summer positioning
- MAXI rewards tier block reinforces loyalty value for members without feeling forced
- No render bugs — clean layout, no broken images or overlapping text
- Subject line is short (22 chars) and non-spammy

## What's weak

- The 20% off offer — expiring tomorrow per the July 8 send — is buried below three product sections, a loyalty block, and a coral banner, never appearing above the fold
- "Your summer must-haves" is the most generic subject Sally has used recently; it carries no offer, no urgency, no product specificity
- No deadline surfaced anywhere in the visible render — the July 10 expiry that drove urgency yesterday has disappeared entirely
- This is the second back-to-back summer hair editorial email; the cadence has no escalation arc, just drift
- "20% off (almost) everything" uses vague exclusionary language without specifying what "(almost)" means

## Recommendations

- 1. **Flip the email structure entirely.** Move the 20% off block — with the July 10 expiry date visible — into the hero. Keep the editorial use-case sections below. The offer is the news; the product sections are the support.
- 2. **Surface the deadline in the subject.** "Offer ends tomorrow" is the most compelling copy in this entire campaign right now, and it's nowhere.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Offer ends tomorrow — 20% off summer hair essentials`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last day: stock up on your summer hair kit`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Swim protection, heat defense, color care — all 20% off through tonight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Today's your last chance to save on almost everything. Here's where to start.`
- 3. **Clarify "(almost)."** Add one line beneath the offer: "Excludes [professional brands / clearance / etc.]." Subscribers who click through only to find exclusions feel burned.
- 4. **Consolidate CTAs.** Three "Shop now" section links plus a hero button fragments attention. Make the deadline offer link the dominant call to action with a countdown-style label ("Shop by July 10").
- 5. **Shorten the pre-offer scroll.** Three product sections before the primary offer is too long for mobile. Either collapse to one featured category or move the offer banner to position two.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issue — `img { white-space: pre; }`**

```css
img { -ms-interpolation-mode:bicubic; white-space:pre; }
```

`white-space:pre` is a text-layout property and has no valid effect on `<img>` elements. It is harmless in most clients but is noise that could interact unexpectedly with image-inside-`<td>` whitespace handling in older Outlook builds. Should be removed.

**Confirmed issue — `Founders Grotesk` declared but never loaded**

```css
a { font-family:'Founders Grotesk', Helvetica, sans-serif; }
```

No `@font-face` rule for Founders Grotesk exists in the `<head>`. All anchor text falls back to Helvetica/sans-serif silently. If brand consistency requires Founders Grotesk on CTAs, a self-hosted woff2 `@font-face` block (with an `mso-` guard for Outlook) is needed.

**No `role="presentation"` audit possible** — layout table markup is cut off before body tables appear.

**`<title>` is generic** — `<title>Sally Beauty</title>` renders in some preview panes and tab titles; a campaign-specific title (e.g., "Summer Must-Haves | Sally Beauty") is a minor improvement.
