# Up to 50% off Early Black Friday^ deals starts NOW! (Terms & excl. apply) 🖤
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-25**
## Executive summary

- This is a solid, loud Early Black Friday kickoff. The pink hero ("EARLY BLACK FRIDAY, up to 50% off with new deals each week") is unmistakable. The "This week's deals end Oct 31" line under it gives a credible deadline. It sells a rolling event, not a single code. That is a smart structural change from the Oct 6-7 SAVE20 sends, which pushed one sitewide coupon.
- The body is a long, tidy grid of brand tiles. Each has an "Ends Oct 31" tag and a percent-off callout: Tarte 30%, Clinique 25%, Milani 30%, OPI BOGO 50%, Benefit 30%, Virtue 30%, Kopari 50%, Tree Hut $9.99. It's easy to scan. But the hero says "up to 50%" while the headline tile is 30%, and 50% shows up only on Kopari near the bottom. There are about a dozen equal-weight tiles with no clear lead product, so the eye wanders. Most of the discounts are brand-wide percentages, not specific products.
- Overall it's a good, competent seasonal send. It would score higher with a stronger single hook and a tighter body. The loyalty strip at the top, "earn points that turn into $ off", is easy to miss, and the credit card and TikTok blocks dilute the deal story.

## What's working

- The hero is bold and clear: "Early Black Friday" with "up to 50% off" and a "Shop now" button.
- The "Ends Oct 31" tags on every tile make the deadline consistent and credible.
- Brand tiles are consistent in layout, with big percentages that scan quickly.
- The "new deals each week" framing gives a reason to keep opening future sends.
- Free shipping on $35+ is shown in the top banner.
- The in-store events and app download blocks at the bottom add loyalty touchpoints without crowding the deals.

## What's weak

- The "up to 50%" headline is carried by only one tile (Kopari), far down the page, while the top tiles are 30%.
- With twelve or more equal-weight tiles and no hero product, nothing stands out.
- The email is very long (about 8,400px), so the lower deals get little attention.
- The credit-card "free gift" block and the TikTok Shop block compete with the sale message.
- The Rewards strip at the top is small and low-contrast against the promo, so member value is easy to miss.
- A small stray text link area near the bottom looks like leftover or unstyled content.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the best deal up. Put the 50% offer in the first tile row, or make a featured "Best deal of the week" tile, so the hero's claim is proven immediately.
- 2. Cut the grid to 6-8 tiles and group them by category (skin, makeup, hair, fragrance) so it's easier to scan.
- 3. Move the credit card and TikTok blocks below the in-store content, or drop them from sale sends.
- 4. Give members a stronger hook, such as a point multiplier on the week's deals, in a bigger banner.
- 5. Add specific product names and prices to two or three tiles, to give people a reason to click through.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a Salesforce Marketing Cloud-style send (`image.e.ulta.com`, `emltrk.com`) with Movable Ink dynamic images and 10 third-party Dotomi pixels. Automated QA found no hard failures, but header-based compliance and authentication checks couldn't be verified. Many images have no alt text, and 10 pixels load over plain `http://`.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **48 tracking links were not probed.** The checker skipped them as click-redirect domains, so destinations, redirect chains and 404s are unverified. The truncated HTML shows none of the anchor `href`s, so I can't confirm them either.
- **10 Dotomi pixels use `http://`.** `http://ads.dotomi.com/cookieredir/2997/pub1.php` through `pub10.php?afceee870be08769d39f955496b70f67b=1` are cookie-sync calls to a third-party ad network.
  - Most clients block or rewrite non-HTTPS images (Gmail's image proxy, Apple Mail Privacy Protection), so these likely never fire.
  - Where they do load, they add 10 extra third-party requests.
- **Some images appear twice.** `852db034-b7e7-4f49-bcce-3b881d164154.png` and `4f7c3146-d437-4161-b9bd-a1e8ce719ffd.gif` are each flagged twice. This may be intended reuse (such as a repeated spacer or divider), but I can't confirm that from the truncated source.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Images missing alt text (confirmed by QA):**
  - Content-hosted images: `08964789-…png`, `414d3e76-…jpg`, `4f7c3146-…gif` (twice) and `852db034-…png` (twice), all under `image.e.ulta.com/lib/fe35117171640578741677/m/…`.
  - Movable Ink images: `mi.ulta.com/p/rp/01fa2fab9849e714.png` and `3eb758650b65389e.png`. If these carry meaningful content, they are invisible to screen readers and to image-blocked clients.
  - Pixels: the 10 Dotomi pixels and the open pixel `ixtw8lub.emltrk.com/v2/ixtw8lub?i=202740837&t=163148`. These should have `alt=""` plus zero dimensions so screen readers skip them.
- **XHTML 1.0 Strict doctype with unclosed void tags.** `<meta name="color-scheme" content="light dark">`, `<meta name="supported-color-schemes" …>` and the Google Fonts `<link … rel="stylesheet">` are not self-closed, unlike the neighboring `<meta … />` tags. Clients tolerate this, so it's low severity.
- **Dead CSS.** The `<style>` block ships a large commented-out hamburger-menu section (`/* 91925 Commenting out hamburger menu … */`) to every recipient. Total HTML size isn't known from the truncated source, so I can't say whether this pushes it toward Gmail's ~102 KB clipping limit.
- **Web font dependency.** The `fonts.googleapis.com` DM Sans `<link>` is stripped by Gmail and Outlook. The font-stack fallbacks are not visible in the truncated source, so I can't confirm them.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unrendered merge tokens (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`) are visible in the available source or subject. The per-recipient identifiers in the Movable Ink and `emltrk` URLs (`mi_u=202740837`, `i=202740837`) are populated.

4. Resolve a sample of the 48 tracking links with a real HTTP client that follows redirects. Confirm each lands on a 200 `ulta.com` page and record the UTMs. Also pass the complete HTML so the footer and the anchors can be audited.
