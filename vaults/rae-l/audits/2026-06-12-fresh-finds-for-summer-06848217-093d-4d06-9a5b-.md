# Fresh Finds for Summer ✨
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-12**
## Executive summary

- "Fresh Finds for Summer" is Sally Beauty's catch-all editorial send — a kitchen-sink that strings together touch-up nail products, press-ons, eye liner, glitter, and a Pride Month callout with no single organizing hook strong enough to carry the whole email. The hero is fine: a beautiful close-up of a model with fresh nails, a 30% off sitewide banner, and "no salon required" copy that lands the DIY positioning cleanly. But the email immediately fractures into a tabbed product grid — Root Touch-Up Spray, gel kits, press-ons — and keeps stacking categories (L.A. Girl liner, glitter sets, Pride, an app download) until any sense of curation is gone.
- The 30% off sitewide offer is the email's best asset and it lives in the hero banner, but it doesn't reappear as reinforcement further down. By the time a reader scrolls to the L.A. Girl or glitter sections, there's no persistent reminder of the deal that would push them to click. The Pride "Celebrating Pride Month" block at the bottom is underdeveloped — a small image and a "Shop Now" button, which feels like a box-checked afterthought rather than a genuine callout.
- Verdict: a solid promotional chassis (big offer, clear CTA, good photography) undermined by category sprawl and no editorial through-line. Compared to the tight BOGO 50% off hair sends from June 3–11, this feels like a broadcast-list blast, not a curated send.

## What's working

- **30% off sitewide banner** is front-and-center in the hero — immediately legible at a glance, no fine-print ambiguity in the visible area.
- **Hero photography** is strong: close-up model shot with polished nails reinforces the "touch up your nails quickly & easily" sub-section naturally.
- **"No salon required" copy** nails the DIY brand positioning that Sally Beauty owns.
- **Press-on sub-section** ("Get a salon look in minutes") is tightly themed within its block — cohesive product grouping.
- **Shop Now CTAs** are consistently styled and visible throughout each section.

## What's weak

- **No category cohesion** — hair color touch-up, press-ons, eye liner (L.A. Girl), glitter sets, Pride, and an app download all compete in a single scroll; there's no editorial thread tying them.
- **30% off offer disappears after the hero** — a subscriber who scrolls past the first fold sees no persistent reminder of the discount that motivated the open.
- **Pride block is underdeveloped** — small image, generic "Shop Now" CTA, and no actual products shown; reads as obligatory rather than celebratory.
- **Product density is high at small size** — the two-column grid items (Glitter set, etc.) render very small; product names and prices are barely legible at mobile scale.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is the fourth Sally Beauty send since June 5, and the "fresh finds" framing doesn't differentiate this from prior summer sends.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor every section to the 30% off offer** — add a persistent "30% off sitewide · use code at checkout" banner or reminder strip above each product row. The hero sets up a discount that the rest of the email doesn't follow through on.
- 2. **Pick one category and commit** — this send has nail DNA (hero model, press-ons, touch-up products). Cut the L.A. Girl liner and glitter into a separate send and let nails own this creative.
- 3. **Upgrade the Pride block** — show 2–3 actual products with a benefit line ("Celebrate with bold color") and keep the "Shop Now" CTA. Or skip it and give it its own dedicated send next week.
- 4. **Tighten the subject line** — "Fresh Finds" is vague; the 30% off sitewide is the news. Lead with the offer.
- **Subject Alt A:** `30% off everything — fresh summer beauty picks`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Sitewide 30% off: nails, color & more ✨`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Touch up your nails. Refresh your routine. No salon needed.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Press-ons, touch-up spray, liner & more — all 30% off today.`

**[LOW] Stale Google Fonts version**

**[LOW] `img { white-space: pre; }`**
`white-space` is not a valid property for replaced elements (`<img>`). It's harmless in compliant renderers but may produce unexpected whitespace behavior in older Outlook or WebKit-based clients. Remove or scope it.

`@media only screen and (max-width: 499px)` is below the common 480px threshold and misses devices in the 500–599px range that won't receive mobile layout overrides. Standard practice is ≤600px for a single breakpoint.
