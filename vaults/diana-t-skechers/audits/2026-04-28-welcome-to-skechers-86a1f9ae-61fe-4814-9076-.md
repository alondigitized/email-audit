# Welcome to Skechers!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-04-28**
## Executive summary

- A clean, by-the-book welcome email from Skechers. The 20% off first-purchase offer is front and center and easy to understand, the style-navigation grid gives a quick sense of the catalog, and the Skechers Plus loyalty upsell is visible below the fold. Nothing is broken. That said, the email is impersonal, completely templated, and misses every opportunity to do what a welcome email should do best: create a moment. There's no warmth, no brand story, no personalization signal. It reads like a coupon mailer, not an invitation into a brand relationship.
- The offer will drive some first-purchase conversions, but the email leaves engagement equity on the table. A welcome email is the highest-opened message a brand will ever send — this one spends that goodwill entirely on a discount and walks away.

## What's working

- **The offer is crystal clear.** 20% off, a visible promo code (`WEL20-HKV9WKPQBD`), and a "SHOP NOW" CTA appear above the fold. No hunting required.
- **"FIND YOUR STYLE" grid is useful.** Four lifestyle categories (Street, Casual, Athletic, Work) with product photography help the new customer self-select quickly.
- **Skechers Plus upsell is well-placed.** The teal banner mid-email surfaces the loyalty program with concrete benefits (free shipping, returns, birthday surprises) and a secondary CTA.
- **Utility footer is comprehensive.** App download, curbside pickup, find-a-store, SMS opt-in, pay-later options (Afterpay, Klarna) — covers the practical bases.
- **Visual hierarchy is functional.** Logo → Hero offer → Style navigation → Loyalty → Utility. Flows logically.

## What's weak

- **Zero personalization.** "Welcome to Skechers" to an anonymous subscriber. No first name, no acknowledgment of what they browsed or clicked to sign up, no "we're glad you're here" moment. This is a mass-blast wearing a welcome badge.
- **Promo code is ugly and hard to copy.** `WEL20-HKV9WKPQBD` — the long alphanumeric string looks autogenerated and erodes trust. A clean short code (e.g., `WELCOME20`) would convert better.
- **Hero image is generic stock athleticwear.** A woman in activewear sitting on a step has no direct product relevance. It doesn't show a Skechers shoe. For a footwear brand's welcome email, the hero should be footwear.
- **No brand voice or storytelling.** This email could be from any footwear retailer. Nothing about comfort technology, the Skechers DNA, or what makes the brand worth caring about.
- **"FIND YOUR STYLE" images feel small and low-hierarchy.** The grid gets equal visual weight as the loyalty banner and footer links. For a new customer with no brand familiarity, the style categories deserved a bigger moment.
- **Fine print is dense and buried.** The offer has a visible asterisk, but the terms (expiration, exclusions) are in a tiny gray footer block that most recipients won't read — and won't remember at checkout, creating potential friction.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a first name** in the hero headline. "Welcome, [Name]" costs nothing and has measurable open/click lift.
- 2. **Replace the promo code with a clean short code.** `WELCOME20` is readable, memorable, and trustworthy vs. a 20-char string.
- 3. **Put a Skechers shoe in the hero.** The brand's core product should anchor the welcome moment — not lifestyle filler imagery.
- 4. **Add one sentence of brand voice** below the headline. "Built for comfort. Made to move. Here's 20% off to get started." — something that positions the brand, not just the discount.
- 5. **Tighten the style grid.** Make it 2-up instead of 2x2, with larger imagery and a "Shop [Category]" micro-CTA per tile, to drive category-specific engagement.
- 6. **Surface offer expiry prominently.** Move the expiration date out of fine print and into the offer block so recipients know the clock is ticking — urgency drives conversion.

- | P0 | Fix the doubled-domain URL (`skechers.com/www.Skechers.com`) — identify the broken merge template field and re-send or suppress affected segment |
- | P0 | Add `https://` to the Return Path pixel src — it is currently protocol-less and firing no data |

### 1. Technical Summary

One critical broken link (doubled-domain merge error) will 404 for affected recipients. Secondary issues include a protocol-missing tracking pixel, 13 HTTP image sources at risk of being blocked by major clients, and two compliance header gaps.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[CRITICAL] Doubled-domain merge error**
- URL: `http://www.skechers.com/www.Skechers.com`
- A merge token that should have resolved to a URL path instead emitted the full domain. This will 404. Likely caused by a misconfigured dynamic content block where `%%link%%` or equivalent rendered the full domain string rather than a relative path.

**[WARN] Protocol-relative / malformed Return Path pixel**
- `src="pixel.app.returnpath.net/pixel.gif?r=964ce1dbf0dcef79cbad82c12fe25342701e4499"`
- Missing `https://` prefix. Browsers and email clients will not resolve this — the pixel fires zero data. All other tracking pixels in the send have explicit protocols; this one was likely copy-paste truncated.

**[INFO] 26 click-tracking links via `click.emails.skechers.com`**
- All redirect through the ESP click tracker. QA skipped HTTP probes on these, so destination URL integrity (including the broken doubled-domain link above) was not fully enumerable. A manual spot-check of key CTAs is recommended.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

---

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent**
- Not captured by the AgentMail relay. CAN-SPAM technically requires a functional unsubscribe mechanism in the email body (which likely exists), but the `List-Unsubscribe` header is expected by Gmail and Yahoo for bulk senders post-2024 requirements. Its absence increases spam folder risk.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent (RFC 8058)**
- One-click unsubscribe not signaled at the header level. Gmail has required this for bulk senders (>5K/day) since February 2024. If Skechers hits that threshold (near-certain for a welcome series), this is a compliance gap.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header absent**
- SPF/DKIM pass status not captured via relay. Cannot confirm DMARC alignment. If authentication is failing upstream, deliverability impact would be severe. Recommend verifying SPF/DKIM records for `emails.skechers.com` directly against DNS.

**[OK] CAN-SPAM physical address**
- Not visible in truncated source — assume present in footer (standard for Skechers sends). Flag if missing on full review.
