# THE FINAL AWAITS
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- This is the email where adidas goes full FIFA mode and forgets I run marathons. The "LA FINAL" hero — Spain vs. Argentina, New Jersey, July 19 — is legitimately cinematic, and I'll admit the subject had a pulse in my inbox. But within two seconds of opening I'm counting soccer players and looking for the exit. No running shoe, no pace talk, no hint that adidas has any idea I own more Ultraboosts than dress shoes.
- This is the third straight adidas email with zero running relevance. After the Dropset Pro send — which I actually forwarded to the running club — they've served me an app-only sale, a World Cup newsletter full of match ball giveaways, a basketball shoe, and now the FIFA Final. That Dropset Pro email was the last time adidas felt like it knew me. Everything since reads like I got accidentally added to a soccer fan segment and nobody noticed.
- I won't click. I might linger on the hero because the production quality is genuinely strong, but "SHOP SPAIN →" and "SHOP ARGENTINA →" belong in someone else's inbox. I'll mark this as read and move on.

## What's working

- **Hero design earns its keep** — "LA FINAL" in red over a clean player lineup with date, venue, and player names visible. Production value is high; this doesn't look cheap.
- **Preview text is the best line in the email** — "Made for this moment." doesn't repeat the subject, adds drama, and makes the envelope genuinely tempting before you open it.
- **"ONE STEP LEFT" is tight copy** — short, present-tense, kinetic. Works as campaign language even if it doesn't work for me.
- **Dual CTAs are decisive** — "SHOP SPAIN →" and "SHOP ARGENTINA →" make the action binary and clear. No friction on intent.
- **Timing is sharp** — sending the day before the Final is exactly when you'd want this to land if your audience was the right audience.

## What's weak

- **Wrong audience entirely** — I'm a running-club captain buying long-run gear, not a soccer fan impulse-buying a kit 12 hours before kickoff. This needed to be segmented away from running customers; this is a list hygiene failure.
- **No product, no price** — "SHOP SPAIN" is a category shrug. The July 15 app-only sale at least gave me 25% off to weigh. This is a browse request with no incentive attached.
- **ALL-CAPS subject is inbox noise** — lululemon used "Summer Sale, on its final run" to hit the same urgency beat with actual wit and lowercase confidence. "THE FINAL AWAITS" just shouts.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — after the July 13 adiClub newsletter with the World Cup match ball giveaway and a signed Adizero soccer item, I'm already numb to the FIFA drum. A fourth soccer-adjacent send in five days reads like brand tunnel vision.
- **Banner undercuts the cinematic opener** — "Explore the latest adidas best sellers | SHOP NOW →" sitting above the LA FINAL hero is tonal whiplash. It makes the whole thing feel like a department store circular trying to be a movie poster.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment this away from running buyers.** If adidas has any behavioral signal on me — running gear purchases, Ultraboost history, race-adjacent browsing — this email should not have landed in my inbox. Nike would have gated this on soccer engagement data. Fix the list before fixing the creative.
- 2. **If you must send it to runners, build the bridge.** Give me "The Adizero the players warmed up in" or a "Run your own Final" pace challenge. lululemon consistently finds the crossover angle between cultural moment and what I actually buy; adidas dropped it here completely.
- 3. **Kill the top banner.** The "best sellers" module and the World Cup hero are fighting each other. Pick one tone and commit.
- 4. **Add a member hook.** Even a "adiClub members get early access to Final kits" line turns a cold browse into a loyalty moment. There's nothing here for me as a member — compared to the July 13 newsletter that at least tried to activate adiClub points, this is a step backwards.
- 5. **Lowercase the subject.** ALL CAPS is a 2012 email-marketing tell. Bring it down a register.
- **Subject Alt A:** `The Final is tomorrow. Whose side are you on?`
- **Subject Alt B:** `One game. Spain or Argentina?`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Final day to rep your team — Spain and Argentina kits are live.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The jersey you wear to watch matters. Shop before kickoff.`
