# The adidas Originals Chinese New Year Jacket
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-07**
## Executive summary

- A Chinese New Year jacket in the first week of August — that's the "highly anticipated drop," and it's a nice-looking piece, but I have no idea why it's landing in my inbox six months out of season with zero context on why now. Then it just... pivots into another back-to-school sale halfway down, which is the fourth one of these I've gotten from adidas in about two weeks. There is not a single running shoe, not a Sunday-long-run signal, not a club nod anywhere in this email — it's straight fashion/lifestyle Originals content bolted onto a discount blast.
- The jacket photography is genuinely solid — moody, well-lit, the 3-Stripes detail is easy to read — but "Originals" jackets and basketball-in-a-parking-lot back-to-school shots tell me adidas has me filed under "general apparel shopper," not "guy who runs weekly miles with a club." Compare that to lululemon's cotton tee email a couple weeks back — also not performance gear, but at least it was a clean single-product send, not two unrelated campaigns crammed together.
- I'm not clicking. I'm not forwarding this to anyone in the run club — there's nothing here for them. Mark as read and move on.

## What's working

- The jacket hero shot is clean and well-composed — good lighting, model looks natural, the red pops against the neutral backdrop.
- "SHOP NOW" and "FIND A STORE" buttons are both clear, high-contrast, and easy to tap.
- The Apple TV perk for adiClub members spending $130+ is a real, specific, understandable incentive — better than vague "member benefits" language.

## What's weak

- Zero connection to my sport. No running shoe, no performance piece, no club or training angle — this is 100% Originals/lifestyle plus a school sale.
- Two completely unrelated campaigns (Chinese New Year jacket, Back to School sale) stapled into one email with no transition — it reads like two emails got merged by accident.
- "Highly anticipated drop" for a jacket tied to a holiday that was seven months ago is a stale, confusing hook — feels like a leftover template, not a real launch.
- This is the fourth back-to-school-flavored email from adidas in two weeks (per my last three audits) — the cadence is exhausting and I'm tuning it out at this point.
- No sizing or availability info on the jacket itself beyond a vague "available online and at select stores" — as someone who actually buys gear, I want to know if my size is in stock before I click through.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split these into two separate sends — the jacket drop and the back-to-school sale are different audiences and different urgency levels; cramming them together dilutes both.
- 2. If you're emailing a running-club guy, lead with something from Running or Training, not Originals — even a single line like "New Adistar restock" would do more for me than another jacket.
- 3. Drop or contextualize "highly anticipated drop" — if this is a heritage/reissue piece, say that plainly instead of drop-hype language that doesn't match a jacket that's been out of season for months.
- 4. Add stock/size availability signal near the jacket CTA — even "available in men's sizes XS-3XL" beats silence.
