# What's new: bold gear for your run
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-15**
## Executive summary

- Another all-women lineup, another running-adjacent lifestyle email from lululemon. The hero is a Seamless Stripe Tank and Short — fine, but that quote from Colleen Quigley, "a Track & Field Athlete, Champion, Mentor," is the first real running credential lululemon's given me in a month of these, and it's buried under a stock studio shot instead of an actual track photo. Then it pivots straight into a fleece half-zip ("doesn't do things halfway" — cute line, wrong season for me right now), a "shop new styles" grid, and closes out with the Megan Roup Edit again, which I already saw on 9/8 and scored a 5.
- This is the fifth lululemon send in two weeks and it's blurring together — fall drop, tracksuit, Megan Roup, fleece, and now this — same close-cropped studio photography, same lack of pace/mileage/gear-test language, same "shop the vibe" structure. I'm not clicking. Nothing here is time-bound, nothing tells me availability, and the one athlete credential they dropped in gets one line of italic text instead of being the headline. Marking as read, not forwarding.

## What's working

- The Colleen Quigley pull-quote is the one moment that speaks to me specifically — an actual track athlete, actual title, actual sport credibility. Nike's done this better with real athlete tie-ins historically, but at least lululemon tried.
- "Light, warm, naturally breathable" on the half-zip is a real product claim, not filler — same energy as the Polartec fleece email from 9/6 that I gave a 4.
- Clean, scannable layout — no render bugs, no overlapping text, images all loaded fine.
- The "Shop new styles this week" grid at least signals fresh inventory rather than a single hero product.

## What's weak

- Every single model is female, every shot is studio-lit lifestyle — for an email titled "bold gear for your run," there is zero actual running footage: no track, no trail, no pace clock, nothing that reads as performance testing.
- This is deja vu — I just saw the Megan Roup Edit on 9/8 (scored it 5/10 for the same reason: it's a sculpt-studio partnership, not a running edit) and here it is again as a closing module.
- No offer, no discount, no urgency anywhere in this email — just an Amex card teaser buried near the footer. Compare that to the Bad Bunny F50 drop from adidas (4/10) — at least that one had a concrete product story, even if it was soccer, not running.
- "The confidence shows" as a hero headline tells me nothing about the product, the season, or the sport.
- No club/community signal, no size-availability info, no gear-test detail (weight, breathability numbers, terrain).

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with Colleen Quigley's quote as the hero, not buried mid-scroll — put her on a real track, in real running gear, with a pull-quote about a specific run or race. That's the only asset here with actual sport authority.
- 2. Cut the Megan Roup Edit repeat — I've already seen this module twice now; it's cadence fatigue, not a running audience play.
- 3. Add one concrete offer or member perk up top — right now the only incentive is an Amex teaser near the footer, and it's not even lululemon's own loyalty program.
- 4. Show men's running gear in at least one module — I'm a 33-year-old male running-club captain, and this entire email skews female lifestyle photography start to finish.
- 5. Add a real running visual — track, trail, or race context — instead of studio backdrops for a "bold gear for your run" subject line.
