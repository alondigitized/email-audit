# After class, new French Terry’s on
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-14**
## Executive summary

- Six lululemon emails in roughly a week, and this one lands in the same place as the tennis-set email from July 7 — written for someone who isn't me. "After class" could mean anything, but everything I see the moment the email opens screams yoga studio, not road miles. Every single model in the grid is a woman. The hero is a blue French Terry sweatshirt draped on a woman. The secondary section is hoodies, shorts, and bags — all shot on women. I'm a dude who drops real money at lululemon on Surge shorts and the Pace Breaker. This email has zero acknowledgment that I exist.
- That said, lululemon's production quality is still locked in. The layout is clean, the photography is good, and the "naturally breathable" copy on the French Terry section is actually something I could see resonating — if it were aimed at me. It's not. The closest thing to a runner-relevant hook is "just-right stretch adds easygoing energy," which is fine but reads lounge-first, performance-never.
- I'm marking this read and moving on. I wouldn't click, wouldn't forward. If a female teammate in my club asked about lululemon sweatshirts, I might send it her way — but even then I'd think twice because the Summer Sale cadence from earlier this week already covered a lot of this ground.

## What's working

- **Layout is genuinely clean** — three editorial hero blocks stacked in a clear hierarchy; eye goes hero → secondary → product grid without fighting the design.
- **"Naturally breathable" in the hero copy is a real product claim** — that's the kind of specific detail lululemon does better than Nike on the newsletter side; Nike's OTC auth code email last month didn't even try to sell me anything.
- **No render bugs visible** — nothing overlapping, no broken images, the product grid at the bottom loads fully; adidas's July newsletter had tighter execution but comparable render quality.
- **Subject line brevity is good** — 36 characters, reads in a glance, no ALL CAPS desperation.

## What's weak

- **100% female models, no exception** — I scrolled the whole email and never saw a man; this is the same whiff as the mesh tennis sets email from July 7.
- **French Terry is loungewear, not a runner's fabric** — Pace Breaker, Surge, Commission — those are my lululemon; I have no mental hook for French Terry and the email doesn't try to build one.
- **No offer** — the Summer Sale emails at least had a discount to weigh; this is a pure new-arrivals push with zero price incentive to act now vs. later.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — sixth email in a week from the same brand and the creative doesn't feel differentiated enough to justify the send frequency; adidas's monthly newsletter model sets a calmer, actually-anticipated cadence by comparison.
- **"Shop What's New" and "Shop Bags" CTAs go nowhere I care about** — not a single CTA routes me toward men's or running.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment by gender, full stop.** I own Surge shorts and Commission pants. Lululemon knows this. Route the male subscribers to a version of this email that features the male French Terry lineup if it exists, or skip us on this send entirely. Nike doesn't send me women's sports bras. This is a solved problem.
- 2. **Add one runner-adjacent hook** — even a single "recovery run done, reach for this" framing would give a runner a mental slot for French Terry. Right now it's positioned as post-yoga lounge, which isn't my world.
- 3. **Drop the cadence to 3x/week max.** The Summer Sale frequency has pre-fatigued my attention so that a legit new-product email like this one gets filtered as noise on arrival.
- 4. **Give me a reason to act today** — free shipping threshold, a limited colorway, something. "Shop What's New" with no urgency goes to the mental queue that never gets revisited.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Post-run comfort just got a new fabric`
- **Subject Alt B:** `French Terry just landed — here's what it's for`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Breathable, stretchy, built for the cool-down. New styles in now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New French Terry arrivals — the fabric worth knowing about this summer.`

### 1. Technical Summary

All five custom web fonts return HTTP 403, meaning every subscriber receives the fallback system font stack. Three decorative footer images also lack `alt` attributes, and both one-click unsubscribe headers are absent from the captured message — a compliance gap under 2024 Google/Yahoo bulk sender rules.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Broken font assets (403 — all five fail):**

| Asset | URL |
|---|---|
| Saans-Light | `https://globalstaticassets.lululemon.com/fonts/Saans-Light.woff` |
| Saans-Regular | `https://globalstaticassets.lululemon.com/fonts/Saans-Regular.woff` |
| Saans-Medium | `https://globalstaticassets.lululemon.com/fonts/Saans-Medium.woff` |
| Saans-SemiBold | `https://globalstaticassets.lululemon.com/fonts/Saans-SemiBold.woff` |
| KHGiga-Light | `https://globalstaticassets.lululemon.com/fonts/KHGiga-Light.woff` |

The CDN is blocking external `GET` requests to the font origin. Every `@font-face` rule in the `<head>` references these paths; all will silently fail. The fallbacks (`Helvetica Neue, sans-serif` and `Times New Roman, serif`) are reasonable but the brand-specific editorial weight (`KHGiga-Light`) has no close system analog.

**Note on extracted URL syntax:** The QA tool captured a trailing `)` on four of the five URLs (e.g., `Saans-Light.woff)`). This suggests the CSS `url()` closing paren may be unquoted and the extractor grabbed it — worth verifying the raw `@font-face` src values to rule out a double-paren parse error in addition to the 403.

**Tracking links:** 26 click-redirect URLs skipped by the HTTP probe — destination URLs and UTM parameter correctness cannot be confirmed from this audit (see §6).

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing `alt` text — three instances:**

```
src: https://images.lululemon.com/is/image/lululemon/SFMC_Email_Footer_Arrow-Right?$pngstandard$
```

All three occurrences of `SFMC_Email_Footer_Arrow-Right` are missing `alt` attributes. These appear to be navigational arrow icons in the footer. Screen readers will announce the raw filename. Fix: `alt=""` for decorative use, or `alt="→"` / `alt="Next"` if they are functional links.

**Font fallback rendering:** With the 403s above, `h1`/`h2`/`h3` headers (56px / 40px / 30px, `letter-spacing: -2px`) will render in `Helvetica Neue`. The tight negative tracking may look incorrect on Windows where Arial substitutes — not catastrophic but visually off-brand at large display sizes.

**Dark mode:** Implementation is present and correct — Gmail targeting via `u + .body`, Outlook.com via `[class~="x_cta-white"]`, and the legacy Outlook Android hack (`.cta-white\0`). No issues found.

**Doctype / structure:** `HTML 4.01 Transitional` with VML namespace declarations — standard and correct for Outlook compatibility.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header — not found.**
Required for Google and Yahoo bulk senders (>5k/day threshold, enforced February 2024). Absence means Gmail may suppress the native unsubscribe button and flag deliverability. SFMC can inject this via Send Classification or a custom header on the sending profile.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) — not found.**
One-click machine unsubscribe is now mandatory under the same Google/Yahoo policy. Without this, automated list-hygiene tools (e.g., Apple Mail's "Unsubscribe" button, Gmail's one-click) cannot process removals server-side.

**Caveat:** Both headers may be present on the actual SMTP envelope but not captured by the AgentMail relay. Verify directly against a raw `.eml` or Postmaster Tools header trace before filing a defect against SFMC config.

**`Authentication-Results` — not captured.**
SPF/DKIM pass/fail status is unknown from this audit. If DKIM is failing at the relay boundary, `d=lululemon.com` alignment would be broken and DMARC could reject or quarantine. Confirm via Google Postmaster Tools or a `check-auth2` at mail-tester.com.

**CAN-SPAM physical address:** Not visible in the truncated source, but standard for lululemon sends — confirm footer contains street address before flagging.
