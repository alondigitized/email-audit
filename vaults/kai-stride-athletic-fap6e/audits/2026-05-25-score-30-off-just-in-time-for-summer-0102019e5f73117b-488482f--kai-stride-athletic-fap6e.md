# Score 30% off—just in time for summer
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- Nine emails in a week from adidas — nine — and today they're going back-to-back. I already got the Anthony Edwards 2 basketball shoe drop this morning, and now it's a sitewide 30% off with a promo code. I run fifty miles a week and captain a running club. The hero graphic says "End of May Savings" and "Save 30% On Summer Staples" and the three CTAs are Shop Men, Shop Women, and Shop Kids. That's the least targeted thing I've seen since lululemon showed me a red athletic dress and called it trending. There is nothing in this email that tells adidas knows I run. Not a single product image. Not a single category hint. Just a discount code and a wall of legalese.
- I won't forward it. I might click if I'm already in the market for running shoes — that 30% is real money on Adizero or Solarglide — but I'm doing the work, not adidas. They're not earning the click. They're just throwing a discount at me and hoping something sticks.
- Mark as read. Maybe come back later if I'm planning a shoe purchase. But the truth is the FIFA World Cup email from May 22 earned my attention in a way this one never will — that send had a cultural hook, a reason to care, and it still felt like adidas. This is just volume.

## What's working

- **Offer is crystal clear.** 30% off, code SCORE, sitewide including sale. No ambiguity, no "up to" hedging buried in footnotes.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean.** My eye goes hero → headline → code → buttons, exactly as intended. No clutter, no competing elements.
- **Seasonal framing lands.** "End of May Savings" with a summer-sky vibe is at least timely — it's May 25, this is contextually accurate.
- **Preheader adds info.** "Use code SCORE at checkout" in the preview gives me the mechanic before I even open, which is useful and doesn't just parrot the subject.

## What's weak

- **Total category blindness.** Three CTAs — Men, Women, Kids — and not one of them says "Run" or "Train" or even "Sport." Nike would at least route me to a Run category. Even lululemon puts a "Men's Training" tab somewhere.
- **No product imagery.** Zero. Just big text and buttons. If you're going to sell me 30% off summer gear, show me summer gear. The Adizero Boston 13 in a summer colorway would have made me tap immediately.
- **Cadence is broken.** Two emails in one day. I already got the AE2 basketball sneaker pitch this morning. Two sends before noon on the same day is spam behavior regardless of what the offer is.
- **No running signal whatsoever.** I've bought Solarglide and Supernova from adidas. They have my purchase history. There's zero evidence they've looked at it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment by category before batch-blasting.** Pull my last three purchases, confirm I'm a run buyer, swap the hero to a running shoe or apparel shot. Even a single product image — "30% off your next pair of Solarglide" — transforms this from noise to signal.
- 2. **Cut the cadence.** One email per day max. Two before noon is aggressive enough to train me to batch-delete. The Man Utd email and the FIFA email earned attention because they felt deliberate. This feels like a cron job fired twice.
- 3. **Add a run-specific CTA block.** "Shop Running" alongside Shop Men/Women. Takes ten minutes to add. Instantly more relevant for a non-trivial portion of the list.
- 4. **Hero copy could tighten.** "End of May Savings" is generic enough to be any retailer. "30% Off Your Summer Training Haul" at least implies sportswear, not socks.
- **Subject Alt A:** `30% off the gear you're actually training in`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your code for 30% off drops today — SCORE`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Full-price and sale items. Ends soon.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Running shoes, tights, everything — this weekend only.`
