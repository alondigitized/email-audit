# Back to school in Samba
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-23**
## Executive summary

- Two Samba emails in three days. Three. Days. The July 21 send was "Samba, reimagined just for you" — which I already called out as a bait-and-switch for runners — and now here's the follow-up doubling down on lifestyle sneakers while I'm deep into marathon training block. The email itself is technically clean: clear headline, simple layout, three segmented CTAs. But there's zero running DNA in this send. The hero is a wine-colored Samba sitting in a puddle. The copy is "trend-setting sneaker that goes with everything." That's not me — that's the guy at my club who shows up to group runs in Sambas and walks the last two miles.
- The July 18 Adizero Evo SL ZIP email felt like adidas finally remembered I'm a runner. Five days later they're back to lifestyle mode. It's whiplash. And the "SHOP KIDS" button in the middle of the email is doing me no favors — I don't have kids, adidas doesn't know I have kids, so why is it there? That button alone signals this is a blast-to-the-whole-list send, not a targeted one.
- Mark as read. No click, no forward. My club group chat would not find this relevant.

## What's working

- **Clean visual hierarchy** — hero image → bold headline → three stacked CTAs. The eye moves in exactly the right order without confusion.
- **Render quality is solid** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum. Layout holds on a small viewport.
- **Seasonally credible** — back to school in late July is on-calendar. At least it's not a stale promo.
- **Gendered CTA segmentation** — "SHOP MEN" sitting first in the stack shows some intent to route me correctly, even if the product itself doesn't match.

## What's weak

- **Two Samba emails in three days is cadence abuse** — the July 21 send already positioned Samba for the style-forward crowd. This is the same message with a back-to-school wrapper slapped on it.
- **Zero performance signal** — no mention of a running shoe, no trail or road context, nothing that connects to why I actually open adidas emails. The Adizero Evo SL ZIP email five days ago set a bar this one ignores entirely.
- **"SHOP KIDS" actively breaks persona targeting** — the three-button layout reads like a family retailer. If lululemon sent me a "SHOP KIDS" button I'd unsubscribe.
- **No offer** — "shop early for fresh colors" is not a reason to click. lululemon's Pace Breaker™ email gave me a specific product with a clear reason to act. This gives me a vague lifestyle pitch and calls it urgency.
- **Preheader bleeds into Samba again** — the top line appears to reference Samba before the subject even loads. If you're going to spend preheader real estate, at least use it to say something the subject doesn't — a colorway name, a price anchor, a drop date.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rebalance the cadence** — one Samba lifestyle email per week maximum. After the Adizero send, follow it with another performance product, not a second lifestyle repeat. The runners in my segment will tune out if every other email is a Samba.
- 2. **Kill the SHOP KIDS button** — unless I've demonstrated family-purchase behavior, this button tells me I'm not a known customer. Segment it out.
- 3. **Add a price anchor or specific colorway** — "fresh colors" isn't copy, it's a placeholder. Name the colorway. Give me a price. "New Collegiate Green from $110" does more work than this entire body copy block.
- 4. **Tie the back-to-school hook to something runners actually use** — even a "running gear for the fall semester crowd" angle would have worked. There are college runners buying September race gear right now.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Six HTTP image sources (same asset, repeated)**

The arrow icon is served over plain HTTP six times:

```

```

Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail all block mixed-content images by default. This asset will be blocked or replaced with a broken-image placeholder in the majority of modern clients.

**[WARN] HTTP tracking pixel**

```

```

In addition to potential blocking, this means open-rate data may be undercounted in clients that enforce HTTPS-only content.

**[WARN] Missing `alt` text on two images**

- Hero/CTA image: `74a47bcf-6a8d-4607-af9b-5c7e4e1eade9` — no `alt` attribute
- Tracking pixel: `XxPCA1xuEmzD3SnpCRmEI5y9WnOqta46wVm1LhFBciU=452` — no `alt` attribute

The missing `alt` on the hero image is the higher severity of the two: when images are blocked, the CTA conveys nothing.

**[INFO] Custom font declarations fall back silently**

`AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` are declared via `@font-face` but `src` points only to `local()` system fonts (Arial, Helvetica, Calibri). No remote WOFF/WOFF2 is loaded. This is a deliberate pattern for email (avoids remote-font blocking), but `font-display: swap` and `mso-font-alt` are correctly set — no rendering defect, noting for completeness.

**[INFO] Viewport meta uses `initial-scale=1.0` only**

`<meta name="viewport" content="initial-scale=1.0">` omits `width=device-width`. On narrow viewports this can prevent the responsive breakpoints from firing correctly in some WebKit clients. The CSS breakpoints at `max-width: 639px` and `max-width: 480px` are present, but they may not activate without the `width` directive.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header not captured**

The AgentMail relay did not surface a `List-Unsubscribe` header. This header is required by Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender policies (enforced since February 2024) for senders at adidas's volume. If the header is present upstream but stripped by the relay, this is a monitoring gap — confirm against raw SMTP headers. If it is genuinely absent, this is a deliverability risk.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not captured (RFC 8058)**

One-click unsubscribe (`List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`) is required by Gmail and Yahoo for bulk senders. Same caveat as above regarding relay capture — verify in raw headers.
