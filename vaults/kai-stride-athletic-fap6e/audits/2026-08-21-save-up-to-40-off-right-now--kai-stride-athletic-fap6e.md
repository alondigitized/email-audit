**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-21**
## Executive summary

- Another generic "% off" blast — same close-up fabric-texture hero, same stacked SHOP MEN/WOMEN/KIDS buttons adidas has run before. No shoe, no runner, no gear I can identify, just a moody swatch of what might be a shoe or might be a jacket. This is the fourth adidas email in two days ("Another Miami original" on 8/20, the August adiClub newsletter twice on 8/19, plus the Collaborations Archive Sale on 8/19) and none of them are talking to a guy training for a weekly long run.
- 40% off is a real number, so it's not nothing, but "Summer Essentials Sale" tells me nothing about running product, and "SHOP MEN" just dumps me on a full-catalog page. I'm not digging through joggers and hoodies to find a shoe worth my time.

## What's working

- The 40% off headline is clear and big — I don't have to hunt for the offer.
- "No code needed at checkout" appears twice, which removes friction I'd actually worry about.
- Three CTAs (Men/Women/Kids) load fast and are easy to tap on mobile.
- Clean, uncluttered layout — no render bugs, nothing overlapping.

## What's weak

- The hero image is abstract fabric texture — I genuinely can't tell if it's a shoe, a jacket, or a bag. Compare that to lululemon's "Meet the new Fast and Free™" send, which at least showed two people mid-stride in actual running form.
- "Summer Essentials Sale" and "Shop Men/Women/Kids" is category-blind — nothing here says running, nothing says performance vs. lifestyle.
- No product shown at all — no shoe model, no price, no size availability. I have zero idea if this touches anything I'd wear on a long run.
- No adiClub loyalty tie-in, which is disappointing given adidas's own newsletter has been pushing the Strava/adiClub integration hard this week — that block was the one thing that made an adidas email feel built for a runner. This one ignores it completely.
- Zero community or club signal — nothing here treats me like a repeat customer, just a generic segment.

## Recommendations

- 1. Swap the abstract hero for an actual running shoe or a runner mid-stride — give me something to recognize in under a second, the way lululemon's Fast and Free hero did.
- 2. Split the CTA by category, not gender — "Shop Running" needs to exist as its own button above "Shop Men," or I'll never find it.
- 3. Pull in the adiClub/Strava point angle that showed up in the August newsletter — that's the one thing this week that felt built for someone who actually logs runs.
- 4. Name at least one hero product with a price, so I can judge the offer without a click.
