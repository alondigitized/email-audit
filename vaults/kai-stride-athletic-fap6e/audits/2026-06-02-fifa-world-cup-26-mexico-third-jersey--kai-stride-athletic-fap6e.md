# FIFA World Cup 26™ Mexico Third Jersey
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-02**
## Executive summary

- This one arrived with World Cup hype energy, and I understand the play — Mexico Third Jersey, tournament timing, it's a legitimate moment. But I'm a runner, not a soccer fan, and this is now the fifth straight adidas email where the hero has nothing to do with why I'm actually in their list. The jersey photography is so dark I can barely tell what colorway this thing is. The emotional copy — "pride becomes a symbol" — is fine for a die-hard El Tri supporter, but it's not doing anything for a guy whose next buying decision is whether to rotate in a new pair of Adizero SLs before his club's weekend long run.
- Here's what actually saved this email: buried in the second half is a Strava x adiClub section. That's the first time in this entire adidas campaign stretch — going back to at least the May 26 voucher send — where I thought "okay, they know I run." The voucher email felt personalized and urgent. This one opens like a stranger knocked on my door to sell me soccer gear, then casually mentioned on the way out that they had something useful. Compare to lululemon's June 1 Father's Day guide — all men, athletic products, clear persona targeting from the first pixel. adidas still can't do that consistently.
- I would not click any of the jersey CTAs. I would scroll to the Strava section and hit REDEEM NOW. I would not forward this to anyone in my running club — the Mexico jersey lead would kill their attention before they ever get to the running-relevant content.

## What's working

- **Strava x adiClub partnership** — first time in this campaign stretch where adidas drops an offer tied directly to running culture; as a club captain whose whole group runs Strava segments together, "unlock a Strava subscription with adiClub" is the most relevant sentence in the email.
- **Clean, readable CTAs** — SHOP NOW, SHOP ALL FIFA WORLD CUP 26™, FIND STORE NOW, and REDEEM NOW are all legible, well-spaced, and unambiguous.
- **World Cup timing** — if you're in the soccer-fan segment, this is clearly a timely, contextually relevant send.
- **No spam aesthetics** — no caps-lock screaming, no exclamation stacking, clean layout throughout.

## What's weak

- **Hero product is nearly invisible** — the jersey photography is so dark I genuinely cannot make out the colorway or design details. The whole purpose of a jersey email is to show me the jersey; this doesn't do that.
- **Soccer content leading a runner** — three competing soccer CTAs before a single running-relevant mention is the wrong order for my segment. This is adidas guessing at who I am, same energy as the retro Japan lifestyle sneaker from May 30.
- **Strava section is buried** — the one offer I'd actually click is below the fold, sandwiched after the primary content. A runner in a quick inbox scan may never reach it.
- **Zero price signal anywhere** — no discount, no price anchor, nothing. The May 26 voucher email gave me a specific dollar amount tied to my account; "SHOP NOW" into full retail after that is a step backward.
- **Cadence fatigue** — I've tracked 10+ adidas emails in 8 days. This one reopening with a generic product launch rather than anything account-personalized makes it feel like they forgot I exist as a specific customer.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with or split out the Strava section** — "Unlock Strava Premium through adiClub" in the subject line would get me to open immediately. Burying the runner hook under three soccer CTAs is a segmentation failure, not a creative one.
- 2. **Shoot the jersey in real light** — put it on a body in motion, show the design. Right now it's a dark silhouette. Nike at least shows you the product when they launch colorways.
- 3. **Add a price anchor** — even "Starting at $90" gives me a financial signal to calibrate against. This email gives me nothing to act on except sentiment.
- 4. **Segment the list harder** — Mexico jersey content belongs in the soccer/futbol segment. lululemon doesn't send me golf gear. adidas has enough purchase data to stop sending replica jerseys to performance runners.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Log your miles, track your PRs — unlock Strava Premium free with your adiClub points.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Unlock Strava + shop the Mexico Third Jersey before the tournament kicks off.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard failures: a broken dynamic-content link and a missing plain-text MIME part. Additionally, a single HTTP-only image asset is referenced eight times, which will be blocked in security-hardened clients, and two images carry no alt text.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken dynamic content link**
The QA prober resolved `https://dv.adidas.com/o/3e9b4806-236c-4ed2-8488-2cf3a6ffef40?cp_tp=v3.eJ...` and received an internal redirect to a relative path with an unknown URL scheme:

```
unknown url type: '/g/3e9b4806-236c-4ed2-8488-2cf3a6ffef40?txn=dc562580-5e94-11f1-bbb9-26d090a92706'
```

This is a server-side routing error on `dv.adidas.com` — the dynamic content service returned a relative URI as if it were an absolute redirect target. Any link or image tied to this token ID will fail to resolve for recipients.

**[INFO] 36 tracking links skipped**
All click links route through `click.comms.adidas.com` redirect chains. Landing page destinations and UTM parameters cannot be verified without following the full redirect. No further action here unless a destination audit is required separately.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] HTTP-only image asset — 8 occurrences of the same file**
```
http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```
This is the "arrow" CTA icon, served over plain HTTP, repeated across all call-to-action rows. Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail all proxy or hard-block non-HTTPS image sources. Every CTA arrow will render as a broken image in those clients. The fix is to serve this asset from `https://image.link.adidas.com/...` — the CDN almost certainly supports TLS already.

**[WARN] Missing alt text on at least 2 images**
- `3e9b4806-236c-4ed2-8488-2cf3a6ffef40` (the broken dynamic content image above) — `alt=""` or absent
- `3eCkozz58krV0JT_nGgpSnKrDt1BrOiCPqWw-F568a4=452` (pixel/tracker via `click.comms.adidas.com`) — alt absent

The tracker pixel with no alt is standard practice, but the product/hero image missing alt text is an accessibility gap and will render as a blank in image-off environments.

**[INFO] Font-face declarations load local system fonts only**
Both `AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` `@font-face` blocks reference `local()` sources only — no remote font URLs. This is intentional for email (avoids external font fetch failures) and causes no rendering defect; system fallbacks (Arial/Helvetica) load as designed.

**[INFO] HTML 4.01 Transitional DOCTYPE**
Standard for broad Outlook/legacy client compatibility. No issue.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header not captured**
The QA relay did not surface a `List-Unsubscribe` header. This may be a relay-capture gap rather than a true absence — but if the header is genuinely missing, Gmail and Yahoo will suppress the native unsubscribe UI for bulk senders, which violates both platforms' 2024 sender requirements.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not captured**
One-click machine unsubscribe is required by Gmail/Yahoo for senders above the volume threshold adidas certainly exceeds. Absence of this header (again, possibly a relay artifact) is a compliance gap.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` not captured**
SPF and DKIM pass/fail status for `us-news.comms.adidas.com` is unverifiable from the relayed headers. This is likely a relay-capture limitation; adidas's sending infrastructure is well-established. If headers can be pulled from raw SMTP delivery, verify SPF/DKIM alignment between `adidas.com` and `us-news.comms.adidas.com`.

**[INFO] `ROBOTS: NOINDEX, NOFOLLOW` and `referrer: no-referrer` meta tags are present** — correct defensive posture for email HTML.
