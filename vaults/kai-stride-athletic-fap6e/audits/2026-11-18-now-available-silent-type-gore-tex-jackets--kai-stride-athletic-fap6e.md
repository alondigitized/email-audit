# Now available: Silent Type™ GORE-TEX® jackets
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-18**
## Executive summary

- My gut reaction is that this is the best-looking lululemon email I've had this week, and it still isn't a running email. Silent Type GORE-TEX is a rain-shell drop. The hero is a person in a hooded jacket in falling snow, and "waterproof, windproof, breathable" is stamped on the photos. I'd be interested if I were deciding what to wear for a wet November long run. But nothing in here says "run." There's no reflectivity, no packability, no mention of a vented back, nothing about whether it works at tempo. Those are the things I'd need to know before I spend $300+ on a shell.
- The layout is clean. It's calm and editorial, and it has that seminar tone I've come to expect from lululemon. Compare that to the 11/17 "pillowy High Loft Puffers" email, which was a mood board with no running angle. This one at least has a real technical hook in GORE-TEX. Then the Black Friday banner is stuck at the very bottom like an afterthought, right after a $300+ jacket drop. That feels off.
- Would I click? Probably one tap on the men's rain jacket, mostly out of curiosity about the price. Would I forward it? No. I'd only send it to the club chat if someone asked for a rain-run layer.

## What's working

- The "Introducing Silent Type™" headline is clear, and the line "warm, dry…and focused" tells me what the product does in one breath.
- The close-up on the black hooded shell with water beads on the shoulders and "waterproof, windproof, breathable" overlaid sells the function better than any copy could.
- The email splits into Women's and Men's sections, each with its own "Shop Men's GORE-TEX®" button. I got to my category fast.
- The men's models look about my age and build. That's the first time in a while an email didn't feel pointed at someone else.
- The launch date is clear: "now available online and in select stores on November 20." That gives me a real date, not vague urgency.

## What's weak

- Nothing here says running. The models are standing around or posing in the rain. No stride, no pace, no trail. I can't tell whether this is a run shell or a commuter coat.
- There's no price anywhere. A GORE-TEX jacket is a serious purchase, and I'd have to click just to find out if it's $200 or $500.
- The links are the vague "Shop GORE-TEX® Rain Jacket →" repeated over and over. I can't tell the products apart without clicking.
- The Black Friday "early access" banner is buried at the bottom and says "only on the app." That reads as a bait-and-switch next to a jacket I'd buy on the web. It also contradicts the "online and in select stores" line up top.
- The email is very long, and the last image, a puffer coat, drifts off topic. It looks like the 11/17 puffers email again.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add one runner-specific line per product, such as "vented back, reflective trim, packs into its own pocket, tested on 15-mile wet long runs." That would lift this email more than any other change.
- 2. Put the price on the jacket tiles, along with a size-availability note since this is a new drop.
- 3. Move the Black Friday member banner up or cut it. Don't make a technical launch compete with a sale.
- 4. Make the link text specific: "Shop the men's rain jacket — $X."
- 5. Drop the puffer tile at the end. It dilutes the GORE-TEX story.
