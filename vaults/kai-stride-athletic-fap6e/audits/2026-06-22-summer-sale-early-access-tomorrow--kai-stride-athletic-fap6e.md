# Summer Sale: Early Access tomorrow
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- Orange. Big. Bold. That's the first thing that hits — and honestly for a sale announcement it works. lululemon leaned into the heat-wave energy with a warm coral-to-orange hero and "SUMMER SALE" in all-caps white. As a member, seeing "Early Access" at the very top of the email made me feel like I was getting let in a side door before the general public. That part landed.
- But then I actually read the body and my enthusiasm dropped. The whole message is essentially: *sale coming tomorrow, go download the app*. That's it. No products. No discount amount. No "here's a preview of what's dropping." After five lululemon emails in the past week — including the golf detour on June 16th and the vague "summer styles easing your way" whiff on June 20th — I need more than a teaser. I've already got the app. Telling me to download it is friction that shouldn't exist for a member email.
- I'd mark this as read and check the app tomorrow morning before my run, but I'm not clicking "Download the App" because I have it. And I'm not forwarding this to anyone in the club because there's nothing to actually share yet.

## What's working

- **"Membership Early Access" tag at the top** — feels like a badge, not a blast. That's the right tone for a loyalty play.
- **Orange hero is seasonally right** — it reads "summer heat" immediately. Better visual decision than the wishy-washy blue from the June 20th send.
- **"Tomorrow" in the subject** — concrete urgency that actually means something. It got me to open.
- **Subject line length** — 35 characters, mobile-clean, no gimmicks. Beats the adidas "earn 50 points for completing your profile" email which still felt wordy even at 7/10.
- **Visual hierarchy is tight** — hero text dominates, CTA button is clearly black on white. No confusion about where to look.

## What's weak

- **"Download the App" CTA for a member email is a miss** — I have the app. The CTA should be "Get Early Access" or "Set a Reminder" — something that acknowledges I'm already in the ecosystem.
- **Zero product preview** — Nike's transactional email (the one-time code send) at least delivered exactly what was promised and got out of the way. This email promises something — a Summer Sale — and shows me nothing. That's a tease with no payoff.
- **No specific discount or category signal** — Is this a running sale? Menswear? 20% off? 40%? I don't know. Adidas's profile-completion email was cleaner about what the reward was worth; this one makes me wait.
- **Fifth email in a week** — The cadence is showing. June 16, 18, 20, 21, and now 22. Each one has been a slightly different flavor of "here's something we're excited about." The credibility of the early access tease is diluted by how often this inbox is getting hit.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Swap the CTA** — Change "Download the App" to "Unlock Early Access" or "Open the App." Acknowledge I'm a member who probably already has it. A deep link to the sale section in the app would be even better.
- 2. **Give me one product or one category** — Show me a Surge Short, a pace layer, anything. Even a silhouette. "Here's what's dropping" previews drive anticipation; a blank orange hero just creates FOMO with no substance.
- 3. **Add a discount anchor** — Even "up to 40% off" in the subheadline would make this email worth forwarding to someone in the running club. Right now there's nothing to share.
- 4. **Respect the cadence** — This is the 5th email since June 16. Consider whether the teaser and the launch email need to be separate sends, or if one well-loaded launch email would hit harder.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your early access window: Summer Sale is live at 6am`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Up to 40% off — tap to set your reminder before it opens to everyone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Members only. Surge, Metal Vent, and more — previewing now.`
