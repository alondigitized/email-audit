# Out now: adidas Originals by JENNIE
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-02**
## Executive summary

- K-pop star launching an Originals capsule with a tagline about "a futuristic vision of modern femininity" — cool, I'm exactly zero percent of the audience this was written for. This is JENNIE from BLACKPINK, not a runner, not a coach, not anyone with a training résumé, fronting a lifestyle collab shoot on a plain backdrop. There's no shoe on her feet in the hero, no gear I'd wear on a Saturday long run, nothing performance-adjacent at all — just a tracksuit top styled as fashion.
- This lands right after adidas's "Collaborations Archive Sale" (2026-08-27), which was the same move: no performance context, gallery-style art direction, celebrity or archive nostalgia instead of a product I'd actually train in. Now they're two-for-two on skipping running gear entirely in favor of streetwear drops. Compare that to Nike's one-time-code email (6/10) — boring as hell, but at least it was *for me* and did its one job. This one isn't even trying to be for me.
- Straight to archive, no click, no forward. Maybe I'd mention it to a friend who's into K-pop, but nobody in my running club is getting this one.

## What's working

- Clean, uncluttered layout — no render bugs, no overlapping text, image loaded fine.
- "SHOP NOW" and "FIND STORE NOW" buttons are unambiguous and high-contrast, easy to tap on mobile.
- The fine print with specific store locations (Garden State Plaza, Melrose, etc.) is a nice honest touch — no bait-and-switch, sets real expectations for in-store availability.
- Brand mark (Trefoil) and JENNIE logo are legible and on-brand visually.

## What's weak

- Zero connection to running, training, or anything performance-related — this is a fashion collab, full stop.
- No offer at all — no %, no $ off, nothing but "shop now." After adidas's "30% off End of Summer Sale" (2026-09-01) yesterday, this feels like a stylistic 180 with no payoff.
- Model, styling, and copy ("modern femininity") are aimed squarely at a different demographic than a 33-year-old male running-club captain — I don't see myself anywhere in this.
- "FUNCTIONAL GRACE" as a header is trying to borrow performance language for a lifestyle piece, and it reads hollow — Lululemon does the same trick with its tee emails and it doesn't land there either.
- No sizing, fit, or availability detail beyond "Product shown is representative" — for a "first-come, first-served" limited drop, that's thin.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment this — send it to a lifestyle/streetwear list, not a running-club subscriber. If adidas has any purchase-history data on me (running shoes, training gear), this shouldn't have hit my inbox at all.
- 2. If it must go to a broad list, add one line connecting it back to performance heritage — even "inspired by our running archives" would give me a reason to care.
- 3. Lead with urgency/scarcity copy in the body, not just buried in the legal footer — "limited drop, in stores first-come-first-served" is a real hook that's currently wasted.
- 4. Add a second CTA module for people like me — "Not your style? Shop running essentials" — so the email isn't a dead end for non-lifestyle subscribers.
