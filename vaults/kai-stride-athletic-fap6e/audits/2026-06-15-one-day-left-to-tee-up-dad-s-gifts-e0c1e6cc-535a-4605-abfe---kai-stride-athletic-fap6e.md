# One day left to tee up Dad’s gifts
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- Third Father's Day email in two weeks from lululemon, and this one at least has a ticking clock. "One day left" does actual work — unlike the June 10 Jordan/Cali Clarkson send which relied on celebrity pull without urgency, this one gives me a reason to act now. The golf framing, though, is the same sideways move they've been making all month: technically men's athletic, technically premium, but not running. As a running-club captain I'm not reaching for a polo and swing shorts for my Saturday long run. The "Shop Run Gifts" tile is in there — I saw it — but it's below the fold behind three rows of golf content I had to scroll past to find it.
- That said, the email is clean. Male models in actual athletic contexts, a credible deadline with a specific time and timezone, a clear CTA. The production quality is what I expect from lululemon. The June 3 "New, breezy gear to power hot summer runs" send — the one that actually led with Outpace the Heat hero copy — set a bar that the last three emails have been coasting on without matching.
- I'd click to the run gifts section if I were shopping for my dad. I wouldn't forward this to my running club.

## What's working

- **Deadline is specific and credible.** "Order by June 18, 11:59pm PT, get by Father's Day, June 21" with the asterisk for exclusions — this is honest urgency, not fake countdown energy. adidas's last-day voucher email did this well too; lululemon matches it here.
- **Male models, athletic settings.** Golfers on a course, not a lifestyle kitchen. The demo signals are right for a 33-year-old buying gifts for an active dad.
- **Visual hierarchy is tight.** Hero copy, product grid, CTA, shipping deadline — read order is clear. No visual clutter fighting for attention.
- **"Shop Run Gifts" section exists.** Small tile, below the fold, but it's there. For a runner, that's the only section I'd actually use.

## What's weak

- **Golf hero misses my category entirely.** I run. My dad runs. Lululemon knows this. The June 3 running email proved they can lead with performance — why bury run gifts behind three golf panels?
- **"Shop now" is lazy CTA copy.** Nike's transactional emails are generic but at least context-appropriate. "Shop golf Father's Day gifts" or "Get it by Sunday" would do more work here.
- **This is the third Father's Day send.** June 1 guide, June 10 basketball athletes, now June 15 golf. The cadence is grinding. The "one day left" subject line is doing all the heavy lifting to make this feel different from the last two.
- **No discount or explicit free shipping visible.** adidas has been swinging "up to 40% off" banners this month — lazy as that is, at least it answers the "why now besides the deadline" question. Lululemon gives me urgency but no price incentive.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with run, not golf.** Swap the hero to running gear and put golf in the secondary grid. I'm subscribed as a runner-buyer — one behavioral signal should have triggered a different hero block.
- 2. **Rewrite the CTA.** "Get it by Father's Day" or "Shop run gifts for Dad" — either one is specific enough to pull a running-community buyer.
- 3. **Add free shipping to the CTA area.** If lululemon offers it (and they usually do), make it visible adjacent to the deadline line. Right now I'm being asked to trust the deadline without a price perk.
- 4. **Rest the Father's Day cadence.** This is the third send. The next email should be a hard reset — something new from the men's performance line — not a fourth angle on the same occasion.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last chance: running gear Dad will actually use`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Order by tonight, arrives by Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Run gifts, golf gifts, and everything in between — shipping cutoff tonight at midnight PT.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `He'll log miles in this. Shop run gifts before the deadline.`
