# What’s new: mesh tennis sets
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-07**
## Executive summary

- Another lululemon, another day, another email written entirely for someone who is not me. "Gain the Ace Advantage" is a sharp headline — I'll give them that — but three seconds in I clocked that every single model is a woman in a tennis skirt and the only named athlete is Tennis Coach Madison Appel. I run. I do not play tennis. And even if I picked up a racket, I'd still be asking myself where the men's version of this set is.
- What makes this sting a little more is the July 2 send — that was the one Summer Sale email where they actually put a "Shop Men's Summer Sale" button in the hero, front and center. That was a signal they know I'm here. This email completely forgets I exist. The performance copy is genuinely good (ventilation, cooling capacity, airflow state) — this brand clearly understands the product — but it's all written to attract someone else, not me.
- I won't click. I won't forward it to anyone in my running club. I'll mark it read and move on, slightly more skeptical that lululemon's list segmentation is doing any real work.

## What's working

- **"Gain the Ace Advantage" is a strong opener.** Tennis pun, performance promise, clean headline hierarchy — the copy team knows what they're doing.
- **Performance-forward body copy.** "All-over ventilation has the cooling capacity to keep you in the zone" and "find your airflow state" read like a brand that actually tests this stuff in the heat, not just puts it on a hanger.
- **Madison Appel as named talent adds credibility.** A named Tennis Coach with a title beats a faceless model every time. Compare this to adidas's "Update Your Sneaker Roster" send from the same week — that email had no coach, no name, no story.
- **Render is clean.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum — the layout is tidy and mobile-readable.
- **Summer Sale mention at the bottom.** It's buried, but at least it's there.

## What's weak

- **Zero male demographic signals.** Every model, every product, every inch of this email is aimed at women. After the July 6 "softest sets" send pulled the same move, this is two consecutive misses on gender targeting.
- **Tennis is not running.** I'm a running-club captain. I own lululemon Surge shorts and Pace Breaker tanks. Sending me a tennis set email is like Nike sending me pickleball content — adjacent but wrong.
- **"Shop Now" is inert CTA copy.** After the specific product storytelling (named dress, named athlete, named ventilation tech), the CTA gives me nothing to latch onto. Adidas's adiClub points email at least told me exactly what I'd get for clicking.
- **Summer Sale is an afterthought.** Tacked on at the bottom with no discount percentage shown — why is the promotional hook buried behind the editorial content?
- **Daily cadence fatigue is real.** This is the fifth lululemon email in eight days. Even a brand I like starts feeling spammy at this frequency.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment this out of my inbox entirely.** If lululemon's CRM knows I've only ever bought men's running gear, this email should not land for me. Send it to women's buyers and tennis-category browsers. This is the single highest-ROI fix.
- 2. **If you're keeping me on list, add a men's block.** The July 2 Summer Sale email proved they can do it — one "Shop Men's" button in the hero. Do the same here: "Men's Ace Advantage" section, even a single product. Don't make me feel like a ghost in your database.
- 3. **Elevate the Summer Sale offer with a specific discount.** "150+ new styles added" is volume, not value. Tell me 25% off, or free shipping, or member pricing. Nike's transactional sends are spare but they always tell you what you get.
- 4. **Replace "Shop Now" with something tied to the product.** "Shop the Ace Advantage Dress" or "See Men's New Arrivals" — verb + noun + specificity.
- 5. **Dial back cadence or vary the format.** Five emails in eight days means each one competes with itself. A running-specific editorial — training recap, pace tips, a lululemon ambassador's race prep — would break the pattern and re-earn the open.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Ventilation tech that keeps you in the zone, on court or on the track`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New mesh. Summer Sale picks. Pick your pace.`
