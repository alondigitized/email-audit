# New workout styles that go beyond
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- Six, maybe seventh lululemon in a week and a half, and this one opens on a woman mid-stretch in a dark sports bra. I scrolled the whole thing in about four seconds. "Sweat-wicking fabric powers through" is honestly the most performance-adjacent copy I've seen from lululemon in this run of sends — but it's parked under a hero built entirely for women. The July 16 send at least slipped a running pun into the subject ("on its final run"), and that earned a half-second of goodwill. This one gives me "go beyond," which sounds like a manifesto for people who've never timed a mile.
- The product grid at the bottom has four or five thumbnail items and none of them read men's. No Surge short. No Metal Vent Tech. No ABC. I'm looking at tanks and tights cut for women and a "Shop What's New" footer link that could go literally anywhere. Compare that to how adidas structured the adiClub completion email — they pointed me to a specific loyalty action with a concrete reward. lululemon is pointing me at a category page with a vague verb.
- I'd mark this read, not delete — the brand is still earned trust — but I'm not clicking. And I'm definitely not forwarding it to anyone in my running club, because this email doesn't know my running club exists.

## What's working

- **Performance copy in the hero:** "When your reps heat up, sweat-wicking fabric powers through" is actual product-benefit language, not a vibe. That's rare in this recent batch.
- **Clean render, no layout breaks:** Every image loaded, text sits where it should, hierarchy is hero → feature grid → product thumbnails → footer. No visual junk.
- **Track shot in the feature grid:** There's at least one outdoor track photo in the middle section — that's the closest this email gets to runner-adjacent visual language, and it earns a nod.
- **lululemon brand trust is real:** I still open these because the brand has earned it. That carry is doing heavy lifting here.

## What's weak

- **Entire email is female-coded:** Hero model, product grid items, even the way "Summer sets in" reads (matching sets — I figured that out by image two). I am a 33-year-old man who spends real money here and I'm invisible in this send.
- **"Go beyond" is empty calories:** What does that mean? Beyond what? The July 14 French Terry email at least had a named product. This subject and header copy have no hook to hang anything on.
- **No men's product, no men's CTA:** If there's a men's equivalent of whatever this launch is, I don't see it. Nike at minimum segments by gender before sending.
- **Sixth email in ten days, same summer template:**  The "Shop new styles this week" section feels copy-pasted from the July 11 send. Cadence fatigue is real — even brand-loyal subscribers zone out by email six.
- **No offer or urgency:** The Summer Sale was running two emails ago. Now we're back to full-price "new styles" with no reason to act today.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment by gender before hitting send.** If there's men's new product in this collection, build a separate hero block or a dedicated men's send. This is table stakes — adidas nails it, even when their product is off (that metallic Samba email was at least pointed at a relevant customer).
- 2. **Name the product or the line.** "New workout styles" means nothing. "Surge 7" shorts or "Metal Vent Tech tops" is something I'd actually read. One specific anchor in the hero.
- 3. **Give me a running signal above the fold.** If lululemon has running-specific product in this drop, put a runner in the hero. Not a stretch. Not a "set." A runner.
- 4. **Add urgency or an offer.** The July 16 sale send had "Move fast" — it still felt like lululemon, but it gave me a reason to click now. "New styles" with no deadline is a catalog, not an email.
- 5. **Rest the cadence.** Six in ten days is too many. Nike sends fewer and gets more attention per open.

**Bug — invalid `letter-spacing` values on `.guest-voice-*` classes:**

```css

.guest-voice-md { letter-spacing: -2%; }
.guest-voice-sm { letter-spacing: -1%; }
```

`letter-spacing` does not accept percentage values in CSS. All three will silently fall back to `normal` letter-spacing in every email client. Intended value is likely `-0.02em` / `-0.01em`.

**Bug — `supported-color-schemes` declared inside `:root` CSS block:**

```css
:root {
  color-scheme: light dark;
  supported-color-schemes: light dark;  /* not a valid CSS property */
}
```

`supported-color-schemes` is valid only as a `<meta>` attribute (already present correctly elsewhere in the head). The `:root` declaration is a no-op and will be ignored by all CSS parsers. No functional impact, but it's dead code that suggests a copy-paste error.


Off-by-one from true black `#000000`. This is a known workaround to prevent certain email clients from treating a pure-black `#000000` as transparent, so it may be intentional — but should be documented internally to avoid future "is this a bug?" confusion.
