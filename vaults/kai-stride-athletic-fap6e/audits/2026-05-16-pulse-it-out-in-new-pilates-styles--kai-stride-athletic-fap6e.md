# Pulse it out in new Pilates styles
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- I'm a lululemon guy — the ABC pants are basically my uniform on recovery days, I do my long runs in the Surge shorts, and I've bought club kit through them. So the sender alone gets an open from me. But when I land on this, I'm right back in the same headspace as that yoga email from May 12th: "This isn't for me." The hero is a Pilates studio, all the models are women working the reformer or the mat, and the copy is literally about "finding your space" in a Pilates context. There's not a single running shoe, a split, a track, or a male model anywhere in this email.
- This is lululemon's fourth send in under a week that's landed in my inbox and the fourth where I leave feeling like they accidentally put me on the women's wellness list. The NuLü fabric story is legitimately interesting — that's the kind of performance-fabric nerd content I'd read if it were applied to something I train in. But it's wrapped around Pilates bras and cross-training fits I will never buy.
- I will not click. I will not forward. I'll scroll to the bottom and wonder, again, why lululemon's CRM has no idea I'm a runner.

## What's working

- **NuLü fabric callout is the right instinct.** Naming and explaining a proprietary fabric (stretches, breathes, soft support) is how a performance brand talks — not a fashion brand. I just wish it were attached to something I'd wear on a 16-miler.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean.** Hero image, headline, short copy, CTA, fabric story, testimonial, product grid — the scroll progression is logical and nothing fights for attention.
- **No render bugs.** Clean render, readable text, no broken images or overlapping elements. Adidas's adiClub welcome email had this same cleanliness and it made the whole experience feel premium.
- **Testimonial/quote section adds a voice.** Showing a real person's words grounds the product claim. It's a small trust signal.
- **"Shop new styles this week" grid keeps product discovery low-friction.** The thumbnail grid approach at the bottom is easy to scan.

## What's weak

- **Zero male signals.** Every model, every pose, every hero frame reads women's Pilates. I'm a 33-year-old dude who runs 40+ miles a week. Nike's recent sends at least acknowledge that men exist; this email treats my inbox like a mistake.
- **Pilates ≠ my sport.** Not just a demographic miss — a sport-category miss. lululemon sells excellent running gear for men. None of it is here.
- **"Shop now" is a throwaway CTA.** Compare to how adidas structured its adiClub welcome: clear product benefit, clear code, clear expiry. "Shop now" with no discount, no specificity, and no urgency is a dead end.
- **No offer of any kind.** The May 13th "Almost gone: your 15% off" at least had a number attached. This is a pure product-launch email with no hook beyond "new." For a lululemon subscriber who's already bought in, "new" alone doesn't move the needle.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** This is the fifth email in less than a week. High-energy hues (May 7), yoga styles (May 12), Mesh Soft Jersey (May 13), 15% expiring (May 13), now Pilates. I'm maxed out on lululemon sends, and none of them have been about running.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment men out of this send entirely.** If I've bought men's running gear, I should not be receiving a women's Pilates launch. This is table-stakes CRM hygiene. Nike and adidas both do it.
- 2. **Give me a men's Pilates or cross-training angle if you must reach me.** A "recovery and mobility" frame with male athletes doing active stretching or barre-adjacent training would at least make this feel relevant. Cross-training is real for runners.
- 3. **Add a performance-specific CTA.** "Shop men's training" or "Built for your recovery days" changes the targeting signal entirely.
- 4. **Put a real offer in.** Even a "free shipping on orders over $100" would give me a reason to click instead of archive.
- 5. **Surface the fabric story at the hero level.** NuLü as a hook — "the fabric that moves before you do" — could work in the hero headline instead of the vague "Find your space."

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Lightweight, breathable, and ready for every rep — shop NuLü now`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed bug — invalid `letter-spacing` values in `.guest-voice-*` classes:**

```css
.guest-voice-lg { letter-spacing: -2%; }   /* INVALID */
.guest-voice-md { letter-spacing: -2%; }   /* INVALID */
.guest-voice-sm { letter-spacing: -1%; }   /* INVALID */
```

`letter-spacing` does not accept percentage values per CSS spec — only `<length>` (px, em) or `normal`. These declarations will be silently dropped by all rendering engines, so the intended tight tracking on KHGiga font elements will not apply. The equivalent heading rules (`h1`/`h2`/`h3`/`h5`) correctly use `px` values and are unaffected.

**Fix:** Replace with em equivalents, e.g. `letter-spacing: -0.02em`.

**Dark mode implementation — no issues:**
- `#000001` (`.black`) and `#fdfeff` (`.white`) are intentional near-black/near-white hex tricks to prevent Gmail and Outlook from inverting true `#000000`/`#ffffff` in dark mode. Correct practice.
- Three-tier dark mode targeting (Gmail `u + .body`, Outlook.com `[class~="x_"]`, Outlook Android `\0` hack) is complete and technically sound.

**`img` reset CSS — no issues:**
```css
img {display:block; border:0; padding:0; outline:0; height:auto;}
```
Prevents image gaps in table-based layouts. Correct.
