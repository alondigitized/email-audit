# Almost gone: your 15% off
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-13**
## Executive summary

- This is a discount-reminder email, not a running email. Lululemon sent me a "your 15% is expiring" transactional notice and dressed it up with "Go go go" to make it feel urgent. It worked on a surface level — I noticed the deadline, I understand the offer — but lululemon is supposed to know me. This email treats me exactly like a stranger who forgot to use a coupon.
- The thing that stings most: the 4-step "Here's how it works" section. That's a premium brand telling me their own redemption experience is confusing enough to require instructions. For a brand I think of as the Apple of activewear, that's a UX crack I can't unsee.
- I'd click because 15% off my next long-run kit is real money and I was probably going to buy anyway. But I wouldn't forward this — there's nothing worth sharing. Mark it as a functional email I tolerate, not one I actually like.

## What's working

- **Offer is immediately legible.** "15% off ends soon." is the first thing my eye hits. No hunting required.
- **Deadline is specific and credible.** Thursday, May 14, 2026 at 11:59pm PT — I believe it, I'll act on it.
- **Subject line punches.** "Almost gone: your 15% off" is short, specific, no fluff. Opened without hesitation.
- **Layout is clean.** No broken images, no overlapping text, lululemon logo anchors brand trust immediately.
- **Exclusions are named in the body.** They don't bury the accessories/bags/We Made Too Much carveouts in 6pt footnote — it's in the main copy. Honest.

## What's weak

- **Zero running content.** Not a single product, not a shot of a runner, not a nod to the ABC pant or the Fast and Free short. I'm a guy who runs 40+ miles a week in lululemon gear and this email has no idea.
- **"Here's how it works" kills the vibe.** A 4-step process to redeem a discount is a red flag. That's a brand admitting its own checkout is broken enough to need a manual.
- **"We Made Too Much" is excluded.** That's exactly where a value-conscious runner looks for deals on older colorways. Excluding it feels like a bait-and-switch.
- **No personalization.** Not my name, not my size, not even a category nod to men's or running. Just a generic blast dressed up as a personal offer.
- **"Go go go" tries too hard.** This is lululemon, not Spirit Halloween. The brand's signature seminar-calm is what separates it from Nike hype. This copy misreads its own voice.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show me one product I'd actually buy.** Drop a hero image of the Pace Breaker short or the Surge jogger with the 15% already reflected in the price. Make the discount feel real against something tangible, not theoretical.
- 2. **Kill the "Here's how it works" section or make it one tap.** If redemption requires 4 steps, fix the UX. If it's simpler than it looks, just say "Click the button below — your discount is automatically applied."
- 3. **Personalize the exclusion warning.** Instead of listing every exclusion generically, frame it: "Works on everything in men's run — including shorts, tights, and jackets." That's a filter I trust.
- 4. **Inject one social signal.** Even "Your club members are stocking up" or a review quote from a runner. Something that makes this feel like a community moment, not a mass coupon.
- 5. **Rewrite the opening line.** "Go go go" is filler. Use that real estate to reference the season — spring race season, heat training, whatever's relevant in mid-May.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Kai, your 15% off runs out Thursday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `15% off your next run kit — 2 days left`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Works on shorts, tights, jackets — not We Made Too Much.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stack it on the Pace Breaker or Surge before midnight Thursday.`
