# Now in stock
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-12**
## Executive summary

- My gut reaction was that this is a sneaker email, and I run a running club. The top of the email promises "best sellers" and then shows a Samba-style shoe. The grid underneath is a Handball Spezial and three Pokémon Superstars. I scrolled the whole thing and found no running shoe, no Adizero, no Ultraboost and nothing for a long run. It's the fifth or sixth adidas send this week. The 11/10 "Gift iconic style with Samba" and the 11/09 Metallica email did the same thing: lifestyle, not performance.
- The one thing I didn't expect was the MLS Cup Playoffs and Apple TV block at the bottom. It's a decent loyalty hook, because redeeming adiClub points for a subscription is a real benefit. But it has nothing to do with the subject line "Now in stock," and it makes the email feel like two campaigns stitched together.
- Would I click? No. Would I forward it? Only to a friend who collects Pokémon sneakers. I'd mark it read and move on.

## What's working

- The product grid is clean. Each shoe shows a price ($110 or $180), a star rating (4.7 to 4.9) and a Prime note. That's more useful than adidas's usual all-mood, no-info layout.
- The ratings give me real social proof, which I rarely get in promo emails.
- The adiClub x Apple TV block gives my points a use. After the 11/12 "We've got something for you" raffle email, this is a more concrete redemption.
- The CTAs are clear: "SHOP NOW" and "REDEEM NOW" are black buttons, easy to see and easy to tap.

## What's weak

- There's nothing for runners. Every shoe is a terrace or novelty sneaker. A running-club captain has no reason to stop on this email.
- The hero says "best sellers," but the products are Pokémon collabs. That mismatch feels like bait-and-switch.
- "Now in stock" tells me nothing. It gives no product, no drop and no sizes. I can't tell what just landed.
- The MLS/Apple TV section is unrelated to the shoes above it and has its own headline. It splits my attention, and the hero is already ambiguous.
- The "Curated with style and freshness in mind" preheader line sits in small underlined text at the very top and reads like filler.

- 1. Segment by category. If I've bought or browsed running, lead with Adizero or Ultraboost and show sizes or stock. Nike and lululemon do segment, and lululemon's "Steady State" email finally shot men, which is why it earned a 6.
- 2. Make the subject say what's in stock. "Now in stock" is too vague to act on.
- 3. Pick one story. Either run the product drop or run the adiClub/Apple TV loyalty play, not both in one email.
- 4. Drop the "best sellers" claim, or show products that actually are best sellers.
