# Summer Sale: 200+ picks added
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- Look, I knew exactly what this was before I even opened it. Last week it was "150+ new picks added," today it's "200+ picks added" — lululemon just found-and-replaced the number and hit send. I'll give them this much: they at least kept the "Shop Men's Summer Sale" button from the July 2nd send, and the hero copy actually namedrops Zeroed In — which is more than I got on the July 6th "softest sets" email that was wall-to-wall lavender Align sets. So there's marginal improvement in the men's acknowledgment. But the hero is still a woman in an orange Define jacket, and I'm still doing that thing where I scroll halfway down just to find something that applies to me.
- If I'm comparing to what adidas did with their adiClub 50-points email — that was a palate cleanser after a week of promos — lululemon is doing the exact opposite. This is the fifth Summer Sale beat in roughly two weeks. Nike sent me exactly one email this cycle. Even at the running-club level, I'm starting to sound like a broken record to my group chat: "another lululemon sale." The drip is undermining the offer's credibility.
- I'll scroll it, click Men's, probably add nothing to cart. I won't forward this. A friend would've already seen it twice.

## What's working

- **Men's button is there, upfront.** "Shop Men's Summer Sale" in the hero, not buried three scrolls down. Small win, but real.
- **Zeroed In in the copy.** Calling out a men's bestseller by name in the subhead says someone thought about the audience split, even if it's sandwiched between Define and Align.
- **Clean render, zero layout bugs.** Orange SUMMER SALE header, hero image, product grid — everything is crisp and loads correctly on the screenshot.
- **Men's presence in the product grid.** A guy in a gray relaxed-fit top makes an appearance mid-scroll — that's one more man than the July 6th send.
- **Subject length is tight.** Thirty characters, mobile-friendly, no screaming caps or exclamation stacks.

## What's weak

- **Hero image is still a woman.** Opening frame is a woman in an orange Define jacket — first visual impression is not me.
- **"200+ picks" is a recycled beat.** July 2nd was "150+ new picks added." Same structure, incremented number. Zero creative lift — feels like a mail-merge, not a campaign.
- **No running content anywhere.** This is a performance brand and the email reads like a seasonal fashion catalog. No mention of pace, trail, long-run gear — nothing that speaks to why I pay $138 for shorts.
- **No deadline or urgency mechanism.** "Don't miss the moment" is placeholder language. How long does this sale run? When do the new colorways sell out in my size? Adidas put actual scarcity signals in their Dropset Pro email; lululemon just vibes.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** Five Summer Sale emails in roughly two weeks blunts this one's punch. What felt urgent on June 22 reads like wallpaper now.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the hero with a men's runner or a running-specific product.** One frame of a guy mid-stride in the Zeroed In Pant or Surge Short would flip my engagement immediately. It doesn't have to replace the women's hero — just rotate or split the hero by segment.
- 2. **Name a specific men's performance item with a sale price.** "Zeroed In Pant — now $89" beats "200+ picks" every time for someone like me who's comparing lululemon vs. Arc'teryx vs. Nike on a specific product.
- 3. **Add a deadline.** "Ends Sunday" or "while sizes last" forces a decision. Nike's one-time-code email did this without even trying. lululemon has the leverage and isn't using it.
- 4. **Break the subject-line pattern.** "200+ picks added" is the nth variation of the same format. A runner-targeted subject would make me stop scrolling.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Long-run gear just hit the Sale`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Men's Sale: Zeroed In, Pace Breaker, Surge — all in`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New colorways in the styles you actually train in — through Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `200+ picks added. Here's what's worth your time.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Font CDN — 403 Forbidden (5 assets)**

All five custom fonts fail with HTTP 403:

```
https://globalstaticassets.lululemon.com/fonts/Saans-Light.woff
https://globalstaticassets.lululemon.com/fonts/Saans-Regular.woff
https://globalstaticassets.lululemon.com/fonts/Saans-Medium.woff
https://globalstaticassets.lululemon.com/fonts/Saans-SemiBold.woff
https://globalstaticassets.lululemon.com/fonts/KHGiga-Light.woff
```

Impact: every client that attempts `@font-face` loads falls back to `Helvetica Neue` / `Arial` / `Times New Roman`. The `guest-voice-*` serif display classes are particularly affected.

**Tracking links — 32 skipped**

32 click-redirect URLs were not probed. No confirmed broken destination links, but no confirmed live ones either. These should be spot-checked manually or via a click-redirect resolver before deployment.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text — 3 instances**

The footer arrow icon (`SFMC_Email_Footer_Arrow-Right`) appears three times with no `alt` attribute:

```
src: https://images.lululemon.com/is/image/lululemon/SFMC_Email_Footer_Arrow-Right?$pngstandard$
```

If the image fails to load, these links render as invisible/unlabeled click targets. Fix: add `alt=""` (decorative) or a descriptive string like `alt="→"`.

**Custom font weight mismatch**

`font-weight: 380` is declared on `p`, `li`, `h1–h5`, and `a` elements, but `Saans-Regular.woff` is broken (403). Browsers that don't synthesize weight 380 will snap to 400, affecting the intended typographic weight across the full email.

**Dark mode — no issues found**

Dark mode targeting uses three correct patterns: `u + .body` Gmail hack, `[class~="x_cta-white"]` for Outlook.com, and `\0` hack for the Outlook Android app. Implementation is solid.
