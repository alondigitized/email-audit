# First chance to dive into 30% off
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-30**
## Executive summary

- After two weeks of US94 soccer nostalgia and licensed-product spam, seeing "30% off" in the subject was genuinely refreshing. Clear math, no gimmick. I opened it in under two seconds — that subject line does its job.
- Then I'm inside the email and adidas is showing me the Runfalcon and the Duramo SL. Entry-level shoes. The Runfalcon 7 is $54 before the discount — that's a $38 shoe after the code drops. I run 40-mile weeks and captain a club where people are debating Boston 12s vs Adizero SLs. These are not my shoes. Not even close. This is the same bait-and-switch I felt in the June 19 "Fresh Arrivals" email — the hook is strong, the product delivery is mismatched.
- I'm not clicking anything here. I'd maybe open the "SHOP MEN" link hoping there's hidden performance inventory behind it, but nothing in this email earns that click. I wouldn't forward it. I'd send it to someone just getting into running who needs a budget pair for the gym.

## What's working

- **"30% OFF IS LIVE" hero headline** — blunt, zero-fluff. After the vague '94 nostalgia hook from June 20, this is a welcome return to direct offer communication.
- **Promo code SAVE surfaced at the very top** — "Encode SAVE at checkout" is the first line in the email. lululemon buried their early access code below the fold in their last send; adidas wins this specific execution.
- **Star ratings next to prices** — 4.4/5 next to $54 on each product card is useful at a glance. Doesn't require a click to evaluate the product.
- **"SHOP MEN" CTA is first** — correct gender order, at least someone ran basic segmentation logic.

## What's weak

- **The products are entry-level, not performance** — Runfalcon 5, Runfalcon 7, Daily 4.2, Duramo SL 2. None of these are shoes a running club captain is recommending to anyone. If Adizero SL or Supernova Rise is in the 30% off pool, it's invisible in this email.
- **Zero performance language** — no mention of cushioning, stack height, intended distance, midsole tech. Nike at least gestures at product feel. This reads like a generic apparel sale, not a running brand.
- **The hero image reads lifestyle, not running** — the person in the hero shot looks like they're about to get coffee, not log miles. Adidas is a running brand; the email doesn't act like one.
- **No visible sale deadline** — "First chance" implies something ends, but there's no "Ends July 3" in the hero or subhead. The fine print buries the date. Nike puts the deadline right below the offer every time.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is the fifth email in 13 days (June 18, 19, 20, 27, and now June 30). The June 18 "$30 on orders $100+" send was more relevant than this one and got drowned out by the frequency.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with performance running shoes, not just budget ones** — if Adizero SL or Supernova is in the sale, lead with it. The Runfalcon audience and the running-club-captain audience are genuinely different people. Even one performance shoe in the product grid sends a completely different signal.
- 2. **Add a runner-specific sub-hook line** — between the hero and the CTAs: "From long runs to race day — 30% off the shoes built for both." Zero cost. Signals you know who I am.
- 3. **Put the deadline in the hero** — "Ends July 3" or "3 days only" in the subhead. The fine print date counts for nothing. lululemon's Summer Sale email last week had the access window right in the first fold.
- 4. **Replace the lifestyle hero with someone mid-stride** — or at minimum a shoe in a running context. Adidas is a running brand. The email should look like one.
- 5. **Slow down the cadence** — pick two sends this month that matter, let the other three go. Frequency is eroding the open rate goodwill built by cleaner subject lines like this one.

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] Plain-text fallback missing**
The text/plain MIME part is 0 characters. CAN-SPAM does not explicitly require a plain-text part, but its absence triggers aggressive spam filtering in many MTAs (SpamAssassin, Postfix-based ISP filters). It also makes the email inaccessible to text-only clients and some assistive technologies.

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header not captured**
The `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) headers were not detected. Should be verified against raw SMTP headers, not relay-captured metadata.
