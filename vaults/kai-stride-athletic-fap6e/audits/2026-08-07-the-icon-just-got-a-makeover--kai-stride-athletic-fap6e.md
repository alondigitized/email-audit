- Okay, this one's actually not bad — a metallic Samba on a lifestyle-not-catalog background, and it's a genuine lifestyle drop, not another kids'-branded mess in my inbox. I'm not going to pretend the Samba is a running shoe — it's not, it's a lifestyle sneaker adidas has been riding hard all year — but at least the email doesn't pretend otherwise either. - Then it immediately undercuts itself by pivoting straight into "THE BACK TO SCHOOL SALE" with a stock photo of three kids and a bike, which is now the fourth back-to-school email from adidas in three weeks (30% off on 7/30, the Stories edit on 7/31, and now this bolted onto a completely unrelated Samba drop). - Clean single-product hero on the Samba — no clutter, the shoe itself looks great in that blue-toned lighting.
- "SAMBA GOES METALLIC" is a real headline about a real product change, not vague hype language like "highly anticipated."
- adiClub perk (spend $130+, get a free month of Apple TV) is a genuine loyalty benefit and it's called out clearly, not buried.

## What's weak

- Zero performance or running content — no Adistar, no Ultraboost, nothing club-relevant, same gap I called out in the 7/31 "Stories" email.
- The Back to School section is a jarring tonal whiplash from a metallic-sneaker style drop straight into family backpack-and-bike stock photography — two unrelated campaigns crammed into one send.

- "Shop the latest adidas best sellers" top banner is generic filler that adds nothing before I even get to the Samba.

- 2. If adiClub actually knows I've never bought kids' sizes, suppress back-to-school content for me entirely and replace it with a second colorway or a styling angle for the Samba.
- 3. Add one line of substance runners would care about — sizing/restock info, or just an explicit "lifestyle, not performance" note so I stop having to guess.
- 4. Drop the top "best sellers" banner — it's dead weight above a much stronger hero.
