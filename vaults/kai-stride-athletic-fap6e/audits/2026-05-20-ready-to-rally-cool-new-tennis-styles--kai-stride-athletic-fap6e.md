# Ready to rally: cool, new tennis styles
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- Second tennis email in two days. Yesterday it was "New, fast-drying tennis kits are a must-get," and now it's "Ready to rally" with the same clay-court aesthetic and presumably the same inventory. I haven't picked up a racket since college intramurals. I run 50-plus miles a week, I captain a club, and lululemon is sending me Roland Garros cosplay twice in a row.
- The design is genuinely clean — warm clay tones, editorial photography, male models who look the right age and build. Alex Aleman is a real name-drop with credibility, not a hollow lifestyle placeholder. The "breathable-lite / fast-drying" copy at least gestures at performance language. But at this point lululemon has sent me yoga, mesh jersey lifestyle, Pilates, tennis, and now tennis again — all in eight days — and not a single email has touched running. I buy their Surge shorts for long runs. They have the purchase data.
- Mark as read. Definitely not forwarding to club members. If anything, I'd forward it to my one friend who actually plays USTA leagues.

## What's working

- **Clay-court photography is cohesive and warm** — the orange-red tones are intentional, seasonal, and look premium. This is lululemon's editorial quality at its best.
- **Alex Aleman name-drop lands** — "Fast-drying gear hits hard — just like Alex Aleman" is specific athlete-as-proof, which is more than adidas gave me when they sent the Mercedes-AMG F1 lifestyle drop with zero runner connection.
- **Male models match my age and build** — young athletic guy, not a fashion-week silhouette or a 50-year-old club pro. Demographic signals at least align on gender and age.
- **"breathable-lite" and "fast-drying" are real performance claims** — they're not just saying "stylish," they're saying something about the fabric. That's the right instinct even if it's in the wrong sport.
- **No render bugs** — clean layout, nothing broken, good reading hierarchy from hero to CTA.

## What's weak

- **Second tennis email in 48 hours, no variation** — yesterday's subject was "fast-drying tennis kits are a must-get," today is "cool, new tennis styles." That's essentially the same email re-skinned. Even if I played tennis, this cadence would feel like they ran out of ideas.
- **No running content anywhere in the email** — I buy Surge shorts and ABC pants from lululemon. This email does not acknowledge I exist as a customer type.
- **No concrete offer** — no discount, no free shipping, no member price. "Shop What's New" is aspirational, not persuasive. Nike at least gave me a functional one-time code that did its one job.
- **"Carry yourself with confidence" is empty brand-speak** — after a specific performance claim like "fast-drying," that headline is a step backward. It tells me nothing about the product.
- **Five emails from lululemon this week and zero running** — the cadence itself has become the message: lululemon either doesn't know I'm a runner or doesn't care.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment the list by purchase history and kill the spray-and-pray approach.** I have Surge shorts and ABC pants in my order history. Send me running content. If they need to wait until they have a running drop to say something meaningful, wait. A silent week beats a fifth irrelevant email.
- 2. **Collapse the two tennis emails into one.** If lululemon is running a full tennis campaign, own it with one strong send — athlete story, product specs, a real CTA — not two thin emails back-to-back.
- 3. **Add a performance claim or sizing detail to the hero CTA.** "Shop What's New" is dead air. Even "Shop the clay-court collection" or "Find your fit" would be more specific. "Shop Breathable-Lite Tennis" would tell me exactly what I'm clicking into.
- 4. **Give the "Carry yourself with confidence" section a product-specific headline.** What's the actual item? What's the tech? Lead with that.
- 5. **Add member pricing or a time-bound hook.** No offer, no urgency, no reason to click today versus next week.

- **Preheader Alt B:** `New styles Alex Aleman trains in — shop now`
