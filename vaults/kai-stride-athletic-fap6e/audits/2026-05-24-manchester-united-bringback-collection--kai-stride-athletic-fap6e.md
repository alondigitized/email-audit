- Seven emails in seven days from adidas. This one wants me to feel nostalgic about Manchester United's 1990–92 cup run. I was eight years old in 1992. I captain a running club in 2026. These two facts are not having a conversation.
- The copy is actually legitimately good — "This early '90s belonged to Manchester United" is a real opener. Specific season, specific kit, coherent story all the way to the CTA. ## What's working

- **Subject line is honest and tight** — 38 characters, zero bait-and-switch. You know exactly what's inside before you open.
- **Copy is genuinely good.** "This early '90s belonged to Manchester United" is a specific cultural claim, not generic lifestyle fluff. The 90/92 Away jersey, the cup run, the heritage Trefoil — the story is coherent.
- **Visual hierarchy holds.** MAN UTD / ORIGINALS banner splits cleanly, headline reads fast, SHOP NOW button sits right where the eye lands.
- **No render bugs.** Images loaded, text is legible, layout held on the render. Cleaner execution than the Nike one-time code email I saw recently, which at least had a job to do.
- **Heritage storytelling is consistent** from subject through to CTA — the email knows what it is.

## What's weak

- **Wrong category, full stop.** I'm a running club captain. Adidas has running gear I'd actually buy. None of it is here.
- **Seven emails in seven days** — and this is the second pure lifestyle/heritage drop after the F1 Mercedes-AMG capsule on May 20. They're burning list trust for product categories that have nothing to do with why I'm on this email list.
- **No offer.** No percentage off, no member early-access, no unit scarcity. "The archive is open. Own the legacy." is nice copy but it's not a buy trigger. The May 22 World Cup email had FIFA on home soil as real urgency. This one has vibes.
- **Preview text is the shop banner.** "Explore the latest adidas best sellers" is the in-email navigation strip bleeding into my inbox preview. It adds nothing to the Manchester United hook before I open. Lululemon runs better preheader discipline than this, even on their generic "what's trending" sends.
- **Zero runner signals.** No crossover product, no "built to move" bridge, no acknowledgment that some of us buy adidas for pace, not provenance.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment the list.** Run enthusiasts and soccer heritage fans have completely different buying triggers. Nike doesn't mix my Pegasus updates with FC Barcelona drops. Send this to the soccer/football interest segment and watch conversion lift.
- 2. **Add a scarcity or access mechanic.** Even "Members get first access — 500 units only" or a countdown turns "the archive is open" into a reason to act today.
- 3. **Fix the preheader.** The banner text is not preview copy. Write an actual one — something that extends the 90/92 story before the open.
- 4. **Throttle the cadence.** Seven emails in seven days is too hot. Nike sends me one targeted run email per week and I open every single one. Adidas is training me to skim and move on.
