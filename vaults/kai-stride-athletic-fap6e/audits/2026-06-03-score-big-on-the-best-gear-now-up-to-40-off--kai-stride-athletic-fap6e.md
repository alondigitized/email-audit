# Score big on the best gear, now up to 40% off.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-03**
## Executive summary

- Another adidas blast, another generic sale. I'm six emails deep into this brand's inbox blitz — World Cup jerseys, Stella McCartney, Anthony Edwards Kids, a vintage Japan sneaker — and now they're hitting me with the "Summer Kickoff Event." The offer is right there in the hero, I'll give them that. Forty percent off, clearly stated, no mystery. But there are zero products in this email. It's basically a branded announcement that a sale exists and I should go find it myself.
- The Americana flag treatment in the background is a fine summer creative choice, but it's doing nothing to tell me this is a running sale versus a sandals-and-crewneck clearance. "Score big on the best gear" could be selling football pads for all I can tell. There's not a single shoe, short, or technical fabric callout anywhere in the email. Adidas has Ultraboosts, Adizero 17s, SL20s — I need to know which of those are 40% off. Instead I get three identical-weight buttons: SHOP MEN, SHOP WOMEN, SHOP KIDS.
- I would not click. I might skim the subject in the inbox and mentally log "adidas is having a sale," but I'm not punching SHOP MEN on a blind link with no product context. Mark as read, keep moving.

## What's working

- **Offer clarity is strong.** "UP TO 40% OFF STARTS NOW" in the hero, body copy that qualifies it as "newly marked down favorites" — no bait-and-switch tension, which is more than some brands manage.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean.** Hero headline → offer statement → three CTAs, nothing fighting for attention. The dark background/bold text contrast works on mobile.
- **Subject line is tight.** 48 characters, reads clean on mobile lock screen, concrete discount number front and center — no emoji spam, no ALL CAPS shrieking.
- **Seasonal timing lands.** Early June, summer sale, flag graphics — at least the creative is current and the campaign isn't mismatched to the calendar.

## What's weak

- **Zero product imagery.** Not one shoe, short, or piece of gear in the entire email. lululemon shows product even when they get the gender split wrong; adidas is asking me to click into the void.
- **"SHOP KIDS" CTA.** This is the second time in a week they've put kids content in front of me — after that Anthony Edwards Kids colorway email I already called out — and it's still wrong segmentation. I have no kids. Every time that button appears it tells me they're not thinking about who they're mailing.
- **"Score big" is basketball language, not running language.** It reads like the World Cup FIFA email from yesterday got the copy recycled. I'm a club captain and I care about splits, not scorelines.
- **No product-level specificity on what's actually 40% off.** "Newly marked down favorites" is meaningless. Is it Ultraboost? Running apparel? A soccer cleat clearance? The vagueness makes me suspicious the 40% is on lifestyle gear I don't want.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** This is email number six-plus in roughly ten days. The June 2 voucher send scored a 7 from me because it felt like *my account, my code*. This one has completely forgotten that moment exists.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show one hero product.** Drop a marquee performance runner — Adizero SL2, Boston 12 equivalent, whatever's in the sale — full bleed, with price before and after. I need to know the 40% off is on something I want, not last season's Cloudfoam.
- 2. **Segment KIDS out.** Remove the SHOP KIDS CTA entirely for a male running segment. Put a second MEN option in its place or drop it to two buttons. Every kid-facing CTA is wasted real estate that signals poor list hygiene.
- 3. **Pull in adiClub.** The June 2 voucher email showed adidas knows how to personalize. Extend that: "Your running gear, now up to 40% off" with even a nod to adiClub loyalty adds friction back to deleting.
- 4. **Add a deadline.** "STARTS NOW" with no end date kills urgency. Even "Through June 8" would make me reconsider closing the tab.
- 5. **Swap "Score big" for running-coded language.** "Your next PR just got cheaper" or literally anything with split/pace/miles vocabulary tells me this is for a runner.
- **Subject Alt A:** `40% off running gear — starts today`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your summer miles just got cheaper`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Adizero, Ultraboost, and more — newly marked down through June 8`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The sale your training budget's been waiting for`

### 1. Technical Summary

Two hard failures: a broken redirect destination and a completely absent plain-text MIME part. Six HTTP (non-HTTPS) image refs for the same asset will trigger image-blocking in modern clients, and missing authentication-header capture leaves deliverability status unverifiable.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken redirect destination**
The click-tracking URL resolves to a relative path rather than an absolute URL:

```
Decoded destination: /g/ad4237a9-c327-4aca-8f76-c1da5036c5e2?txn=169fef80-5f87-11f1-b14d-0690aac23d16
Tracking wrapper:    https://dv.adidas.com/o/ad4237a9-c327-4aca-8f76-c1da5036c5e2?cp_tp=v3.eJzj...
```

The redirect target starts with `/g/` with no host — this will 404 or produce an unresolvable URL depending on how the click server handles relative paths. Any recipient clicking this link lands nowhere.

All 28 click-redirect links were skipped during HTTP probing because they pass through `click.comms.adidas.com`. Destination health for the remaining links is unconfirmed.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] Plain-text MIME part absent**
```
Text version: 0 chars
```
CAN-SPAM and many ISP filtering heuristics expect a `text/plain` alternative part. A multipart/alternative email with a 0-byte text body will trigger spam filters and fails accessibility for text-only mail agents.

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header not captured**
Neither `List-Unsubscribe` nor `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058 one-click) headers were detected at the relay layer. These are required for Gmail's one-click unsubscribe UI and for bulk-sender compliance under Google/Yahoo's February 2024 requirements. The relay may be stripping them — needs verification at the raw SMTP level.

**[WARN] Authentication-Results not captured**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unverifiable via the AgentMail relay. Sending domain is `us-news.comms.adidas.com`; authentication posture for this subdomain should be confirmed directly against the receiving MTA headers.

`ROBOTS: NOINDEX, NOFOLLOW` and `referrer: no-referrer` meta tags are present and appropriate for email HTML.
