# Get a $50 gift card for $40
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-15**
## Executive summary

- A gift card upsell — that's a first from adidas in my inbox, and after last week's Real Madrid jersey, the metallic Samba, the Chinese New Year jacket in August, and the Care Bears sneaker, at least this one isn't another random drop. But it's also completely generic: no running shoe, no gear, no product at all, just a fabric-swatch hero and a straight-up "give us $40, we'll credit you $50 you can only spend on more adidas." That's a decent math trick for someone already planning a big purchase, but there's nothing here for a guy trying to figure out if the next Adizero is worth the money.
- I'm not clicking this. It's not for me right now — I don't have anything specific queued up I'd spend a gift card balance on, and the email gives me zero reason to go browse. It's the kind of email I skim in three seconds and archive. Not forwarding it to the run club either; nobody's hyped about a store-credit discount.

## What's working

- The offer itself is dead simple and honestly stated — $50 for $40, clear math, no games.
- Fine print is upfront about the dates (Aug 14–28) and limits (4 per order, no stacking) instead of burying it in a footnote nobody can find.
- One clear black "SHOP NOW" button, easy to tap, no ambiguity about what to do next.

## What's weak

- Zero product in the hero — just draped jersey fabric. Compare that to last week's Samba send, which at least showed the shoe on a real lifestyle background; this is more abstract than that.
- No sport-specific anything — no running shoe, no performance callout, nothing that tells me adidas knows I'm a runner and not a fashion browser. Nike's one-time-code email last week at least felt like it was talking to *me specifically* as a member; this feels like it's talking to nobody.
- A gift card discount isn't a purchase incentive on its own — I need a reason to spend $40 today, and there isn't one here.
- "SAVE ON SEASONAL STAPLES" is vague filler copy that doesn't tell me what's actually on sale.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pair the gift card offer with an actual product tie-in — "buy the card, get X% off your next running shoe purchase" would give me a reason to act now instead of banking value for later.
- 2. Swap the fabric-texture hero for an actual runner or running shoe — even a small product shot would signal this isn't purely a lifestyle/fashion play.
- 3. Segment this — if adidas knows I've bought performance running gear before, tell me that explicitly ("stock up before your next Adizero drop") instead of the generic "fresh looks for end of summer."
- 4. Tighten the subject — it's decent as-is but could be sharper on the value.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Load up before fall: $50 gift card, $40 today`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stock your card before your next run — limited time.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `4 cards per order. Ends August 28.`
