# What’s new: breezy LuluLinen™
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-09**
## Executive summary

- I'm going to be straight: I opened this, clocked "LuluLinen™" in the subject, and immediately knew I was about to scroll through wide-leg linen trousers and flowy lifestyle content — and I was right. The hero copy "Feel the refresh of LuluLinen™" is polished, the photography is clean, and I do not care. Not one image in this email features someone running. Not one callout mentions pace, sweat-wicking, or even a vague nod to movement. This is lululemon the fashion brand, not lululemon the company that made my ABC Joggers.
- What makes this sting a little is the June 3 send — "New, breezy gear to power hot summer runs" — which actually used the same "breezy" seasonal hook but pointed it directly at performance. That email earned the click. This one uses nearly identical atmospheric language and then walks me straight into a linen wide-leg pants catalogue. The pivot feels lazy, like they grabbed the summer-vibes template and swapped the product without updating the targeting.
- I wouldn't click, wouldn't forward to anyone in my run club, and I'd mark-as-read in about three seconds. If someone in my club asked "did you see the new lululemon drop?" and this was what they meant, I'd tell them to check the ABC Shorts restock from May 30 instead.

## What's working

- **Hero copy is clean.** "Feel the refresh of LuluLinen™" is economical and seasonally on-brand — lululemon's copywriters are consistently better than Nike's right now, whose "here's your one-time code" transactional emails have zero brand voice.
- **Visual hierarchy holds up.** Logo → headline → product shot → sub-copy lands in the right order; nothing fights for dominance.
- **No render bugs.** Email displays cleanly, images load, no overlapping text — adidas's World Cup blasts earlier this month had banner cropping issues; this doesn't.
- **Seasonal relevance.** "Breeze, now circulating." is the kind of confident, slightly-poetic copy lululemon does well when they lean into it.
- **The grid section works as a browse layer.** The lower product grid gives me a sense of range even if the range isn't for me.

## What's weak

- **Zero performance signal, zero running signal.** This is entirely lifestyle/casual. My running club has zero use for linen pants on a Tuesday 10-miler. adidas at least segments their sale blasts by sport; lululemon sent me their interior-design-influencer collection.
- **All female models, no gender variation.** Given lululemon knows I'm a male subscriber — I'm buying men's shorts — this is a missed targeting call. The June 1 Father's Day send got it right. This undoes that progress.
- **No offer or incentive.** "Shop new styles this week" is not an offer. Nike's voucher email this month had a personalized code; this has nothing to anchor a click.
- **Cadence fatigue is building.** Six emails this month. The June 2 Nulu™ fabric send already started drifting me back toward passive engagement; this accelerates it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment men away from this send entirely.** LuluLinen™ women's launch should not land in the inbox of a male ABC-Shorts buyer. The June 1 Father's Day email proves the segmentation capability exists — use it.
- 2. **If you must send to men, find the men's linen piece.** If there's a men's LuluLinen shirt or short in the line, lead with it and build the email around that — don't show me women's wide-legs and hope I buy something for someone.
- 3. **Anchor the send with a product detail runners care about.** Even one callout like "breathable enough for post-run errands" ties lifestyle to my actual behavior.
- 4. **Add a concrete incentive.** "New" is not a reason to click. A first-access window, a member-only early-drop, anything.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Invalid CSS unit — `letter-spacing` with `%`:**
```css
/* .guest-voice-lg, .guest-voice-md */
letter-spacing: -2%;
/* .guest-voice-sm */
letter-spacing: -1%;
```
Percentage is not a valid unit for `letter-spacing` in CSS. The property accepts `<length>` (px, em, rem) or `normal`. Browsers and email clients will ignore these declarations and fall back to default letter-spacing, breaking the intended typographic compression. Should be `-0.02em` / `-0.01em`.

**Non-standard `font-weight` values:**
`font-weight: 380` and `font-weight: 570` appear throughout (e.g., `.eyebrow`, `h1–h5`, `p`). These are valid only for variable fonts; if the custom fonts (`Saans-Regular`, `Saans-Semibold`, `KHGiga-Light`) fail to load, user agents will round to the nearest standard weight (400 or 500), which may produce visible weight shifts in fallback rendering.

**Custom font declarations without visible `@font-face`:**
Five custom families are referenced (`Saans-Regular`, `Saans-Light`, `Saans-Medium`, `Saans-Semibold`, `KHGiga-Light`) but no `@font-face` rules appear in the visible CSS. If loaded elsewhere in the `<head>` (truncated), confirm each src URL is HTTPS and the host allows cross-origin font loading. If absent, all text falls back to `Helvetica Neue` / `Arial` / `Times New Roman`.

**Dark mode — `h4` excluded from commented-out override:**
