# Welcome Penn State
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-30**
## Executive summary

- Penn State just signed with adidas — okay, that's actually a real story, and college sports jerseys are a whole different lane than the Samba pile-up I've been getting for two weeks. But "Nittany Lions apparel" is fan gear, not run gear, and I don't have a single connection to Penn State. This is a collegiate licensing push, not a running email, and adidas didn't bother to check whether that's remotely relevant to me before sending it.
- The three people in the stands wearing throwback jerseys look like a solid shoot for whoever's actually a PSU alum, but for me it's just three strangers in navy and white cosplay. There's no run product, no pace, no gear — just "repping the basic blues you know and love," which, no, I don't.
- I'm not opening a second tab on this one. Not a click, not a forward — unless I've got a buddy who went to Penn State, and even then this feels more like a screenshot-and-mock than a genuine "hey check this out."

## What's working

- The hero photo is clean, no render bugs, no broken images — technically a well-built send.
- "SHOP NOW" appears twice with a clear black button and arrow, so the CTA is unambiguous.
- The blue/white/black palette is consistent and on-brand for adidas.
- If you're actually a Penn State fan, "the newest chapter of Penn State excellence" is a real, timely hook — new licensing deals don't happen every day.

## What's weak

- Zero running relevance — no shoes, no performance fabric, no club angle, nothing that says adidas knows I'm a runner and not a football tailgater.
- The subject "Welcome Penn State" reads like it's addressed to the university, not to me — I felt like I opened someone else's email.
- No offer at all. No %, no BOGO, no free shipping — just "shop the collection," which after two weeks of adidas emails is starting to feel like they don't have anything real to say.
- Compare this to Nike's one-time-code email — that one had a specific, personal reason to open it. This has none.
- Compare to adidas's own adiClub points email from last week — at least that felt like it was talking to my account. This feels like a mass blast to anyone within 50 miles of State College.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment this — collegiate licensing drops should go to fans/alumni segments, not blanket-blasted to people who signed up for running gear. That's the single biggest fix.
- 2. If adidas insists on sending this to me anyway, at least pair it with something relevant — a "new to the roster, here's what's new in running" cross-sell instead of pure fan apparel.
- 3. Add an actual offer. "Shop the collection" with no price incentive isn't enough to get a click from someone outside the target fanbase.
- 4. Show product, not just lifestyle photography — I want to see the jersey, the price, sizing, not three people sitting in bleachers.
