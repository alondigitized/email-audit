# Fall is here and so are up to 40% off savings
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-10**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "FALL KICKOFF SALE" in that pink ribbon hero is at least a clean seasonal visual — better than another kid in a gym, I'll give it that. But then I hit the product grid and it's Cloudfoam Slounge, Gazelle, Lite Racer, Cuxxion Sock — four lifestyle/casual shoes, zero running shoes, and weirdly there are "Prime options available" badges under every product like this got mixed up with an Amazon listing feed. I run a weekly long-run club and there's not a single trainer, not an Ultraboost, not an Adizero in sight.
- This is 40% off stuff I have no use for. Compare that to the Dropset Pro/Polar Grit hybrid kit email from 2026-09-05 — that one at least had gear I'd actually consider. This one's just a discount dump on casual shoes with three generic "SHOP MEN/WOMEN/KIDS" buttons up top, same pattern as the 09-09 sale email. Not opening a second look, not forwarding, marking as read.

## What's working

- The pink ribbon "FALL KICKOFF SALE" hero is genuinely seasonal and visually clean, no kids-in-a-gym cliché this time.
- "UP TO 40% OFF" and "no code needed" is a clear, honest offer stated plainly.
- Prices, original price, and % off are all visible per product — no hidden math.
- Star ratings (4.6-4.7) give a little social proof I don't usually see from adidas.

## What's weak

- Every single product is a lifestyle/casual shoe — Cloudfoam Slounge, Gazelle, Lite Racer, Cuxxion Sock. None of these are running shoes.
- "Prime options available" tags look like they leaked in from an Amazon feed — feels like a mismatched template, not a real adidas.com send.
- Three generic "SHOP MEN/WOMEN/KIDS" buttons dump me into the whole catalog instead of a running category.
- No mention of running, training, or performance anywhere — this is a fashion-shopper email wearing an athletic brand's logo.

## Recommendations

- 1. Swap at least two of the four hero products for actual running shoes — Adizero, Ultraboost, Supernova — if this is going to a subscriber who's shown interest in running gear.
- 2. Replace the generic MEN/WOMEN/KIDS buttons with a "SHOP RUNNING" CTA that segments by activity, not just demographic.
- 3. Drop or explain the "Prime options available" tags — they read as a template error and erode trust that this is really from adidas.
- 4. Add one line of performance-specific proof (cushioning tech, weight, drop) on at least one product to signal this brand knows runners vs. fashion-shoppers.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Fall Kickoff: 40% off select performance shoes`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Restock your rotation before your next long run.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New markdowns on select training and running styles.`

- 16 tracking/click-redirect links (`click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/...`) were skipped from HTTP probing per standard practice for redirect domains — no broken-link status confirmed either way; flagged as unverified, not failed.
