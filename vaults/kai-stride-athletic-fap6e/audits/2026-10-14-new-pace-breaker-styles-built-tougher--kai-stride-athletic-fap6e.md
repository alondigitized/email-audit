# New Pace Breaker styles, built tougher
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-14**
## Executive summary

- This is the first lululemon email in a while that looks like it was made for a runner. The hero is a man mid-stride at dusk, with dreadlocks and a jacket, shot from the side. He's running, not posing against a pastel wall. After the Steady State stripes email on 10/13, which gave me nothing, this is a real step toward my world.
- The copy undercuts it. "Pace Breaker's new strength" and "Retro design lines meet our hard-wearing fabric to keep you going, whatever's ahead" are the seminar voice I keep pointing at. There's no price, no weight, no distance, and no mention of what water-resistant means in a drizzle at mile 8. Nike would at least hype a launch. lululemon gives me a mood board of shorts and a jacket and expects me to infer the rest. I'd probably click through to look at the shorts, but I wouldn't forward it. It's a decent read, not a great one.

## What's working

- The hero is a runner in motion on a bridge at golden hour. It reads as running, not lounging, which is what I've been asking lululemon for since 9/29's Smooth Spacer email.
- The models look like my club: a man in his 30s, lean, in a running kit. It's a big contrast with the ballet-studio emails from 10/07 and 9/22.
- The "Set for your next run" grid has real run context: yellow shoes, a crouched stretch, and shorts with a jacket layered over the top.
- The product grid at the bottom is clean and easy to scan, with clear "Shop What's New" and "See all" pill buttons.
- The Glyde fabric callout, water-resistant and "just got tougher", gives me one concrete product claim to hold onto.

## What's weak

- There's no price, no length options, and no weight. I'm buying for the long run, and I can't tell what a Pace Breaker short costs or what it does.
- "Shop What's New" is a generic CTA, and it appears twice. The hero is about Pace Breaker, so the button should say "Shop Pace Breaker." The only direct link is a tiny text link under the jacket photo.
- The copy is vague. "Hard-wearing fabric" and "built tougher" say nothing about reflective details, pockets, or liner length. I can't tell whether this is a run short or a fashion short.
- The final product grid is mostly hoodies, quarter-zips, and joggers, none of them running. It dilutes the run story, which is the same drift I saw on 10/13.
- It's very long, about six scrolls, and the actual Pace Breaker content ends before the halfway point. The tiny footer text is hard to read.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the CTA specific and put it up top. Use "Shop Pace Breaker Shorts" and "Shop the Jacket," with a price and a "from $X" line.
- 2. Add one run-specific proof line, such as "Tested on 20 mi long runs: 7" inseam, zip pocket, reflective trim." That would beat the "retro design lines" copy.
- 3. Cut the bottom grid or restrict it to running pieces. Adidas and Nike keep launch emails focused on one product family.
- 4. Add a club or community hook, like a run-club tie-in or a local store group run. That's what a captain would forward.
