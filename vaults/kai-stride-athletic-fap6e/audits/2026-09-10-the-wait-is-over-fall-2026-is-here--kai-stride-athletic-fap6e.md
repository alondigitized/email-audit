# The wait is over: fall 2026 is here
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-10**
## Executive summary

- Fall drop email, and it's the most "seasonal collection" lululemon's sent me in weeks — actual new pieces, actual campaign shoot, actual "next drop: October 6" date stamped at the bottom. That's more concrete than the Megan Roup edit (2026-09-08) or the tee/legging catalog dumps. But it's still lululemon doing what lululemon does: gorgeous editorial photography, zero actual product info. I see a guy in a track jacket, a woman mid-pose on a yoga mat, another guy against a brick wall doing a pull-up — and I genuinely could not tell you what fabric, what price, or what's actually new versus what's just the same core lineup restyled for fall.
- There is one William Dandjinou credit — "500m Indoor Track Silver Medalist" — which is the first real athlete callout I've seen from this brand in this stretch of sends, and it's buried under a moody black-and-white shot with no stats, no PR, nothing. Compare that to Nike's one-time-code email (6/10) which at least had a job to do and did it fast — this has ambition (multiple athlete quotes, a full "shop the fall lineup" grid) but drowns it all in the same soft-focus lifestyle mush.
- I'd scroll through it once for the photos, not click, not forward. If a training partner asked "is lululemon dropping anything new," I'd say "yeah, October 6, apparently" and move on.

## What's working

- The "next drop: october 6" footer banner is the first real urgency/date signal I've seen from this brand all week — that's something I'd actually calendar.
- William Dandjinou's athlete credit is a genuine performance signal, even underused — a step up from lifestyle-only sends like the tracksuit email (09-10, 4/10).
- The "Shop the fall 2026 lineup" grid at least shows six different looks side by side instead of one hero shot repeated — gives me a sense of range.
- Clean single-CTA-per-section structure (Shop Fall 2026, Shop Yoga, Shop Workout, Shop Now) — no clutter, easy to scan on a phone.

## What's weak

- Not one price, discount, or "new fabric" callout anywhere — after last week's Polartec® email (09-06, 4/10) actually named a material, this regresses back to pure mood-board copy.
- "Moving in lululemon feels like becoming me" and "the gear feels weightless, like it's just my body working" — customer quotes with zero specifics. No mention of sizing, availability, or even what garment she's wearing.
- The hero banner text "fall 2026 / a new baseline" is style-over-substance — I still don't know if this is running gear or just city-lifestyle pieces until I scroll deep into the grid.
- Nothing club- or community-facing — no group run tie-in, no team/relay angle, despite having an actual track athlete in the email.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put William Dandjinou's stat and a real product spec (fabric, weight, price) directly under his photo — that's the one credible performance hook in the whole email and it's wasted as a caption.
- 2. Add price or member-pricing under at least the "Shop Now" grid tiles — six product looks with zero prices is a hard pass for someone deciding whether this is a $70 tee or a $250 jacket.
- 3. Move "next drop: october 6" up near the top, not buried at the very bottom after the shop grid — that's the actual news in this email.
- 4. Cut one of the three lifestyle quote/photo blocks (Estefania Lopez / Dandjinou repeat the same "feels like X" formula) — tighten to one strong athlete story with real numbers instead of two vague ones.
