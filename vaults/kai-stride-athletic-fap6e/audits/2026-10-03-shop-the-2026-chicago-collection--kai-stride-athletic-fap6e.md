# Shop the 2026 Chicago collection
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-03**
## Executive summary

- Finally, a running email. After the cow-print Samba on 10/2 and the Pikachu drop on 9/30, adidas sent me something I'd actually open on a Saturday. Two runners on the Chicago lakefront in Adizero kit, with the "Adios Pro 5. Built for Chicago." line sitting in the preheader. That reads like a brand that knows the marathon is next weekend.
- It stops short, though. The body copy is "Run to break records with elite innovation." That's a generic hype line. I want to know what's different about the Pro 5. I'd like a stack height, a weight, a price, and whether my size is in stock. I don't get any of that. There's one SHOP NOW button, and a second SHOP NOW sits in a black bar that says "best sellers," which dilutes the Chicago message. Nike would have given me a race-day story, and Lululemon would at least have a calm line about the long run. adidas gets the right subject and then gives me almost nothing under it. I'd click once to see the shoe. I wouldn't forward it.

## What's working

- The hero photo is real running. Two people in motion by the lake, in Adizero kit. It doesn't look like a fashion shoot in running gear.
- "2026 Chicago Collection" in big plain type is clear. I know what the email is about in one second.
- The preheader line "ADIZERO Adios Pro 5. Built for Chicago." is concrete and on-category. That's more than adidas gave me with Pikachu.
- Clean, uncluttered layout with a high-contrast black CTA. The tap target is big enough for a thumb.

## What's weak

- The "Explore the latest adidas best sellers" banner at the top fights the hero and sends me to a different place than Chicago.
- The copy is hype with no facts. "Elite innovation" tells me nothing about the shoe's foam, plate, weight, or price.
- No product shot, price, or sizing. The hero is lifestyle only, so I can't see the shoe on the page.
- No race-week urgency or loyalty hook. I'm an adiClub member and nothing acknowledges that.
- The adidas logo is tiny, and the preheader renders as a plain underlined link at the top. It looks unfinished.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the best-sellers bar and make Chicago the only message. One email, one job.
- 2. Put the actual shoe in the hero or right under it, with the price, weight, and "sizes 7–13 in stock." Show me the product, not just the vibe.
- 3. Replace "elite innovation" with a real claim: the plate, the foam, and what it did for last year's Chicago finishers.
- 4. Add a race-week deadline, such as "ships in time for Oct 11," and a line for adiClub members.
- 5. Add a second link for the apparel and a "find a run-club shakeout" link, to speak to the community.
