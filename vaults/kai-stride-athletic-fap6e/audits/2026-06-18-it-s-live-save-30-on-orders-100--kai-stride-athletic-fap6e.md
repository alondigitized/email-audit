# It's live: save $30 on orders $100+
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-18**
## Executive summary

- Opened it immediately — "$30 on orders $100+" in the subject is the kind of math I can do in my head in under a second. That's actually the strongest thing this email does. But then I'm inside the email and it's... a white rectangle with two sentences and three buttons. No shot of the Ultraboost 25 I've been watching. No running anything. "SPEND LESS GET MORE" could be the headline for a Kohl's mailer.
- What makes it worse is I've seen this movie before. Three weeks ago adidas hit me with "Score big on the best gear, now up to 40% off," then another wave of "Summer Kickoff: up to 40% off," and now this. The discount offer is getting incrementally more specific (dollar amount vs. percentage, which is a genuine improvement), but the creative is getting lazier. No imagery. No product. Just a promo code — and that code is "DEAL." That's the energy of someone who ran out of time on Friday afternoon.
- I'd click SHOP MEN because $30 is $30 and I have a race vest I've been thinking about. But I would not forward this to anyone in my running club. This is a personal impulse purchase if I happen to need something today, not a "hey, check this out" email.

## What's working

- **The offer is instantly legible.** "$30 on orders $100+" clears the bar Nike set last week, where the transactional code email told me nothing about value until I was already logged in.
- **Subject line "It's live" signals freshness.** It's not much, but compared to the "Summer Kickoff" language that adidas has been recycling since early June, "it's live" at least implies this is a new thing starting now.
- **No visual clutter.** The email renders cleanly — no overlapping elements, no broken images, no lorem ipsum leaking through. Compared to some of the FIFA jersey blasts earlier this month, this is technically well-executed.
- **Dollar amount beats percentage.** "$30 off $100" is more psychologically concrete than "up to 40% off" — I know exactly what I'm getting.

## What's weak

- **Zero running DNA anywhere.** No Ultraboost, no Adizero, no trail shoe, not even a gesture toward performance. The three CTAs — SHOP MEN, SHOP WOMEN, SHOP KIDS — are so generic they could belong to any category email adidas sends. This is the same creative DNA as the Stella McCartney email I got two weeks ago, just with different button labels.
- **"DEAL" as the promo code.** This is a small thing that reads loud: a generic, obviously auto-generated code signals mass broadcast. lululemon, when they do member exclusives, at least makes the code feel like it was meant for you (MEMBER, SURGE, SWEAT — something that implies segmentation).
- **Ultraboost exclusions buried in tiny fine print.** The one shoe I'd actually buy with this offer is "select Ultraboost," which is in the exclusions list at the bottom in 8-point type. That's the kind of thing that makes me feel like I got played after I've already loaded my cart.
- **Offer deadline exists only in the fine print.** There's no urgency in the body copy — no "ends Sunday," no countdown. The subject says "it's live" but doesn't say when it dies.
- **Fourth discount blast in three weeks.** At this point, adidas has trained me to never pay full price. That's not great brand equity.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put one running product in the hero.** Even a single image of the Ultraboost 25 or the Adizero SL2 changes the entire signal of this email — it says "this is for a runner" instead of "this is for everyone in our list."
- 2. **Add a deadline to the body copy**, not just fine print. "Ends Sunday, June 22" right below the offer would create real urgency without feeling spammy.
- 3. **Segment the CTA for running specifically.** "SHOP RUNNING MEN" instead of "SHOP MEN" — takes 10 seconds to change and signals to me that someone thought about why I'm on this list.
- 4. **Surface the exclusion prominently** — or remove Ultraboost from it. Burying it rewards nobody; running customers will feel misled the moment they hit checkout.
- 5. **Upgrade the code.** "STRIDE30" or "RUN30" over "DEAL" — zero engineering lift, meaningfully better targeting signal.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Kai, run in something new — $30 off your next order`
- **Subject Alt B:** `$30 off ends Sunday — stock up for your next long run`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Use code RUN30 at checkout on orders $100+. Ends June 22.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your next long-run gear just got $30 cheaper. Grab it before Sunday.`

## Technical Audit — adidas "Save $30 on orders $100+" Email

### 1. Technical Summary
The email has one confirmed broken click-redirect and a systematic HTTP/HTTPS downgrade issue affecting a repeated image asset. Compliance headers are unconfirmed at the relay layer, and the absence of a plain-text part is a deliverability risk.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken click redirect**
The QA checker resolved one tracking URL to an unroutable internal path:
```
dv.adidas.com/o/1c9d9603-5323-4bf8-b044-a0629eb7ddc9?cp_tp=v3.eJzj...
  → resolves to: /g/1c9d9603-5323-4bf8-b044-a0629eb7ddc9?txn=2d97b480-70e3-11f1-ba85-ae32f7ad8416
```
The redirect target is a relative path with no hostname — `unknown url type`. This is either a misconfigured redirect mapping in the ESP's link table or a missing `https://www.adidas.com` prefix on the destination. If this maps to a CTA button, it is a broken user journey.

**[INFO] 29 tracking links skipped**
All remaining click-redirect URLs (`dv.adidas.com`, `click.comms.adidas.com`) were skipped by HTTP probe. Destination URLs and UTM parameter carriage cannot be confirmed from this audit.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] HTTP image sources — 6 instances of the same asset**
The arrow/chevron image is served over plain HTTP in every instance:
```
src: http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62047e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```
Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail block mixed-content images by default. All six instances will fail to render in those clients. The asset needs an HTTPS URL or should be re-hosted.

**[WARN] HTTP tracking pixel**
```
src: http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019edcc1eeef-...
```
Open-tracking pixel also served over HTTP. Same blocking risk; additionally flagged as missing alt text (expected for pixels, but the HTTP issue stands).

**[WARN] Missing alt text — 2 images**
- Primary content image: `dv.adidas.com/o/1c9d9603-5323-4bf8-b044-a0629eb7ddc9` — no `alt` attribute. If images are blocked, this renders as a blank block with no context.
- Tracking pixel: `click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/...` — no `alt=""` (empty string should be set explicitly on decorative/tracking images).

**[INFO] Custom fonts use `local()` only**
`AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` `@font-face` declarations reference only `local()` sources — no remote `url()`. Font rendering is entirely dependent on the client machine having those fonts installed, which is near-zero probability in consumer email. Fallback to Arial/Helvetica is correct behavior, but this means the `@font-face` blocks serve no functional purpose and add CSS weight.

**[INFO] `no-referrer` meta tag**
```html
<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">
```
Suppresses the HTTP Referer header from the email web-view. For users who open the browser version and click through, this strips referrer context at the HTTP layer. UTM params in URLs remain unaffected, but any server-side referrer-based routing or analytics will see `(direct)`.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header unconfirmed**
The QA relay did not capture a `List-Unsubscribe` header. This header is required for Gmail bulk-sender compliance (Google's February 2024 enforcement) and is strongly expected from a sender at adidas's volume. Likely present at the MTA but not captured by the AgentMail relay — should be verified against raw headers received at the inbox.

**[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post missing (RFC 8058)**
One-click unsubscribe (`List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`) not confirmed. Gmail and Yahoo require this for bulk senders. Same caveat: may exist at MTA level but not relayed.

**[WARN] Authentication-Results unconfirmed**
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status unknown via this relay. Sender domain `us-news.comms.adidas.com` should be covered by adidas's DMARC policy — cannot confirm from available data.

**[INFO] `ROBOTS: NOINDEX, NOFOLLOW`** — correct; prevents search indexing of the email web version.

**[FAIL] Plain-text fallback: 0 characters**
No `text/plain` MIME part. Aggressive spam filters (SpamAssassin, Proofpoint) score against HTML-only messages. CAN-SPAM does not technically mandate a text part, but its absence is a deliverability negative and reduces accessibility for plain-text clients.
