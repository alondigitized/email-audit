# Unlock the magic with adidas | Care Bears
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-27**
## Executive summary

- Care Bears again. That's twice from adidas in four days — I flagged the Superstar Care Bears collab on the 24th and now they've circled back with running shoes wearing the same pastel bear graphics. My running club would clown me mercilessly if I showed up to Sunday's long run in a baby-blue Adistar with a Care Bear sticker on the tongue. This isn't a running email, it's a nostalgia-merch email wearing a running shoe as a mascot.
- The one thing that stops this from being a total waste of my scroll: the Adistar Control 5 is a real stability trainer, not a lifestyle silhouette, so there's a thread of actual running product buried in here. But the execution — yellow star patches, cartoon bear on the hero banner, a purple fuzzy bucket hat — tells me adidas's email system doesn't know I'm a 4.9-star-review, weekly-long-run kind of buyer. It knows I clicked on running shoes once and now I'm getting bears.
- Not clicking, not forwarding — unless it's to the club group chat as a joke about adidas losing the plot.

## What's working

- The top black banner ("Explore the latest adidas best sellers") is a clean, honest CTA if I want to skip the theme entirely — smart escape hatch.
- Adistar Control 5 is a legitimate stability running shoe, and the 4.9-star rating with visible review count builds real trust.
- Layout is clean, no rendering bugs, price and rating sit right under each product — easy to scan on mobile.
- "Prime options available" is a nice concrete logistics signal for actual purchase intent.

## What's weak

- Care Bears, again — this is now back-to-back kids'-nostalgia sends after the 9/22 Superstar collab, and it reads like adidas doesn't segment me as a runner at all.
- Half the grid is a kids' backpack and a fuzzy bucket hat — zero relevance to my long run.
- No pace, mileage, cushioning stack height, drop, or any actual performance spec — just stars stuck on the shoe like stickers.
- No club, crew, or community angle at all, which is exactly the hook that would've gotten me to open this.
- Compare this to the 9/23 "race-day supershoe" email on the Adizero Adios Pro 5 — that one spoke my language. This one speaks toddler.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split the send — Care Bears goes to the lifestyle/family segment, and separately give me an Adistar Control 5 email built around stability, mileage, and club use, no bear graphics anywhere near it.
- 2. Add real performance copy under the shoe: drop height, cushioning type, what run type it's built for (easy days vs. tempo).
- 3. Cut the kids' backpack/hat from a grid that's ostensibly about "unlocking magic" for a shoe I might actually buy — they're diluting the one real product.
- 4. Pull in a community hook — "brought this pair to my Saturday long run" type UGC — the way lululemon's golf send used real athletes instead of just product shots.
