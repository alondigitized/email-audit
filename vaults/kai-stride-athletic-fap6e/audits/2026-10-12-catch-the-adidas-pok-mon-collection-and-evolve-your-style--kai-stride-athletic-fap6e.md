# Catch the adidas | Pokémon collection and evolve your style.
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-12**
## Executive summary

- Pikachu in my inbox on a Monday. I'm a running-club captain, so my gut reaction was "cool, not for me." This is the second time in under two weeks adidas has pitched me the Pokémon collab. The first was 9/30. The Spezial "gift" block is a repeat too, because I got the gold metallic Samba gift pitch on 10/8. It's the eleventh adidas email in about ten days, and I still haven't seen a shoe I'd run 16 miles in.
- Visually it's clean. The big Pikachu hero loads fast, and the two black SHOP NOW buttons are impossible to miss. But there's no price, no deadline, no size or availability info, and no runner anywhere. lululemon's "Just in: a new drop of fall styles" at least gave me a point of view, even if it was a barre studio. Nike's one-time-code email did exactly one job and did it well. This one asks me to care about a collab and a lifestyle sneaker with nothing to hang the click on.
- Verdict: I'd skim it and mark it read. I wouldn't click. I'd forward it only to my buddy who collects Pokémon stuff, and he's not in the club.

## What's working

- The Pikachu hero is bold and instantly readable. Black background, yellow character, and the adidas Originals × Pokémon lockup tell me what this is in about one second.
- Both SHOP NOW buttons are big, black, and easy to tap on a phone. There's no hunting for the CTA.
- The layout is simple: hero, headline, two lines of copy, button, repeat. Nothing overlaps or breaks.
- The Spezial photo is nice. The gum sole and suede look premium and the shoe is shown in context, which is better than a floating cutout.
- "This is one collection you won't want to trade" is a decent bit of copy. The brand voice is playful and consistent.

## What's weak

- There's nothing for a runner. A Pokémon collab and a handball-derived terrace shoe are about as far from a long-run shoe as adidas could get.
- There's no offer at all: no price, no percent off, no free shipping, no points. Compare that to the 10/10 text-message email, which at least offered 500 points.
- The "Explore the latest adidas best sellers" banner at the top is generic filler. Its SHOP NOW is a third CTA that competes with the two real ones.
- There's no urgency, and nothing tells me whether the collection is limited, selling out, or in my size. "Drop" hype with no drop info is the opposite of what I'd get from Nike.
- It repeats creative I've already seen: Pokémon on 9/30 and Spezial gifting on 10/8. Two blocks and neither one is new.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one story. The Pokémon collab and Spezial gifting don't belong in the same email. Send the collab as a real drop with a date and sizes, and send the gift guide closer to the holidays.
- 2. Add one concrete hook, such as a launch time, "limited run", or an adiClub early-access perk. With 50 points I'd notice a member angle.
- 3. Segment by category. If I've clicked Adizero or Ultraboost before, show me a performance block instead of a second lifestyle one. Brooks and Nike already treat runners as runners, and lululemon at least separates its running line.
- 4. Cut the generic best-sellers banner and make the CTAs specific ("Shop the Pokémon drop", "Shop Spezial").
- 5. Show the product. Pikachu doesn't show me a single shoe, so give me the actual silhouettes and prices.
