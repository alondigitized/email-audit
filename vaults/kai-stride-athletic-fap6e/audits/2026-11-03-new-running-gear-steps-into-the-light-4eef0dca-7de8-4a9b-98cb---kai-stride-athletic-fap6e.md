# New running gear steps into the light
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-03**
## Executive summary

- This is the first lululemon email in a while that's actually for me. After the 10/28 "Early Access" send, which was a woman in a Define Jacket and a pink belt bag, and the 10/27 contoured-sets email, I'd stopped expecting anything. Then the hero is a runner on a dusty road at dusk, and the subject says "running" out loud. The whole email is dark, reflective-trim night running, which is what my club does from November on, when the Sunday long run starts in the dark.
- The body backs up the hero. I see a runner in reflective-trim shorts mid-stride, a close-up of a shoe and a sock, and a yellow short in motion. That reads like people who run, not people who stand in running gear. It's the same lane as the 10/21 "New fall layers" men's email, but better. My gripes are that it's all mood and no information. There's no price, no sizing, no product names, and the button just says "Shop What's New." I'd open it and scroll all of it. I'd probably click once, and I'd text a screenshot to a club member who's complaining about headlamps.

## What's working

- The hero is a runner on a road in low light. It matches the subject and tells me this is a running email in the first second.
- The reflective, visibility-first story is a real running need, not a fashion one. Club runs in November are dark by 5pm.
- There are men and women in motion, and the shots are sweaty, mid-stride, and lit like real dusk. That's more credible than the studio stretching in the 10/20 "Groove goes further" send.
- The Montana Fairbanks-Seaton ambassador tag adds a face and a name, a bit of community signal.
- The layout is clean. The dark palette and the two red buttons make the hierarchy obvious, with no broken images or overlapping text.

## What's weak

- There's no price, no product name, and no sizing anywhere. I'm looking at moody photos and have no idea what I'd be buying.
- "Shop What's New" is a generic button on a very specific story. It would drop me on a catalog page, not on the reflective gear.
- "Flips a switch" is vague, and the line about visibility-boosting accents is the only real product claim. I'd want to know which pieces are reflective and what it's made of.
- The Holiday Shop block in dark red, with a woman in a dance-pose stretch, pulls me out of running. It's the same studio-lifestyle drift I keep seeing from lululemon.
- The "We think you'll also love" grid is mostly dark, low-contrast images. I can't tell what the products are, and it's a long scroll to get there.

## Recommendations

- 1. Name the gear and put a price on it. Put "Fast Free Hoodie, Surge reflective short, from $68" under the hero. Nike's running emails at least name the shoe.
- 2. Make the buttons specific, like "Shop reflective running gear" or "Shop night-run layers." Link each button to that exact collection.
- 3. Add one line on how bright it is, such as reflective surface area or wet-weather performance. That's the proof a club captain needs to buy for 20 people.
- 4. Cut the Holiday Shop block, or move it below the running content. It dilutes a good running email.
- 5. Lighten the product grid so I can see what the items are.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Reflective jackets, shorts and tights built for dark winter miles.`
