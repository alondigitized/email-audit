# Gift iconic style with Samba
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-10**
## Executive summary

- My gut reaction was that this is a Samba gift guide, and I'm not the audience. The three-shoe stack against red brick is a nice photo, but Samba is a terrace shoe. I don't run in it, and nobody in my club does either. It's the seventh or eighth adidas email I've gotten in about two weeks. The 11/09 Metallica send and the 11/08 Anthony Edwards send had the same problem: nothing for the weekly long run.
- The one running mention is buried in the second block. "The latest tech in running like Evo SL" is a single clause in a paragraph, with no image and no button of its own. Lululemon's winter drop put a man and a woman running on a dirt road in the hero. Here the runner gets a footnote. There's also no offer, no deadline and no member pricing, so nothing makes me act today.
- Would I click? No. Forward it? No. I'd mark it read and move on. If the Evo SL line had been the headline, I might have tapped.

## What's working

- The Samba hero is a well-shot product image. The three colorways are clear, and the cow-print one is a fun detail.
- The copy is short and easy to read on a phone. Nothing is cramped.
- The black buttons are big, high-contrast and easy to tap with a thumb.
- The Men / Women / Kids split is useful when I'm buying for other people, and a gift email should do that.
- The Evo SL mention at least shows adidas knows running exists.

## What's weak

- No offer anywhere. No percent off, no free shipping, no gift-by date. It's the holiday hustle with no deadline, so there's no urgency.
- Running is an afterthought. Evo SL has no image, no link of its own and no detail. After a week of Week of Deals and band-merch emails, it's the same lifestyle-only pattern.
- Three stacked shop buttons (Men, Women, Kids) all go to the same Samba idea. It feels like a menu, not a recommendation.
- The black "Explore the latest adidas best sellers" banner above the hero competes with the real hero and sends me to a generic best-sellers page.
- The second image of two models in jackets and a scarf has nothing to do with the "Gifts that stand out" copy. It's mood, not product. I can't tell what to buy.
- The preheader sits as small underlined grey text at the very top, and it reads like a tagline. It's not a hook.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with a running gift. Put the Evo SL in the second hero with its own image, a price and a "Shop Running" button. Seed that block for anyone who has clicked running before.
- 2. Add one concrete reason to act now: a gift-by date, free shipping, or adiClub member pricing. Nike and lululemon at least tell me when something ends.
- 3. Replace the three identical Men/Women/Kids buttons with one "Shop Samba" button and one "Shop Running" button.
- 4. Cut the "best sellers" banner, or move it below the hero so it doesn't compete.
- 5. Show real products in the second image, not two models standing around.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Gift picks for runners: Evo SL and Samba`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Gift by Dec 15: Samba, Evo SL & more`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free shipping on gifts, plus a running pick that actually delivers.`
