# Up to 60% off for Black Friday is live
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-23**
## Executive summary

- My gut reaction: another adidas Black Friday email, and this one is as generic as it gets. A couple in a red track jacket and a knit sweater, "UP TO 60% OFF" in big letters, then three buttons sorted by gender. The 11/20 "Supercharge your training" email had a Hyperboost Edge and a Runner's World badge. This one has none of that. I see no shoe, no run, and nothing a club captain would stop for.
- This is the fourth or fifth adidas Black Friday blast in a week: 11/17 Spezial, 11/18 "first access ends today," 11/21 "Save up to 60%," and now this one. The 11/21 send already felt thin, and this repeats it with a fresh subject line. "Is live" also doesn't land after a week of "first access" emails. If it's live now, what was the last three?
- Would I click? Probably not. I might tap Shop Men out of habit, but I wouldn't forward it. lululemon also buries me in Black Friday sends, but at least their 11/19 email gave me real prices. Nike's code email at least did one job well. This one doesn't tell me anything.

## What's working

- The "UP TO 60% OFF" hero is huge and legible. You see the offer in about a second.
- The page is clean. Logo, hero, one headline, one paragraph, three buttons. Nothing is overlapping or broken.
- The black buttons with arrows are big and easy to tap with a thumb.
- It's a brand I know, and it's clearly the same Black Friday campaign as the earlier sends.

## What's weak

- Nothing here says running. I see a lifestyle shot, and the copy lists "jerseys, fleece, sneakers, and more." I can't tell if the sale includes Ultraboosts or Adizero, and that's the thing I care about.
- The strip at the top says "Explore the latest adidas best sellers," but it shows no actual best sellers. It's a banner pointing to a banner, and it sits right above the real hero.
- The three buttons are Men, Women and Kids. They don't send me to a product or category. "Up to 60%" with no hero product means I'd land on a sitewide page and have to dig.
- There's no deadline. "Savings season" and "get a head start" are vague, and the sale has run for a week. Urgency has been burned.
- The headline "It's savings season" is flat. Nike would at least tell me which product to look at.
- Nothing recognizes me as an adiClub member or as a repeat runner.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with one real product and the price. For example, a running shoe with "$X, was $Y" next to the 60% claim. Right now the offer is a ceiling and I can't tell what it applies to.
- 2. Replace the gender buttons with category buttons that include Running. "Shop running" is a verb and a noun, and it gets a runner to the right place.
- 3. Add a real deadline with an end time, not "season." If it's the same sale as last week, say what's new today.
- 4. Remove the "best sellers" banner or make it show actual product.
- 5. Use the adiClub status for member pricing or early access. I'd notice that.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email's HTML has a viewport meta tag, `lang="en"` and responsive media queries. Automated QA recorded 1 FAIL (no plain-text part) and several WARNs: header capture gaps, `http://` image sources and missing alt text. Two of these, the plain-text and header results, may come from the AgentMail relay rather than the sender.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 22 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped in the HTTP probe, so their destinations and redirect health are **unverified**. This is a QA coverage gap, not a confirmed defect.
- Click tracking uses `click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/...` and image hosting uses `image.link.adidas.com`. Both are on a sending subdomain of the brand domain, which is good for alignment.
- The `click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/...` image URL is served over `http://`, so the tracking request is unencrypted and may be blocked or rewritten by clients.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Mixed content:** six instances of the same image, `http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg` (alt="arrow"), use a non-HTTPS source. A seventh, the `click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/...=452` image, also uses `http://`. Clients that block mixed content will show broken images.
- **Missing alt text:**
  - `https://dv.adidas.com/o/5e58caf6-b9e4-46a5-b71b-0eecff8af634?cp_tp=...`
  - the `click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/...=452` image
- If the `=452` image is a tracking pixel, an empty `alt=""` is appropriate. Otherwise, add descriptive alt text.
- **Viewport:** `<meta name="viewport" content="initial-scale=1.0">` omits `width=device-width`. The email relies on media queries, so this can affect scaling in some clients.
- The `@font-face` rules use `local()` sources with Arial fallbacks. I found no rendering issue there.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. The truncated source showed no unresolved merge tokens such as `%%name%%`, `{{...}}` or `[FIRSTNAME]`. The visible portion is also too short to confirm that no personalization exists.

## 5. Compliance
- **[FAIL] Plain-text fallback:** the text version is 0 chars. A missing `text/plain` part can hurt deliverability and accessibility. Confirm against the raw MIME to rule out a relay capture artifact.
- **[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058):** neither header was found. The QA tool notes AgentMail may strip them, so verify them in the original message headers. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require one-click unsubscribe for senders of this volume.
