# Save up to 60% during Black Friday
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-21**
## Executive summary

- My gut reaction: this is the third adidas Black Friday email in a week, and it's the thinnest. On 11/18 I got "Up to 60% off: Black Friday first access ends today." Now it's "Save up to 60% during Black Friday" with the same offer and less detail. The hero is a woman in a red track suit in front of a brick wall, with "UP TO 60% OFF" in huge type. It's loud and clear, and it has nothing to do with running.
- I captain a run club, so I'm looking for a shoe or a performance angle. The body copy says "classic sneakers, jerseys, cozy fleece." That's lifestyle and fan gear. On 11/20 "Supercharge your training" gave me a Hyperboost Edge with a Runner's World badge. That one felt like it knew me. This one treats me like anyone on the list.
- Would I click? Probably not. I'd mark it read. I'd only forward it to someone who wants a retro track jacket, and that isn't anyone in my group chat.

## What's working

- The offer is unmissable. "UP TO 60% OFF" is the biggest thing on the screen, so I got the point in a second.
- The layout is clean. There's one hero, one short paragraph and three buttons, with no clutter and no render bugs.
- The black buttons are large and easy to tap with a thumb.
- It's unmistakably adidas. The 3-Stripes logo, the track jacket and the stripes all say so.

## What's weak

- Nothing here is running. There's no shoe, no Ultraboost or Adizero, and no mention of performance gear. Every product word is lifestyle.
- "Up to 60%" with no product attached is a vague headline. I can't tell whether any running shoe is part of it.
- Men, Women and Kids is the whole menu. There's no Running button. Compare Nike's category-led structure, or lululemon's 11/20 "Black Friday Sale is in motion," which at least gave me real prices.
- It has no urgency. There's no end date, no countdown and no "ends Monday." The 11/18 email had a countdown, and this one dropped it.
- The "3-Stripes way" line in the preheader is brand fluff, and the "Stories, styles… since 1949" footer line adds nothing.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment by interest. Send me a running version that leads with a Supernova or Adizero deal and a "Shop Running" button.
- 2. Show real products with prices. A row of three items with "$X, was $Y" beats a model in a track suit.
- 3. Add a real deadline, like "Ends Cyber Monday 11:59 PM," so the sale feels credible.
- 4. Add a size and availability cue. A line like "Popular sizes selling fast" helps me decide to click.

- **Mixed scheme.** Hero and other assets use `https://dv.adidas.com/o/...`, while the arrow icon uses `http://image.link.adidas.com/...`.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Non-HTTPS images (QA WARN).** `http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg` is used 6 times (`alt="arrow"`). The tracking image `http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/...` is also `http://`. Clients that block mixed content may not load them. The tracking one would then miss opens.
- **Missing alt text.** `https://dv.adidas.com/o/2307719c-090e-494b-8ac1-8cd62673ecaa?cp_tp=...` has no alt. `http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/...` (`...XI=452`) has no alt. The second is probably a tracking pixel, where `alt=""` is the correct fix.
- **Arrow icon alt.** `alt="arrow"` on a purely decorative icon adds screen-reader noise. `alt=""` would be better.
- **Head and layout.** `<html lang="en">` is present. The viewport meta is `initial-scale=1.0` only, with no `width=device-width`. The page uses an HTML 4.01 Transitional doctype with table layout and media queries at 639/640px. Nothing there is broken per the source I have.
