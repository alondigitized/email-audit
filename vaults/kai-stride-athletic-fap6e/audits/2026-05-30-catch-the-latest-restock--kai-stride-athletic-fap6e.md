# Catch the latest restock
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-30**
## Executive summary

- "Catch the latest restock" lands and I feel a flicker of real anticipation — restocked items at lululemon historically means the ABC Short or Surge tights or the Pace Breaker came back after selling out. That's the kind of restock that sends a message in my running-club group chat. So I open it. And I'm staring at the Define Cropped Jacket on a woman, a Wide-Leg Pant on a woman, the "Like a Cloud Spaghetti-Strap Bra \*Light Support A/B Cup," and the Align Cami Dress. The product name literally has the cup size in it. There is no ambiguity about who this email was built for. It wasn't me.
- This is the send that follows May 27's swim trunks email — the one time in this recent stretch they put a man in the hero and I felt like a known subscriber. That lasted exactly two sends. Adidas, for all their cadence problems (nine emails in eight days wasn't great), at least hit me with a personalized voucher code in my adiClub account. Nike's one-time-code send was transactional but functional and correctly gendered. Lululemon is the only brand in my inbox that keeps sending me women's A/B Cup bras and cami dresses with full confidence.
- The email renders beautifully. I'll give them that. Clean hierarchy, no broken assets, membership early access is a nice loyalty signal. Technically well-executed, personally irrelevant. Marking as read. Not clicking. Not forwarding.

## What's working

- Clean render end-to-end — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum anywhere in the layout
- The "Back in Stock" hero tag + urgency headline ("don't wait till they're gone again") is genuinely punchy — scarcity copy that's earned because restocks do vanish fast
- Visual hierarchy flows naturally: back-in-stock tag → bold headline → Define Jacket close-up → product grid → membership section — the eye moves the way it's supposed to
- Color swatches on the product tiles are useful at a glance — I can see options without clicking into every PDP
- Membership Early Access with the Citrus Sizzle drop at least acknowledges I'm a member, not a random cold visitor

## What's weak

- Every model is a woman. Every product is women's apparel. "Women's Steady State SuperLoft Short" literally opens with "Women's" in the product name — this email was built for a different subscriber list and landed in my inbox by mistake
- "Catch the latest restock" is the vaguest possible subject line — restock of what? Running shorts? A cami dress? I don't know until I open, and the curiosity is entirely borrowed interest from hoping it's men's gear
- Still zero running signal in send number eight-ish from lululemon in the last ten days — no Surge, no ABC Short, no pace reference, no club content, nothing that suggests they know I log 50 miles a week
- "Shop Now" as the primary CTA is the lowest-effort option — Nike's transactional one-time-code email had more directional copy than this, and that was a password reset
- Membership Early Access is buried below four product rows — if I'm a member and that's the hook, it belongs above the fold, not as an afterthought

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment by gender before you send anything.** This email is well-made for its actual audience. That audience is not me. A men's restock version — ABC Shorty, Surge tights, training gear — should have been built in parallel and routed correctly. The demographic miss is not a creative problem, it's a list problem.
- 2. **Name what was restocked in the subject.** "Catch the latest restock" means nothing. "The ABC Short is back — men's restocks just dropped" gives me a reason to open immediately. Adidas named my voucher code in their subject line and it worked — specificity converts.
- 3. **Pull Membership Early Access above the fold.** If early access to a restocked drop is the loyalty hook, it's the headline for members. Put it in a top banner or hero callout, not buried below the A/B Cup bra.
- 4. **Swap "Shop Now" for a destination-specific CTA.** "Shop Men's Restocks" or "Grab Your Size" tells me what I'm clicking into before I click. "Shop Now" could go anywhere.
- 5. **Surface one running product anywhere in this email.** One module. One shot of the Surge on a guy mid-stride. It signals that lululemon knows who I am.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Back in stock: the men's training gear you've been waiting on`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Surge tights, ABC Shorts, and run essentials — before they sell out again`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Member Early Access · Men's bestsellers restocked now`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`letter-spacing` uses invalid percentage values**
```css
.guest-voice-lg { letter-spacing: -2%; }
.guest-voice-md { letter-spacing: -2%; }
.guest-voice-sm { letter-spacing: -1%; }
```
`letter-spacing` accepts only `<length>` values (`px`, `em`) or `normal` — percentages are not valid per the CSS spec and will be silently ignored by all email clients. The intended tracking compression on these display classes will not render.

**Non-standard `font-weight: 380`**
Applied to `p`, `ul`, `ol`, `li`, `h1`–`h5`, and multiple named classes. CSS Fonts Level 4 permits arbitrary numeric weights for variable fonts, but email client support is inconsistent. Clients without variable-font support will round to `400` (normal), which may shift visual weight noticeably.

**No `@font-face` declarations visible**
Custom fonts `Saans-Regular`, `Saans-Light`, `Saans-Semibold`, `Saans-Medium`, and `KHGiga-Light` have no `@font-face` or `<link>` import in the visible `<head>`. If they are absent from the full document, all clients will fall back to `Helvetica Neue` / `Arial` / `Times New Roman`. Fallbacks are acceptable but confirm the declarations are present later in the `<head>`.

**`alt` text** — cannot verify; no `<img>` tags visible in truncated source.

**`<title>lululemon</title>`** — renders as tab/window label only; not a deliverability or accessibility defect, but some older clients surface the `<title>` as a preheader fallback.
