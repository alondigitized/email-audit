# New Beach Ball Blue rolls deep
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-18**
## Executive summary

- Electric blue. That's my first reaction — and I mean that as a compliment. Beach Ball Blue is a genuinely eye-catching colorway and lululemon photographs it beautifully. The female model in the hero zip-up looks great, the male model in the tank beside her is there, and for about three seconds I thought "okay, they're talking to me too." Then I scrolled down and it's wall-to-wall women's product — sports bras, skirts, leggings, bags — and by the fourth row I felt like I wandered into the wrong section of the store.
- This email is a color-story launch, not a runner's email. There's no "built for pace," no long-run framing, nothing that signals lululemon knows I'm covering 50 miles a week in their shorts. It's beautiful and well-produced. But it's aimed at someone doing Sunday pilates, not someone pacing out a 10-miler. After the June 16 golf send that I wanted nothing to do with, and LuluLinen on June 9, this at least feels like energetic sportswear territory. That's progress. But it still doesn't close the gap.
- I'd probably click once out of curiosity to see if the men's Beach Ball Blue options are actually interesting. I wouldn't forward this to anyone in my running club.

## What's working

- **The cobalt is genuinely stunning** — the grid is cohesive, all-blue all the way down, and it reads as a real launch moment, not one new SKU buried in a digest.
- **"Toss on Beach Ball Blue" is right-register hero copy** — casual, confident, suits the colorway's energy. Not "Introducing" or "Meet." That matters.
- **Male model appears in the hero** — at least the opening frame signals this is nominally for me too, even if the product grid tells a different story.
- **Seasonal timing is on** — electric blue in June, after the golf detour and linen wave, feels like a return to the brand's core sportswear identity.
- **Clean render** — no layout breaks, no overlapping text, no janky spacing. Looks sharp on mobile and that's the baseline.

## What's weak

- **The grid is overwhelmingly female product** — rough count puts men's items at maybe 3 of 15+. I'm not the primary audience and the email doesn't bother hiding it.
- **Zero running signal anywhere** — not a single word about performance, miles, or training. This could be a yoga studio's color launch. lululemon has my purchase history; they know I run.
- **No offer at all** — adidas put "$30 on orders $100+" right in the subject line, math I can do in under a second. This is pure browse-drive. Nothing here shifts me from "interesting" to "buying."
- **The credit card module at the bottom is a hard pivot** — "$200 lululemon credit" buried after a color-story email is a mood-killer. That's a separate send with a separate job.
- **Cadence is starting to wear on me** — this is the fifth lululemon email in nine days. Golf, LuluLinen, two Father's Day pushes, now this. Even a good send suffers when the inbox is saturated.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead men's first in the grid** — if there's a Beach Ball Blue Metal Vent Tech tee or Pace Breaker short, that's my hero. Put the men's running pieces in row one, not buried behind the leggings.
- 2. **Add one runner-signal line under the hero headline** — something like "Your next long run just got louder." One sentence tells me this gear has a purpose beyond looking good at brunch.
- 3. **Pull the credit card module into its own send** — it breaks the color-story entirely. It deserves its own dedicated email with its own subject line.
- 4. **Create a "Run It" section in the grid** — segment the Beach Ball Blue pieces by activity so I'm not hunting through the women's catalog for the two items that fit my use case.
- 5. **Add a light urgency hook** — even "New color. Limited quantities." works. Nothing here gives me a reason to click now rather than later.

`letter-spacing` only accepts length units (`px`, `em`, `rem`). Percentages are silently ignored by all CSS parsers, meaning tracking collapses to 0 in these display-font blocks. Intended values are almost certainly `-0.02em` and `-0.01em`.
