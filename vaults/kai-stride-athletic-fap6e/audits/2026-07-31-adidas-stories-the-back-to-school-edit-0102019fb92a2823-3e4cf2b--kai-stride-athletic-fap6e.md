# adidas Stories: The Back-to-School Edit
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-31**
## Executive summary

- Third back-to-school email from adidas in two weeks and this one's the least offensive of the bunch — at least there's Sambas and an Adistar in here, which is more than I can say for the Care Bears leotard nonsense from the 27th. But "Adidas Stories" as a hero treatment for three teenagers on a bench with a BMX bike is squarely aimed at a 16-year-old, not a 33-year-old running club captain shopping for his weekly long run. There's zero actual running content — no trainers built for miles, no pace, no drop, nothing performance-coded, just lifestyle sneakers and size guides for shoes I'd never lace up before a tempo run.
- The Samba and Adistar size guides are the one genuinely useful idea in here — sizing content is something I actually want from a footwear brand — but both are for retro/lifestyle silhouettes, not anything in the running lane. Compare that to Nike's one-time-code email, which at least respected my time and got me in and out of my account in under a minute. This is adidas dressing up a fashion mood board as "stories" and hoping I don't notice there's no running shoe anywhere in it.
- I'm not clicking, not forwarding. Straight to archived.

## What's working

- The hero photo is at least well-shot and not another stock-photo trio — feels like an actual campaign asset, not filler.
- Samba and Adistar "size guide" content is a smart idea in theory — sizing help is something runners genuinely want from a shoe brand.
- Clean black-and-white layout, no render bugs, buttons are legible and tappable on mobile.
- Copy is short and scannable — I'm not wading through paragraphs to find the CTA.

## What's weak

- No running product anywhere — Sambas and Adistars are lifestyle/retro, not anything I'd train in.
- Hero imagery is three teens and a BMX bike — that's a high schooler's back-to-school moment, not a 33-year-old's.
- "BACK TO SCHOOL STYLE" as the lead message has nothing to do with me — I don't have a first day of school, and neither does my running club.
- No offer anywhere in this email — no %, no BOGO, no free shipping, just "Learn More" three times.
- No loyalty/adiClub content, no mention of the run club angle adidas actually has (Runtastic heritage, city running crews) that would've made this relevant.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split the send — back-to-school lifestyle content should never share a subject line with sizing guides for people who actually train. Give me a running-specific edit instead: trainers, drop dates, adiClub run-club perks.
- 2. Put an actual offer in this email. Three "Learn More" buttons with zero discount or urgency gives me no reason to act today.
- 3. If sizing content is the hook, lead with it — the Samba/Adistar guides are the only useful thing here, bury the back-to-school mood board below it or cut it for a segment like mine.
- 4. Swap the hero model cast for something that signals "this is for adults who train," not teenagers on a bench — even a lifestyle shot could at least skew older.
