# Born from the spirit of '94, built for the lifestyle expression of today
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-20**
## Executive summary

- Adidas went full nostalgia here — the '94 US Soccer throwback is a decent creative hook and I get it, the World Cup is back on American soil for the first time since then. But I'm a runner, not a soccer dad, and this email lands in my inbox like a random jersey someone handed me at a race expo. The copy is moody and intentionally poetic ("Rockets come and go, but the attitude lasts forever"), the visual is bold, and the production is clean — but I'm looking at heritage lifestyle merch for a sport I don't follow, not the new Adizero colorway or the training tight I actually care about.
- What kills me is that adidas was doing this same FIFA play just four days ago with the adiClub FIFA Rewards send (June 16th — which I rated a 9 because the loyalty angle made it personally relevant). This one drops that loyalty wrapper entirely and goes pure licensing merchandise. I'm now two FIFA emails deep in one week with zero running content in sight. It's starting to feel less like a brand that knows me and more like they licensed the World Cup and are recouping the spend on every subscriber regardless of segment. Nike does this too, but they at least rotate categories by profile.
- I wouldn't click. I wouldn't forward it. I'd clock the hero for half a second, register "soccer stuff," and hit archive.

## What's working

- **The hero is visually commanding** — the retro "FROM '94 TO '25" lock-up with that oversized typography commits to the creative direction fully; it's confident.
- **adidas renders clean** — no broken images, no overlapping text, the three-button CTA stack is readable and tappable.
- **"SHOP ALL FIFA WORLD CUP 2026™" is anchored to a real thing** — it's specific enough to know what you're getting into, which is more than lululemon's "Explore the edit" vagueness.
- **The World Cup hook is genuinely timely** — they're riding a cultural moment happening right now, not a manufactured urgency.

## What's weak

- **Zero runner relevance** — not one word about performance, running, or anything that explains why I'm on this list. Compare this to how Nike segments their drop emails; this one treats me like a generic adidas consumer.
- **The hero year is off** — the graphic says "FROM '94 TO '25" and this email landed in June 2026. Either that's stale creative recycled from a 2025 campaign or it's an intentional collection name, but it introduces doubt either way.
- **No offer** — "SHOP NOW" is not an offer. The June 18th send nailed the math ("$30 off $100+") instantly. This one gives me nothing to act on — no discount, no points, no urgency.
- **Store exclusivity buried** — body says "shop online or at your local adidas store," but the footnote quietly limits this to 12 specific US locations. If it's a physical-retail-first drop, lead with that — it's actually a scarcity hook.
- **FIFA fatigue** — after the June 16th rewards email, this is the second soccer-themed send this week. Cadence is off for a non-soccer subscriber.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment this away from running subscribers.** My adiClub profile signals running purchases. This email should never have landed in my inbox. This is the highest-leverage fix — a segmentation problem, not a creative one.
- 2. **Lead with the store-exclusivity scarcity.** "Only at 12 stores nationwide" is a real hook buried in footnotes. Flip it: make that the hero. Even as a non-soccer person I respect limited-location drops.
- 3. **Fix the year inconsistency.** If that hero says '25 in a June 2026 email, it reads like recycled creative. One character, massive trust signal.
- 4. **Bring the loyalty angle back.** The June 16th FIFA email worked for me because adiClub points were in play. A single line — "US94 purchases earn double adiClub points" — would have bridged the gap even for a non-soccer subscriber.

- **Subject Alt B:** `The '94 attitude. The '26 look. Limited locations.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Limited locations. Gone when it's gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop the US94 Official Licensed Collection before your store sells out.`

### 1. Technical Summary

One CTA link has an unresolvable relative-path URL that will produce a broken experience for recipients who click it. Additionally, the email ships with zero plain-text fallback content, six HTTP image references, and missing RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe headers.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken link — invalid relative URL in click redirect**

The QA probe resolved one tracked link to a relative path:

```
unknown url type: '/g/10d3cd7d-07ef-4914-ad7d-619c746930d6?txn=d40ba600-7133-11f1-b61b-ae32f7ad8416'
```

The outer tracking URL is:
```
https://dv.adidas.com/o/10d3cd7d-07ef-4914-ad7d-619c746930d6?cp_tp=v3.eJz...
```

The redirect destination resolves to `/g/10d3cd7d-07ef-4914-ad7d-619c746930d6` — a path with no host, which is not a valid absolute URL. Any recipient who clicks this link will land on a broken page or receive an error. The destination URL must be corrected to an absolute `https://` URL in the ESP link configuration.

**[INFO] 28 tracking links skipped**

All remaining links route through `click.comms.adidas.com`. These were not probed. UTM destination validity is unverifiable without resolving the redirects (see Section 6).

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] 6× same arrow image served over HTTP**

The image `b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg` (alt: "arrow") appears 6 times, all over HTTP:
```
src: http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```
Email clients with mixed-content blocking (Gmail on HTTPS, Outlook 365) will suppress these images silently. Change the scheme to `https://`.

**[WARN] 1× tracking pixel served over HTTP**

```
src: http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019ee5c8a3e9-3690e5fc-ecc2-42de-82c6-9c95687aa3bb-000000/vDIEyUTlbSKc_h4uGpb6fXkwc...
```
Open-tracking pixels over HTTP are suppressed in the same clients listed above, degrading open-rate measurement accuracy.

**[WARN] 2 images missing `alt` text**

- Tracking/open pixel: `src: https://dv.adidas.com/o/10d3cd7d-07ef-4914-ad7d-619c746930d6?...`
- HTTP tracking pixel: `src: http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/...`

Both have no `alt` attribute. While tracking pixels intentionally render invisibly, a missing `alt=""` (empty-string alt) on decorative/invisible images is an HTML validation gap and can produce screen reader noise. Set `alt=""` explicitly.

**[INFO] Custom web fonts (`AdihausDIN`, `AdineuePRO`) declared via `local()` only**

Both `@font-face` blocks reference only `local()` sources — no remote font URLs. This is standard email practice (web fonts are unsupported in most clients), but it means the rendering falls back to Arial/Helvetica everywhere. No bug, flagged for awareness.

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] Plain-text fallback is 0 characters**

The multipart/alternative text part is empty. CAN-SPAM does not mandate a text part, but it is a strong deliverability requirement for major ISPs (Gmail, Yahoo Mail) and is required for CASL compliance. A missing text part increases spam score and disqualifies the email from some inbox placement filters.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header not confirmed**

The AgentMail relay did not capture a `List-Unsubscribe` header. This may be a relay artifact, but if the header is genuinely absent, Gmail and Yahoo (post-February 2024 bulk sender requirements) will inject their own unsubscribe mechanism or suppress delivery for lists above 5,000/day. Verify the header is present in the raw SMTP envelope.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header missing (RFC 8058)**

One-click unsubscribe (the `POST` method required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy) is not confirmed. Without it, Gmail replaces the one-click UI with a slower flow, increasing complaint rates. Required header:
```
List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click
```

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header not captured**

SPF and DKIM pass/fail status is unknown due to relay interception. Cannot confirm DMARC alignment from available data. Adidas's ESP (`us-news.comms.adidas.com`) should be publishing SPF and signing with DKIM; this should be verified in the raw message headers from a seed inbox.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**[UNVERIFIABLE] UTM parameter presence on 28 links**

All destination URLs pass through `click.comms.adidas.com` redirects, which were not probed. It is not possible to confirm whether UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, etc.) survive the redirect chain. A manual spot-check of 2–3 links by following the full redirect to final destination is recommended.

**[FAIL] One CTA resolves to no valid destination**

As documented in Section 2, one link's final destination is a relative path (`/g/10d3cd7d-...`) with no host. This is a site continuity failure — the traffic from that CTA is completely lost.
