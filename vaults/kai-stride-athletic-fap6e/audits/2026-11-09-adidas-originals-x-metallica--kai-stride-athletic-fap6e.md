# adidas Originals x Metallica
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-09**
## Executive summary

- My gut reaction was that this is a band-merch email, and I run a running club. A white Metallica ringer tee on a hanger in a backstage road-case scene looks great. But I scrolled through the whole email and found no shoe, no run, and no mention of anything I'd wear on a long run. I counted the Anthony Edwards 3 email on 11/08, the Week of Deals blasts, and "Fresh arrivals" on 10/30. This is another adidas send with no running in it.
- The second half is a football-rookie giveaway for adiClub members. It's a separate story under a second headline, so the email feels like two emails stacked together. I'm a member, but I have no use for signed footballs from linebackers. I can't see a price, a percent off, or a date anywhere, so nothing tells me to act now.
- Would I click? No. Would I forward it? Maybe to the one friend in my run group who's a metalhead, and only for the tee. Otherwise I'd mark it read and move on.

## What's working

- The hero photo is a strong lifestyle shot. The Trefoil tee with the red Metallica logo against the road case and tape reels feels authentic, not staged.
- The headline is big and the copy is clean. The black "SHOP NOW" button is easy to find and easy to tap.
- The collab angle is a real hook. "First collaboration" is a legitimate drop moment for the right buyer, and adidas does tie-ups better than most.
- The giveaway section names real athletes and says what members get, so the loyalty benefit is at least specific.
- The layout renders cleanly. I saw no broken images or overlapping text.

## What's weak

- There's no running, no performance product and no sport-specific honesty. This is pure fashion-merch, so it reads as "fashion shoppers in streetwear," not "a brand that knows runners."
- There's no price, offer, size or availability info. Is this a limited run? Which sizes? Nothing is stated.
- The black "Explore the latest adidas best sellers / SHOP NOW" banner sits above the hero. It competes with the real message and pushes the tee down the page. It also sits slightly offset from the image below it.
- Two themes in one email split my attention. The Metallica collab and the football giveaway have nothing to do with each other.
- The Metallica body copy is a dense, marketing-speak paragraph ("reimagines... through the lens of... connecting the energy..."). That's the seminar tone without the substance.
- Three more black buttons stack at the bottom (Shop Now, Learn More, Participate), and "Learn More" and "Participate" sit right next to each other. That's ambiguous.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one story per send. Give the Metallica drop the whole email, with 3-4 individually linked products, prices and a "drops Friday, limited" date. Move the football giveaway into a member-only send.
- 2. Cut the generic best-sellers banner above the hero so the tee is the first thing I see.
- 3. Rewrite the copy as short, concrete lines: what's in the range, the sizes, the date, the price range.
- 4. Segment by category. Send this to Originals and lifestyle buyers, and send me an Adizero or Adios email.
- 5. Merge "Learn More" and "Participate" into one clear button, such as "Enter the giveaway."
