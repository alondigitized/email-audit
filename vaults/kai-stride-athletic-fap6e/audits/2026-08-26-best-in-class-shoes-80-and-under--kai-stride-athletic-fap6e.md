# Best-in-class shoes $80 and under
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-26**
## Executive summary

- So this is adidas's third "back to school" swing this month, and once again it's lifestyle sneakers dressed up as a promo. Three pairs of Sambas and Gazelles staged on concrete steps, "$80 and under school shoes" — none of that has anything to do with a guy who runs long on Sundays. I run a club, I care about drop / stability / mileage on a shoe, and this email is selling me suede casual kicks for hallway fits. Same complaint I had on the 8/23 Samba email, just with a price anchor bolted on this time.
- It's clean, it's not broken, the type is legible, the CTAs are obvious — I'll give it that. But there's zero performance content, zero running signal, and "shop men / shop women" is the laziest possible segmentation for a company that has an entire run division. I'm not clicking, and I'm definitely not forwarding this to my club — nobody in that group chat needs suede trainers for the school run.

## What's working

- The subject line at least has a real number in it — $80 and under is a concrete anchor, unlike the vague "best sellers" banner copy above it.
- Product shot is clean and well-lit, three colorways (black/white, red, black) so there's some visible variety.
- Two unambiguous CTAs — "Shop Men" / "Shop Women" — both above the fold on desktop, no hunting required.
- No render bugs, no broken images, no lorem ipsum — technically sound send.

## What's weak

- Zero running relevance — Samba, Gazelle-style silhouettes are lifestyle/terrace shoes, not anything I'd log a mile in. Third time this month adidas has run this exact "casual shoe on concrete steps" playbook (Samba 8/23, this one now).
- The top banner says "Explore the latest adidas best sellers" and then the hero says "$80 and under school shoes" — two different messages fighting for my attention in the first two inches.
- "Back to school" targeting a 33-year-old with no kids in the house (that I've told adidas about) is a swing and a miss — no personalization, no run-club angle, nothing that says they know who I am.
- No specific price per shoe, no member/adiClub pricing shown even though I'm in that program — compare to adidas's own adiClub profile email from earlier this month, which at least felt like it knew I was a member.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment this out of my inbox entirely, or if I'm getting it, at least lead with a performance/training shoe under $80 instead of three lifestyle silhouettes — give me a reason to think "runner" not "freshman."
- 2. Kill the redundant top banner — it's competing with the hero for the same real estate and saying something different.
- 3. Show adiClub member pricing or points multiplier on this sale — I'm in the program, use it.
- 4. Replace "Shop Men / Shop Women" with something that routes me to run-specific gear if that's a category adidas has for me.
