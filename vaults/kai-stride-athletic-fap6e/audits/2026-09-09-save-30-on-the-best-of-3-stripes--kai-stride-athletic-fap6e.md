# Save 30% on the best of 3-Stripes
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-09**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "END OF SUMMER SALE" in that moody green fabric shot is actually a decent hero — clean, seasonal, not another kid in a gym. But then I scroll past three "SHOP MEN/WOMEN/KIDS" buttons into the product grid and it's an Adistar Cushion XLG lifestyle sneaker, track pants, a slip-on called PureChill, and a denim track jacket. Not one running shoe. I run a weekly long run club — my whole reason for opening an adidas email is "did they drop something for my long run," and the answer here is a hard no, it's a general closet-refresh sale.
- Worse: I checked the fine print because that's a habit at this point with adidas, and the offer is valid "September 1 through September 8, 11:59pm PST." It's September 9 when I'm reading this. They sent me an expired sale. That's not a nitpick — that's the kind of thing that makes me stop trusting the "SHOP NOW" button entirely.
- Compare this to adidas's own "Win the go full hybrid kit" send from 9/5 — that one had actual Adizero Dropset Pro shoes and a real training kit flat-lay, and it at least felt like someone on their team knows a runner exists. This one's a step back toward the generic-lifestyle-sale mode. I'm not clicking, not forwarding, marking as read.

## What's working

- The "END OF SUMMER SALE" hero image is clean and seasonally appropriate — no lorem ipsum, no broken renders, just a nice moody visual.
- 30% off with a clear code (EXTRA) is a real, specific offer — better than vague "shop the sale" copy.
- Product cards show price, star rating, and review count, which at least gestures at social proof.
- Prime badges on two items signal fast delivery, a small trust cue.

## What's weak

- Zero running product in the entire grid — sneakers, trackpants, a denim jacket. Nothing here says "we know you run."
- The fine print reveals the promo expired September 8, and I'm opening this September 9 — they sent me a dead coupon.
- "SHOP MEN / SHOP WOMEN / SHOP KIDS" is just gender/age segmentation, not category segmentation — no "SHOP RUNNING" option anywhere.
- The exclusions list buries a huge chunk of the catalog (Ultraboost, NMD, Stan Smith, Superstar, adizero) — so the shoes I'd actually want are the ones least likely to qualify.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the dated fine print before it goes out again — an expired-by-the-time-you-open-it offer is the single biggest trust killer here, worse than any copy issue.
- 2. Add a "SHOP RUNNING" CTA row alongside Men/Women/Kids so category-loyal subscribers like me aren't forced through a gender/age filter to find gear I actually buy.
- 3. Swap at least one hero product for something with real running specs (drop, cushioning stack, GORE-TEX, whatever) — even one adizero or Ultraboost callout would beat four lifestyle SKUs.
- 4. Move the exclusions summary above the fold or at least flag it near the code, since Ultraboost/adizero being excluded is the kind of thing that kills the click once someone notices.
