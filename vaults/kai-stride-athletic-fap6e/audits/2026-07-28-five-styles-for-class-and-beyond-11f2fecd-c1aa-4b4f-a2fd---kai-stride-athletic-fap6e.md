# Five styles for class and beyond
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-28**
## Executive summary

- "Class" is the tell here — this is a between-classes-and-errands email, styled around a woman walking through a city holding a tote, not a runner. Five items — a bralette, a hoodie, crop tights, a tee, and shorts — are stitched together as "flexible styles," which is lifestyle-brand-speak for "we don't know what you're doing today and neither do you." I scrolled the whole thing in under ten seconds because nothing stopped my thumb.
- This lands right in the mushy middle of lululemon's recent stretch. It's not the Pace Breaker™ email from 7/22 that actually named a product I'd race in, but it's also not as tone-deaf as 7/21's dark-sports-bra opener. It's just... inventory. Five styles, two lifestyle shots at the bottom in maroon and grey that could be J.Crew product photography, zero mention of pace, mileage, or gear that survives a long run.
- I'm not clicking, and I'm definitely not forwarding this to the run club — there's nothing club-relevant in here, no drop, no performance claim, nothing to rally around. This gets archived, maybe skimmed for the hoodie since I could use one for cool-down walks, but that's a stretch.

## What's working

- The hero shot is clean and shot well — no render bugs, no overlapping text, the layout is airy and easy to scan.
- "Shop What's New," "Shop Summer Clothes," "Shop Accessories," "Shop Shoes" links are unambiguous even if the surrounding copy is vague — I know exactly what happens if I tap.
- The Steady State SuperLoft Hoodie is genuinely something I'd wear post-run — it's the one item in here that crosses into my world even if the framing doesn't sell it that way.
- No fake urgency, no ALL CAPS, no bait-and-switch — at least it's an honest "here's some stuff" email, not a manufactured deadline.

## What's weak

- Every single model shot — six of them — is a woman. This is styled 100% as a womenswear send; nothing here signals lululemon thinks I exist as a customer.
- "Mix five flexible styles to match the pace of your day" borrows running language ("pace") for what is clearly a campus/errands lookbook — that's the kind of soft-focus copy that made 7/21 forgettable too.
- No price, no discount, no member pricing anywhere in this email — I'm an Align/ABC-pant guy and I still don't know what any of this costs.
- Zero performance specs — no sweat-wicking callout, no compression rating, no "4-way stretch," nothing that tells me this survives a long run versus a coffee run.
- Compare this to Nike's one-time-code email from a few weeks back — that one did exactly one job (get me my code) and did it in under a minute. This one does five vague jobs at once and none of them land.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split sends by gender/category the way Nike segments its transactional stuff — a men's-focused "five styles" email with a guy in it would immediately feel less like a miss-send.
- 2. Cut "pace of your day" — either commit to running language with a real product (like 7/22's Pace Breaker™ send) or drop the running vocabulary entirely and just say "five easy pieces."
- 3. Add one concrete price or member-pricing callout — right now there's no reason to click versus just browsing the site cold.
- 4. Lead with the hoodie, not the bralette — it's the one crossover piece a guy like me would actually consider, bury it less.
- 5. Steal a page from adidas's adiClub points email — even a low-stakes send felt more purposeful because it asked for one specific action instead of five vague ones.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Breathable layers that go from studio to street.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New this week: SuperLoft, Align, and more.`
