# 'Tis the season for footwear gifting wins
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-26**
## Executive summary

- My gut reaction: another adidas email that isn't for me. It's the fourth in a week with no running in it. The hero is two terrace sneakers on a tiled stairwell, one tan suede and one black leather with a gum sole. They look like the Bad Bunny Gazelle from 10/22, and they're indoor-soccer-style lifestyle shoes. I run a club and buy for the Sunday long run, so nothing here helps me.
- The one useful thing is the "$80 and under" price cap. That's a concrete hook. The top line says "From running to streetwear," and that's the only time running gets a mention. It's tiny gray underlined text up in the preheader slot. The email itself shows no running shoe, no Adizero, no Supernova, and no sign anyone at adidas knows what a long run is.
- I wouldn't click, and I wouldn't forward it. It's a gift-guide send, much like the 10/20 "Gear up for an active holiday season" one. I'd mark it read. If someone in my club asked for a cheap gift idea, I might remember the price cap, but I'd have to go find it myself.

## What's working

- "Gifting wins for $80 & under" is a clear, concrete price promise, and it's the only offer I can act on.
- The hero photo is a clean product shot. The warm suede and the black leather read well against the dark steps.
- Two buttons, Shop Men and Shop Women, are big black blocks with obvious labels and decent tap targets on a phone.
- The layout is simple: banner, image, headline, one paragraph, buttons. There are no render bugs and nothing overlaps.

## What's weak

- There's no running anywhere. "From running to streetwear" is buried in a gray link at the top, and the product shown is a terrace sneaker. Lululemon's recent men's send at least put a guy in actual layers.
- "$80 and under" isn't a discount. It's a price ceiling. I can't tell whether anything is marked down or whether these are just cheaper shoes.
- The black "Explore the latest adidas best sellers" banner competes with the hero, and its SHOP NOW button points at a different goal than the two buttons below. That's three CTAs fighting each other.
- Copy like "You read that right" and "made for gifting all season long" is filler. There's no model name, no price, and no size info. I don't know what the shoes are.
- No adiClub or member benefit shows up. Compare the 10/24 "We've got something for you" loyalty send, where adidas at least acknowledged I'm a member.

## Recommendations

- 1. Name the shoes in the hero and put a price on each. "Gazelle Indoor, $80" gives me something to click. Right now the image is just mood.
- 2. Add a running row. Even one Duramo or Supernova under $80 would make this feel like it knows runners. Nike sends me a one-time code that does its job in under a minute, and this email does less than that for a runner.
- 3. Cut the banner at the top, or make it the only CTA. Pick one path.
- 4. Add Shop Kids, since the copy says "entire family" and the buttons don't back that up. Also state the end date and whether the price cap is a real markdown.
- 5. Move the "running to streetwear" line out of the gray preheader and into the headline area.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML is a conventional table-based ESP template (Salesforce Marketing Cloud-style `image.link.adidas.com` and `click.comms.adidas.com` hosts) with a responsive `@media` setup. Its confirmed defects are cleartext `http://` image sources, missing alt text, and a viewport meta tag without `width=device-width`. The compliance and authentication checks could not be verified because of relay capture limits.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **17 tracking links were not probed.** QA skipped them as click-redirect domains, so destination status, redirect chains, and final URLs are unverified.
- **Cleartext tracking image.** `http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/010201a17a41a6e6-a21c51e5-.../RrgldzL8ARIho-0mohJ-cTMzivBtRQ21lmKKaLbs2n4=452` loads over `http://`. It has no alt text and looks like the open-tracking pixel. Gmail and Apple Mail proxy images, but a client that blocks mixed or non-HTTPS content will drop it, so open tracking will under-count.
- **Duplicate `http://` asset, four times.** The `arrow` image `http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg` is referenced four times. The same asset is likely available over HTTPS on the same CDN.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text.**
  - `https://dv.adidas.com/o/680c0b40-56b6-487e-b45b-eacd6d7c7ed8?cp_tp=...`
  - The tracking image above (`...n4=452`)
  - Both carry no alt, so with images off the content is unlabeled. If the first is a content or CTA image, it is also inaccessible to screen readers. The tracking pixel should use `alt=""`.
- **Viewport meta.** `<meta name="viewport" content="initial-scale=1.0">` has no `width=device-width`. Some mobile clients may not scale as intended. The `@media` rules still work in most clients that honor them.
- **Plain-text part.** QA reports the text version as 0 chars (**FAIL**). This could be a relay capture artifact rather than a missing part, so confirm against the raw MIME. If it is real, the email is HTML-only (spam-score penalty, no fallback for text-only clients).
- **Fonts.** The `AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` `@font-face` rules use only `src: local('Arial'), local('Helvetica'), ...`. No web font file is loaded, so every client renders system fallbacks. This is harmless but redundant.
- **Mixed-content risk.** Items under section 2 apply here too: a mixture of `http://` and `https://` image sources.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. The truncated HTML shows no unresolved tokens (`%%...%%`, `{{...}}`, `[FIRSTNAME]`). The truncated source cannot rule out tokens further down.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)

- **Authentication-Results** is absent from the relay, so SPF, DKIM, and DMARC status is unknown. The `us-news.comms.adidas.com` sender subdomain is aligned with the brand domain, but no pass or fail can be reported.
- **Footer unsubscribe link and physical address.** The truncated source does not show them, so I can't confirm either.
