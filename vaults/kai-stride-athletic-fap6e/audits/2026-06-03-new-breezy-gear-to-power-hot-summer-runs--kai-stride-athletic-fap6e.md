# New, breezy gear to power hot summer runs
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-03**
## Executive summary

- Three consecutive days, three emails from lululemon — and the pattern is becoming a habit. "Outpace the heat." is legitimately strong hero copy. When I read that in the subject and see it in the email, I'm briefly in the right headspace: hot morning, long run, need gear that doesn't turn into a sweat sponge by mile three. Then I scroll two blocks down and hit "Shop Womens Run >" twice in a row. That's not a creative miss. That's a segmentation failure.
- The June 1 Father's Day guide was the most demographically coherent thing lululemon had sent me in weeks — all men, real athletic products, stuff I'd legitimately consider buying. Yesterday's Nulu email (June 2) reverted to form, and now today feels like the same playbook: lead with running energy, then hand me a women's catalog. The Split Shift shoe section redeems some of it — "forefoot rocker" and "responsive cushioning" are words I actually care about — and the "Shop new styles this week" block finally surfaces what looks like a male figure in a blue tank. But by that point I've already moved my thumb toward the back button.
- I'd click the shoes section if it's a road trainer. I would not click the hero CTA. I would not forward this to anyone in my running club.

## What's working

- **"Outpace the heat."** is the best three words in this email — punchy, seasonal, and specific to running in summer conditions
- **Performance copy is legit**: "mesh kits and flow," "open-knit zones," "forefoot rocker" — lululemon is writing for someone who actually runs, not just wears the gear to brunch
- **Layout is clean**: no render bugs, clear product-block hierarchy, nothing overlapping or broken
- **Summer timing is right**: mid-June heat training is a real moment; this email exists at the correct point in the season

## What's weak

- **"Shop Womens Run >"** appears twice, back-to-back — as a male subscriber this is the email equivalent of clicking the wrong restroom door and then doing it again immediately
- **"Shop What's New" CTA** is a cop-out; after five lululemon emails in a week I still can't find a specific men's product from a single email click
- **No price, no drop time, no scarcity** — there's nothing that separates "I'll look later" from "I should look now"
- **Cadence fatigue is real**: this is at least the fifth send in seven days from this brand; the June 1 Father's Day email was good enough to buy goodwill, but that account is close to empty
- adidas has been blasting me weekly too (Summer Sale, Anthony Edwards, World Cup gear) and even their generic 40%-off send had a clearer CTA — "Score big on the best gear" at least told me why to click; "Shop What's New" tells me nothing

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment women's product blocks out of male subscriber sends.** The Textured Mesh Running Short and Fast and Free Wrap Speed Tank should never be the first two product sections a male runner sees. Swap them for men's equivalents — Surge Short, Pace Breaker, whatever launched this week.
- 2. **Name a specific product in the hero CTA.** "Shop the Surge Short" beats "Shop What's New" every time. Nike does this in launch mode and it earns the click.
- 3. **Give the hero an urgency signal.** "Just dropped" with a date, "limited colorways," or a 48-hour window converts "maybe later" to "right now." adidas does time-bounded urgency with their voucher sends (e.g., the "⏳ Last day to redeem" send I saw last week) and it works because it feels personalized and finite.
- 4. **Pull the Split Shift shoe section to position two.** It's the most gender-neutral and product-specific block in the email — it should be the first product section I see after the hero, not the fourth.
- 5. **Thin the cadence or consolidate.** Five sends in a week from one brand — batch the women's sends separately, or ship one well-targeted men's send instead of three mixed ones.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Off-spec color values — low risk, intentional:**
- `.black` = `#000001` (not `#000000`) and `.white` = `#fdfeff` (not `#ffffff`) — a known near-black/near-white trick to suppress dark mode color inversion in some clients. Functionally acceptable, but the `#fdfeff`-on-`#000001` pairing should be verified against WCAG AA (4.5:1 minimum); the 1-step offset does not meaningfully affect contrast, but the pair should be spot-checked.

**Heading selector inconsistency:**
- `h1, h2, h3, h5` are grouped in one rule; `h4` is styled separately with `Saans-Light` fallback. `h5` also picks up the grouped rule, so if any `<h5>` element is rendered, it inherits `letter-spacing: -1px` from the group — which is likely intended, but the grouping excludes `h4` without comment.

**Custom font fallback chain — acceptable:**
- `'Saans-Regular', Helvetica Neue, sans-serif` and `'KHGiga-Light', 'Times New Roman', serif` are correctly defined. Since most email clients block web fonts, rendering will fall back to system fonts; no functional issue.
