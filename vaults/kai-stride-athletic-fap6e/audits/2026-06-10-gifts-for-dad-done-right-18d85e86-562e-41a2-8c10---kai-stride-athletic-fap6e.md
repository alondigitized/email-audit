# Gifts for Dad, done right
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-10**
## Executive summary

- So lululemon comes back nine days after the June 1 Father's Day guide — which I genuinely liked — and swings a second time with Jordan and Cali Clarkson as the hook. I respect the athlete angle. The orange basketball-court aesthetic punches harder visually than anything else in my recent inbox, including adidas' generic "up to 40% off" sale blast from last week. Lending a current NBA player to the creative is something Nike would never do with a competitor, and the result actually reads as sports-serious.
- But here's the issue: I run. The "playtime into overtime" copy and the NBA endorsement are basketball language. The gear visible in the frames is lifestyle-forward — athleisure that could work at a coffee shop, not a tempo run. When lululemon sent me "Outpace the heat." on June 3, I felt seen. This one treats me like a dad who coaches youth rec-league ball. The "Father's Day in the New Parent Bag" section halfway through is a hard demographic pivot into a life stage I'm not at yet.
- I'll click to browse — lululemon men's product is reliably good and it's five days before Father's Day — but I'm not forwarding this to my running group. Mark as read with a side scroll.

## What's working

- **Jordan Clarkson is a real cultural signal.** A current NBA player, not a ghost-written "athlete," gives this credibility adidas would've spent on a 40%-off banner instead.
- **All-male, athletic-build models throughout.** Consistent with the June 1 guide that scored an 8 — they held the demographic line.
- **Orange basketball-court hero image.** Visual hierarchy is immediate. The eye lands on the court before it lands on anything else.
- **"Father's Day in the New Parent Bag" is a genuinely creative gifting angle.** A bag as a Father's Day gift isn't predictable — it's the freshest idea in the scroll.
- **Clean render, no bugs.** Everything loaded sharp. Compare this to Nike's transactional "one-time code" email that did one job and left — lululemon at least built a visual world here.

## What's weak

- **Zero running DNA.** I'm a lululemon customer specifically for run gear — Surge shorts, Pace Breaker, Swift. None of that is present. adidas' sale blast was also generic, but it wasn't actively repping a different sport.
- **No offer, no urgency hook.** No discount, no member pricing, no delivery deadline. The June 1 send had the same problem. Two Father's Day emails in a row with no reason to act today vs. next week.
- **"Playtime into overtime" is basketball copy, not run copy.** It's actually good writing — just aimed at someone else.
- **The New Parent Bag section loses me mid-scroll.** That's a different buyer. Smart product, wrong audience segment for me.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a delivery deadline.** Father's Day is five days out — say it. "Order by Thursday for guaranteed delivery" turns a browse into a decision.
- 2. **Add one run-specific row.** Keep the Clarkson angle, but give me a section: "What Dad runs in." Surge shorts, a run vest, a hat. Nike segments their Father's Day send by sport; lululemon should too.
- 3. **Rewrite the primary CTA.** "Shop What's New" is filler. "Build the Kit" or "Find Dad's Gift" earns the tap.
- 4. **Rethink the basketball-court set.** It competes directly in Nike Jordan territory where lululemon doesn't win. The road, the trail, the studio — those are lululemon's lanes.
