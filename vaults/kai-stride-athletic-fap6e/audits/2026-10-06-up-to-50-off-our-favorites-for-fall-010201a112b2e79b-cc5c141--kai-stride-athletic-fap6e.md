# Up to 50% off our favorites for fall
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-06**
## Executive summary

- Sixth adidas email in about a week, and for the sixth time nothing in it is for a runner. Pikachu on 9/30, cow-print Samba on 10/2, the Chicago running email on 10/3, rhinestone SL72 on 10/4, the Hellstar football drop yesterday. Now a plaid-textured banner that says "ADIDAS FAVORITES" and a discount with no product attached. I captain a running club. I can't tell what "favorites" means here, and a plaid hero doesn't point me toward a long-run shoe.
- The 50% off is clear, "no code needed" is a nice honest touch, and the layout is clean. But there's nothing to look at. I don't see one shoe, one jacket, or one price. Nike at least gives me a Pegasus or a member price to react to. lululemon tells me which fabric I'm feeling, even if it's seminar-speak. This email is only a coupon with a logo on it.
- Would I click? Maybe, on a slow Sunday, to see whether the Adizero line is in the sale. Would I forward it to the club? No. I'd mark it read.

## What's working

- "UP TO 50% OFF FOR FALL" is big, plain and impossible to miss. I know the offer in one second.
- "No code needed" removes the scavenger hunt that other brands make me do.
- The three black buttons are large and easy to tap with a thumb.
- The layout is clean and uncluttered, with no broken images or overlapping text.
- The seasonal framing fits early October.

## What's weak

- There isn't a single product in the whole email. "Fan fave pieces" doesn't tell me what's discounted.
- No running content at all. The 10/3 Chicago email showed the brand can speak to runners, and this one doesn't try.
- "Shop Men / Women / Kids" is a sort-by-demographic menu. It doesn't help me find anything.
- The hero is moody plaid with wide-spaced lettering. It reads as lifestyle fashion, not performance.
- "Up to 50%" gives no sense of how deep the cuts are on the stuff I'd buy. There's no deadline either, so no reason to act today.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put two or three real products with sale prices in the body. Add an Adizero Boston or Evo SL tile for runners, with "was / now" prices. That one change would lift this email more than anything else.
- 2. Add a deadline, like "ends Sunday", so there's a reason to click now.
- 3. Replace Men/Women/Kids with category buttons: "Shop running", "Shop jackets", "Shop sale shoes".
- 4. Segment by purchase history. Someone who clicked the Chicago send should get running sale items, not the generic blast.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Tracking links unverified:** 21 links were skipped as click-redirect domains, so none were HTTP-probed. Destination validity, redirect chains and expiry are unconfirmed.
- **Insecure image sources:**
  - The "arrow" image is loaded over HTTP six times: `http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg`.
  - A second `http://` image is served from `http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/010201a112b2e79b-.../qgLw4Qp7LyjB3M_7vq48ntTkQnLlczYk5R8II2OzFag=452`.
  - Clients that block or upgrade non-HTTPS sources may fail to render these.
- **Likely open pixel (inference):** The `click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/...=452` image has no alt text and sits on the tracking domain. Its URL pattern is consistent with an open-tracking pixel, but the truncated source doesn't confirm this. If it is one, it is the only open-tracking element and is sent over plain HTTP.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Plain-text fallback missing (QA FAIL):** The text version is 0 chars. This hurts deliverability, text-only clients and accessibility tooling.
- **Missing alt text (QA WARN):** `dv.adidas.com/o/05720a1d-60b8-4475-93c2-e89be7147abf?cp_tp=...` has no alt attribute. The `click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/...=452` image has none either, though it is probably a non-content pixel.
