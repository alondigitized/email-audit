# In new Nulu™ fabric sets, summer's on
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-02**
## Executive summary

- Yesterday's Father's Day guide was the most demographically aware thing lululemon had sent me in weeks — actual men, actual athletic gear, products I'd legitimately buy. Twenty-four hours later and we're back to this: three modules of women in yoga poses talking about "finding their flow." The hero is a woman in a pink sports bra. Module two is another woman in white studio wear. Module three is a woman in a breezy jacket. The shop grid at the bottom is dresses and tanks in hot pink and orange. I'm a 33-year-old guy who runs 40-mile weeks. None of this is for me.
- To lululemon's credit, the email itself is clean. No render bugs, solid hierarchy, the Nulu™ story is told coherently across three sections. If I were a woman who does yoga and barre, this probably lands nicely. But I'm not, and the signal-to-noise problem from the Breezily Shorts email on May 26 hasn't been fixed — that was the one where every single module was women's wide-leg pants. We did one detour into Father's Day territory and now we're right back.
- I'm marking this read and moving on. Wouldn't click, wouldn't forward — maybe to my girlfriend if she's into this stuff.

## What's working

- **Visual hierarchy is clean** — the three-module editorial flow (hero stretch, studio support, breathable everyday) reads like a coherent brand story, not a cluttered promo blast.
- **Nulu™ fabric story is communicated well** — "buttery-soft," "nearly weightless," "naturally breathable" are concrete fabric claims, not vague puffery.
- **No render bugs** — images load, text doesn't overlap, the shop grid at the bottom renders correctly. Technically well-executed send.
- **Seasonal relevance** — "summer's on" is timely, and the warm-palette, outdoor shots feel right for early June.

## What's weak

- **Every single model is a woman in yoga/studio settings** — as a male subscriber who runs, this might as well be a Loft or Free People email. The Father's Day send (June 1) proved they have men's content; why is none of it in rotation for me?
- **Zero running content** — no Pace Breaker, no ABC Short restock tease, no mention of the run category. Nike's drip sequence would never let a week pass without touching my actual use case.
- **"Shop Now" is the laziest CTA in email marketing** — adidas at least writes "Shop the Jersey" or "Redeem Your Voucher." Here it's three identical "Shop Now >" links with no differentiation.
- **No offer, no price signal, no urgency** — "Shop new styles this week" isn't a reason to click. The May 30 restock email at least implied scarcity. This one is just vibes.
- **The shop grid looks like women's dresses** — the color palette (hot pink, tangerine orange) and silhouettes read women's summer dresses, not performance gear. Reinforces that nothing here is for me.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment me out of women's-only sends** — this is the highest-leverage fix. If I've been browsing men's Surge Tights and ABC Shorts, there's zero excuse for three consecutive modules of women's yoga content. Nike personalizes by last click; lululemon should too.
- 2. **Add one men's module** — even a single mid-email row of "New in Nulu for him" with the men's Metal Vent Tech or T.H.E. Short would salvage this. The fabric story is genuinely good; translate it to the men's line.
- 3. **Write specific CTAs** — "Shop Nulu Sets" or "Find Your Fit" over "Shop Now >" every time.
- 4. **Introduce a light urgency mechanic** — "New this week, restocking fast" or similar. The May 30 restock email used implied scarcity effectively; carry that forward.
- 5. **Preheader needs work** — whatever is showing in inbox preview should complement the subject, not re-state or leak junk.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New Nulu™ sets just dropped — made for summer`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Buttery-soft, barely there: meet Nulu™`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Weightless fabric, built for wherever summer takes you`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Three layers. Zero compromise. Shop the new Nulu drop.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Non-standard font-weight values** — `font-weight: 380` and `font-weight: 570` appear across multiple selectors (`p`, `ul`, `ol`, `li`, `h1–h5`, `.eyebrow`, `.product-callout`). CSS Fonts Level 4 permits numeric values 1–1000, but values outside the standard 100-increment ladder (100, 200, 300…) are silently snapped to the nearest supported weight by most email clients. In practice, `380` will render as `400` (normal) and `570` as `600` (semi-bold) in Outlook, Gmail, and Apple Mail — likely the intended result, but the code implies precision that doesn't exist.

**Heading hierarchy gap** — `h3` and `h5` share identical computed styles (`font-size: 30px`, `line-height: 112%`, `letter-spacing: -1px`). `h4` is defined in a separate ruleset with the same values but uses `font-family: 'Saans-Light'` instead of `'Saans-Regular'`. The h3/h5 duplication suggests a copy-paste artifact or an h4 skip in the template system; screen readers will announce the level difference even though they visually match.

**`lang` attribute present** — `<html lang="en" dir="ltr">` is correctly set. No issues.

**Dark mode implementation** — Three separate `<style>` blocks target Gmail (`u + .body`), Outlook.com (`:root`/`[class~="x_*"]`), and the Outlook Android app (`\0` hack). Coverage appears intentional and complete. `color-scheme: light dark` declared in both `<meta>` and `:root`.
