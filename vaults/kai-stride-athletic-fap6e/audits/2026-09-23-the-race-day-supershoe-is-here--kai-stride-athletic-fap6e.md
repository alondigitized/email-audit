# The race-day supershoe is here
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-23**
## Executive summary

- Finally. After Care Bears, a soccer tracksuit, an Anthony Edwards basketball drop, and a Bad Bunny F50 — adidas actually sent me a running shoe email. The Adizero Adios Pro 5 is a legit race-day supershoe, the mid-stride shot of the carbon plate flexing is genuinely great running photography, and the copy leads with real specs: 9% more energy return, 23g lighter, carbon-infused ENERGYRIM, LIGHTSTRIKE PRO foam. That's the performance vocabulary I actually want from a running brand.
- That said, I'm not sprinting to check out. There's no price, no release date, no sizing/availability info, and nothing about whether this is even available in my size before race season. It reads like a product announcement, not a shopping email — which is fine for awareness, but I need more to click "Shop Now" and not just bounce to a product page with no stock.
- I'd read this one all the way through on my commute, unlike the last five adidas sends I've deleted after two seconds. Would I forward it to my running club group chat? Maybe — if I first confirm it's actually in stock in my size, since I'm not going to hype a shoe that's sold out by the time someone clicks.

## What's working

- Actual running content — the hero product is a marathon racing shoe, not a lifestyle sneaker or another sport's jersey.
- Real performance specs in the copy (energy return %, weight drop, foam/plate tech) instead of vague lifestyle language.
- The photography earns its space — mid-toe-off shot with visible carbon plate flex is exactly the kind of image a runner stops scrolling for.
- Clean, uncluttered layout — one hero, one headline, one CTA, no clutter competing for attention.
- "Race day" framing speaks directly to someone training for one, which is basically my whole running club right now.

## What's weak

- Zero price. I want to know if this is a $250 shoe before I click through.
- No release/drop date or "available now" language — is this preorder hype or can I buy it today?
- No sizing or stock signal at all, and adidas has burned me before with drop-hype emails that lead to sold-out sizes.
- No social proof — no "as worn by [elite marathoner]" or race results, which is the kind of credibility Nike leans on hard with its Vaporfly/Alphafly messaging.
- Generic "Explore the latest adidas best sellers" top banner undercuts the race-day focus — that's a completely different intent than someone hunting a marathon supershoe.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add the price and a stock/availability line right under the headline — "Available now, $XXX" or "Ships [date]" removes the single biggest friction point for me clicking through.
- 2. Cut the top "best sellers" banner entirely — it dilutes a genuinely on-target send by pointing me somewhere unrelated before I even reach the shoe.
- 3. Add one line of proof — a marathon time, a pro runner's name, or "as raced at [Marathon]" — Nike does this well with athlete tie-ins and it would close the trust gap here.
- 4. Segment this send to people who've bought running shoes before (like me) vs. the lifestyle/Superstar crowd who got Care Bears — this shoe deserves its own dedicated performance audience, not a blast to everyone.
- 5. Given the club angle, a line like "reserve your size" with real inventory urgency would push me from "I'll look later" to "I'm clicking now."
