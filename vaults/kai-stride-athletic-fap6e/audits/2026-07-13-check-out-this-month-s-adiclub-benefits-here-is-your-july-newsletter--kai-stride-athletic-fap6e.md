# Check out this month's adiClub benefits, here is your July Newsletter
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-13**
## Executive summary

- Monthly newsletter energy, zero running relevance. I opened this as an adiClub member and found myself looking at a World Cup match ball giveaway and a signed Adizero soccer item — two things I genuinely do not care about. The word "Adizero" caught me for a split second because that's the line that gave us the Adios Pro and the Dropset Pro (the one I forwarded to my running club eight days ago). That catch lasted maybe three seconds before I realized it was a signed soccer cleat sitting in a display case, not a race shoe. Classic bait-and-switch on a brand name I trust.
- The production quality here is fine. adidas knows how to build a clean email — hierarchy is readable, the CTAs are visible, nothing is broken. But clean execution in service of content that doesn't apply to me is still a miss. I'm a dues-paying adiClub member and a runner. The only nod to that identity in this entire email is the adiClub badge in the header. Beyond that, it's the World Cup channel in newsletter clothing.
- Marking this read and moving on. I won't click, I won't forward it to the club.

## What's working

- Brand recognition is instant — the three-stripe logo and adiClub framing load cleanly and signal immediately that this is my loyalty account talking.
- Visual hierarchy is solid: match ball giveaway is the undisputed hero, CTAs (JOIN NOW, ENTER NOW, DOWNLOAD THE APP) are all easy to find.
- No render bugs anywhere — crisp layout, images loaded, nothing overlapping.
- The "STAY IN THE KNOW" recap is a smart loyalty angle — reminding me what I missed is more useful than a raw promo push.
- Monthly cadence feels appropriate; this doesn't feel like the fourth identical blast this week the way lululemon's Summer Sale stretch did (five emails in six days, just swapping the angle each time).

## What's weak

- Total soccer monoculture — match ball giveaway, Road To Glory cleats, FIFA tickets, adidas x Prime soccer gear — not a single running touchpoint in the entire email.
- "Adizero" in the giveaway headline reads like a running cue, then immediately delivers a signed soccer item. That pivot is jarring when I associate Adizero with the Adios Pro and the Dropset Pro.
- The subject line is 70+ characters — on mobile it's getting brutally truncated before it gets to anything specific. It reads like someone wrote two subject lines and forgot to pick one.
- No real personalization anywhere. "YOUR adiClub NEWSLETTER" uses YOUR in all-caps but it's clearly the same for every subscriber. Hollow for a loyalty-tier send.
- The preheader is just the top banner escaping — "Explore the latest adidas best sellers" — which has nothing to do with the newsletter content below it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment by persona** — running subscribers get a version where at least one module features a current running release or training-relevant content. The Dropset Pro email from 7/5 proved adidas CAN write for runners when they try; one module of that energy belongs here.
- 2. **Cut the subject in half and make it tease the actual win:**
- **Subject Alt A:** `Win a FIFA match ball — your July adiClub perks`
- **Subject Alt B:** `July perks live: match ball drop + Adizero history`
- 3. **Write a dedicated preheader — stop letting the top bar bleed in:**
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Match ball giveaway + Adizero history drop — members only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Two FIFA giveaways, zero cost to enter. Your July perks.`
- 4. **Make the Adizero module earn its name** — for running subscribers, "Adizero: Win a piece of history" should link to a race shoe or training gear, not a signed soccer cleat. The brand name is doing real work for runners; don't waste it.
- 5. **Show me my loyalty balance** — I've been a member long enough to have points. Put my tier status or point total somewhere in this email instead of generic "here's what you missed" copy. Even Nike's transactional one-time-code email felt more personalized than this.

## Technical Audit — adidas adiClub July Newsletter

### 1. Technical Summary

The email scores a 38% QA pass rate, driven by two hard failures: a broken click-tracking redirect and a missing plain-text fallback. Several HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources and missing RFC 8058 unsubscribe headers compound the risk profile.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — Broken click redirect**
The QA resolver returned `unknown url type: '/g/f0732c31-784c-42ac-80a3-e92ae2da76bf?txn=2383fd00-7ebc-11f1-a9f3-4ec26c495bcf'` for the following tracked URL:

```
https://dv.adidas.com/o/f0732c31-784c-42ac-80a3-e92ae2da76bf?cp_tp=v3.eJzj...
```

The ESP's redirect chain is resolving to a relative path (`/g/...`) rather than an absolute destination URL. Any subscriber clicking this link lands on a dead end.

**WARN — 26 tracking links unverifiable**
All outbound links are wrapped in click-redirect domains (`click.comms.adidas.com`, `dv.adidas.com`). The checker skipped HTTP probing on these, so destination URL correctness cannot be confirmed programmatically.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**WARN — 4× HTTP image source (same asset)**
The arrow icon (`b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg`) is embedded four times via `http://`:

```
http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```

Gmail and Apple Mail block mixed-content images by default. All four instances will likely render as broken images.

**WARN — 2 images missing `alt` text**

| Image | src fragment |

| `f0732c31-784c-42ac-80a3-e92ae2da76bf` (linked image) | `https://dv.adidas.com/o/f0732c31-...` |

**INFO — Custom fonts not loaded remotely**

### 5. Compliance

**FAIL — Plain-text alternative is 0 characters**
The `text/plain` MIME part is empty. CAN-SPAM and general ESP best-practice require a readable text fallback. Some spam filters penalize HTML-only messages.

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe` header unconfirmed**
The AgentMail relay did not surface this header. If it is present at the ESP but stripped by the relay, this is a relay configuration issue. If it is genuinely absent, Gmail and Yahoo (as of February 2024 bulk sender requirements) will suppress the native unsubscribe UI.

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) absent**
One-click unsubscribe — required by Gmail/Yahoo for bulk senders — depends on both `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` being present. Neither is confirmed.

**WARN — SPF/DKIM status unknown**
`Authentication-Results` header not captured. Cannot confirm DKIM signing by `us-news.comms.adidas.com` or SPF alignment for the `adidas.com` domain. Needs verification at the raw SMTP level.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**WARN — `no-referrer` meta suppresses HTTP Referer on all clicks**
```html
<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">
```
This strips the `Referer` header from every outbound link click. Any server-side referral attribution relying on the HTTP Referer (rather than UTM parameters) will be blind to email as the source.

**UTM coverage unverifiable**
With 26 tracking links behind redirect domains that were skipped, UTM parameter presence on final destination URLs cannot be confirmed. The one broken redirect (`/g/f0732c31-...`) guarantees at least one destination receives zero UTM-attributed traffic.
