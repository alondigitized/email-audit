# $50 gift cards are only $40
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-26**
## Executive summary

- A gift card promo — that's it. "Buy a $50 gift card for $40" is a straightforward 20% off, no shoe, no gear, no runner in sight until the World Cup jersey model shows up further down, and even that's a soccer kit, not a running silhouette. This is the fifth adidas email I've reviewed in two weeks and none of them have actually shown me a running shoe. Compare to the Billie Jean King send from yesterday (2026-08-25) which at least had heritage and a real athlete story — this one is just a finance mechanic dressed up in a jersey-texture hero.
- The "Own a Piece of History" block with a signed Yamal match ball sweepstakes is a decent hook if you're a soccer fan, but I run a club, I don't play pickup football. It's not irrelevant to adidas as a brand, but it's irrelevant to me specifically.
- Would I click? No. Would I forward it? No — unless a teammate happens to be shopping gift cards for someone else. I'd mark this read and move on, same as I did with the Samba back-to-school email on 8/23.

## What's working

- The offer itself is crystal clear — "$50 gift card for $40" is unambiguous math, no mental gymnastics required.
- Two distinct, well-labeled CTAs (SHOP NOW, ENTER NOW) — I know exactly what each button does.
- The Yamal signed match ball giveaway is genuinely exciting IP if you follow the sport — real athlete, real prize, not vague hype copy.
- adiClub/FIFA Rewards gating on the sweepstakes signals they know I'm a member, which is a nice touch — Nike's one-time-code email did something similar with recognizing my member status.

## What's weak

- Zero running content — no shoe, no pace, no club angle, nothing that says "this brand knows I run long on Sundays." Lululemon's tennis email at least showed a racket and real motion; this is a gift card and a soccer jersey.
- The hero model is in full kit holding a soccer ball on a hot pink backdrop — cool photography, wrong sport for me.
- A gift card is the least personal offer adidas can send — it's not a product, it's a coupon for buying a coupon. Feels like a margin play more than a "we thought about you" send.
- The fine print block at the bottom is a wall of legal text (4 gift card limit, no combining offers, no retail redemption) — buried enough that I'd never read it, but it undercuts the "simple deal" framing up top.
- This is back-to-back generic-offer energy after the 8/21 "Save up to 40% off" blast — cadence is getting repetitive, two money-off sends in five days with no running gear between them.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment this — if adidas knows I'm a running-club captain from my purchase history, don't send me a soccer-kit gift card promo; send me the gift card offer paired with a running shoe I'd actually spend it on.
- 2. Move the Yamal giveaway higher or make it the lead story — it's more emotionally interesting than a payment mechanic and would pull me in further.
- 3. Trim the legal fine print or summarize the top 2 restrictions (4-card limit, no combining) near the CTA instead of a six-line paragraph at the bottom.
- 4. Show me what I could actually buy with the $40 — a running shoe thumbnail next to "here's what $40 gets you" would close the loop between offer and product.
