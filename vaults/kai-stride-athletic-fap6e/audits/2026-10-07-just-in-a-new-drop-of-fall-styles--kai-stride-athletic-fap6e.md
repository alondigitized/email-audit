# Just in: a new drop of fall styles
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-07**
## Executive summary

- This is the second ballet-studio email from lululemon in about two weeks. On 9/22 it was "Dance Studio Pants loosen up." Now it's Lip J. in a barre studio, stretching in a quilted jacket and wide-leg pants. It's a good-looking email. But I run a club long run, and I can't find a single stride in it. Everyone is posed against a radiator, mid-stretch.
- The copy is the usual seminar voice: "A new baseline," "Anything but routine," "limitless potential." That's lululemon's tone, and I'd rather get Nike's hype than that. Nothing here tells me what the gear does when I'm 14 miles in. No price, no offer, no product named in a button. The one real thing is a quote, "It is housing me. I live here. I love." That's about a halter tank.
- Would I click? No. Would I forward it? Only to my partner, who wears Align. For me it's a mark-as-read. It's not as bad as the 9/29 Smooth Spacer email, because the photography is genuinely strong. But the 10/6 puffer email with Lewis Hamilton had an actual face and an actual athlete. This one has a model and a mood.

## What's working

- The hero is striking. A quilted puffer, a tight crop and a clean layout make the eye land on the top image first.
- The Lip J. quote is a real human voice. It's the best line in the email.
- Product captions under the Groove Wide-Leg Pant and the Featherweight Down Quilted Jacket name what I'm looking at.
- The layout is tidy. I saw no broken images or overlapping text, and the type is readable without zooming.
- "Fall 2026: Drop 02" gives it a drop-style hook and a real season marker.

## What's weak

- Nothing in it is for a runner. It's all studio, stretch and lounge. The Dance Studio Pants email on 9/22 had the same problem.
- There's no offer, price or member benefit. I'm a member and it treats me like a stranger.
- The email is very long, and the CTAs are generic: "Shop Fall 2026," "Shop the Look," "Explore Fall 2026: Drop 02." Those are verbs with no product attached.
- The copy is vague ("limitless potential," "new levels of ease"). It tells me nothing about fabric, fit or performance.
- The photos are mostly women. The one male-adjacent beat I got on 9/23 with the golf email isn't here.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with one product and a reason to buy it. Put the jacket in the hero with its name and price. If it's lightweight and packable, say that, and say whether it works for cold-morning warm-ups.
- 2. Cut the length by half. Two story beats and one grid is enough.
- 3. Make the CTAs specific, like "Shop the Featherweight Down Jacket."
- 4. Add one run-relevant moment, like a warm-up layer before the group run. Nike and adidas at least pretend to know I run. adidas sent me a Chicago running email on 10/3, even if the other five that week weren't for runners.
- 5. Show member perks. adidas at least sent a loyalty email, "We've got something for you," even if the points didn't get me anything.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- **Font weights:** `font-weight:380`, `570` and `300` are variable-font values. Clients that don't load the custom fonts will fall back to Helvetica Neue, Arial or Times New Roman at normal or bold weight. The fallback stacks are present, so this is informational.
