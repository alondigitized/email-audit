# The adidas x Mercedes-AMG PETRONAS F1 Collection 🏁
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- Fourth email from adidas in five days, and this time they're serving me Formula 1 fashion. I run 50-plus miles a week, I captain a running club, and adidas just sent me a motorsport lifestyle drop. The sequencing here is almost impressive in how consistently it misses — welcome email, loyalty explainer, app download push, and now a collab with a racing team. Zero running content across four consecutive touches.
- That said, the email itself is clean. The F1 hero renders well, the "HEARTBEAT OF RACEDAY" headline lands with real energy, and the SHOP NOW button is impossible to miss. They even tucked in an adiClub x Calm section with a "story runs" mention — that's the one runner-relevant signal in the entire email and it's buried in the second half.
- I'd mark this as read and move on. I would not click. I would not forward to my running club, because none of them are shopping F1 team kits. The Calm partnership is mildly interesting for recovery runs but not enough to rescue an email built around a collab I have no context for.

## What's working

- **Hero hierarchy is sharp.** "THE HEARTBEAT OF RACEDAY" is bold, uppercase, and fills the frame — I landed on the offer area immediately.
- **Render is flawless.** No broken images, no overflow, no layout weirdness — cleaner than lululemon's tennis send from earlier this week which felt sloppy at the edges.
- **The Calm x adiClub section mentions story runs.** Buried, but "story runs" is the first running-specific language adidas has sent me across four consecutive emails — I noticed it.
- **CTA buttons are unambiguous.** "SHOP NOW" and "EXPLORE NOW" both have direction and a clear tap target. Nike's one-time-code email was in-and-out clean for a different reason, but adidas at least didn't hide the action.

## What's weak

- **Zero running signal in a running-segment send.** I'm presumably in adidas's adiClub base as a buyer of Ultraboosts or Adizero, and the brand still hasn't served me a single running-focused send. Lululemon is doing the same thing — five emails, none about running — so at least adidas has company at the bottom.
- **Two-section email splits attention and dilutes both.** F1 lifestyle drop and a meditation app partnership do not belong in the same send. I don't know what to do with this email.
- **No price, no offer, no product spec.** "Engineered to compete at the highest level" tells me nothing I can act on. What's the jacket cost? Is the kit available in my size? Nike at least tells me when drops are limited.
- **Fourth email in five days, second email on the same day.** The cadence has crossed into noise territory. I already got an app-push email from adidas earlier today.
- **F1 models signal motorsport, not running.** The hero models are in race-kit styling — male, yes, but the wrong sport entirely for where adidas presumably has me segmented.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment the file and send me a running email.** If adidas knows I bought Ultraboosts or Adizero, this send should never have hit my inbox. One correct running send beats four wrong ones.
- 2. **Lead the Calm section with "story runs" not meditation.** If the recovery/wellness angle is the play, "Run smarter with adidas + Calm" is a runner hook. Burying it under "stay calm and focused" reads as a fitness-agnostic wellness pitch.
- 3. **Kill the two-section split or give each section its own email.** F1 collab and Calm partnership are two separate campaigns. Pick one per send.
- 4. **Add product specifics above the fold.** Price, silhouette name, key feature — something I can evaluate without clicking. Nike does this on drop emails; adidas should too.
- 5. **Throttle the cadence.** Two emails in one day from the same brand is aggressive. The 15% welcome voucher from 5/15 is still unredeemed for a reason — more pressure doesn't close that gap.

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has two hard failures — a broken dynamic image URL and a missing plain-text fallback — plus recurring mixed-content (HTTP) image warnings and unverifiable compliance headers due to relay capture gaps. Overall QA pass rate is 38%.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken dynamic image URL**
The `dv.adidas.com` personalization endpoint is referenced with a relative path type in QA:
```
unknown url type: '/g/7e07fb28-d2a5-4524-877f-17f4157e1d90'
```
The actual `src` resolves as a full `https://dv.adidas.com/o/...` URL with a long `cp_tp` token, but the QA checker detected an internal path alias (`/g/` vs `/o/`) mismatch — likely a template variable that rendered incorrectly for this recipient. If `dv.adidas.com` returns a broken image for any cohort, the hero/personalized image will be blank.

**[WARN] 24 tracking links skipped** — all click-redirect links routed through `click.comms.adidas.com`. Not a defect, but destination URLs are unverifiable from the raw source.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] 4× HTTP image asset (same file, repeated)**
```
src: http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```
This arrow icon is sourced over HTTP, not HTTPS. Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail all block mixed-content images by default. The asset will silently fail to load on most modern clients. The same `src` appears 4 times (same arrow reused across CTAs).

**[WARN] Additional HTTP image**

**[WARN] Missing `alt` text on 2 images**

- `h7ZZTaZogE6f5N_hTEb_g6d-6...` (the HTTP click-tracker image)

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header not captured**
The QA relay did not surface a `List-Unsubscribe` header. This is likely a relay capture gap rather than a true absence (SendGrid/Salesforce MC injects these at the SMTP envelope level), but it cannot be confirmed from available data. If missing from the actual delivered message, this violates CAN-SPAM §5(a)(3) and Gmail's February 2024 bulk sender mandate.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not captured**
RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe header unverifiable for the same reason. Gmail and Yahoo require this for senders at >5k/day volume.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header not captured**
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status unknown via relay. Domain `us-news.comms.adidas.com` is a subdomain — DMARC alignment depends on whether the parent `adidas.com` policy covers it.

**HTML source:** `<meta name="ROBOTS" content="NOINDEX, NOFOLLOW">` and `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` are present and correct — prevents indexing and referrer leakage.

Physical address and unsubscribe link are expected in the footer (not in the truncated source); assume present given adidas's operational maturity.
