The subject "adidas by Stella McCartney SS26" tells me exactly what this is before I even open it: a luxury fashion collaboration, historically a women's line, runway-adjacent positioning. - The hero delivers exactly that confirmation. A Black woman in a long, flowing silver-grey outfit strikes a dynamic pose, but this is not running gear — it's athletic fashion. "BREATHE. FOCUS. - **Seasonally current** — SS26 framing means this isn't recycled inventory copy; it's announcing new product.

## What's weak

- **Total demographic miss** — adidas by Stella McCartney is a women's line. The model is a woman in women's fashion athletic wear. I'm a 33-year-old male running club captain. Nike at least attempts to segment me by activity; this feels like it landed in my inbox by accident.
- **Fashion positioning for a performance subscriber** — "Redefining running through the lens of fashion" is fine copy for someone else's inbox. I want to know if the shoe holds up at mile 18, not whether it clears Fashion Week.
- **Gift card and Stella McCartney in the same email is jarring** — These two modules have nothing to do with each other. The $100/$80 promo would hit harder in an email I actually care about, like the 30% off send two days ago (which I scored at 8/10).
- **Cadence is exhausting** — This is the eleventh email I've gotten from adidas in eight days. Two today. After a kids' colorway miss this morning, I'm already fatigued before I open this one.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment men's performance subscribers off this list entirely.** adidas by Stella McCartney is a women's line. The segmentation failure here is the root issue — no copy change fixes it. Ship this to women's fashion/athleisure buyers; pull running-primary male subscribers.
- 2. **If you must send to performance subscribers, reframe around function.** Lead with what the technology does for running, not what it looks like on a runway. Even lululemon's misses (like their all-women Breezily Pants email from last week) at least speak to performance function occasionally — adidas by StellaMcCartney doesn't even try.
- 3. **Move the gift card to a standalone email or a relevant hero.** The $100/$80 deal is a good offer. Burying it as a secondary module in a fashion collab email wastes it. Give it its own send tied to a performance product I actually buy.
- 4. **Respect the cadence limit.** Two emails in one day from the same sender with two consecutive misses is how you train subscribers to batch-delete. Nike sent me a single transactional code this week (6/10) — clinical, yes, but at least it didn't cry wolf.

us-promotions-vouchers%2Fwhat-should-i-know-about-gift-cards/1/...

This is a merge/encoding error in the ESP template. The redirect engine cannot resolve it correctly.

**[FAIL] Destination URL returns 403**
```
https://www.adidas.com/us/help/us-promotions-vouchers/what-should-i-know-about-gift-cards
```
Independently probed; returns HTTP 403. The page either requires auth, is geo-blocked, or has been removed. Both the wrapped and unwrapped variants of this link fail.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent**
Not captured by the AgentMail relay. If genuinely missing from the SMTP envelope, this violates Google/Yahoo 2024 bulk sender requirements for >5k/day senders. Adidas unambiguously qualifies. Needs server-side verification before concluding it's missing vs. relay-stripped.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent (RFC 8058)**
One-click unsubscribe (required by Google/Yahoo policy) depends on this header pairing with `List-Unsubscribe`. Same caveat — verify at SMTP layer.
