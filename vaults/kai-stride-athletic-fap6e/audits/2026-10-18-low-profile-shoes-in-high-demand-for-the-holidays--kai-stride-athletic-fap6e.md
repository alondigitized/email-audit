# Low-profile shoes in high demand for the holidays
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-18**
## Executive summary

- My gut reaction was "pretty shoes, wrong guy." I run a club and buy for the Sunday long run. This is three terrace sneakers, a lilac suede, a cow-print and a washed denim blue, on a wood bench. They look good in a flat-lay. There's nothing here I'd run in, and nothing I'd gift a runner either.
- The email is also thin. There's a banner, a hero, one paragraph and one button, and no price, no offer and no deadline. "Win gifting this year" is the only hook, and it's aimed at "trend-lovers," which isn't me. It's the third adidas lifestyle send in about a week, after the Metallica collection on 10/16 and Bad Bunny Gazelle on 10/13. adidas keeps pitching me the fashion side of the catalog and never the running side.
- Lululemon's Pace Breaker email was the opposite: a man mid-stride at dusk, a product built for a runner. Nike's one-time-code email was dull, but it did what it said. This one just sits there. I'd mark it read and wouldn't forward it.

## What's working

- The hero is clean and the three shoes are easy to read. The colorways are distinct and the photo is well lit.
- There's one CTA, a big black "SHOP NOW" button that's easy to tap on a phone.
- The copy is short, so I get the point in about five seconds.
- The holiday gifting angle is seasonal and fits the calendar.

## What's weak

- Nothing in it is for runners. The Japan, Tokyo and Taekwondo shoes are low-profile terrace and martial-arts-heritage sneakers with no performance story, which is the same miss as the Metallica and Bad Bunny emails.
- There's no offer. No percentage off, no free shipping and no member perk, even though I'm an adiClub member with 50 points. Nike and Lululemon at least tell me what I get.
- The two "SHOP NOW" buttons, one in the black banner and one under the copy, point at different things. The banner says "best sellers" and the body says "Style must-haves." That's mixed messaging.
- The three shoes have no names, prices or sizes. I can't tell which one is which, and there's no hint whether my size is in stock.
- The hero image and banner look slightly off-grid on the left, and the banner sits flush against the hero. The layout is plain.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment by category. Send runners something from the running line, such as Adizero or Ultraboost, and keep the terrace shoes for the lifestyle audience. That one change would do more than any copy fix.
- 2. Put a concrete hook in the hero: a price, a member perk or a ship-by date for the holidays. "Order by Dec 15 for delivery" would be credible urgency.
- 3. Name the shoes and show prices under each one, and link each to its own product page instead of one generic button.
- 4. Drop the "best sellers" banner or make it match the body. Two CTAs with two messages dilutes both.
- 5. Use the preheader to say something new instead of repeating the headline.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Gifts they'll actually wear: Japan, Tokyo, Taekwondo`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Order by Dec 15: 3 low-profile gifts, ship free`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `adiClub members get free shipping on holiday orders. Sizes are going fast.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Suede, cow print and washed denim, from $90. Pick one for the trend-lover on your list.`
