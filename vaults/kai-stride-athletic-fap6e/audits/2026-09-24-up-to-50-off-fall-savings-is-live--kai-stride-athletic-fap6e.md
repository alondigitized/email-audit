## Executive summary

- Okay, this is a real sale — pink "FALL KICKOFF SALE" hero, "UP TO 50% OFF STARTS NOW," no code needed. That's a genuine step up from the past week of Care Bears and Z.N.E. tracksuits. But look at what's actually in the grid: Handball Spezial, a Coca-Cola Samba collab, and a Mexico EQT Track Top. Three of the four hero products are lifestyle/retro sneakers, not running gear. The one exception — the Adizero EVO SL — is a running shoe, but it's buried third in a four-up grid, not the hero.
- The Adizero Adios Pro 5 email from 09-23 scored a 7 with me because it led with a race-day supershoe and talked mid-stride geometry. ## What's working

- Real, credible discount math — $130→$65, $150→$90, with star ratings and Prime availability tags, so it doesn't feel like vague "up to 50%" bait.
- The Adizero EVO SL Woven Running Shoes card gives me one legitimate running product with a visible price ($90), rating (4.7), and discount (-40%).
- Clean Shop Men/Women/Kids segmentation right under the offer — no forced click into a mixed catalog.
- No render bugs — everything loaded, spacing is clean, hierarchy from hero banner to offer copy to product grid is easy to follow.

## What's weak

- Hero product mix is lifestyle-first: Handball Spezial, Coca-Cola Samba, Mexico EQT Track Top — three retro/streetwear picks against one running shoe. That's a miss for a segment adidas presumably knows runs weekly.
- The Coca-Cola Samba collab is a fun collector item but has zero relevance to my training life — it's the Care Bears problem again, just with better production value.
- Nothing about the sale is running-specific — no "gear up for fall training," no mention of mileage, race season, or club discounts.
- Preview text ("Get ready for fall with new styles.") is generic and doesn't hint at the 50% off or any product category.

## Recommendations

- 1. Swap the hero product to the Adizero EVO SL (or a running silhouette) instead of leading with retro Sambas — if you know a subscriber's category affinity is running, the top-of-fold image should reflect that, not a blanket sitewide mix.
- 2. Add a "Shop Running" button alongside Men/Women/Kids — right now the segmentation is gender-based only, with no path for someone who just wants performance shoes.
- 3. Tie the sale copy to fall training/race season language instead of pure "refresh your rotation" retail-speak — even one line about fall races would connect to my calendar.
- 4. Reorder the product grid so performance items lead and collabs/lifestyle follow, not the reverse.
