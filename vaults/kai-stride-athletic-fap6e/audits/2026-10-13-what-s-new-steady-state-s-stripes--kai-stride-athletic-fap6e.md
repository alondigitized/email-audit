# What's new: Steady State's stripes
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-13**
## Executive summary

- My gut reaction was that this is a good-looking email with nothing in it for me. The cobalt puffer against the pale backdrop is striking. Then I scrolled through a woman in stripes, a woman in a hoodie, a woman squeezing a puffer and a grid of mostly women's looks. I'm a run-club captain who buys for the weekly long run, and nothing here told me whether the brand knows runners. It's fashion-shopper-in-athleisure, again.
- "Emphasis on comfort" and "Confidence is the throughline in naturally breathable fleece" are seminar-room lines, which is exactly the tone I flagged on 9/29 with "An elevated form of ease." Compare Nike. When it emails me, it at least pretends there's a pace or a session behind the product. Here there's no mileage, no weather, no run. This is the third lounge or studio story in a few weeks, after the 10/7 ballet-studio email and the 9/29 Smooth Spacer one.
- Would I click? Probably not. I'd skim it and mark it read. I wouldn't forward it, because nobody in my club is asking for striped fleece.

## What's working

- The blue puffer hero is a real thumb-stopper. The color pops and it's a strong first frame.
- The "Complete the look" block pairs shoes with coats and jackets, so the email at least points at a footwear category.
- The layout is clean. Big images, short headlines, and one black pill button ("Shop What's New") in the first screen.
- The footer links (Download the App, Find a Store) are tidy and easy to tap.

## What's weak

- There is no offer at all. No %, no free shipping, no member perk. Sending me to "Shop What's New" is a weak reason to open the app.
- It has no running content. I can't see any run, pace or weather, and nothing about durability, fit or sizing. It's all studio and lounge.
- The copy is vague. "Emphasis on comfort." doesn't tell me what the product is or who it's for.
- The models are almost all women, and the drop grid is mostly women's looks. I only found a couple of unisex-looking pieces, so I can't tell where I fit.
- The CTAs are generic: "Shop What's New", "Shop Steady State →", "See all". The "Shop Shoes →" link shows one cropped sneaker and doesn't tell me which shoe.
- It's a long scroll. The last third is a grid with no hierarchy, and I lost interest before the end.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment by category. Send me a run-first version, with a men's runner in the Steady State layers at a real run, and with specs like weight, reflectivity or temperature range.
- 2. Put one concrete reason to act in the hero: a member price, early access, or a "back in stock in your size" note.
- 3. Replace the mood copy with a product claim, such as "Fleece that keeps up on a 5 a.m. long run."
- 4. Make the CTAs specific and trim the grid to 4 items. Name the shoe in the shoe link.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Breathable fleece, stripes and a puffer that packs down for the warm-up.`
