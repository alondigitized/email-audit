# New, fast-drying tennis kits are a must-get
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- Five emails from lululemon in a week — yoga, mesh jersey, a 15% reminder, Pilates, and now tennis. I'm a runner who buys ABC pants and Surge shorts from these people, and they have yet to send me anything that touches a road, a track, or a race. The "must-get" line in the subject is very Nike energy — it's the kind of hype-machine copy that doesn't feel like lululemon, and it especially doesn't feel like something aimed at me.
- Once I'm inside, the execution is clean. The clay-court aesthetic is sharp, "Focus is power" sets a decent brand tone, and the "Court Champ Tennis Skirt" callout is genuinely specific — that's more product-forward than what I usually see in their sends. But every model I scrolled past is a woman, every product is clearly female, and there's zero crossover content for a male runner. This isn't a miss on quality; it's a miss on segmentation.
- Mark as read, don't click, don't forward. If my girlfriend played tennis I might actually send it to her — that's as close as this gets to a win for me.

## What's working

- **"Focus is power" preheader / hero tone** — lululemon's premium-performance voice, not discount-bin language. Feels like the brand I know.
- **Product specificity** — naming the "Court Champ Tennis Skirt" with a performance call ("quick-drying") is more concrete than the vague lifestyle headlines from the May 16 Pilates send.
- **Seasonal fit** — late May, clay-court energy, spring tennis. The campaign timing is genuinely on-point even if it's not my sport.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no lorem ipsum, no overlapping text. The layout holds on mobile. That sounds basic but after some of adidas's recent club emails, I notice when it just works.

## What's weak

- **100% female models, 100% female product** — I'm a 33-year-old male. Nothing in this scroll suggests I exist as a customer, even though lululemon absolutely sells men's tennis gear.
- **No offer, no hook** — this is a "new arrivals" launch email with no price, no discount, no member-exclusive access. At least the May 13 "Almost gone: your 15% off" email gave me something to act on. This is just a catalog page.
- **"Must-get" is hype-machine language** — lululemon's positioning is supposed to be "we know serious athletes." Must-get reads like a Nike flash-sale subject line. It clashes.
- **Five sends in one week, zero running content** — adidas's adiClub sequence at least tried to map emails to my club registration. lululemon has had me for years and can't figure out I'm not a yoga-Pilates-tennis person.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment men separately** — lululemon makes the Everlux Court short and men's tennis tees. Either build a men's tennis version of this email or pull male-runner subscribers out of this list entirely. Sending a tennis skirt launch to a running-club captain is a waste of a send.
- 2. **Add a performance crossover hook** — "Fast-drying" is language I care about as a runner. One sentence acknowledging that the same fabric tech is in the Surge or Metal Vent Surge would create a bridge. Right now there's zero pull for someone outside the tennis bubble.
- 3. **Introduce an offer or access mechanic** — even "members see it first" framing creates a reason to click. Right now there's no urgency, no price, no reason not to just check the site later (or never).
- 4. **Dial back "must-get"** — it's a subject line lululemon shouldn't be writing. Replace it with something that trusts the product.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Court Champ Tennis Skirt — quick-dry, built for heat`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New tennis kits. Built to perform in every set.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fast-drying fabric, clean lines — the full kit for clay or hard court.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Court Champ Tennis Skirt drops now — shop before it sells out.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`letter-spacing` percentage values are invalid CSS** (three instances confirmed):
```css

.guest-voice-md { letter-spacing: -2%; }   /* invalid */
.guest-voice-sm { letter-spacing: -1%; }   /* invalid */
```
`letter-spacing` only accepts length units (`px`, `em`). All three values will be silently ignored; affected headings will render with default letter-spacing across all clients.

**Non-standard `font-weight` values** across multiple selectors:
```css
font-weight: 380   /* used on p, h1–h5, a, h4 */
font-weight: 570   /* used on .eyebrow, .product-callout */
```
These are variable-font axis values. Email clients that do not load the hosted `Saans-*` font files will fall back to system sans-serif and clamp to the nearest standard weight (400 or 600 respectively). No visible breakage, but intent is lost on ~40–50% of clients.

**`<title>` is generic** (`lululemon`) — inconsequential for email but does appear in some browser/webmail tab titles.

**`<h4>` not included in dark mode font-color rules** — `h1–h3, h5` are covered; `h4` uses a separate declaration with no dark-mode override in the visible CSS block.
