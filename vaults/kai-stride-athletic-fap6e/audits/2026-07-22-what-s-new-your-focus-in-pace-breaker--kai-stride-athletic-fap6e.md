# What’s new: your focus in Pace Breaker™
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- Finally. That's my honest gut reaction. After a week of golf emails, French Terry loungewear, and a women's workout set that opened with a woman mid-stretch, lululemon sent me an email about a product I'd actually race in. The Pace Breaker™ short is not a fashion item — it's what I pull out for tempo Tuesdays and long Sunday runs. "Lock In. Level up." with a guy doing pull-ups in those shorts is the first hero image from lululemon this month that landed without me immediately scrolling past it.
- The problem is the email doesn't hold the line. The Pace Breaker promise in the subject — "your focus in Pace Breaker™" — drifts by the third editorial section. "Understated. And never underestimated." sounds like shoes or a tonal colorway story, not a performance running short. Then the "Shop new styles this week" product grid at the bottom is the same product dump that showed up at the bottom of the July 21 email, the July 14 email, probably every lululemon email I've gotten this year. The focus evaporates.
- I'd click the Pace Breaker CTA — I want to check the liner length and see the new colorways. I would not forward this to my running club. Compare it to the July 16 "Summer Sale, on its final run" email: that one had a hook ("Move fast," the orange urgency copy) that this one doesn't. This is a better subject line than anything lululemon's sent me this month, but the body execution is scattershot.

## What's working

- Pace Breaker™ by name in the subject — that's an actual product serious runners recognize, not brand-speak
- Male athlete doing a real athletic movement in the hero, not a posed lifestyle shot — the pull-up visual says "performance," not "athleisure"
- "The lighter the fabric feels, the harder you go." is the best performance copy I've seen from lululemon in this batch — sounds like it was written by someone who actually runs intervals
- Men's content top to bottom, clean render, no layout breakage — this is clearly not the July 21 send (women's hero, irrelevant to me the entire scroll)
- "Your focus in Pace Breaker™" subject line structure implies segmentation — feels like they know I'm an athlete, not a fashion shopper

## What's weak

- The email fractures: Pace Breaker hero → training performance section → "Understated. And never underestimated." (a completely different product vibe) → product grid. That's three different stories fighting for attention in one send
- No offer, no urgency, no member pricing — adidas at least threw 50 points at me for a profile completion; lululemon gave me editorial with no hook
- The product grid at the bottom is filler — "Shop new styles this week" with eight thumbnails teaches me nothing about fit, liner length, or what distance these shorts are built for
- CTA buttons almost certainly say "Shop Now" — after that strong Pace Breaker hero, that's a whiff. Nike, even in their transactional one-time-code email, didn't waste a CTA on vague copy

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the "Understated. And never underestimated." section entirely** — or fold it into the Pace Breaker story as a colorway note. One product, one edit.
- 2. **Add one concrete performance spec** — liner length, weight in oz, whether it has a key pocket. Runners compare these. "Lightweight and ventilated" is a start; give me the number.
- 3. **Rewrite the CTA** — "Shop Pace Breaker" or "Find Your Pace Breaker" beats "Shop Now" every time.
- 4. **Trim the product grid** — two hero products max, with actual performance context. The "new styles this week" dump makes this feel like a newsletter blast, not a targeted send.
- 5. **Add a drop signal** — "New colorways just landed" or "Limited sizes" would give me a reason to click now versus later.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Pace Breaker™ — built for your next tempo run`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New Pace Breaker styles just dropped`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Ventilated. Lightweight. The short serious runners actually train in.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New colorways for the short that goes the distance.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Font CDN — 5 broken assets (403 Forbidden)**

All five web font files used in `@font-face` declarations are inaccessible:

```
https://globalstaticassets.lululemon.com/fonts/Saans-Light.woff
https://globalstaticassets.lululemon.com/fonts/Saans-Regular.woff
https://globalstaticassets.lululemon.com/fonts/Saans-Medium.woff
https://globalstaticassets.lululemon.com/fonts/Saans-SemiBold.woff
https://globalstaticassets.lululemon.com/fonts/KHGiga-Light.woff
```

Impact: All text renders in fallback fonts (`Helvetica Neue`, `Arial`, `Times New Roman`). Weight and tracking distinctions between `h1`–`h5`, `.eyebrow`, `.guest-voice-*`, and body copy are lost. The editorial serif (`KHGiga-Light`) used for hero/quote blocks falls back to `Times New Roman`.

Likely cause: The font CDN may block requests from non-browser user-agents (curl/HTTP probes). If confirmed live in-client, the CDN origin or CORS/Referer policy needs review. If the 403s are probe artifacts only, this should be validated in a real mail client.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header — not found**

The `List-Unsubscribe` header was not captured via the AgentMail relay. RFC 2369 compliance cannot be confirmed. Gmail and Apple Mail use this header to surface the native unsubscribe UI; its absence increases the probability of spam classification on high-volume sends.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post header — not found**

RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe (`List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`) was not found. Google and Yahoo's 2024 bulk sender requirements mandate this for senders above 5k/day. A missing header is a deliverability risk at lululemon's send volume.

**Authentication-Results — unknown**

```
Authentication-Results header not found: Expected via AgentMail relay — SPF/DKIM status unknown
```

SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status is unverifiable from the captured headers. Sending domain `e.lululemon.com` should be validated against the brand's published DMARC record separately.

Note: these three findings may be relay artifacts — AgentMail may strip or not forward these headers. They should be re-checked against raw headers from a direct inbox delivery.
