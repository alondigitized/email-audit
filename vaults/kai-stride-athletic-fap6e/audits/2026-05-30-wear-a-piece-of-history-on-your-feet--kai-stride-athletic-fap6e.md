# Wear a piece of history on your feet
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-30**
## Executive summary

- Two products, two completely different audiences, crammed into one send — and adidas picked the wrong one to lead with. A retro Japan lifestyle sneaker as the hero to someone who runs 50+ miles a week is a swing and a miss. I glanced at "BRING OUT VINTAGE VIBES" and my thumb was already moving toward archive.
- Then I scrolled down and saw **adidas × SATISFY** — and actually stopped. If you don't know SATISFY Running, you're not their target. But a running club captain absolutely knows them: cult Paris brand, technical trail-to-street pieces, the kind of thing that gets passed around in the group chat after long runs. The SkillTech™ tee sounds genuinely interesting. Laser-cut perfs, stealth-reflective, lived-in construction — that's legitimately runner-coded language.
- The problem? It's a **contest gated behind the adidas app**. Not a buy. Not a waitlist. A sweepstakes you can only enter after downloading and signing into an app. That's two friction points stacked on top of a product I was actually interested in. I close the email without clicking. I definitely don't forward it — there's nothing forwarding-worthy here unless someone specifically asked me about the SATISFY collab, in which case I'd just send them a SATISFY direct link instead. Mark as read, move on.

## What's working

- **SATISFY name recognition is a genuine signal** — dropping that brand name in an email to a serious runner is smart targeting, even if the execution fumbles the landing
- **Email renders clean** — no broken images, no overlapping copy, layout is tight and readable on what appears to be a mobile-width render
- **Japan shoe photography is strong** — the leopard-print lining detail and stacked footwear shot is visually arresting, even if it's irrelevant to me personally
- **SATISFY copy is actually good** — "Worn through ritual" and "designed to feel lived-in from day one" is the right voice for that audience
- **No lorem ipsum, no empty merge tokens** — after reviewing a broken adidas send earlier this week with the voucher, at least this one is technically clean

## What's weak

- **Japan shoe as the hero is a demographic miss** — retro lifestyle sneaker with "vintage vibes" messaging is fashion, not performance; I buy for the run, not the look
- **SATISFY is buried second and then app-gated** — the one thing worth being excited about requires a download and an account to enter a contest; Nike's "here's your one-time code" send I reviewed was transactional but frictionless, this is the opposite
- **"Sold out now" in the top banner is incoherent** — if the product is sold out, why is there a SHOP NOW button? That copy creates confusion, not urgency
- **Subject never signals running** — "Wear a piece of history" is pure fashion/heritage language; lululemon's "New swim trunks are comfortable from the jump" at least told me the product category immediately, adidas is making me guess
- **This is email number eleven-ish from adidas in about nine days** — the brand history here is brutal; even a send I'd otherwise open gets taxed by the cadence, and this one doesn't earn the goodwill back

## Recommendations

- 1. **Split this into two emails.** SATISFY x adidas deserves its own send — that story is strong enough to stand alone. The Japan shoe retro launch is a separate audience entirely. Combining them dilutes both.
- 2. **Lead the SATISFY story with a buy option, not a contest.** If the tee is truly limited or app-exclusive, that's the hook — but give me a way to queue, waitlist, or buy, not a sweepstakes entry. This collab has real demand.
- 3. **Fix the top banner copy** — "Low profile shoes are here and sold out now" is contradictory. Either it's available (say so) or it's gone (say that and build FOMO with a restock CTA). Don't say both at once.
- 4. **Reframe the subject around the SATISFY collab** — that's the news. The Japan shoe is catalog product.
- **Subject Alt A:** `adidas × SATISFY just dropped — enter to win`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The most technical tee in running. Yours to win.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `SkillTech™ laser-cut tee, SATISFY collab. Sign up in the app.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Runner-grade details. App-exclusive entry. Limited window.`
- 5. **If you're going to app-gate it, say so in the subject** — "app-exclusive" is a credible urgency signal for a running community member, not a deterrent. Own it.

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has one confirmed broken click-through link and a zero-byte plain-text fallback, both of which directly degrade user experience and deliverability. Several `http://` image sources and missing compliance headers round out the critical issues.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — Broken destination link**
The QA probe reports `unknown url type: '/g/2c2d6fd9-27db-43e6-ad09-bae249debde3'` against:
```
https://dv.adidas.com/o/2c2d6fd9-27db-43e6-ad09-bae249debde3?cp_tp=v3.eJx1kD...&cp_cid=9dcbb8d2633c8852&mi_mid=019e79d4-0a80-7000-8b88-1e5f644d40d7
```
The redirect resolver returned an `/g/` path it cannot process — the final destination URL is broken or the decode of `cp_tp` produced an invalid route. Any recipient clicking this link lands nowhere.

**INFO — 24 tracking links not probed**
All click-redirect domains (`click.comms.adidas.com`, `dv.adidas.com`) were skipped by the HTTP probe. No additional broken links can be confirmed or ruled out without direct probe access.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**WARN — HTTP image sources (4 occurrences, same asset)**
The arrow image is served over plain HTTP:
```
http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```
This appears 4 times in the template. Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook block mixed-content images by default; the arrows will render as broken image icons on most clients.

**WARN — HTTP tracking pixel**

An open-tracking pixel served over HTTP will be blocked or proxied by image-safe clients (Apple MPP, Gmail proxy), corrupting open-rate data.

**WARN — Missing `alt` text on 2 images**
- Tracking pixel at `dv.adidas.com/o/2c2d6fd9...` — `alt=""` acceptable for pixels but not present
- Pixel at `click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/...` — same

Screen readers will announce these as unlabeled interactive elements if they are wrapped in `<a>` tags.

**INFO — Custom fonts reference only local fallbacks**
`AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` are declared via `@font-face` with no remote `src` URL — only `local()` system font names (Arial, Helvetica, Calibri). This is likely intentional for email, but confirms the custom font declarations serve no function.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

### 5. Compliance

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe` header absent**
Not detected by the relay. Gmail and Yahoo require this header for bulk senders (>5K/day) as of February 2024. Its absence risks inbox tab demotion or spam classification.

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent (RFC 8058)**
One-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail and Yahoo's bulk sender policy. Without `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, automated unsubscribe requests from those clients will fail silently.

**WARN — `Authentication-Results` not captured**
SPF and DKIM pass/fail status is unknown via the AgentMail relay. Cannot confirm authentication posture; should be verified directly against the received headers.

**FAIL — Plain-text alternative is 0 bytes**
CAN-SPAM does not mandate a text part, but a zero-byte `text/plain` MIME part triggers spam heuristics in SpamAssassin and similar filters. Most ESPs auto-generate a fallback — the fact that this is empty suggests the text part was explicitly set to blank or the MIME structure was malformed in transit.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**`no-referrer` meta tag is set in `<head>`**
```html
<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">
```
This suppresses the `Referer` header on any outbound navigation from the email's webview rendering (rare), but more relevantly signals to link-preview fetchers that no referrer should be passed. It does not affect click-tracking redirects directly, but landing page analytics that rely on `document.referrer` as a secondary attribution signal will receive nothing.

**UTM parameters** are encoded inside the encrypted `cp_tp` blob and cannot be inspected from the source. Whether downstream landing pages receive `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` is not verifiable from this audit — confirm via a live click trace through the redirect chain.

**Broken link (§2) is also a continuity failure** — whatever product or collection page `dv.adidas.com/g/...` was meant to resolve to is unreachable.
