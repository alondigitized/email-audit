# Summer Sale faves, moving fast
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-11**
## Executive summary

- Fifth lululemon email in six days and my first thought was, "oh, they just swapped the angle on the same Summer Sale." They did. Last week it was "200+ picks added" with the number-swap. Before that, "softest sets." Now it's "faves, moving fast" — same inventory, new subject line. I'm not mad, but I'm definitely fatigued.
- The hero is "Make soft, stretchy styles in Walnut Crunch your next move." That sentence was not written for me. Walnut Crunch is a beautiful deep brown — I actually own the Surge Short in it — but every single product in this email is an Align skirt, a Define jacket, a bike short styled on women, for women. There's no ABC pant, no Surge, no men's liner. lululemon knows I run. They have my purchase history. They have no excuse for this.
- I'm marking it read and moving on. I'm not clicking, and I'm definitely not forwarding to anyone in my running club.

## What's working

- **Orange Summer Sale banner renders instantly** — the visual hierarchy is clean, eye goes straight to the SUMMER SALE header, no ambiguity about what kind of email this is.
- **"Shop Summer Sale" CTA is unambiguous** — a verb plus a noun, placed twice (hero and footer), big enough to tap on mobile without zooming.
- **"Moving fast" has some real FOMO** — scarcity language in the subject is one of lululemon's better moves this week; it beats the flat "200+ picks added" swap from July 9th.
- **No render bugs** — clean product tiles, images load, layout holds. Compared to Nike's occasional image gaps, lululemon's production quality is consistently solid.

## What's weak

- **Zero men's product, zero running product** — not a single Surge, ABC, or Metal Vent in sight. adidas sent me the Dropset Pro email days ago and I forwarded it to my running club captain group chat. lululemon can't clear that bar with Align skirts.
- **"Walnut Crunch" is the hero hook, and I had to work to care** — a colorway name is not a value proposition. adidas led with "Earn 50 points for completing your profile" last week — boring, but at least it spoke to me personally. This speaks to nobody.
- **No specific discount percentage visible** — "Summer Sale" is three words I've now read in five consecutive emails. Without a price drop shown (no strikethrough, no % off), it's just a shelf label, not an offer.
- **Cadence is wrecked** — five sends in six days, all Summer Sale variants. The urgency of "faves, moving fast" is undermined by the fact that they've been "moving fast" since July 6th.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Drop a men's module.** Even one row — three products from the men's Summer Sale — would flip this email from irrelevant to clickable for me. lululemon knows I buy men's. Use it.
- 2. **Show the actual discount.** Put "up to 40% off" or a strikethrough price in the hero. "Summer Sale" without a number is noise by now.
- 3. **Make the urgency credible.** "Moving fast" + "Don't miss the moment" without a countdown or end date is hollow. Add a date: "Ends Sunday."
- 4. **Slow down the cadence.** Skip Monday. Give "Summer Sale faves" space to breathe instead of following four near-identical sends.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Men's Summer Sale picks running low`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Surge Shorts, ABC Shorts: Summer Sale ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your go-to training styles are on sale — here's what's left.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Up to 40% off the styles you actually run in. Ends Sunday.`
