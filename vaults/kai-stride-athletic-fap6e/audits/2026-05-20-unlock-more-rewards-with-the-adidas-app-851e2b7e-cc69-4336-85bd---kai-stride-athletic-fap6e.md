# Unlock more rewards with the adidas app
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- Three emails in five days from adidas, and this one wants me to download their app. I get the business logic — app installs matter for their engagement numbers. But every app benefit listed here is written for a sneakerhead, not a runner. "Hot items restock" and "early access to drops"? That's StockX-speak. I captain a running club and log 50+ miles a week — I want to know when the Adizero Boston drops in my half-size, not when the latest lifestyle collab restocks. There's a real runner-relevant pitch buried in this email: the Spotify partner offer ("3 months of Spotify with your purchase") is tucked into a tiny app mockup screenshot in the hero when it should be 36-point type front and center. That's the one benefit that would actually make me pull out my phone.
- The 15% adiClub voucher from the May 15 welcome email resurfaces here with the same code (WELCOME-STRL-8TVH) and the same June 03 expiry — and honestly, resurfacing that is good email citizenship. I appreciate that they're not letting it expire silently. But note that the May 17 send already used the phrase "unlock more rewards" in its subject line, and now it's back in this subject. The creative well is already running dry on email three.
- I'd scroll straight to REDEEM ONLINE and use the 15% on something I've been watching. I'm not downloading the app from this email. Nothing in the copy earns that ask for someone like me. Mark as read, move on.

## What's working

- **The 15% voucher is resurfaced cleanly with code visible and June 03 deadline in plain type** — honest, no buried asterisks, useful to keep in front of me three emails in.
- **"GET THE APP" and "REDEEM ONLINE" are unambiguous button CTAs** — verb plus noun, zero guesswork, exactly what buttons should do.
- **Clean render top to bottom** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no layout breakage; the visual hierarchy from hero to voucher to recommendations flows correctly.
- **The Spotify partner offer is genuinely runner-relevant** — long-run playlists are real life; they just buried it in a tiny app screenshot instead of making it the lead.
- **Voucher terms are disclosed upfront** ("valid only on full-priced items") — I don't feel baited.

## What's weak

- **Every app benefit is coded for sneakerhead culture, not running culture** — "hot items restock" and "early access to drops" means nothing to someone chasing a marathon PR; Nike's app emails talk training plans and run streaks, this doesn't get close.
- **Three emails in five days and still zero running-specific copy or product** — lululemon sent me five category-wrong emails in a week (yoga, Pilates, tennis, twice) and adidas is running the same miss from a different angle.
- **"Unlock more rewards" appeared verbatim in the May 17 subject line** — creative is already recycling on email three, which erodes the sense that I'm being spoken to deliberately.
- **The Spotify hook is buried in a thumbnail app screenshot** — that's the one genuinely compelling, runner-relevant benefit and it's doing its work in the equivalent of fine print.
- **Recommended for You shows lifestyle silhouettes, not performance product** — nothing in those three images says Adizero, Boston, or Supernova; I'm being shown the same catalog page any guest would see.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the Spotify offer at full-width hero scale** — "Download the app. Get 3 months of Spotify Premium." is a specific, runner-relevant, time-bounded incentive. That's the email. Build around it.
- 2. **Rewrite the benefits list with one running SKU reference** — swap "hot items restock" for "be first when the Adizero Boston or Supernova restocks in your size." One product name changes the entire perceived targeting.
- 3. **Surface the 15% voucher above the app pitch** — the voucher is the stronger immediate-action offer for anyone who already has the app. Don't make them scroll past the install ask to get to the thing they actually want.
- 4. **Pull a specific running product into the Recommended section** — if my account has any signal at all that I'm a runner, show me performance product with a name, not lifestyle silhouettes.
- 5. **Cool the cadence** — three emails in five days is a week's worth of inbox noise. Nike sent me one transactional email this week and landed better by default just by not crowding.
