- The tone here also matches the moment — this reads like a legit trail-running product drop, not a lifestyle ad dressed up in running language. It's short, it's confident, and the visual sells the shoe's purpose (grip, terrain, all-conditions) better than any copy could.
- Would I click? Yeah, I'd tap through to see the GTX 2 details — I run in the rain a lot and a Gore-Tex trail shoe is relevant to me. Would I forward it? Maybe to one guy in my running club who's been eyeing a new trail shoe, not the whole group chain.

## What's working

- The hero image is genuinely great — three athletes actively running on technical rock terrain, which is exactly the "does this brand know runners" test I keep applying to every email.
- "THE AGRAVIC FAMILY'S GROWING" signals this is a franchise/lineage shoe, not a random SKU — that's the kind of continuity a runner actually tracks.
- Body copy calls out two specific models (Agravic SL and Agravic GTX 2) instead of vague "new arrivals" language.
- Preheader ("Go fast and get dirty in the latest Agravic trail running shoes") sets context before I even open — no mystery, no bait.
- Clean, minimal layout — logo, one promo banner, hero, copy, one CTA. No clutter competing for my eye.

## What's weak

- Zero pricing or offer anywhere — this is 100% a product announcement, no %, no sale, nothing time-bound. Fine as a launch email, but it's not going to convert me today.
- No mention of GTX 2's actual differentiator (waterproofing) in the copy — I have to infer it from the name alone. Compare that to the Adizero Adios Pro 5 email from the 23rd, which actually described why the supershoe mattered for race day.
- "SHOP NOW" is generic — for a launch like this, "Meet the Agravic Family" or "See the New Trail Shoes" would tell me exactly what I'm clicking into.
- No sizing, availability, or "shop men's" segmentation — as someone who buys specific sizes for long runs, I'd want to know if these are in stock before I click.
- The top black banner ("Explore the latest adidas best sellers") competes with and slightly undercuts the actual hero message — it's advertising something completely different one screen above the real story.

## Recommendations

- 1. Kill or replace the top "best sellers" banner — it's a distraction sitting right above the actual launch message and dilutes the one clear story adidas is finally telling.
- 2. Make the CTA specific: "Shop the Agravic Family" instead of generic "SHOP NOW" — ties the button directly to the product just shown.
- 3. Add one line about the GTX 2's Gore-Tex waterproofing — that's a real trail-runner decision point (wet vs. dry conditions) and it's currently invisible.
- 4. Add a price or "available now" cue — even without a discount, confirming these are purchasable today (not a teaser) would push me to act faster.
