# Earn adiClub points to unlock more rewards
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- Two days after adidas welcomed me to adiClub with a 15% voucher dangling front and center, they're back with the loyalty-mechanics explainer. The sequencing makes programmatic sense — welcome, then educate. But where the welcome email had a clear "here's your money," this one asks me to do homework. "Complete my profile" is the headline action, and I'm already skeptical. I signed up two days ago; if adidas doesn't know my name yet, that's their CRM problem, not mine.
- The running-specific hook — earning points through the adidas Running app — is the single most relevant thing in this email for me. I track every long run, I'm in Strava-level obsession territory. But adidas buried that below the fold as the third bullet in a generic three-column "MORE WAYS TO EARN" grid. lululemon would've led with my habit. Instead adidas led with a profile form.
- Mark as read. Not forwarding this to anyone in the club. The 15% welcome email was a 8 — this is a step backward.

## What's working

- **Clean layout, no visual chaos** — the hierarchy from headline to body copy to CTA reads without confusion, no render issues.
- **"STAY ACTIVE" earn opportunity exists** — crediting runs tracked in the adidas Running app is the right idea for someone like me. Points per run is a hook; it just needs to be the hero, not the footnote.
- **"COMPLETE MY PROFILE" CTA is at least specific** — verb + noun, not the vague "Discover" garbage Nike used in their one-time-code send.
- **Points mechanics explained clearly** — Level points vs. spend points distinction is explained in plain language. I actually didn't know adidas had a two-track system.

## What's weak

- **"Hello," with no first name** — adidas just onboarded me; they have my name. This is a segmentation failure that makes me feel like a stranger. lululemon manages to personalize their transactional sends; adidas can't pull my first name for a loyalty explainer?
- **Running is buried, lifestyle is the lead** — the hero is generic loyalty language. A brand that wants to compete with Nike Running for serious runners should be leading with sweat, not spreadsheets.
- **Two emails in 48 hours is aggressive for a new subscriber** — the welcome email was May 15, this is May 17. That's fast enough to trigger my "is adidas going to be spammy?" alarm. Nike paces better.
- **"SHOP NOW →" as a secondary CTA is dead weight** — takes me where? The homepage? A running-specific "Shop Ultraboost" would convert far better for someone like me.
- **The image in the "HOW ADICLUB POINTS WORK" section is very dark** — whatever's in that image is almost unreadable. Could be an athlete; could be a shoe; I genuinely can't tell.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the run-tracking earn hook as the hero** — swap the generic "UNLOCK MORE REWARDS WITH POINTS" headline for something like "Every mile earns. Track runs, get rewarded." Make the STAY ACTIVE CTA the primary button.
- 2. **Add my first name to the greeting** — zero excuse not to. "Hi Kai," immediately shifts this from blast to member communication.
- 3. **Replace "SHOP NOW" with a category-specific CTA** — "Shop Running" or "Explore Ultraboost" gives me a destination I'd actually click.
- 4. **Slow the send cadence** — 72-hour gap minimum after the welcome. Let the 15% voucher breathe before flooding me with education.
- 5. **Fix the dark hero image** — if there's an athlete in there, light it up. That's adidas's brand equity; don't hide it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your runs now earn points — here's how`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Track a run. Earn points. That's it.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Every mile you log in the adidas Running app counts toward rewards.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Complete your profile for 50 bonus points — takes 60 seconds.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — adidas "Earn adiClub Points" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

Eight custom font files are returning 403 from S3, causing fallback rendering across all clients. One primary CTA link is broken (403), and a staging image CDN domain is live in production.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — Broken CTA link (403)**
The "Learn more about points" link returns HTTP 403:
```
https://go.adidas.com/ihha/kbqustlc?cm_mmc=AdiEmail_CLC-_-None-_-MemOnb_Comms1.1...
```
This is a primary conversion action — broken link means zero clicks recorded and users hit a dead end.

**WARN — 23 tracking links not probed**
Click-redirect domains were skipped by the checker. Manual spot-check of `go.adidas.com` redirect chain is recommended given the 403 above on the same domain.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**FAIL — All 8 custom font files return 403**
Both `AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` weights/variants fail to load from:
```
https://s3-eu-west-1.amazonaws.com/adidas-adl/foundation/dist/adidas/assets/fonts/adidas/
```
All clients will fall back to `Arial` (`mso-font-alt:Arial` is set, so the fallback is defined — but the brand typography will not render for any recipient).

**WARN — 13 images missing `alt` text**
Affected images include decorative GIFs and product JPGs. In image-blocking clients (Outlook, corporate environments), these cells render blank with no context. Notable repeated offender: `2373748f-4913-4dfd-88b6-ca5c712d055b.gif` (4 instances, no alt).

**WARN — 3 images served over HTTP (not HTTPS)**
Two instances of the "arrow" image and one GIF use `http://image.link.adidas.com/...`:
```
http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/1/37231f63-3f72-48f3-8047-2650e11742e8.gif
```
Mixed-content blocking in Gmail (browser) and some mobile clients will silently suppress these images.

**WARN — Staging CDN domain in production send**
The majority of images reference `image.staging.link.adidas.com` rather than `image.link.adidas.com`. If the staging CDN has restricted access or rate limits, images will fail at scale.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**FAIL — Empty greeting name field**
QA detected an unfilled merge token in the salutation, producing output like `"Hi ,"`. The subscriber's first name variable was not resolved before send — either the merge tag is malformed or the profile field is blank without a fallback default (e.g., `{{first_name | default: "there"}}`).

---

### 5. Compliance

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe` header not confirmed**
The header was not captured by the AgentMail relay. If absent, this violates Google/Yahoo bulk sender requirements (enforced Feb 2024) and creates CAN-SPAM risk. Needs verification against raw SMTP headers from a direct inbox receipt.

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not confirmed**
RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is required for bulk senders to Gmail/Yahoo. Absence means Gmail will not show the native unsubscribe button, increasing spam complaints.

**WARN — `Authentication-Results` not captured**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status is unknown via this relay. Given the sender domain `us-news.adidas.com`, alignment must be confirmed independently — a DMARC failure would suppress delivery to major providers.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The broken `go.adidas.com` CTA carries UTM parameters (`cm_mmc`, `cm_mmc1`, `af_adset`, etc.) but since the link itself 403s, no attribution data reaches the site for this send. All click-through revenue and engagement metrics for this email will be zero regardless of open rate.

The `cm_mmc` parameter structure is present and correctly formatted on the broken URL — the issue is the redirect target, not the UTM tagging itself.
