# We’ve just added new sneaker styles
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-05**
## Executive summary

- I just got the Dropset Pro email from adidas this morning — the one with the race-day energy that actually made me lean in — and now, same afternoon, here comes a second send: "Update Your Sneaker Roster." The hero product is a Pixar Toy Story Samba OG. I'm not kidding. A Woody-and-Buzz sneaker collab is leading the email for a guy who's planning his Sunday long run. This is adidas treating me like a streetwear collector instead of the runner who's actually been spending with them.
- The layout is clean, the star ratings are a nice touch, and the Dropset Pro does show up in the product grid — twice, oddly, same name and same $150 price tag, which reads like a broken product feed rather than a deliberate two-colorway push. But the framing is all wrong. "Explore the latest adidas sneaker drops" is the hype-machine voice, not the runner-first voice I got this morning. They had the right register in the Dropset Pro email; this one abandons it.
- Mark as read. I'm not forwarding this. The one runner-relevant product in here already had its own dedicated email today.

## What's working

- **Star ratings on every product** — social proof rendered cleanly, 4.8 on the hero unit, I actually clocked those
- **Four-product grid layout** — visually easy to scan, no walls of text, loads fast
- **"SHOP NOW" button** — unambiguous, high contrast, doesn't make me hunt for the CTA
- **adidas brand voice is clean and consistent** — no typos, no layout collapses, type is readable without pinching

## What's weak

- **Pixar Toy Story Samba as hero product** — a licensed IP collab with a cartoon is front and center for a running club captain; adidas has the Adizero line and they're leading with Woody's shoe
- **Second email of the same day** — the Dropset Pro already had its own dedicated send this morning; cadence is broken, inbox fatigue is real, and receiving two adidas emails before dinner feels spammy
- **Preheader is legal copy** — "Content aids and footwear as sold. See our newest adidas dr..." is disclaimer language exposed as preview text; that's what I see before I open, and it's a dead weight
- **Dropset Pro appears twice in the grid** — same product name, same $150 price, both rows; either two colorways with no differentiation or a feed bug, and neither is a good look
- **Zero discount, zero urgency** — lululemon's Summer Sale email gave me 150+ new picks at reduced prices; adidas gave me full-MSRP Pixar sneakers with no reason to act today

## Recommendations

- 1. **Don't send a lifestyle sneaker email the same day as a performance drop email.** Segment the sends or hold the sneaker lifestyle push for a different day. Two adidas emails on July 5 is too many.
- 2. **Lead with the Dropset Pro, not the Pixar Samba.** If the target is runners (which it should be for me), put the training shoe in hero position. The Samba is a cool sneaker but it's fashion-category; route it to a different segment.
- 3. **Fix the preheader immediately.** Legal copy as the inbox preview is a silent killer — it's the first thing I read before deciding whether to open, and "Content aids and footwear as sold" tells me nothing about why I should care.
- 4. **Differentiate the two Dropset Pro listings** — if those are two different colorways, show them that way with colorway callouts; if it's a data error, pull one.
- 5. **Add a time-bound hook.** Nike at least anchors their transactional emails to a specific moment. "Just dropped — limited run" or "through Sunday only" would give me a reason to act.
