# New tennis gear. That’s the commotion.
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-25**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is the first lululemon email in weeks that's actually about performance and not another studio lifestyle shoot — and it shows. Pro tennis ambassadors mid-swing, real racket, real court, real motion blur. Ethan Quinn's actual name and title are captioned under his photo, and they've got Leylah Fernandez, Frances Tiafoe, and Alfie Hewett (wheelchair tennis) all repping different looks. That's a real athlete roster, not stock photography with a lifestyle vibe slapped on.
- I'm not a tennis guy — I run — so this isn't going to convert me on the product itself. But as a signal of "does this brand actually know athletes," it's the strongest send I've gotten from them since the Fast and Free launch on 8/18. Compare that to adidas's Billie Jean King drop (5/10, heritage story, real athlete) — this is in the same lane, arguably better executed because it's four athletes deep, not one hero shot.
- I wouldn't click — no running gear anywhere in this email — but I wouldn't roll my eyes either. Not forwarding it to anyone in my run club since it's not relevant to us, but if I had a tennis buddy I'd probably send it his way just for the Alfie Hewett inclusion, that's a nice touch.

## What's working

- Four different pro athletes, each captioned by name and shot mid-action — that's sport-specific credibility, not generic fitness cosplay.
- Including Alfie Hewett (wheelchair tennis) alongside able-bodied pros is a genuinely good, non-tokenistic representation move I don't see competitors doing.
- "Gear so light, your whole game turns up. Hit the court in pro-approved styles" — that's actual performance copy, not vague lifestyle language.
- Clean single-column layout, one photo per athlete, easy to scan on a scroll even if it's a long email.
- Grid of new styles at the bottom gives a quick visual "what's actually for sale" without overwhelming the hero section.

## What's weak

- Zero relevance to me — I run, this is 100% tennis, so as a send to my inbox it's a swing and a miss on targeting.
- No offer anywhere — no %, no BOGO, nothing. It's a pure launch/awareness email, which is fine for tennis players but gives me zero reason to act.
- Email is really long for the payoff — five full-bleed athlete photos before you even hit the product grid is a lot of scrolling for someone not in-category.
- "Bring the racket" as the hero line is cute but not concrete — doesn't tell me what's actually new (fabric tech? silhouette? price point?).

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment this — if lululemon knows I've only ever engaged with running content, don't send me the tennis drop at all. That's the single biggest lift here: relevance targeting, not creative.
- 2. Add a "Shop Running" module at the bottom for cross-category subscribers like me who aren't going to convert on tennis — give me a reason to stay in the email.
- 3. Trim the hero sequence — four full-bleed athlete shots before the product grid is heavy; two hero athletes + a "see all ambassadors" link would move people to product faster.
- 4. Put a concrete detail in the subhead — "lightest tennis fabric we've ever made" beats "gear so light" as a claim with actual substance.
