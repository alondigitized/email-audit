- So lululemon partnered with Megan Roup — founder of The Sculpt Society — and every single model, every single quote, every single piece of copy is built around a boxing-and-sculpt studio workout. - The quote "the fabric is so soft and lightweight, perfect for being on the move all day" is the closest thing to a fit callout, and it's about lifestyle movement, not pace or mileage. - Genuinely strong hero photography — the black-and-white boxing shot has real editorial polish, more premium-feeling than anything Nike's sent me.
- The Megan Roup / Sculpt Society partnership gives this a specific personality instead of generic catalog copy — I can tell who this is for, even though it's not me.
- "Join the movement" with the 5-day Sculpt Society trial + 20% off is a concrete, legible offer, clearly stated near the bottom.
- Clean, single-column layout, no rendering bugs, easy to scroll on a phone.

## What's weak

- Zero running content — no shoes I'd wear on a long run, no mileage or pace language, no club/community nod to people like me.
- The one sneaker visible is a lifestyle silhouette, not a trainer — actively signals "not a runner's email."
- This is boxing/sculpt-studio hype dressed as "New In" — if I'm on this list because I buy running gear, the targeting missed entirely.
- Offer (5-day trial, 20% off Sculpt Society) is a partner perk for a workout style I don't do — no relevance lever for me to pull.
- Fourth email in two weeks (following 9/1, 9/4, 9/6) with no running-specific product — cadence is fine, category fit isn't.

- 3. Swap the generic "Shop the Edit" CTA for something that routes runners to running product specifically, so the email can serve two audiences without diluting the hero story.
