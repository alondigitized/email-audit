# What’s new: Groove goes further
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-20**
## Executive summary

- My gut reaction was that this is a nice yoga email, and I don't do yoga. I run a club and buy for the Sunday long run. I scrolled a full screen of women in warm orange studio light, twisting and stretching. There's no stride, no road, no pace, and no shorts or tights I'd wear for 16 miles. It's well shot, but it isn't for me.
- It also doesn't help that this is the third "What's new" in two weeks, after 10/6 and 10/13, with a Pace Breaker send in between. On 10/14 the "New Pace Breaker styles, built tougher" hero showed a man mid-stride at dusk, and I wrote that it looked made for a runner. Now lululemon is back to the studio, a week after the 10/7 barre email and a month after the 9/22 Dance Studio Pants send. That's the fashion-shopper-in-running-gear problem I keep flagging. Nike would bury me in hype, but at least its emails know what a runner is. adidas's "Gear up for an active holiday season" (4/10) was filler too, but this one at least looks expensive.
- Would I click? No. Forward it? Only to my partner, who does yoga. I'd mark it read and wait for the next Pace Breaker or run-focused send.

## What's working

- The photography is gorgeous. The orange and rust palette is warm and cohesive, and it's the best-looking email in my inbox this week.
- The layout is clean: big hero, short headline, black pill button, repeat. It's easy to scan on a phone.
- Every image has its own labeled link ("Shop Groove Cinch →", "Shop Flow Y Bra →"). I know exactly where each tap goes.
- The "Nulu™ fabric brings softness so light, it feels uplifting" line gives me one real product detail, even if it's about yoga pants.
- The 2x3 grid at the bottom, "Shop new styles this week," is a quick way to see everything without hunting.

## What's weak

- There isn't a single running cue. It's all studio yoga, with no road, no shoes, no pace, no distance and no weather.
- There's no offer, no member perk and no reason to act today. Compare adidas's "We've got something for you," where the loyalty angle at least gave me something to act on.
- It's all women in all-yoga contexts. Nothing signals that a 33-year-old guy who runs is part of the audience.
- The copy is soft and vague ("Endlessly flexible yoga pants flow beyond"). It doesn't tell me whether the pants hold up on a cold morning or a long run.
- The cadence is heavy. A "What's new" every week, plus a studio theme repeating from 9/22 and 10/7, makes the sends blur together.
- There are no sizes, no prices and no availability. The grid at the bottom is just faces and clothes.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment by category.** Send me the run-adjacent version, with Pace Breaker shorts, Fast and Free tights and a long-run layering story. Keep the yoga hero for yoga buyers. This one change would lift more than everything else combined.
- 2. **Add one concrete offer or reason to act.** That could be member early access, a restock date or a low-size warning. Right now it's browsing with no pull.
- 3. **Give the headline real info.** Say what the product does ("four-way stretch, no-dig waistband") instead of "flow beyond."
- 4. **Vary the cadence and creative.** Don't stack studio themes back to back. Alternate yoga, run and train, the way Nike does with its category-led sends.
- 5. **Add price or "from $" labels** on the product grid so the tap feels informed.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Nulu fabric, 4 new fits, and the Flare you asked about`
