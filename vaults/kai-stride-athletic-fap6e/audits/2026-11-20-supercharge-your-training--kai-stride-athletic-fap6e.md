# Supercharge your training
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-20**
## Executive summary

- This is the first adidas email in a week that mentions running. I've been getting court shoes, Care Bears and Bad Bunny. The top half is a Hyperboost Edge feature with a Runner's World 2026 Shoe Awards badge on the hero. It describes a "non-plated super trainer" with "seamless heel-to-toe cushioning." That's language a run club captain actually uses, and it sounds like someone who knows what a daily trainer is for.
- Then it falls apart. There's no price, no member perk and no deadline. The bottom half switches to a Mercedes-AMG F1 leather bag giveaway, which has nothing to do with my Sunday long run. It reads like two emails stapled together. I'd click "Shop Hyperboost" out of curiosity, but I wouldn't forward it. I'd probably read the Runner's World review instead of buying off this.

## What's working

- The Runner's World 2026 Shoe Awards badge on the hero is third-party proof. A runner trusts it more than brand copy.
- "First adidas non-plated super trainer" is a real product claim, not hype. It tells me where the shoe sits in the lineup.
- The two-panel hero shows a runner in motion next to a close-up of the shoe. I can see the red-orange midsole and the stack.
- The copy is readable without zooming, and the black buttons are big, easy tap targets.
- Having both "Shop Hyperboost" and "Shop Marathon" lets me pick my lane.

## What's weak

- There's no price, offer or member benefit anywhere. Nike and lululemon at least give me a reason to act now. lululemon's "Black Friday Sale is in motion" had real prices, and this has nothing.
- The F1 bag giveaway is a hard left turn into lifestyle and motorsport. It dilutes the running message and I scrolled past it.
- The black "Explore the latest adidas best sellers" banner at the top competes with the Hyperboost hero. It's a generic best-sellers CTA sitting above a focused product story.
- The email never says which Hyperboost shoe it means. The headline is "Hyperboost Edge," but the button just says "Shop Hyperboost" with no price, sizing or availability.
- The very top line is an underlined text link that reads like leftover preheader text. It looks a bit unfinished.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the F1 giveaway, or move it to its own send. Give the running story the whole email. Add weight, drop, stack height and a price so I can decide without leaving.
- 2. Make the buttons specific. "Shop Hyperboost Edge – $X" beats a category dump.
- 3. Add a runner-voice proof point, like a club testimonial or a pacing and mileage use case ("built for easy miles and tempo days"). Nike would lean on its athlete story here, and adidas should lean on the Runner's World win.
- 4. Drop the generic best-sellers banner so the hero lands first.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Testers loved the heel-to-toe feel. See why it won.`
