# Cozy classroom essentials that set the tone
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-29**
## Executive summary

- Okay, "back to school" for the fourth time this month? I opened this expecting fresh, and instead I got Alijah Arenas — a basketball prospect I vaguely recognize but who has zero connection to running — shilling "back to school fits" that are just a hoodie and a basketball. Then the whole back half of the email pivots to the exact same $50-for-$40 gift card promo I already got on August 26th. That's not a new idea, that's a rerun with a different athlete slapped on top.
- The subject promised me specific fits an athlete is "rocking," which got a click out of curiosity, but the moment I saw "SHOP MEN / SHOP WOMEN / SHOP KIDS" with zero running product in sight, I checked out. Compare this to Nike's one-time-code email — boring, sure, but it did exactly what it said. This one dresses up a stale gift card offer with a basketball star and calls it back-to-school. Not clicking, not forwarding, marking as read.

## What's working

- The hero shot of Alijah Arenas is a real athlete, not a stock model — at least there's a face and a name attached.
- "SHOP NOW" button up top is a clear, unambiguous CTA with visible contrast.
- The $50-for-$40 gift card offer is at least a concrete, quantifiable discount, not vague "save big" fluff.
- Clean black-and-white layout, no rendering bugs, no overlapping text — technically it's a tidy send.

## What's weak

- Basketball athlete, hoodie-and-ball hero shot — there is nothing here for a runner. Not one running shoe, not one performance callout.
- "Back to school" themed for the third or fourth time this month per my inbox — adidas has now run this beat into the ground, and this send doesn't even have new hero product, just Arenas in a hoodie.
- The gift card promo is a straight copy-paste of the August 26th "$50 gift cards are only $40" email — same offer, same fine print, just a different banner treating it like new news.
- SHOP MEN/WOMEN/KIDS links go to a generic catalog, not to running gear or even basketball gear tied to the model — zero product specificity.
- No mention of adiClub points, no member pricing, nothing that says "we know you're a runner" — this reads like a mass blast, not a segmented send.

## Recommendations

- 1. Ditch the reused gift card block entirely — if I got the identical offer two days ago, running it again in a "cozy classroom" wrapper just tells me adidas doesn't track what it already sent me.
- 2. If you're going to use an athlete hook, use a runner — you have marathoners and track athletes in the roster, use one instead of basketball for a running-focused segment.
- 3. Replace generic SHOP MEN/WOMEN/KIDS links with a "SHOP RUNNING" CTA tied to actual training gear — leggings, tech tees, trainers — not a hoodie-and-basketball lifestyle shot.
- 4. Segment this list — adidas clearly has my running-club/performance profile from prior sends; use it instead of firing the same "back to school" template at everyone.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Alijah Arenas' hoodie, and 3 picks for your long run`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh layers for early morning miles`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus: your $50 gift card for $40, ends Friday`
