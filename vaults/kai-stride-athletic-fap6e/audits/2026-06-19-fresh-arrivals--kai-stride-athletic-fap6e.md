# Fresh arrivals
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- I opened this one and immediately felt the bait-and-switch. The subject says "Fresh arrivals" — fine, whatever, that's adidas being lazy with subject lines again — but the actual freshness here is three colorways of Toy Story Sambas. I'm a running club captain. I'm not cosplaying as a Pixar character on my long run. The hero shoe is beautiful if you're a sneakerhead, but I'm not shopping for a shelf piece. I'm shopping for something that can handle 50 miles a week.
- To be fair, there's one product in the grid that actually matters to me: the Adidas Dropset Pro Training Shoe at $150, sitting in the bottom-right corner like it snuck in through the back door. And then the giveaway section at the bottom mentions Adizero running shoes — buried so deep that most people scrolling fast on a phone will never see it. That's backwards. The runner product is the afterthought; the Pixar collab is the headliner.
- Compared to the June 16 "adiClub x FIFA Rewards" send — which at least knew it was talking to a loyalty member — this feels like a blast to the full list with zero segmentation. Mark as read and move on. Would not click, would not forward to anyone in my running crew.

## What's working

- **Star ratings are visible on every product card** — 4.8 on the hero Samba, 4.6 on the Junior version. That's the kind of social proof I scan for instantly.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean** — hero product first, grid below, giveaway at the bottom. The eye flows naturally without confusion.
- **The Adizero giveaway hook is genuinely interesting** — if it had been the *lead*, I'd have kept reading. A signed cleat + running shoes is a real runner prize.
- **Prices are clear and upfront** — $110, $90, $110, $150. No games.
- **No render bugs** — the email displays cleanly, images load, nothing is broken or overlapping.

## What's weak

- **Wrong hero for my segment** — "UPDATE YOUR SNEAKER ROSTER" is sneakerhead copy. I don't have a sneaker roster. I have a training log.
- **Three out of four grid products are the same Toy Story Samba collab** in different colorways — if you're going to send me a grid, diversify it. This reads like a clearinghouse for one SKU.
- **No offer** — no discount, no loyalty reward, no member exclusivity. lululemon's Beach Ball Blue email had no discount either but at least it sold me on a specific colorway story. This is just a price list.
- **"Fresh arrivals" as a subject line** is the laziest possible call — it says nothing about what's fresh, who it's for, or why I should care. Nike's transactional "here's your one-time code" was more useful and it was literally just a password email.
- **Dropset Pro Training and Adizero running shoes should both be above the fold** for a runner like me — they're invisible unless I scroll.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment this send.** If adidas knows I'm a runner (and adiClub should know that), I should never be the primary audience for a Toy Story Samba email. Send Sambas to the sneakerhead segment; send Adizeros and Dropsets to me. The June 13 "complete your profile" email was presumably collecting exactly this data — use it.
- 2. **Lead with the Adizero giveaway.** That's the runner hook. Flip the email: hero = signed cleat + Adizero win, secondary = "also, fresh Sambas if that's your thing." Now you're talking to multiple segments in one email without alienating either.
- 3. **Add one concrete offer.** Even adiClub points on fresh arrivals would do it. The June 18 "$30 on orders $100+" email had a mechanic I could do the math on in my head. This has nothing.
- 4. **Rewrite "UPDATE YOUR SNEAKER ROSTER"** to something that doesn't immediately signal "this email is not for you" to anyone who runs.

### 1. Technical Summary

One confirmed broken redirect and a missing plain-text body are the highest-severity issues; they create a broken click experience and risk inbox filtering. Several HTTP (non-HTTPS) asset URLs and absent RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe round out the findings.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken redirect — relative URL leaked as click destination**

The QA checker resolved a `dv.adidas.com` click-tracking URL to a relative path instead of an absolute `https://` URL:

```
Resolved destination: /g/243c7129-21f6-41c7-9ee0-a07bda1215c2?txn=4a4edf00-7119-11f1-9775-ae32f7ad8416
```

A relative path is not a valid standalone URL; the redirect chain is returning a location header without the scheme+host, which means the click lands nowhere (or 404s, depending on the client). The originating tracking URL:

```
https://dv.adidas.com/o/243c7129-21f6-41c7-9ee0-a07bda1215c2?cp_tp=v3.eJzj...
```

This is likely a misconfigured redirect rule in the adidas click-tracking service for this specific content block.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Four HTTP image loads (same asset, repeated)**

The arrow icon is loaded four times via HTTP, not HTTPS:

```
http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```

Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook all block mixed-content images by default. All four instances will render as broken images on affected clients.

**[WARN] Tracking pixel loaded over HTTP**


Open-tracking pixel via HTTP will be blocked by the same mixed-content rules, making open-rate data for this send unreliable.

**[WARN] Two images missing `alt` text**

- `dv.adidas.com/o/243c7129-21f6-41c7-9ee0-a07bda1215c2?...` — no `alt`
- `click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/...` (tracking pixel) — no `alt`

The first is a content image; a missing `alt` breaks the plain-text-images-off experience and fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.1.1.

**[WARN] `viewport` meta missing `width=device-width`**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="initial-scale=1.0">
```

Without `width=device-width`, iOS Mail may not respect the scale. Standard value: `width=device-width, initial-scale=1.0`.

**[INFO] `@font-face` declarations are no-ops**

Both `AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` are declared using only `local()` sources — no remote font URLs. These fonts are not installed on any typical end-user device, so the declarations silently fall through to Arial/Helvetica on every client. The blocks are inert but harmless. `font-display: swap` inside `@font-face` in email CSS has no effect (it's a browser-only hint).

**[INFO] `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">`**

This strips the referrer on all outbound clicks. If the landing page uses `document.referrer` for attribution logic, it will see an empty value. This may conflict with email-to-site analytics if the landing page is not purely relying on UTM params.

### 5. Compliance

**[FAIL] Plain-text fallback is absent**

QA confirmed the `text/plain` MIME part is 0 characters. CAN-SPAM does not explicitly require a plain-text part, but:
- Gmail and several ISPs use its absence as a spam signal.
- RFC 2045/2046 multipart/alternative etiquette requires a plain-text part.
- Certain accessibility tools and corporate email gateways only process plain text.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header not detected**

The relay may have stripped it, but if it is genuinely absent, this violates Gmail/Yahoo 2024 bulk sender requirements (mandatory for >5k/day sends). Expected format:

```
List-Unsubscribe: <mailto:...>, <https://...>
List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click
```

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` absent (RFC 8058)**

One-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail and Yahoo for bulk senders as of February 2024. Without it, Gmail surfaces its own unsubscribe UI, which may route to a spam report instead.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header not captured**

SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown due to the AgentMail relay. The sending domain `us-news.comms.adidas.com` should have SPF and DKIM aligned to `adidas.com` under DMARC. Cannot confirm without raw headers.

**[INFO]** `<meta name="ROBOTS" content="NOINDEX, NOFOLLOW">` is present — correct practice to prevent email archive indexing.
