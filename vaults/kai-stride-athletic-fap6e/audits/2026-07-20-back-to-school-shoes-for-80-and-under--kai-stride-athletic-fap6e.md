# Back to school shoes for $80 and under
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## What's working

- **Price anchor is concrete and visible** — "$80 AND UNDER SNEAKERS" lands immediately, no hunting for the offer.
- **SHOP MEN is unambiguous** — gender-split CTAs are exactly right for this catalog size; no "shop all" laziness.
- **Render is clean** — no broken images, no overlapping copy, layout holds at this width.
- **adiClub 2x points perk is visible** — loyalty members can see there's a stacking benefit in the app section, which is a legitimate hook.
- **Timing is defensible** — Back-to-school in late July is seasonally correct, even if the audience targeting isn't.

## What's weak

- **Zero running DNA** — not a single running shoe, no performance copy, no acknowledgment that I'm a subscriber who clicks on Adizero content. lululemon's "Summer Sale, on its final run" at least used runner language; this email doesn't try.
- **Cadence fatigue on the 25% off app pitch** — this is the exact same offer from July 15, just dressed up. If I didn't download the app then, a back-to-school rebrand isn't changing that.
- **Lifestyle sneaker hero** — the flat-lay imagery reads teenager, not 33-year-old who just got back from a 20-miler. Demographic signals are off.
- **No deadline visible** — the 25% off section says "for a limited time" but gives no date. The July 15 send was the same. Nike's transactional emails at least have timestamps; adidas leaves this floating.
- **Whiplash from yesterday** — going from FIFA World Champions cinematics to classroom sneakers in 24 hours is jarring. The brand voice swings hard and loses coherence.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment this send.** Running subscribers should not get the back-to-school lifestyle push. Pull Kai Stride types out of this list entirely and hold for the next Adizero or Ultraboost drop. This is the single highest-leverage change.
- 2. **Give the 25% off a hard deadline.** "For a limited time" is the weakest urgency signal in email marketing. Put a date on it — Nike's transactional emails do this, even the bare-bones ones.
- 3. **If you must send to runners, pivot the hero.** Even one shot of a road running shoe in the $80-under range would make this worth opening. The Dropset Pro email (July 5, the one I actually forwarded) showed how to do this.
- 4. **Fix the preheader.** "Explore the latest adidas best sellers" is the banner ad leaking into the inbox — that's not a preheader, it's dead space.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your fall training shoes, $80 and under`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Long run. New shoes. Under $80.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `adiClub members: 2x points + 25% off in app — ends [date]`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Road-ready picks under $80. Shop men's now.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

Modern email clients (Gmail, Outlook 365, Apple Mail) enforce mixed-content blocking. All six instances will fail to load in any client that proxies images over HTTPS. The image should be served from `https://`.
