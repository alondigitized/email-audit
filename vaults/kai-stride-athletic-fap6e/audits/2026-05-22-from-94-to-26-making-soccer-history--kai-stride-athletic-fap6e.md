# From '94 to '26, making soccer history
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- Six emails in seven days from adidas, and this one at least has a legitimate cultural anchor — FIFA World Cup 2026 is happening on home soil, and the US94 heritage play is genuinely smart calendar marketing. The "From '94 to '26" headline is clean, the nostalgia arc is earned, and this is miles ahead of the F1 collab from two days ago, which had zero narrative logic for a runner in my adiClub segment.
- But I run. I captain a running club. My adidas footwear rotation is the Adizero, not the Tiro 24. Adidas literally makes the racing flat I wore at my last half, and six emails in, they still think I'm a soccer supporter looking for a throwback jersey. The adiClub x Prime Giveaway at the bottom is the first thing they've sent me all week that feels like a member benefit with real dollar value — and they buried it below two soccer sections.
- Mark as read. No click. Won't forward. If a friend in the running club happens to be into soccer nostalgia, maybe — but I'm not that friend.

## What's working

- The "From '94 to '26" headline is a 32-year arc told in six characters — legitimately good copywriting, more story than adidas usually brings
- FIFA World Cup 2026 timing is airtight; this isn't a stale promo, it's calendared to a real event and the campaign has the receipts
- adiClub x Prime Giveaway is a genuinely substantial member benefit — $2,000 in adidas + Amazon gift cards is a real number, not a token 10% off like what they dangled in the welcome email earlier this week
- All three CTAs (SHOP NOW, SHOP ALL FIFA WORLD CUP 2026, ENTER NOW) render as clean, legible buttons — no ambiguity about the intended action
- Email renders without a single visual glitch — no broken images, no collapsed layout; cleaner than a lot of what Nike serves on mobile

## What's weak

- I run 50-plus miles a week and adidas hasn't mentioned a single running product across six emails — Lululemon does this too, but at least they're a lifestyle brand; adidas actually makes performance footwear I race in
- The giveaway is the most relevant section for me as a member and it's dead last — buried below two screens of soccer jerseys I have no purchase intent for
- "Enter now for a chance to win" is pure sweepstakes language with no deadline visible; Nike member pricing feels like earned access, this feels like a raffle where clicking feels optional
- "Shop online or at your local adidas store" is a generic locator CTA that lands flat in 2026 — Nike would have linked me to a geo-targeted store page or at minimum a filtered category page, not a homepage

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the giveaway for adiClub members** — if segmentation data shows I'm a member, that section earns the hero slot; the licensed product is a secondary play for soccer fans
- 2. **Add a deadline to the giveaway** — "Enter by June 8" creates urgency that converts today; "Enter now" means I'll do it later and then forget
- 3. **One sentence that bridges soccer energy to running** — something like "Whether you're on the pitch or the pavement, this is your summer" keeps me in the email instead of clicking away at the jersey section
- 4. **Subject Alt A:** `Win $2K in gear — adiClub x Prime giveaway`
- 5. **Subject Alt B:** `World Cup summer + a $2,000 giveaway for members`
- 6. **Preheader Alt A:** `Enter now for a $2,000 adidas + Amazon prize box`
- 7. **Preheader Alt B:** `US94 is back. Plus a giveaway you'll want to enter.`

## Technical Audit — adidas "From '94 to '26" (2026-05-22)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

One confirmed broken redirect link will result in a dead click for affected recipients. Three additional infrastructure issues (missing plain-text body, HTTP image assets, absent unsubscribe headers) pose deliverability and compliance risk.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**FAIL — Broken redirect chain**

The QA probe resolved the following click-tracker to a destination containing a relative URL path instead of an absolute URL:

```
Outer redirect: https://dv.adidas.com/o/3a1e0220-2c7b-41a0-9a03-fc6a07c9d775?cp_tp=v3.eJ...
Inner destination (decoded): /g/3a1e0220-2c7b-41a0-9a03-fc6a07c9d775?txn=98aa2300-562d-11f1-9d5b-4e61e507eb6e
```

The inner URL `/g/3a1e0220...` is a bare path — no scheme, no host. The redirect host (`dv.adidas.com`) may resolve it relative to itself, but this depends on server-side handling and is not safe to assume. Any recipient clicking a CTA wired to this token will likely land on an error page or incorrect URL.

**INFO — 36 tracking links not probed**

All links through `click.comms.adidas.com` were skipped. UTM parameter presence on final landing pages is unverified.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**WARN — 8 HTTP image sources (same asset, repeated)**

The arrow CTA icon is embedded 8 times via HTTP, all pointing to the same origin:

```
src: http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```

Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail all block mixed-content (HTTP) images by default when the email is rendered over HTTPS. This asset will silently fail to load in those clients.

**WARN — Pixel tracker served over HTTP**

```
src: http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019e51b98199-27a07dff-d0a8-4c73-ba93-a68d66d71283-000000/wuxhDdNG8i4QOAkBf5KYeXfC0...
```

Open-tracking pixel is HTTP. Same mixed-content blocking applies; open rates will be undercounted in clients that enforce HTTPS.

**WARN — 2 images missing `alt` text**

- Pixel tracker (`wuxhDdNG8i4QOAkBf5KYeXfC0JO...`) — expected to be empty string `alt=""` for decorative/tracking pixels.
- Hero/content image (`3a1e0220-2c7b-41a0-9a03-fc6a07c9d775`) — missing `alt` entirely. Screen readers will either skip it or read the raw filename.

**INFO — HTML 4.01 Transitional DOCTYPE**

```html
<!DOCTYPE html PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN" "http://www.w3.org/TR/html4/loose.dtd">
```

Intentional for broad email client compatibility. No action needed, but noted.

**INFO — Custom fonts (`AdihausDIN`, `AdineuePRO`) use only `local()` sources**

All `@font-face` declarations reference `local()` only (Arial/Helvetica/Calibri as fallbacks). Web fonts will never load; fallback stack renders everywhere. Likely intentional — no issue.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `[EMAIL]`) visible in the truncated HTML source. No issues found.

---

### 5. Compliance

**FAIL — Plain-text fallback is 0 characters**

QA confirmed the `text/plain` MIME part is empty. CAN-SPAM does not mandate plain-text, but a missing part increases spam-filter score across multiple providers (SpamAssassin rule `MIME_NO_TEXT`). Some corporate mail gateways reject zero-length plain parts outright.

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe` header not captured**

Header was not present in the relay capture. If it is genuinely absent from the delivered message, this violates Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender requirements (enforced since February 2024 for senders >5k/day). Requires verification against raw SMTP headers, not just relay metadata.

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found**

One-click unsubscribe (the `POST` variant required by Gmail bulk sender policy) was not detected. Same caveat — verify raw headers before treating as confirmed absent.

**WARN — `Authentication-Results` not captured (SPF/DKIM/DMARC status unknown)**

Sending domain is `us-news.comms.adidas.com`; the relay did not surface authentication results. SPF/DKIM alignment should be confirmed via a direct header dump from a test send.

**INFO — `ROBOTS: NOINDEX, NOFOLLOW` and `referrer: no-referrer` present** — correct.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Unverified — UTM parameters on 36 click-tracked links**

All links route through `click.comms.adidas.com`. Because the QA prober skipped these (tracking domain), it is unknown whether UTM parameters survive the redirect chain to the final `adidas.com` destination. The one resolved link (`dv.adidas.com/o/...`) carries `cp_tp` and `cp_cid` (adidas internal campaign params) but no standard `utm_source`/`utm_medium`/`utm_campaign` were visible in the decoded payload.

**FAIL — Broken link above has no valid landing page** — the `/g/...` relative URL destination is unresolvable as discussed in §2.
