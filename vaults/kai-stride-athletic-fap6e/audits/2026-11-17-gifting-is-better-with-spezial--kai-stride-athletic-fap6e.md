## Executive summary

- My gut reaction: it's a gift email for a sneaker I'm not going to run in. The Handball Spezial is a court-and-street shoe, and I captain a run club. Nothing here says running. The countdown timer (1 day, 8 hours) made me look for a few seconds, and then I saw it was a Black Friday banner stuck on top of a lifestyle email.
- It's the fifth adidas send in a row where I got lifestyle product and no runner. The 11/16 BadBo Bad Bunny collab was a sneaker drop. The Care Bears email was baby tracksuits. The 11/15 "big race 🏁" email had no race in it. The 11/14 "up to 60% off" email had no product. This one at least has a real shoe, but it's still not for my Sunday long run.

## What's working

- The black countdown banner is the first thing I see. "Up to 60% Off for Members" is a clear, specific offer.
- The pink Spezial hero shot is a nice photo. It's clean and the shoe is easy to read.
- The preheader link works as a hook. "Iconic design, everyday comfort" is honest about what the shoe is.
- The text is big and easy to read on a phone, and the black buttons are easy to tap.
- The adidas logo is centered up top, so I know who sent it right away.

## What's weak

- There's no running anywhere. No runner, no run shoe, no mention of Adizero or Boston. adidas knows I'm a run person and still sent me this.
- The hero is a women's-style pink suede shoe. The Men / Women / Kids split doesn't tell me which shoe I'm looking at, and none of the buttons lead to one specific product.
- The three buttons are stacked and look the same. Three equal CTAs with no price in any of them feels like a catalog.
- Three of the apparel models are in cropped, oversized fashion looks. That's the opposite of how I dress for a run.
- The "Win an exclusive adiSignature style kit" giveaway buries the real offer. After the 60% banner, I have to wonder whether I'm shopping or entering a raffle. The 11/16 "We've got something for you" email had the same raffle problem.
- The 60% banner has no product tied to it, and "up to" with no examples gives me nothing to trust.

- 1. Segment by purchase history. If I've bought running shoes, lead with something like Adizero Evo SL or Ultraboost with the member price shown. Most of the lift is in showing me my own category.
- 2. Put one real price on the 60% offer. "Spezial $67, was $100" would do more than "up to".
- 3. Replace the three Shop Men / Women / Kids buttons with one hero link to the Spezial and one secondary link to the sale.
- 4. Move the giveaway lower or send it as its own email.
- 5. Add size availability for the hero shoe. Even "most sizes in stock" would help.

- **No destination URLs are visible in the truncated source**, so I can't confirm any broken link.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Plain-text fallback is missing.** QA reports a text version of 0 chars. Text-only clients get nothing, and some spam filters penalize HTML-only mail.
- **Two images have no alt text:**
  - `https://dv.adidas.com/o/cbf6dd86-30bc-4a6a-87d8-f146f994ce79?cp_tp=...`
