# adiClub September Newsletter: See what’s new this month
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-23**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this one's actually a decent surprise — the hero is a Hyperboost Euphoria running shoe on a green adiClub background, "September Shoe of the Month Giveaway." That's the second running-relevant send in a row after the Adizero Adios Pro 5 email on 9/23, so maybe adidas is finally figuring out I run more than I collab-shop. But then it's a giveaway, not a sale — I have to enter to "win a chance" at a shoe, not just buy one, and the newsletter immediately buries that under a Tennessee Vols football kit, signed footballs from rookies I don't follow, and a hybrid training backpack. It's a grab-bag, not a running newsletter.
- The one thing that actually made me sit up: adiClub x Strava, "Connect Accounts, Get More Rewards." That's a real running-club-captain hook — I track every long run on Strava, and if adidas is tying points to real GPS activity instead of just purchases, that's the first genuinely club-relevant feature I've seen from them all month.
- Overall: I'd open it (shoe hero caught my eye), I'd skim past the football/Tennessee/hybrid stuff, and I'd probably click "Connect Accounts" out of curiosity. Wouldn't forward it — it's not exciting enough for the group chat, and it's not a shoe I can just go buy.

## What's working

- The hero shoe shot (Hyperboost Euphoria) is clean, well-lit, and unmistakably a running shoe — no confusion like the Care Bears Superstar debacle from 9/22.
- adiClub x Strava integration is genuinely on-brand for me — a running club captain wants his GPS data to count for something.
- Clear black CTA buttons throughout ("ENTER NOW," "CONNECT ACCOUNTS") — no ambiguity about what to tap.
- "adiClub" branding front and center tells me this is a loyalty/member email, not a blind blast — feels earned since I'm signed up.

## What's weak

- The headline offer is a giveaway/sweepstakes, not a shoe I can actually buy — "enter for your chance to win" is a much weaker hook than a real discount or drop.
- Tennessee Athletics Legends giveaway and signed footballs are pure noise for a runner — I don't care about Vols merch or rookie NFL players.
- The "Go Full Hybrid Kit" backpack giveaway again isn't running gear, it's general training — dilutes the one running section further.
- No pricing, no sizing, no availability info on the shoe itself — I can't even shop it, just enter a raffle.
- Four giveaways stacked in one email starts to feel like a lottery ticket dispenser, not a newsletter for people who actually run.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the adiClub x Strava integration as its own hero — that's the most differentiated, running-specific hook in the whole email and it's buried near the bottom. Nike's account-linking emails (like the one-time code send I scored 6/10) win because they're fast and functional; adidas should make Strava-connect the star, not an afterthought.
- 2. Split this into a running-specific adiClub send and a general "everything giveaway" send — right now it's four unrelated raffles crammed together, which is exactly the unfocused pattern that's dogged this brand since the Care Bears/Z.N.E. stretch.
- 3. Add a real "Shop the Hyperboost Euphoria" CTA alongside the giveaway — let me buy it now instead of only entering a sweepstakes.
- 4. Cut the Tennessee Athletics and signed-football sections entirely from a newsletter that opens with a running shoe — it whiplashes the reader.
