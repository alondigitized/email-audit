# The adidas Football x Hellstar drop is here
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-05**
## Executive summary

- Football cleats and an NFL rookie giveaway. I captain a running club, so my gut reaction was "wrong sport, wrong inbox," again. This is the fifth adidas send in about a week that isn't for me: the Pikachu collab on 9/30, the cow-print Samba on 10/2, and the rhinestone SL72 on 10/4. Only the Chicago running email on 10/3 was relevant. The Hellstar cleat photo is moody and well shot, but I don't play football and Hellstar isn't my lane.
- The second half is a loyalty giveaway that reads like a different email stapled on. The same adiClub raffle idea showed up in the "We've got something for you" email, where my 50 points weren't enough for anything. Here I get three rookie names and two buttons, "Learn More" and "Participate," with no mention of what I'd win or how to enter. Nike would have picked one job per send. lululemon would have made me feel something about the product. This one asks me to do two things, and I don't care about either.
- I'd mark it read. I wouldn't click and I wouldn't forward it. My one friend who plays flag football gets a pass, but the cleats aren't even priced.

## What's working

- The hero shot of the cleats on dark turf is striking, and the flame-pattern upper reads as a real collab, not a logo slap.
- The "ADIDAS X HELLSTAR" headline is big and clear, so I understood the drop in about a second.
- The black CTA buttons are chunky and easy to tap on a phone.
- The rookie portraits are warm and well lit, with an editorial feel that fits sports culture.

## What's weak

- Nothing here is for runners. It's cleats and a football giveaway, so the "performance vs. lifestyle" question doesn't even come up.
- The black "Explore the latest adidas best sellers" banner at the top has a "Shop Now" button that competes with the hero. It also looks like a generic template block.
- There are three different "Shop Now" or "Learn More" asks, and none of them is specific. I don't know the price, the sizes or the release date.
- The giveaway copy is vague. "Unlocks fresh prizes, new stories" says nothing about what I win, how many points it costs or when it ends. The "II" in the headline wraps onto its own line, which looks sloppy.
- The preheader link at the very top is tiny underlined text with emojis, and it looks unfinished.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split this into two sends. Send the Hellstar drop to football and streetwear segments, and send the rookie giveaway to adiClub members as its own email.
- 2. Cut the giveaway to one CTA, a single "Enter the giveaway" button that says what's at stake, such as the prize and the deadline.
- 3. State the price, the release date and the sizes on the cleat hero. Hype buyers want the drop details up front.
- 4. Remove the generic best-sellers banner, or turn it into a segment-aware strip. For me, that would be Adizero Evo SL or Adios Pro.
- 5. Use runner segmentation. Someone who bought Adizero shouldn't be getting this many fashion and football sends in a row.
