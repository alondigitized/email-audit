# What’s new: Your Groove’s back
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- I opened this because lululemon is in my rotation and I'll give them the benefit of the doubt — but about three seconds in I realized this email wasn't written for me. At all. The Groove Wide-Leg is a women's product. The tote bag is a women's product. Every model I can see is a woman. I'm a guy who buys the Surge Short and the Metal Vent Tech tee. The only moment where this email even acknowledges the Summer Sale is a small orange "SUMMER SALE" banner about two-thirds down — after three full women's product blocks I'd already scrolled past in confusion.
- This is the fifth lululemon email in roughly ten days and it's the first one that felt like it landed in the completely wrong inbox. The June 30 "hottest picks" send was the same Summer Sale cadence but at least the orange creative made it obvious there was a deal. This one opens on wide-leg trousers and a tote bag — and I'm a runner. The gap between brand (which I trust) and execution (which missed me entirely) is wide.
- I'm marking this read. Not clicking, not forwarding. If a female runner in my club got this I'd say "yeah, that's actually about you," but it's not about me.

## What's working

- **Brand familiarity:** lululemon logo, clean typesetting, that minimalist editorial layout — I know where I am immediately.
- **Nulu fabric callout:** "Buttery soft Nulu™ fabric stretches above and beyond" is the kind of material-level specificity that makes lululemon feel like they know their product. That's the seminar-tone I value versus Nike's hype machine.
- **Summer Sale banner lands above the fold-ish:** The orange SUMMER SALE block does appear mid-email and at least signals a sale exists.
- **No render bugs:** Clean layout, images load, text isn't overlapping anything — technically sound send.

## What's weak

- **100% women's product, men's subscriber:** Groove Wide-Leg, Nulu women's wide-leg, Ruched Tote Bag — not one men's running item in sight above the fold or mid-scroll.
- **"Your Groove's back" means nothing to me:** I associate Groove with the women's pant line. If lululemon is targeting me on a list, this subject line is a miss — I thought maybe it was a vibe thing, not a literal product name.
- **Summer Sale buried and vague:** Adidas led with "30% off" in the subject line and I opened in two seconds. lululemon hides "SUMMER SALE" in block three with no percentage, no dollar figure. Classic lululemon luxury restraint, but when adidas is running concrete math in the subject, the restraint starts to feel evasive.
- **Tote bag feels fashion-forward, not runner-forward:** After the June 21 "restock" email that I opened expecting Surge Short restocks and got lifestyle gear, this is the same let-down, different item.
- **Cadence fatigue:** This is email five in ten days. Even as a loyalist, I'm starting to skim-and-archive faster than I normally would.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment by gender before sending.** This should never hit a male subscriber's inbox. A men's block — ABC Jogger restock, Metal Vent Tech, Surge Short — could live in this exact template with zero other changes.
- 2. **Lead with a concrete sale mechanic.** The Summer Sale banner with no discount figure is doing almost nothing. Even "up to 40% off" would give me a reason to scroll.
- 3. **Hero swap:** Replace the Groove Wide-Leg block with men's running product for the men's segment. Women get the Groove hero. Men get the Pace Breaker or Surge hero. Same template, audience-split.
- 4. **Reduce send frequency or consolidate:** Five emails in ten days is burning list equity. Bundle the Sale + New Arrivals into one send.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issue — invalid `letter-spacing` values:**
Three classes use percentage values for `letter-spacing`, which is not a valid CSS unit for that property (only length values like `px`/`em` are accepted per spec):

```css
.guest-voice-lg { letter-spacing: -2%; }   /* invalid */
.guest-voice-md { letter-spacing: -2%; }   /* invalid */
.guest-voice-sm { letter-spacing: -1%; }   /* invalid */
```

These declarations will be silently ignored by all rendering engines, meaning the intended tight tracking on the serif "guest voice" headlines won't apply. Replace with `em` values (e.g., `-0.02em`).

**Web fonts — fallback chain is sound, but load in email is client-dependent:**
Five custom fonts are referenced (`Saans-Regular`, `Saans-Medium`, `Saans-Semibold`, `Saans-Light`, `KHGiga-Light`) with no `@font-face` or `<link>` visible in the truncated `<head>`. Gmail strips `<link>` tags and `@import` rules, so custom fonts will not load in Gmail. The fallback stacks (`Helvetica Neue, sans-serif` / `Times New Roman, serif`) are correct and will render safely — no action required unless brand requires serif rendering in Gmail.

**`h4` excluded from heading reset:**
`h1, h2, h3, h5` share a combined reset rule; `h4` has a separate rule using `'Saans-Light'` instead of `'Saans-Regular'`. This appears intentional (stylistic differentiation) but is worth confirming against the design spec since it's easy to introduce `<h4>` elements expecting the standard reset.
