# Anthony Edwards 3 With Love
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-08**
## Executive summary

- This is a basketball shoe email, and I run a running club. My gut reaction was "not for me." It's the sixth or seventh adidas email in about two weeks, and after the Week of Deals blasts (the 11/05 "last call" and the 10/31 one) I was hoping for Adizero or Adios. I got a hooper's sneaker. Ant's Hyperboost is a nice piece of tech, but I'm not putting it on at 6 AM for a long run.
- The hero shoe is shot well. The black-to-peach glow is the best creative adidas has sent me in this run of emails. But the page has no price, no release date, no sizing and no reviews, and the copy is mostly slogan. Compare Nike, which at least tells me what a shoe does for my stride. This email doesn't tell me why I should care unless I already follow Ant.
- I wouldn't click and I wouldn't forward it. I'd mark it read. If I played ball, I'd maybe tap through once for the Hyperboost, but only because I'm curious whether it ever shows up in a runner.

## What's working

- The hero shot is striking. The outsole lit orange on black is a real product image, not a lifestyle blur.
- The copy has a spec: "Hyperboost… high cushioning and high energy return… lightweight." That's the one line that talks like a product.
- It's one product with one story. That's cleaner than the gift-catalog feel of the 10/31 Week of Deals email.
- Two CTAs, Shop Now and Find Store Now, are big black buttons that are easy to tap on a phone.
- The store list in the fine print tells me this is a real in-store launch.

## What's weak

- It's basketball. There's no running angle at all, and my last four adidas reads had the same problem (soccer F50 on 10/29, lifestyle on 10/30).
- There's no price or release date, and nothing about sizing. I'd have to click to learn anything useful.
- The "Explore the latest adidas best sellers" banner at the top sends mixed signals and competes with the hero.
- The model photo is a guy pulling at a track jacket. It's generic fashion and has nothing to do with the shoe above it.
- The "Where you from is how you play. Believe That." line is hype with nothing behind it. Nike does hype better, and lululemon at least gave me the winter drop with runners on a dirt road.
- The fine print is a long list of malls, and "first-come, first-served… while supplies last" feels like a hedge.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the price and the launch date in the headline area. "$140, in stores and online 11/8" would beat the slogan.
- 2. Cut the "best sellers" banner, or segment it. Send running-affinity subscribers a run-relevant section. I buy for the long run, and adidas knows that from my history.
- 3. Replace the jacket model shot with the shoe on court, or a second angle with sizing and colorway chips.
- 4. Collapse the two CTAs into one. Make Find Store a text link.
- 5. Add a Hyperboost "what it means" line so the tech has a payoff: what it does for runners and what it does for players.

## 1. Technical Summary

The HTML is truncated, so findings about the body below the head are limited to what the QA data shows.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unprobed tracking links:** 18 links on `click.comms.adidas.com` (path prefix `/CI0/...`) were skipped as click-redirect domains. Destinations, redirect chains and final status codes are unverified.
- **Insecure tracking host:** at least one tracked asset is requested over plain HTTP: `http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/010201a1b8b6514b-f71c68ad-.../iL0yVmABEdp2nkCP_GbBgFZDk...=452`. It is an `<img>` with no alt text, which looks like the open-tracking pixel. Clients that block mixed or non-HTTPS content may fail to record opens.
- **No other defects found:** the QA data shows no broken or malformed `href`s.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **[WARN] Non-HTTPS images:** the "arrow" image `http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg` is flagged four times. It is the same asset, reused four times, and the host is HTTP-only in the source. Some clients may block or rewrite it, so the arrow icons could drop out.
- **[WARN] Missing alt text:**
  - `https://dv.adidas.com/o/9bb6976d-ef9d-4d38-a681-69929b2463ba?cp_tp=...` has no `alt`. It appears to be a content image served from `dv.adidas.com`. With images blocked, screen readers and image-off clients get nothing for it.
  - The tracking pixel (`...=452`) also has no alt. An empty `alt=""` would be correct there, but the attribute is absent.
- **Viewport meta:** `<meta name="viewport" content="initial-scale=1.0">` omits `width=device-width`. Behavior varies by client, and the mobile media queries may not apply consistently.
- **Fonts:** `AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` are declared with `src: local('Arial'), local('Helvetica')...`, so no webfont is actually loaded. They always render as Arial or Helvetica. This is harmless, but the `@font-face` blocks, duplicated in a second `<style>`, add weight without benefit.
- **Doctype:** HTML 4.01 Transitional is acceptable for email and not an issue in itself.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved merge tokens (`{{...}}`, `%%...%%`, `[FIRSTNAME]`) appear in the visible source or QA output. Body personalization can't be confirmed, since the HTML is truncated.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
