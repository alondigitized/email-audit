# The US94 Official Licensed Product Collection
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-27**
## Executive summary

- This is the second US94 soccer nostalgia email in 10 days. Adidas ran basically the same '94 throwback angle on June 20 — "Born from the spirit of '94, built for the lifestyle expression of today" — and now they're back with an Official Licensed Product label stapled on top. If I weren't paying attention, this would have scrolled past me as a duplicate. I'm a runner. 5am Saturday long-run guy. Soccer jerseys are not my purchase category, and adidas has enough purchase data on me to know that.
- The $30 off $100 is the one thing that catches me — but I saw that exact offer on June 18 with code DEAL. There's zero acknowledgment in this email that it was extended or refreshed. So now I'm wondering if the code still works, or if adidas just didn't clean up their template. That's trust friction adidas can't afford after the adiClub June 16 email set such a high bar.
- Would I click? No. Would I forward? Only to my buddy who coaches a U12 soccer team — and even he's already seen the collection.

## What's working

- The $30 off $100 offer is immediately legible; simple math, no squinting required
- The jersey artwork is crisp and the '94 USA colorway is genuinely iconic — clean render, no broken images
- "SHOP ALL FIFA WORLD CUP 2026™ >" is the most useful CTA in the whole email; at least it's a specific destination rather than a homepage dump
- World Cup 2026 is live and topical — adidas isn't flogging something stale, the campaign hook is timely

## What's weak

- Three CTAs stacked in the hero — SHOP NOW, SHOP ALL FIFA WORLD CUP 2026, FIND STORE NOW — is decision paralysis baked into a single scroll; lululemon's Summer Sale email had one hero action and it was cleaner for it
- The preheader appears to be a broken fallback string: "For this necessary visit our website" — that is not copy, it's a dead template variable, and it's the first thing my inbox shows next to the subject line
- Same code DEAL from June 18 with no context that it was extended; Nike's transactional emails always state exactly when a code expires, adidas should too
- This is the second US94 soccer email in 10 days — I'm a running-club captain getting World Cup'd into the ground
- Zero running product, zero running context, zero signal that adidas knows I'm a runner

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment this send.** Running-focused buyers should not be in this list. If adidas wants me in a World Cup email, give me the Ultraboost "FIFA Pack" or the trail shoe with a US94 colorway — that exists, use it.
- 2. **Collapse the hero to one CTA.** SHOP ALL FIFA WORLD CUP 2026 is the right one — specific, timely, confident. Kill the other two.
- 3. **Explicitly acknowledge the DEAL code is extended.** "We extended it — code DEAL still works through Sunday" builds trust instead of leaving me wondering if the template is stale.
- 4. **Fix the preheader before send.** This is a QA failure, not a design call. Any preview text beats "For this necessary visit our website."
- 5. **Add one runner-relevant hook even in a soccer-first email.** A single "Also running this week →" module at the bottom costs nothing and keeps me from feeling like I'm on the wrong list.

## Technical Audit — adidas "US94 Official Licensed Product Collection"

### 1. Technical Summary

One broken redirect link and a missing plain-text fallback are confirmed hard failures. Nine HTTP image sources and two missing alt attributes are confirmed rendering/accessibility issues. Compliance headers were not captured by the relay, requiring sender-side verification.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken redirect — malformed internal path**
The QA resolver surfaced `unknown url type: '/g/fb6625a2-5c12-474a-8530-7e9dac31468a?txn=5816b280-74d3-11f1-a016-9651305e4573'` for the click-tracking URL that resolves through `https://dv.adidas.com/o/...`. The `/g/` path is being parsed as a relative URL, indicating the adidas dynamic content redirector (dv.adidas.com) is returning a relative location header rather than an absolute URL. This will produce a dead link in clients that do not resolve relative redirects. Confirmed URL:
```
https://dv.adidas.com/o/fb6625a2-5c12-474a-8530-7e9dac31468a?cp_tp=v3.eJzj...
```

**[WARN] 35 tracking links not probed** — all route through `click.comms.adidas.com`. Not an error, but means link health for the bulk of CTAs is unverified.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[FAIL] 9× HTTP image sources — mixed content**
The arrow icon (`b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg`) is referenced **8 times** via plain HTTP:
```
http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```
A ninth HTTP image is the open/track pixel routed via `click.comms.adidas.com`. Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail all block mixed-content images by default; the arrow will silently fail to render in these clients.

**[WARN] 2 images missing `alt` text**
- Dynamic content image: `https://dv.adidas.com/o/fb6625a2-5c12-474a-8530-7e9dac31468a?...`
- Tracking pixel: `http://click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/0102019f07429253-...`

The first is a visible content image; missing `alt` breaks accessibility for screen readers and degrades images-off rendering.

**[INFO] Duplicate `@font-face` declarations**
`AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` are declared twice — once in the main `<style>` block and again in the Yahoo Android App comment block, with identical `src: local(...)` fallbacks. No web font URLs are present in either declaration; both custom font families silently fall back to Arial/Helvetica in all clients. Not a breakage, but the declarations are dead weight.

**[INFO] Legacy DOCTYPE**
`HTML 4.01 Transitional` is used. Gmail strips the `<html>` and `<head>` anyway, but this DOCTYPE triggers quirks-mode parsing in some webmail clients. Modern email best practice is to omit DOCTYPE or use the HTML5 doctype.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header not captured**
The QA relay did not observe a `List-Unsubscribe` header. If absent at delivery, this violates Google/Yahoo bulk sender requirements (mandatory for >5K/day senders) and CAN-SPAM best practice. The relay may have stripped it — sender must confirm via raw message headers at the MTA.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not captured (RFC 8058)**
One-click unsubscribe (required by Google/Yahoo for bulk senders since February 2024) cannot be confirmed. Same caveat about relay stripping applies.

**[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header not found**
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status are unknown due to relay interception. Sending domain `us-news.comms.adidas.com` must be verified to have valid DKIM signature and SPF alignment independently.

**[INFO] `<meta name="ROBOTS" content="NOINDEX, NOFOLLOW">` present** — correct practice for email HTML.

**[INFO] `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` present** — suppresses referrer on link clicks, which is intentional but means UTM attribution relies entirely on link parameters.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The broken `dv.adidas.com/o/` redirect (§2) is the primary continuity risk — if the `/g/` relative redirect is not resolved server-side, users clicking that CTA reach a dead end rather than the product landing page.

The `no-referrer` meta tag means all attribution depends on those parameters being correctly appended by the ESP — no browser referrer fallback exists.
