# New Mesh Soft Jersey gear, airing now
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-13**
## Executive summary

- I'm a lululemon regular — club kit, long-run shorts, recovery fits, the whole rotation. So the sender alone gets this opened, no question. But once I'm inside, I'm doing that slow-blink: this is breathable casual wear, not running gear. The mesh and ventilation angle is genuinely relevant — anything that moves air during a humid 12-miler has my attention — but the styling says "look athletic at the farmers market," not "survive August intervals." The models are standing cool under a blue sky. Nobody is running. Nobody is sweating.
- The execution is clean. That's the lululemon seminar voice I respect — no hype, no exclamation marks, no manufactured urgency. The breathability copy ("knit with hundreds of tiny openings, so cool air can always come through") actually speaks runner logic. But the collection is positioned as lifestyle first, and for a club captain who actively notices when a brand knows runners versus fashion-shoppers in running gear, that positioning costs it.
- Would I click? Probably once — a quick tap on the collection to check if anything has actual run specs. Would I forward to someone in the club? No. This goes to a friend who wants to look athletic on a weekend, not to someone building a summer training wardrobe.

## What's working

- Male models throughout, with athletic-adjacent but not over-styled looks — the demographic read is accurate for me
- The breathability construction detail is the strongest line in the email — "hundreds of tiny openings" is specific, credible, and performance-minded
- Multiple colorways presented efficiently — I can visually edit to the navy/grey palette I'd actually wear in one scan
- Clean lululemon layout with real breathing room between modules — no cluttered sale banners, no competing CTAs shouting at each other
- "Airing now" is smart copy — seasonal, light, doesn't try too hard

## What's weak

- Zero running context in the imagery — nobody moving, no road, no track, no sweat — it reads as athleisure lifestyle, not performance wear
- "Soft Jersey" positioning pulls toward comfort/lounge rather than performance; that's a meaningful distinction when I'm deciding what to buy for actual training
- No price anywhere in the email — even a range; I can't calibrate whether this is an impulse buy or a considered purchase before I click
- "Another set? Easy." module at the bottom is completely unanchored — I don't know what's being offered, and it reads like an orphaned idea rather than a second CTA
- "Shop Now" is the most generic CTA in the lululemon toolkit — they usually write smarter than this

## Recommendations

- 1. **Shoot the hero in motion** — put this fabric in real running conditions (early morning, humidity, actual sweat visible). The breathability copy does the work; the photography needs to back it up.
- 2. **Add at least one price** — anchor the collection entry point. Even "$88 shorts" tells me what category I'm in before I click.
- 3. **Rewrite the CTA** — "Shop Mesh Soft Jersey" or "See the Collection" beats "Shop Now" every time when the product has a name worth using.
- 4. **Give "Another set? Easy." its own moment** — one image, one specific set name, one shop link. Right now it's dead real estate.
- 5. Sharpen the runner-specific angle in the body — something like "built for the humid long run" would convert the breathability claim into a use-case signal I act on.
