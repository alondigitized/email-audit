# What’s new: your choice of tees
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-01**
## Executive summary

- Four fits of the same white tee, walked through one by one, and every single model in this email is a woman. This is a lifestyle tee catalog with zero connection to running — no mention of moisture-wicking, no mile counter, no club, nothing. I'm not the customer for "four fits, all ease," I'm the customer for "this stays dry at mile 8."
- It's basically last week's "Yoga pants that go anywhere" email wearing a different top — same studio-lifestyle photography, same all-female cast, same complete absence of anyone doing anything athletic. At least the "First look: fall 2026" email from two days ago had a guy in it, on a brick wall, with some texture to the shot. This one doesn't even clear that low bar.
- Would I click? No. Would I forward it? Definitely not — nothing here would interest a single person in my running club. Straight to archive.

## What's working

- Clean, uncluttered layout — one tee, one line of copy, one CTA per section, easy to scan even skimming on my phone during my commute.
- Product photography is sharp and the tees themselves look genuinely well-made (the Pima cotton Classic Tee callout is a nice materials detail).
- The four-fit breakdown (slim, balanced, relaxed, oversized) is useful information if I were actually shopping for a tee — clear sizing logic.

## What's weak

- Every model is a woman — this reads like it went out to the wrong list, or lululemon just isn't bothering to segment men out of a men's-relevant product drop.
- Not one action shot. No running, no sweat, no motion blur — same complaint I had about the yoga pants send and the same gap that made "New tennis gear" stand out by comparison.
- "Four fits, all ease" — ease isn't a word that gets me to open my wallet; I want to know if it breathes on a hot July long run, not how relaxed it drapes at brunch.
- Zero performance specs — no fabric tech callouts beyond "naturally breathable" and "organic Pima cotton," which is lifestyle-brand language, not running-brand language.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split this send by gender/purchase history — show me men's fits with a guy actually wearing them, the same way Nike segments its member emails instead of blasting one generic version to everyone.
- 2. Swap at least one static shot for movement — a guy mid-stride in the Classic Tee would do more for me than four static poses ever could.
- 3. Lead with a performance hook, not a fit descriptor — "sweat-wicking, zero chafe" beats "all ease" for someone shopping for a long run, not a lounge session.
- 4. Add a real offer or member perk — this is a pure product-intro email with nothing time-bound or price-related, so there's no urgency to act now versus next month.
