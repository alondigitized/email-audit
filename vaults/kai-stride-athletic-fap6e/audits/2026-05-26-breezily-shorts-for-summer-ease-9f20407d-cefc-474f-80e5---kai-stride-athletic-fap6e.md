# Breezily Shorts for summer ease
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-26**
## Executive summary

- Seven emails from lululemon in roughly ten days, and this one might be the most egregious demographic miss yet. The hero is a woman in wide-leg Breezily Pants. The second module is Breezily Pants again. Module three is the Scuba Windbreaker — on a woman. The "Shop new styles this week" grid at the bottom is dresses and open-back tops. I run 50 miles a week and buy the Surge shorts and ABC pants from these people. There is not a single pixel in this email addressed to me.
- This isn't "wrong sport" the way the tennis emails were. This is wrong gender, wrong lifestyle, wrong everything. adidas, for all their cadence sins, sent me a personalized voucher code last week that at least had my name on it. Nike's emails are often transactional and cold, but they don't send me a women's demi-fine jewelry circular and call it a day. lululemon is not just missing my sport — they're missing my existence as a male customer entirely.
- I mark this one as read without clicking. I would not forward it. If a friend asked what lululemon's emails are like, I'd show them this one as Exhibit A.

## What's working

- **lululemon brand equity still buys the open.** The sender name alone gets a glance every time, even when the content doesn't deserve it.
- **The Scuba Windbreaker copy is tight** — "windproof protection with a weightless feel" is a real performance claim, not fashion-catalog fluff. It's the only line that sounds like it was written for someone who moves.
- **Clean render, clear hierarchy.** Hero at top, product modules stacked below, no broken images or layout chaos. The email does what it's supposed to do technically.
- **Seasonal hook is sound.** "Summer ease" and the lightweight-fabric angle are correctly timed for late May.

## What's weak

- **Every model in this email is a woman, every product is women's.** Breezily Shorts are a women's line. The Breezily Pants module is women's. The "new styles" grid at the bottom is dresses. I'm a man. This should never have landed in my inbox.
- **Still no running DNA after seven sends.** The 5/21 "What's trending" email had a woman in a red athletic dress. The 5/19 and 5/20 emails were tennis. Now Breezily Shorts. Not one email has touched a run, a trail, a finish line, or the word "pace."
- **Zero offer.** No discount, no member pricing, no early-access hook. adidas gave me a personalized voucher with a deadline last week. lululemon is asking me to "Shop Now" with nothing on the table.
- **The "Shop new styles this week" grid feels like a homepage dump.** Dresses, back-tie tops — if I squint, I can imagine they're trying to cross-sell, but it reads as a default fallback module, not intentional curation for me.
- **Cadence is burning goodwill fast.** This is the seventh email in ten days, and the irrelevance-to-volume ratio is getting worse.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment me out of the women's sends entirely.** If my purchase history is ABC pants and Surge shorts, there is no scenario where Breezily Shorts belong in my inbox. The most impactful change here isn't copy — it's list hygiene.
- 2. **Send me one running email.** I spend real money in this category. A send around the Surge Shorts refresh, Fast and Free tights, or even a "club kit builder" angle tied to summer race season would have me clicking in seconds. One email. I've been waiting.
- 3. **Put an offer in.** Even member-exclusive early access or a free-shipping threshold would make "Shop Now" feel less like shouting into the void.
- 4. **If cross-selling is the goal, make it gender-neutral.** The Scuba Windbreaker is legitimately unisex — feature it on a male model or at least neutral copy and it's relevant to me. The Breezily line is not.
- 5. **Trim send frequency or vary the creative harder.** Nike got roasted for the same thing with the Anthony Edwards kids email — wrong audience, right brand, still a wasted send. lululemon is doing this at scale, every week.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issue — invalid `letter-spacing` units:**
CSS `letter-spacing` does not accept percentage values. Three classes use this pattern:

```css
.guest-voice-lg { letter-spacing: -2%; }   /* invalid */
.guest-voice-md { letter-spacing: -2%; }   /* invalid */
.guest-voice-sm { letter-spacing: -1%; }   /* invalid */
```

All three values will be silently ignored by every rendering engine. If these elements are used in the email, they will render with default letter-spacing, not the intended tight tracking. Fix: convert to `em` units (`-0.02em` / `-0.01em`).
