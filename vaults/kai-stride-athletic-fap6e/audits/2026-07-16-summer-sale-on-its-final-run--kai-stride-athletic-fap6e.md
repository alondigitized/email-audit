# Summer Sale, on its final run
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- I'll give whoever wrote "on its final run" some credit — that's the cleverest subject line in the last five lululemon sends, and "Move fast" in the orange hero copy lands with me in a way that "softest sets" and "200+ picks added" never did. For a second I thought they finally figured out they have a runner on their list.
- Then I looked at the actual email. The men's section shows a guy in what looks like a polo. The categories are Women's, Men's, Accessories, Shoes — the same summer sale grid in a different outfit. And then, buried so far below the fold that most people won't see it, there's a Seawheeze banner that says "HERE COMES THE DROP." Seawheeze is a lululemon-branded half marathon. It's the most runner-coded thing they do all year. And they put it after the belt bag.
- I'll mark this read. The Seawheeze tease is the only thing worth a click, but the structure buries the lede three product rows deep. My running club won't hear about it from me this time.

## What's working

- "On its final run" subject pun is earned — first lululemon subject in five sends that reads like someone on the team runs
- "Ends soon. Move fast." preheader is tight, adds urgency without screaming, and complements not repeats the subject
- Seawheeze "HERE COMES THE DROP" block is genuinely exciting to anyone in the running community — limited-edition race drops hit differently than a generic sale
- Clean visual hierarchy: orange hero lands hard, CTAs are legible, zero render bugs
- Under $50 / Under $100 category tiles are a practical navigation aid when a sale is this big

## What's weak

- The men's model above the fold looks like he's networking at a resort, not running a long run — lululemon keeps confirming they think their male subscriber base is golfers, not runners
- Seawheeze — the one legitimately runner-relevant piece — sits after three rows of generic category tiles; most runners won't scroll to it
- "Ends soon" doesn't feel credible anymore: this is the fifth lululemon sale email since July 9, and "200+ picks added / faves moving fast / now its final run" is a cadence that trains subscribers to ignore urgency signals
- No specific savings number anywhere — not a percent off, not a dollar anchor, nothing to make "sale" mean something concrete
- Women's CTA button comes before Men's for a male subscriber — at minimum that sequence should flip based on my gender signal

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with Seawheeze.** Make the DROP the hero. Runners treat Seawheeze releases the way sneakerheads treat Air Jordan drops — put it above the fold, give it a countdown, and the running-club forward rate goes up immediately.
- 2. **Swap the men's lifestyle model for a runner.** One shot of someone mid-stride in Surge or Fast and Free tights and this email is competing with the adidas Dropset Pro send I actually forwarded to my club last month. Right now the men's imagery is actively working against me.
- 3. **Name the discount.** "Up to 40% off" or even "starting at $34" anchors this sale as real. Adidas puts "25% off" in the subject line. Nike has a dollar amount in the CTA. "Summer Sale" alone is doing too much lifting.
- 4. **Put Men's first.** If lululemon has gender data on my account, use it. The Women's button being the default primary CTA tells me they're not segmenting on something basic.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Seawheeze DROP is live — Summer Sale ends this weekend`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to X% off running, training + more — last chance`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Seawheeze Collection dropping now. Sale wraps soon.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Running, training + club gear — up to X% off, ends soon`
