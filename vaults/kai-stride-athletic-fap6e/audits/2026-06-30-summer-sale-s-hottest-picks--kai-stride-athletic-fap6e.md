# Summer Sale's hottest picks
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-30**
## Executive summary

- Same orange. Same hero. Same vibe. This is the third or fourth time I've seen this lululemon Summer Sale treatment land in my inbox — the June 22 "Early Access" send had nearly this exact coral-to-orange gradient and white all-caps hero — and I'm starting to feel like I'm getting forwarded the same email twice a week. That said, I clicked in because it's lululemon and "hottest picks" implied some curation. Instead I got a category grid: Men's Shorts, Men's Hoodies, Women's Shorts, Women's Pants, Accessories, Under $100. No Surge Short. No Pace Breaker. No mention of anything performance-running adjacent.
- What actually stopped me mid-scroll was the "$300 lululemon credit" block near the bottom. A credit card pitch, tucked into my sale email, after I've already been trained to see lululemon as a premium performance brand. That's an adidas move — adidas has been mailing me loyalty-program upsells for two weeks straight — not what I expect from the brand that sponsors my running club.
- I'd mark this read and tab over to the Men's Shorts link directly. I would not forward this to anyone in my running club. There's nothing specific enough to say "hey, this deal is worth five minutes of your time."

## What's working

- **Orange hero still pops.** "SUMMER SALE" in that big white type on a warm red-orange reads clearly on my phone, even at a glance.
- **Men's categories are surfaced.** Men's Shorts and Men's Hoodies/Sweatshirts tiles are both present, so I at least know the sale applies to me.
- **"Don't miss the moment" mid-email CTA** is nicely placed — breaking the grid scroll with a full-width moment before the footer works rhythmically.
- **Under $100 tier is smart.** Giving me a price anchor without saying "30% off" still gives me a deal hook. Better than adidas, which ran "30% off" without a ceiling, so I spent 10 minutes math-ing out the real price.

## What's weak

- **No running-specific product called out.** I came in hoping "hottest picks" meant a curated shortlist. The Surge Short on sale? Pace Breaker restocked and discounted? Instead I got category buckets — same as the storefront nav. This isn't curation; it's a sitemap with photos.
- **The hero creative is recycled.** June 22 "Summer Sale: Early Access tomorrow" ran this same orange/coral palette and all-caps white treatment. I'm eight days out from that email. The aesthetic fatigue is real.
- **Credit card offer is a total non-sequitur.** The "$300 lululemon credit" module feels like a financial services insert that got dropped into the wrong template. Nike wouldn't put an AppleCard pitch in a running shoe email.
- **No sale percentage or proof of savings.** Every competitor running this kind of email — adidas literally subject-lined "First chance to dive into 30% off" — gives me the math upfront. "Summer Sale" is a promise without a number.
- **Women's categories outnumber men's 2-to-1 in the grid.** Women's Shorts, Women's Pants, Women's Summer Sale, versus Men's Shorts, Men's Hoodies — the visual weight skews female even though I'm the target here.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with one hero performance product.** Pull a specific men's runner item — Surge Short 6", Pace Breaker Linerless, T.H.E. Short — into the hero with its discounted price. "Surge Short: now $49" beats "Summer Sale's hottest picks" every single day.
- 2. **Show me the discount math.** Add a "Up to X% off" or "$XX and under" sub-headline under SUMMER SALE. Adidas earned my open this month specifically because the math was in the subject.
- 3. **Drop the credit card block or move it to a standalone send.** It pulls me out of the purchase mindset right at the conversion moment near the footer.
- 4. **Refresh the hero creative.** Eight days between sends and the same orange gradient reads as a resend, not a new offer. Even a color shift to the Beach Ball Blue from the June 18 send would signal "new."
- 5. **Re-order the men's/women's grid balance.** If you know I'm male, surface Men's first. One quick segmentation rule would make this feel like a personalized send instead of a mass blast.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Men's Shorts & Tees: Summer Sale prices now live`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to [X]% off the styles you actually run in`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Surge Short, Pace Breaker, and more — while sizes last`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Sale ends soon — grab your summer kit before it's gone`
