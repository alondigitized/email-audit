- Okay, this one actually made me stop scrolling. The hero shot is three people mid-stride on what looks like a track or path at golden hour, legs cropped tight on the shoe — that's a performance-brand photo, not a lifestyle lookbook.
- That said, it runs out of gas fast. No price, no drop date, no cushioning-stack numbers, no mention of the Hyperboost Pro foam beyond the buzzword, and two identical black CTA buttons stacked on top of each other feels like a placeholder, not a finished send. ## What's working

- "Non-plated super trainer for long runs" in the preheader is legit run-nerd language — plated vs. non-plated is a distinction people in my club actually argue about.
- Hero image is runners in motion, cropped on the shoe, outdoors — reads as performance, not studio lifestyle.
- Product name gets its own bold headline treatment ("HYPERBOOST EDGE") — clear this is a single-product spotlight, not a catalog dump.
- Two clear CTAs ("Shop Now" and "Find Store Now") acknowledge that some of us still want to try shoes on before buying.

## What's weak

- Zero pricing anywhere. Nike's one-time-code email at least got me somewhere useful in under a minute — this one dangles a new shoe and makes me go find the price myself.
- No stack height, drop, weight, or foam-tech specifics beyond "Hyperboost Pro" as a name — that's the stuff I actually care about before I click buy on a trainer.
- "Explore the latest adidas best sellers" black bar up top competes with the actual hero product below it — undercuts the single-product focus adidas is going for here.
- The "Shop Now" and "Find Store Now" buttons are visually identical (same black box, same arrow) — nothing tells my eye which one is primary.
- No size-availability signal at all, and the fine print literally warns "purchase is not guaranteed" — that's a real turnoff for a shoe drop.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a price under the headline. This is the single biggest miss — I'm not clicking blind on a new trainer.
- 2. Cut the top "best sellers" bar entirely on a single-product email like this — it dilutes the Hyperboost Edge launch and doesn't belong above the hero.
- 3. Differentiate the two CTAs visually — make "Shop Now" the bold/colored primary and "Find Store Now" a secondary outline button.
- 4. Add one line of actual spec: drop height, weight, or "our lightest non-plated trainer yet" — something a runner can hang a decision on.
- 5. Drop or soften the "purchase is not guaranteed" fine print, or at least pair it with a stock/size checker link.
