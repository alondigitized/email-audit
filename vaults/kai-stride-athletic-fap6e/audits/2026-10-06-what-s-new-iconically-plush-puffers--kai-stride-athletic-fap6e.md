## What's working

- The Hamilton hero is a strong, confident first screen. It reads as a person, not a mood board.
- "Quiet the weather." is short and has some attitude. It's far better than "An elevated form of ease."
- The "Travel light in Featherweight" section is the closest this gets to my life. The stuff-sack shot of a puffer packed small tells me something real.
- The models are mixed and include men, so I'm not hunting for myself in a wall of women's studio gear.
- The layout is clean, with generous white space and readable body copy, and nothing is broken or overlapping.

## What's weak

- There's no offer at all. No price, no member perk, no shipping hook. Compare adidas's "Up to 50% off our favorites for fall," which at least gave me a number to react to.
- The CTAs are generic. "Shop What's New," "Shop Coats & Jackets," and "Shop Women's Outerwear" are all stacked pairs of black pills, and none of them names a product.
- Nothing is runner-specific. These are 600-fill down puffers for standing around. I'd want a running shell or vest, and a mention of reflectivity, packability, or temperature range. Nike at least talks to me with run-club cues.
- The text links ("Shop Wunder Puff →") are tiny on a phone, and the real buttons are the only decent tap targets.
- No sizing, price, or availability anywhere. I can't tell whether a puffer costs $198 or $598.
- It's long and repetitive, with five or six image grids saying "puffer" in slightly different ways.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a price or a perk in the hero, even just "from $XXX" or member early access. The email has nothing to act on now.
- 2. Collapse the stacked buttons into one specific CTA, such as "Shop Wunder Puff."
- 3. Add one run-specific module. A weatherproof running vest or shell would make me feel like the brand knows runners, not fashion shoppers in running gear.
- 4. Cut the repeated image grids by a third. The story is told by the second section.
- 5. Put real specs next to each product: fill power, weight, packed size.
