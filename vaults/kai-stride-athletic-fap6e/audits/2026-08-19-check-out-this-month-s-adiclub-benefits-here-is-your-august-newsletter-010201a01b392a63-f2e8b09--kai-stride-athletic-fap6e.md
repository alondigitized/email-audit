## Executive summary

- Okay, the adidas x Strava tie-in genuinely made me perk up — that's the first block in weeks that felt like it was built by someone who knows what a "eligible activity" and a tracked run actually are. Then it's immediately back to World Cup match ball sweepstakes and a Penn State mascot tracksuit giveaway, neither of which has anything to do with me or my Sunday long run. This is a benefits digest, not a shopping email, and it reads like five unrelated teams each got one block and nobody coordinated a throughline.
- Compare this to the Strava-connect concept itself — that's a real idea, better than anything Nike or Lululemon has sent me lately about member perks. But it's buried as block two of five, sandwiched between a soccer jersey hero and a college mascot contest. I'm not entering to win a signed World Cup ball and I'm definitely not entering a Nittany Lion tracksuit raffle — I don't go to Penn State, nobody in my run club does.
- I wouldn't click today, but I'd bookmark "connect Strava to adiClub" as something to actually do later this week. Not forwarding this one — it's too scattered to send a friend without a caveat.

## What's working

- The adiClub x Strava block is the one piece of copy here that speaks runner, not fan — "earn points every time you track an eligible activity" is concrete and relevant to me.
- "UNLOCK SAVINGS ON YOUR FAVES" closes with an actual mechanism — points into discount vouchers — which is more useful than another generic "shop now."
- Clean member-only framing throughout ("Exclusively for adiClub & FIFA Rewards members," "exclusively with adiClub") — I feel like I'm getting something for being signed up, not just being sold to.

## What's weak

- Hero slot is a soccer kit and a signed World Cup ball giveaway — I run, I don't play club soccer, and this is prime real estate wasted on a persona that isn't me.
- The Penn State Nittany Lion tracksuit contest is completely irrelevant to a national/general list — feels like a leftover local promo that got pasted into the wrong newsletter.
- Five different offers crammed into one email with no hierarchy — nothing tells my eye where to land first, so the one thing I actually care about (Strava) gets equal visual weight to a mascot costume photo.
- Zero running product shown anywhere — no shoes, no apparel, despite "your favorite running shoes" being name-dropped in the closing copy.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the Strava integration as the hero — that's the actual news here for someone like me, not a raffle for a signed ball. Put a real running shot up top, not a soccer kit on a pink backdrop.
- 2. Cut or regionally-target the Penn State tracksuit block entirely — it reads as filler to a national list and actively signals "this wasn't made for you."
- 3. Turn "UNLOCK SAVINGS ON YOUR FAVES" into a specific running-shoe callout with an actual model name, mirroring what worked in "Hyperboost Edge" (2026-08-17) — that email spoke my language once, this one only does it in the Strava block.
- 4. Add one line of social proof to the Strava block — how many miles/points members have earned so far — to make it feel alive, not just a feature announcement.
