- Okay, this actually got my attention for a second — Adizero Dropset Pro shoes, a Polar Grit X2, an actual training kit laid out flat-lay style in the hero shot. That's real running gear, not a Samba on a school hallway or a K-pop capsule. But then I read it twice and realized it's just a giveaway entry, not a sale, not a drop I can buy. "Enter the giveaway" and "SIGN UP" instead of a hard CTA to shop the shoes themselves — so the one product in this email I'd actually want, I can't buy from this email.
- Adidas has been sending me sale codes, skate collabs, and back-to-school Sambas all week, so at least this one's on-category — first genuinely running-relevant send from them in five days. Compare it to Nike's one-time-code email: that one respected my time and got me in and out with something I actually asked for. This is the opposite motion — dangling gear I can't just buy, in exchange for my email and probably a sweepstakes opt-in.
- Would I click? Maybe, out of curiosity, since the odds of winning anything are basically zero and I know it. Would I forward it? No — nothing here to share with the run club, it's a "you personally might win a smartwatch" pitch, not a community thing.

## What's working

- The hero image is legit running kit — shoes, shorts, gym bag, smartwatch, shot together like actual training gear, not a lifestyle flat-lay.
- Adizero Dropset Pro and Polar Grit X2 are both named specifically — real product names, not vague "top picks."
- The copy ("preparing for race day," "pushing for a new PB") speaks directly to someone training, not just shopping.
- One clear headline, one clear ask — no split-personality email like the Tyshawn/backpack mashup from yesterday.

## What's weak

- It's a giveaway, not a purchase — the one product I want in this whole email, I can't actually buy through this CTA.
- "Explore the latest adidas best sellers / SHOP NOW" banner at the top competes with the giveaway CTA at the bottom — two different asks, unclear which one adidas actually wants me to take.
- No odds, no entry deadline, no rules link visible — "enter the giveaway" with zero specifics feels like filler compared to the End of Summer Sale email's clean dates-and-code offer.
- Polar Grit X2 is a third-party brand shown as the marquee prize item — odd that adidas's own product isn't the hero of its own giveaway.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a real "Shop the Adizero Dropset Pro" link right under the shoe in the image — if I can't win it, let me buy it. That's the single biggest miss here.
- 2. Cut the "Explore best sellers / SHOP NOW" banner entirely — it's competing with the giveaway CTA and diluting the one clear action this email should drive.
- 3. Add entry deadline and odds/rules link near the SIGN UP button — "ends 9/12" or similar would make this feel less like a lead-gen trap.
- 4. If this is really about the Adizero Dropset Pro launch, say so — tie the giveaway to a real release date so it reads like a launch event, not a random prize wheel.
