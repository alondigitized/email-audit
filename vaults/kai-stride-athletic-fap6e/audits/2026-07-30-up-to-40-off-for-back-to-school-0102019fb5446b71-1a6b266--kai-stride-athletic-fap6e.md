# Up to 40% off for back to school
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-30**
## Executive summary

- Another back-to-school email from adidas, and this one's basically the same lockers-and-backpack mood board every retailer runs this time of year — except there's not a single running shoe, a single piece of performance gear, or a single word about training in the whole thing. "Up to 40% off" sitewide with a locker backpack hero tells me nothing about whether there's anything for someone who runs 30+ miles a week. The adiClub "spend $130+, get a free month of Apple TV" line is a genuinely weird pairing for a sportswear brand and it made me raise an eyebrow more than it made me want to click.
- Three flat CTAs — Shop Men, Shop Women, Shop Kids — dump me on a generic category page with zero signal on whether it's running shoes or backpacks and notebooks in there. I'm not opening this on my commute expecting to find my next long-run shoe, and I'm right not to.
- Not clicking, not forwarding. This is the "Style before the bell" (7/25) energy all over again — broad seasonal lifestyle copy with a sitewide discount slapped on top, no running angle at all. Marking as read and moving on.

## What's working

- Clean hero image — no render bugs, no broken assets, the locker/backpack shot is well-composed and legible.
- The offer is specific and visible immediately: "Up to 40% off," no scrolling required to see it.
- Fine print is upfront and honest about dates (July 30–Aug 13) and terms rather than buried at the very bottom in six-point type — I'll give them that.
- Three clear category CTAs (Men/Women/Kids) mean I'm not guessing where to click, even if the destination is generic.

## What's weak

- Zero performance or running content — no shoes, no training gear, nothing that speaks to "I run a weekly long run with my club." This is a lifestyle/back-to-school sale, full stop.
- The adiClub Apple TV perk is bizarre and off-brand for a sportswear email — I want member pricing on running shoes, not a streaming subscription tie-in.
- "Stock up for back to school" as an opener assumes I'm shopping for kids or myself as a student — I'm 33, this doesn't apply to me at all.
- Generic Shop Men/Women/Kids CTAs route to broad category pages, not a curated running or performance collection — high friction for finding anything relevant.
- No social proof, no product-specific detail (sizing, drop, fit) — nothing here treats me like a known runner, just a demographic bucket.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment this — if adidas knows I've engaged with running content before (Ultraboost, adizero, whatever), lead with a performance-specific sale view instead of the generic backpack hero. This is the single biggest lift available.
- 2. Swap the Apple TV incentive for something running-relevant — a free pair of socks, an entry to a local run club event, adiClub XP toward gear I'd actually buy.
- 3. Add at least one performance product in the hero or below the fold — even a single adizero or Ultraboost callout would make this feel like it knows I run.
- 4. Replace "Shop Men/Women/Kids" with a fourth option like "Shop Running" so runners aren't forced through a generic gender funnel.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your back-to-school shoe upgrade is 40% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New season, same long runs — save on adizero and more`
