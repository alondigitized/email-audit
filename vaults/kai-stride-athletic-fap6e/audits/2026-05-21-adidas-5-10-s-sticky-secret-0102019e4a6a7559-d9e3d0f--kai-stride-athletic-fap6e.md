# adidas 5.10's Sticky Secret
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- Five emails from adidas in six days, and this is the first one with actual storytelling chops — I'll give them that. The STEALTH® origin narrative is genuinely compelling. Yosemite 1985, a climbing grade turned brand name, rubber that migrated from rock faces to MTB pedals. That's a real story, and the copy actually sells the *reason* the product exists rather than just barking "LIMITED DROPS."
- Problem is I'm a running club captain who just crushed a 16-miler this morning. I run in adidas Adizero Adios Pros. I've been waiting six days for adidas to send me literally one email about shoes I might actually buy on my next long run. Instead I got F1 fashion, an app download prompt, an adiClub points explainer, and now climbing shoes. The segmentation here is completely broken. This email belongs in my buddy Marcus's inbox — he boulders at the climbing gym three times a week. Not mine.
- I'm not clicking. I'm not forwarding to anyone in my running club. I might text Marcus the subject line and tell him to check his email.

## What's working

- **The origin story is tight.** "Five Ten took its name from the rock climbing grade 5.10" — that's a two-sentence brand history lesson that actually lands. adidas finally showed they can write.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean.** Headline bold, copy beneath, single CTA below. No cluttered secondary offers fighting for attention like the F1 email from yesterday.
- **The hero image is coherent.** The colorful climbing holds reinforce the STEALTH® grip story without ambiguity. You know exactly what this email is about in under two seconds.
- **No discount, no desperation.** Compared to the adiClub welcome (15% off, code visible, expiry date) this is brand-building content. That's the right call for a heritage product story.
- **"Sticky Secret" subject line earns curiosity.** I opened it. For the wrong reasons, but I opened it.

## What's weak

- **Wrong category entirely.** I run. I do not climb. I do not ride MTB. This email treats my adidas profile like a catch-all outdoor-sports contact rather than a runner. lululemon sent me tennis content two days in a row last week — that's also off-base for me — but at least tennis has a cardio connection. Climbing shoes are a completely different distribution channel.
- **No offer, no reason to click today.** "SHOP NOW" with no price, no discount, no stock signal, no launch window. Compare that to the adiClub welcome email where the promo code and expiry were front and center. This gives me zero purchase trigger.
- **Fifth email in six days.** The cadence is burning trust. Even if this email were about the Adizero Boston 12, I'd be fatigued. Nike sends me one email a week. This rate makes adidas feel like they're panicking.
- **The preheader is dead weight.** "Explore the latest adidas best sellers" is the laziest possible inbox banner — it doesn't connect to Five Ten or STEALTH® at all. It's the kind of generic link that makes inbox preview text look like a nav bar accident.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment runners out of this send.** The single highest-impact fix: don't send a climbing/MTB email to users whose purchase/browse history is running. adidas has the data. Use it. Nike wouldn't send me a basketball shoe story unprompted.
- 2. **Add a performance hook if you insist on sending to a mixed list.** One line like "The same grip science is in your next racing shoe" would at least build a bridge to running. Right now the copy treats the climbing-to-MTB migration as the whole story — it stops there.
- 3. **Give me a reason to act today.** A launch date, a new colorway dropping this week, even a 48-hour free shipping window. The storytelling is good; the close is absent.
- 4. **Fix the preheader.** It should earn the subject's curiosity hook, not undercut it with a generic retail banner.
- 5. **Dial back the send frequency.** Five emails in six days is newsletter fatigue territory. Batch the loyalty mechanics and app promos; save the inbox for the content that actually converts.
- **Subject Alt A:** `The rubber that conquered Yosemite — now on the trail`
- **Subject Alt B:** `40 years of grip science. Still sticking.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `STEALTH® rubber was born on a 5.10 wall. Here's what it took.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The climbing shoe that invented a category — and never stopped.`
