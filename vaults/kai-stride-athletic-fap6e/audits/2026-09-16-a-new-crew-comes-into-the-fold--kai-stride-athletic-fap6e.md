# A new crew comes into the fold
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-16**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this one's actually men's-forward for once — a Cotton-Blend Crew, a guy in a hoodie standing in a field with a giant sheet flapping behind him like a photoshoot mishap. That's new. Lululemon's been running me through the women's Sculpt Society edit and Colleen Quigley quotes for three sends straight, so seeing an actual dude in actual men's gear as the hero is a relief — but "Softness, doubled up" and "non-stop comfort" is still comfort/lifestyle copy, not a single word about performance, mileage, or moisture-wicking. It's cozywear dressed as apparel news.
- I'm not clicking to run in this. I might click "Shop Men's What's New" out of curiosity because the sweatshirt actually looks decent, but this is a lounge-and-coffee-run email, not a long-run email. Not forwarding it to the crew — nobody in my running club cares about a "dual-sided texture" crewneck.

## What's working

- Lead model is male, styled in muted olive/black, which finally matches me instead of the last three all-women sends.
- Two clear CTAs up top ("Shop What's New" / "Shop Fall Clothes") right under the hero copy — no hunting for the button.
- The "Shop new styles this week" grid is a smart mid-email reset — gives me a scannable product wall instead of another giant lifestyle photo.
- Amex "up to $75 back" card placement is legit useful and I hadn't seen lululemon push a card perk like that before.

## What's weak

- Zero running content. No shoe, no mileage claim, no "engineered for your long run" — this is recovery/lounge wear front to back, same beat as the Spacer Tracksuit "cooldown" email from 9/10.
- "The rest, is comfort" section reintroduces a woman as the split hero next to the guy — fine for balance, but the copy never distinguishes men's vs. women's performance intent, it's all just "comfort."
- No price, no % off, no BOGO anywhere in the body — after Nike sending me an actual working member code and adidas running Bad Bunny football boots at me, this is the third brand in a row giving me zero concrete offer.
- Product grid has no names, sizes, or prices under the thumbnails — I'd have to click through blind to even know what I'm looking at.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put one running-specific product in the mix — even a single "engineered for the run club" line would separate this from a Gap loungewear email.
- 2. Add a price or intro-price callout under the hero CTA — Nike's transactional code email beat this on usefulness for exactly that reason: it gave me something concrete to act on.
- 3. Label the product grid — names/prices under each thumbnail, not just an image wall.
- 4. Drop the Amex card block lower or make it its own send — it competes with the actual product story right now.
