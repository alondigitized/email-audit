# New, relaxed golf gear in full swing
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-16**
## Executive summary

- I'm a running-club captain. I spend money on lululemon because the Surge Short and the Metal Vent Tech tee have genuinely made my long runs better. When I see "golf" in a subject line from them, I click out of brand loyalty curiosity more than real interest — and this email confirmed the mental model I already had: lululemon is spreading itself thin trying to be the premium everything brand, and golf is where they keep doubling down while I'm out here looking for a new long-run kit.
- The Roger Steele execution is fine. The photography is clean, the desert golf course aesthetic is premium and cohesive, and the product copy — "keeps scores low and comfort high" — is actually clever for the right audience. But I am not that audience. I've been through five lululemon emails in two weeks (June 3 had the hot-summer-runs send that actually got me, June 9 was the LuluLinen lifestyle drift, June 10 went Jordan/Cali Clarkson for Father's Day), and now we're pivoting to golf? The cadence is starting to feel scattershot.
- I would not click. I would not forward this to any of my running club regulars. I'd maybe mention it to my one buddy who just started playing golf, then move on.

## What's working

- **Roger Steele as the anchor** — using an actual Golf Advocate (not a vague lifestyle model) signals lululemon did some homework; it reads like insider credibility, even if golf isn't my world
- **Hero copy is punchy** — "Cut to stay cool." is a tight, warm-weather line that lands fast on mobile; it earns the scroll
- **Visual hierarchy is clean** — the eye goes hero → product → grid, no noise; compare this to adidas's "adiClub x FIFA Rewards" email (scored 9/10 in my feed) which buried the actual rewards detail below the fold — lululemon doesn't make that mistake here
- **Clean render, no bugs** — every image loaded, the layout held, no overlapping text anywhere; the June 10 Jordan email had similar clean rendering, so at least the production quality is consistent

## What's weak

- **Golf is the wrong category for me** — lululemon knows I've bought running gear; sending me a purely golf-vertical email with zero running content is a segmentation miss; Nike's "here's your one-time code" email (scored 6/10) at least knew I was a member doing something account-specific — this email treats me like a stranger
- **No offer, no reason to act now** — no price, no discount, no loyalty perk, no member exclusive; adidas's voucher send ("Last day to redeem your voucher") created urgency with my actual account balance — this is just new-arrivals browsing
- **The secondary headline is soft** — "Roger's always laid back and never laying up in this gear" is fine wordplay but it's a line that only lands if you already care about golf; it does nothing to pull in a crossover buyer
- **No running thread to bridge me in** — lululemon could have anchored the "relaxed fit for range-of-motion" angle to running recovery or trail days; they didn't try

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment this out of the running purchaser list** — this is the single highest-leverage fix; someone who buys Surge Shorts and Metal Vent Tech should get a running email, full stop
- 2. **Add a running carve-out module at the bottom** — even one row with "Also new this week for runners →" gives me a reason to scroll and a path to revenue
- 3. **Give me a reason to act now** — tie new arrivals to an early-access member perk, a limited colorway, or a time-bound offer; the June 15 "One day left" Father's Day send worked because the deadline was real
- 4. **Sharpen the headline for a crossover reader** — if you must send golf to non-golfers, "Built for the range. Works on the road." at least puts motion in my frame
