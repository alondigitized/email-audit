# Style before the bell
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-25**
## Executive summary

- "Style before the bell" — right, because I'm definitely rushing to buy denim shorts and a red bomber jacket before the school bell rings. This is a back-to-school lifestyle email dressed up with a stock-photo trio posing with a BMX bike on a brick sidewalk, and not one thread of it is aimed at me. No shoes I'd run in, no gear I'd wear on a long run, nothing.
- This is the third adidas send in a week chasing the same back-to-school lifestyle beat — July 20 was "Back to school shoes for $80 and under," July 21 was the Samba "reimagined just for you" bait-and-switch, and now this is basically a remix of both with looser framing. At some point the algorithm needs to notice I've clicked exactly zero of these.
- I'm not opening a friend's forward on this, and I'm archiving it in about two seconds. Lululemon's July "Pace Breaker" email at least gave me a product I'd actually race in — this is adidas doing the opposite of that.

## What's working

- The hero image is clean and well-shot, no rendering issues, no broken assets.
- "SHOP MEN" and "SHOP NOW" buttons are unambiguous, high-contrast, easy to tap.
- Layout is simple and scannable — one hero, one headline, two CTAs, no clutter.

## What's weak

- Zero running or performance content — no shoes, no training gear, no club angle, nothing that speaks to why I'm subscribed to a sportswear brand.
- The models are styled for a lookbook, not a track — cargo shorts, a bomber jacket, loafers-adjacent sneakers. This is streetwear, not sport.
- "First Day Fits" and back-to-school framing doesn't apply to a 33-year-old — this reads like it's aimed at teens or their parents, not me.
- Repetitive cadence: this is the same back-to-school lifestyle push adidas has run twice already this week, just with new photography.
- No offer specifics at all — "Explore the latest adidas best sellers" isn't a deal, it's a category link with no percentage, no price point, no urgency.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment your subscriber base — if I've never clicked a lifestyle or back-to-school email in three sends, stop sending me the fourth. Put me in a run/train segment instead.
- 2. Lead with an actual offer. "Best sellers" isn't a hook — give me a number (% off, a price threshold like the July 20 "$80 and under" one at least tried).
- 3. If you're going to email a runner, show a runner — a training shoe, a race-day story, anything with a stopwatch attached to it.
- 4. Cut the redundancy — three back-to-school variations in a week is adiClub fatigue, not a campaign.

- **Subject Alt B:** `New week, new drops: shop what's trending`
