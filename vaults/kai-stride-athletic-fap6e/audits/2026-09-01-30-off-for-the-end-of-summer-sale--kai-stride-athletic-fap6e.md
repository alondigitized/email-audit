- Okay, at least this one's a real, clean offer — 30% off with a code, dates spelled out, gear I'd actually consider. But it's a lifestyle sale wearing a sport-brand's logo. Adidas has hit "back to school" three or four times this month per my own history with them, and now it's "end of summer" — same greenery hero-shot vibe, different season copy. The product grid is Sambas-adjacent trackpants, a low-top PureChill slip-on, and a denim track jacket. None of that is a runner's shopping list.
- I wouldn't click. The offer is legible and honest — no fine print buried, exclusions are disclosed up front — but there's not one running shoe or performance stat in this whole email. - Prime-style "options available" tags under two products signal size/availability transparency I actually care about.
- Clean three-button CTA row (Shop Men/Women/Kids) is unambiguous, easy to tap on a phone.

## What's weak

- Zero running product. AdiStar Cushion is a chunky lifestyle sneaker, PureChill is barely a shoe, the rest is trackpants and a denim jacket — nothing here says "we know you have a long run this weekend."
- The hero is moody green leaves and serif type — gorgeous for a fragrance ad, but it tells me nothing about performance gear or a drop.
- This is the same seasonal-sale format adidas has run me through repeatedly (back-to-school twice, archive sale, now end-of-summer) — the cadence is fatiguing and the creative template barely changes.
- No club/community signal at all — compare to my running club's own group chats about race calendars; adidas never once acknowledges I might actually train.

- 2. Lead the product grid with at least one performance shoe with pace/mileage-relevant specs (drop height, cushioning, weight) instead of four straight lifestyle pieces.
- 3. Cut a seasonal sale template that's now run 4+ times this summer — rotate in a genuine performance-launch email instead of another generic % off blast.
