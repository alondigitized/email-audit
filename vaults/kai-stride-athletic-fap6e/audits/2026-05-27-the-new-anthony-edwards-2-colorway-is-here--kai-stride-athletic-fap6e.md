# The new Anthony Edwards 2 colorway is here 🏀
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- I opened this one purely on muscle memory — adidas in the inbox, I've been trained to at least glance. But within two seconds I'm reading "KIDS EXCLUSIVE" in the hero headline and the whole thing collapses. This is the second Anthony Edwards 2 email in three days. Three days ago I gave the adult version a 3/10 because I run fifty miles a week and basketball shoes have no place in my rotation. adidas took that signal and said: what if we sent him the same shoe again, but for children?
- The email itself is clean. Layout works, shoe renders well, CTA is right there. If I had a ten-year-old who played AAU ball this would land differently. I don't. I captain a running club for adults who are fanatical about their Vaporfly stacks and Adizero splits. Nothing in this email exists in my world.
- This is eleven adidas emails in nine days. The voucher one last week at least had my name on it and a ticking clock — that felt like the brand knowing me. This is the brand strip-mining its send list. Mark as read, no forward, no click.

## What's working

- **Clean visual hierarchy.** Shoe image drops in strong, headline is large and immediate, SHOP NOW button is impossible to miss — adidas knows how to lay out a product launch.
- **Subject line discipline.** Under 45 characters with the emoji, concrete product name, no junk punctuation. From a pure inbox-craft standpoint, it's not embarrassing.
- **No render bugs.** Everything loads, no broken images, footer is legible — compared to some of the lululemon sends I've seen recently where spacing gets weird, this is technically competent.
- **Preheader adds information.** "The best kids styles in Anthony Edwards 2" tells me what the subject didn't — that it's a kids drop — which is at least transparent about the product, even if the transparency makes me want to unsubscribe.

## What's weak

- **KIDS EXCLUSIVE.** I'm 33. I do not need a children's basketball shoe. The demographic mismatch isn't subtle — it's in the hero copy in capital letters.
- **Second AE2 email in three days.** The May 25 send got a 3/10 from me because basketball isn't my world. The logical next step was not: send him the kids version. This is a sequencing failure.
- **No offer.** No price, no promo, no member benefit — just "available now." Compare this to the May 25 30%-off sitewide email, which at least gave me a reason to act. This is a launch announcement with nothing to launch me toward.
- **Eleven emails in nine days.** adidas is burning through whatever goodwill the voucher email built. Nike sent me one clean, functional verification email and left me alone. That restraint is respectable. This isn't.
- **No running DNA anywhere.** Not a single Adizero, not a pace reference, not a club angle. lululemon at least shows athletic product even if the person is wrong. This is pure basketball with zero translation to my world.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment out the basketball audience** before sending a basketball-kid shoe to subscribers whose only adidas purchase history is running footwear. This email belongs in a very different list.
- 2. **If you're going to send to runners, lead with running.** Even a small "also new in running" block in the footer gives someone like me a reason to stay subscribed.
- 3. **Add an offer.** A product-launch email with no incentive is just an ad. The May 25 send had a 30% code — this one asks me to pay full retail for a children's shoe I don't need.
- 4. **Respect the cadence.** Drop to 2-3 emails per week max. The voucher email landed because it felt distinct. Email eleven in nine days feels like noise.

### 1. Technical Summary

Two confirmed broken URLs — one a doubled-domain redirect bug, one a malformed merge token — will send a subset of clicks to error pages. Plain-text fallback is absent (0 chars), which harms deliverability and is a common spam-filter trigger.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Doubled-domain redirect** (2 flagged instances, same URL):
```
https://click.comms.adidas.com/CL0/https:%2F%2Fwww.adidas.com%2Fus%2Fstores/1/...
```
The click-tracker URL wraps an already-absolute `https://www.adidas.com/us/stores` destination. When the tracker resolves the redirect, it will double-expand to a broken path. Affects the link labelled *"Shop online or at select adidas stores.*"*

**[FAIL] Malformed merge token / broken internal URL:**
```
/g/36d4ab07-4312-4bba-8847-fa349fa15d46?txn=31da9000-59c6-11f1-b774-8e0ec7ba23ea
```
This relative path was detected as an unknown URL type inside the `dv.adidas.com` redirect chain. The merge token produced a relative URL instead of an absolute one, making the link unresolvable from an email client context.

**[WARN] 25 tracking links** via `click.comms.adidas.com` were skipped from HTTP probe — not confirmed broken, but unverified.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] 4× HTTP (non-HTTPS) image source** — same arrow asset repeated:
```
src: http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```
Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail all proxy/block mixed-content images. This asset will render as a broken image in clients with strict HTTPS enforcement.

**[WARN] 2 images missing `alt` text:**
- `36d4ab07-4312-4bba-8847-fa349fa15d46` (dv.adidas.com tracking pixel / dynamic image)
- Tracking pixel via `click.comms.adidas.com/CI0/...`

Both are likely tracking pixels, but missing `alt=""` (empty string) means screen readers may announce the raw URL. Convention for non-content images is `alt=""`.

**CSS note:** `@font-face` declarations for `AdihausDIN` and `AdineuePRO` map `src` entirely to `local()` system fonts — no remote webfont is loaded. This is intentional for email compatibility, but means the custom typeface never renders unless the user has it installed locally (unlikely). Not a bug, but worth confirming this is deliberate.
