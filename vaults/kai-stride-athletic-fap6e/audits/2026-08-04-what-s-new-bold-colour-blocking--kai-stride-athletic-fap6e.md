# What’s new: bold colour blocking
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-04**
## Executive summary

- Yep, another lululemon. This is easily the eighth or ninth one in my inbox in under two weeks, and this time it's a woman doing a backbend pose against a lavender backdrop with "sets the tone" in lowercase serif type — that's a photo shoot, not a training email. I scrolled the whole thing and counted: Like a Cloud Bra, Align Jogger, Flow Y Bra, yoga accessories, Align Shorts, scuba sets, dance studio sets, and then a bag. That's a studio/yoga/lifestyle catalog top to bottom — not one running shoe, not one mention of mileage, pace, or a long run in sight.
- I flagged this exact pattern back on 7/21 with "New workout styles that go beyond" and again on 7/22 I actually liked "your focus in Pace Breaker" because it was built around a runner. This one's a full regression back to the studio-pose playbook, worse than 7/28's "Five styles for class and beyond" because at least that one had some daily-wear utility. Nike's one-time-code email from earlier this week did one boring thing perfectly — got me into my account fast. This one does one thing perfectly too: convinces me lululemon doesn't know I run.
- Mark as read, no click, definitely not forwarding this to the run club group chat — nothing here is relevant to a Saturday long run.

## What's working

- Clean visual hierarchy — hero image, headline, two clear CTA buttons above the fold, no clutter.
- No render bugs — images loaded, text is legible, buttons are tappable.
- "Shop the Monthly Edit" section groups by theme cleanly, easy to skim on mobile.
- Brand voice and photography are consistent with prior sends, so at least it's recognizably lululemon.

## What's weak

- Every single model shot is a woman in yoga/studio poses — bras, joggers, dance studio sets. If I'm the target, where's a single guy or a single running silhouette?
- Zero performance language — no pace, no mileage, no "engineered for your long run," just "unmatched energy" and "August goes for bold," which is pure aesthetic copy.
- No offer anywhere. No %, no BOGO, no member pricing visible — just a product catalog dressed up as a campaign.
- This is the same "new colorway drop" beat lululemon has run three times this month with different color stories; nothing here says why August's palette matters to me specifically.
- Subject "bold colour blocking" reads like a fashion editorial headline, not something a runner opens for.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment me out. I've clicked/opened running-specific content before (Pace Breaker got an 8 from me) — if lululemon has any purchase or click history on me, this email shouldn't have gone out as-is. Put me in a running-gear track, not the studio/yoga rotation.
- 2. Lead with one running product and one concrete spec — shoe drop, breathable fabric %, reflective detailing for early runs — instead of five unrelated studio categories in one scroll.
- 3. Add an actual offer. Compare to adidas's adiClub points email — even a low-stakes "50 points" mechanic gave me a reason to engage. This email gives me zero reason beyond "look at pretty colors."
- 4. Cut the catalog sprawl. Eight product categories in one email dilutes the "bold colour blocking" premise the subject promised — pick 2-3 and commit.
