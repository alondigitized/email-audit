- The detail crops of the thumbhole cuff and the zip collar show the jacket's features without a lot of copy.
- The closing app, store and what's-new links give me an easy way to browse beyond the two products.

## What's weak

- Nothing here is for runners. A fitted studio jacket and a belt bag are lifestyle products. adidas's "hybrid race shoe" email earlier this month at least talked about racing, and this one doesn't.
- There's no price and no offer. "Members get first pick" is access, not a deal. Nike's member emails usually pair access with a specific drop or product. This one has no date for when early access ends.
- I see no sizing or availability info. "Early access" with no stock cue means I don't know if my size will even be there.
- Every model is a woman, so there's no signal for me as a male buyer. The belt bag is the only item I could imagine using, and it's shown in pink and green with no run context.
- The lower half is nearly all product photography, with just two short copy blocks. It's a long scroll for a two-product story.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment by buyer. Send men and runners something relevant, like a Pace Breaker or run-specific layer. If the Everywhere Belt Bag is the only runner-adjacent item, put it in the hero and show it on a runner's hip.
- 2. Say what early access means. Put in a deadline, like "Members shop first through Friday," and add a stock or size line.
- 3. Add one line of function, like "Fits a phone and two gels." That is the kind of detail I'd click for.
- 4. Show the products in use, such as the belt bag on a run, so it reads as a performance product.
