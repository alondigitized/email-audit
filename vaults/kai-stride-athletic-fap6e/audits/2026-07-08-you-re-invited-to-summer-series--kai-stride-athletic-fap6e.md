# You’re invited to Summer Series
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-08**
## Executive summary

- "You're invited" — two words that actually made me pause. After a week of lululemon Summer Sale blast after blast, something that sounds like an exclusive event broke through my filter. I opened expecting something genuinely different, and it is: this isn't a discount email, it's a community play. Fifty thousand free Yoga, Pilates, and Sculpt classes all summer. Interesting concept. I respect the left turn away from the markdown machine they've been running since June.
- Here's the problem: I run. I lead a club. I'm not a mat person, and lululemon knows enough about me to know that. When I scrolled the product grid — and credit where it's due, this is the first email this week with actual labeled men's sections built into the grid architecture — I still landed on Yoga and Pilates and Sculpt. No run events. No run clubs. No trail. They have a genuine running community play and they left it entirely off the table here. The Summer Series concept is intriguing but it was built for someone else.
- I'd mark this as read. I wouldn't forward it. If the Summer Series includes outdoor running events or run clubs in my city, they forgot to mention it — and that's a real miss.

## What's working

- The pivot away from another sale email was immediately noticeable — "Summer Series" community framing earned a second look after the barrage of Summer Sale sends
- "50,000 free spots. One incredible summer." is clean, concrete copy — specific number, no fluff
- Men's sections actually built into the product grid with explicit labels (Men's Yoga, Men's Workout) — compared to the July 6 "softest sets" email that had literally zero men's product, this is structural progress
- The orange blob hero is clean and renders perfectly — no layout issues, no broken images
- "Find your space" is soft enough to work as a community headline without being a hard-sell

## What's weak

- Zero running content — this is an email about mats and studios for a guy who runs 40 miles a week
- "Learn More" is the laziest CTA in email marketing; learn more about what, exactly, and where am I going
- The "We think you'll also love" grid at the bottom visually skews back toward women's product and undercuts the men's sections they built higher in the email
- "All summer long" is not urgency — adidas's adiClub email last week gave me a specific points deadline and I actually clicked; this gives me nothing to act on
- Fifth lululemon email in seven days — the cadence is eroding my goodwill faster than the content can earn it back

## Recommendations

- 1. **Surface the run-club angle if it exists** — lululemon runs community run clubs; if Summer Series includes outdoor running events, that's the lead for me, not the mat. "Join a Summer run near you" would have stopped me cold.
- 2. **Kill "Learn More" and write a real CTA** — "Find Your Class," "Register Near Me," or "See Events in [City]" — anything that tells me what I'm clicking into.
- 3. **Replace "We think you'll also love" with men's running gear** — if you know I'm in the men's running segment, serve me that; catch-all product recs undo whatever targeting you did in the grid.
- 4. **Add a spot-scarcity signal** — "50,000 spots" sounds infinite; "spots fill fast in [City]" would create urgency that "all summer long" actively kills.
- 5. **Add one outdoor/run image** — a trail or road, not just a studio mat, to signal that Summer Series isn't exclusively yoga.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Summer Series: free classes (+ runs) near you`
- **Subject Alt B:** `50,000 free spots. Find yours this summer.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Yoga, Pilates, Sculpt, run events — all free, all summer. Register now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free classes in your city this summer — spots go fast.`
