# F50 Sparkfusion is designed for her
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-22**
## Executive summary

- This one actually stopped my thumb — an F50 Sparkfusion cleat in this zebra-holographic teal/blue colorway is legitimately cool-looking, and the "designed for her" framing plus Trinity Rodman's name gives it real credibility instead of generic hype. Problem is, I'm a road runner captaining a running club, not a soccer player, and this is a women's football boot launch — so as sharp as the creative is, none of it is for me. It's the adidas equivalent of Nike sending me a perfectly executed email about a product I'll never buy.
- The $50-gift-card-for-$40 block at the bottom is the only thing in here that's actually category-agnostic and could pull me in, but it's buried after two full screens of a cleat I have zero use for. I'd scroll past this in about four seconds, not click, and definitely not forward — unless I know a friend who plays club soccer, in which case maybe.

## What's working

- The hero shot of the athlete holding the boot up is a real photo, not a swatch — feels like an actual launch, not filler.
- "The new F50 Sparkfusion player pack celebrates Trinity Rodman's fearless and dynamic style of play" gives me an actual athlete and a reason for the design — more storytelling than adidas usually bothers with (compare to 2026-08-21's anonymous fabric-texture hero with zero athlete or product ID).
- Clean black SHOP NOW buttons with arrows are unambiguous and repeated at every section — no confusion about what to tap.
- The gift-card offer ($50 for $40) is a concrete, honest discount with a real deadline (Aug 14–28) — no fine-print bait-and-switch, it's clearly disclosed even if buried at the bottom.

## What's weak

- This is a women's soccer cleat email — I'm a male road runner, so the entire hero content (two of three product sections) is irrelevant to me before I even get to the gift card.
- Zero connection to running, my long runs, or my club — no Ultraboost, no road shoe, nothing in my actual category.
- "ALL HERS" as a section header, for a male subscriber, just reinforces this wasn't segmented to me at all — feels like a broadcast blast, not adiClub knowing who I am.
- The one universally-relevant offer (gift card) is stranded at the very bottom, after two irrelevant product pushes — buries the lead for someone like me.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment this by gender/sport interest — adiClub clearly has my running preference data (I've clicked/opened running content before), so don't send me a women's soccer cleat drop as the entire hero. Save the segment mismatch and lead a runner-targeted subscriber with the gift card + a road shoe.
- 2. If you must send broad-catalog news to non-soccer subscribers, move the $50-for-$40 gift card block up top — it's the only line that applies to everyone, don't make me scroll past two irrelevant products to find it.
- 3. Add a "shop by sport" quick-nav at the top (Running / Training / Soccer) so mismatched sends like this can still self-route me somewhere useful in one tap.
- 4. Compare to adidas's own 2026-08-19 Strava/adiClub newsletter — that one felt built for a runner because it referenced actual tracked activity; this one has no such personalization signal anywhere.

The destination domain (`www.adidas.com`) is single-encoded inside the tracking domain (`click.comms.adidas.com`), which is expected behavior for SFMC Email Studio. No action needed.
- 24 tracking/redirect links were skipped by the automated HTTP prober by design (expected for `click.comms.adidas.com` / `image.link.adidas.com` CDN and redirect domains) — no conclusion can be drawn on link validity from this run; recommend a manual click-through pass if link integrity needs confirming.
