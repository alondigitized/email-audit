# This restock’s ready to play
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-21**
## Executive summary

- I opened this because the subject line said "restock" and I thought — okay, maybe the Surge Short is back in my size, or the Pace Breaker got restocked in navy. That would actually matter to me. Instead I'm looking at capris. Women's capris. With copy that says "just like that, capris are back" and a retro vibe that has nothing to do with race pace or club runs. I scrolled to the bottom hoping to find a Swiftly or a Metal Vent section — there's an AmEx card ad instead.
- This is the same problem as the June 20 "New summer styles easing your way" send — lululemon keeps pulling me in with a subject line that sounds athletic and then delivering a women's fashion-forward catalog. Beach Ball Blue on June 18 at least had some crossover appeal. This has none. I'm genuinely a loyalist. I own six pairs of Surge Shorts and I recommend lululemon to every guy in my club. This email treats me like I don't exist.
- Mark as read. Not forwarding this to anyone. If I pass my phone to a female friend and say "check this," maybe she buys something — but that's not how email marketing is supposed to work.

## What's working

- **"Back in Stock" badge** works hard — restocks have real urgency and lululemon's popular items genuinely sell out. That signal is credible.
- **Hero headline "This gear moves fast."** is punchy. It's short, active, and sounds like something a runner would say. The problem is everything below it contradicts it.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, color swatches under product tiles are legible. Production quality is solid.
- **Product copy has specifics** — "buttery-soft," "narrow waistband," "notched hems" — that's actual product detail, not marketing fluff. I appreciate when lululemon describes the gear rather than just aspirationally posing it.

## What's weak

- **Every single product is women's.** Groove High-Rise Crop, Align Short, Crochet Mesh Tank, Steady State SuperLoft Pant — these are all women's items. I'm a 33-year-old male subscriber. This is a targeting failure, not a creative failure.
- **The AmEx card ad in the middle of the product grid** kills the shopping flow. Nike's transactional emails (like the one-time code send earlier this month) at least stay on-task. A credit card upsell buried between athletic pants and tanks is confusing.
- **"Retro-inspired cropped length"** tells me exactly who this email is for — and it's not me. The moment I hit "retro-inspired," I've mentally checked out.
- **No pricing anywhere.** Adidas's profile-completion email this month was a palate cleanser specifically because it had a clear value exchange (50 points = real benefit). Here I'm expected to click just to find out if I can afford these items.
- **Cadence.** This is the fifth email in six days from lululemon. June 15, 16, 18, 20, 21. Even if the content were relevant, the volume is exhausting.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment men out of women's-only sends.** This is the fix that matters most. If my purchase history is 95% men's performance, don't send me a capri restock. Nike and adidas have both figured out gender segmentation — lululemon clearly hasn't applied it to my account.
- 2. **Lead the hero with what's restocked in the subject.** "Groove Crop 10" is back" is infinitely more clickable than the current subject for the woman this email is actually meant for — and for me, a segmented send would say "Surge 7" restocked."
- 3. **Kill the AmEx block or move it to the footer.** Credit card ads don't belong in a product scroll. If lululemon wants to promote member credit, put it below the unsubscribe link.
- 4. **Add a price.** At least show a starting price point in the product tile. Adidas does this. Even Nike's most minimal sends show "$110" under a shoe. This email makes me do work to find the number.
- 5. **Introduce a credible countdown.** "Back in Stock" implies scarcity — lean into that. "Restocked Wednesday. Popular sizes go fast." That's an honest urgency signal, not manufactured pressure.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Groove Crop 10" is back — shop your size`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Restocked: the buttery-soft crop you missed`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Limited restock — narrow waistband, notched hem, and it sells out fast.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Back for a limited time. The Groove High-Rise Crop 10" is in.`
