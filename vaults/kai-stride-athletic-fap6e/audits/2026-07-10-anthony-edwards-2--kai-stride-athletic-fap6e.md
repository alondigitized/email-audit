# Anthony Edwards 2
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- Five days ago adidas sent me the Dropset Pro email — race-day energy, a shoe built for the track, copy that sounded like it was written by someone who has actually cracked 4:30 pace. I forwarded that one to the running club group chat. Today I'm looking at Anthony Edwards 2, a basketball shoe, and I feel like I missed the turnoff on a long run. Same brand. Completely different conversation.
- The visual execution is genuinely strong — bold blue shoe popped against a burnt-orange background is a confident art direction call, and the "INNOVATION MEETS PERFORMANCE" section at least gives me something technical to chew on. But no amount of "Articulated Fangs for control" copy lands when I have never in my life laced up for a tempo run in basketball shoes. The segmentation miss is the whole story here.
- I'd mark this as read. I wouldn't click, and I definitely wouldn't forward it to anyone in the running club. If one of my guys who plays pickup ball on Fridays were on my list I'd send it his way — but that's his email, not mine.

## What's working

- **Hero visual is sharp.** Blue-on-orange is a strong contrast choice; the shoe reads instantly even at mobile thumbnail size.
- **Single-product focus.** No carousel, no "shop the collection" sprawl — just the AE2. That discipline is something adidas lost in the FIFA World Cup collab sends.
- **Subject line is tight.** 18 characters, zero clutter, no emoji crutch. Opens clean in every inbox.
- **No render bugs.** Layout is clean top to bottom — both shoe images load, text and buttons are properly spaced.
- **"Innovation Meets Performance" section tries to earn the purchase.** The technical sub-copy (Propulsion Plate, Lightstrike cushioning) at least signals that adidas knows the shoe's specs. More than I can say for the "explore 200+ picks" dump lululemon keeps running.

## What's weak

- **Wrong person, wrong shoe.** I run 40+ miles a week. Anthony Edwards plays basketball. The connection to my inbox is zero — and I clicked on Dropset Pro five days ago, so adidas has the signal to do better.
- **"On the court and beyond" is the lamest lifestyle stretch.** That phrase is trying to tell me I can wear basketball shoes on the street like it's 2014. I've been through that era. It doesn't land.
- **No price point, no stock info, no urgency.** Nike's athlete shoe drops come with a launch time, size availability, and a "notify me" hook. This email gives me nothing to act on except "shop now" — which could mean anything.
- **The top banner is dead weight.** "Explore the latest adidas best sellers" with a generic SHOP NOW button above the hero is wasted real estate. If you're launching a signature shoe, commit to it — don't hedge with a catch-all banner.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment the list by last-clicked category.** I clicked Dropset Pro five days ago. Show me the next running shoe. The AE2 belongs in the basketball segment, not the running segment. This is the single biggest lever.
- 2. **Add a price and size-availability signal.** Nike drops athlete shoes with "available in your size" hooks and launch timestamps. Give me a reason to act now vs. later.
- 3. **Kill the generic "best sellers" banner.** Replace with the runner-relevant "While you're here: Dropset Pro — in stock now" cross-sell, at minimum.
- 4. **Sharpen the CTA copy.** "SHOP NOW" says nothing. "Shop Ant's Blue" or "Get the AE2" at least ties back to the product story.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Ant's boldest blue just dropped`
- **Subject Alt B:** `AE2 Blue — Ant's new colorway is live`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `One colorway. One goal. The Anthony Edwards 2 is here — shop now or find a store near you.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Bold blue, court-ready tech — the AE2 just landed. Shop before it sells out.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**[FAIL] Broken redirect URL — unknown protocol scheme**

The click-tracking wrapper contains a relative-style path (`/g/...`) rather than an absolute URL as the destination argument:

```
unknown url type: '/g/7eb1239b-65f8-4a08-9d8e-29d16225fe50?txn=18fc0600-...'
```

The outer URL is:
```
https://dv.adidas.com/o/7eb1239b-65f8-4a08-9d8e-29d16225fe50?cp_tp=v3.eJz...
```

The redirect resolver at `dv.adidas.com/o/` is decoding the `cp_tp` payload and producing a relative path instead of an absolute destination. This will 404 or error in any client that tries to follow it. The destination path should be `/g/7eb1239b...` prefixed with `https://www.adidas.com` (or the appropriate origin).

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[WARN] Mixed-content HTTP images — may be blocked**

The same arrow image is served over HTTP six times:
```
http://image.link.adidas.com/lib/fe6515707c62007e7715/m/3/b0c011a2-d468-414b-a8a2-8595b3ec7b34.jpg
```
Gmail, Outlook (modern), and Apple Mail all block mixed-content HTTP resources when the email is viewed over HTTPS. The arrow asset will fail to render in the majority of environments. Fix: change the `src` scheme to `https://`.

### 5. Compliance

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header not captured**

The QA relay did not surface a `List-Unsubscribe` header. This header is required for Gmail bulk-sender compliance (Google's Feb 2024 mandate for >5k/day senders) and expected by most major ISPs. If the header is present at the MTA but stripped by the AgentMail relay, this is a relay configuration issue — but if it is genuinely absent, the send is out of compliance.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found**

One-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender policy) depends on `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` appearing alongside a `https://` unsubscribe URL in `List-Unsubscribe`. Its absence puts the send at risk of deliverability penalties from Gmail and Yahoo.
