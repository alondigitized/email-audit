# The Toys Are Back! Shop NEW Disney & Pixar Toy Story Arrivals!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- This is a product-launch email for Build-A-Bear's Disney & Pixar Toy Story collection — stuffed-animal versions of Woody, Jessie, and Buzz Lightyear. The hero lands well: bold Toy Story logo, bright blue background, three instantly recognizable characters, and "YOU'VE GOT A FRIEND IN OUR NEW COLLECTION" — a clean nod to "You've Got a Friend in Me" that any Toy Story fan will catch. The IP does genuine heavy lifting here.
- The problem is the email doesn't ask for the sale, it just announces it. No price anchor, no discount, no urgency window — just "new stuff exists, come look." After the Hello Kitty flash sale five days ago (two sends, scored 7/10 and 8/10), subscribers have been trained to expect a price hook. This send offers none. The Toy Story moment is then further diluted by three secondary modules — a "Friendship Pops" coming-soon tease, a Beardusa recap from yesterday's dedicated send, and a "Most Loved" bestseller grid — turning a strong launch into a kitchen-sink browse catalog.
- The execution is clean and bug-free, and Toy Story is one of the most beloved IPs in Build-A-Bear's licensing portfolio. But the email trusts the IP to close the click when a price anchor or a limited-time hook would have converted intent into action. New arrivals without a "starting at $XX" hook is a wasted launch moment.

## What's working

- **"YOU'VE GOT A FRIEND IN OUR NEW COLLECTION"** — the headline is a franchise callback that lands immediately and earns the open.
- **Hero visual** — Woody, Jessie, and Buzz are prominently featured against a clean blue background; the eye goes straight to the product.
- **"Most Loved Stuff This Week"** — the social proof grid gives browsing subscribers a secondary entry point beyond the hero IP.
- **No render bugs** — layout is clean across all modules; no broken images, no overlapping text.

## What's weak

- **No offer** — "new arrivals" alone gives subscribers zero reason to click today rather than next week; not even a "starting at $XX" anchor appears anywhere in the email.
- **Subject line is 62 characters** — likely truncated on most mobile clients; "Shop NEW Disney & Pixar Toy Story Arrivals!" loses its tail.
- **Beardusa recap feels recycled** — Beardusa got her own dedicated email yesterday (scored 5/10); dropping her into this send as a filler module dilutes the Toy Story launch and reminds subscribers they already saw this.
- **"SHOP NOW" appears ~6 times** — every module, every CTA, same copy; no differentiation between "buy Toy Story" and "browse bestsellers."
- **Daily send cadence is visibly aggressive** — seven sends in six days; this is the kind of frequency that drives unsubscribes regardless of IP quality.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a price anchor to the hero** — "Woody, Jessie & Buzz Starting at $XX" converts awareness into intent. Even a modest anchor gives the subscriber a decision frame; without it, "new arrivals" is just browsable noise.
- 2. **Cut the Beardusa module entirely** — she had her email yesterday. Repurpose that real estate for a second Toy Story product shot or a "complete the collection" bundle suggestion.
- 3. **Collapse the "Friendship Pops coming soon" tease** — a coming-soon announcement in a launch email splits attention and delivers no conversion value.
- 4. **Give each "Most Loved" item a category-specific CTA** — "Shop Sandy Kitty" beats six identical "SHOP NOW" buttons.
- 5. **Shorten the subject line and add IP specificity** — name the characters in the subject, not just the franchise.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Woody, Jessie & Buzz Are Here — Shop Toy Story`
- **Subject Alt B:** `You've Got a Friend in Build-A-Bear 🤠`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The Toy Story crew just landed. Complete your collection starting at $XX.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Woody. Jessie. Buzz. All available now — shop before they sell out.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Non-standard CSS property casing — confirmed bug:**
```css
p { Margin: 0px !important; Padding: 0px !important; }
```
`Margin` and `Padding` with capital letters are not valid CSS property names. Most clients will silently ignore these declarations, leaving paragraph spacing uncontrolled. Should be lowercase `margin` / `padding`.

**Viewport meta trailing whitespace:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1.0 " />
```
Trailing space inside the content attribute value. Benign in most parsers but technically malformed.

**Responsive breakpoint gap:** Two media queries cover `481px–639px` and `≤480px`. The template's assumed 600px fixed-width table sits outside both breakpoints (640px+ gets no responsive override). If the 600px container renders at 600px on a mid-size viewport, no fluid rules apply.

**Image alt text:** Cannot confirm from truncated source. All `<img>` tags must carry descriptive `alt` attributes — critical for accessibility and for clients that block images by default.

**Dark mode:** `color-scheme` and `supported-color-schemes` meta tags are present — good. Cannot confirm whether background/foreground colors invert correctly without full source.
