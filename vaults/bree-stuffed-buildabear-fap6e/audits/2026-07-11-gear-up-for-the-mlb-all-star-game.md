# Gear Up for the MLB® ALL-STAR GAME®!
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-11**
## Executive summary

- This is Build-A-Bear's tie-in to the MLB All-Star Game, a licensed-IP awareness and catalog send in the same vein as the July 10 Sanrio/Hello Kitty email — swap the property, keep the format. The creative is clean: the MLB All-Star lockup hero is recognizable, the bear plushes wearing team jerseys are charming, and the nav CTA strip at the bottom is well-organized. But there's no offer here. No discount, no bundle deal, no event deadline — just "here are licensed bears, go shop." After three weeks of Cub Condo 50%-off events and flash sales, this reads as a cool-down/catalog send, not a conversion driver.
- The persona fit is a stretch for a general deal-seeker. Licensed MLB product is a real niche — it lands for a baseball fan shopping a gift before the All-Star break, but falls flat for anyone outside that window. The timing is defensible (All-Star Week is imminent), but the email doesn't manufacture urgency around it. "Gear Up for the MLB All-Star Game" implies there's somewhere to gear up to; the body delivers a product catalog, not a reason to act today.
- Bottom line: solid licensed execution with a clear visual hierarchy and no render bugs, but this is a 3/10 commercial intent email cosplaying as a 7/10. No offer = no lever to pull.

## What's working

- **Timely licensing peg** — All-Star Week is a real calendar moment; the MLB shield + ALL-STAR GAME lockup in the hero is instantly recognizable and legitimizes the send.
- **Clean visual hierarchy** — Hero → team bears grid → fanatics cross-sell → nav strip. The eye follows it without confusion.
- **No render bugs** — All images load, text is legible, no broken elements or layout blowouts.
- **Good product breadth** — Four team bears (Dodgers, Blue Jays, Phillies, Rays) give the grid variety without feeling cluttered.
- **Bottom nav strip is excellent** — Four specific CTAs (Build Online, Personalize with Sound, Gift Shop, Find a Workshop) cover every conversion path without being pushy.

## What's weak

- **Zero offer** — No discount, no bundle, no free shipping — just MSRP product. After the June 24 Cub Condo 50%-off emails, this feels like a step back in value.
- **"All-Star Gifts" framing is vague** — The subhead "Rep your favorite MLB team this ALL-STAR WEEK" is aspirational copy with nothing to act on; it's telling me to feel something, not do something.
- **Team selection is arbitrary** — Dodgers, Blue Jays, Phillies, Rays. No clear logic (not the top-selling teams, not the All-Star rosters, not localized). A subscriber in Atlanta sees a Tampa Bay bear but not a Braves bear.
- **Fanatics cross-sell block is visually weak** — The gray box with a small "BATTER UP!" header sits awkwardly between the product grid and the nav strip; it looks like an afterthought ad insertion.
- **No urgency signal** — "All-Star Week" implies a deadline but the email never states one. Contrast with the June 22 "Stuff Your Cub Condo This Wednesday" — that email owned the date. This one doesn't.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a time-bounded offer tied to All-Star Week** — "20% off all MLB bears through Sunday" or "free personalization this All-Star Week" gives subscribers a reason to act now rather than bookmark it. This is the single highest-impact change.
- 2. **Localize or explain the team selection** — Either show the subscriber's local team first (if segmented by zip), or frame the grid explicitly: "The All-Star rosters are set — shop your team." Arbitrary grids feel like catalog filler.
- 3. **Sharpen the hero subhead** — Replace "Rep your favorite MLB team this ALL-STAR WEEK" with something that creates pull: "All-Star Week starts Monday — get your bear before the first pitch."
- 4. **Fix the Fanatics block** — Give it a visual frame that matches the rest of the email, or cut it. Right now it reads as a display ad dropped into editorial.
- 5. **Add a deadline line above the primary CTA** — Even "Shop before July 15" would double the click pressure.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Shop Your Team Before the First Pitch`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `All-Star Week starts Monday. Get your licensed bear before it's over.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Dodgers, Blue Jays, Phillies, Rays — and more. Free personalization this week only.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CRITICAL] `X-UA-Compatible` meta tag is broken.**
```html
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" Aontent="IE=edge" />
```
`Aontent` is not a valid attribute — the directive is entirely ignored. Outlook on Windows (which uses the IE/Trident engine for some rendering paths) will not receive the `edge` mode signal. Fix: `content="IE=edge"`.

**[HIGH] Malformed MSO VML conditional block — duplicated closing tags.**
```xml
<o:PixelsPerInch>96</o:PixelsPerInch></o:PixelsPerInch>
</o:OfficeDocumentSettings></o:OfficeDocumentSettings>
```
Both tags are closed twice. This is invalid XML inside the conditional comment. Outlook's XML parser may reject the block, disabling PNG rendering and DPI settings for Outlook desktop clients.

**[MEDIUM] `#table-container` has an invalid `border` shorthand.**
```css
#table-container { border: 10px; ... }
```
`border: 10px` is missing a style keyword (e.g., `solid`) and a color. The shorthand is incomplete; most clients will discard it entirely, which is likely the intended behavior — but if this is meant to produce a visible border, it is broken.

**[LOW] Capitalized CSS property names in `p {}` rule.**
```css
p { Margin: 0px !important; Padding: 0px !important; }
```
CSS property names are case-insensitive per spec, so this works in all major clients. Non-standard and should be normalized to lowercase.

**[LOW] Trailing space in viewport `content` attribute.**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1.0 " />
```
Parses correctly but is technically malformed.

**[LOW] Soft-hyphen (`&shy;`) entities in preheader-padding div.**
The spacer block mixes `&zwnj;&nbsp;` with `&shy;` characters. In some older clients, soft hyphens may render as visible `-` characters rather than zero-width breaks, injecting artifacts into the preheader preview text.

**[INFO] Duplicate `display: none` in `.gmailfix`.**
```css
.gmailfix { display: none; display: none !important; }
```
Redundant but harmless.
