# Shop Early Black Friday Deals!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-22**
## Executive summary

- This is a clear, festive Black Friday send with a strong hierarchy. "BLACK FRIDAY DEALS" in big red type on a cream ribbon panel lands first. Directly below it, "SELECT FURRY FRIENDS 30% OFF" gives a concrete number. The free-shipping bar at the top ($100+) adds a second incentive. The red-and-cream holiday framing looks seasonal and on-brand.
- The weakness is that it repeats yesterday's send. On 2026-11-21 the brand sent "30% OFF Online: Early Black Friday Starts NOW!", which scored 8/10. Today's email makes the same 30% claim with a vaguer subject and no new reason to act. The hero says "Shop festive deals" without naming a deadline, and the real deadlines (11/25 and 11/28) sit in the small print at the bottom. There are also eleven SHOP NOW buttons and almost no copy telling you what each one leads to.
- Verdict: a solid, competent promo, but it's a second pass at a deal the subscriber saw 24 hours ago, and the subject doesn't mention the 30% that makes it worth opening.

## What's working

- The "30% OFF" in big red type is the clearest element in the email and reads at a glance.
- The free-shipping banner in orange at the very top adds a second, unambiguous incentive.
- The festive ribbon-frame design signals the season and holds the three offer panels together.
- The product grid (Pawlette Bunny, Cookiebara, Gingerbread Axolotl, Axolotl PJ set) gives concrete products to click.
- The footer is clean, with workshop, best-sellers and party links for non-shoppers.

## What's weak

- The deadlines are only in the footer fine print (Nov 25 for shipping and 30% off, Nov 28 for clothing). The hero says "limited time" with no date.
- Eleven identical "SHOP NOW" buttons with no verb or noun specificity dilute the call to action.
- The "Start Earning Points!" pill at the top right is tiny and floating, and it's disconnected from any offer.
- The hero panel ("Shop festive deals and exciting markdowns") is generic copy that takes a lot of space without naming the offer.
- Product tiles show no prices or markdown amounts, so the 30% off isn't tied to any product.
- The two "More to Explore" tiles (the Grinch and the gingerbread bear) have no labels.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the deadline in the hero: "Ends 11/25" next to the 30% off. This is the biggest lift for urgency.
- 2. Merge the hero and the 30% panel so the first screen carries the offer. Drop the generic "Don't miss out" panel.
- 3. Add sale prices (for example "$24 $35") under each product tile.
- 4. Vary the button copy: "Shop 30% Off Plush", "Shop Outfits", "Get Free Shipping".
- 5. Label the Grinch and gingerbread tiles, or cut them.
- 6. Differentiate from yesterday's send, for example with "Day 2: new markdowns added".
- **Subject Alt A:** `30% off plush ends Tuesday 🐻`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Black Friday at Build-A-Bear: 30% off + free shipping`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Select furry friends 30% off online, plus free shipping on $100+. Ends 11/25.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New markdowns on outfits and accessories, too. Online only.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email's HTML source is a standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional table layout with MSO conditionals and responsive media queries. The QA data shows 4 product CTAs returning HTTP 403, 4 images without alt text, and a UTM term containing "Testing". The 403s are probably bot-blocking rather than dead pages. The header warnings are limited by the relay, not confirmed defects.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **4 "SHOP NOW" CTAs returned 403** on probe. All are on `www.buildabear.com`:
  - `/pawlette-bunny-plush-party-gift-set/33639_32815_20389.html`
  - `/cookiebara-stuffed-animal/032817.html`
  - `/gingerbread-axolotl-stuffed-animal/034239.html`
  - `/axolotl-stuffed-animal-pj-sleeper-gift-set/29486_31016.html`
- **The 403s are unconfirmed as broken.** A 403 (rather than 404/410) from a retail domain usually means WAF or bot protection rejecting the automated probe. The pass rate of 30% is probably understated for this reason. Verify with a real-browser request before treating these as live defects.
- **The CTA anchors have no recorded text.** The QA output shows an empty field before "(link text: "SHOP NOW")". The anchor text may sit in an image or VML button. This can't be confirmed from the truncated source.
- **24 tracking/redirect links skipped the HTTP probe**, so their resolution is unverified.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **4 images are missing alt text.** All are product images served from `/dw/image/v2/BBNG_PRD/.../`:
  - `94231331222601_32815_20389~x20010012.jpg`
  - `74240700232817~x20116045.jpg`
  - `96250802134239~x20328030.jpg`
  - `16231321229486_31016x20958016.jpg`
- **These are probably the images linked to the failing "SHOP NOW" CTAs.** If so, image-blocking clients and screen readers get no product context.
- **`<title>` is `Build-A-Bear - Build-A-Bear_BroadcastTemplate_1A`.** This is an internal template name leaking into the document title. It is low severity, and only matters in web-view or some assistive tech.
- No other rendering defects are evident. The viewport meta, `X-UA-Compatible`, MSO settings, preheader and media queries look correct.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved merge tokens (`{{ }}`, `%%`, `[FIRSTNAME]`, etc.) appear in the visible source. The source is truncated, so the footer and later sections are unchecked.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **No `List-Unsubscribe` header and no `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) header were found.** The QA tool itself says AgentMail may not capture them. Treat this as unverified, not a confirmed violation. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules expect both for one-click unsubscribe.
- **`Authentication-Results` was not found**, so SPF/DKIM/DMARC status is unknown. The sender domain is `ebuildabear.com`. Check the raw headers at the receiving MTA.
- The in-body unsubscribe link and the physical postal address are not in the truncated source, so neither could be verified.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
All four product URLs carry the same parameters:
`utm_source=Campaign&utm_medium=Email&utm_campaign=BlackFridayEvergreen&utm_term=US-US_Testing_Hot-20261122&utm_content=Promo&utm_id=2807243`
- **`utm_term` contains `Testing`.** This suggests a test or seed-list segment value reached the production send, which would pollute analytics segmentation.
- **`utm_campaign=BlackFridayEvergreen` doesn't match the subject ("Shop Early Black Friday Deals!").** This is an observation about naming only, not a confirmed defect.
- **`utm_content=Promo` is identical on every link.** It can't distinguish placements or products in reporting.
- **`utm_source=Campaign` is a generic value** rather than a source identifier such as the ESP or list name.
- Landing page alignment can't be verified, because the product pages returned 403 to the probe.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Re-test the 4 product URLs from a real browser or allow-listed client. If they load, treat the 403s as probe artifacts and exclude them from the pass rate.
2. Add descriptive alt text to the 4 product images (the product names are in the URL slugs).
3. Check the live send for `utm_term=...Testing...`. If it is present, correct the segment naming and the UTM template.
4. Make `utm_content` unique per link or slot.
5. Verify `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and the SPF/DKIM/DMARC results from the raw headers of a message received directly, not through the AgentMail relay.
