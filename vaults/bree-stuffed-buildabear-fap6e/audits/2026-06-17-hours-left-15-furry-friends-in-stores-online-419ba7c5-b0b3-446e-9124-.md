# HOURS LEFT: $15 Furry Friends in Stores & Online!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-17**
## Executive summary

- This is Build-A-Bear's afternoon urgency bump — the "HOURS LEFT" resend of the same $15 flash-sale that went out earlier this morning (already audited at 8/10 in brand history). Strategically, the playbook is correct: you don't redesign a flash-sale reminder, you just dial up urgency. The creative is almost identical to the morning send — same blue hero, same yellow $15, same bunny plush, same TODAY ONLY badge. That consistency is deliberate and fine.
- The problem is what comes *after* the hero. Secondary blocks for New Summer Mini Beans, Summer Bag Charms, and a "Most Loved Stuff This Week" four-product grid give browsers an easy exit from the only conversion goal this email should have. A "HOURS LEFT" send should be one offer, one button, done. This one is a full catalog email wearing urgency clothing.
- Verdict: The hero works. The structure undermines it. Recipients who opened the morning send and didn't convert will feel the FOMO push — that's the point — but anyone who scrolls past the hero loses the urgency signal entirely.

## What's working

- **$15 hero is unmissable above the fold**: Large yellow price, "TODAY ONLY!" burst, and the "Feeling the Squeeze? GET A HUG!" headline all read in under two seconds.
- **Subject urgency matches hero urgency**: "HOURS LEFT" in the subject sets the expectation; "TODAY ONLY!" in the hero delivers it — rare subject-to-body alignment.
- **Bunny plush character lands well**: The specific product shown in the hero is charming and matches the "furry friend" copy promise; no disconnect between offer and visual.
- **Multi-channel clarity**: "in Stores & Online" in subject and body removes ambiguity about where to redeem.
- **Clean render**: No broken images, overlapping text, or layout issues visible.

## What's weak

- **Second email, same day, same offer**: The morning send was already audited at 8/10. This is the seventh-plus email in six days, and now two in one day on the same deal — cadence fatigue is real for anyone tracking the brand's pace.
- **Secondary content completely undercuts urgency**: Mini Beans, Bag Charms, and a four-product Most Loved grid are catalog email content, not flash-sale content. They give undecided shoppers an escape from the $15 CTA.
- **No hard deadline time in the body**: "TODAY ONLY" and "HOURS LEFT" are vague. "Ends at 11:59 PM CT" creates a concrete, credible cliff — this doesn't have it.
- **Free shipping bar is a mismatch**: "$150 or More" for free shipping pinned above a $15 flash deal creates dissonance right at the top of the email.
- **No loyalty/member hook**: Seven emails in and no Bonus Club callout, points reminder, or member-exclusive angle — a miss for returning customers who need something beyond pure price.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Strip the body to hero + one CTA only.** Kill Mini Beans, Bag Charms, and Most Loved for this resend. A HOURS LEFT urgency bump earns its keep by being short and driving one action.
- 2. **Add a concrete deadline timestamp in the hero subhead.** "Ends tonight at 11:59 PM CT" outperforms "TODAY ONLY" every time — it triggers a real mental deadline.
- 3. **Swap the free shipping banner for a loyalty hook.** "Plus earn Bonus Club points on your $15 bear" — relevant, free to add, and converts loyalists who didn't bite on price alone.
- 4. **Tighten the CTA copy from "SHOP NOW" to "Grab My $15 Bear."** Verb + specific noun, removes the generic feel, increases click intent.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last chance: $15 Build-A-Bear ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your $15 Furry Friend closes in hours`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `TODAY ONLY — pick your bear, bunny, or bestie for just $15. In stores & online.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The $15 deal closes at midnight. Don't miss your hug.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Invalid CSS property casing — `Margin`**
```css
p { Margin: 0px !important; Padding: 0px !important; }
```
`Margin` and `Padding` with capital first letters are not valid CSS properties. Most email clients will silently ignore these declarations, leaving paragraph spacing at browser default. Fix: lowercase `margin` and `padding`.

**Preheader padding div missing `!important`**
```html
<div style="display: none; max-height: 0px; overflow: hidden;">
  &nbsp;&zwnj;...
</div>
```
`display: none` without `!important` can be overridden by client stylesheets (notably older Outlook.com), causing the filler characters to render visibly in the preview pane or body. The earlier preheader div correctly uses `display: none !important` inline — this second div should match.

**Redundant duplicate declaration in `.gmailfix`**
```css
.gmailfix { display: none; display: none !important; }
```
The first `display: none` is dead code shadowed immediately by `display: none !important`. Remove the first declaration.

**Trailing space in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1.0 " />
```
Trailing space before the closing quote is technically malformed. Likely harmless across current clients but should be cleaned up.
