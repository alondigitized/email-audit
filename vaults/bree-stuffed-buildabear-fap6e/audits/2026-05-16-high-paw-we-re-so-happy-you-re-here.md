# High Paw! We're So Happy You're Here
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- This is a welcome email from Build-A-Bear Workshop — a brand-awareness onboarding send designed to orient new subscribers rather than convert them on a specific product. The "High Paw! We're So Happy You're Here" subject sets a warm, playful tone that matches the brand perfectly. The email is clean, well-structured, and free of major render issues.
- The problem is it's a welcome email doing too many jobs at once. It tries to introduce the brand, promote the Bonus Club loyalty program, show three different content tiles, and display social links — all in a single short scroll. Nothing is prioritized. A new subscriber doesn't know where to look first, and there's no single offer compelling enough to pull a click. The "Gifts Under $30" tile is the closest thing to a concrete hook, but it's buried in the bottom third.
- Net judgment: a competent, brand-safe welcome email that will generate goodwill but very few clicks. It plays it safe when a stronger welcome hook (e.g., a sign-up discount or a clear loyalty-join offer) would have meaningfully improved conversion.

## What's working

- **Brand voice is consistent and warm** — "High Paw!" and "Welcome!" land exactly as expected from a family/gift brand; no tonal mismatch.
- **No render bugs visible** — images load cleanly, text doesn't overlap, layout holds together across the visible frame.
- **Loyalty program is prominently surfaced** — the "Bonus Club" tile with "Join Now" CTA is visible and clear, which is the right move for a welcome email.
- **"Gifts Under $30" tile** is a specific, concrete hook that gives a budget-conscious shopper an immediate reason to click.
- **Visual hierarchy is readable** — the hero carousel, then CTA row, then three content tiles flows logically top to bottom.

## What's weak

- **No concrete offer or discount** — a new subscriber gets nothing tangible (no % off, no free shipping, no bonus points) that rewards signing up. That's a wasted onboarding moment.
- **"Let's Shop" CTA is generic** — it's the primary above-the-fold button and the copy does zero work. It could link anywhere; there's no reason to click it over any other button.
- **Three competing tiles fragment attention** — Build Your Own Bear, Personalize Gifts, and Gifts Under $30 all fight for the same eyeballs, diluting click-through on each.
- **"Open Access to Deals" badge** appears in the lower-left but reads as design clutter — unclear what it means or why it's there without more context.
- **Social row at the bottom is predictable padding** — adds visual weight without adding value in a welcome email; better to lean harder into the loyalty program CTA.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a welcome offer** — "Get 10% off your first order" or "Earn 200 bonus points when you build your first bear" gives a new subscriber a reason to act *now*. This is the single highest-leverage change.
- 2. **Rewrite the hero CTA** — change "Let's Shop" to something specific like "Build Your First Bear" or "Shop Gifts Under $30" — match it to a landing page with intent.
- 3. **Collapse to one primary tile** — pick the strongest one (likely "Gifts Under $30" as the lowest-friction entry point) and cut the other two or demote them below the fold.
- 4. **Clarify the "Open Access to Deals" badge** — if it means something for Bonus Club members, say so explicitly; if not, remove it.
- 5. **Strengthen the Bonus Club pitch** — add one concrete benefit ("earn points on every purchase") next to the "Join Now" button so it's not just a brand name.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Welcome! Here's what to build first`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your first bear is waiting — let's go`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New members get 10% off + free Bonus Club points on your first order`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Build something unforgettable — gifts start at under $30`
