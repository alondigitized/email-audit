# Celebrate International Day of Play With Us!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-11**
## Executive summary

- This is Build-A-Bear's cause-marketing send for International Day of Play — a real, calendar-anchored holiday (June 11), so the timing is legitimate. The email leads with a donation ask for The Toy Foundation before pivoting to product, which is a notable structural choice: the first thing a subscriber sees above the fold isn't a product or a deal, it's a child's hands holding a heart and a "GIVE TODAY" button. That's a deliberate values-first framing, and it's on-brand for BAB's foundation positioning — but it means the primary conversion action is a charity donation, not a purchase.
- The rest of the email is a mixed bag: "YOU'VE GOT A FRIEND!" (friendship bear tie-in), "Oh, The Places You'll Go!" (Dr. Seuss/graduation bears), and a "Most Loved Stuff This Week" grid. Three disconnected thematic blocks after the hero fragment the narrative. Compared to this week's tightly focused product launches — Chummy Shark, Lightning Bug, Chococat — this email lacks a clear single star to root for.
- The free shipping code ("SUMMER") is buried in the top announcement bar and never reinforced in the body copy or subject. This is the most actionable offer in the email, and it's doing the least work.

## What's working

- **Cause timing is genuine.** International Day of Play is June 11 — sent on the actual day, not a week out. That earns real relevance.
- **Free shipping with "SUMMER" code** is visible at the very top, giving a transactional reason to act even for subscribers who won't donate.
- **The Toy Foundation co-branding** adds credibility to the charity ask — the logos are visible and legible.
- **"Most Loved Stuff This Week" grid** features recognizable IP (Toothless, Eeyore) that can pull fence-sitters to click.
- **No render bugs.** The email is visually clean throughout.

## What's weak

- **Hero CTA is "GIVE TODAY," not "SHOP NOW."** A donation ask as the primary CTA is a jarring switch for a subscriber accustomed to product-led emails.
- **Three thematic blocks compete with each other.** Day of Play charity → You've Got a Friend → Oh the Places You'll Go → Most Loved — there's no single story; the email tries to be four emails at once.
- **Free shipping code is never restated in the body.** It appears once in the announcement bar and disappears; a subscriber reading the email top-to-bottom will forget it by the time they reach a product they want.
- **Subject line announces a holiday but offers nothing.** Unlike "Our NEW Shark Is a Fin-tastic Friend! 🦈" or "Light Up Summer Nights With Our New Lightning Bug!" — both scored higher this week — this subject has no product hook, no pun, no teaser.
- **No new character or product.** This week's 8–9/10 sends all had a debut to rally around. This email has nothing new.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead subject with the offer, not the holiday.** "Celebrate International Day of Play" tells subscribers nothing they can act on. If the cause-marketing angle is the lead, make the impact concrete: "Play forward: your purchase helps a child today."
- 2. **Restate the free shipping code at minimum once inside the email body** — right before the product grid — so it's salient when a subscriber is deciding to click.
- 3. **Choose one product narrative for the body** (either the friendship bears OR the Dr. Seuss bears, not both) and relegate the other to a secondary module. The "Most Loved" grid is a fine secondary cadence block; it doesn't need two themed blocks fighting above it.
- 4. **Quantify the donation impact.** "Every donation delivers toys to children in need" is vague. "Each donation sends a bear to a child who may not otherwise receive one" is specific and moves people.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Play Forward: Shop Today & Help a Child Play`
- **Subject Alt B:** `It's International Day of Play — Free Shipping + Give Back`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free shipping today with code SUMMER — and every order helps The Toy Foundation.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Today, your purchase plays forward. Shop & donate — free shipping with SUMMER.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

1. **Malformed meta attribute** — Line:
   ```html
   <meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" Aontent="IE=edge" />
   ```
   `Aontent` is a typo for `content`. The attribute is ignored by all parsers; Outlook's IE-edge rendering mode hint is effectively disabled.

2. **Duplicate MSO closing tags** in the VML conditional comment block:
   ```xml
   </o:PixelsPerInch></o:PixelsPerInch>
   </o:OfficeDocumentSettings></o:OfficeDocumentSettings>
   ```
   Double closing tags are malformed XML. Outlook's XML parser may recover silently, but this is a latent Outlook rendering risk.

3. **Invalid CSS shorthand** — `#table-container { border: 10px; }` is missing `border-style` and `border-color`; `10px` alone is not valid per spec and will be ignored by all conforming parsers.

4. **`overflow: hidden` and `border-radius` on `#table-container`** — Neither property is supported in Outlook (any version) due to the VML/Word rendering engine. These styles silently no-op in Outlook clients.

5. **`<style>` block inside `<body>`** — A second `<style>` block (`div.preheader { display: none !important; }`) is injected as the first child of `<body>`. Gmail strips `<style>` from `<head>` but may handle body-level styles inconsistently depending on version. Low risk, but should be consolidated into `<head>`.
