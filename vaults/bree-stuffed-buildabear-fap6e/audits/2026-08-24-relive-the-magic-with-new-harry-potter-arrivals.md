# Relive the Magic with NEW Harry Potter™ Arrivals!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-24**
## Executive summary

- This is a genuine licensed-IP hero send — Build-A-Bear leaning on the 25th Anniversary Harry Potter tie-in with a full lineup shot (Slytherin, Hufflepuff, Ravenclaw bears plus Dobby) in a Hogwarts-corridor setting, which is a much stronger visual anchor than the vault-nostalgia or cryptid-plush sends earlier this month. The "BRINGING BACK THE MAGIC" gold-on-navy starfield panel is genuinely well art-directed and reads instantly, unlike the busier "Pro..." Halloween sends flagged in brand history.
- Where it falls apart is focus: right below the Harry Potter hero, the email pivots hard into an unrelated green Halloween owl banner, then a "Most Loved Stuff This Week" grid mixing Hello Kitty, a cow, a Halloween cat, and a werewolf, then a generic "New Arrivals / Back in Stock" block. The subject and preview promise Harry Potter; only the top ~20% of the email actually delivers on that promise, so anyone who scrolls past the fold gets a completely different, thematically incoherent email.
- Net: strong single hero, undermined by an unfocused body. Worth opening on subject alone, but click intent could easily get diverted or diluted by the mid-email topic switch.

## What's working

- Harry Potter hero image is high-production, on-theme, and immediately legible against the Hogwarts-corridor backdrop.
- "BRINGING BACK THE MAGIC" gold foil-style headline + navy starfield panel is a clean, on-brand visual treatment distinct from prior sends.
- "25 Years of Magic" Harry Potter anniversary lockup adds specific, credible campaign context (not generic promo language).
- Single unambiguous gold "SHOP NOW" CTA sits directly under the hero copy — no scrolling needed to find it.
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, text is crisp, no overlap or broken elements.

## What's weak

- No discount, price, or offer of any kind — this is a pure "new arrivals" awareness push, with zero of the "concrete offer" signals the last few sends (e.g. 2026-08-19's 20% OFF) at least attempted.
- Immediately after the Harry Potter hero, the email jumps to an unrelated green Halloween owl promo, breaking the theme the subject line sold.
- The "Most Loved Stuff This Week" grid (Hello Kitty, cow, cat, werewolf) has no Harry Potter product in it at all — a reader who opened for wizarding-world arrivals never actually sees a specific Harry Potter SKU beyond the hero photo.
- CTA copy is generic "SHOP NOW" repeated 7+ times throughout, with no product-specific or Harry Potter–specific link language.

## Recommendations

- 1. Show actual sellable Harry Potter products (a bear, a wand, a robe) either in the hero or immediately below it — right now the hero is atmospheric but nothing in the visible grid lets a fan actually shop Harry Potter items.
- 2. Cut or move the Halloween owl banner out of this send entirely — it directly contradicts the subject's promise and dilutes an otherwise strong single-theme email.
- 3. Swap the generic "Most Loved Stuff This Week" grid for Harry Potter-specific bestsellers/back-in-stock items to keep the whole email on-theme.
- 4. Add a real hook to the offer — even a soft one ("free house-crest embroidery," "limited holiday quantities") would lift this above pure top-of-funnel awareness.
