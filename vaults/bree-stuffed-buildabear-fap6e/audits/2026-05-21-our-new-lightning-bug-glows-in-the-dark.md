# Our NEW Lightning Bug Glows in the Dark!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- Build-A-Bear's Lightning Bug launch email is a study in a good idea executed with only partial commitment. The "READY. SET. GLOW!" headline is sharp wordplay, the "This is kind of a bug deal" body pun earns a genuine smile, and the dark atmospheric hero image is a smart creative choice — using the darkness to *sell* the glow feature. The problem is the hero never shows the actual plush. You get mood and concept but never see the product. A collector or gift-buyer who missed the subject line wouldn't know what they're looking at until the body copy.
- Below the hero the email falls into catalog mode fast: Octopail Mashimal, Bluey summer, Hello Kitty, Winnie the Pooh — four modules of existing inventory that dilute the lightning bug moment. Compare this to the May 19 Grogu send, which locked onto one product and let it breathe. This email tries to do that but flinches halfway through.
- No discount, no urgency, sixth email in five days from this sender. Good creative on top, but the structural issues cap its conversion ceiling.

## What's working

- "READY. SET. GLOW!" headline and "kind of a bug deal" body copy land as genuine wordplay — rare in plush marketing.
- Dark hero image is a deliberate creative choice that communicates the glow feature viscerally before you read a word.
- "LET'S GLOW" CTA is on-brand, action-oriented, and memorable — better than "SHOP NOW."
- Clean render, rounded card container, and bold section headers create clear visual hierarchy with no layout bugs visible.
- Glow-in-the-dark feature is explicitly called out as the key product differentiator — one clear reason to click.

## What's weak

- The hero image shows an atmospheric silhouette, not the actual Lightning Bug plush — the product itself never appears above the fold.
- No pricing, no discount, no offer hook anywhere in the email — this is a pure "new product" push with nothing to sweeten the buy.
- Catalog sprawl after the hero (Octopail, Bluey, Hello Kitty, Pooh) undercuts the hero product moment and makes the email feel indecisive.
- Sixth email in five days from this sender — open fatigue is a real risk at this cadence, and there's nothing here to re-earn attention from lapsed openers.
- "Most Loved Stuff This Week" section has no social proof attached — no star ratings, no counts, just product names repackaged as popularity signals without evidence.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show the product in the hero.** Add a direct product shot of the Lightning Bug plush glowing in the dark — a dark background with the plush lit up is both on-brand and a concrete sales image. Keep the atmospheric mood; just add the SKU.
- 2. **Add a soft urgency anchor.** Even "arriving in workshops this week" or "limited run" copy would give a reason to act now rather than later. The Grogu email had a movie date — find an equivalent for this one.
- 3. **Cut or consolidate the catalog modules.** Keep Bluey if summer tie-in is the email theme; drop Octopail Mashimal (its own launch email can do the heavy lifting). "Most Loved" can live in its own dedicated send.
- 4. **Rewrite the preheader** to add information the subject doesn't already carry — the subject names the feature, the preheader should name a reason to care or add urgency.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Lightning Bug: New Plush That Glows in the Dark`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Catch the Glow — Our Lightning Bug Plush is Here`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Build your own — then watch it light up the dark.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `A glow-in-the-dark plush you can make your own. Shop now.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>` element.** Screen readers cannot infer language, violating WCAG 2.1 SC 3.1.1. Fix: `<html lang="en" xmlns=...>`.

- **Trailing whitespace in `viewport` meta:** `content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1.0 "` — space before closing quote is technically malformed. Unlikely to cause breakage in modern clients but should be trimmed.

- **`div.preheader` hidden via two separate mechanisms:** a `class="preheader"` rule in `<head>` CSS plus a second `<style>` block injected into `<body>` (`div.preheader { display: none !important; }`). Body-level `<style>` blocks are stripped by Gmail (non-promotional tab), Outlook.com, and some corporate proxies. If the head rule also gets stripped, the preheader text "Online Exclusive: New Lightning Bug!" will render visible in the email body.
  - Mitigation: consolidate into the `<head>` style block and rely on the `&zwnj;` padding div for preview suppression (already present — good).

- **`alt` text on images cannot be audited** — source is truncated before image tags. Require full source to verify.

- **`.gmailfix { display: none; display: none !important; }` duplicate declaration** — redundant, harmless, minor code hygiene issue.
