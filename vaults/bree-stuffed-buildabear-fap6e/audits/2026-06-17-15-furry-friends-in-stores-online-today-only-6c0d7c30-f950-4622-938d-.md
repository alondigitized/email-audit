# $15 Furry Friends in Stores & Online TODAY ONLY!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-17**
## Executive summary

- This is Build-A-Bear's flash sale send — a $15 "furry friends" offer valid in stores and online for one day only. After six consecutive thematic emails (soccer twice, summer wardrobe, Sanrio, graduation, HOT arrivals), this is the first hard-discount, price-anchored send in the run, and it's the right pivot. A concrete price creates fundamentally different urgency than "check out our new Sanrio arrivals."
- The hero is BAB's best creative in this recent stretch: "$15" in a massive yellow callout, "TODAY ONLY!" badge, an on-brand furry friend product image, and a clean SHOP NOW. Visual hierarchy is sharp and the copy is honest. The email earns its flash-sale framing.
- The damage is context. This is day eight of consecutive sending. A subscriber who opened the June 15 Pawlette summer wardrobe email (the 9/10 send that week) and the June 16 soccer email has now seen BAB on seven or eight straight days. Fatigue risk is real. Worse, there's a structural contradiction embedded in the banner: free shipping requires a $100 minimum, but the hero hero is $15. Most buyers clicking through will face a shipping charge at checkout — a friction point BAB is advertising straight into.

## What's working

- **$15 callout dominates immediately**: The price is the largest non-logo element on the page — no scrolling required to find the offer.
- **"TODAY ONLY!" badge reinforces subject urgency**: The time limit is visually restated in the hero, not just the inbox subject.
- **"Feeling the Squeeze? GET A HUG!"** is a charming pun that fits BAB's voice without trying too hard.
- **"Most Loved Stuff This Week" product grid** provides browsable depth without undermining the hero — a solid fallback for subscribers who don't want the specific $15 product.
- **Clean render top to bottom**: No broken images, overlapping text, or layout bugs observed.

## What's weak

- **$100 free shipping threshold actively undermines a $15 hero offer**: Buying the hero item alone will incur shipping — BAB is advertising a deal while quietly charging freight against it.
- **Eighth consecutive send in eight days**: The cadence exhausts list goodwill; the June 15 (9/10) and June 16 (8/10) sends were both strong but left nowhere to go except list fatigue.
- **"Summer Fun in All Sizes" section is vague**: No specific character or product called out — just a generic Disney Summer squad mention with no hooks.
- **Grammar error in the Mystery Plush copy**: "See if you can guess **the** all five this summer!" — the broken phrasing is visible in the render.
- **Five SHOP NOW sections + four channel buttons**: The TODAY ONLY urgency established in the hero is diluted before the subscriber reaches the footer.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Drop the $100 free shipping bar or carve out flash-sale-day exceptions.** Shipping discovery at checkout is the most likely reason this send underperforms its open rate. This is the single highest-leverage fix.
- 2. **Cut to two email sections for flash sale sends.** Hero ($15) + one product grid. Mystery Plush and Summer Fun both compete for attention that should tunnel toward the primary offer.
- 3. **Fix "the all five" in the Mystery Plush copy** — it's a grammatical error in live copy.
- 4. **Add "while supplies last" or a countdown element** below the hero SHOP NOW to reinforce scarcity beyond the subject line.
- 5. **Space out the cadence.** The $15 flash sale is the right anchor for a week; the prior six sends should have been every two or three days so list engagement is still warm when this moment lands.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Squeeze one home before midnight — no code needed, in-store or online`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your $15 furry friend is waiting. Today only, while supplies last.`

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- **Invalid CSS property casing** — `p { Margin: 0px !important; Padding: 0px !important; }` uses capitalized property names (`Margin`, `Padding`). CSS properties are case-insensitive per spec, but some email clients (notably older Outlook WebApp and Samsung Mail) have been observed ignoring capitalized declarations. Correct to lowercase `margin` and `padding`.
- **Missing `lang` attribute** on `<html>` — No `lang="en"` declared. Required for screen readers (NVDA, JAWS, VoiceOver on iOS) to select the correct pronunciation engine.
- **Trailing space in viewport meta** — `content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1.0 "` has a trailing space before the closing quote. Benign in current clients but technically malformed.
- **Body-scoped `<style>` block** — The preheader rule (`.preheader { display: none !important; }`) is injected in a `<style>` tag directly inside `<body>`, not `<head>`. Gmail strips `<head>` styles and re-scopes body styles, so this is intentional and correct for Gmail — no issue, but worth documenting as deliberate.
- **Duplicate `.gmailfix` declaration** — `.gmailfix { display: none; display: none !important; }` declares the same property twice. The redundant non-`!important` line is inert but noisy.
- **Image alt text** — Truncated source prevents verification. Must confirm all `<img>` tags carry descriptive `alt` values; purely decorative images should have `alt=""`.
- **No `prefers-reduced-motion` query** — If the template includes animated GIFs or CSS animation (common in promotional emails), the absence of a `prefers-reduced-motion` rule is an accessibility gap.
