# Introducing NEW Slushie Plushies!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- Build-A-Bear is launching a new product line — Slushie Plushies — and this email does exactly what a product-launch email should: it leads hard with the hero SKU and dedicates multiple modules to explaining the customization system (scents, sounds, carrying straps). The branding is loud, cohesive, and on-theme. The problem is that this feels like four emails compressed into one. By the time you hit the graduation section ("Keep Palm and Celebrate — Class of 2026!") and the "Most Loved Stuff This Week" merchandising grid, the Slushie Plushie launch has already been diluted.
- There's no offer here — no discount, no bundle deal, no urgency mechanic. This is a pure awareness-and-browse send. That's a defensible choice for a new product reveal, but it means the email lives or dies on the novelty of "Slushie Plushies" carrying enough pull to earn a click without price incentive. For a brand that just sent a welcome email today, stacking a second marketing email same-day is also a cadence misstep.
- The render is clean and the visual hierarchy in the hero is solid. As an introduction to a new SKU, this works. As a conversion email, it leaves too much on the table.

## What's working

- The "Make Your Own Slushie Plushies" hero is immediately readable — large type, bright contrast, no ambiguity about what's new.
- The customization stack (scents → sounds → carrying strap) is smart product education; secondary CTAs ("SHOP SCENTS," "SHOP SOUNDS") are more specific than the generic hero CTA.
- The graduation module ("Class of 2026") is genuinely timely for mid-May and adds a secondary reason to browse beyond the new line.
- No render bugs — images load, text isn't clipped, layout holds on mobile width.
- "Collect them all!" framing for the product grid is well-matched to a collector audience; the cup character lineup in the product row reads clearly.

## What's weak

- No offer. A new-launch email with no discount, no free shipping, no BOGO gives the reader zero financial reason to act today rather than next week.
- Same-day double-send: BAB's welcome email arrived earlier today ("High Paw! We're So Happy You're Here"). Two emails in a single day from a brand new subscriber relationship is aggressive and erodes trust.
- Hero CTA "SHOP NOW" is generic — given the specific product being launched, "Build Your Slushie Plushie" or "Shop Slushie Plushies" would reinforce the message.
- The preheader "the stuff you love" is filler — it adds zero context to the subject line and wastes the most valuable free real estate in inbox view.
- Four distinct sections (launch, customization, graduation, "Most Loved") compete for attention and blur the send's core purpose.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a launch offer.** Even a modest "Free scent add-on with your first Slushie Plushie" or "Free shipping this weekend" gives subscribers a reason to act now and turns an awareness send into a conversion send.
- 2. **Shorten the email — cut the "Most Loved Stuff This Week" block.** This launch deserves to breathe. The merchandising grid is a distraction from a focused new-product story.
- 3. **Rewrite the hero CTA.** "Shop Slushie Plushies" is minimal lift; "Build Yours Now" would match the customization angle the rest of the email sells.
- 4. **Rewrite the preheader.** The current "the stuff you love" is brand noise; something like "Scented, sound-enabled, and squeeze-worthy — meet our newest friends" earns inbox space.
- 5. **Space the cadence.** Sending two emails on the subscriber's first day is a fast way to drive list churn. The welcome email should have been the only send; this launch could have gone out 24–48 hours later.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — attribute typo breaks IE compatibility directive:**
```html
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" Aontent="IE=edge" />
```
`Aontent` is not a valid attribute name (should be `content`). The IE=edge directive silently fails; affected Outlook/IE environments may fall back to compatibility-mode rendering.

**Malformed XML in MSO VML conditional comment — doubled closing tags:**
```xml
</o:PixelsPerInch></o:PixelsPerInch>
</o:OfficeDocumentSettings></o:OfficeDocumentSettings>
```
Each closing tag appears twice. This is invalid XML inside the `<!--[if gte mso 9]>` block; Outlook's XML parser may emit a warning or skip the entire block, affecting Outlook image rendering settings.

**`.shop-now-button` scoped only to `max-width: 480px` — no desktop styles:**
```css
@media screen and (max-width: 480px) {
  .shop-now-button { background-color: #0046c8; ... }
}
```
No matching desktop rule exists in the visible source. On desktop clients (Outlook, Apple Mail, webmail), the button renders unstyled.

**Invalid CSS property names in base styles:**
```css
p { Margin: 0px !important; Padding: 0px !important; }
```
Capitalized `Margin`/`Padding` are Outlook-proprietary shorthand — not valid CSS. These rules have no effect in non-Outlook clients; all other clients see the browser default `<p>` margins.

**`#table-container` border shorthand is incomplete:**
```css
border: 10px;
```
Missing `border-style` and `border-color`. All compliant CSS parsers will discard this declaration as invalid.

**Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>` element:**
```html
<html xmlns="..." xmlns:v="..." xmlns:o="...">
```
No `lang="en"` (or equivalent). Screen readers cannot determine content language — fails WCAG SC 3.1.1.

**Minor hygiene:**
- `.gmailfix` has `display: none;` declared twice (once without `!important`, once with) — redundant.
- `<title>Build-A-Bear </title>` — trailing whitespace.
- Viewport meta has a trailing space: `initial-scale=1.0 "`.
