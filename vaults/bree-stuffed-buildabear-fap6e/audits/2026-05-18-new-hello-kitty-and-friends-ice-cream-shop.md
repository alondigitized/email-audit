# NEW Hello Kitty® and Friends Ice Cream Shop! 🍨🍦
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- Build-A-Bear leads with a tight IP + concept pairing: Hello Kitty branded as an ice cream shop, with each character assigned a specific "flavor" outfit name. That's good product naming discipline — Ice Cream Sprinkles, Strawberry Ice Cream, Mint Chocolate Chip, Bubblegum Ice Cream — and it makes the collection feel curated rather than piecemeal. The hero render is clean, the pastel pink-and-white striped awning sets the scene immediately, and four individual product CTAs give fans multiple click points without friction.
- The email falls short in two ways. First, there's no incentive: no discount, no limited-availability signal, no member pricing — it's a pure awareness/launch send in a climate where inbox noise is high. Second, the "Most Loved Stuff This Week" module at the bottom drops in what appear to be dark-colored character plushies (Star Wars/gothic aesthetic) that visually clash hard with the pastel kawaii vibe above. It breaks the scene just before the footer and undercuts the thematic coherence.
- Compared to the May 16 Slushie Plushies launch two days ago, this is a stronger product concept and cleaner execution, but the cadence is already stacking up — two product launches in 48 hours, plus a welcome email on the same day as this send. For subscribers on the fence, that pace starts to read as noise.

## What's working

- **Ice cream flavor naming is inspired** — each SKU name doubles as an appetizing descriptor, making the collection memorable and scroll-stopping.
- **Hero composition earns its space** — striped awning + character + headline land in a single visual unit; no ambiguity about what this email is.
- **Four individual product CTAs in the grid** give highly interested fans a direct path without needing to hunt.
- **"Scoops of All Sizes" messaging** signals size inclusivity mid-scroll — a nice brand trust moment.
- **No render bugs** — layout, type, and images all appear clean at standard mobile width.

## What's weak

- **No price, no incentive, no urgency** — the email asks for a click with zero purchase motivation beyond novelty.
- **"Most Loved Stuff This Week" module** collides aesthetically — dark plushies against the pastel ice cream theme looks like a cross-sell module from a different campaign was pasted in.
- **Navigation text is likely leaking as the preheader** ("NEW ARRIVALS | GIFTS | EXTRAS") — wasted prime inbox real estate.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA copy is generic** across every button — a small copy lift to "Get Hello Kitty" or "Shop Ice Cream" on the hero would add specificity.
- **Back-to-back product launch emails** (Slushie Plushies May 16, this May 18) risk training subscribers to ignore launch sends.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Write a real preheader** — the biggest single lift. Something like "Four new ice cream-inspired Hello Kitty plushies — each named after a flavor" would carry the subject line forward instead of leaking nav junk.
- 2. **Add one hook** to the hero — even "Launching today" or "Limited debut collection" creates urgency without a discount. A low-stock or launch-window signal would work.
- 3. **Replace or relocate the "Most Loved" module** — either make it Hello Kitty-only ("Fans also love...") or push it below the footer CTAs so it doesn't break the pastel scene.
- 4. **Make hero CTA copy specific** — swap "SHOP NOW" for "Shop the Ice Cream Shop" on the primary hero button only.
- 5. **Throttle cadence** — if Slushie Plushies sent May 16, consider a 4–5 day gap before this drop; segment overlap will hurt open rates on both sends.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Hello Kitty® just opened an ice cream shop 🍦`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Ice Cream Sprinkles, Strawberry, Mint Choc Chip & Bubblegum — pick your flavor.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Four new Hello Kitty® & Friends plushies, each named after a scoop.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Bug — Malformed MSO conditional XML (will break Outlook rendering):**
```html
<o:PixelsPerInch>96</o:PixelsPerInch></o:PixelsPerInch>
</o:OfficeDocumentSettings></o:OfficeDocumentSettings>
```
Both tags have duplicate closing elements. This produces invalid XML inside the `<!--[if gte mso 9]>` conditional block, which Outlook's XML parser will reject or handle unpredictably.

**Bug — Typo in `X-UA-Compatible` meta tag:**
```html
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" Aontent="IE=edge" />
```
`Aontent` should be `content`. The attribute is unrecognized; IE/legacy Edge compatibility mode enforcement is silently dropped.

**Bug — `border` shorthand missing style value:**
```css
#table-container { border: 10px; ... }
```
`border: 10px` without a style keyword (e.g., `solid`) is invalid CSS. No border will render. If this is intentional padding/spacing, use `padding` instead.

**Warning — `overflow: hidden` on `#table-container`:**
```css
#table-container { border-radius: 20px 20px 20px 20px !important; overflow: hidden; }
```
`overflow: hidden` is ignored on `<table>` elements in virtually all email clients. Rounded corners will not clip child content in Outlook or most mobile clients.

**Minor — Non-standard CSS property casing in `p` rule:**
```css
p { Margin: 0px !important; Padding: 0px !important; }
```
Capital `M` and `P` are technically invalid identifiers in CSS. Most clients normalize this, but strict CSS parsers (some webmail clients) may skip these declarations.

**Minor — Redundant `.gmailfix` declaration:**
```css
.gmailfix { display: none; display: none !important; }
```
Duplicate `display: none` — second declaration is harmless but indicates copy-paste slop.
