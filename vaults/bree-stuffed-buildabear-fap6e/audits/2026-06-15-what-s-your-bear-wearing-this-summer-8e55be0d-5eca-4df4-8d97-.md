# What’s Your Bear Wearing This Summer?
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- This is Build-A-Bear's summer wardrobe email anchored by Pawlette — their fashion bear character — in a "Summer in Style" hero. The creative is cohesive: Pawlette styled in a beach look ("Paw the Beach") and a pastel ensemble ("Paw-geoise") gives this more editorial energy than the scattered catalog dump from June 12. The seasonal timing is credible — mid-June is the right window for a summer wardrobe push — and the email has a logical narrative arc: fashion hero → new quirky product (Crab 'n' Go Mashimal) → seasonal pet adoption → social proof grid.
- The single biggest miss is that there is no offer. Zero. No percentage off, no free shipping, no loyalty pricing. Build-A-Bear just sent six emails in seven days (Anglerfish on June 9, World Cup on June 10, International Day of Play on June 11, Summer Arrivals on June 12, Graduation on June 13, Sanrio on June 14). Asking subscribers to make a purchase decision based on aesthetics alone — on day seven of daily sends — is a hard ask that will depress conversions.
- Compared to the recent run, this is a cleaner, more character-focused send. But without a price hook, it functions as awareness, not acquisition.

## What's working

- **Pawlette hero is the strongest in the recent run** — dual outfit display (beach vs. pastel chic) gives the character a clear fashion identity; it lands the brief better than any hero since the June 10 World Cup bears.
- **"Summer in Style" headline is clean and seasonal** — short, accurate, not over-promised.
- **"Most Loved Stuff This Week" section is smart** — Sanrio Kuromi, Toothless, and Disney Eeyore are licensed IP magnets that drive cross-category clicks even in a fashion-framed email.
- **Consistent visual rhythm** — the SHOP NOW / ADOPT A PET / four nav buttons at the bottom create a clear action hierarchy without feeling cluttered.
- **No render bugs** — clean layout, no broken images, no overlapping copy, no lorem ipsum.

## What's weak

- **No offer anywhere in the email** — no discount, no free shipping, no member pricing; this is a pure browse email with no price-reduction signal anywhere.
- **"SHOP NOW" is repeated verbatim on every CTA** — four instances of the same generic button copy kills specificity and clickability.
- **Crab 'n' Go Cooler Mashimal section feels tonally dropped in** — a tiny red crab product section immediately below a fashion hero editorial is a jarring pivot; the copy says "quirky and unexpected" but gives almost zero product context.
- **No pricing or product details in the "Most Loved" grid** — four products, four SHOP NOW buttons, no prices or sizes; maximum pre-click friction.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — six emails in seven days and this one has no offer; it's the easiest send to skip in the recent batch.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one time-bounded offer** — even "Free standard shipping today" or "Club members: 15% off summer styles" transforms this from a browse to a buy. The Pawlette creative is strong enough to carry a real offer; don't waste it.
- 2. **Rewrite at least the hero CTA** — "Dress Your Bear" or "Shop Summer Looks" over "SHOP NOW." Verb + noun beats verb + adverb every time. The Mashimal CTA could be "Meet the Crab."
- 3. **Give the Crab its own dedicated send** — the Anglerfish got a standalone email on June 9 and earned a better score for it. Dropping an unexplained new creature into a fashion editorial undercuts both stories.
- 4. **Add prices to the "Most Loved" grid** — even showing "$XX.99" reduces the click-to-purchase cognitive gap.
- 5. **Break daily send cadence before next no-offer send** — if BAB won't slow frequency, at minimum ensure a browse email follows a discount email rather than stacking browse on browse.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

- **Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>` tag.** The opening tag is `<html xmlns="..." xmlns:v="..." xmlns:o="...">` with no `lang="en"`. Screen readers default to the OS language; this fails WCAG 3.1.1 (Level A).

- **Non-standard CSS property casing** in `<p>` rule:
  ```css
  p { Margin: 0px !important; Padding: 0px !important; }
  ```
  Capital `M` and `P` are not valid CSS identifiers. Most clients silently accept them, but it is non-conformant and may fail strict parsers (e.g., some versions of Yahoo Mail's CSS sanitizer).

- **Dark mode meta declared but no `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` rules present** in the visible `<style>` block:
  ```html
  <meta name="color-scheme" content="light dark">
  <meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light dark">
  ```
  Declaring dark mode support without override rules means dark-mode clients (Apple Mail, Outlook iOS) will auto-invert colors — including the `bgcolor="#ffffff"` body and any hardcoded `#252525` link color — potentially producing unintended contrast or invisible text.

- **Duplicate declaration in `.gmailfix`:**
  ```css
  .gmailfix { display: none; display: none !important; }
  ```
  Redundant; harmless but indicates a copy-paste artifact.

- **Trailing whitespace in `viewport` meta value:**
  ```html
  <meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1.0 " />
  ```
  The trailing space after `1.0` is technically invalid. Most parsers trim it, but it should be cleaned.
