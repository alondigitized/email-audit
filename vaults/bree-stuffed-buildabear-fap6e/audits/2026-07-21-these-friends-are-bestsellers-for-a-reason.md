# These Friends Are Bestsellers for a Reason
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- This is a bestsellers catalog send — a "here's what's selling" email with no discount hook, no price anchor, and no time pressure. The visual execution is competent: clean Build-A-Bear blue palette, tidy 2x2 product grid, no layout breaks. But the email is doing two contradictory jobs at once — selling "proven bestsellers" AND launching two new product lines (Slushie Plushies, Mystery Plush). Those are different emails. - The subject "These Friends Are Bestsellers for a Reason" sets up a hook it never pays off. There's no reason given in the body — no price, no review count, no social proof signal. The implied promise ("let me tell you why these are great") is dropped the moment you open it.

- Hero registers immediately: "SHOP BESTSELLERS" in bold on the blue ground, subhead, and CTA button all land above the fold without competing elements
- Named specific products (Sprigatito, Sea Angel, Longhorn, Eevee/Flareon bundles) give the grid real click targets — not just a category thumbnail
- Slushie Plushies sub-section earns its keep with a lifestyle photo and a legit seasonal hook ("cool off all summer")
- Mystery Plush collectible angle ("see if you can collect all five this summer") is a smart repeat-engagement mechanic

## What's weak

- "For a Reason" is a promise the email body never keeps — no price, no reviews, no proof point anywhere
- No offer of any kind: not a dollar-off, not a free shipping threshold, not a member-exclusive price — nothing to convert a browser into a buyer
- Two "NEW" sections contradict the bestsellers premise and split the email's one job into three
- Pokémon products (Sprigatito, Eevee) reappear after the 7/16 Pokémon catalog send with zero new angle — recycled creative without a new frame reads as lazy
- Member benefit ("BEAR YOUR MEMBER EXCLUSIVE!") is buried in sub-9px footer text — a real upsell moment thrown away

- 2. **Split this into two sends** — a true bestsellers catalog (proven IPs, clear pricing) and a separate new-launch email for Slushie Plushies + Mystery Plush; each does its job better alone
- 3. **Surface the member benefit in the body** — move "member exclusive" pricing or early access into a mid-email module, not the footer
- 4. **Retire the Pokémon grid tiles for one cycle** — Sprigatito and Eevee just ran 7/16; sub in Sea Angel and Longhorn as the hero pair to signal curation, not repetition

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Invalid CSS property casing — confirmed bug:**
```css
p { Margin: 0px !important; Padding: 0px !important; }
```
`Margin` and `Padding` are capitalized, making them invalid CSS. Most parsers are lenient, but strict renderers (some Outlook versions, Apple Mail) will ignore these rules, potentially breaking paragraph spacing.

**Duplicate declaration in `.gmailfix`:**
```css
.gmailfix { display: none; display: none !important; }
```
Redundant first declaration. Harmless but indicative of copy-paste debt.

**Trailing whitespace in viewport meta:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1.0 " />
```
The trailing space in the `content` value is technically a malformed attribute. Most clients parse it correctly, but it should be cleaned.

**Dark mode declared but not implemented:**
`<meta name="color-scheme" content="light dark">` and `<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light dark">` are present, signaling dark mode support to Apple Mail and other clients. However, no `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block is present in the visible CSS. This causes Apple Mail's auto-inversion to apply to the entire email unpredictably instead of using controlled overrides.

**Second `<style>` block is inside `<body>`:**
```html
</head><body ...><style type="text/css">div.preheader { display: none !important; }</style>
```
Invalid per HTML spec; `<style>` belongs in `<head>`. Most email clients handle it, but Gmail and some webmail clients strip `<style>` tags in `<body>`.
