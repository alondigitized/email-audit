# Not a Hoax: Our Fresno Nightcrawler Is Here!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-18**
## Executive summary

- Build-A-Bear leads with a genuinely odd, memorable hero — the Fresno Nightcrawler, a cryptid-themed plush inspired by the "walking pairs of pants" internet meme — and "TAKE A STROLL" is a decent pun to go with it. It's a fun, novelty-driven creative choice that stands out from the Halloween/Pokémon/NFL rotation this brand has been running (2026-08-16 Pokémon, 2026-08-14 Sweethearts, 2026-08-11 NFL). But the subject line undersells it with a confusing "Not a Hoax" framing that doesn't clarify what's actually being sold, and the top banner buries the hero offer under an unrelated 20% off Lovable Legends promo that competes for attention before you even see the Nightcrawler.
- Below the fold it turns into the now-familiar Build-A-Bear kitchen-sink layout: a Groovy Goblin block, four "Shop By Product" tiles, two "More to Explore" tiles, then a nav footer. The hero cryptid concept doesn't get reinforced anywhere else in the email — it's one image and then five other unrelated offers competing for the same click.
- Overall this is a decent novelty hook wrapped in the brand's usual overstuffed body. Middle-of-the-pack execution, held down mostly by subject-line confusion and message dilution below the fold.

## What's working

- The Fresno Nightcrawler hero image is genuinely distinctive — a glowing purple-lit forest shot with a novel, on-trend cryptid product, unlike the brand's typical straightforward product-on-white shots.
- Clear single CTA ("SHOP NOW") directly under the hero product with no scroll needed.
- Copy explains the joke/reference ("walking pairs of pants," "cuddliest cryptid yet") so a reader unfamiliar with the meme isn't lost.
- Clean "Shop By Product" grid with distinct SKUs, each with its own CTA button, keeps that section scannable.

## What's weak

- The top banner promotes 20% off Lovable Legends — a completely different product line — before the reader even sees the Nightcrawler, undercutting the hero message from the first pixel.
- Subject line "Not a Hoax: Our Fresno Nightcrawler Is Here!" is vague — it doesn't say this is a plush toy, so it reads more like a conspiracy-theory tease than a product drop.
- After the hero, the email pivots through four more unrelated offers (Lovable Legends, Groovy Goblin, four random SKU tiles, two lifestyle tiles) — no thematic thread ties the cryptid concept through the rest of the send.
- No urgency or scarcity signal on the Nightcrawler itself (no "limited edition," no stock warning), despite "Ends Soon!" being used two blocks down for a different offer — the actual hero product feels like it could disappear without the reader ever knowing to act fast.

## Recommendations

- 1. Rework the subject to name the product type so it's clear this is a new plush drop, not a vague teaser — this is the single biggest lift since a stronger subject drives the open in the first place.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Meet the Fresno Nightcrawler — Our Weirdest Plush Yet`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The Internet's Favorite Cryptid Is Now a Plush`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `A "walking pair of pants" you can actually hug.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Inspired by the viral cryptid sighting — now cuddly.`
- 2. Move the Lovable Legends banner below the Nightcrawler hero (or drop it from this send entirely) so the cryptid story isn't interrupted before the reader even scrolls.
- 3. Add a scarcity or "limited drop" cue directly under the Nightcrawler CTA — cryptid/novelty items convert better with a "while supplies last" hook than generic product drops.
- 4. Trim the body — cut either the "Shop By Product" grid or "More to Explore" tiles; five distinct offers in one email dilutes the Nightcrawler's novelty and splits click intent across too many unrelated SKUs.
