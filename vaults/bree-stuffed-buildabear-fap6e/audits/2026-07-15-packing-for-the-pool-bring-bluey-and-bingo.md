# Packing for the Pool? Bring Bluey and Bingo!
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- This is a licensed-IP catalog send riding the Bluey property into peak summer. The hero is clean and well-executed: Bluey and Bingo stuffed animals in pool/summer styling with an unmistakably kid-focused "MAKE A SPLASH" headline. The creative logic is sound — Bluey is one of the most culturally pervasive children's IPs right now, and tying it to pool-packing season is a natural hook.
- Below the fold the email sprawls into too many threads: Bag Charms (which ran two days ago on July 13), a Winnie the Pooh block, and a "Most Loved Stuff This Week" grid of four unrelated products. The send ends up feeling like a weekly digest with a Bluey header grafted on — not a focused Bluey summer campaign.
- The verdict: the hero earns its keep, but the email loses confidence the moment Bluey is out of frame. It's a solid mid-tier send that will drive Bluey-parent clicks and not much else.

## What's working

- **Hero is immediately legible.** Blue background, Bluey and Bingo front-and-center, "MAKE A SPLASH" in bold white type — the eye lands on the offer in under a second.
- **Seasonal hook is timely.** "Packing for the pool?" is a genuine summer-moment tie-in, not evergreen filler.
- **Bluey IP is the right vehicle right now.** The property has mainstream household penetration; parents of 2-6 year-olds will recognize it instantly.
- **Single primary CTA above the fold.** One blue "SHOP NOW" button before any scrolling is required.
- **No visible render bugs.** Images load cleanly, text doesn't overlap, layout holds.

## What's weak

- **Bag Charms module is stale.** Build-A-Bear pushed "Ends Soon! Bag Charms 2 for $30" on July 13 — showing the same product two days later with no updated urgency language or new offer reads as unsold inventory filler.
- **"Most Loved Stuff This Week" grid is scatter-shot.** A highland cow, a Winnie the Pooh adjacent bear, a sea angel plush, and a Timeless Teddy have nothing to do with Bluey or summer. The section dilutes the send's focus.
- **No price shown for the Bluey hero.** The headline creative gives no anchor — a parent can't make a purchase decision without knowing if this is a $20 or $50 ask.
- **Winnie the Pooh block copy is tiny and hard to read.** "These gifts are sweet as hunny" is charming but the text renders very small against a pale background — low contrast, easy to skip.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show the Bluey price in the hero.** Add "Starting at $X" under the sub-headline — parents comparison-shop and this one friction point is the clearest lift available.
- 2. **Cut or refresh the Bag Charms module.** It ran two days ago. If the deal is still live, say so with new urgency ("Last chance — tonight only"). If it's over, replace it entirely with a second Bluey or summer product block.
- 3. **Replace the "Most Loved" grid with a "Summer Ready" curated picks grid** — keep Bluey/Bingo, add any beach/pool-themed plush, and present two or three items with prices. Makes the whole email feel cohesive.
- 4. **Add a loyalty/Bonus Club hook.** The current send has no mention of member pricing or Bonus Club — a simple "Club members earn points on every purchase" line near the CTA is a no-cost retention signal.
