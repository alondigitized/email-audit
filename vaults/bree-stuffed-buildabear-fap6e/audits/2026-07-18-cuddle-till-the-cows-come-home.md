# Cuddle Till the Cows Come Home
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- This is a thematic catalog send built entirely around Build-A-Bear's cow lineup. The hero "COMPLETE YOUR COW-LLECTION" commits hard to the pun and sets an unambiguous theme — every product in this email moos. Creative execution is clean: bright teal background, bold orange headline block, individual product tiles each with a standalone "SHOP NOW" button. But there is no discount, no urgency mechanism, no price point anywhere visible.
- The structural problem is hard to miss if you've been watching this brand's send history. This is the second "Complete Your [X] Collection"-framed catalog send in three days — Pokémon tiles on July 16 scored 5/10, same template. The cow lineup is real and the creative holds up, but a subscriber who got the Pokémon email will recognize this format on arrival. Seven consecutive days of promotional sends make the cadence itself a liability; the subject-line pun gives no reason to break pattern and open.
- Verdict: competently produced catalog content with a niche audience ceiling, zero offer-hook, and a cadence problem the creative can't solve.

## What's working

- "COW-LLECTION" hero pun is immediately legible — one glance and you know exactly what this email is
- Individual product tiles (Blueberry Highland Cow, Blue Raspberry Cow, Giant Longhorn, etc.) each carry their own "SHOP NOW" button, giving multiple independent click entries
- Teal/orange palette is vibrant and distinctly on-brand; no visual noise
- "Most Loved Stuff This Week" section at the bottom adds lightweight social proof without breaking the catalog flow
- No render bugs — product images are sharp and uniformly sized, text is clean

## What's weak

- No offer anywhere: no price point, no discount, no "new this week" signal — this is browse content with nothing to buy *today*
- Subject leans entirely on a pun and names no product or offer; cold-inbox read is "cute but skip"
- After seven straight promotional days, another catalog send in this identical tile-grid format reads like brand wallpaper
- Hero copy targets "cow lovers" specifically — self-selecting a small slice of the list before the email has done any conversion work
- No urgency mechanism anywhere in the visible layout; nothing creates a reason to act now versus next week

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor the hero to a price.** Pick the Blueberry Highland Cow or Giant Longhorn, put the price in the hero block. One concrete number gives a shopper a reason to click versus save-for-later.
- 2. **Add a scarcity or freshness signal.** "Newly arrived" or "limited summer stock" elevates urgency without fabricating a discount.
- 3. **Rewrite the subject with a product name or price cue.** The pun is forgettable against a full promotional inbox.
- 4. **Break the cadence.** This send and the July 16 Pokémon send are structurally identical. One of them should have been held, merged, or replaced with a different format.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — Malformed `X-UA-Compatible` meta attribute (confirmed)**
```html
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" Aontent="IE=edge" />
```
`Aontent` is a typo for `content`. This meta tag is completely ignored by all parsers. The IE/Outlook rendering-mode hint is silently dropped, removing the intended Outlook edge-mode override.

**Issue 2 — Duplicate VML closing tags in Outlook conditional comment (confirmed)**
```xml
<o:PixelsPerInch>96</o:PixelsPerInch></o:PixelsPerInch>
<o:OfficeDocumentSettings></o:OfficeDocumentSettings>
```
Both `</o:PixelsPerInch>` and `</o:OfficeDocumentSettings>` appear twice. This is malformed XML; strict XML parsers inside Outlook may reject or misparse the entire VML block, potentially breaking image rendering in Outlook desktop.

**Issue 3 — Invalid `border` shorthand on `#table-container` (confirmed)**
```css
#table-container { border: 10px; border-radius: 20px 20px 20px 20px !important; overflow: hidden; }
```
`border: 10px` is missing a required style keyword (`solid`, `dashed`, etc.). This declaration is invalid and will be discarded — no border will render. Additionally, `border-radius` + `overflow: hidden` on a container table is unsupported in Outlook (all versions); the rounded corner effect will not appear there.

**Issue 4 — Non-standard CSS property capitalization (confirmed)**
```css
p { Margin: 0px !important; Padding: 0px !important; }
```
CSS property names must be lowercase per spec. `Margin` and `Padding` (capital M/P) are technically invalid; Gmail's CSS inliner and some webmail CSS strippers may discard these rules, allowing paragraph margins to bleed through.

**Issue 5 — Trailing space in viewport `content` value (confirmed)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1.0 " />
```
Space before the closing quote (`1.0 "`). Technically malformed; most parsers are tolerant but it's a linting failure.

**Issue 6 — Missing tablet breakpoint (confirmed)**
Media queries cover `max-width: 480px` and `481px–639px` only. No responsive rules for 640px–767px (common tablet/large-phone range), leaving those widths at full desktop layout on narrower tablet screens.
