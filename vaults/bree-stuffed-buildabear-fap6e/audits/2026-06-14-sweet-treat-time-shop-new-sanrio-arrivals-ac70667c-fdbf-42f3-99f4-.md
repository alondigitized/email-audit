# Sweet Treat Time! Shop NEW Sanrio® Arrivals 🍨
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-14**
## Executive summary

- This is Build-A-Bear's new Sanrio character launch — "Ice Cream Sprinkles Hello Kitty" — dressed up with a summer ice cream shop theme. The hero is the best thing in the email: a cotton-candy awning, Hello Kitty in a pink confection outfit, and the "SPRINKLE ON THE FUN" headline all pull together cleanly. The summer-sweet framing is genuinely fun and the June timing is on point for a limited-edition character.
- The problem is everything that surrounds it. The International Day of Play donation banner from the June 11 send is still sitting at the very top of the email — "Last Chance: Donate to The Toy Foundation for International Day of Play! GIVE TODAY!" — and in most inboxes that text bleeds into the preheader, completely hijacking the Sanrio story before the subscriber even opens. Mid-email, the cause-marketing section reappears in full, eating real estate that should belong to the Sanrio lineup. Then a "Most Loved Stuff This Week" grid piles in Winnie the Pooh bundles and a Timeless Teddy Bear, diluting the ice cream theme entirely.
- Compare this to the Lightning Bug send (June 8, 8/10): that email committed to one character, one narrative, top to bottom. This one has the same bones but scatters its energy across three unrelated stories. The Sanrio launch deserved its own clean send.

## What's working

- **Hero creative lands.** The "SPRINKLE ON THE FUN" headline, pink awning, and Hello Kitty in her sprinkle outfit are visually cohesive and immediately convey the product idea.
- **IP and timing are well-matched.** Sanrio has broad adult-nostalgia pull; an ice cream character in June is the right product at the right moment.
- **"SCOOPS OF ALL SIZES!"** secondary section does useful work — it expands the offer to Mini Beanz and full-size friends without a jarring tonal shift.
- **Subject line is solid.** "Sweet Treat Time! Shop NEW Sanrio® Arrivals 🍨" is specific, mobile-length, and the emoji earns its place.
- **No render bugs** in the visible render — layout holds, images load, CTAs are clear.

## What's weak

- **Preheader is a disaster.** The leftover International Day of Play donation banner ("Last Chance: Donate to The Toy Foundation...") sits at the top of the email and almost certainly becomes the inbox preview text, replacing any Sanrio hook entirely.
- **International Day of Play section has no business here.** June 14 is the last day of that campaign — including it mid-email forces a hard tonal pivot from dessert fun to charity donation and back.
- **"Most Loved Stuff This Week" kills the narrative.** Winnie the Pooh and a Timeless Teddy Bear alongside the Sanrio grid look like leftover inventory, not a curated recommendation.
- **No price, no urgency, no discount.** "SHOP NOW" appears four times with no concrete offer — no "limited edition," no "only while supplies last," no member pricing — leaving the subscriber with no reason to click today vs. next week.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the International Day of Play section entirely.** It's June 14, it's over, it's eating prime real estate in a Sanrio launch email. If Build-A-Bear needs to close the campaign, send a standalone final-day charity email, not a Sanrio chaser.
- 2. **Set a real preheader.** The first line of copy is currently a charity donation ask. Add an explicit preheader element — something like "Hello Kitty just got a summer glow-up 🍦" — so the inbox preview matches the subject.
- 3. **Add a scarcity or urgency signal.** "Limited Edition" or "While Supplies Last" on the hero would give the SHOP NOW button a reason to click now. The Lightning Bug send made the character feel like a moment; this one feels like a catalog page.
- 4. **Cut or relocate "Most Loved This Week."** Trim it to Sanrio-only products (Chococat is already in the grid — lean into that), or move it below the fold and keep the top two-thirds on a single theme.
- 5. **Make the secondary CTA copy specific.** "SCOOPS OF ALL SIZES! SHOP NOW" could be "SHOP MINI BEANZ" and "SHOP FULL-SIZE" to help subscribers self-select and reduce decision friction.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Invalid CSS property name — `Margin` (capitalized):**
```css
p { Margin: 0px !important; Padding: 0px !important; }
```
`Margin` and `Padding` are not valid CSS property names. CSS identifiers are case-sensitive per spec. Most browser-based clients silently accept this, but strict parsers (some mobile clients) will ignore the rule entirely. Fix: lowercase both.

**Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>`:**
```html
<html xmlns="..." xmlns:v="..." xmlns:o="...">
```
No `lang="en"` present. Screen readers default to the OS language rather than document language — a WCAG 2.1 Level A failure (Success Criterion 3.1.1).

**Trailing space in `viewport` meta:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1.0 " />
```
Trailing space in the `content` attribute is technically malformed. Benign in practice but should be cleaned.

**Duplicate `display: none` declaration:**
```css
.gmailfix { display: none; display: none !important; }
```
The first declaration is overridden by the second. Harmless but indicates copy-paste error.

**`&shy;` soft hyphens as preview-text padding:**
The hidden padding block after the preheader uses `&shy;` (soft hyphen, U+00AD) mixed with `&zwnj;`. In certain email clients (notably older Android Gmail) soft hyphens render as visible `-` characters, leaking filler glyphs into the email body.

**Preheader hiding relies solely on color proximity:**
```html
<span class="preheader" style="color:#f3f3f3;display:none!important;...">
```
`#f3f3f3` on a `bgcolor="#ffffff"` body is near-invisible but not identical. The `display:none!important` is the primary hide mechanism — color is only a fallback. Low risk but `color:#ffffff` would be more defensive.
