# Chase Fun Summer Adventures with Promise Pets!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-12**
## Executive summary

- This is a catalog awareness send for Build-A-Bear's Promise Pets line — dogs and cats styled as "adoptable" summer companions. The creative pulls together cleanly: beach-toned hero, individual product cards for the Ocelot, Pug, Bernese Mountain Dog, and Calico, a mid-email Mint Chocolate Chip Froggy cross-sell, and a "Most Loved Staff This Week" social-proof row at the bottom.
- The problem is the same one that sank the July 10 Hello Kitty send (5/10) and the July 11 MLB All-Star email (4/10): there is no deal. After the brand's own high-watermark sends — the June 24 Cub Condo double-tap at 9/10 and 8/10, both carrying real 50% off event energy — this is a catalog browse dressed up as a campaign. "THE PAW-FECT FRIENDS FOR SUMMER" is a fun headline, but it gives the subscriber zero transactional reason to act today.
- Three consecutive IP-swap catalog sends (Sanrio → MLB → Promise Pets) with no offer in three days risks training the list to skim-and-skip. Build-A-Bear knows how to run urgency; this send doesn't use it.

## What's working

- Summer theming is coherent — beach imagery, warm palette, and the "PAW-FECT FRIENDS FOR SUMMER" hero headline all pull in the same direction
- Individual product cards are actually shoppable: breed name, a short descriptor, and a clear "ADOPT NOW" button on each animal
- "ADOPT NOW" buttons are visually distinct — blue on white, no hunting required
- The "Most Loved Staff This Week" row at the bottom adds a lightweight editorial signal that these products have traction
- Clean render at mobile width; no broken images or layout collapse visible

## What's weak

- No price or offer anywhere in the email — pure browse with no trigger to purchase now
- The Mint Chocolate Chip Froggy mid-email cross-sell is tonally disconnected from the dog/cat Promise Pets narrative and splits focus right at the scroll midpoint
- Third consecutive catalog send in three days — cadence fatigue is real; after the June 24 urgency playbook, this registers as wallpaper
- "Chase Fun Summer Adventures" in the subject is lifestyle mood copy, not a hook; it tells the subscriber nothing about which pet or why today
- No Bonus Club or loyalty pricing surfaced despite Build-A-Bear having a rewards program

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add an offer** — even a lightweight "adopt any Promise Pet, get a free outfit accessory" or member-only price point gives this email a reason to exist beyond browsing. The June 24 Cub Condo playbook converts; apply a version of it here.
- 2. **Pick a hero and commit** — the Pug or Bernese Mountain Dog is the natural above-fold star; give it the full hero frame with a tight emotional hook before cascading the other pets below. The current group shot is pleasant but uncommanding.
- 3. **Cut or reschedule the Froggy cross-sell** — it deserves its own send with its own story, not a mid-email interrupt inside a dog/cat email. Test whether removing it lifts Promise Pets click rate.
- 4. Rewrite the subject and preheader:

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed issues:**

**a) Broken IE compatibility meta tag — attribute typo**
```html
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" Aontent="IE=edge" />
```
`Aontent` is not a valid attribute; the correct attribute is `content`. This meta directive is silently ignored, meaning Outlook on Windows may not trigger edge rendering mode.

**b) Malformed MSO XML — duplicate closing tags**
```xml
<o:PixelsPerInch>96</o:PixelsPerInch></o:PixelsPerInch>
</o:OfficeDocumentSettings></o:OfficeDocumentSettings>
```
Both closing tags appear twice. This produces invalid XML inside the MSO conditional comment, which Outlook's XML parser may reject entirely, causing the `<o:AllowPNG/>` and pixel-density settings to be ignored.

**c) Invalid CSS property casing in `p {}` rule**
```css
p { Margin: 0px !important; Padding: 0px !important; }
```
CSS property names are case-sensitive in strict parsers. `Margin` and `Padding` (capital M/P) are unrecognized; the declarations will be dropped. Paragraph spacing resets will fail in clients that enforce this (WebKit-based renderers).

**d) Invalid `border` shorthand on `#table-container`**
```css
#table-container { border: 10px; ... }
```
`border: 10px` omits the required border-style keyword (e.g., `solid`). This shorthand is invalid and ignored by all compliant CSS engines — no border will render.

**e) Redundant duplicate rule in `.gmailfix`**
```css
.gmailfix { display: none; display: none !important; }
```
Duplicate declarations are harmless but indicate copy-paste artifact; second declaration shadows the first.

**f) `alt` attributes** — not verifiable from truncated source; cannot confirm images have descriptive `alt` text.
