# Smile Big With Beary Goods!

## Executive summary

- This is a straightforward new-collection intro for Build-A-Bear's "Beary Goods" summer line — sandcastle bear, sun plush, wave-shaped character — plus two secondary product intros (Octopail Mashimal, Summer Mini Beans) and a "Most Loved Stuff This Week" grid. The execution is clean and on-brand: bright beach palette, tidy blue frame, consistent logo placement, no render bugs.
- But like several of the brand's recent sends (Beardusa, Lightning Bug, Minion James, Bestsellers), this is a no-discount, no-urgency product-awareness email. There's not a single price, percentage, or loyalty perk anywhere in the render — just five stacked "Shop Now" buttons pointing at different collections. It's pleasant wallpaper, not a purchase trigger.
- Judgment: a solid execution of a weak strategy. This brand keeps sending well-designed catalog/launch emails with zero commercial hook, and the cadence is starting to blur together — this is at least the fourth such send in the last six days of brand history.

## What's working

- Hero concept (sandcastle bear, sunglasses-sun, wave character) is cohesive and clearly reads as a summer/beach theme — timely for late July.
- Clean visual hierarchy: eye lands on "Beary Goods" hero first, then flows down through two secondary launches into the bestseller grid.
- No render defects — no overlapping text, no broken images, no lorem ipsum.
- Brand identity is unmistakable (logo, blue/pink palette, "the stuff you love" tagline) — instantly recognizable in an inbox.
- Bottom utility nav (Build Your Own Bear, Personalize, Giftshop, Find a Workshop) gives multiple paths for different shopper intents.

## What's weak

- Zero concrete offer anywhere in the email — no % off, no price anchor, no BOGO, no loyalty mention. Every CTA is just "SHOP NOW" with nothing to shop for at a discount.
- Subject line ("Smile Big With Beary Goods!") doesn't explain what "Beary Goods" actually is — a first-time reader gets no information from subject + hero alone about the product category.
- "Most Loved Stuff This Week" grid shows four plush items with no prices, no ratings, no review counts — pure image gallery, no social proof.
- Three separate "NEW" product launches (Beary Goods, Octopail Mashimal, Mini Beans) compete for attention in one email with no clear single priority — it reads as three mini-emails glued together.
- Cadence fatigue: per brand history, this is another discount-free "new plush" intro following Beardusa, Lightning Bug, Minion James, and the Bestsellers send — the format is repeating without escalation.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a concrete offer to the hero — even a modest one ("Free shipping on Beary Goods orders $35+" or a bundle price) would convert this from a lookbook into a purchase driver. This is the single biggest lever given the brand's flash-sale sends (Hello Kitty) scored notably higher.
- 2. Cut to one hero launch instead of three. Move Octopail Mashimal and Mini Beans to a follow-up send or a "shop more new arrivals" strip — three competing product stories dilutes all of them.
- 3. Add prices and a star rating to the "Most Loved Stuff This Week" grid — social proof and price anchoring are missing entirely from that section.
- 4. Make "SHOP NOW" specific per section (e.g., "Shop Beary Goods," "Shop Mini Beans") so each CTA carries its own context if a reader skims past the headline.
