# Homepage snapshot · www.buildabear.com · 2026-07-04
**Score:** 2/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-04**
## Executive summary

- The first thing I see when I open the site is not Build-A-Bear — it's a wall-to-wall Cookiebot consent popup. The actual homepage is completely obscured. About the only brand signals I can grab are a sliver of a blue promo bar at the top ("ng on Orders of $75 or More! / Limited Tim[e]") and a sticky "Active Offers (5)" bar at the bottom. As a returning shopper who knows exactly what she's looking for, this hard stop before I see a single bear is genuinely annoying.

## What's working

- **"Active Offers (5) View All" sticky bar** — at least I know there are deals waiting for me the moment I dismiss the modal. That's a smart persistent element.
- **Free shipping on $75+ promo bar** — peeking through at the very top, this is directly relevant to me since I often load up on multiple bears or accessories in one order.
- **"Limited Time" copy** — even partially visible, this nudges urgency. I'm a seasonal collector, so I'm immediately paying attention.

## What's weak

- **Cookiebot modal completely blocks the homepage** — this is the single most damaging UX event on the page. I can't see the hero, the seasonal drops, the Pokémon collab status, nothing. First impression is a legal disclaimer, not a bear.
- **Modal is verbose and cold** — the consent copy ("Build-A-Bear Workshop UK Limited... third party service providers acting on our behalf...") reads like a legal brief. Not the vibe for a brand built on joy.
- **No quick-dismiss interaction** — there's no "X" or "Continue without accepting" visible; only "Accept" or "Manage My Preferences." Either path feels transactional, not warm.
- **Promo bar text is clipped** — "ng on Orders of $75 or More!" is cut off at the left edge. On mobile, that's sloppy.

## Recommendations

- **Slim down the consent interruption** — replace the full-page takeover with a bottom-sheet banner (2-line summary + Accept button). The Pokémon collab hero or seasonal banner should be visible the moment the page loads, not buried under legalese.
- **Auto-accept analytics-only on page load and ask for full consent on the second interaction** — or use a persistent thin bar rather than blocking the entire viewport on first visit.
- **Fix the truncated promo bar text on mobile** — if the offer is "Free Shipping on Orders of $75 or More!" make sure the full string wraps or scrolls; a clipped message loses the deal's value.
- **Surface the "Active Offers (5)" count as a highlight in the cookie-cleared state** — once the modal clears, animate or flash that badge so it's the first thing my eye catches, bridging the gap from consent to commerce.
