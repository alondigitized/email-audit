# NEW Summer Mini Beans®!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-29**
## Executive summary

- This is Build-A-Bear's summer launch for Mini Beans®, a line of small collectible plush figures. The "collect all" hook in the hero copy is genuinely smart — it reframes what is essentially a product catalog into a seasonal collection mechanic, which gives the whole email a purpose beyond "new stuff exists." The summer theme is visually coherent: tropical pink-and-teal palette, palm tree accents, character names like Coconut Lime Koala and Pineapple Pip that telegraph the season instantly.
- The problem is the send context. Yesterday's Sylveon email (9/10) was character IP with cultural pull. The Sanrio send before that was licensed-IP gold. By comparison, Mini Beans is an internal Build-A-Bear product line with no external IP anchor — the creative has to work harder to earn the open, and it doesn't fully succeed. The subject ("NEW Summer Mini Beans®!") is generic enough that anyone who skipped the email wouldn't feel they missed something important, which is the worst outcome for a product launch.
- There is also no offer. No price. No discount. No "buy 2 get 1 free" or even free shipping. For a six-send-in-six-days inbox, a pure discovery email with no hook beyond "look at the new things" is hard to click into.

## What's working

- **"Collect all" mechanic** in the hero subtext is the best copy decision in the email — turns a product grid into a collector challenge with implicit scarcity pressure.
- **"Limited Edition" badges** on Chummy Shark and Ice Cone Tower stand out visually and add selective urgency without spamming every product with the label.
- **Five individually-named characters** with distinct CTAs give the email clear product hierarchy — each Mini Bean is a separate clickable entity, not a "shop the collection" blob.
- **"Most Loved Stuff This Week" module** at the bottom is credible social proof that adds a secondary reason to scroll past the hero grid.
- **Visual consistency** — blue-and-pink Build-A-Bear palette holds across hero, product tiles, and footer CTAs. No layout breakage visible.

## What's weak

- **No price or offer anywhere visible** — a new-product launch with zero price anchoring leaves the subscriber with no reason to act today versus tomorrow versus never.
- **"SHOP NOW" on every single CTA** — five characters, all with identical button copy. Wasted opportunity to differentiate ("Collect Chummy Shark," "Get the Limited Edition") and reinforce the collect-all mechanic.
- **Hero copy buries the collectible angle** — "Summer Fun in All Sizes" leads, but the actual hook ("see if you can collect all") is the second sentence. Swap them and the email has a spine.
- **Daily send cadence creates fatigue** — this is the sixth email in six days from Build-A-Bear. Even a good email gets lower open rates on a saturated list. The Cubbies section and "Most Loved" module feel like padding to justify the send rather than genuinely relevant adds.
- **No deadline or scarcity date** — "Limited Edition" without a "while supplies last" or end-date feels like softcopy urgency that subscribers have been trained to ignore.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the collect-all hook in the subject line** — this is the unique narrative and it's buried. The current subject could be any toy brand's new-arrivals email. Make it mean something.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Can you collect all 5 Summer Mini Beans®?`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New Mini Beans® drop — Limited Edition first`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Chummy Shark, Pineapple Pip & 3 more — only this summer.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `5 new sizes of BIG fun. See if you can get them all.`
- 2. **Add a price or an offer** — even "Starting at $X" in the hero is enough to answer "why act now." Without it, this is a lookbook, not a conversion email.
- 3. **Differentiate the CTAs by character name** — "Collect Chummy Shark" and "Grab the Limited Edition" are five words that cost nothing and reinforce the collectible framing while also surfacing scarcity on the two Limited Edition SKUs.
- 4. **Move "see if you can collect all" to the headline, demote "Summer Fun in All Sizes" to subhead** — right now the generic seasonal line leads and the compelling mechanic follows. Invert it.
- 5. **Drop or condense the Cubbies / Most Loved sections** — they dilute the Mini Beans launch. If this is a product launch, let it be a product launch. Move the social proof module up and cut the Cubbies detour entirely.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] Malformed MSO conditional XML — duplicate closing tags**
```xml
<o:PixelsPerInch>96</o:PixelsPerInch></o:PixelsPerInch>
</o:OfficeDocumentSettings></o:OfficeDocumentSettings>
```
Both `</o:PixelsPerInch>` and `</o:OfficeDocumentSettings>` appear twice. This is invalid XML inside the MSO conditional block. Outlook parses this as raw XML; a malformed document settings block can suppress DPI and PNG rendering fixes in Outlook 2016+.

**[HIGH] `Aontent` typo on IE compatibility meta tag**
```html
<meta http-equiv="X-UA-Compatible" Aontent="IE=edge" />
```
The attribute is `Aontent` instead of `content`. The directive is silently ignored — IE/legacy Edge will not be forced to edge rendering mode. This is a copy-paste error.

**[MEDIUM] Invalid `border` shorthand**
```css
#table-container { border: 10px; border-radius: 20px 20px 20px 20px !important; }
```
`border: 10px` with no `style` keyword (`solid`, `none`, etc.) is invalid per CSS spec and will be dropped by all renderers. If the intent was a border, this renders nothing. If the intent was padding/spacing, `padding` should have been used instead.

**[LOW] Trailing space in viewport `content` value**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1.0 " />
```
The trailing space after `1.0` is technically harmless but is a signal of template hygiene issues.

**[LOW] Redundant duplicate CSS declaration**
```css
.gmailfix { display: none; display: none !important; }
```
The first `display: none` is overridden by the second. No functional impact, but indicates copy-paste debt.

**[LOW] `<title>` has trailing space**
```html
<title>Build-A-Bear </title>
```
Minor, but some spam filters flag low-signal title content.
