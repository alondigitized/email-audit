# Packing for the Pool? Bring Bluey and Bingo!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-31**
## Executive summary

- This is a seasonal character push built around Bluey and Bingo — two of the most commercially potent licensed characters in children's entertainment right now. The "MAKE A SPLASH" hero lands cleanly: the pool context is timely (end of May), the IP is right, and the visual of Bluey and Bingo in summer gear is charming enough to earn the open. Build-A-Bear knows how to dress up a licensed property, and it shows here.
- The email falls apart below the fold. After a reasonably tight thematic top half — Bluey hero, summer bag charms, sun bear — the bottom section drops four unrelated products under "Most Loved Stuff This Week," including Kuromi and Chococat, which got their own dedicated send just five days ago (May 26, Sanrio Ice Cream Shop). That's recycled catalog dressed up as curation, and it undercuts the Bluey focus that made the subject worth opening.
- The bigger structural miss: there's no offer. No discount, no promo code, no free shipping. The May 25 send at least had "SUMMER" for free shipping. This one is pure browse bait — high on IP, zero on incentive. Without a price hook or deadline, most recipients will admire the plush and keep scrolling.

## What's working

- **Subject line is the strongest in recent memory** — "Packing for the Pool? Bring Bluey and Bingo!" is specific, seasonal, and conversational. Lands well under 50 chars.
- **Hero executes the seasonal brief** — Bluey and Bingo in summer styling with "MAKE A SPLASH" is thematically tight and visually immediate.
- **Summer bag charms and sun bear continue the theme** — the secondary modules in the top half maintain the pool/summer throughline rather than jumping to unrelated product.
- **Clean render, no visual bugs** — layout holds, images load, text isn't overlapping.
- **No spam signals** — tone is playful without exclamation stacking or ALL CAPS pressure tactics.

## What's weak

- **No offer whatsoever** — seven "SHOP NOW" buttons and not a single price, percent-off, promo code, or shipping threshold in sight.
- **"Most Loved Stuff This Week" breaks the theme** — Kuromi and Chococat have zero connection to pool/summer Bluey; feels like catalog backfill.
- **CTA copy is uniformly "SHOP NOW"** — every single button reads the same. The Bluey-specific button should say something like "Shop Bluey Styles" to reinforce the IP.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is the sixth email from Build-A-Bear in seven days. A subscriber who opened the Sanrio, Sylveon, and Bestsellers sends is already showing signs of brand numbness.
- **No preview text confirmed** — the inbox can't do work the email body hasn't set up.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a real offer to the hero** — "Free shipping on Bluey orders today only" or "$5 off any Bluey build" would turn this from browse to buy. This is the single highest-leverage change.
- 2. **Replace "SHOP NOW" on the hero CTA** — "Shop Bluey Styles" ties the button back to the character and reduces the generic-CTA fatigue across seven identical buttons.
- 3. **Cut or swap the "Most Loved" product grid** — if the email must have four bottom products, make them summer-themed or Bluey accessories, not recycled Sanrio from five days ago.
- 4. **Add a deadline or urgency signal** — even "Pool season is here — grab Bluey before summer sells out" creates a pull without being dishonest about stock.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `Bluey & Bingo Are Ready for the Pool — Are You?`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `Splash Into Summer with Bluey and Bingo`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `New summer styles for your favorite Heeler pups — shop the poolside collection.`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `Bluey and Bingo are ready to make a splash — free shipping available today.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[DEFECT] Non-standard CSS property casing — `Margin`**
```css
p { Margin: 0px !important; Padding: 0px !important; }
```
Uppercase `M` in `Margin` is not a valid CSS property name. Strict CSS parsers (including some webmail renderers) will silently drop this rule, leaving paragraph margins uncleared. Correct to `margin` and `padding` (lowercase).

**[DEFECT] Soft-hyphen (`&shy;`) characters in preheader padding block**
```html
<div style="display: none; max-height: 0px; overflow: hidden;">
  &nbsp;&zwnj;...&nbsp;&zwnj;&shy;&shy;&shy;...
```
Soft hyphens are a known problem in preheader padding hacks. Outlook Desktop and some older Android clients render `&shy;` as a visible hyphen when the hidden div leaks, causing a row of `-` characters to appear below the subject line in certain preview contexts. Replace all `&shy;` occurrences with additional `&zwnj;&nbsp;` pairs.

**[MINOR] Trailing space in `viewport` meta content value**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1.0 " />
```
Trailing space before the closing quote is technically malformed. Benign in practice since all major parsers strip it, but worth fixing for template hygiene.

**[MINOR] Duplicate `.gmailfix` declaration**
```css
.gmailfix { display: none; display: none !important; }
```
Redundant duplicate property. Harmless but indicates copy-paste artifact in the stylesheet.
