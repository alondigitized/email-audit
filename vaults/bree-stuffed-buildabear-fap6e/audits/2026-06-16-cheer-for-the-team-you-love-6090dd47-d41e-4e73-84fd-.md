# Cheer for the Team You Love ⚽
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-16**
## Executive summary

- Build-A-Bear is back with a second soccer send in six days — "Cheer for the Team You Love ⚽" on June 16, arriving less than a week after "Cheer for the Country You Love! ♥️⚽" on June 10. The creative is solid: a clean green-field hero, a well-lit country bear grid (USA, Mexico, Australia, Argentina), and a genuinely clever "THE GOAT!" section that lands both as an acronym and as a literal goat plush. The email knows what it is for the first two-thirds.
- Then it pivots to baseball. The "HIT A HOME RUN!" MLB co-promotion on a red background lands like an entirely different email was stapled to the bottom of this one. The sport switch has no connective tissue — no "we've got all your summer sports" bridge, nothing — and it saps the coherence the soccer sections had built. That tonal whiplash is the biggest single problem in this send.
- The more structural problem is the subject line. "Cheer for the Team You Love" is functionally a synonym for "Cheer for the Country You Love" from six days ago. Subscribers who opened that one will clock this as a retread before they even open, and subscribers who skipped it will see the same generic signal they already ignored. No discount, no urgency, and a near-duplicate subject in the same week is a recipe for declining engagement on an otherwise competent creative.

## What's working

- **"THE GOAT!" is a smart double-entendre.** Goat plush + Greatest Of All Time + summer of soccer — it's the most brand-personality moment in the email and it earns its hero treatment.
- **Country bear grid is clean and shoppable.** Four distinct national products, each with a direct "SHOP NOW," is the correct format for a gift-browsing audience.
- **Hero visual hierarchy works.** The soccer field background, bold headline, and yellow button create a clear landing zone for the eye.
- **No render issues.** Email is fully visible, no broken images, no layout shifts.
- **World Cup timing remains credible.** The soccer-gift angle is legitimately on-calendar in mid-June 2026.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is a near-copy of the June 10 send.** "Cheer for the Team You Love" vs. "Cheer for the Country You Love!" — subscribers notice this, and it signals creative exhaustion.
- **MLB pivot destroys thematic coherence.** A red baseball co-promo wedged into a soccer email with no transition is jarring and dilutes the campaign message.
- **No offer, no urgency, no discount.** Seven days of daily emails from this brand with zero concrete savings attached to any of them is a pattern; this one continues it.
- **Generic CTA copy throughout.** Every button says "SHOP NOW" — the Argentina "Dual Stuffed Animal" set (a value bundle by another name) deserves its own call-out, not the same label as a single bear.
- **High cadence risk.** This is the seventh email in seven days from Build-A-Bear; the June 10 soccer send was only six days ago. Fatigue is compounding.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Write a subject that doesn't echo June 10.** The GOAT pun is the most interesting thing in this email — put it in the subject line where it actually earns opens.
- **Subject Alt A:** `The GOAT of Soccer Gifts Is Here ⚽`
- **Subject Alt B:** `World Cup Bears — USA, Mexico, Argentina & More`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Build-A-Bear's soccer lineup + the actual GOAT plush.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop country bears + our most viral plush of the summer.`
- 2. **Cut the MLB section or give it its own email.** A separate baseball send on a different day with its own subject ("Big Leaguers in the Making 🏆") would outperform being buried at 70% scroll in a soccer email. If it must stay, add a one-line bridge: "Not a soccer fan? We've got your sport covered too."
- 3. **Give the Argentina "Dual Stuffed Animal" set a distinct CTA.** "Shop the Bundle" or "Shop 2-for" signals value. Right now it looks identical to the single-bear sets.
- 4. **Add one urgency hook.** Even "Shop in time for the next match" tied to a real World Cup match date would give a subscriber a reason to click today vs. tomorrow. The brand has the calendar; use it.
- 5. **Throttle cadence.** After a 7-day daily streak, a 2-day gap before the next send would recover deliverability and inbox goodwill. Consider saving the MLB email for when soccer fatigue actually sets in.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<style>` block inside `<body>`**
The preheader suppression rule is injected as a `<style>` block *after* the opening `<body>` tag rather than inside `<head>`:
```html
<body class="em_body" ...><style type="text/css">
  div.preheader { display: none !important; }
</style>
```
Some clients (notably older Android Gmail) strip `<style>` from `<body>`, which would cause the preheader text ("GOOOALLLL! Celebrate the Summer of Soccer!") to render visibly as body content.

**Dark mode declared but not styled**
Both dark mode meta tags are present:
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light dark">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light dark">
```
However, no `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` rules appear anywhere in the CSS. Declaring support without implementing it means Apple Mail and Outlook for Mac will auto-invert or re-color images and backgrounds unpredictably.

**Responsive breakpoint gap**
Two breakpoints are defined:
- `min-width:481px` and `max-width:639px`
- `max-width:480px`

Viewports ≥640px but below a typical desktop width (e.g., 640–768px — common on tablets in portrait) hit neither rule. Layout likely reverts to fixed desktop widths on those devices.

**Duplicate CSS property**
```css
.gmailfix { display: none; display: none !important; }
```
The non-`!important` declaration is dead code. Harmless but signals a copy-paste artifact.

**Trailing whitespace in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1.0 " />
```
Trailing space after `1.0` is technically malformed; parsers normalize it in practice but should be cleaned.

**Alt text on images** — Cannot confirm; source is truncated before `<img>` tags. Must verify all images carry descriptive `alt` attributes (CAN-SPAM accessibility standard and screen reader requirement).

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**Sending domain mismatch — deliverability risk**

`ebuildabear.com` appears to be a dedicated ESP sending domain. SPF, DKIM, and DMARC records for `ebuildabear.com` must be verified independently. Many spam filters flag lookalike domains (`ebuildabear` vs `buildabear`). Confirm:
