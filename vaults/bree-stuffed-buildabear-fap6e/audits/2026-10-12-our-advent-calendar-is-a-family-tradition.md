# Our Advent Calendar Is a Family Tradition
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-12**
## Executive summary

- This is a catalog-style send with a seasonal hook, and it spends its focus on too many things. The hero, "LET THE COUNTDOWN BEGIN" over a red advent calendar with a plush in the middle, is festive and clear. The copy, "a snuggly surprise waiting behind each door," sells the calendar well enough. The subject promises a "family tradition," but the body never shows a family or says what the calendar contains, costs, or when it ships.
- Below the hero the email turns into a grab-bag. Harry Potter, Cuddly Couture, four unrelated plush, Back in Stock and Best Sellers all get their own Shop button. There are 10 or more CTAs and no offer. The 50+ audience from the persona never gets a reason to act today. It reads as a brand newsletter, not a targeted send.
- Judgement: competent, on-brand and a bit stale. The advent calendar earns the open, then the email dilutes it. It sits in the same 5-6 range as the recent no-offer sends (Oct 9 "Say Hello to What's New", Oct 11 Howl-oween).

## What's working

- The hero is seasonal and legible. The red calendar, the plush and the bold green headline give a clear focal point.
- The subhead is specific and charming: a "snuggly surprise behind each door."
- The Harry Potter block uses a strong dark-blue panel with a white button, so it stands out cleanly.
- The "Start Earning Points!" pill at the top hints at loyalty benefits.
- The product grid is tidy, with consistent buttons and no visible broken images.

## What's weak

- There is no offer, price, or deadline anywhere. Nothing tells the reader to act now.
- The advent calendar gets one CTA, then is buried under Harry Potter, Cuddly Couture, a product grid and explore tiles.
- The "family tradition" subject isn't visually supported. No family imagery, and the calendar's contents and price aren't shown.
- The product grid (Fresno Nightcrawler, Maleficent, Batty Kitty, Sanrio Berry) has nothing to do with the advent theme. It looks like a generic rotation.
- The Back in Stock and Best Sellers tiles are text-only blocks with no product shown. The two full-width blue "SHOP NOW" buttons add little.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the advent calendar the whole email. Show the price, the number of surprises, a peek at 2-3 doors, and a ship-by or "while they last" line. Cut Harry Potter and the grid to one secondary row.
- 2. Change the CTA from "SHOP NOW" to "Shop the Advent Calendar" so the button names the product.
- 3. Add a family image, such as a child opening a door, to back up the "tradition" angle in the subject.
- 4. Drop the Back in Stock and Best Sellers tiles, or show real products in them.
- 5. Add one credible urgency line, such as an early-bird or limited-quantity note.

## Technical Audit

**1. Technical Summary**
The email is structurally sound (XHTML Transitional, MSO/VML fallbacks, responsive media queries, preheader with zwnj/shy padding). However, the automated QA found a 30% pass rate with four product links returning 403, four product images lacking alt text, and no capturable compliance or authentication headers. The 403s are probably bot blocking by the probe, not real breakage.

**2. Link & Tracking Issues**
- **4 "SHOP NOW" links failed with HTTP 403.** All four go to `buildabear.com` product pages:
  - `/fresno-nightcrawler-posable-plush/034979.html`
  - `/disney-villains-maleficent-dragon-plush/032609.html`
  - `/batty-kitty-stuffed-animal/035255.html`
  - `/sanrio-berry-plush/035172.html`

  A 403 on a product page, when other pages on the same host presumably resolve, usually means the CDN or WAF blocked the automated probe. This is unconfirmed. These links should be re-tested from a real browser or a residential user agent before being treated as broken.
- **The QA output shows an empty URL field for the failed links** (`Broken link (403):  (link text: "SHOP NOW")`). That is a reporting artifact, not an email defect.
- **25 tracking links were skipped** because they sit on click-redirect domains, so their final destinations were not verified. The redirect chains are unvalidated.
- **The test send carries `utm_term=US-US_Testing_Hot-20261012`.** The `Testing` segment name suggests this was a QA or seed send, not production traffic. Check that the production send uses a real segment value.
- **The `utm_id` is `2798805`** and is identical across all four product links. That is consistent.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- **4 product images have no `alt` attribute.** They are served from `buildabear.com/dw/image/v2/BBNG_PRD/...`:
  - `08250701634979~x20230105.jpg`
  - `88240802332609~x20101015.jpg`
  - `16250801735255~x20139125.jpg`
  - `47250800435172~x20128124.jpg`

  With images blocked, or with a screen reader, these show as blank space or as the filename. They are also the product images tied to the "SHOP NOW" CTAs, so there is no text fallback for what each product is.
- **The preheader span uses a `<font>` tag**, which is deprecated but works in email clients. No functional issue.
- **The `<title>` is a template name:** "Build-A-Bear - Build-A-Bear_BroadcastTemplate_1A". Screen readers and some webmail clients may read it out. It should be the subject line or a descriptive title.
- **The MSO conditional and VML namespaces are present,** so the Outlook fallback is in place.
- **The dark-mode meta tags are declared** (`color-scheme: light dark`), but the truncated source shows no `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` rules. I can't confirm dark-mode handling from the truncated HTML.
- The `<meta name="color-scheme">` and `<meta name="supported-color-schemes">` tags are not self-closed. This is a minor XHTML validity issue and has no practical effect.

**4. Personalization & Merge Tokens**
No issues found. The visible HTML shows no unresolved merge tokens such as `{{...}}`, `%%...%%`, or `[FIRST_NAME]`. The source is truncated, so the footer was not checked.

**5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)**
- **`List-Unsubscribe` was not found.** The QA tool notes the header may not be captured by the AgentMail relay. This is unverified, not confirmed missing.
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) was not found,** so one-click unsubscribe is unconfirmed. Gmail and Yahoo require it for bulk senders (5,000+ messages per day).
- **`Authentication-Results` was not found,** so SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. The From domain is `ebuildabear.com`, a subdomain-style sender separate from `buildabear.com`. Alignment can't be assessed from the data.
- **The physical address and the in-body unsubscribe link are in the truncated portion,** so I can't confirm them.

**6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)**
- **UTMs are complete and consistent:** `utm_source=Campaign`, `utm_medium=Email`, `utm_campaign=AdventCalendar`, `utm_term`, `utm_content=Promo`, `utm_id`.
- **The campaign matches the subject and the preheader** (Advent Calendar, "25 Individual Pieces & a Birth Certificate"). However, the four flagged links point to unrelated products (Fresno Nightcrawler, Maleficent Dragon, Batty Kitty, Sanrio Berry), not the Advent Calendar. I can't tell from the truncated source whether the hero CTA goes to the calendar page.
- **`utm_content=Promo` is the same on all links,** so click-level reporting can't separate the hero from the product tiles.
- **The capitalized `Campaign` and `Email` values** may split reports in case-sensitive analytics tools (GA4 treats `Email` and `email` as separate values).

**7. Recommendations**
1. Re-test the four product URLs from a real browser. If they load, whitelist the probe or switch its user agent, and don't count the 403s as email defects.
2. Add descriptive `alt` text to the four product images. Use the product names (for example, "Fresno Nightcrawler Posable Plush").
3. Check the raw message headers at the origin, not through the relay. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` are present, and that SPF, DKIM and DMARC all pass and align with `ebuildabear.com`.
4. Replace the template `<title>` with a descriptive one.
5. Use distinct `utm_content` values per placement (for example `hero`, `product_1` to `product_4`).
6. Normalize UTM casing to lowercase (`campaign`, `email`).
7. Resolve a sample of the 25 skipped tracking links to confirm they land on the intended pages with UTMs intact.
8. Confirm that the production send does not carry the `Testing` segment in `utm_term`.
