# High Paw! We're So Happy You're Here
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- This is Build-A-Bear's standard welcome email — a warm, brand-oriented onboarding send designed to orient new subscribers rather than convert them on a specific product. The render is clean, the carousel hero lands with appropriate warmth, and three utility modules at the bottom (Open Access to Deals, Build Your Own Bear, Bonus Club) give the new subscriber practical paths forward. The "High Paw!" opener is perfectly on-brand.
- The core problem is also familiar: this is the exact same template that appeared in the inbox on May 16 — same subject, same creative. Seeing it again on May 18 suggests either a re-send to non-openers or the same automation triggering for a new subscriber segment. Either way, the content hasn't changed and neither has the central critique: no first-purchase offer, no personalization, no reason to act today versus next week.
- As a welcome email, orientation is the job — and this does that adequately. But the best-in-class welcome sends include a first-purchase discount or an editorial "here's our story" moment that earns a click. This one does neither. Adequate is not enough when you're spending welcome-sequence sends on the same subscriber twice in 48 hours.

## What's working

- **Clean, bug-free render** — layout is tight, hero carousel loads, no broken images or overlapping copy visible.
- **On-brand voice** — "High Paw!" and the warm body copy fit Build-A-Bear's personality precisely.
- **Bonus Club CTA surfaced early** — surfacing a loyalty program join in a welcome email is smart; it drives a second conversion action beyond general browsing.
- **"GIFTS UNDER $30" price tile** — gives new subscribers a useful mental anchor for what the brand costs.
- **"LET'S SHOP" button** — clear, high-contrast primary CTA with no ambiguity.

## What's weak

- **No welcome offer** — no first-purchase discount, free shipping threshold, or BOGO. Welcome emails reliably lift with an incentive and this one leaves the conversion on the table.
- **Subject line is too soft** — "High Paw! We're So Happy You're Here" is warm but gives the inbox nothing specific: no offer, no product, no urgency.
- **Zero personalization** — no first name, no signal about why the subscriber signed up (gift-buying, occasion, self-purchase).
- **Carousel hero is risky** — the rotating image may not animate in all email clients and the static fallback frame is not compelling on its own.
- **Resent 48 hours after the May 16 send** — if this is a non-opener re-send with an identical subject and creative, it reads as a stuck automation rather than an intentional follow-up.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a first-purchase offer** — a 15% off code or free accessory with first order turns this from an orientation into a conversion event. Embed it in subject, preheader, hero, and CTA simultaneously.
- 2. **Rewrite the subject to lead with the offer** — the subject line is the gating decision; the warmth can come second.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Welcome gift inside — 15% off your first bear`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your first bear is waiting (and we made it worth it)`
- 3. **Rewrite the preheader to complement, not repeat** — the current preheader is not visible in the render, which suggests it may be leaking footer junk. Replace it.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Valid 7 days — build something you'll keep forever`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New arrivals, deals, and your own custom bear — let's go`
- 4. **Inject first-name personalization** in the "Welcome!" header if the name was captured at signup — even "Welcome, Alon!" dramatically lifts relevance signals.
- 5. **If this is a non-opener re-send**, change the subject entirely — "Still getting to know us?" acknowledges the gap and resets the impression.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Mixed content — 13 `http://` image `src` attributes:**

All social icons (Facebook, Twitter, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Build-A-Bear Radio) and several content images load over plain HTTP. Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail all block or warn on mixed content. Affected assets include:
- `http://image.ebuildabear.com/…/2a45a281-…` (no alt)
- `http://image.ebuildabear.com/…/befb9106-…` ("Build Your Own Bear ONLINE!")
- `http://image.ebuildabear.com/…/c492c57c-…` ("PERSONALIZE Gifts With Sound")
- All 5 social icon PNGs/JPGs

Fix: change all `src` values from `http://` to `https://` — the CDN supports HTTPS as confirmed by the non-broken HTTPS assets in the same path.

**Missing `alt` text — 5 images:**
- `2a45a281-c71e-4b75-8a61-257e92dec041.jpg`
- `597c5852-eb56-4950-9362-adfacdbaf7e2.jpg` (also 404)

**Legacy doctype:** `HTML 4.01 Transitional` is declared, but the body uses modern CSS animations and `@media` queries. While functional, this signals an outdated ESP template that may not receive maintenance for new client quirks.

### 5. Compliance

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe` header not detected:** The AgentMail relay did not surface this header. Gmail and Yahoo require `List-Unsubscribe` for bulk senders; absence risks the email being routed to spam or marked for enforcement action under Yahoo/Google 2024 bulk sender requirements.

**WARN — `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not detected:** One-click unsubscribe is required for bulk senders to Gmail/Yahoo as of February 2024. If the header is present upstream but stripped by the relay, that needs to be confirmed at the ESP level.

**WARN — `Authentication-Results` header not found:** SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown due to relay capture gap. Sender domain `ebuildabear.com` authentication posture cannot be verified from this audit.
