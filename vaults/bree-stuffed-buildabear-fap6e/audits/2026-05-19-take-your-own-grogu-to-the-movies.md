# Take Your Own Grogu™ to the Movies!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- Build-A-Bear is timing this send to the *The Mandalorian & Grogu* theatrical release (May 22 — three days out), which is smart calendar work. The hero is genuinely charming: the Grogu plush photograph is warm and high-quality, the Star Wars logo is cleanly placed, and the "Dadalorian" secondary module is an elegant Father's Day layering that doesn't feel forced. This brief is legitimately good.
- The execution, however, splits the send three ways. After a strong Grogu hero, the email pivots to Super Mario, then to a four-product "Most Loved Stuff This Week" grab-bag that includes Hello Kitty Chococat — a SKU that already had its own dedicated email *yesterday* (May 18). The Grogu moment gets diluted into a general catalog drop. There's no price anywhere, and the body never closes the "to the Movies!" hook from the subject with a visible release date or urgency language.
- This is a 6/10 execution on a 9/10 brief. The movie tie-in deserved a tighter, more focused send.

## What's working

- Grogu hero photography is warm, recognizable, and correctly sized — it earns its space at the top fold.
- Star Wars logo lockup beneath the hero reads as official and adds IP credibility.
- "Dadalorian" module is a clean portmanteau — it slots Father's Day gifting into the Mandalorian universe without feeling retrofitted.
- CTA buttons are consistently styled (yellow on blue), clearly readable, and spaced well throughout.
- Render is clean — no broken images, overlapping text, or empty placeholder fields anywhere.

## What's weak

- No price anywhere — not even the Grogu hero plush shows an MSRP, leaving the "SHOP NOW" CTA floating without a value anchor.
- The subject line's "to the Movies!" hook has zero payoff in the body — the movie release date (May 22) is never stated, so the urgency dissipates on open.
- The Super Mario module mid-scroll is a jarring IP pivot that dilutes the Star Wars moment with no transition.
- "Most Loved Stuff This Week" puts Hello Kitty Chococat in front of subscribers who likely got yesterday's dedicated Chococat email — cadence overlap that will read as repetition.
- Every CTA is the same generic "SHOP NOW" regardless of module — no copy differentiation between Grogu, Dadalorian, and Mario.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add the movie release date to the hero body copy.** One line — "In theaters May 22 — bring yours to the movie!" — closes the subject line hook and creates real deadline urgency. This is the highest-leverage single change.
- 2. **Show the Grogu plush price in the hero module.** Even MSRP converts better than price-blind "SHOP NOW." Price anchors the decision.
- 3. **Cut Super Mario to its own send.** Build-A-Bear has sent five emails in three days; Mario deserves its own subject line, not third billing behind Grogu and Father's Day.
- 4. **Write specific CTA copy per module.** "Get Grogu" on the hero, "Shop the Dadalorian" on the Father's Day module — specificity lifts click rate over repeated generic copy.
- 5. **Kill or reframe "Most Loved Stuff This Week."** If it must stay, tie the picks to the movie occasion ("Great Gifts to Bring to the Theater") rather than presenting them as filler inventory.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Grogu's Going to the Movies. Is Yours Ready?`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Hits theaters May 22 — shop the plush before the weekend rush.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus: Dadalorian gifts for Father's Day and this week's most-loved bears.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Dark mode declared but not implemented:**
```html
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light dark">
<meta name="supported-color-schemes" content="light dark">
```
No `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block exists in the `<style>` section. Declaring dark mode support without CSS overrides causes Apple Mail and Outlook iOS to auto-invert colors on dark-mode images and backgrounds unpredictably.

**Non-standard CSS property casing:**
```css
p { Margin: 0px !important; Padding: 0px !important; }
```
`Margin` and `Padding` with capital first letters are technically invalid (CSS properties are case-insensitive in most parsers, but strict renderers or future parsers may reject them). Use lowercase.

**Duplicate declaration in `.gmailfix`:**
```css
.gmailfix { display: none; display: none !important; }
```
Redundant; the first `display: none` is dead code. Minor, but indicates a copy/paste artifact.

**Trailing whitespace in viewport meta:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1.0 " />
```
Trailing space after `1.0` — harmless in current parsers but non-conformant.

**Breakpoint gap:** Two media queries cover `481–639px` and `≤480px`. Devices at exactly `480px` hit the second query; devices at `640px` hit neither. Confirm this is intentional and that the `640px`+ desktop layout is stable without any responsive overrides.

**Table accessibility:** Layout tables should carry `role="presentation"` to prevent screen readers from announcing them as data tables. Not visible in the truncated source — verify in full HTML.

**Alt text:** Not visible in truncated source — confirm all `<img>` tags carry descriptive `alt` attributes (or `alt=""` for decorative images).
