# Today Only: 2 for $25 Lil’ Cubs in Stores & Online!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-09**
## Executive summary

- This is the best-structured Build-A-Bear send in the recent run: a genuine occasion (National Teddy Bear Day), a genuinely simple offer (2 for $25, one-day-only, in stores and online) stated once and clearly in the hero, and a bonus cause-marketing module (Give a Dollar, Give a Bear) that adds warmth without competing for the same click. It doesn't have the "stack four modules and hope" problem that dragged down 2026-09-05 and 2026-09-06.
- The confetti-circle hero photo works — two kids mid-hug with plush bears reads warm and on-brand, and the pink "2 for $25" badge is legible at a glance. The order is right too: hero offer → cause module → best sellers/new arrivals discovery grid → footer nav. Compared to 2026-09-07's flat "free shipping, no minimum" banner, this one layers an emotional hook (the holiday) on top of a real, undiluted discount, which is the stronger combination.
- Judgement: this is a strong send for Build-A-Bear's cadence — clear offer, clear occasion, clean hierarchy — docked mainly for a soft/generic subject and a "SHOP NOW" CTA repeated four times with no differentiation.

## What's working

- The hero badge states the exact offer (2 for $25) with no decoding required, unlike several past sends that led with vague product pushes.
- "HAPPY NATIONAL TEDDY BEAR DAY!" gives the promo a real occasion instead of a generic "New Season" framing.
- The Give a Dollar, Give a Bear module adds brand warmth (donation tied to a children's hospital) without distracting from the primary CTA.
- Visual hierarchy flows cleanly top to bottom: top offer banner → hero deal → cause module → discovery grid → footer links.
- No render bugs — images, text, and buttons all display cleanly.

## What's weak

- Every CTA button says "SHOP NOW" (five times counting the top banner) — no differentiation between the Lil' Cubs deal, Best Sellers, and New Arrivals.
- The top orange "Free Shipping on Orders of $100 or More" banner competes with the "one-day-only" urgency below it and requires a fairly high spend to unlock.
- Subject line doesn't mention the National Teddy Bear Day occasion, which is actually the most differentiated hook in the email.
- "Lil' Cubs" is a specific SKU name a non-regular customer won't recognize from the subject or preview alone.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the occasion in the subject — "Happy National Teddy Bear Day! Lil' Cubs 2 for $25" pulls in the holiday hook that's currently buried in the body, which is the single biggest lever here.
- 2. Vary CTA copy — "Get the Bundle," "Shop Best Sellers," "See What's New" — so the four buttons aren't visually and semantically identical.
- 3. Move or trim the $100 free-shipping banner so it doesn't dilute the one-day urgency of the hero offer directly below it.
- 4. Add a line clarifying what "Lil' Cubs" are (e.g., "mini plush friends") for shoppers unfamiliar with the SKU name.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Happy Teddy Bear Day! Lil' Cubs 2 for $25 🐻`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Today Only: Mix & Match Lil' Cubs, 2 for $25`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Celebrate National Teddy Bear Day in stores and online — today only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus: every $1 you give sends a bear to a children's hospital.`
