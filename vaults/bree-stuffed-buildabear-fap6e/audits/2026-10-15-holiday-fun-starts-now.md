# Holiday Fun Starts Now!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-15**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, on-brand holiday catalog send with no offer. The hero is strong: two kids at a lit tree holding a gingerbread bear and a candy-cane bunny, under a maroon "Get Cozy This Holiday" banner. The eye lands there first, then drops to the Shop Now button. It reads as Christmas at a glance and has no visible render bugs.
- The email then dilutes itself. After the hero come a Grinch banner, a llama banner, a four-product grid, two more tiles and three text links. That is eleven or more Shop Now buttons, and none of them tells me why to click today. Compared with the 10/12 advent calendar send and the 10/9 "Say Hello to What's New" roundup, this is the same catalog-style pattern again with a holiday skin. There is no price, no deadline and no member perk.
- Judgement: pleasant and polished, but forgettable. It is a 5 or 6 that could be a 7 with one clear reason to act.

## What's working

- The hero photo is warm, seasonal and emotional, with a clear focal point.
- The "Get Cozy This Holiday" headline and the one-line copy are short and on-voice.
- The Grinch and Llama banners add licensed and character variety, and they are visually distinct.
- Each product tile in the grid has a name and its own button, so the layout is easy to scan.
- The layout is clean, mobile-friendly and free of broken images.

## What's weak

- There is no offer, price, deadline or free-shipping hook anywhere in the email.
- Over eleven identical "SHOP NOW" buttons dilute the primary action, and the copy never says what each one leads to.
- The "Start Earning Points!" pill sits in the top corner, small and easy to miss, with no explanation of the benefit.
- The product grid mixes Halloween-leaning items (the Batty Kitty, Maleficent and Cherry plush) into a "holiday" email, which weakens the seasonal story.
- The "More to Explore" tiles are cryptic. "the holiday stuff you love" and an unlabeled ornament image don't say what's behind them.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add one concrete reason to click, such as a gift-with-purchase, a free-shipping threshold or a "order by Dec X for Christmas delivery" deadline. Put it in the hero banner.
- 2. Cut the Shop Now buttons to three or four. Give each a specific label, such as "Shop Grinch plush", "Shop llamas" or "Shop gifts under $40".
- 3. Merge the Grinch and Llama banners into one themed "Holiday favorites" block. This cuts length and sharpens focus.
- 4. Swap the Halloween-leaning plush in the product grid for actual holiday items.
- 5. Turn the "Start Earning Points!" pill into a real loyalty strip with a benefit, for example "Earn 2x points on holiday gifts."

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email's markup is sound: it uses an XHTML table layout, a preheader, and responsive media queries. Four product links returned HTTP 403 in the QA probe, which is most likely bot-blocking rather than dead pages. Two metadata problems are confirmed in the source: the template name leaks into `<title>`, and `utm_term` contains "Testing". The 30% pass rate is driven almost entirely by the 403s.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **4 × HTTP 403 on "SHOP NOW" links**, all on `www.buildabear.com` product pages:
  - `/batty-kitty-stuffed-animal/035255.html`
  - `/disney-villains-maleficent-dragon-plush/032609.html`
  - `/sanrio-cherry-plush/035170.html`
  - `/timeless-teddy-bear/026518.html`
- A 403 (not 404 or 410) on valid-looking Demandware product URLs most likely means the CDN or WAF is rejecting the QA probe's user agent. It does not confirm the pages are broken for subscribers. Re-test with a browser-like user agent before treating these as defects.
- **Test value in a live tracking param.** All four links carry `utm_term=US-US_Testing_Hot-20261015`. "Testing" suggests a test or seed segment name is in the production send. "US-US" is also duplicated, which looks like a templating artifact.
- **Generic source value.** `utm_source=Campaign` is a generic value, not a source identifier. This is a minor attribution-quality issue.
- **23 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped** by the HTTP probe. Their destinations are unverified, so the pass rate understates the unknowns.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text on 4 product images.** The QA check flagged these four images, which are the clickable product images for the same four SHOP NOW destinations:
  - `16250801735255~x20139125.jpg`
  - `88240802332609~x20101015.jpg`
  - `43250800435170~x20125127.jpg`
  - `69231211226518x20714018.jpg`
- With images blocked, the links have no accessible name, and screen readers may read the filename or URL instead.
- **Dark-mode declaration without dark-mode CSS.** The head declares `<meta name="color-scheme" content="light dark">` and `supported-color-schemes`. The visible `<style>` block has no `prefers-color-scheme` rules. Clients that honor the meta tag may apply automatic color inversion with no control over the result. The HTML is truncated, so this holds only for the CSS shown.
- **Template name leaks into `<title>`:** `Build-A-Bear - Build-A-Bear_BroadcastTemplate_1A`. Some clients and web-view versions display it.
- No other rendering problems are confirmed in the visible source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. The truncated source shows no unresolved merge tokens (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`, `[FIRSTNAME]`).

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) were not found. The QA tool itself notes that the AgentMail relay may strip these headers, so this is **unverified, not confirmed missing**. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require both headers.
- `Authentication-Results` was not captured, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. The cause is the relay, not the sender.
- The in-body unsubscribe link and physical postal address are not visible in the truncated source, so they cannot be assessed.
- The From domain `ebuildabear.com` is a subdomain of the brand domain. That is normal for ESP sending, and no issue is evident.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- UTM parameters are complete and consistent across the four product links: `utm_source`, `utm_medium=Email`, `utm_campaign=HolidayEarlyLaunchLifestyle`, `utm_term`, `utm_content=Promo` and `utm_id=2800292`.
- Each link goes to a specific product detail page, not a generic landing page.
- `utm_content=Promo` is identical for all four links. Clicks can't be told apart by module or position.
- Landing page content could not be checked because of the 403s.
- The `utm_term` value "Testing" is flagged in section 2.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Re-probe the 4 product URLs** with a realistic browser user agent or a headless browser. If they load, allowlist or ignore the 403s as bot-blocking false positives.
2. **Check the send configuration.** If "Testing" in `utm_term` came from a live send, replace it with the production segment name, fix the duplicated "US-US", and confirm no test segment is polluting analytics.
3. **Add descriptive `alt` text** to the four product images, for example the product name, such as "Timeless Teddy Bear".
4. **Rename the `<title>`** to the subject or campaign name and remove the template identifier.
5. **Give each module a distinct `utm_content`**, such as `product-1` through `product-4`, for click-level reporting.
6. **Verify the headers on the raw message.** Check the `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results` headers at the source, such as in a Gmail "Show original" view, because the relay may be hiding them.
7. **Either add `prefers-color-scheme` styles or drop the `color-scheme` meta tags** so dark-mode rendering is deliberate.
