# Homepage snapshot · www.williams-sonoma.com · 2026-07-16
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- I came to browse All-Clad and maybe check the new fall bakeware, and instead I'm staring at a sweepstakes popup before I've seen a single pot or pan. Williams-Sonoma has clearly partnered with Hill House on something — the imagery is pretty, very garden-party Nantucket — but this modal swallowed the entire homepage and I cannot see what the brand is actually selling today. It feels aspirational and tasteful, but also like someone prioritized email capture over actually welcoming a returning customer.

## What's working

- **The Hill House imagery is beautiful.** A woman in a white dress against a garden backdrop, a styled table with florals and green produce — this is exactly the aesthetic Williams-Sonoma should own, and it lands for me visually.
- **The collaboration framing is clear.** "Hill House x Williams Sonoma" tells me immediately this is a real brand moment, not a generic promotion. I register it as culturally current.
- **Modal hierarchy is legible.** Prize description, dual images, email field, phone field — even at a glance I know what's being asked. That's decent design.
- **The copy is clean and not over-cute.** "Win the ultimate trip to Nantucket, including VIP shopping at the new Hill House store" — that's specific, adult, and reads clearly without being breathless.

## What's weak

- **The entire homepage is blocked.** I cannot see any cookware, bakeware, or kitchen products. I came for the product, not a sweepstakes. This modal costs me the browse before I've even started.
- **No discount or promotional hook.** There is zero price incentive visible — no "enter to win AND get 15% off your next order," nothing that bridges the sweepstakes moment back to shopping.
- **The SMS fine print is overwhelming.** A solid paragraph of carrier-compliance boilerplate is the last thing I read before the form. It kills any warmth the pretty imagery built up.
- **No returning-customer acknowledgment.** If I have a loyalty account or rewards balance, there is absolutely no evidence of it — the modal offers no "sign in" path.
- **The prize doesn't match why I'm here.** A photo session and a Nantucket trip are aspirational, but I opened Williams-Sonoma to browse copper pans, not to enter a contest. The value exchange feels misaligned with purchase intent.

## Recommendations

- **Add a second CTA below the form:** "No thanks — shop the Hill House collection now" with a direct link, so shoppers who skip the entry still have a warm exit into the collab product pages rather than just closing the modal cold.
- **Surface one concrete offer inside the modal:** "Plus, all entrants get free shipping on their next order" or "Get 15% off the Hill House collection when you enter" — bridge the engagement moment to a transaction.
- **Trim or relocate the SMS consent block:** move the carrier-compliance paragraph to a toggled "learn more" link so the modal ends on the form, not a wall of legal text.
- **Add a loyalty sign-in nudge at the top of the modal:** a single line — "Already a member? Sign in to enter faster" — turns a cold email capture into a returning-customer touchpoint.
