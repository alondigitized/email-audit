# Homepage snapshot · www.williams-sonoma.com · 2026-08-06
**Score:** 2/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-08-06**
## Executive summary

- I tapped in expecting to browse cookware and got a full-screen sweepstakes pop-up asking for my email and phone number before I've even seen a single product. It reads like a well-heeled entertaining brand — pretty floral tablescape, a woman in a white shirtdress — but right now it's pushing a "win a trip to Nantucket" contest, not anything I can actually put in my kitchen.

## What's working

- The Hill House x Williams Sonoma tablescape photo — the floral china, the cucumber salad plated nicely, the lilacs — that's exactly the kind of table-setting inspiration I'd screenshot and send to my sister before Thanksgiving.
- The legal fine print under the phone field is refreshingly upfront ("consent is not required to make a purchase," "text HELP for help and STOP to cancel") — at least they're not hiding that part.
- Clean, elegant typography on the "ENTER FOR A CHANCE TO WIN" headline — feels on-brand for Williams Sonoma, not gimmicky.

## What's weak

- The entire homepage is blocked by this modal — I can't see a single product, category, or offer without dismissing it first.
- Two different close icons (a small X on the photo, a bigger circled X top-right) is confusing — I genuinely paused to figure out which one actually closes the thing.
- The background header text is garbled/overlapping behind the modal — looks like a layout glitch, not intentional design.
- The model in the photo looks like she's in her late 20s or early 30s — doesn't really read as "someone like me" shopping for copper cookware and monogrammed linens.
- Asking for my phone number before I've browsed a single item feels like a big ask for a company I haven't even seen the homepage of yet.

## Recommendations

- Let me see the homepage first — trigger the sweepstakes modal after a scroll or a short delay, not as the very first thing on load.
- Fix the overlapping nav text behind the modal before it ships to more phones.
- Drop the phone number field, or at least visually de-emphasize it — email alone is enough of an ask on a first visit.
- Make the actual submit/CTA button visible without scrolling inside the modal — right now I can't tell what happens after I type my email.
