# Homepage snapshot · www.williams-sonoma.com · 2026-05-06
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-06**
## Executive summary

- What I landed on was not a homepage — it was a modal signup wall, front and center, asking me for my email and phone number before I could see anything. Behind it I can make out a gorgeous food photography shot (roasted tomatoes or peppers in a wide bowl, hands reaching in) that feels exactly like my kind of cooking content. Williams-Sonoma clearly knows its brand; I just couldn't actually experience it because the lightbox ate the entire screen.

## What's working

- **The food photography behind the modal** — the bowl of roasted vegetables with hands reaching in is exactly the kind of lifestyle imagery that pulls me in; it telegraphs "serious home cook" not "gadget shopper"
- **"Recipes" in the modal headline** — I actually paused on that word; it's the right value-add hook for someone like me who reads cookbook recommendations
- **Clean modal layout** — email field, optional phone, one big CTA; nothing confusing about what they want
- **California privacy footer** — I appreciate the transparency about my data; it doesn't feel sneaky

## What's weak

- **The modal is the entire homepage experience** — I cannot see what's on sale, what the season's focus is, what new cookware landed; I'm being asked to hand over my contact information before I've seen a single reason to trust this visit
- **"Exclusive Offers" is meaningless without a number** — 10% off? Free shipping? A gift with purchase? This is how you lose a Gen X shopper who has seen a thousand of these
- **Phone number field** — optional, sure, but asking for my phone on the first interaction feels aggressive; I'm here to look at Dutch ovens, not opt into SMS
- **No loyalty hook visible** — I've almost certainly shopped here before; there's no "sign in to see your rewards" or member-pricing callout anywhere on screen

## Recommendations

- **Show one real offer in the modal headline** — swap "Discover New Arrivals, Exclusive Offers & Recipes" for something like "Get 15% off your first order + new recipes weekly"; give me a reason before you ask for something
- **Add a clear "No thanks, let me browse" dismiss link** styled as text, not just the tiny X — friction on dismiss makes me want to leave entirely
- **Pull the phone number field** — or move it to a second step after email capture; first-touch phone asks read as spam intent to a 44-year-old who lived through the telemarketing era
- **Surface a returning-member sign-in prompt** in the modal corner — "Already a member? Sign in" would immediately bypass this wall for repeat shoppers
