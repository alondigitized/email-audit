# Homepage snapshot · www.williams-sonoma.com · 2026-07-23
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-23**
## Executive summary

- Before I could see a single skillet or set of monogrammed napkins, Williams Sonoma threw a sweepstakes modal in my face — a full-screen pop-up for the "Hill House Nantucket Getaway" blocking everything underneath. The lifestyle imagery is genuinely my aesthetic (a beautifully set table, florals, that effortlessly elegant woman), and the Hill House x Williams Sonoma collaboration sounds exactly like the kind of thing I'd want to explore. But I can't get there because the email/phone capture form is standing between me and the actual store.

## What's working

- **The Hill House lifestyle imagery** — both the outdoor portrait (elegant woman, garden setting) and the styled table with layered ceramics and botanicals are exactly the aesthetic I'm drawn to. This isn't activewear or teen fashion; it's my world.
- **The collaboration hook** — "Hill House x Williams Sonoma" with VIP shopping in Nantucket is aspirational in the right direction for a woman who owns copper cookware and graduated to All-Clad via a registry. It implies curation and elevated taste.
- **The form layout** — email first, phone optional and clearly labeled as such. That's the honest way to present a dual-field capture.

## What's weak

- **The modal itself is the first thing I see** — no grace period, no browse time, straight to "give us your email and phone number." On a phone screen this fills the entire viewport. I haven't even seen a single product.
- **No visible submit button** — the form cuts off before the CTA. I'd have to scroll within the modal just to submit. That's friction on top of friction.
- **The SMS fine print is a wall of legalese** — "Wireless carriers are not liable for undelivered messages. Text HELP for help and STOP to cancel." Four sentences of small-print consent copy on a phone screen, right below the opt-in. That reads as a warning label, not an invitation.
- **No offer tied to email capture** — there's no "get 15% off your first order" incentive to fill this out. A sweepstakes entry for a trip I probably won't win is a weak trade for my phone number.
- **The entire homepage is invisible** — I cannot see a navigation category, a hero campaign, a sale banner, or a single product. For a medium-tech shopper like me who came here to browse, this is a wall.

## Recommendations

- **Delay or suppress the modal for 10-15 seconds** — let me land, breathe, and see something that earns my interest before the ask. Or trigger it on exit intent rather than entry.
- **Add a capture incentive** — "Sign up and get 15% off your next order" or "Members get early access to the Hill House collection" gives me a concrete reason to share my email beyond a slim-odds sweepstakes.
- **Make the submit button visible without scrolling the modal** — compress the fine print into a linked disclosure ("View SMS terms") so the button sits on screen without needing to scroll.
- **Surface a shop CTA alongside the sweepstakes** — a small "Shop the Hill House x WS Collection" text link below the form captures the shopper who's intrigued but won't enter a contest, without losing the promotional moment.
