# Homepage snapshot · www.williams-sonoma.com · 2026-06-04
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-06-04**
## Executive summary

- Williams-Sonoma is doing what it does best — making me want to redo my whole back patio before my next dinner party. The "Alfresco Dining" hero is gorgeous, very much in my wheelhouse, and the seasonality feels exactly right for early June. That said, they've gone full editorial when I was kind of hoping for a reason to spend money — no offer, no rewards nudge, nothing that makes me feel like a VIP returning customer versus a first-time browser.

## What's working

- **The hero lifestyle scene** is exactly my aesthetic — blue-and-white plates, a fresh green salad, a gorgeous ceramic jug, that wine bottle half in frame. I felt it immediately.
- **"Alfresco Dining" as a concept** rather than just a product name is smart. It sells the occasion, and I think in occasions when I shop.
- **The search bar placement** — oversized and front-and-center below the header — works well for someone like me who often comes in knowing what I want ("copper mixing bowl," "monogrammed napkins").
- **The overall page cleanliness** — no cluttered banners, no pop-up modal, nothing fighting for attention. Very on-brand for the aspirational WS experience.
- **Cross-brand nav strip** at the top ("Williams Sonoma Home | Business to Business | Pottery Barn") gives me a quick hint of the ecosystem without overwhelming me.

## What's weak

- **No offer in sight** — I'm a loyal WS customer and I know they have a rewards program, but there's zero acknowledgment of that here. No member pricing, no "free shipping on orders over $X," nothing that would tip me from browsing to buying.
- **"SHOP TABLE SETTING INSPIRATION" is a soft CTA** — the word "inspiration" signals editorial browsing, not shopping. I'm here to buy things, not look at mood boards.
- **No people in the hero** — the table is beautiful but there's no one sitting at it. I connect more when I can picture myself and my friends there.
- **Category navigation is buried** behind the hamburger menu — as someone with medium tech comfort shopping on her phone, having to tap through a hamburger to find "Cookware" or "Table Linens" is one extra friction point.

## Recommendations

- **Swap "SHOP TABLE SETTING INSPIRATION" for a split CTA** — one button for "Shop the Look" (shoppable hero items) and a secondary "Free Shipping on $X+ Orders" strip directly below so there's an actual reason to tap now.
- **Surface the rewards/Key Rewards program above the fold** — even a small badge that says "Key Rewards Member? Earn 5% back" would acknowledge that I'm a repeat customer and not some stranger.
- **Add a face to the table** — a 40-something woman pouring wine or finishing a plate arrangement would make this scene feel inhabited, not staged.
- **Expose one category pill row** beneath the hero ("Cookware · Table Linens · Serveware · Outdoor") so I can get to my section without opening the full hamburger nav.
