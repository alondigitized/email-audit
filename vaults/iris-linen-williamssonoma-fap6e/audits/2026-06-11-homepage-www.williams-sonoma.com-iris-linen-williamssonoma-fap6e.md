# Homepage snapshot · www.williams-sonoma.com · 2026-06-11
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-06-11**
## Executive summary

- Williams-Sonoma is doing what it does best — clean, aspirational photography and a kitchen-first identity that makes me feel immediately at home. Right now the push is Father's Day, and the brand is leaning into espresso machines as the "gift for him" hero. As someone who already has a Jura on her wish list and buys for her husband every June, this is not irrelevant to me — but it's not speaking to *me*, the one holding the phone.

## What's working

- **"Coffee Headquarters" headline** — direct, confident, kitchen-authority language that fits the Williams-Sonoma register I trust. No fluff.
- **Jura machine photography** — the product shot is gorgeous. The latte, the croissants just peeking in from the right, the herringbone tile backdrop — this looks like my kitchen, not a showroom floor.
- **"SHOP ESPRESSO MACHINES" CTA button** — filled black, full-width on mobile, impossible to miss. I know exactly where this tap takes me.
- **Father's Day timing** — I'm absolutely in gift mode for my husband right now. The campaign is timely even if the copy talks past me.

## What's weak

- **No offer above the fold** — I'm an email-driven shopper. When I land from an email with a promo code, I expect to see the offer reflected on the homepage. There's nothing here. No "Free shipping on orders over $X," no member pricing badge, nothing.
- **The "Gift for Dad" framing** — I am the buyer. Speak to me. Something like "Upgrade Your Morning Ritual" or "Espresso at Home, Finally Right" would pull me in as the person who also covets this machine, not just as a gift-giver.
- **Zero loyalty signal** — I have a Williams-Sonoma Visa and a Rewards account. The fact that I can see my cart icon but nothing about my points balance is a missed opportunity right here.
- **Croissants are teasing me** — that corner pastry shot is doing more emotional work than anything on this page, and it goes nowhere. No "Shop Baking" secondary CTA for people like me who'd rather browse cookware than espresso machines today.

## Recommendations

- **Add a secondary CTA row below the hero** — one tap for "Shop Espresso Machines," one tap for "Shop Cookware" or "Shop Bakeware." Father's Day shoppers aren't monolithic; I might be here for a Le Creuset, not a Jura.
- **Surface the free-shipping threshold** — a simple sticky banner ("Free Shipping on Orders $99+" or whatever the current threshold is) converts browsers to buyers. I shouldn't have to hunt for this.
- **Reframe hero copy from recipient to shopper** — "The Perfect Gift for Dad — and Honestly, for You Too" or "Coffee Headquarters: Father's Day + Every Morning After." Acknowledge that I'm the one holding the phone.
- **Show my rewards balance in the header** — even a "Hi, Iris — 1,240 pts" next to the account icon would make me feel seen and give me a reason to convert.
