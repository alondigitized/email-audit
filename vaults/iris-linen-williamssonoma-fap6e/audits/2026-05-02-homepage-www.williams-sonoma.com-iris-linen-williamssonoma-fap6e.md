# Homepage snapshot · www.williams-sonoma.com · 2026-05-02
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-02**
## Executive summary

- Williams-Sonoma opens strong — a Spring Cookware Event with up to 60% off top brands is basically the headline I'd forward to my sister. The sage-green palette feels seasonal and elevated, not bargain-bin. That said, on mobile I'm getting one hero and one product callout, and this phone is doing all the heavy lifting selling a single Le Creuset Dutch oven — which, honestly, I might already own.
- **8/10**

## What's working

- **"Spring Cookware Event" hero** — "Up to 60% off top brands + free shipping" is exactly the kind of email subject line I open on a Saturday morning. Seeing it as the homepage hero validates that I'm in the right place.
- **Le Creuset callout below the fold** — naming Le Creuset explicitly earns trust. This isn't a generic "cookware sale"; it's a brand I actually covet and buy as gifts. The was/now pricing ($460 → $289.95) is convincing without feeling cheap.
- **"LIMITED TIME DEAL" + "FREE SHIPPING" strip** — the urgency framing is clear and the free shipping promise removes my biggest mobile checkout hesitation.
- **Search bar above the fold** — I know what I want when I come here (usually from an email link). Having search immediately accessible saves me from scrolling a nav I'll never figure out on my phone.

## What's weak

- **No lifestyle imagery in the hero** — the sage-green block with serif type is elegant but cold. I want to see a Dutch oven on a wooden prep counter, not just text. I connect emotionally to the *kitchen*, not the discount percentage.
- **No editorial hook or voice** — Williams-Sonoma has always felt like a trusted food authority to me. Nothing here says "Spring is for braising" or leans into a recipe angle. It reads like any cookware retailer running a sale, not the brand that publishes actual cookbooks.
- **Top nav tabs are tiny and confusing** — "Williams Sonoma Home | Business to Business | Pottery" at the very top of my phone screen is a crammed link bar. "Business to Business" in particular feels out of place when I'm a consumer browsing weekend cookware. It breaks the mood immediately.
- **Only one product visible below the hero** — I can see the bottom edge of what looks like gold cookware items but they're cut off. The scroll invite is weak; nothing is pulling me down.

## Recommendations

- **Swap the flat-color hero for a lifestyle photo** — a cast iron Dutch oven on a marble counter with a linen dish towel (or monogrammed napkins — hello) would speak directly to me. Keep the sale text as an overlay.
- **Add a "Shop by Occasion" or "Spring Baking" editorial strip** — two or three tiles ("Bread Season," "Brunch Upgrade," "Mother's Day Registry") would give me a reason to browse instead of just converting on the one featured product.
- **Collapse or remove the "Business to Business" top nav link** on mobile — it wastes prime real estate and signals this page isn't fully optimized for a consumer like me.
- **Show the product grid teaser** — let me see 3–4 items from the cookware event without scrolling. Even a peek-row of pots would confirm the sale has depth.
