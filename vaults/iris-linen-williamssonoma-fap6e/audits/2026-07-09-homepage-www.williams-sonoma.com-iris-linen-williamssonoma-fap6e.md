# Homepage snapshot · www.williams-sonoma.com · 2026-07-09
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- I know this brand. Williams-Sonoma is where my wedding registry lived and where I still go when I want to treat myself to something serious in the kitchen. But the very first thing I see when I land isn't a beautiful copper pot or a back-to-school baking moment — it's a sweepstakes popup asking for my email and my phone number before I've even glimpsed the homepage. The Hill House Nantucket Getaway imagery is genuinely lovely — that table setting is *me* — but leading with a data-capture modal feels like being handed a clipboard the second you walk through the door.

## What's working

- **The table setting photography** — the right-panel image of a styled place setting with florals, blue-and-white china, and fresh produce is exactly the kind of visual that stops my scroll. It's aspirational without being unreachable.
- **The Hill House collaboration framing** — "Hill House Nantucket Getaway" lands as a lifestyle event I'd actually want to win. The preppy-coastal register fits perfectly with how I entertain.
- **The modal's visual cleanliness** — no clutter, no competing banners, the two images bracket the copy neatly. It doesn't look cheap.
- **The woman in the left-panel photo** — she reads like a peer, not a 22-year-old influencer. White linen dress, relaxed outdoor setting. I see myself there.

## What's weak

- **The modal itself is the problem** — I came to browse, maybe check what's new in cookware or tableware, and instead I'm immediately intercepted. Even if I love the brand, this friction makes me want to hit X and then leave.
- **No above-the-fold shop entry point** — the "X" close button is present but the homepage behind it is invisible. There's no "Shop the collection" link within the modal to let me convert naturally.
- **The SMS consent block** — three lines of fine print about "automated marketing text messages," wireless carrier liability, and STOP to cancel reads like a legal disclaimer, not a brand that respects my inbox. It undercuts the elegant Nantucket aesthetic entirely.
- **No loyalty hook** — I've been a Williams-Sonoma customer for years. My points balance, my member status, any personalized "welcome back" signal — none of it is visible. I feel like a stranger.
- **No price signal** — I don't know if there's a sale, a free-shipping offer, or any reason to shop today versus closing this tab and coming back later.

## Recommendations

- **Let me in first, then ask** — move the email capture to a timed scroll-trigger or an exit-intent popup. Show me the homepage first; let the beautiful cookware and tableware earn the ask.
- **Embed a "Shop the Hill House x WS Collection" CTA inside the modal** so the sweepstakes entry also drives immediate commerce — two birds, one popup.
- **Add a single-line member hook** above the form fields: "Already a member? Sign in to earn points on your next purchase." That transforms a data grab into a loyalty moment.
- **Condense the SMS fine print** to a single linked line ("Standard message rates apply. See terms.") — the current wall of legalese reads as distrust and undermines the premium brand position.
