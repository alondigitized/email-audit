# Homepage snapshot · www.williams-sonoma.com · 2026-05-13
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-13**
## Executive summary

- The first thing I see isn't Williams-Sonoma's homepage — it's a modal demanding my email address before I can look at anything. The lifestyle image inside it is lovely (someone tossing roasted tomatoes in what looks like a wide sauté pan), and I appreciate that they're promising recipes alongside offers, but I didn't come here to sign up for a list before I've even seen a single product. This feels like a brand that knows it's premium but is acting desperate at the door.

## What's working

- **Modal lifestyle image:** Hands tossing roasted tomatoes in a white wide-pan — exactly the kind of Sunday-afternoon cooking I do. The imagery is aspirational without being intimidating.
- **"Recipes" in the modal copy:** Tying email signup to recipes is smart for someone like me who reads cookbook recommendations. That's a real value-add, not just a coupon list.
- **Clean modal typography:** The "Discover New Arrivals, Exclusive Offers & Recipes" headline is elegant and in the Williams-Sonoma register — not shouting, not overly casual.
- **Phone field is optional:** Small thing, but I noticed it says "(Optional)" — I appreciate not feeling coerced into giving my cell number.

## What's weak

- **The modal itself is the biggest weakness:** It blocks the entire homepage before I've seen a single product, category, or reason to stay. For a returning shopper like me, this is friction, not welcome.
- **"SIGN UP NOW" as the only CTA:** I came here to browse cookware, not to hand over my email before I've even seen what's new. There's no "skip" button that's easy to spot — just a small X in the corner.
- **No visible offer inside the modal:** It promises "Exclusive Offers" but doesn't tell me what I'd get for signing up. 15% off? Free shipping? Without a hook, I'm less likely to comply.
- **Actual homepage is invisible:** Everything behind the modal is unreadable. I can vaguely make out a wine bottle on the left edge and what looks like a floral tablecloth on the right — I cannot evaluate the real page at all.
- **California privacy fine print at the bottom:** Dense legalese right after the CTA is a trust-dampener, even if it's required by law.

## Recommendations

- **Show the offer before asking for the email:** Lead with "Get 15% off your first order" (or whatever the actual incentive is) inside the modal — give me a reason, then ask for my address.
- **Add a visible, prominent "No thanks, let me browse" link** below the "SIGN UP NOW" button — right now the X is small and easy to miss; a text link would reduce bounce for people like me who want to explore first.
- **Surface one category or product in the modal background:** The blurred content behind is wasted real estate — show me a new copper pan or a seasonal baking set so the modal feels like part of the shopping experience, not a tollbooth.
- **Tie the recipe promise to a specific piece of content:** "Sign up to unlock our Spring Pasta Bake recipe + exclusive offers" is more compelling than the generic version — specificity works on cookbook readers.
