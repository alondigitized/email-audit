# Homepage snapshot · www.williams-sonoma.com · 2026-07-02
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-02**
## Executive summary

- I opened Williams-Sonoma and the first thing I see is a full-screen email-capture modal blocking the entire homepage. Underneath it I can just barely make out a dark navigation bar and some rich red category tiles — the brand clearly has aesthetic gravitas — but I can't actually see any of it. The modal is asking me to enter a sweepstakes to win a "Hill House Nantucket Getaway," complete with a request for my phone number and a wall of SMS consent fine print. Before I've seen a single piece of cookware, Williams-Sonoma is already asking me for two forms of contact.

## What's working

- **Table-setting photo (right side of modal):** Plate with snap peas, white ceramic, purple florals, linen texture — this is my visual language. One glance and I know this brand understands how I set a table.
- **Hill House collaboration framing:** "VIP shopping at the new Hill House store, a professional photo session" — aspirational and specific. This doesn't feel like a generic giveaway; it feels curated.
- **Modal typography:** The headline hierarchy — all-caps "ENTER FOR A CHANCE TO WIN" above the large serif "Hill House / Nantucket Getaway" — is clean and legible at phone scale.
- **Seasonal fit:** Nantucket in July lands correctly. The brand is not running a stale campaign.

## What's weak

- **The modal IS the homepage experience:** I can see literally nothing behind it. No hero, no offer, no category navigation, no featured cookware. A new visitor or a returning one who bounced the modal gets zero product impression in the first five seconds.
- **Phone number ask feels aggressive:** Email, fine. But asking for a phone number on a sweepstakes entry — with a mandatory paragraph of SMS consent text — is a lot of trust to extend before I've even seen the shop. I'm 44, not 24; this reads as a data grab.
- **No shop CTA anywhere visible:** There's an X to close and there are form fields. That's it. If I came here to buy a Le Creuset or browse holiday linens, nothing on this screen helps me do that.
- **The consent fine print is anti-aspirational:** "Wireless carriers are not liable for delayed or undelivered messages" sits directly below the Nantucket dream. The brand voice whiplash is jarring.
- **Left-side modal image:** Woman in a white flowing dress in a garden is pretty but generic — this could be any lifestyle brand. It doesn't say "cookware" or "baker" or even "table."

## Recommendations

- **Make the X button larger and immediately obvious** — right now the close button is small and the modal is the only thing I can interact with. Let me see the homepage faster; I'm more likely to engage if I choose to stay rather than feel trapped.
- **Swap the phone field for a single-field email-only entry** — reduce friction on the sweepstakes, then ask for SMS opt-in as a secondary step after the initial conversion. The current consent block is conversion-killing.
- **Swap the left modal image for a shot of the actual Hill House × Williams-Sonoma product** — a copper bowl, a monogrammed linen, a baking dish. Show me what I could win in terms of things, not just a lifestyle vibe.
- **Add "Shop the collection" as a secondary CTA below the entry form** — even while the modal is open, give me a path to the collaboration product page so a browser (not just a contest-enterer) can act.
