# Homepage snapshot · www.united.com · 2026-07-21
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- I opened united.com on my phone and immediately got wall-to-wall friction: a sign-in modal eating the entire screen, then a cookie consent banner stacked on top of the bottom of that modal. I have been a 1K member for eleven years and I cannot see a single flight deal, route, or upgrade path — just a form asking for my MileagePlus number before letting me breathe. United is presenting itself right now as a company that prioritizes data capture over customer service.

## What's working

- **MileagePlus number field as the primary identifier** — as someone who has that number memorized, this shortcut feels right. United is betting I'll log in rather than browse anonymously, and honestly they're not wrong about me.
- **"Continue" button is dominant and blue** — clear affordance, obvious next step if I decide to comply.
- **"Need help signing in?" link** — small touch, but useful for members who forget their number format. Typical for the road-warrior crowd who have multiple accounts.

## What's weak

- **The entire homepage is invisible** — I cannot see a hero, a fare deal, a route promotion, an upgrade-clearing tool, or a mileage balance. The sign-in modal covers 100% of the content.
- **Cookie banner stacked on top of the sign-in modal** — these two overlays compete for the same real estate. The cookie banner obscures the second button on the sign-in form (text cut off). That's a layout collision, not a considered UX decision.
- **No value exchange offered before the login gate** — United is asking me to sign in but not telling me why right now. "Sign in for the best experience" is marketing copy that says nothing. Best experience compared to what? Show me a PQP offer, a companion cert reminder, a route I fly — *then* ask me to sign in.
- **Zero flight or fare content above the fold** — I fly 100k miles a year. I came here to check fares or a connection. I can't even see the booking widget.

## Recommendations

- **Dismiss the sign-in modal by default on the homepage** — or at minimum, render it as a bottom-sheet over visible content so the hero and booking widget remain partially visible. The gate should not be the entire first impression.
- **Add a loyalty-specific value prop to the sign-in modal** — swap "Sign in for the best experience" for something like "Sign in to see your upgrade availability and PlusPoints balance" — give me a reason that is actually personal to a 1K.
- **Sequence the overlays** — accept/decline cookies first, then show the sign-in prompt (or vice versa), never simultaneously. Two overlapping modals on first open is textbook friction stacking.
- **Surface the booking widget or a fare promotion behind a semi-transparent modal treatment** — let me see there's something worth signing in for.
