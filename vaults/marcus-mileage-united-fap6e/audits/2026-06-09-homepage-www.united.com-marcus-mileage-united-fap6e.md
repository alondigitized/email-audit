# Homepage snapshot · www.united.com · 2026-06-09
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-06-09**
## Executive summary

- First thing I see on united.com is a sign-in modal shoved in my face before I can even look at the homepage, with a cookie consent banner stacked right on top of it. For a carrier I've given 100k+ miles a year to, this is a cold handshake — no fare alerts, no upgrade availability peek, no "welcome back, 1K" — just an auth wall and a GDPR banner fighting for screen real estate. United's brand should be aspirational business travel; what I'm looking at is a friction machine.

## What's working

- **"Email or MileagePlus® number" field label** — they're speaking my language right in the input label. I know exactly where to type my MP number without thinking.
- **"Continue" CTA** — full-width blue pill button, no ambiguity, I know exactly what tap does what.
- **"Need help signing in?" link** — small but useful; I've had MP account issues before and appreciate the escape hatch being visible.
- **"Sign in for the best experience" headline** — the promise is directionally right for a 1K who expects personalization, even if it's not delivering on it yet.

## What's weak

- **Double-overlay sandwich** — sign-in modal AND cookie consent banner are simultaneously competing for screen space. The cookie banner covers the lower half of the sign-in modal and clips whatever button sits below "Continue." On a mobile viewport this is a mess.
- **Zero homepage content visible** — I opened united.com to check fare sales or upgrade inventory, and I'm staring at a form. The homepage — hero, flight search, promotions — is completely invisible.
- **No loyalty status signal** — if I'm already cookied as a 1K member, there's no recognition. Same cold sign-in wall a leisure traveler would see. I should be seeing something like "Welcome back, 1K Elite."
- **"Sign in for the best experience" is vague** — *what* best experience? Upgrade tracking? Saver award availability? Give me a reason before demanding my credentials.
- **Cookie copy is dense** — "We use cookies for site functionality, use experience, to analyze traffic and to show personalized ads from United or our third-party partners" is a wall of fine print while I'm already fighting a modal.

## Recommendations

- **Show upgrade availability or a fare alert teaser behind the modal** — even a blurred "3 upgrade requests cleared this week" would give me a reason to sign in. Make the auth modal earn its keep.
- **Collapse the cookie banner to a single-line ribbon at the bottom**, not a full overlay. It shouldn't compete with sign-in; it's burying the second button and reading like a bug.
- **Surface 1K status recognition on return visits** — if the browser has a prior session or cookie, replace the generic "Sign in for the best experience" with something like "Welcome back — check your upgrade queue." It costs nothing and signals that they remember me.
- **Add a "Continue as guest / Search flights" escape link** below the modal close button — casual visitors and new users shouldn't feel cornered into an account before searching fares.
