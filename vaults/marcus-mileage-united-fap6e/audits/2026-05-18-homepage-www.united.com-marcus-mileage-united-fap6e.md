# Homepage snapshot · www.united.com · 2026-05-18
**Score:** 2/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- I pulled up united.com and the first thing I see is a sign-in modal covering the entire screen, stacked on top of a cookie consent banner at the bottom. I can't see a single pixel of the actual homepage — no hero, no deals, no flight search, nothing. United is a utility brand for me, not a destination I browse for fun, but this wall of friction before I can even see what's on the page is a bad first impression even by airline standards.

## What's working

- **Sign-in field labels the MileagePlus number explicitly** — United knows its core user identifies by MP number, not email, and the field label reflects that. That's correct product thinking.
- **"Continue" CTA is large, blue, full-width, and impossible to miss** — no ambiguity about what to do next if you want to sign in.
- **"Need help signing in?" recovery link** — a small but smart addition; I've been locked out of my MP account before and that link being right there earns a point.
- **Modal has a clear X dismiss** — at least I can close it and presumably get to the actual homepage. That's not nothing.

## What's weak

- **The entire homepage is hidden** — a full-viewport sign-in modal is the single biggest UX friction United could have put in front of a logged-out returning customer. I came to search for a flight or check upgrade availability, not to be intercepted at the door.
- **Cookie banner competes with the sign-in modal** — two overlays active simultaneously means the lower 30% of the sign-in modal is visually fighting with the cookie consent text. On a phone this is a real usability problem.
- **No personalization hook for a returning 1K** — if United's app or cookie knows I'm Marcus Mileage who flies ORD-SFO weekly, this modal could say "Welcome back" and pre-fill my MP number. It doesn't. It treats me like a stranger.
- **Zero value proposition above the fold** — no fare sale, no upgrade benefit, no route-specific deal. I have no reason to sign in other than the vague promise of "the best experience." That's weak copy for a loyalty-driven customer.
- **"Sign in for the best experience" is hollow** — I already know signing in unlocks my upgrades and saved trips. Tell me something specific: "See your upgrade list" or "Check your 1K benefits." Generic aspirational copy is wasted on someone who knows this product cold.

## Recommendations

- **Show the homepage first, sign-in second** — demote the sign-in to a persistent sticky header element or a slim banner ("Sign in to see your upgrade status"). Reserve full-viewport modals for checkout, not the front door.
- **Collapse the cookie banner into a slim bottom strip** — a single line with "Accept" and "Preferences" links frees the lower screen and eliminates the dual-overlay collision.
- **Personalize the modal copy for returning visitors** — if a MP cookie is present, swap "Sign in for the best experience" for something earned: "Check your upgrade queue" or "You have [X] miles — see what they're worth." Even a detected device signal would do.
- **Add a 1K/status-specific hook** — a small badge or line under the field that reads "1K and Premier members: sign in to access your upgrade waitlist and PlusPoints" would make me feel recognized instead of anonymous.
