# Homepage snapshot · www.kay.com · 2026-06-07
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-06-07**
## Executive summary

- Okay, I opened Kay.com and immediately got hit with a full-screen email capture popup before I could see a single product — classic move. The offer is "Spend More, Save More" on Fashion Jewelry with a $2,000+ minimum, which sounds like it *could* apply to an engagement ring... until I read the fine print and noticed it excludes loose diamonds and gemstones. That's literally the main thing I'm here for.

## What's working

- **Concrete dollar savings** — "Save Up To $1,000" on a $2,000+ spend is a real number that catches my attention, especially as someone who tracks sales calendars and watches for promotions before proposing
- **Offer expiration date** — "Offer valid through 06/07/26 at 11:59 PM ET" creates legitimate urgency; I respect a real deadline over a fake countdown
- **Clean modal design** — no broken images, no overlapping text, renders fast; the Kay Jewelers logo is clear and the email field is obvious

## What's weak

- **Loose diamonds excluded from the offer** — the fine print callout right on the modal says "Excludes loose diamonds and gemstones," which is the exact thing I'd be spending $2,000+ on; the offer evaporates the moment I read it
- **Entire homepage blocked before I've seen anything** — this modal fires cold, before I've had any reason to trust the brand or feel excited; it's the worst moment to ask for my email
- **No engagement or bridal framing anywhere** — the word "Fashion Jewelry" signals fashion accessories, not a life purchase; Kay literally has a bridal business and none of that is surfacing here
- **Exclusion list is enormous** — the fine print lists over ten excluded categories in six-point font; that's a bad look and feels deliberately buried
- **No financing language** — I shop financing terms closely (APR, deferred interest, 0% promos); none of that is here and it's a key decision driver for me

## Recommendations

- **Segment the popup by browsing intent or URL** — if I land on `/engagement-rings/`, show a bridal-specific offer, not a fashion jewelry promo that actively excludes diamonds; this is table-stakes personalization
- **Lead with a financing hook, not just a discount** — swap or supplement the hero copy with "0% APR for 12 months on diamond rings" or similar; that's what moves me vs. Zales or Jared
- **Shrink the exclusion language or surface it as a toggle** — the paragraph of fine print reads as a red flag; at minimum, pull the most relevant exclusion ("does not apply to loose diamonds") into the offer copy itself so I'm not surprised later
- **Delay the email capture** — let me see at least one product rail or the hero before asking for my email; a modal that fires before I've seen anything converts worse and creates a bad first impression
