# Homepage snapshot · www.kay.com · 2026-05-22
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- I literally cannot see the homepage — a full-screen email-capture modal jumped in front of everything the second I landed. Kay is running a Memorial Day sale, that much is obvious, but right now the brand looks like it values my email address more than my actual ring purchase. For someone who came here specifically to look at engagement rings and compare against Zales and Jared, this is the worst possible first impression.

## What's working

- **The offer headline lands hard.** "Up To 50% OFF Memorial Day Specials" in huge type is the kind of number that makes me pause before I hit the X — exactly what a financing-aware shopper needs to see.
- **The deadline is specific and credible.** Fine print explicitly says "Offer valid through 05/25/26 at 11:59 PM ET" — that's a real countdown I can act on, not a vague "limited time."
- **The announcement bar is persistent.** Even behind the modal I can see "Memorial Day Sale: Up to 35% Off Everything" scrolling at the top — good reinforcement.
- **Dismiss path is clear.** "No, Thanks" is right there in plain text; no hunting for an X in the corner.

## What's weak

- **The entire homepage is blocked.** I came here to look at engagement rings, not to hand over my email. I can't see a single category, product image, or navigation link — the modal replaced the page entirely.
- **35% vs. 50% discrepancy creates distrust.** The top bar says "35% Off Everything" but the modal says "50% OFF†" — as someone who cross-shops and reads every promotion carefully, that inconsistency makes me wonder what's actually on sale.
- **Email gate is the very first interaction.** This is a dark pattern. I haven't seen one product yet and Kay is already asking for something from me. Jared and Zales will let me browse first.
- **The exclusions block is dense and alarming.** "Excludes previous purchases. Cannot be combined with any other offer. Prices, styles and availability may vary by store and online." — that fine print immediately undercuts the 50% headline.
- **Zero bridal/engagement context.** A 26-year-old researching an engagement ring gets no signal that Kay even sells what she needs — no "Engagement Rings," no diamond imagery, nothing.

## Recommendations

- **Delay the email modal by 30 seconds or one page scroll.** Let me see the engagement ring collection before asking for my email — I'm far more likely to subscribe once I've seen something I want.
- **Resolve the 35% vs. 50% conflict in copy.** Pick one number and use it consistently across the banner and the modal, or explain clearly that 50% is category-specific. Right now it feels like bait-and-switch.
- **Add a single "Shop Engagement Rings" CTA alongside the email form.** One extra button that bypasses the gate entirely — it costs nothing and captures intent from shoppers like me who aren't ready to subscribe.
- **Surface a diamond ring image behind the modal** (visible in the bleed area at bottom). Even a glimpse of a solitaire would make the 50% offer feel immediately relevant to my actual reason for being here.
