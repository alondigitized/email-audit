# Homepage snapshot · www.kay.com · 2026-05-21
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- Okay, so the first thing I see is not the homepage — it's a full-screen email signup modal completely blocking the site. Kay is clearly a traditional jewelry chain pushing a Memorial Day sale right now, which I appreciate because I literally track holiday sale windows for ring shopping. But slamming me with a pop-up before I've even seen a single product? That's a bad first impression.

## What's working

- **The Memorial Day timing is spot-on.** I literally plan my engagement ring research around holiday sale windows — Mother's Day, Memorial Day, July 4th. Seeing this right now makes me pay attention.
- **"Up To 50% OFF" is a hard number.** Vague "save big" language wouldn't get my attention; a percentage does.
- **The deadline is specific and credible:** "Offer valid through 05/25/26 at 11:59 PM ET" in the fine print. I will screenshot that and use it to negotiate with Jared.
- **The modal is visually clean** — no broken images, the text hierarchy (big percentage, then the offer name, then the CTA) is easy to scan even if I hate being interrupted.

## What's weak

- **The modal is a wall between me and the product.** I came here to look at engagement rings or compare diamond specs, and the first thing I have to do is decide whether to hand over my email address. That's friction right when my intent is highest.
- **"Up to 35% off everything" vs. "Up to 50% off Specials" in the same viewport** — those two headlines contradict each other before I've even scrolled. Which is it? As someone who reads every fine print line of diamond-clarity emails, this immediately makes me suspicious.
- **Zero jewelry imagery visible.** The background shows what I think is a couple but the modal buries it. No engagement ring, no diamond shot, nothing to anchor me to what I actually came here for.
- **No loyalty or financing hook.** I shop financing-aware and I know Kay offers payment plans. None of that is mentioned here, and that's a missed shot for a shopper like me.
- **The CTA is for email signup, not for shopping.** "SIGN UP TO BE THE FIRST TO KNOW ABOUT OFFERS LIKE THIS" — there's an offer right there on screen! Why would I sign up to hear about it later when I'm already looking at it?

## Recommendations

- **Kill the hard gate, make the modal a slide-in banner.** Move the email capture to a sticky bottom bar or a non-blocking corner overlay — let me see the homepage first, then ask for my email after I've seen something I want.
- **Pick one discount number and own it.** "Up to 50% off Memorial Day Specials" in the modal and "35% off Everything" in the banner at the same time is confusing. Either unify the message or clearly label them as separate tiers (e.g., "35% sitewide, up to 50% on select styles").
- **Add a "Shop Engagement Rings" CTA to the modal itself.** If you're going to hijack my screen, at least give me a shortcut to my category — one black button for "Email me deals" and one ghost button for "Shop Engagement Rings Now."
- **Surface the financing offer in the modal fine print or adjacent copy.** A line like "0% APR for 12 months available" next to the 50% off headline would be a meaningful signal for a shopper like me who is actively budgeting.
