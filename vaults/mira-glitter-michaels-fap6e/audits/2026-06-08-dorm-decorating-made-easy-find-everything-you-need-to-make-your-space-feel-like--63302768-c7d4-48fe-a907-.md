# Dorm decorating made easy – find everything you need to make your space feel like home.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-08**
## Executive summary

- This is Michaels' twelfth-ish promotional email in roughly four days, and it pivots hard to a narrow back-to-school/dorm-prep audience — a bet that will land for maybe 5–10% of their subscriber base and feel completely irrelevant to everyone else. The "Ultimate Sale" at 60% off was still live in yesterday's send; this email quietly walks that down to a 30% off banner without explanation, which creates an implicit price regression for anyone who opened yesterday's email first. That's a trust problem.
- Execution inside the dorm lane is actually solid. The hero is clean, the Classmate Collection and Dorm Essentials grids show coherent product families, and the visual hierarchy is disciplined — something Michaels has struggled with in the blunt "UP TO X% OFF" blasts earlier this week. The idea is right; the timing and list segmentation are the culprit.
- Verdict: a legitimately well-crafted email sent to the wrong list at the wrong cadence point. Score it high on click mechanics, low on open because the inbox is already saturated with Michaels volume and the subject gives away nothing about urgency or value.

## What's working

- **Coherent editorial theme.** Dorm décor is a real seasonal category and the product selections (Classmate notebooks, string lights, frames, storage) all hang together — a stark improvement over the scattershot "everything's on sale" grids of June 5–6.
- **30% off banner is immediately visible.** Unlike earlier sends where the offer was buried mid-scroll, the discount appears in the first screenful.
- **Specific collection naming.** "Shop the Classmate Collection" is meaningfully better CTA copy than "Shop Now" — it signals a curated edit, not just a category dump.
- **Multiple concrete price points visible.** The product grid shows actual dollar amounts ($20-range items) so the shopper can calibrate before clicking.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no layout breaks visible in the screenshot.

## What's weak

- **Offer regression from yesterday.** The June 7th email led with 60% off the Ultimate Sale; today's banner shows 30% off. No explanation is offered. A subscriber who opened both will feel like they missed the real deal.
- **Subject line is 86 characters of theme, zero value.** "Dorm decorating made easy – find everything you need to make your space feel like home" reads like an SEO title tag. No percentage, no product, no deadline — nothing to click for in the inbox preview.
- **Audience mismatch at scale.** Most of Michaels' general subscriber base is not actively dorm-shopping. This needed list segmentation (recent buyers of organization/storage SKUs, or ZIP-coded near college towns) to land with impact.
- **Cadence fatigue compounds everything.** This is approximately email twelve in four days. Even the most engaged buyer is starting to tune out, and the subject gives them zero reason to reverse that trend today.
- **No urgency signal anywhere.** Yesterday's send at least implied "Ultimate Sale." Today's email carries no deadline language in either the subject or visible hero copy.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the offer and a deadline in the subject line.** The email has a 30% off hook — lead with it. "30% off dorm essentials — plus everything from The Ultimate Sale" is longer but earns the click. Better: strip it down (see alts below).
- 2. **Resolve the offer regression.** Either tie this explicitly to the still-live Ultimate Sale (so subscribers don't feel the deal shrank) or create a dorm-specific stacking mechanic ("30% off dorm + still up to 60% off select items"). Don't silently drop the discount floor.
- 3. **Segment this send.** Run it against the portion of the list who bought organizational or home storage items in the past 12 months. A 20% open rate on a targeted 400K segment beats a 6% open rate on a 2M blast.
- 4. **Cut the email length by 40%.** Three sections — hero, Classmate Collection, Dorm Essentials — are enough. The Scrapbook memories section and lower product blocks dilute the editorial focus and push the scroll far enough that click-through drops off.
- 5. **Give the hero a hard deadline.** A visible "Ends Sunday" or "Ship by [date] to arrive before move-in" banner converts browsers into buyers.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Dorm essentials — 30% off this week only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `String lights, storage, frames & more — everything ships fast`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The Classmate Collection + Dorm Essentials: shop the full edit`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Michaels "Dorm Decorating" Email (2026-06-08)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has a misplaced `<!DOCTYPE>` declaration and two orphaned `</custom>` closing tags in the rendered source, indicating template assembly defects. Several tracking pixels use plain HTTP and lack alt text, and the plain-text part is URL-dominated to a degree that creates real deliverability risk.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**DOCTYPE mis-position — structural defect**
The `<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN" ...>` declaration appears *after* an opening `<table>` block. DOCTYPE must be the first token in the document. In strict-mode parsers this triggers quirks mode throughout.

**Orphaned `</custom>` tags**
```html
<img src="https://click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx?NDDBZ42XPYSETGZDR2PXPKYE74.100220...">
</custom>
</custom>
```
Two `</custom>` closing tags with no matching open elements appear inside the open-pixel `<div>`. This is invalid HTML and will cause unpredictable DOM repair across email clients.

**22 click-redirect links unverifiable**
All CTA links route through `click.emdeals.michaels.com`. QA probes were skipped for all 22; destination URL health (404s, redirect chains) is unconfirmed.

**Plain-text body is 83% raw URLs**
QA finding: 31,663 of 38,006 plain-text chars are URLs. A well-formed plain-text alternative should have human-readable copy alongside links. This ratio will trigger URL-density heuristics in Gmail and Outlook spam filters.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Invalid CSS value — `max-height: auto`**
```css
img { max-width: 100% !important; height: auto !important; max-height: auto !important; }
```
`max-height: auto` is not a valid CSS value. The declaration is silently dropped in all clients; no functional harm here, but it indicates the stylesheet was not linted.

**HTTP image source — mixed-content risk**
```
http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/lib/fe4015707564057d701173/m/1/e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg
```
Apple Mail (iOS 15+) and some Gmail clients will block or proxy this. Should be `https://`.

**Second HTTP pixel source**
```
http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true&sv_svemi=...
```
Same issue — plain HTTP tracking pixel, likely blocked on secure clients.

**Missing alt text on 4 images**
- `e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` — content image, alt="" not set
- Thunderhead pixel (`na5.thunderhead.com/one/rt/track/...`)
- igodigital collect pixel (`nova.collect.igodigital.com/c2/...`)
- Silverpop open pixel (`svopn.e.michaels.com/...`)

Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` (empty, not absent) so screen readers skip them without emitting an "image" announcement.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Subscriber PII embedded in link parameters**
The header "View at Michaels.com" link includes:
```
customerEmail=bWlyYS1nbGl0dGVyLW1pY2hhZWxzLWZhcDZlQGV0ZWxsLmFwcA==
sfmc_Sub=6a0b108e8b75a227d95acdb55aac28a1c4325c544a88543985a0a0aa55b1b862
emId=c258532227e6840b4eee7a4c0dffb3d5660996c95a1dca0c0011637917ca154f
```
`customerEmail` is base64-encoded but not encrypted — trivially decodable. SFMC subscriber hash and email ID are also in the clear. If any link is forwarded or logged, subscriber identity leaks. This is standard SFMC practice but worth flagging as a privacy surface.

**igodigital pixel exposes raw email address**
```
track_event?payload={"user_info":{"email":"mira-glitter-micha...
```
Email address is passed as a URL query parameter in plain text to a third-party analytics domain (`nova.collect.igodigital.com`). This is a data-sharing disclosure issue depending on applicable privacy policy.

**Coremetrics slot is empty**
```html
<!--MASTER_Coremetrics_SLPH_Content-->
<!-- Email content lookups code -->
```
The `<td>` containing this comment is empty. If this slot is supposed to inject personalized content or analytics initialization code, the merge/injection step did not fire.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent at relay layer**
QA finding: header not captured by AgentMail relay. If the header exists only at the originating MTA and is stripped by the relay, Gmail's one-click unsubscribe UI will not render, increasing complaint rate.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) absent**
Without this header, Gmail and Apple Mail cannot offer machine-driven one-click unsubscribe. As of February 2024 Google requires this for bulk senders (>5k/day). Absence is a deliverability compliance gap.

**`Authentication-Results` not captured**
SPF/DKIM pass/fail status is unknown through the relay. Cannot confirm DMARC alignment. This should be verified at the MTA level directly.
