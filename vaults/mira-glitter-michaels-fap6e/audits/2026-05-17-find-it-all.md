# Find. It. All.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- This is Michaels' third email in three days — welcome on the 15th, Rewards enrollment on the 16th, now a kitchen-sink brand statement on the 17th. The subject "Find. It. All." telegraphs the problem: this email is trying to do everything at once. Summer sale hero, six services tiles (framing, parties, classes, Pro, Pro Education, free projects), a loyalty earnings pitch, a 25% off coupon, a savings checklist, and an Affirm financing mention — all in one scroll. The visual metaphor of the prism/rainbow matches a "we have everything creative" brand story, but the email never commits to a single reason to click.
- The 25% off coupon and the "Up to 50% off" summer sale are both visible and credible. Those are real signals. But burying the coupon four or five modules down — after services tiles the average subscriber won't care about today — is a hierarchy mistake. The offer should open the door; the services section should deepen the relationship once trust is established, not compete with it above the fold.
- Judgement: this email is mailing-list maintenance dressed as a promotional send. A new subscriber on day three doesn't need a brand brochure — they need a reason to buy something this week. The Summer Sale hero exists, but the email doesn't let it breathe.

## What's working

- **Summer Sale hero is visually clear.** The "Up to 50% off" badge lands immediately with the colorful prism graphic giving it seasonal energy.
- **25% off coupon is prominent.** The orange/gold treatment in the coupon block makes it visually distinct and scannable even mid-scroll.
- **Loyalty touchpoint is visible.** "$5 in Rewards when you spend $20+" is a low-barrier call to action that reinforces the enrollment email from day two.
- **Services grid adds depth for new subscribers.** Custom Framing, Birthday Parties, and Classes are things a new subscriber may not associate with Michaels — useful awareness.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, overlapping text, or obvious layout bugs visible at any module.

## What's weak

- **Subject "Find. It. All." is too vague.** Three full stops don't create intrigue — they create friction. A subscriber skimming 40 emails won't stop for this.
- **Email tries to do seven things at once.** Summer sale + services + loyalty + coupon + savings checklist + Affirm + brand values is too many competing jobs.
- **Coupon is buried below the fold.** The most actionable offer — 25% off any regular price item — doesn't appear until after the services grid, by which point many subscribers have already bounced.
- **Third email in three days strains cadence.** Welcome → Rewards enrollment → brand brochure is a fast ramp for a fresh subscriber who hasn't bought anything yet.
- **No urgency or deadline visible anywhere.** Summer sale with no end date, coupon with no expiry visible — nothing creates a reason to act today vs. next week.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the 25% off coupon to the hero or directly below it.** The sale headline sets the frame; the coupon should close the deal. Every module between them is a leak.
- 2. **Kill or defer the services grid.** Custom Framing and Birthday Parties are consideration-stage content. Day three of a relationship is too early. Save it for a dedicated "Did you know Michaels does this?" nurture.
- 3. **Add a deadline to both offers.** Even "through Sunday" lifts urgency without overpromising.
- 4. **Collapse to one CTA above the fold.** The hero CTA should say something specific — "Shop Summer Sale" rather than just "Shop."
- 5. **Throttle the cadence.** Give this subscriber one rest day before the next send; three consecutive days from a brand they've never bought from will accelerate unsubscribes.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your 25% off coupon is inside`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Big Summer Sale — up to 50% off everything`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus earn $5 in Rewards on your first $20 purchase.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Ends Sunday — shop all departments on sale now.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Michaels "Find. It. All." (emdeals.michaels.com)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Three raw merge tokens were not substituted before send, creating broken personalization strings visible in rendered output. Additionally, two tracking pixels use unencrypted HTTP, two images lack alt text, and email authentication headers are unverifiable through the relay.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**No issues found with link structure.** All 23 CTAs route through `click.emdeals.michaels.com` with consistent query-string encoding. No bare URLs or malformed hrefs detected in the truncated source.

**Tracking pixel concerns:**
- `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true&sv_svemi=...` — HTTP, not HTTPS. Gmail and Outlook proxy all images over HTTPS; this pixel will likely be blocked or proxied in a way that breaks the open-tracking signal.
- `https://nova.collect.igodigital.com/...` and `https://na5.thunderhead.com/...` — two additional third-party open-tracking pixels beyond the primary `click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx` pixel. Triple-counting opens is a data quality issue, but not a deliverability risk.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image source (render-blocking):**
```
src: http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/lib/fe4015707564057d701173/m/1/e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg
```
This is the only content image served over HTTP. Modern clients (Gmail web, iOS Mail) block mixed-content images — this asset will appear broken for a significant share of recipients.

**Missing alt text** on four images:
- `e323ae89...617b.jpg` — appears to be a content/product image; blank alt means screen readers and image-off clients get no fallback.
- `pixel` (na5.thunderhead.com) — tracking pixel; alt="" is acceptable but was flagged.
- `track_event` (igodigital.com) — same as above.
- `svopn.e.michaels.com` open-tracker — same as above.

For the one content image missing alt text, this is an accessibility gap (WCAG 2.1 §1.1.1) and an image-off fallback failure.

**HTML validity note:** Two unclosed `</custom>` tags appear immediately after the primary open-tracking pixel:
```html
<img src="https://click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx?..."> </custom> </custom>
```
`<custom>` is not a valid HTML element. While most email clients ignore unknown tags, this is a template authoring artifact that should be cleaned.

**`display: flex` on a `<tr>`:**
```html
<tr style="display: flex; justify-content:safe center; flex-direction: row; align-items: center;">
```
Flexbox on table rows is ignored in Outlook (all versions) and some older email clients that enforce table rendering. Any layout centering intended by this rule will silently fail in Outlook.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**FAIL — 3 unsubstituted tokens confirmed by QA:**
- `%%EMAIL_ID%%`
- `%%_subscriberkey%%`
- `%%subscriberID%%`

These are Salesforce Marketing Cloud (ExactTarget) syntax tokens. Their presence in the sent message means the ESP's substitution step failed or the tokens were placed outside a send-time rendering context (e.g., in a suppressed/preview send that bypassed AMPscript). Recipients will see the raw token strings. This is the highest-severity issue in this send.

---

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header not found** — not captured by the AgentMail relay. This header is required for bulk senders under Gmail/Yahoo 2024 sender requirements. Its absence (or non-capture) means the inbox-level unsubscribe button may not render for Gmail recipients.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post not found** — RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is not confirmed present. Gmail enforces this for bulk senders; missing it contributes to spam classification.

**Authentication-Results not captured** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown. The sending domain `emdeals.michaels.com` is a subdomain; DMARC alignment depends on whether the subdomain policy inherits from the parent `michaels.com` DMARC record. Cannot confirm without raw headers.

No CAN-SPAM violations are visible in the HTML: physical mailing address and unsubscribe mechanism are present in the footer (visible from context), sender identification is clear.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 23 click-redirect URLs pass through `click.emdeals.michaels.com` before resolving to destination pages. UTM parameters cannot be confirmed from the truncated source since they are encoded in the `qs=` base64 payload of the redirect URLs. If the redirect handler injects UTM params server-side this is acceptable; if not, GA4/analytics attribution will be lost and sessions will appear as direct traffic. This should be verified by decoding a sample redirect.
