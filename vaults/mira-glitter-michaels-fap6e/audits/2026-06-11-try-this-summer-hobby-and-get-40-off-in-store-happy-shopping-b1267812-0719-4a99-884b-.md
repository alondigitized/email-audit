# Try this summer hobby AND get 40% off in store! Happy shopping →
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-11**
## Executive summary

- This is a more intentional Michaels send than the relentless "Ultimate Sale" blasts that have dominated the past four days. The subject leads with a curiosity hook ("Try this summer hobby") before landing the offer, and the email opens with a bold red 40%-off-one-item coupon as the dominant hero — the right priority order. The birdhouse crafting section in the upper body gives the email a seasonal editorial identity that makes it feel genuinely distinct from the June 10 "restock your craft stash" send.
- That said, Michaels can't resist stuffing the bottom half with the usual Ultimate Sale banner, a 30% off Rewards block, and a "load up on kids' supplies" section — so by mid-scroll you're back in the same cluttered multi-message territory. The summer hobby identity evaporates entirely before the fold ends.
- The in-store restriction on the 40% off coupon is the email's most honest friction point: it's clearly labeled (credit where due), but it's a real barrier for the online-first shopper. The offer is still recognizable and trusted — Michaels subscribers know this coupon format cold — but it doesn't drive an immediate digital click.

## What's working

- **Unambiguous hero offer** — the red 40%-off banner dominates the top third; no hunting for the deal.
- **Birdhouse summer hobby angle is fresh** — specific seasonal editorial hook that stands apart from the relentless "Ultimate Sale" noise of the past week.
- **$7.99 entry-price point visible** — gives hesitant subscribers a low-cost on-ramp to try the hobby.
- **Michaels Rewards "Earn $5" present** — reinforces loyalty value mid-funnel for members.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, overlapping text, or layout breakage visible.

## What's weak

- **In-store restriction kills click conversion** — the hero offer drives foot traffic, not online revenue; an online shopper has no reason to tap the main CTA.
- **Bottom-half clutter kills the birdhouse theme** — Ultimate Sale banner, 30% off block, and kids' supplies section pull the email in four directions; the summer hobby narrative collapses mid-scroll.
- **No expiry date on the coupon** — "40% off in store" with no deadline reduces urgency to near-zero.
- **Subject line tacks on "Happy shopping →"** — filler phrase eats 17 characters of mobile subject line and weakens the close.
- **Cadence fatigue** — this is approximately the seventeenth Michaels email in four days; even a solid send arrives against burned-out subscribers.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add an expiry date to the coupon** — "Valid through Sunday" or a visible count-down callout turns this from clip-for-later into act-now. Single biggest lift available.
- 2. **Kill "Happy shopping →" from the subject** — reclaim those 17 characters for urgency copy or the expiry date.
- 3. **Carry the birdhouse theme through the full email** — if the hook is "try this summer hobby," every section should support it (paints, brushes, outdoor sealant, glue). Drop the generic Ultimate Sale banner from this specific send; it belongs in a different email.
- 4. **Add an online complement** — pair the in-store 40% coupon with a secondary "20% off sitewide today" CTA so the large online-first segment has a reason to click.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Try a summer birdhouse project — 40% off one item in store`
- **Subject Alt B:** `40% off in store + build something this weekend`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Birdhouse kits from $7.99 — bring the whole family. Valid through Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Grab paint, wood, and 40% off — craft store run incoming.`

## Technical Audit — Michaels "Try this summer hobby" (2026-06-11)

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has a valid responsive skeleton and standard Coremetrics/SFMC instrumentation, but ships with two HTTP-sourced assets, four images missing alt text, a misplaced DOCTYPE, non-standard `<custom>` tags, and missing anti-spam/unsubscribe headers — yielding a 43% QA pass rate that warrants fixes before the next send.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Stacked tracking systems — no consolidation**
Four independent tracking stacks fire simultaneously:
- **Coremetrics** (`cm_mmc`, `cm_mmca2`) on click links
- **Thunderhead ONE** open pixel: `https://na5.thunderhead.com/one/rt/track/ONE-PFSLHYI824-2685/pixel`
- **iGoDigital / SFMC** open pixel: `https://nova.collect.igodigital.com/c2/100010552/track_event`
- **SurveyVitals** open pixel: `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847` (HTTP — see §3)

This isn't a bug per se, but four simultaneous open-pixel fires inflates open-rate numbers and creates payload weight on render.

**22 click links unverifiable**
All 22 CTAs route through `click.emdeals.michaels.com` redirect. The QA probe correctly skipped them; final destination URLs and UTM consistency cannot be confirmed without a redirect trace.

**PII exposed in URL parameters**
The header link encodes the subscriber email in base64 as a query param:
```
customerEmail=bWlyYS1nbGl0dGVyLW1pY2hhZWxzLWZhcDZlQGV0ZWxsLmFwcA==
```
Base64 is not encryption. This value is logged in click-server access logs, browser history, and any intermediate proxy. `sfmc_Sub` (hashed) and `mi_u` also appear in the same URL string.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**DOCTYPE misplaced (invalid document structure)**
The `<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN">` declaration appears *after* an opening `<table>` block, not at the top of the document. Some ESPs prepend content before the DOCTYPE. Outlook's Word renderer is sensitive to this and can cause layout shifts.

**Non-standard `<custom>` tags**
```html
<img src="https://click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx?..." width="1" height="1" alt="">
</custom>
</custom>
```
Two unclosed `</custom>` tags appear in the open-pixel block. These are not valid HTML and will generate parser errors in strict renderers.

**Invalid CSS — `max-height: auto`**
```css
img { max-height: auto !important; }
```
`auto` is not a valid value for `max-height` (valid values: `none`, a length, or a percentage). Browsers silently ignore it; the rule has no effect but signals template slippage.

**Images missing alt text (4 confirmed)**

| Asset | Issue |
|---|---|
| `e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` | No `alt` attribute |
| Thunderhead ONE pixel | No `alt` attribute |
| iGoDigital track_event pixel | No `alt` attribute |
| SurveyVitals svopn pixel | No `alt` attribute |

Tracking pixels conventionally use `alt=""` (empty string, not absent). Absent `alt` causes screen readers to read the full URL string aloud.

**HTTP image sources (2 confirmed — assets may be blocked)**

| Asset | HTTP URL |
|---|---|
| Product image | `http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/lib/fe4015707564057d701173/m/1/e323ae89...` |
| SurveyVitals pixel | `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847...` |

Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook.com block mixed-content HTTP images. The product image being HTTP means it will not render for a large portion of recipients.

### 5. Compliance

**Missing List-Unsubscribe header**
The QA relay did not capture a `List-Unsubscribe` header. Gmail requires this header for bulk senders (>5k/day) under its 2024 requirements; absence risks placement in spam or promotions with no visible unsubscribe button.

**Missing List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058)**
No `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` header detected. Without it, Gmail and Apple Mail cannot surface the native one-click unsubscribe UI — users must click through to the web page instead, increasing friction and complaint rates.

**SPF/DKIM status unknown**
No `Authentication-Results` header was captured by the AgentMail relay. Cannot confirm DMARC alignment. The sending domain `emdeals.michaels.com` is a subdomain of `michaels.com`; subdomain DMARC policies are commonly set to `none` or absent, which would allow spoofing.

**Plain-text URL density: 82% (28,349 of 34,640 chars)**
The plain-text part is predominantly URLs — consistent with an auto-generated plain-text version that includes every redirect URL verbatim. Bayesian spam filters penalize this heavily. A curated plain-text version with readable link labels would reduce this ratio.

- No standard UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) were observed alongside Coremetrics params. If the site analytics stack reads UTM rather than Coremetrics, body-link traffic will appear unattributed.

### 7. Recommendations

**High priority**
1. **Fix HTTP image URLs** — upgrade `image.emdeals.michaels.com` and `svopn.e.michaels.com` pixel to HTTPS. The product image will not render for Gmail/Apple Mail users on HTTP.
2. **Add `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers** at the ESP level (SFMC supports this via Sender Profile settings). Required for Gmail bulk sender compliance.
3. **Remove or encode PII from click-link query params** — the base64 email is plaintext-equivalent. Use an opaque subscriber token instead.

**Medium priority**
4. **Add `alt=""` to all tracking pixels** — replace absent `alt` with empty string on Thunderhead, iGoDigital, and SurveyVitals pixels.
5. **Add descriptive `alt` text to the product image** (`e323ae89...jpg`) for screen reader users.
6. **Resolve the misplaced DOCTYPE** — move it to the absolute top of the document before any ESP-injected content, or coordinate with the ESP to preserve header position.
7. **Remove the `</custom>` tags** — these are invalid HTML remnants and should be cleaned from the template.
8. **Audit plain-text part** — replace auto-generated URL dumps with readable link labels to bring URL-char ratio below 50%.

**Low priority**
9. Fix `max-height: auto` → `max-height: none` in the mobile media query.
10. Confirm `cm_mmc` tagging is consistently applied to all 22 click links, especially the in-store offer CTA — run a redirect trace on each URL before the next send.
11. Resolve SPF/DKIM/DMARC status for `emdeals.michaels.com` — confirm `Authentication-Results` passes through the relay on the next capture.
