# Your creativity deserves a Reward. 🏆Earn Rewards on every purchase and score VIP perks➡️
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- This is Michaels' Day-2 loyalty enrollment email — sent less than 24 hours after the welcome — and it does a lot of work at once: enroll in Rewards, understand the tier table, grab a $5 bonus, redeem a 25% coupon, download the app. That ambition is the email's biggest liability. There's no single hero moment; the eye ricochets through five distinct value propositions before finding the most actionable offer (the 25%-off in-store coupon) buried near the bottom.
- The underlying content is solid — the tier comparison table is genuinely useful, the $5-for-$25 threshold is a smart enrollment nudge, and the dashed-border coupon has real urgency-adjacent energy. But all of that is undermined by a subject line that clocks in near 90 characters, mentions "VIP perks" without ever saying what they are, and arrives the morning after a welcome email that already promised 50% off the Anniversary Sale.
- The 25% coupon is the email's strongest asset and its most buried one. Lead with it, cut the rest in half, and this becomes a much stronger send.

## What's working

- **Recognizable brand identity** — Michaels logo, red palette, and signature coupon styling make origin instantly clear.
- **Tier table is genuinely informative** — Red/Gold/Platinum laid out side-by-side; for a new member deciding whether to engage, this is exactly the right content.
- **$5-for-$25 threshold is concrete and low-friction** — Easy math, easy win; this is the kind of micro-incentive that actually drives first purchases.
- **25% off coupon has strong visual treatment** — The dashed border reads "real coupon" and stands out from the section above it.
- **Affirm integration visible** — Buy-now-pay-later mention adds flexibility signal for larger cart purchases.

## What's weak

- **Five competing CTAs with no clear primary** — "Join now," another "Join now," "Join now to earn $5," "Get Coupon," app download icons — the reader has no obvious next step.
- **Subject line is ~88 characters** — Gets truncated on every major mobile client; "score VIP perks➡️" likely disappears entirely in preview.
- **25% off coupon is below four full content sections** — The best concrete offer requires significant scroll to reach; most mobile readers won't get there.
- **No campaign deadline visible** — "Big Summer Sale" in the hero banner but no end date on the coupon or Rewards bonus; urgency is implied but not real.
- **Cadence is aggressive** — Second email in under 24 hours; no new subscriber needs a tier-comparison email the morning after their welcome. This risks an early unsubscribe.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the 25% coupon, not the enrollment pitch.** Move the coupon to the hero slot. "Your first Rewards email comes with a bonus: 25% off anything" is a far stronger open than a tier comparison table.
- 2. **Cut the email to three sections max.** Hero (coupon) → Why Rewards (3% earn + tier one-liner) → Download the app. Delete the full tier table from this send; save it for a dedicated "how Rewards works" nurture email.
- 3. **Shorten the subject line to under 50 chars.** The first clause alone ("Your creativity deserves a Reward") is better than the full run-on.
- 4. **Add a hard expiration to the 25% coupon.** Even "valid through Sunday" creates real urgency without bait-and-switch risk.
- 5. **Delay this send by 48–72 hours.** Day-2 loyalty pitch after a Day-1 welcome is too compressed; the 50% Anniversary Sale in the welcome email already outbids this one.
- **Subject Alt A:** `25% off anything — your Rewards welcome gift`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your first perk is waiting: 25% off one item`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Earn 3% back on every purchase. Redeem your coupon in-store or online.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus: earn $5 in Rewards when you spend $25.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Three unresolved merge tokens will render as raw placeholder text for all recipients. One content image is served over HTTP and will be blocked in most modern clients.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**No issues found with link structure itself** — all CTA links correctly route through `click.emdeals.michaels.com`.

However:
- **19 tracking/click links were skipped** by HTTP probe due to redirect-chain architecture — destination URLs and their HTTP status could not be verified programmatically.
- **No UTM parameters confirmed** on destination URLs (obscured by click-redirect wrapping); see §6.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

| Issue | Evidence |
|---|---|
| `display: flex` on a `<tr>` element | `<tr style="display: flex; justify-content:safe center; flex-direction: row; align-items: center;">` — flexbox on table rows is invalid and ignored in Outlook, Yahoo Mail, and many mobile clients |
| Mixed HTTP/HTTPS image content | `http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/.../e323ae89-...jpg` and `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/...` — HTTPS clients (Gmail, Outlook.com) will block or warn on non-secure image sources |
| 5 images missing `alt` text | `44ad8775-b64c-4c01-8352-640dc93e85d0.png`, `e323ae89-...jpg`, tracking pixel at `thunderhead.com`, `igodigital.com` collect pixel, `svopn.e.michaels.com` open pixel — images-off rendering shows blank space with no fallback label |
| Malformed custom tags | `</custom>` appears twice in the pixel `<div>` immediately after the open-pixel `<img>` — not valid HTML and may confuse parsers |
| `p { margin: 1em 0; }` declared twice | Duplicate rule in `<head>` `<style>` block (lines ~18 and ~25) — minor, no functional impact but indicates template hygiene issues |
| `max-height: auto` | Used in media query `img` rule — `auto` is not a valid value for `max-height`; should be omitted or set to `none` |

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**CRITICAL — broken experience for all recipients.**

QA detected 3 unresolved merge tokens in the rendered HTML:

```
%%EMAIL_ID%%
%%_subscriberkey%%
%%subscriberID%%
```

These are Salesforce Marketing Cloud (ExactTarget) personalization tokens that were not substituted before send. They will render as literal strings in the email body or, if embedded in URLs, produce broken tracking links. Confirm whether these appear in link `href` values or visible body text — either case constitutes a deliverability and data-integrity failure.

---

### 5. Compliance

| Check | Status | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| CAN-SPAM unsubscribe link | Not verified from truncated source — must confirm physical address and opt-out mechanism present in footer | |
| `List-Unsubscribe` header | **WARN** — not captured by AgentMail relay; unknown if present at SMTP layer | Required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender rules (>5K/day) |
| `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header | **WARN** — absent or not captured; one-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058) not confirmed | Gmail enforces this for bulk senders since Feb 2024 |
| SPF/DKIM/DMARC | **WARN** — `Authentication-Results` header not captured by relay; pass/fail status unknown | `emdeals.michaels.com` subdomain must have aligned DKIM signing |

**Risk:** Missing `List-Unsubscribe-Post` with bulk send volume will cause Gmail to suppress the one-click unsubscribe UI and may trigger spam classification.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

- All destination URLs are wrapped in `click.emdeals.michaels.com` redirect chains — UTM parameter presence on final landing pages **cannot be confirmed** without resolving the redirect chain manually.
- If the three broken merge tokens (`%%EMAIL_ID%%`, `%%_subscriberkey%%`, `%%subscriberID%%`) appear in tracking link query strings, click-tracking and subscriber attribution will be corrupted for this entire send.
- Multiple third-party tracking pixels are firing: `thunderhead.com` (Thunderhead ONE), `igodigital.com` (Salesforce CDP), and `svopn.e.michaels.com` (SilverPop/Acoustic open tracker) — three separate open-tracking systems active simultaneously, which may inflate open metrics across platforms inconsistently.

---

### 7. Recommendations

**P0 — Fix before any resend:**
1. Resolve the 3 unresolved SFMC merge tokens (`%%EMAIL_ID%%`, `%%_subscriberkey%%`, `%%subscriberID%%`) — identify whether they appear in link HREFs or body text and correct the template data extension binding.
2. Upgrade the `http://` image source (`e323ae89-...jpg`) and the `http://svopn.e.michaels.com` open pixel to HTTPS — mixed content blocks will suppress these images in Gmail, Outlook.com, and iOS Mail.

**P1 — Fix in template before next campaign:**
3. Remove `display: flex` from `<tr>` elements — use `<td>` with `align`/`valign` attributes or restructure with `<div>`-based layout for the affected row.
4. Add descriptive `alt` text to the two content images (`44ad8775` and `e323ae89`); tracking pixel `alt=""` (empty string) is acceptable and already handled for the open pixel if set correctly.
5. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are present at SMTP layer — required for Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender compliance. Verify with a full header capture (not relay-filtered).
6. Remove the stray `</custom>` tags from the open-pixel `<div>`.

**P2 — Template hygiene:**
7. Remove duplicate `p { margin: 1em 0; }` declaration.
8. Replace `max-height: auto` with `max-height: none` in media query.
9. Audit whether three concurrent open-tracking pixels (Thunderhead, iGo, SilverPop) is intentional — deduplicate if not, as conflicting open counts will skew reporting.
