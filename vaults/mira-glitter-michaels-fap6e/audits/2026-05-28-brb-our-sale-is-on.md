# BRB: Our Sale is ON 💃 🕺
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-28**
## Executive summary

- After two back-to-back content pivots — the Party Shop launch on 5/27 and the outdoor family fun send the same day — Michaels is back in full sale mode. The "BRB" framing is the sharpest line in this entire email: it acknowledges the brief pause and signals a fresh sale with personality. The problem is everything after that subject line undercuts the freshness. The body is a classic grab-bag scroll — 25% off cut flowers, 60% off floral, 50% off two more categories, 20% off something else, balloons, small kit balloons, and an Earn $5 Rewards block — all stacked vertically with no single narrative thread.
- The visual execution is clean but the editorial strategy is absent. There's no "this is the summer sale, here's what makes it different from the Memorial Day one you just lived through for two weeks" framing. The render is tight and no bugs are visible, but the email reads as a re-skinned version of the Memorial Day deal dumps, just with slightly different discount percentages and a cheeky subject line.
- The Rewards "Earn $5" block at the bottom is a nice loyalty touch but buried so deep in the scroll it will only reach the committed buyer — exactly the person who needed zero persuasion to open this.

## What's working

- **"BRB" subject hook** is genuinely playful and earns attention after two content-shift sends — it sets up a return correctly.
- **Floral hero** is seasonally appropriate for late May and the 25% + 60% dual-offer layout reads quickly.
- **Specific price anchors** ($29.99, $9.99) break up the percentage-heavy blocks and give the eye something concrete to land on.
- **Michaels Rewards "Earn $5"** call-out at the bottom is good loyalty reinforcement, even if placement is weak.
- **No render bugs** — clean images, no overlapping text, no broken layout sections visible.

## What's weak

- **No single sale identity** — five distinct discount tiers across flowers, floral, two unnamed 50%-off categories, and balloons makes this feel like inventory liquidation, not a curated summer sale launch.
- **"BRB" promise isn't paid off** — the subject implies something new is back; the body delivers the same scatter-shot deal format as every Memorial Day send.
- **Balloons section sits oddly** — after the Party Shop launch email two days ago, a second balloons feature without clear differentiation looks like repeated creative, not a campaign.
- **No visible deadline** — the sale is "ON" but there's no "ends Sunday" or "this week only" hook anywhere in the visible scroll to drive urgency.
- **CTA copy is generic** — the shop buttons appear to use standard "Shop Now" phrasing rather than something specific to each offer.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Give the sale a name and a deadline** — "BRB: Summer Sale is ON — ends Sunday" immediately solves the urgency gap and differentiates this from the just-finished Memorial Day run. Without a deadline, "sale is on" is noise.
- 2. **Collapse to 2-3 hero categories max** — choose flowers + one craft supply category and go deep. Five discount tiers in one scroll trains subscribers to skim and click nothing.
- 3. **Lead the Rewards block** — "Earn $5 back this weekend" in the top third would make the sale feel exclusive to members and drive loyalty behavior before price fatigue sets in.
- 4. **Differentiate balloons from the Party Shop email** — if you're featuring balloons again 48 hours after the Party Shop launch, at minimum add a line like "New kit sizes just dropped" to justify the repeat.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Summer Sale is live — up to 60% off this weekend`
- **Subject Alt B:** `We're back. 60% off flowers + more inside.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New sale, new savings — ends Sunday. Shop flowers, crafts, balloons & more.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Cut flowers 25% off, plus earn $5 Rewards this weekend only.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Michaels "BRB: Our Sale is ON" (2026-05-28)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally functional but carries three confirmed HTTP mixed-content violations, malformed HTML tags, missing alt text on multiple images (including a content image), and absent authentication/unsubscribe headers that will flag spam filters. The plain-text version's URL density (81%) is an active deliverability risk.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**22 click-redirect links unverifiable** — all CTAs route through `click.emdeals.michaels.com`. Final destination URLs could not be HTTP-probed; broken landing pages would not be caught by automated checks.

**Four separate tracking systems active in one send:**
- `click.emdeals.michaels.com` — Salesforce Marketing Cloud ESP click tracking
- `na5.thunderhead.com` — One by Thunderhead behavioral pixel
- `nova.collect.igodigital.com` — Salesforce Interaction Studio (iGoDigital) event pixel
- `svopn.e.michaels.com` — secondary open-tracking domain

This is a high pixel load; some security-conscious clients (Outlook, Apple Mail MPP) will block or pre-fire all of them, corrupting open/click attribution across all four systems.

**PII in link parameters** — the header logo CTA exposes a base64-encoded subscriber email as a URL query parameter:
```
customerEmail=bWlyYS1nbGl0dGVyLW1pY2hhZWxzLWZhcDZlQGV0ZWxsLmFwcA==
```
This decodes to the recipient address. URL parameters are logged by proxies, CDNs, and browser history — a PII leak vector.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources (2 confirmed)** — will be blocked by Gmail, Outlook.com, and most corporate email clients that enforce HTTPS:
- `http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/lib/fe4015707564057d701173/m/1/e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` — a visible content image
- `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true&...` — open-tracking pixel

**Missing alt text (4 images):**

| Image | Issue |
|---|---|
| `e323ae89-...013cd1.jpg` | Content image, no `alt` — renders blank with images off |
| `thunderhead.com` pixel | No `alt=""` — minor but flags accessibility linters |
| `igodigital` track_event pixel | No `alt=""` |
| `svopn.e.michaels.com` pixel | No `alt=""` |

Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` (empty, not absent) to satisfy validators cleanly.

**Malformed HTML — unclosed custom tags:**
```html
<img src="https://click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx?..." width="1" height="1" alt="">
</custom>
</custom>
```
Two `</custom>` closing tags appear with no corresponding opening tags. While most parsers will ignore these, they violate the declared `HTML 4.01 Transitional` DOCTYPE and can cause unexpected rendering in strict parsers (some Outlook versions).

**Duplicate CSS rule** — `p { margin: 1em 0; }` is declared twice in `<head>`. No rendering impact but indicates a template assembly issue.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens visible in the truncated source (no `%%FIELD%%`, `{{field}}`, or `[FIRST_NAME]` literals). Salesforce MC subscriber tokens (`sfmc_Sub`, `mi_u`) appear resolved to hashed values in the header link. No issues found in the visible source.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM / RFC 8058 / Authentication)

**List-Unsubscribe header absent** — not captured by the AgentMail relay. Major ISPs (Gmail, Yahoo) use this header for the native "Unsubscribe" button; its absence increases user-reported spam rates and risks inbox placement demotion under Gmail/Yahoo 2024 bulk sender requirements.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post absent** — RFC 8058 one-click machine unsubscribe not supported. Required for bulk senders (>5K/day to Gmail) as of February 2024 Google policy.

**Authentication-Results header not found** — SPF/DKIM pass/fail status is unknown via the relay. Cannot confirm `emdeals.michaels.com` is properly aligned. If DKIM is failing or missing, combined with the missing List-Unsubscribe, this send is at elevated risk of Gmail/Yahoo spam folder placement.

**Plain-text URL density: 81%** (28,533 of 35,207 chars are URLs) — well above the ~70% threshold that triggers Spamhaus and Gmail heuristics. The plain-text part appears to be auto-generated from HTML without stripping tracking parameters.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM / Landing Page)

**Tracking parameter schema: Coremetrics, not GA4.** Links use `cm_mmc` (IBM Coremetrics / Acoustic) format, e.g.:
```
cm_mmc=EMAIL-_-DCE-_-20260528_S1_US_TempA_DCE_FF_US-_-HEADER_HOME
```
Standard `utm_source` / `utm_medium` / `utm_campaign` parameters are absent on visible links. If the site has migrated to GA4, these `cm_mmc` values will not populate GA4 session dimensions without a Coremetrics→GA4 mapping layer in GTM.

**All 22 CTAs route through the click-redirect domain** — final landing page UTM integrity cannot be verified without resolving the redirects. If the redirect chain drops parameters (e.g., a 301 to a URL without passthrough), attribution is lost server-side regardless of the `cm_mmc` values.
