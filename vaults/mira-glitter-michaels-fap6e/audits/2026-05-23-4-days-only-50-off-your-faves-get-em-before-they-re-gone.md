# ‼️4 days only— 50% off your faves— Get’em before they’re gone‼️
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- This is Michaels' eleventh email in eight days — and it is structurally identical to the May 22 send ("50% off 📣 4 days only 📣 Sale starts TODAY!" — scored 6/10 here). The creative team has rotated the lead emoji from 📣 to ‼️, shuffled the offer blocks, and called it a new send. The red-white-blue Memorial Day palette is well-timed and the discount depth (30%, 50%, 60%, B1G2) is genuinely aggressive, but the kitchen-sink format means the eye has no anchor — it just free-falls through a stack of competing offer blocks.
- The fatal problem is cadence-plus-sameness. A subscriber who opened the May 22 email has already seen this exact pitch. This send adds nothing: same urgency hook, same % off, same "your faves" pseudo-personalization. The ‼️ bookends on the subject are the kind of thing spam filters notice and fatigued subscribers skip on sight.
- Net judgment: technically competent, strategically exhausted. The Memorial Day window justifies one more send; this creative does not justify it.

## What's working

- **Patriotic design matches the moment.** The red-white-blue Memorial Day Sale header is well-executed and seasonally on-point.
- **Discount depth is real.** 30%, 50%, 60% off tiers plus B1G2 and B1G1 give even a casual browser a reason to click.
- **Rewards anchor at the bottom.** The "Earn $5" Michaels Rewards block is a smart loyalty reminder for members who are close to a threshold.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, overlapping text, or layout breakage visible anywhere in the email.
- **"4 days only" is reinforced in-body.** The time-bound urgency from the subject is echoed inside, which makes the deadline credible rather than pure click-bait.

## What's weak

- **Eight competing offer blocks, zero hierarchy.** The email opens with 30% off, immediately jumps to 50% off, then 60%, then B1G2 — the eye has no hero moment. This is identical in structure to the May 20 and May 22 sends.
- **Subject-to-hero mismatch.** The subject promises "50% off your faves" but the visible top-of-email block is a 30% offer. That's a bait-and-scroll that erodes trust on open.
- **‼️ bookends are inbox-view red flags.** Opening and closing the subject with ‼️ reads as desperation and triggers spam-association in subscribers who have seen this pattern before.
- **Eleventh email in eight days.** The cadence has long since crossed into fatigue territory; this send is working against itself regardless of offer quality.
- **"Your faves" is hollow.** The subject implies personalization; the email body is a generic sale grid. That gap is noticeable.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one hero offer and commit to it.** If the best offer is 50% off Cricut/frames/summer supplies, open with that full-width hero. Kill the 30% top block or move it below the fold. The disconnect between subject promise and scroll reveal is the single biggest click-killer.
- 2. **Cut the ‼️ bookends entirely.** Rewrite the subject as concrete copy without punctuation spam.
- **Subject Alt A:** `4 days left: 50% off summer crafts & more`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Memorial Day Sale ends Sunday — 50–60% off`
- 3. **Write a real preheader that complements the subject.** The subject covers the %, the preheader should name a category or product.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Frames, Cricut, yarn, floral — and hundreds more. Shop before Monday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your Michaels Rewards count toward this sale. See what's in store.`
- 4. **Drop to one email per day through Sunday.** A single well-targeted send on Sunday morning (day-before deadline) will convert better than daily blasts that are training subscribers to ignore the brand.
- 5. **Personalize at least one section by purchase history.** "Your faves" in the subject is a promise. One block — "Based on what you've bought: 50% off Cricut accessories" — would make that promise real and lift CTR meaningfully.

### 1. Technical Summary

The email renders via a multi-vendor stack (SFMC, Thunderhead ONE, iGoDigital, Sievert) with structural HTML defects and mixed-content HTTP assets that will trigger blocking in modern clients. Authentication headers are unverifiable through the AgentMail relay, leaving SPF/DKIM status unknown.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**DOCTYPE placement — critical structural defect**
The `<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN">` declaration appears *after* an opening `<table>` block, not at the document root. This puts the parser into quirks mode for the preceding markup and may cause inconsistent rendering in Outlook and older WebKit clients.

**Orphaned `</custom>` tags**
Two `</custom>` closing tags appear immediately after the open-tracking pixel `<img>`:
```html
<img src="https://click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx?..." width="1" height="1" alt="">
</custom>
</custom>
```
These are non-standard elements and will generate parse errors; no matching opening tags are present in the truncated source.

**21 click-redirect links unverified**
All outbound links route through `click.emdeals.michaels.com`. QA skipped HTTP probing on these, so final destination URLs and redirect chain health are unconfirmed for this send.

**Legacy Coremetrics comment**
```html
<!--MASTER_Coremetrics_SLPH_Content-->
```
IBM Coremetrics was sunset in 2023. If this comment is a placeholder for injected tracking code, that injection either silently failed or is no longer wired. Confirm whether Coremetrics scripts are still being injected or this is dead markup.

**PII in click URL query string**
The header home link passes the recipient's email as base64 in a URL parameter:
```
customerEmail=bWlyYS1nbGl0dGVyLW1pY2hhZWxzLWZhcDZlQGV0ZWxsLmFwcA==
```
Base64 is not encryption. This exposes the email address in server access logs, referrer headers, and browser history on the landing page.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Mixed-content HTTP image (will block in Gmail, iOS Mail)**
```
src: http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/lib/fe4015707564057d701173/m/1/e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg
```
Non-HTTPS image sources are blocked by Gmail and most modern clients. This asset will render as a broken image.

**HTTP open-tracking pixel**
```
src: http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true&...
```
Also HTTP. Open-rate data from this pixel will be undercounted in any client enforcing HTTPS-only content.

**Four images missing `alt` text**

| Asset | Source |
|---|---|
| Product/promo image | `e323ae89-...f33897013cd1.jpg` |
| Thunderhead pixel | `na5.thunderhead.com/.../pixel` |
| iGoDigital pixel | `nova.collect.igodigital.com/.../track_event` |
| Sievert open pixel | `svopn.e.michaels.com/` |

Tracking pixels conventionally use `alt=""` (empty string) — omitting the attribute entirely triggers screen-reader announcements and may fail accessibility audits (WCAG 1.1.1).

**Invalid CSS value**
```css
max-height: auto !important;
```
`auto` is not a valid value for `max-height`. Valid values are a length, `none`, or a percentage. This appears twice in the mobile media query block and may cause unpredictable image height behavior on Android.

**Duplicate CSS rule**
```css
p { margin: 1em 0; }
```
Declared twice in the same `<style>` block. No functional impact but indicates copy-paste assembly without linting.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header — unconfirmed**
QA did not capture the header, attributed to AgentMail relay behavior. CAN-SPAM requires a functional unsubscribe mechanism; if this header is absent at the inbox level, Gmail's one-click unsubscribe UI (which depends on `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, RFC 8058) will also be absent. This increases spam complaint risk.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) — not found**
Same root cause. Without this header, Gmail downgrades the unsubscribe affordance from a one-click action to a redirect flow, which Google treats as a deliverability signal.

**SPF/DKIM/DMARC — unverifiable**
`Authentication-Results` header not captured by relay. Sending domain is `emdeals.michaels.com`. Authentication posture cannot be confirmed from available data. A raw header export from Gmail or MXToolbox verification of `emdeals.michaels.com` is needed.

**Physical address**
Not visible in the truncated source. CAN-SPAM requires a valid postal address in the email body. Assumed present in the footer (not shown) — verify.
