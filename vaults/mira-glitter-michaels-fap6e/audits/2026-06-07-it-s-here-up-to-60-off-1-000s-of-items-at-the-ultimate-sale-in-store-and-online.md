# 👉 It's here! Up to 60% off 1,000s of items at The Ultimate Sale in store and online.
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-07**
## Executive summary

- This is Michaels' tenth promotional email in roughly four days — and the creative is identical to yesterday's online-only 60%-off send. The one genuine news hook is buried at the tail of a long subject: "in store and online" vs. yesterday's "online only." That channel expansion is meaningful information for a subscriber who missed the online window, but it arrives dressed in the same hero, the same BOGO tile stack, and the same Rewards footer the brand has run on loop since June 3.
- Inside the email, the execution is competent. The 60%-off "Ultimate Sale" hero is loud and legible. The BOGO section, the 30%-off "Today Only" urgent strip, and the Rewards $5 earn are all clearly rendered without layout breaks. But the sheer count of competing offers — five or six distinct discount mechanics on one scroll — creates choice paralysis rather than momentum toward a single click.
- The bottom line: there is a real email here, but it's obscured by accumulated send fatigue and a subject line that front-loads a vague "It's here!" instead of leading with the one thing that makes this send different from yesterday's.

## What's working

- The 60% / "ULTIMATE Sale" hero lands immediately — visual hierarchy puts the biggest number up first.
- The "Today Only" 30%-off urgency strip near the top is a genuine time-bound lever, clearly visible.
- BOGO mechanics (Buy 1 Get 1 Free, Buy 2 Get 1 Free) are cleanly broken into scannable tiles with no layout breakage.
- Michaels Rewards "$5 Earn" section is prominent and relevant for loyalty members.
- No visible render bugs — images load, text sits cleanly inside its containers.

## What's weak

- This is the tenth send in ~four days; the creative is materially indistinguishable from the June 6 "online only" send — even the tile order looks identical.
- "It's here!" is weak urgency for the lead of a subject line that could have opened with the actual news: "Now in store, too."
- Five or six competing discount mechanics (60%, 30%, BOGO ×2, $5 Rewards, plus category %-offs) dilute focus and reduce the likelihood of any single click.
- Subject line is ~86 chars — truncated on most mobile clients before reaching "in store and online," which is the differentiating detail.
- No specific product hero or category spotlight; everything is equally weighted, so nothing pulls the eye to a high-margin item.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the subject with the actual news.** "In store and online" is what makes this email different from yesterday — it should be first, not buried after 50 characters. Every mobile preview cuts off before reaching it.
- 2. **Collapse to two or three offer mechanics max.** Pick the BOGO + 60% off and let the 30%-off strip carry urgency. Drop the redundant mid-page percentage tiles or push them far below the fold.
- 3. **Give the hero a specific product or category.** One "Summer crafts starting at $X" hero product with a single CTA would outperform a grid of category badges for click rate.
- 4. **Name the in-store dimension explicitly in the preheader.** If yesterday was online-only and today adds stores, that's the hook — subscribers who came in yesterday should hear "stores are now open for the sale."

- **Subject Alt B:** `The Ultimate Sale hits stores today — up to 60% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `BOGO deals, 30% off full price today only, and $5 Rewards to stack.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop The Ultimate Sale in person or online — 60% off 1,000s of items.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Michaels "Ultimate Sale" Email (2026-06-07)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a table-based HTML build sent via Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC) using the `emdeals.michaels.com` sending infrastructure. The primary technical concerns are a malformed HTML document structure, two HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources, missing compliance headers at the relay layer, and subscriber PII exposed in cleartext URL parameters.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Malformed open-pixel tags**
The open pixel uses non-standard `</custom>` closing tags immediately after the `<img>`:
```html
<img src="https://click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx?..." width="1" height="1" alt="">
</custom>
</custom>
```
`<custom>` is not a valid HTML element. These are likely leftover ESP template artifacts. Benign in most clients but will generate parse warnings and may cause unexpected rendering in strict-mode parsers.

**22 click-redirect links unverifiable**
All CTA links route through `click.emdeals.michaels.com/?qs=...` (opaque base64 query strings). The QA prober correctly skipped these — destination URLs and HTTP status codes are not confirmed. If any redirect target returns a 4xx/5xx, there is no signal in this audit.

**Multiple independent tracking pixels active simultaneously**
Three distinct third-party open/event pixels fire on render:
- `https://na5.thunderhead.com/one/rt/track/ONE-PFSLHYI824-2685/pixel` (Thunderhead ONE)
- `https://nova.collect.igodigital.com/c2/100010552/track_event` (igodigital / SFMC)
- `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true` (Silverpop/IBM)

Redundant pixels inflate open-rate counts unpredictably across platforms.

**Plain-text body is 81% URLs by character count**
QA reports 26,772 of 33,128 plain-text characters are URLs. A well-formed plain-text alternative should be human-readable prose, not a URL dump. This will flag spam filters that score the plain-text part independently.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**DOCTYPE placement is invalid**
The `<!DOCTYPE HTML>` declaration appears *after* a `<table>` block and Coremetrics template comment:
```html
<table ... class="stylingblock-content-wrapper">...</table>
<!-- -->
<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN" ...>
<html>
```
A DOCTYPE must be the first node in the document. This triggers quirks-mode rendering in clients that parse the full stream (Gmail web, Outlook 365). This is a structural defect.

**Invalid CSS value: `max-height: auto`**
In the `@media (max-width: 480px)` block:
```css
img { max-width: 100% !important; height: auto !important; max-height: auto !important; }
```
`max-height: auto` is not a valid CSS value (`auto` is only valid for `height`, not `max-height`). The declaration is silently ignored; intended behavior depends on what `max-height` was meant to cap.

**Two HTTP image sources — may be blocked**
QA confirmed:
- `http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/.../e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` — content image, no alt text
- `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true` — open pixel

Apple Mail (iOS 15+), Gmail, and Outlook 365 block mixed-content HTTP resources when the email is rendered over HTTPS. The content image will fail to load for a significant portion of recipients. Both should be HTTPS.

**Missing alt text on 4 images**
QA flagged 4 images with empty or absent `alt` attributes:
- `e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` (content image — highest impact)
- Thunderhead pixel
- igodigital pixel
- Silverpop open pixel

Tracking pixels legitimately carry `alt=""` (empty string, not absent). The content image (`e323ae89...`) requires descriptive alt text for screen readers and image-off rendering.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Subscriber email exposed as base64 in URL parameters**
Multiple click URLs include `customerEmail=bWlyYS1nbGl0dGVyLW1pY2hhZWxzLWZhcDZlQGV0ZWxsLmFwcA==`. Base64 is encoding, not encryption — this decodes trivially to `mira-glitter-michaels-fap6e@etell.app`. The subscriber's email address is in the URL, logged by every CDN, proxy, and web server in the redirect chain. This is a PII exposure risk.

**SFMC subscriber ID also in URLs**
`sfmc_Sub=6a0b108e8b75a227d95acdb55aac28a1c4325c544a88543985a0a0aa55b1b862` and `emId=c258532227e6840b4eee7a4c0dffb3d5660996c95a1dca0c0011637917ca154f` are present in click URLs. These are hashed/opaque but still constitute linkable identifiers in server logs.

**No broken merge tokens observed** in the truncated source. Personalization substitution appears to have fired correctly.

---

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header absent at relay**
QA: `List-Unsubscribe header not found`. This header is required by Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender policies (enforced since 2024) for senders above 5,000/day threshold. Its absence at the AgentMail relay layer means it may have been stripped in transit — or it was never set. Either way, Gmail may suppress the one-click unsubscribe UI and route to spam more aggressively.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post absent (RFC 8058)**
QA: `List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found`. Required for Gmail/Yahoo one-click unsubscribe compliance alongside `List-Unsubscribe`. Without it, unsubscribe requires a click-through rather than a POST, which Gmail counts against sender reputation.

**Authentication-Results header not captured**
QA: `Authentication-Results header not found`. SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status is unknown from the relay sample. Cannot confirm the sending domain is authenticated. This is the highest-risk compliance gap — unauthenticated bulk mail will be rejected or junked by major providers.

**Physical address and unsubscribe link in body** — cannot confirm from truncated source. Standard for Michaels transactional/promo mail but should be verified in full HTML.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM/Coremetrics tagging present and date-stamped correctly**
At least one URL carries:
```
cm_mmc=EMAIL-_-Circular/EOW/Promo-_-20260607_S2_Circular_UltimateSale_WKND_FF_US-_-HEADER_HOME
cm_mmca2=74861933
```
Campaign ID `20260607` matches today's send date. Tagging convention is consistent with Coremetrics (IBM) attribution.

**Click destinations unverified**
Because all 22 CTAs route through `click.emdeals.michaels.com` redirect URLs that require a live browser session to resolve, landing page alignment (correct sale page, correct UTM passthrough on destination) cannot be confirmed programmatically. Manual spot-check of at least the hero CTA is recommended.
