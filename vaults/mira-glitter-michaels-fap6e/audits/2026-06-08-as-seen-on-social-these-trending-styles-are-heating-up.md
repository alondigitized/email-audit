# As seen on social: These trending styles are heating up! ☀️
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-08**
## Executive summary

- After eleven straight promotional emails from Michaels in roughly four days — almost all anchored by a blunt "up to X% off" subject — this send finally pivots to editorial posture. "As seen on social: These trending styles are heating up!" is the first subject in this run that leads with curation rather than a discount ceiling. That's the right instinct. The problem is the pivot is only skin deep: the email body still stacks multiple percentage-off callouts (a visible 30% banner at the top, at least one 60% section mid-scroll, and what appears to be another deep-cut offer near the footer), which immediately collapses whatever editorial credibility the subject line was building.
- The rendering is clean — no broken images, no layout breaks visible — and the summer color palette (teal, pastel pink, warm yellow) does reinforce the seasonal "trending" narrative. The email is long and section-heavy, with multiple product grids that fragment attention rather than driving toward one clear action. The "as seen on social" framing in the subject promises specific, curated picks; the body delivers a wall of products that could have come from any of the prior ten sends.
- Net judgment: a good instinct, poorly executed. The subject will earn opens from lapsed subscribers who tuned out the discount blitz, but the body won't convert them because it doesn't follow through on the editorial promise.

## What's working

- **Subject-line voice break.** First email in this run that doesn't open with a percentage, which creates enough pattern interruption to earn a second look from fatigued subscribers.
- **Summer seasonal alignment.** Teal and pastel palette, sun emoji, and product selection all visually reinforce the "heating up" framing — the look and the claim are coherent.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no layout breaks visible — a technically sound execution in a complex multi-section format.
- **Multiple price anchors.** Even if it undercuts the editorial tone, the visible 30% and 60% callouts ensure value-seekers who do open have an immediate reason to click.

## What's weak

- **Editorial premise abandoned in the body.** "As seen on social" implies curated hero picks with social context; the body delivers a generic multi-section product grid indistinguishable from the prior sends.
- **No social proof actually shown.** No UGC imagery, TikTok screencaps, Instagram-style framing, or creator attribution visible. The social hook is purely asserted, not demonstrated.
- **Visual hierarchy is fragmented.** Multiple sections compete at roughly equal visual weight — there's no single hero moment where the eye lands and says "this is the offer."
- **Cadence damage.** This is the twelfth Michaels email in about four days; even a fresh creative hook carries real unsubscribe risk at this volume. Nothing in the visible email acknowledges or compensates for the fatigue.
- **Offer clarity is muddled.** At least three different percentage-off callouts are visible across scroll depth, making it hard to identify what the actual lead offer is.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to the social angle in the body.** If the subject promises "as seen on social," the hero section should show it — a product styled like a TikTok shelf clip, a "trending on our feed" badge, or a UGC-style layout. Right now the subject and the body are two different emails.
- 2. **Pick one offer and own it.** Promote the 30% front-and-center; move 60% to a secondary callout or cut it. The multi-discount layout trains subscribers to scroll for the best deal and never act on the first one.
- 3. **Add a visible deadline.** "Trending styles" has no urgency signal. One credible end-date line ("through Sunday only") would lift click rate without requiring a redesign.
- 4. **Compress the email.** Cut at least two product grid sections. Fewer products, each given more visual space, convert better than walls of thumbnails.
- 5. **Reduce send cadence.** Operational, not creative, but this email would land measurably better if it were the sixth send this week rather than the twelfth.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical: DOCTYPE misplaced mid-document.** The `<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN" ...>` declaration appears *inside* an already-open `<table><tr><td>` element, after the Coremetrics wrapper block. **Two `</custom>` closing tags with no matching opening tag** appear immediately after the open-tracking pixel `<img>` — non-standard elements that may trigger parse errors in strict clients.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent** — not captured by the AgentMail relay. Google and Yahoo's 2024 bulk-sender requirements mandate this header for lists >5,000/day. Its absence risks Gmail bulk-folder placement and may trigger enforcement actions.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent** — RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe not supported or not relayed. Required by Gmail/Yahoo for bulk senders; without it, Gmail renders its own "unsubscribe" link that bypasses the ESP's suppression list.

**Authentication-Results not captured** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown for this message as received through the AgentMail relay. Cannot confirm alignment. Sending domain is `emdeals.michaels.com`; if DMARC policy is `p=reject` or `p=quarantine` and alignment fails, deliverability impact is severe.

**Plain-text part is 82% URLs** (28,672 of 35,087 chars) — the multipart/alternative plain-text version consists almost entirely of raw redirect URLs. This is a known spam-filter signal and also renders as unusable for plain-text email clients.
