# Up to 50% off! ☀️ Shop Screen Free Summer Savings for deals on creative supplies ➡️
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-14**
## Executive summary

- This is Michaels' "Screen Free Summer Savings" send — a thin pivot from the relentless "Ultimate Sale" drum-beat that has dominated the past four or five days. The body is the same dense promotional grid the brand has been running all week, with overlapping discount tiers (25% off at the top, "up to 50%" scattered through the product blocks, 60% in the Ultimate Sale mid-section) that compete instead of stack. The "Screen Free Summer" hook in the subject is the most interesting creative idea here, but it evaporates the moment you open the email — nothing inside connects to that concept explicitly.
- The Michaels Rewards "Earn $5" block at the bottom is a nice loyalty signal, and the email renders cleanly. But after seven-plus nearly identical sends in four days, subscriber fatigue is the primary story. The June 13 "END SOON" send already played the urgency-close card; this send can't credibly escalate further, so it just… repeats.
- Clear judgment: this email is competent but fatigued. The "Screen Free Summer" angle deserved its own send with a cohesive creative concept, not a subject-line costume over the same grid.

## What's working

- **Loyalty block is present and specific** — "Earn $5" Michaels Rewards CTA near the bottom rewards members visibly without requiring a scroll to fine print.
- **Seasonal hook in subject** — "Screen Free Summer Savings" is a fresher angle than "Ultimate Sale" for the fifth day running; it's the one new idea in this send.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no layout breaks visible.
- **Tiered price points visible** — the $10/$15/$25 gift-style grid gives concrete anchors that help impulse decisions.

## What's weak

- **"Screen Free Summer" is a ghost** — the subject promises a thematic angle; the body delivers the same generic product grid from every other send this week with no narrative tie-in.
- **Discount stack is confusing** — 25% off at top, 50% in hero, 60% mid-body, and $10/$15/$25 tiers all in one scroll makes it hard to know what the actual offer is.
- **Subject is far too long** — at roughly 78 characters, it will be truncated on most mobile clients; the interesting "Screen Free Summer Savings" phrase gets cut.
- **Cadence is broken** — this is the seventh or eighth near-identical send in four days; the June 13 "END SOON" email already played the urgency close, so this send has nowhere to escalate.
- **No single hero product** — the grid format diffuses attention; there's nothing to click *toward*, only a wall of deals to click *through*.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Build the "Screen Free Summer" concept inside the email** — lead with one hero category (watercolor, journaling, outdoor crafts) that maps to the "screen free" lifestyle angle. One product family, one CTA, one story. If the concept isn't going to land in the body, cut it from the subject.
- 2. **Shorten the subject to under 50 characters** — front-load the offer or the hook, drop the tail.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Screen Free Summer: 50% off creative supplies ☀️`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Put down the phone. Pick up 50% off. 🎨`
- 3. **Write a preheader that adds information** — the current send (based on the render) appears to let junk or repetition leak into the preview slot.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Watercolors, journals, kits and more — no screen required.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus earn $5 Rewards on your next order.`
- 4. **Pause or collapse the cadence** — one send per day during a sale is already aggressive; at 7+ sends in four days the unsubscribe cost is real. Batch remaining sale days into one "final hours" send rather than daily blasts.
- 5. **Pick one discount tier and lead with it** — 25% vs. 50% vs. 60% in the same scroll is noise. If the hero offer is 50%, say 50% and let the other tiers exist as secondary copy.

### 1. Technical Summary

The email renders functional HTML but carries structural anomalies (DOCTYPE mid-document, `</custom>` tags, duplicate CSS rules), two HTTP-served image assets that modern clients will block, and four untagged images that fail accessibility checks. Compliance header capture is uncertain due to the AgentMail relay layer.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**21 click-tracking links skipped** by the HTTP probe — all route through `click.emdeals.michaels.com`. Destination URL health is unverified for this send.

**PII in click URLs** — `customerEmail` parameter in nav links is base64-encoded but not encrypted:
```
customerEmail=bWlyYS1nbGl0dGVyLW1pY2hhZWxzLWZhcDZlQGV0ZWxsLmFwcA==
```
Decodes to the recipient's full email address. Base64 is encoding, not protection — this value is trivially readable in browser history, server logs, and any intermediate proxy.

**Four distinct tracking pixel vendors** firing on open:
- `click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx` (Acoustic/Coremetrics)
- `na5.thunderhead.com` (Thunderhead ONE)
- `nova.collect.igodigital.com` (iGoDigital/Salesforce MC)
- `svopn.e.michaels.com` (SilverPop)

Four concurrent open-tracking systems is unusual; may indicate unconsolidated ESP migrations leaving stale pixels in the template.

**Plain-text version is 81.6% URLs** (28,383 / 34,767 chars) — well above the 70% threshold that triggers spam classifier flags.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**DOCTYPE is mid-document.** The `<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN">` declaration appears after a `<table>` and two `</custom>` closing tags. Browsers/clients that encounter a DOCTYPE after markup begin will trigger quirks mode, which can break layout in Outlook and older webmail renderers.

**`</custom>` tags** (two instances) immediately precede the DOCTYPE — not valid HTML 4.01 or HTML5. These are rendering no-ops in most clients but signal template injection artifacts left in production markup.

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta** — disables pinch-to-zoom on mobile. WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 violation; also fails Apple's mail accessibility guidelines.

**Two HTTP image sources** (not HTTPS) — will be blocked or downgraded by Gmail, Outlook 365, and Apple Mail:
- `http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/…/e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` — content image, will render broken
- `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true…` — SilverPop open-tracking pixel, will silently fail

**Four images missing `alt` text:**
| Asset | Issue |
|---|---|
| `e323ae89…f33897013cd1.jpg` | Content image, no alt — screen readers skip it entirely |
| Thunderhead pixel (`/pixel?uri=outbound…`) | No alt="" (should be `alt=""` explicitly) |
| iGoDigital pixel (`track_event?payload=…`) | No alt="" |
| SilverPop pixel (`sv_emopen=true…`) | No alt="" |

Tracking pixels must have `alt=""` (empty, not absent) to be valid and suppress screen reader announcements.

**Duplicate CSS rule** — `p { margin: 1em 0; }` declared twice in the `<head>` style block. Minor, but indicates copy-paste template debt.
