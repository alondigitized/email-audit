# Up to 50% off creative supplies for less screen time, more crafting. ✨
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-16**
## Executive summary

- This is Michaels' third bite at the "Screen Free Summer / up to 50% off creative supplies" apple in 48 hours. The June 14 send ("Up to 50% off! ☀️ Shop Screen Free Summer Savings…") scored 7/10 in this audit trail; this email is effectively the same campaign running back through the list with a tweaked subject and a marginal visual refresh. The 25% off coupon sits at the top, which gives the email a clear entry point, but below that it collapses into the same dense promotional grid Michaels has been running for weeks — eight competing sections, five separate discount tiers, a patriotic July 4th block, and a birthday party sidebar, all fighting for the same scroll.
- The craft-supply positioning is real and the rewards tie-in (Earn $5) works. But the email reads as a bulk-blast artifact, not a targeted creative-lifestyle send. There is no single compelling hero product, no urgency deadline, no personalization, and no creative voice — just stacked discount banners. For a subscriber who got the near-identical June 14 email, this feels like inbox clutter.
- Judgment: functional but fatiguing. The cadence problem is starting to erode the individual email's effectiveness. This is an engagement-safe send that likely generates some click-throughs from deal-hunters but leaves creative shoppers unmoved.

## What's working

- **25% off coupon is the clear entry point.** It sits at the top and the eye lands there first — the hierarchy at the fold works even if everything below it doesn't.
- **Michaels Rewards "Earn $5" tie-in** is visible in the lower section and rewards members have a concrete reason to keep reading.
- **Multiple discount tiers cover different budget intents** — $9.99 price point, 30% off, 50% off — so different shopper mindsets have at least one anchor.
- **Patriotic/USA block** is timely for a mid-June send; the July 4th runway is 2–3 weeks out and this positions the brand correctly for seasonal decor shoppers.

## What's weak

- **Same concept as June 14, almost word-for-word.** "Up to 50% off creative supplies" + "Screen Free Summer" was the subject of two days ago. Repeat exposure at this cadence signals to subscribers that the offer never actually ends, which kills urgency.
- **No single hero product.** Eight grid sections mean no product earns the spotlight. The scroll reads like a category browse, not a curated recommendation.
- **Subject line is 70 characters — over mobile truncation threshold.** The meaningful part ("Up to 50% off creative supplies") is in the first 30 chars; the "for less screen time, more crafting" tail gets cut on most phones and adds no informational value.
- **No credible deadline anywhere visible.** "Up to 50% off" with no end date is a standing offer, not a reason to act today.
- **Dense grid dilutes focus.** Birthday party supplies, July 4th decor, Screen Free Summer, Cricut pricing, and Rewards all compete. A subscriber interested in one loses thread of the others.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one hero and build the whole email around it.** The $9.99 price-point item or a single Cricut/Cricut-adjacent accessory at 50% off would out-perform eight competing grid tiles. Give it a full-width hero image, one CTA, one price.
- 2. **Add a credible end date.** Even "Sale ends Sunday" does more work than "up to 50% off" with no clock. It converts browsers into buyers.
- 3. **Retire the Screen Free Summer concept for 48 hours minimum.** Running the same campaign theme June 14 → June 16 trains subscribers to ignore it. Swap in the July 4th angle as the lead concept and tease Screen Free Summer as a secondary section.
- 4. **Tighten the subject line.** Trim the "for less screen time, more crafting" tagline — it reads as filler and gets cut on mobile anyway. Use those chars for an urgency signal or a product name.
- **Subject Alt A:** `25% off + up to 50% off — Screen Free Summer savings`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Craft more this summer: 50% off supplies ends soon`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus a $9.99 deal and a Rewards bonus — see what's on now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your 25% coupon is inside, plus July 4th decor from 30% off.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Michaels Email (2026-06-16)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a Salesforce Marketing Cloud / Coremetrics-tracked send with functional responsive CSS, but it has two significant infrastructure gaps: missing one-click unsubscribe headers that violate current bulk-sender requirements from Gmail/Yahoo, and multiple HTTP (non-HTTPS) image assets that will be blocked or proxied by modern email clients. Authentication status is unverifiable through the AgentMail relay.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**22 click-tracking URLs unverifiable** — all links route through `click.emdeals.michaels.com` redirect domain. Destination health cannot be confirmed; any broken product landing pages would be silent.

**Header logo link is commented out in source:**
```html
<!-- <td> <a href="https://www.michaels.com?cm_mmc=...HEADER_HOME...
```
The logo is present as an image but the wrapping `<a>` tag is inside an HTML comment, making the logo unclickable. This is the highest-value tap target on mobile.

**`cm_mmca2` parameter is empty** in the header logo URL:
```
cm_mmca2=&mi_u=6a0b108e...
```
An empty Coremetrics dimension will cause attribution gaps in campaign reporting.

**Multiple third-party tracking services fire on open:**
- `click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx` — primary ESP pixel
- `na5.thunderhead.com/one/rt/track/ONE-PFSLHYI824-2685/pixel` — Thunderhead/One CDM
- `nova.collect.igodigital.com/c2/100010552/track_event` — iGoDigital/Marketing Cloud Personalization
- `svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847` — SilverPop open tracker

Four separate third-party systems receiving open events increases data-relay latency and multiplies privacy/consent surface area.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**DOCTYPE is HTML 4.01 Transitional** — this is the legacy email standard and functionally acceptable, but it precludes some modern CSS features.

**Invalid HTML tags in body** — immediately after the primary open pixel:
```html
<img src="https://click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx?...">
</custom>
</custom>
```
`</custom>` is not a valid HTML element. Some parsers will handle this gracefully; strict-mode parsers or security scanners may flag or strip surrounding content.

**Two HTTP (non-HTTPS) content/tracking images:**

| Asset | Issue |
|---|---|
| `http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/...e323ae89...jpg` | Content image on HTTP — will be blocked by Gmail image proxy rewrite or flagged as mixed content |
| `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847...` | SilverPop tracking pixel on HTTP — same risk |

Gmail rewrites all `http://` image sources through its proxy; the rewrite changes the URL and may break SilverPop's open-tracking fingerprint.

**Four images missing `alt` text:**

| Image | Type | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| `e323ae89-...jpg` | Apparent content image | Screen readers get nothing; images-off renders silently |
| Thunderhead pixel | Tracking pixel | Should be `alt=""` (empty string, not absent) |
| iGoDigital `track_event` pixel | Tracking pixel | Same |
| SilverPop `svopn` pixel | Tracking pixel | Same |

For tracking pixels, the fix is `alt=""` (explicitly empty), not a description — the attribute must be present.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Email address exposed in plaintext to third-party services.** Two separate tracking pixels transmit the recipient email in URL parameters:

```
https://nova.collect.igodigital.com/c2/100010552/track_event?payload={"user_info":{"email":"mira-glitter-micha...
```
```
https://na5.thunderhead.com/.../pixel?uri=outbound%3A%2F%2Femailpixel%2F?emailaddress%3DMIR...
```

The iGoDigital payload sends a JSON-encoded email address as a GET parameter — visible in server logs and any intermediate proxy. The Thunderhead pixel passes it as a query string (`emailaddress=MIR...` truncated in capture but clearly a PII parameter).

**Subscriber ID duplicated across two parameters** in click URLs:
```
sfmc_Sub=6a0b108e8b75a227...
mi_u=6a0b108e8b75a227...
```
These appear identical, suggesting redundant tracking identifiers from SFMC and iGoDigital running in parallel without deduplication.

**No unfired or broken merge tokens detected** in the visible HTML — personalization fields appear to have rendered correctly.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not captured** — QA found no evidence of this header through the AgentMail relay. Gmail and Yahoo require this header for all bulk senders (>5k/day). Its absence risks inbox placement and bulk-sender policy violations.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found** — One-click unsubscribe (the `mailto:` + POST mechanism) is now a hard requirement for Gmail bulk senders as of February 2024. Without it, Gmail may suppress delivery or move mail to spam.

**CAN-SPAM physical address** — not visible in the truncated source; cannot confirm presence. Must appear in every commercial email.

**Authentication-Results header missing from relay capture** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown. If DKIM is failing on the `emdeals.michaels.com` subdomain, delivery to major ISPs would be degraded. This is a relay observability gap, not necessarily a sending failure, but it should be confirmed.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Logo link is non-functional** (see §2) — the most common "home" navigation tap target is dead.

**Coremetrics (`cm_mmc`) is used instead of standard GA4 UTM parameters.** This is a deliberate Michaels platform choice, not a bug, but it means Google Analytics will not attribute this campaign without a parallel UTM strategy or GA4 import.

**Campaign ID in `cm_mmc`:** `DCE-_-20260616_S2_US_TempA_DCE_FF_US` — date-stamped, suggesting this is a one-time send, consistent with the email send date (2026-06-16). No issues found with the ID format itself.

**Plain text version is >70% URLs** (31,571 / 38,265 chars) — the plain-text alternative is effectively a URL dump. Spam filters that weight plain-text URL density (SpamAssassin `URI_COUNT`, Barracuda) will penalize this. Plain text should contain readable summary content with links inline, not a list of raw tracking URLs.

---
