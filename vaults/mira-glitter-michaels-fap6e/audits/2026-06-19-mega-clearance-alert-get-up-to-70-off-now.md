# Mega Clearance alert! 🚨 Get up to 70% off now!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- This is Michaels leaning hardest into discounting they've ever gone in this audit window — 70% off is the biggest number in the recent cycle and the "Mega Clearance Event" hero earns that billing. The problem is Michaels then buries the lead by padding around it with five separate modules: a site makeover announcement, a Lemax Spooky Town reveal (in mid-June), a 40% off coupon, a Rewards earn callout, and a savings checklist. The clearance narrative never gets to breathe.
- Coming off six consecutive heavy-promo sends between June 14–18 — Screen Free Summer, Ultimate Sale, Jonathan Adler collab, Party Shop — this is the seventh promotional email in under a week from the same sender. The "alert! 🚨" subject framing is doing its best to cut through that fatigue, but it reads more desperate than urgent, especially when no actual deadline appears anywhere in the body.
- The Lemax Spooky Town placement is the single weirdest choice here. Collectors will recognize it and some will click — but wedging it between a clearance event and a 40% coupon, in June, is incoherent. That's a standalone send with its own story, not a mid-scroll pivot.

## What's working

- **70% off is the boldest number in the cycle** — frames and spring items at 70% is genuine news and the hero callout makes it visible immediately.
- **Category CTAs are sharp** — "Up to 70% off Frames ›", "Up to 40% off Fabrics ›", "Up to 70% off Spring Items ›" give motivated shoppers a direct entry point.
- **"Shop clearance ›" button is unambiguous** — no mystery in the primary CTA.
- **Lemax Spooky Town will land for collectors** — this is a real annual event Michaels fans know; the early-access framing has genuine pull for that segment.
- **Rewards $5 earn is low-effort but visible** — loyalty callout doesn't require a click and reinforces membership value.

## What's weak

- **"Guess who got a makeover?" delays the clearance hook** — the site redesign module is the first thing below the header; it eats scroll before the hero lands.
- **No expiration date anywhere in the body** — "now!" is not a deadline; every urgency signal evaporates without a "Ends Sunday" anchor.
- **The 40% coupon and 70% clearance coexist without explanation** — are they stackable? On competing products? The ambiguity creates hesitation mid-scroll.
- **Spooky Town in June 19 breaks the seasonal logic** — it's early even by Michaels standards and interrupts the clearance narrative mid-email for subscribers who aren't in collector mode.
- **Subject spam signals** — 🚨 + "alert!" + "NOW!" stacked in one line; individually forgivable, together they signal volume-promo desperation.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the makeover module or drop it to footer.** The clearance is the lead; nothing should precede it above the fold except the header.
- 2. **Add a hard expiration date to the hero.** "Ends Sunday, June 22" transforms a vague claim into a real reason to click today. This is the single highest-lift change.
- 3. **Pull the 🚨 from the subject line.** "Mega Clearance: Up to 70% off — ends Sunday" is cleaner and the 70% number is alarming enough on its own.
- 4. **Surface one clearance hero product with old/new pricing** — e.g., "Was $29.99 → Now $8.99 — Photo Frames" — rather than just category banners. Price comparisons outperform percentage claims.
- 5. **Move Spooky Town to its own send next week** — it's a real event that deserves a dedicated story, not a 200px module competing with frames and fabric.
- 6. **Clarify coupon + clearance stacking** — one line of copy ("Coupon valid on regular-price items only") removes the friction and makes both offers feel cleaner.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Mega Clearance: Up to 70% off ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your 70% off clearance starts now — frames, fabric, spring`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Frames at 70% off. Fabric at 40% off. This weekend only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Spring items slashed — shop clearance before it's gone.`

## Technical Audit — Michaels "Mega Clearance" Email (2026-06-19)

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has a misplaced `<!DOCTYPE>` declaration that will cause parsing failures in strict-mode clients, two HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources that major clients will block, and missing `List-Unsubscribe` headers that put one-click unsubscribe compliance at risk. SPF/DKIM status is unverifiable from the relay capture.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Tracking redirect domain**: All 24 CTA links are wrapped through `click.emdeals.michaels.com`. QA skipped HTTP probes on all of them — destination URLs and their status codes are unverified.

**Multiple open-pixel vendors in a single send** — three separate tracking beacons fire on open:
- `click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx` (primary ESP)
- `nova.collect.igodigital.com/c2/100010552/track_event` (Salesforce Marketing Cloud / iGoDigital)
- `svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847` (SilverPop/Watson Campaign)

Each adds a round-trip on load. The `svopn.e.michaels.com` beacon fires over HTTP (flagged below).

**PII in query strings**: The commented-out header link exposes a base64-encoded subscriber email as `customerEmail=bWlyYS1nbGl0...` and a raw Salesforce subscriber ID as `sfmc_Sub=6a0b108e...` in the URL. If that pattern exists in live links behind the click wrapper, it puts subscriber PII in server logs, referrer headers, and browser history.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Malformed document structure — `<!DOCTYPE>` misplaced**: The HTML source shows a `<table>` and a `<!--MASTER_Coremetrics_SLPH_Content-->` block *before* the `<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC ...>` declaration. Doctype must be the absolute first line. Email clients that enforce this (Outlook, some webmail) will fall back to quirks mode or drop the doctype entirely, causing layout shifts.

**Unclosed/invalid ESP template tags**: Two `</custom>` closing tags appear in the open-pixel `<div>` block with no matching opening tag. These are likely un-stripped ESP template placeholders and will appear as orphaned markup in some parsers.

**Invalid CSS value — `max-height: auto`**: The mobile media query sets `max-height: auto !important` on `.fluid` and `.fluid-centered`. `auto` is not a valid value for `max-height`; correct values are `none`, a length, or a percentage. This is a no-op at best and a parse error in strict validators.

**5 images missing `alt` text** (QA-confirmed):
| Image | src |
|---|---|
| Product image | `...31528c47-4196-49bb-a540-b78900f21a07.jpg` |
| Product image | `...e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` |
| Thunderhead pixel | `na5.thunderhead.com/one/rt/...` |
| iGoDigital pixel | `nova.collect.igodigital.com/c2/...` |
| SilverPop beacon | `svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=...` |

Decorative tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` explicitly; product images must have descriptive alt text for screen readers and image-off rendering.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent** (QA): The AgentMail relay did not capture this header. Without it, Gmail and Yahoo Mail cannot display their native unsubscribe UI, and bulk-sender compliance with Google/Yahoo's 2024 requirements is not met.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) absent** (QA): One-click unsubscribe is not supported. This is required for Gmail bulk senders (>5k/day threshold) as of February 2024.

**`Authentication-Results` header absent** (QA): SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status cannot be confirmed from this capture. Given the sending domain is `emdeals.michaels.com` (a subdomain), alignment with the `michaels.com` DMARC policy needs verification.

**Plain-text body is 73.6% URLs** (19,057 of 25,887 chars): This ratio is a known spam-filter signal. The plain-text version should contain readable copy, not just link dumps.

CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe mechanism were not visible in the truncated HTML but are assumed to be in the footer (not confirmed).

All CTAs are wrapped in `click.emdeals.michaels.com` redirects, so destination URL parameters cannot be directly inspected. The one link visible in an HTML comment uses **Coremetrics** (`cm_mmc=EMAIL-_-Circular/EOW/Promo-_-...`) rather than standard UTM parameters — this is expected for IBM/Acoustic-tracked properties but means GA4 / non-Coremetrics attribution will be blind to this traffic unless cross-tagging is in place.
