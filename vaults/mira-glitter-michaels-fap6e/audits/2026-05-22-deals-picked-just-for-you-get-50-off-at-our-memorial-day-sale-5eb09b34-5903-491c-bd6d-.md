# Deals picked just for you: Get 50% off at our Memorial Day Sale.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- This is Michaels' Memorial Day sale email — the brand's tenth send in roughly eight days. The "deals picked just for you" framing in the subject promises personalization, but the body is organized around category-affinity blocks ("You'll love these Party Deals," "You'll love these Summer Deals," "You'll love these Frame Deals") rather than any individual user signal. That distinction matters: the approach is smarter than a straight dump, but it still resolves as a five-scroll kitchen-sink email, not a curated one.
- The 30% off single-item coupon at the top is the clearest hook, but it competes immediately with at least six other discount tiers (40%, 50%, 60%, BOGO, category deals) stacked below. The hierarchy problem is real — there's no visual anchor that says "start here." The Rewards module at the bottom is correctly placed but won't be seen by most recipients who don't make it through five screens of competing offers.
- Verdict: a competent but exhausted promotional send. The personalization language in the subject isn't matched by the body. After ten emails in eight days, this one will slide into ignore territory for most subscribers.

## What's working

- **Memorial Day timing is credible.** The patriotic banner and holiday framing are on-calendar and appropriate.
- **30% off coupon is the clearest single-offer anchor** — it's visible above the fold and stands apart from the category blocks.
- **Category segmentation ("You'll love these…") is a reasonable personalization gesture** — better than a flat product dump.
- **Michaels Rewards "Earn $5" module is present** — loyalty reinforcement at the close is the right structural choice.
- **No visible render bugs** — text renders cleanly, no broken images or layout breakage visible in the screenshot.

## What's weak

- **"Deals picked just for you" is hollow.** The body is a broad multi-category blast; the personalization promise in the subject is unearned.
- **Too many discount tiers competing simultaneously** — 30%, 40%, 50%, 60%, BOGO, and up-to-X offers all appear within a single scroll session. The reader can't prioritize.
- **No dominant CTA.** Every section has its own small action, which means no single click path gets emphasis.
- **Cadence is now a liability.** This is the 10th email in 8 days. The subject's "Sale starts TODAY!" pattern ran yesterday (May 22, same-day sale launch email). Sending two Memorial Day sale emails on the same day erodes urgency.
- **Frame deals and candy/baking feel off-persona** if the top-of-funnel hook is party supplies — no coherent thread connects the category blocks.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Honor the "picked for you" promise.** If the category blocks truly reflect purchase/browse history, lead with one category only and cut the others. A one-category email that matches what I actually bought converts better than six categories I have to parse.
- 2. **Reduce to three discount tiers max.** 30% off one item + one category deal + Rewards offer is enough. More than three tiers creates decision paralysis and visually flattens everything to the same importance.
- 3. **Give the primary CTA a button with specific copy** — "Shop Party Deals" or "Grab My 30% Off" beats any variation of "Shop Now."
- 4. **Move the loyalty module higher.** "Earn $5 this weekend" is a tangible reason to act — burying it below five category blocks wastes it.
- 5. **Separate same-day sends.** The May 22 "Sale starts TODAY!" email and this one landed the same day. One email per day is the floor; two Memorial Day sale emails on the same day trains subscribers to ignore both.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your 30% off coupon — Memorial Day Weekend`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Memorial Day deals in your categories (50% off inside)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Party supplies, frames, and summer faves — all on sale through Monday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus earn $5 in Rewards on your next purchase this weekend.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Michaels Memorial Day Sale Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has two blocking infrastructure issues: non-HTTPS image sources that will be suppressed by Gmail and Outlook, and missing `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers that violate current Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender mandates. Several lower-severity issues compound deliverability risk.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**PII in landing page URL parameters**
The header logo link (visible in HTML comment block) appends `customerEmail=bWlyYS1nbGl0dGVyLW1pY2hhZWxzLWZhcDZlQGV0ZWxsLmFwcA==` — a base64-encoded recipient email — directly to `https://www.michaels.com`. Base64 is not encryption; the value decodes trivially. This address is also repeated in `mi_u` and `sfmc_Sub` parameters. Any browser history, server log, or referrer header on the destination site captures the recipient's address in cleartext.

**Empty UTM segment parameter**
`cm_mmca2=` is present but empty in the header logo URL. If this field is used for campaign segmentation in Coremetrics/Adobe Analytics reporting, it will produce unattributed traffic.

**21 click-tracking links unprobed**
All CTA links route through `click.emdeals.michaels.com` redirects; the QA prober correctly skipped them. No broken destination URLs can be confirmed or ruled out from this audit.

**Multiple overlapping open-tracking pixels**
Four separate open-tracking systems fire on load:
- Coremetrics: `click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx?...`
- Thunderhead ONE: `https://na5.thunderhead.com/one/rt/track/ONE-PFSLHYI824-2685/pixel`
- IGo Digital (SFMC): `https://nova.collect.igodigital.com/c2/100010552/track_event`
- Silverpop: `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847` (HTTP — see §3)

Four pixels is not inherently a bug, but any one firing increases the mixed-content surface.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Non-HTTPS image sources — will be blocked**
Two sources use `http://`:
- `http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/lib/fe4015707564057d701173/m/1/e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` — a content image; Gmail strips mixed-content images since 2023.
- `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true&sv_svemi=...` — Silverpop open-tracking pixel.

Both will be blocked or proxied/broken in Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail. The content image is the higher-severity issue — it will render as a broken image for a significant share of recipients.

**Missing alt text on content image**
`e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` has no `alt` attribute. Screen readers will skip it silently; if images are disabled, no fallback text is shown. The three tracking pixels also lack `alt=""` — technically they should carry empty alt to avoid screen-reader announcement of image URLs.

**Malformed HTML artifacts**
- `</custom> </custom>` appears immediately after the open-tracking pixel `<img>` — non-standard tags, likely ESP template remnants that were not stripped before send.
- The opening comment block reads `<!-- <!-- -->` — a malformed nested comment. While most parsers recover, it is a sign of template stitching errors.

**Duplicate CSS rule**
`p { margin: 1em 0; }` is declared twice in `<style>` blocks. Not a rendering bug in practice, but indicates copy-paste template assembly.

**Invalid CSS value**
`max-height: auto !important;` (mobile media query, `.header img` and `.photo img`) — `auto` is not a valid value for `max-height`. Browsers silently ignore it; the intended effect (removing a fixed height constraint on mobile) likely still works via the `height: auto` rule, but it should be removed.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

The `customerEmail` base64 value decodes to a plausible etell.app persona address, so token resolution ran — but see §2 for the PII concern this introduces.

---

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header absent**
QA confirmed no `List-Unsubscribe` header was captured by the AgentMail relay. Gmail and Yahoo have required this header for all bulk senders (>5,000/day) since February 2024. Its absence increases the probability of junking and can result in delivery throttling.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post absent (RFC 8058)**
One-click unsubscribe (the `POST` variant) is also required by the same Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy. Without it, Gmail cannot surface the one-click unsubscribe button in its UI, pushing users toward the spam button instead.

**Authentication-Results header not captured**
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status is unknown. The sending domain is `emdeals.michaels.com`; authentication should be verifiable, but the AgentMail relay did not surface these headers. If DKIM is misconfigured on the subdomain, deliverability will degrade.

**Plain-text body is 82% URLs**
QA measured 30,600 of 37,271 plain-text chars as URLs. Spam filters (SpamAssassin, Proofpoint) treat high URL-density plain-text parts as a signal. This is typical of auto-generated plain-text from HTML-heavy emails, but the ratio is high enough to warrant attention.

**CAN-SPAM physical address** — not verifiable from truncated source. Assumed present in footer (standard for Michaels). No finding.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Tracking parameter format: Coremetrics, not UTM**
Links use `cm_mmc` / `cm_mmca2` (IBM Coremetrics / Adobe Analytics) rather than standard `utm_source` / `utm_campaign`. This is intentional for their stack, but `cm_mmca2=` is empty (see §2), which will produce a gap in campaign segmentation reports.

**Date in campaign code is correct**
`cm_mmc=EMAIL-_-DCE-_-20260522_...` matches today's send date (2026-05-22). No stale date issue.

**Email address passed to landing page**
`customerEmail=<base64>` is appended to `https://www.michaels.com`. If the site uses this for pre-filling a login or personalization, the value is readable in GA4/Adobe event logs, browser history, and any third-party tag on the page. This is a data hygiene and potential privacy-regulation issue (CCPA) beyond the email itself.
