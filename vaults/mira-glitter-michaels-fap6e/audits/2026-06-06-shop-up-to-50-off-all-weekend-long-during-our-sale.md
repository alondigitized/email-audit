# Shop up to 50% off all weekend long during our sale! 🛒
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-06**
## Executive summary

- This is Michaels' eighth promotional email in three days — and it shows. The subject line "Shop up to 50% off all weekend long during our sale!" is essentially the June 5th send ("Our sale is still going on with up to 50% off deals!") re-worded by a thesaurus. Same discount ceiling, same "our sale" framing, same grid of cascading deals inside. If the June 5th email was defensive, this one is exhausted.
- The render itself is technically competent — logo loads, images display, no broken blocks — but the layout is a fire hose: eight or more distinct promotional modules stacked vertically, each with its own percentage and CTA. The 30% off hero contradicts the 50% subject promise above the fold, the Party Shop LIVE event promotion (last seen in the June 4th invite) reappears mid-scroll, and the discount range quietly climbs to 60% by the time you hit the bottom. There is no single offer this email is asking you to act on.
- The only genuine addition here is the Michaels Rewards "Earn $5" block at the bottom — a loyalty hook that adds mild retention value. It's buried too deep to matter.

## What's working

- **Brand render is clean** — logo, images, and product photography all load without gaps or breakage.
- **Loyalty block present** — Michaels Rewards "Earn $5" is visible, which adds a member retention signal even if it's at the bottom.
- **Weekend urgency framing** — "all weekend long" at least implies a finite window, which is better than the timeless "our sale is going on" from yesterday.
- **Product variety** — candy-making, party supplies, floral, and kids' craft categories are all shown, covering broad audience bases.

## What's weak

- **Hero discount (30%) undercuts the subject line (50%)** — the first number a reader sees in the email body is lower than what the subject promised. That's a credibility gap.
- **Eight modules, zero primary offer** — no single CTA dominates; the eye has nowhere to land and the email demands the reader do the editorial work.
- **Party Shop LIVE recycled** — this event block appeared in the June 4th invite email; its presence here two days later with no context (is it a replay? a new date?) just adds clutter.
- **Cadence is unsustainable** — eight emails in three days from the same sender, with nearly identical offers, is inbox-fatigue in real time.
- **Discount range (30% to 60%)** across the same email obscures what the real deal is and reads as fine-print bait-and-switch.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill six of the eight modules.** Pick one offer — the highest, most defensible one — and build the entire email around it. One hero, one CTA, one reason to click. Every additional module after the third drives net click rate down.
- 2. **Align the hero number with the subject line.** If the subject says 50% off, the hero must say 50% off. The 30% opener actively undermines trust at first glance.
- 3. **Drop the Party Shop LIVE block** or replace it with something fresh. Recycling the June 4th event two days later without context wastes real estate and signals lazy assembly.
- 4. **Surface the Rewards hook to the hero.** "Earn $5 in Rewards on every $50 you spend this weekend" is a more compelling frame than yet another percentage-off — and it's already in the email; just move it up.
- 5. **Rest the cadence.** One email, not two, on a weekend sale is the correct send count.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Earn $5 in Rewards + up to 50% off — this weekend only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `50% off ends Sunday — here's what to grab`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your Rewards points are waiting. Shop crafts, party, and more at half price.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `This weekend: 50% off the good stuff. Earn $5 in Rewards while you shop.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Michaels "Up to 50% Off" Weekend Sale Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has a structural malformation (content precedes the DOCTYPE declaration) and carries two HTTP-only image sources that will be blocked by modern clients. Compliance headers are absent at the relay level, and four images lack alt text — including one product image.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**22 click-tracking links unverifiable**
All outbound links are wrapped in `click.emdeals.michaels.com` redirects. QA skipped HTTP probing on all 22, so broken destinations cannot be confirmed. Manual spot-checks recommended before send.

**Multiple redundant open-tracking pixels**
Three separate analytics fires on open:
- `click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx?W6JNDB2T...` (Acoustic/Silverpop)
- `na5.thunderhead.com/one/rt/track/ONE-PFSLHYI824-2685/pixel` (Thunderhead ONE)
- `nova.collect.igodigital.com/c2/100010552/track_event` (SFMC iGoDigital)
- `svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true` (Selligent)

Four systems firing on open is unusual and increases payload size. Two of the four pixels use HTTP (see §3).

**Coremetrics block appears empty**
```html
<!--MASTER_Coremetrics_SLPH_Content-->
<!-- Email content lookups code -->
```
The Coremetrics/Acoustic content injection block contains no rendered output. If this is supposed to inject personalized content or tracking tags dynamically, it did not fire for this send.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**DOCTYPE placed after live HTML — malformed document**
A `<table>` element and content appear *before* the `<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN">` declaration. Conforming parsers must reset state at DOCTYPE; some email clients will treat pre-DOCTYPE content as a separate fragment and render it twice or discard it.

```html
<table ... role="presentation" ...>
  <tr><td><!--MASTER_Coremetrics_SLPH_Content--></td></tr>
</table>
<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC ...>  ← DOCTYPE appears here, too late
```

**Stray `</custom>` closing tags**
Two orphaned `</custom>` tags appear immediately after the open-tracking pixel with no corresponding opening tag. Non-standard element; parsers will emit parse errors that may corrupt surrounding table layout in strict-mode clients.

```html
<img src="https://click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx?..." width="1" height="1" alt="">
</custom>
</custom>
```

**Two image sources use HTTP (not HTTPS)**
Both will be blocked by Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook.com under mixed-content rules:
- `http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/lib/fe4015707564057d701173/m/1/e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` — appears to be a content image, not just a pixel
- `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true&sv_svemi=...` — Selligent open-tracking pixel

**Four images missing alt text**
- `e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` — product/content image; blank alt in image-blocked clients means zero information conveyed
- Thunderhead ONE pixel — alt="" acceptable for tracking pixels but flagged by QA
- iGoDigital `track_event` pixel — same
- Selligent `svopn` pixel — same

For the product image specifically, a descriptive alt attribute is required for WCAG 2.1 AA compliance.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens detected (no `%%field%%`, `{{field}}`, or `[FIRST_NAME]` placeholders left in visible source).

`customerEmail` parameter is Base64-encoded in link query strings:
```

```
Decodes to the walker address — correctly personalized. However, Base64 email exposure in URLs is a minor privacy consideration; the value is trivially decodable by any link-scraper.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header not captured**
QA found no `List-Unsubscribe` or `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) headers at the AgentMail relay layer. Gmail and Yahoo require `List-Unsubscribe-Post` for bulk senders (>5k/day) as of February 2024. If Michaels is injecting these headers at the MTA level and AgentMail is not preserving them, the relay pipeline needs to be audited — the QA system cannot confirm compliance is intact.

**SPF/DKIM authentication status unknown**
`Authentication-Results` header absent from captured message. Cannot confirm pass/fail on SPF, DKIM, or DMARC. Sending domain is `emdeals.michaels.com`; alignment should be verified at the MTA.

**Plain-text body is 81% URLs**
27,445 of 33,784 plain-text characters are URLs. This ratio will trigger spam filters at several providers (SpamAssassin `URI_LONG_LIST`, Barracuda). The plain-text part should summarize content in human-readable sentences with selective linking.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

Links use `cm_mmc` Coremetrics tagging rather than standard UTM parameters:
```
cm_mmc=EMAIL-_-Circular/EOW/Promo-_-20260606_S1_Promo_MichaelsSale_WKND_FF_US-_-HEADER_HOME
```

If site analytics has migrated to GA4 or any non-Coremetrics tool, these parameters will not be parsed and session attribution will be lost. No `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, or `utm_campaign` parameters are present on any link in the visible source.

The campaign date in the `cm_mmc` value (`20260606`) matches today's send date — correct.
