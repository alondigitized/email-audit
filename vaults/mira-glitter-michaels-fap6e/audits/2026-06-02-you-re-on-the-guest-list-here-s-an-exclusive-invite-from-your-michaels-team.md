# You’re on the guest list! 🕶️ Here’s an exclusive invite from your Michaels team ➡️
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-02**
## Executive summary

- The Party Shop LIVE concept is the most genuinely event-driven email Michaels has sent in this entire recent run. Positioning Michaels as a party destination rather than just a craft supply store is a smart summer angle, and the $149 birthday-party-in-a-box visible mid-scroll is legitimately differentiated from anything in the recent archive. That's the ceiling.
- The floor is familiar: the email wants to be an exclusive event invitation AND a coupon blast at once, and the tension kills both. The "exclusive invite" subject evaporates the moment you see the same 25%/20% stacked coupon blocks that appeared in essentially every send going back to May 28. The "guest list" hook promises something special and delivers the standard discount drop.
- The cadence situation is now critical. This is the fourth Michaels email to land on June 2 — following this morning's Camp Creatology online-classes send (scored 8/10), the June 2 cart-fill sale send (7/10), and the adult creative from June 1 (9/10). No single email can survive that inbox density. The Party Shop LIVE deserved to be a standalone campaign with room to breathe; instead it's arriving as noise in a cluttered day.

## What's working

- **Event framing is genuinely fresh.** "The Party Shop LIVE" positions Michaels as an experiential destination, not just a retailer — first time in this audit window the brand has tried this angle.
- **$149 birthday party concept is ownable.** The curated-party price point shown mid-scroll is a real differentiator and more compelling than any of the percentage-off callouts.
- **"Explore the Party Shop" CTA is clean.** Button copy is specific, verb-forward, and doesn't fall back on the generic "Shop Now."
- **Invitation visual language is consistent.** The hero graphic's party aesthetic matches the subject's "guest list" premise at least stylistically.
- **Rewards section is present.** The Earn $5 block surfaces loyalty value without feeling tacked on.

## What's weak

- **"Exclusive invite" promise is hollow.** The subject claims guest-list access; the body delivers the same stacked coupons every subscriber got five times last week.
- **Subject line is 78 characters with two emojis and an arrow.** It's over-loaded and will truncate badly on most mobile clients — the most important creative real estate in the email is wasted.
- **Stacked coupons undercut the event positioning.** The 25%/20% coupon blocks are visually identical to the May 28–31 sale emails; dropping them here signals this isn't a special event, it's a sale with a party hat on.
- **The Party Shop LIVE date/time detail is too small to parse.** The key fact that would make someone actually attend is buried in small type inside the invitation graphic.
- **Fourth send on the same day.** By the time this lands in an inbox already holding three other Michaels emails today, even the strongest creative hook faces near-zero open probability.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Isolate the event email from the discount email.** Strip the 25%/20% coupon stack entirely. If you want to honor the event with a perk, offer a single attendee-only discount redeemable in-store on the day. Don't dilute the invite with the standard promo template.
- 2. **Make the date/time the hero, not a footnote.** "The Party Shop LIVE — Saturday, June 7 at 2PM" should appear in large type above the CTA, not buried in the invitation graphic.
- 3. **Shorten and specify the subject line.** Drop the emojis and arrow; put the date or the unique hook front and center.
- **Subject Alt A:** `You're invited: Party Shop LIVE at Michaels June 7`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The Party Shop LIVE — exclusive in-store event this weekend`
- 4. **Rewrite the preheader to carry the date and RSVP urgency** — the current preview is doing nothing distinct from the subject.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Join us Saturday for live demos, party inspo, and a $149 birthday setup you'll love`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Seats are limited — here's everything on the agenda for Party Shop LIVE`
- 5. **Make the $149 birthday party package the hero CTA.** It's the single most distinctive offer in this entire audit window; it should be above the fold with a direct link, not discovered mid-scroll.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Michaels "Party Shop Event Invite" (2026-06-02)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has a structurally malformed document (content rendered before `<!DOCTYPE>`), two HTTP image sources that will be blocked by modern clients, and missing authentication/unsubscribe headers that create compliance exposure. Core functionality appears intact, but several issues will silently degrade rendering and deliverability.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**22 click-redirect links** all route through `click.emdeals.michaels.com` — standard for Salesforce Marketing Cloud. Not probeable, so destination health is unverified.

**Three separate open/tracking pixel systems** are firing simultaneously:
- Coremetrics/HCL: `click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx?V3OYEUAP2XIUFK7Z5ZML...`
- Thunderhead ONE: `https://na5.thunderhead.com/one/rt/track/ONE-PFSLHYI824-2685/pixel?...`
- SilverPop/IBM: `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true...` *(HTTP — see §3)*
- iGoDigital (SFMC Personalization): `https://nova.collect.igodigital.com/c2/100010552/track_event?...`

No broken destination URLs were confirmed, but four-pixel stacking increases page weight and creates redundant open-rate discrepancies across reporting systems.

The `View in Browser` link routes through the click-redirect domain — expected, not an issue.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Critical — malformed document structure:**
Content (a `<table>` block containing `<!--MASTER_Coremetrics_SLPH_Content-->`) is rendered *before* the `<!DOCTYPE HTML>` declaration. This puts the email client into quirks mode for that leading block and may cause layout inconsistencies in Outlook and older WebKit renderers.

```html
<!-- This table appears BEFORE <!DOCTYPE> -->
<table ... class="stylingblock-content-wrapper">...
</custom>  <!-- unmatched closing tag -->
</custom>  <!-- unmatched closing tag -->
</div>
<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN" ...>
```

**Invalid CSS — `max-height: auto`:**
```css
img { max-height: auto !important; }  /* invalid — not a legal value */
```
`auto` is not a valid `max-height` value per CSS spec. Browsers treat it as `none`, which is likely the intended behavior, but it's a linting error and may trigger unexpected behavior in some rendering engines.

**HTTP image source (will be blocked by Gmail, Apple Mail, Outlook.com):**
```
src="http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/lib/fe4015707564057d701173/m/1/e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg"
```
This is a content image (not just a tracker), missing alt text. Subscribers on HTTPS-enforcing clients will see a broken image with no fallback text.

**HTTP tracking pixel (also blocked):**
```
src="http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true..."
```
SilverPop open-tracking over HTTP — will be blocked or proxied (Apple MPP), producing inaccurate open counts.

**Missing alt text — 4 images:**
- Content image `e323ae89...jpg` — no `alt` attribute
- Thunderhead pixel — no `alt` attribute (acceptable for pure trackers, but adds validator noise)
- iGoDigital pixel — no `alt` attribute
- SilverPop pixel — no `alt` attribute

Tracking pixels should use `alt=""` explicitly to satisfy accessibility validators cleanly.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens observed in the truncated source (no `%%FIELD%%` or `{{variable}}` patterns left unfilled).

Subscriber identity is embedded in multiple places via encoded/hashed values:
- `customerEmail=bWlyYS1nbGl0dGVyLW1pY2hhZWxzLWZhcDZlQGV0ZWxsLmFwcA==` — Base64-encoded email in UTM parameter on header link
- `sfmc_Sub=6a0b108e8b75a227d95acdb55aac28a1c4325c544a88543985a0a0aa55b1b862` — SFMC subscriber hash in UTM
- `emId=c258532227e6840b4eee7a4c0dffb3d5660996c95a1dca0c0011637917ca154f` — email ID in UTM

The Base64 email in a click URL (`customerEmail=bWly...`) is effectively plaintext — anyone who captures the click URL has the subscriber's email address. This is a common but worth-noting PII-in-URL pattern.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

| Check | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| List-Unsubscribe header | **MISSING** | Not found — may not pass through AgentMail relay |
| List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) | **MISSING** | One-click unsubscribe unsupported at header level |
| SPF/DKIM Authentication-Results | **UNKNOWN** | Header not captured by relay; SPF/DKIM status unverifiable |
| Physical mailing address in body | Unverifiable | HTML truncated — assumed present in footer |
| Unsubscribe link in body | Unverifiable | HTML truncated — assumed present in footer |

**The missing `List-Unsubscribe` header is the highest-priority compliance issue.** Gmail and Yahoo have required this header for bulk senders (>5k/day) since February 2024. Its absence will degrade sender reputation and may cause bulk/spam classification.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM / Landing Page)

Coremetrics UTM parameters are present on at least the header link:
```
cm_mmc=EMAIL-_-AdHoc/Other-_-20260602_S4_PartyShopEventInvite_AdHoc_FF_US-_-HEADER_HOME

```

This is HCL/IBM Coremetrics format, not Google Analytics (`utm_source`/`utm_medium`). If the site has migrated to GA4 or another analytics platform, these params will not be captured and the campaign will show zero email-attributed traffic in those tools.

The landing domain (`michaels.com`) click-redirects through `click.emdeals.michaels.com` — standard and expected.

Campaign date in the parameter (`20260602`) matches the send date. No stale campaign codes observed.
