# 🤯 40% off coupon + 70% off summer decor + THOUSANDS of items on sale = BEST. WEEKEND. EVER.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-12**
## Executive summary

- This is Michaels' latest "Ultimate Sale" blast — by this point probably the seventeenth-ish email in four days — and it reads exactly like the last three. The subject line takes the kitchen-sink approach to its logical extreme: stack every offer they have (40% coupon + 70% summer decor + THOUSANDS of items + "BEST. WEEKEND. EVER."), throw in a mind-blown emoji, and hope something sticks. Inside, the email delivers on every promise, but the visual experience is a wall of discount tiles with no clear hierarchy. Every block screams at equal volume.
- To its credit, the 40% coupon is surfaced immediately at the top — that's the highest-value single action and they didn't bury it. The rewards "Earn $5" block near the bottom is a nice loyalty touch. But after the June 11 "THOUSANDS of items are on sale! 😲" send (7/10), this is functionally the same email with a different subject-line formula. Subscriber fatigue is real, and the creative team is burning it fast.
- The clearest signal this email is in trouble: the subject line is doing the work of an entire email body. When your best copy is already in the preview pane, there's nothing left to reward the open.

## What's working

- The 40% coupon is the first thing you see — top placement, prominent, actionable immediately.
- Multiple percentage-off tiers (30%, 40%, 60%, 70%) give different price-sensitivity segments a hook.
- "Earn $5" rewards block is well-placed near the bottom as a retention signal for loyalty members.
- Summer decor tie-in (70% off) is genuinely timely for mid-June and gives the send a seasonal rationale.
- The email renders cleanly — no broken images or overlapping elements visible.

## What's weak

- The visual hierarchy collapses entirely — every promotional tile has equal visual weight, making the eye bounce rather than land anywhere.
- "BEST. WEEKEND. EVER." in the subject is loud superlative copy that signals desperation more than urgency; it's been heard before.
- The subject is a run-on list (~98 characters) — truncated on most mobile clients before the punchline lands.
- No single hero product or category — the Buy 1 / 70% / 60% / 30% blocks fight each other for attention.
- This is the same "Ultimate Sale" creative as the June 11 "THOUSANDS" send; visually indistinguishable to a subscriber who opened that one yesterday.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one offer and lead with it.** The 70% summer decor discount is the sharpest, most seasonal story — build the whole email around it instead of competing with five other discounts.
- 2. **Kill "BEST. WEEKEND. EVER."** — replace with a concrete deadline ("Ends Sunday midnight") so urgency is credible, not hype.
- 3. **Break the "Ultimate Sale" fatigue** — even a small creative pivot (a summer craft project idea, a product shot instead of percentage badges) would reset the visual monotony.
- 4. **Consolidate the discount tile grid** — surface the top 2-3 offers with bigger images and one CTA per block instead of six small tiles.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Michaels "Best Weekend Ever" (2026-06-12)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is built on SFMC with standard click-redirect tracking, but has a misplaced `<!DOCTYPE>` declaration, two non-standard `</custom>` tags, four concurrent open-tracking pixels (two over HTTP), and missing `List-Unsubscribe` headers that create deliverability and compliance exposure.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**DOCTYPE placement — structural bug**
The `<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN">` declaration appears mid-document, after an already-open `<table>` containing the Coremetrics block. Correct placement is the very first line of the document. Rendering in strict parsers (some Outlook versions, Gmail web) may fall back to quirks mode unpredictably.

**Malformed `</custom>` tags**
Two `</custom>` closing tags appear immediately after the open-pixel `<img>` with no matching opening tags:
```html
<img src="https://click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx?...">
</custom>
</custom>
```
These are non-standard and will produce DOM parse errors in strict renderers. Likely a template artifact from an ESP content block that wasn't cleaned up.

**Four concurrent open-tracking pixels**
| Pixel | Protocol | Note |
|---|---|---|
| `click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx` | HTTPS | Primary ESP pixel |
| `thunderhead.com/one/rt/track/ONE-PFSLHYI824-2685/pixel` | HTTPS | Thunderhead/One CDP |
| `nova.collect.igodigital.com/c2/100010552/track_event` | HTTPS | SFMC iGoDigital |
| `svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true` | **HTTP** | SilverPop legacy pixel |

Four open pixels is redundant instrumentation and each fires a network request on open. The `svopn.e.michaels.com` pixel is HTTP-only and will be blocked by Apple Mail Privacy Protection and most modern clients.

**22 tracking/click-redirect links — not probed**
All outbound links route through `click.emdeals.michaels.com`. QA skipped HTTP probing on all 22. Broken destination URLs cannot be confirmed clean from this audit.

**Recipient email in URL parameters (base64, trivially decodable)**
The commented-out header link contains:
```
customerEmail=bWlyYS1nbGl0dGVyLW1pY2hhZWxzLWZhcDZlQGV0ZWxsLmFwcA==
```
Base64-decodes to `mira-glitter-michaels-fap6e@etell.app`. Passing recipient email as a URL query parameter — even encoded — exposes PII in server logs, referrer headers, and any intermediary proxy on click. This is present on navigational links (homepage, etc.), not just unsubscribe.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image source — blocking risk**
```
src: http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/lib/fe4015707564057d701173/m/1/e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg
```
This is a content image (not a tracker), served over HTTP. Apple Mail, Gmail, and Outlook 365 will either block it or proxy it — in both cases the intended image will not render reliably.

**Missing alt text on content image**
The same `e323ae89` image has no `alt` attribute. When blocked (see above), recipients see a broken-image icon with no fallback text describing the promoted content.

**Missing alt text on tracking pixels**
Three pixels have no `alt=""` attribute set:
- `thunderhead.com` pixel
- `nova.collect.igodigital.com` pixel
- `svopn.e.michaels.com` pixel

Pixels should carry `alt=""` (empty string, not omitted) to suppress screen-reader announcements and suppress validator warnings.

**`max-height: auto` in CSS — invalid value**
```css
img { max-height: auto !important; }
```
`auto` is not a valid value for `max-height`. Browsers silently ignore it, but it indicates a copy-paste error in the mobile stylesheet that may mask intended height constraints on specific clients.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

Subscriber identity is passed via `sfmc_Sub` (hashed) and `emId` (hashed) in link parameters, which is standard SFMC behavior.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent**
QA confirms this header was not captured. Google and Yahoo's 2024 bulk sender requirements mandate `List-Unsubscribe` for senders over 5,000/day. Its absence increases the probability of inbox filtering at both providers.

> Evidence: `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header not found`

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent (RFC 8058)**
One-click unsubscribe (the mailto-free POST mechanism) is not present. Gmail's bulk sender policy requires this for compliant one-click unsubscribe UI. Without it, Gmail may show the manual unsubscribe flow or suppress the button entirely.

> Evidence: `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found (RFC 8058)`

**Authentication-Results not captured**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown because the AgentMail relay did not surface the `Authentication-Results` header.

> Evidence: `[WARN] Authentication-Results header not found`

This is a relay/pipeline gap — the underlying send is almost certainly authenticated (SFMC enforces it), but the audit cannot confirm pass status from available data.

**CAN-SPAM physical address**
Not visible in the truncated source. Footer was cut off — cannot confirm presence or absence.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

UTM parameters are present on navigational links, e.g.:
```
cm_mmc=EMAIL-_-Circular/EOW/Promo-_-20260612_S1_EOW_UltimateSale_WKND_FF_US-_-HEADER_HOME
```
This is Coremetrics-style tagging (IBM/Acoustic convention), not GA4 `utm_*` params. If the site has migrated to GA4, these parameters will not populate the standard `utm_source`/`utm_campaign` dimensions — sessions will appear as direct/none in GA4 reports.

The `emId` and `sfmc_Sub` subscriber hash params are appended to destination URLs. If `michaels.com` reads these for personalization (likely — this is a standard SFMC web-handoff pattern), the linkage will work, but any `emId` appearing in GA4 URL parameters will be captured as a custom dimension or pollute the page path, depending on GA4 configuration.

Destination URL probing of the 22 tracked links was not performed — landing page alignment cannot be verified from this audit.
