# 50% off! Sale ends tomorrow in stores.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-24**
## Executive summary

- This is Michaels' Memorial Day finale — the brand's thirteenth send in nine days, arriving on May 24 with "ends tomorrow in stores" as the urgency hook. That deadline is finally real and channel-specific, which is the best thing the subject line has done all campaign. The patriotic red/white/blue creative is consistent and seasonally tight.
- The single biggest problem is the subject/hero disconnect: the inbox promises "50% off" as the lead offer, but you open to a prominent "30% off Memorial Day Sale" hero. The 50% blocks do appear below, but leading with the weaker number in the most visible real estate — then promising the stronger number in the subject — is a minor bait-and-switch that erodes trust at exactly the wrong moment.
- Thirteen emails in nine days has neutralized the urgency mechanics. "Ends tomorrow" has been preceded by "4 days only," "3 days left," and at least three other countdown variants. Real urgency has been replaced by fatigue.

## What's working

- "Ends tomorrow in stores" is the most credible and channel-specific deadline the campaign has used — it names both a horizon and a channel.
- Subject line at 39 characters is clean and mobile-safe.
- Multiple discount tiers (30%, 50%, Buy 2 Get 1) give different buyer motivations something to latch onto.
- Michaels Rewards "Earn $5" section adds loyalty reinforcement for members visible without hunting.
- Patriotic visual theme is coherent and seasonally accurate for May 24.

## What's weak

- Subject promises "50% off" but the hero displays "30% off" — the strongest promise isn't the strongest hero.
- The body is a six-plus-block discount grid with no single product or category focus; the eye has no clear path after the hero.
- Thirteenth email in nine days — the "ends tomorrow" countdown lost credibility after the third "last chance" send.
- No personalization at all; the same generic grid goes to every subscriber regardless of purchase or browse history.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Align subject and hero on the same number** — either make the hero "50% off" or rewrite the subject to lead with 30%. The mismatch is the highest-leverage fix in this send.
- 2. **Feature one hero category with a specific product image** — frames, yarn, or paint with a single linked SKU beats a 6-block grid every time for click-through.
- 3. **Clarify online vs. in-store scope** — "ends tomorrow in stores" excludes online shoppers from the urgency; add "and online" if applicable.
- 4. **Reduce future cadence to 1 email per day maximum** on sale days — the campaign has devalued its own urgency signals.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last day: 50% off — stores close at 9`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Ends Sunday: your 50% off Memorial Day picks`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Frames, yarn, art supplies — grab them before the sale ends tonight`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your 50% off sale ends tomorrow — here's what to grab first`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Michaels "50% off! Sale ends tomorrow in stores" (2026-05-24)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has a structurally malformed document (DOCTYPE appears mid-stream after live content) and runs three simultaneous ESP tracking stacks, two of which use unencrypted HTTP. Authentication headers are unverifiable through the relay, and the plain-text body is disproportionately URL content — a deliverability risk.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**DOCTYPE injection / document structure break**
The `<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN">` declaration appears after a `<table>` block and a `<!--MASTER_Coremetrics_SLPH_Content-->` comment. A doctype mid-document is ignored by parsers; the email effectively has no doctype. Affects rendering in strict-mode clients.

**Three overlapping tracking stacks**
| Vendor | Domain | Protocol |
|---|---|---|
| Salesforce ONE / Thunderhead | `na5.thunderhead.com` | HTTPS |
| iGoDigital / SFMC | `nova.collect.igodigital.com` | HTTPS |
| SilverPop / Acoustic | `svopn.e.michaels.com` | **HTTP** |

The SilverPop pixel (`src: http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true&sv_svemi=...`) will be blocked or flagged as mixed content in Gmail, Outlook 2019+, and Apple Mail. Open attribution for that pixel will be silently lost.

**22 click-tracking links not probed** — all route through `click.emdeals.michaels.com`. Destination validity unverified; any broken landing pages would not be caught by QA.

**PII in click-through URLs (high severity)**
The `View in Browser` link and at least one content link carry:
```
customerEmail=bWlyYS1nbGl0dGVyLW1pY2hhZWxzLWZhcDZlQGV0ZWxsLmFwcA==
```
This is Base64 for the subscriber's email address, passed in cleartext query parameters. It lands in server logs, CDN logs, and browser history at the destination. Combine with `sfmc_Sub=6a0b108e8b75...` (subscriber hash) and the Thunderhead pixel appending `emailaddress%3D...` and there are three independent PII-in-URL exposures in a single send.

**Empty Coremetrics slot**
`<!--MASTER_Coremetrics_SLPH_Content-->` resolves to an empty `<td>` — the content lookup did not populate. Coremetrics tagging for this slot is absent.

**Malformed closing tags**
Two `</custom>` closing tags appear immediately after the open-pixel `<img>` with no corresponding `<custom>` opening tag. This is benign in most parsers but indicates template corruption at that injection point.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources (2 confirmed)**
- `http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/.../e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` — content image, no alt text
- `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847...` — tracking pixel

Both will be blocked by Gmail's image proxy when loaded over HTTP. The content image will appear broken for a subset of clients.

**Missing alt text (4 images)**
Confirmed by QA on:
- `e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` (content image — highest impact, visible to user)
- Thunderhead pixel
- iGoDigital `track_event` pixel
- SilverPop open pixel

Tracking pixels missing alt text is low impact; the content image missing alt text fails WCAG 1.1.1 and degrades the images-off experience.

**Invalid CSS value**
```css
img { max-height: auto !important; }
```
`auto` is not a valid value for `max-height` (valid values: none, length, percentage). Browsers tolerate it, but it may produce unexpected behavior in Outlook's Word renderer.

**Duplicate CSS rule**
`p { margin: 1em 0; }` appears twice in the same `<style>` block — minor, no functional impact.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens (e.g., `%%FIELD%%` or `{{field}}`) visible in the truncated source. The subscriber email and ID are populated in tracking URLs (see §2 PII issue), confirming personalization fired correctly at send time. No issues beyond the PII-in-URL exposure already flagged.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header — not captured**
QA could not confirm the header via the AgentMail relay. If absent at the MTA level, major inbox providers (Gmail, Yahoo) will suppress the native unsubscribe button, increasing spam complaints. RFC 2369 requires this header for bulk mail.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post — not captured**
RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is required by Google and Yahoo for bulk senders (>5k/day) since February 2024. Unverifiable through relay; should be confirmed at the MTA or SFMC send-classification level.

**Authentication-Results — not captured**
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status cannot be confirmed via the relay. `emdeals.michaels.com` is a subdomain ESP sender — alignment with the `michaels.com` DMARC policy must be verified independently.

**Plain-text body is 80% URLs**
28,323 of 35,224 characters in the plain-text part are URLs (QA: "Plain-text is >70% URLs"). Spam filters treat this pattern as a signal of low-quality bulk mail. The plain-text part appears to be an auto-generated URL dump rather than a readable version of the email content.

**CAN-SPAM physical address** — not visible in truncated source; assumed present in footer (standard for Michaels sends) but not confirmed from this excerpt.
