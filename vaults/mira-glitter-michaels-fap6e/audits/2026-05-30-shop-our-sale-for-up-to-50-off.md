# 🛍️ Shop our sale for up to 50% off! 💐
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-30**
## Executive summary

- Another day, another stacked-discount scroll from Michaels. The 5/29 send "Score up to 50% off during our sale!" and today's "Shop our sale for up to 50% off!" are functionally the same email — same ceiling, same hero structure, same multi-tier gauntlet of competing percentage-off sections. That's two nearly identical sale pitches on back-to-back days, which means anyone who opened yesterday has essentially been sent a duplicate.
- The email itself is clean — no broken images, no layout failures — and the Michaels Rewards section anchoring the bottom with an "Earn $5" hook is a genuine loyalty signal. But the structural problem is the same one we flagged on 5/29: six or more competing discount tiers (30%, up to 50%, BOGO, 60%, up to 40%, 25%) destroy any single-message clarity. The eye doesn't know where to land after the hero.
- The Rewards section and the 10 Minute Crafts partnership block are the two things that differentiate this from pure re-send territory, but they're buried mid-to-lower scroll. Judgement: competent execution of the wrong creative brief.

## What's working

- **Hero 30% graphic lands first** — the eye goes directly to the discount number before anything else; hierarchy at the top works.
- **Michaels Rewards "Earn $5" section** is visible and gives members a concrete reason to click beyond the general sale.
- **No render bugs** — clean layout, images load, no overlapping text or broken gaps.
- **10 Minute Crafts partnership block** adds editorial texture to an otherwise all-promo scroll.
- **Sender is instantly recognizable** — Michaels brand identity is consistent throughout.

## What's weak

- **Cadence failure** — this is essentially a re-send of yesterday's "Score up to 50% off" email; a subscribed shopper who opened 5/29 gets nothing new.
- **Six-tier discount stacking causes decision paralysis** — 30%, 50%, BOGO, 60%, 40%, 25% all fighting for attention means none of them win.
- **No deadline visible** — "our sale" with no end date removes the credibility from any urgency; it reads as perpetual.
- **CTAs are generic** — every "Shop Now" button is identical; no CTA is tied to the specific offer in its section.
- **Hero product is vague** — the 30% banner leads with the discount but no single anchor product; it points to everything and therefore nothing.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the duplicate send — or differentiate it hard.** If you're hitting the list two days in a row with the same ceiling offer, the second send must lead with something visually and editorially different. Add a deadline ("Sale ends Sunday midnight"), change the hero category (e.g., lead with the 60% section instead of 30%), or make the Rewards angle the whole subject.
- 2. **Pick one offer and make it the email.** The 60% section is the strongest number visible — that's worth a dedicated send, not sixth position in a stack.
- 3. **Anchor the deadline in the subject.** "Ends Sunday" or "48 hours left" is the single highest-lift change for open and click rates.
- 4. **Give each CTA section-specific copy.** "Shop 60% off" outperforms "Shop Now" every time.
- 5. **Promote the Rewards "Earn $5" hook earlier** — it's a member-retention signal that belongs in the top third, not the basement.
- **Subject Alt A:** `60% off ends Sunday — don't miss it, Rewards members`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Michaels Rewards unlock an extra 25% this weekend`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Biggest discount of the sale: 60% off select items. Ends Sunday night.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus Rewards members earn $5 back on today's purchase.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has a malformed document structure (`<!DOCTYPE>` appears mid-body after live table content) and carries four simultaneous open-tracking pixels, two of which fire over HTTP. Compliance headers are absent or uncaptured, creating a one-click unsubscribe gap that affects Gmail bulk-sender requirements.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Misplaced DOCTYPE / pre-DOCTYPE content block**
Content renders before `<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN">` is declared. The outer wrapper table and Coremetrics block (`<!--MASTER_Coremetrics_SLPH_Content-->`) exist before `<html>`, forcing clients into quirks mode for that segment.

**Stray `</custom>` tags**
Two unmatched closing tags appear immediately after the primary open pixel:
```html
<img src="https://click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx?SUXW3GP2L4KE7G…" width="1" height="1" alt="">
</custom>
</custom>
```
No matching `<custom>` opener is present. Some ESPs emit these as template artifacts; they should be stripped before send.

**Four parallel open-tracking pixels**
| Pixel | Protocol | System |
|---|---|---|
| `click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx` | HTTPS | SFMC primary open pixel |
| `na5.thunderhead.com/one/rt/track/ONE-PFSLHYI824-2685/pixel` | HTTPS | Thunderhead ONE |
| `nova.collect.igodigital.com/c2/100010552/track_event` | HTTPS | iGoDigital / SFMC Collect |
| `svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847` | **HTTP** | SilverPop/Acoustic |

The `svopn.e.michaels.com` pixel fires over plain HTTP — blocked by Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook when image proxying is active. Open count for that signal will be systematically undercounted.

**22 click-redirect links skipped by QA probe**
All routed through `click.emdeals.michaels.com`. Destination URL integrity cannot be confirmed from this capture; spot-check recommended.

**Commented-out header link**
The homepage `<a href="https://www.michaels.com?cm_mmc=EMAIL-_-…-HEADER_HOME…">` is inside an HTML comment (`<!-- <td> <a href=…`), meaning the header logo is either unlinked or this block is dead code left in the template.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="…maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0">
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, AA). Use `user-scalable=yes` or omit the attribute entirely.

**`max-height: auto` — invalid CSS**
```css
img { max-height: auto !important; }
```
`auto` is not a valid value for `max-height` (spec allows `none`, length, or percentage). Browsers silently ignore it; behavior is client-dependent.

**Duplicate `p { margin: 1em 0; }` rule**
Declared twice in the same `<style>` block. Not functionally harmful but indicates copy-paste template hygiene issues.

**Four images missing `alt` text (QA confirmed)**
- `e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` — product/hero image, HTTP src (see §2)
- Thunderhead pixel — tracking, `alt=""` acceptable but absent
- iGoDigital collect pixel — tracking, same
- `svopn.e.michaels.com` pixel — tracking, same

For the product image, missing `alt` is a WCAG 1.1.1 failure and degrades experience for image-blocked recipients.

**Inline `<style>` in `<body>`**
The preheader hide rule (`div.preheader { display: none !important; }`) is injected inside `<body>` rather than `<head>`. Several email clients (notably older Lotus Notes, some Yahoo renderings) do not process body-level `<style>` blocks.

**`@-ms-viewport` declaration present**
Deprecated and no longer affects any supported client. Dead code, minor.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent (QA: WARN)**
Not captured by the AgentMail relay. If genuinely missing from the SMTP envelope, Gmail and Yahoo will not render the one-click unsubscribe button in the message chrome. Google's bulk sender policy (>5,000/day) requires `List-Unsubscribe` + `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058). Absence risks inbox placement demotion.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` absent (QA: WARN)**
Required for RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe. Without it, Gmail's unsubscribe button triggers a mailto rather than a POST, creating friction and risking spam complaints.

**`Authentication-Results` header absent (QA: WARN)**
SPF/DKIM pass/fail status unverifiable from this capture. Given the sending domain is `emdeals.michaels.com` (subdomain of `michaels.com`), SPF alignment and DKIM signing must be verified directly against SMTP headers of a delivered copy.

**Plain-text body is 80.6% URLs (QA: 28,602 / 35,489 chars)**
Spam filters weight plain-text URL density heavily. The plain-text part appears to be auto-generated from click-redirect URLs with no human-readable content. This is a deliverability risk; the plain-text part should contain readable copy mirroring the email body.
