# Meet MakerPlace by Michaels 👋 The online marketplace that’s made for handmade ➡️
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- This is Michaels' sixth email in five days — and the first one with no offer whatsoever. After a welcome, a Rewards enrollment, a brand manifesto, and a Jonathan Adler collab launch, we're now getting a MakerPlace platform introduction. That's a defensible sequencing choice — onboarding someone to the broader ecosystem — but the timing is wrong. A new subscriber who just got five emails in four days doesn't need a sixth one explaining a sub-brand; they need a reason to buy something. The email is clean and well-structured, but it brings nothing that creates urgency or pulls a purchase trigger.
- The MakerPlace concept is legitimately interesting — an Etsy-adjacent handmade marketplace under the Michaels umbrella. But the email treats it like a soft brand awareness play when it should have been a harder conversion moment: here's something you can't get at Michaels stores, here's a maker you should know, here's a product to own today. Instead, we get four category bubbles and a testimonial from Worcester, MA.
- The net verdict: correct strategic move (introduce MakerPlace to new subscribers) but poorly timed and underloaded. It should have been email #7 or #8, after the onboarding cadence cooled, and it needed at least one hero product to anchor the click.

## What's working

- **MakerPlace branding is clean and distinct** — the logo, color palette (warm terracotta/amber), and tagline "Your new home for handmade goods" establish a clear identity separate from the Michaels store brand.
- **Customer testimonial is well-placed and specific** — "I love the variety of handcrafted items I could buy. It was like an online craft fair!" with a real city attribution feels credible, not fabricated.
- **"START SHOPPING" CTA is unambiguous** — verb + noun, high contrast button, placed above the fold.
- **Category grid is scannable** — Home & Living, Jewelry, Art, Crafting & Fiber Arts gives a quick sense of the assortment without overwhelming.

## What's weak

- **Zero offer, zero incentive** — every prior Michaels email in this sequence had a hook ($5 Rewards bonus, 25% off, 50% sale, designer collab). This one asks you to just "start shopping" with nothing in return.
- **Subject line is 83+ characters** — will truncate to an ugly mid-sentence cut on most mobile inboxes; the trailing arrow emoji gets swallowed.
- **No featured product or maker** — a marketplace intro email without a single hero SKU or featured artisan is a missed conversion moment; the category bubbles are too generic to create desire.
- **Cadence is burned out** — the sixth email in five days from Michaels; subscriber goodwill is being depleted faster than it can be built.
- **No urgency signal anywhere** — nothing tells you why to click today rather than close and forget.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with one hero maker + hero product** — pick a specific artisan (name, photo, 1-line story) and one shoppable item. "Meet Sarah, a ceramicist from Portland — her mugs are $34 and ship in 3 days." That's a MakerPlace story; four category circles are not.
- 2. **Add a new-subscriber incentive** — "10% off your first MakerPlace order" or free shipping on the first purchase. The welcome email got 50% off; MakerPlace gets nothing. That hierarchy signals MakerPlace is less valuable.
- 3. **Hold this email until day 8–10** — the cadence here is unsustainable. The Jonathan Adler collab was May 19; this dropped May 20. Space it out so MakerPlace gets proper attention instead of inbox fatigue.
- 4. **Trim the subject line ruthlessly** — get it under 50 characters.
- **Subject Alt A:** `MakerPlace: handmade goods, made easy`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The handmade marketplace inside Michaels`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop one-of-a-kind pieces from real makers — free shipping on your first order`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Jewelry, ceramics, fiber arts & more — discover what's trending on MakerPlace`

### 1. Technical Summary

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text URL density — deliverability risk**
QA reports 7,850 of 8,608 plain-text characters (91%) are URLs. Many spam filters (SpamAssassin `URI_RATIO` rule, Gmail bulk sender heuristics) penalize plain-text parts that are overwhelmingly link content. This likely means the plain-text alternative was auto-generated from the HTML without stripping tracking redirects.

**All 20 CTAs route through a single tracking domain**
Every link uses `click.emdeals.michaels.com/?qs=<encoded-blob>`. Final destination URLs are fully opaque in the source and were skipped during HTTP probing — broken links cannot be confirmed absent.

**Open pixel placed before DOCTYPE**
```html
<div style="display: none;"> </div>
<!DOCTYPE html>
...
<img src="https://click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx?4SD6YR..." width="1" height="1" alt="">
```
The tracking pixel `alt=""` is acceptable, but the stray `<div>` before `<!DOCTYPE html>` forces the DOCTYPE out of first position — see Rendering below.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**DOCTYPE not first node in document**
A `<div style="display: none;"> </div>` precedes `<!DOCTYPE html>`. Per HTML spec, DOCTYPE must be the very first token; anything before it triggers quirks mode in some clients (notably older Outlook WebView). Move or remove the leading div.

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Screen readers (NVDA, JAWS) announce the window title when the email opens. An empty title provides no context. Set to something like `"MakerPlace by Michaels"`.

**Invalid `width` attribute casing on two images**
```html
<img src="...3113536e...jpg" alt="Shop Now" width="Auto" ...>
<img src="...75cfcadf...jpg" alt="Browse Classes" width="auto" ...>
```
`width="Auto"` (capital A) is not a valid HTML attribute value; the lowercase variant on the second image is also incorrect for a `width` attribute (should be an integer or omitted when `style="display:block"` is already set). These may render at 0px in some clients.

**Logo image intrinsic dimensions exceed container**
```html
<img src="...a18805c6...jpg" width="600" height="294" ...>
```
This image is nested inside a `<table style="width:152px">`. The hardcoded `width="600"` will overflow the container in clients that ignore CSS scaling on images (Outlook 2016/2019). The `width` attribute should match the container or be removed in favor of `max-width:100%` CSS.

**Missing alt text on GIF**
```
[WARN] Image missing alt text: 821f65f7-85f0-44da-bf20-3a119d1228a1.gif
src: https://image.emdeals.michaels.com/lib/fe36117170640579751372/m/1/821f65f7-85f0-44da-bf20-3a119d1228a1.gif
```
Decorative images should carry `alt=""` explicitly. Omitting the attribute entirely causes some screen readers to read the filename or URL aloud.

The AgentMail relay may be stripping these before capture, but they must be verified at the MTA level. Absence = deliverability penalty + potential inbox suppression from Gmail.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
