# 💝 Next-level, handmade gifts are trending! Open to find out more and get inspired to make your own.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- This is Michaels shifting gears — after six consecutive "Ultimate Sale" emails (culminating in that June 14 "Extended online – TODAY ONLY! Get up to 60% off" send), this email pivots to inspiration-led gifting content: handmade gifts, embroidery, fiber arts, doll kits. That pivot is strategically smart. The list has been pounded with hard discount beats, and leading with "Every gift tells a story" is a real tonal reset. The email still embeds promotions (25% off at the hero, 40% midway, 30% at the bottom), but for once the product content is doing work rather than just holding a banner in place.
- The execution is mixed. The email is very long and scrolly, with at least six distinct product sections competing for attention before you hit the footer. The hero discount (25% off) is visible immediately, but the sectional structure dilutes momentum — by the time you reach the 40% and 30% offers you've already scrolled past a full fiber arts catalog. A tighter edit would have made this a genuinely strong send.
- The subject undercuts the creative quality. "Open to find out more and get inspired to make your own" is meta-commentary about the email itself, not a reason to open it — and at ~100 characters it will be slaughtered on mobile. The content inside is better than the subject line deserves.

## What's working

- **Tonal pivot lands.** After six back-to-back sale blasts, the "Every gift tells a story" inspirational framing actually feels fresh. A subscriber who was tuning out the sale drumbeat might pause here.
- **25% off hero is visible immediately** — the discount is front-loaded before the scroll begins.
- **Category coherence.** Embroidery, fiber arts, doll kits, and pattern crafts all belong in the same "handmade gifts" frame. The sections reinforce each other rather than feeling random.
- **Michaels Rewards callout** in the footer is present, relevant for loyal members.
- **No obvious render bugs** — images load, layout holds, no overlapping elements visible.

## What's weak

- **Three discount tiers (25%, 40%, 30%) in one email create confusion**, not value — it's hard to know which offer to act on and which applies to what.
- **Subject line is ~100 chars and meta-weak.** "Open to find out more and get inspired to make your own" is a call to open the email, not a reason. It gets truncated on every major mobile client.
- **No deadline visible anywhere in the email** — all three offers feel ambient and therefore skippable.
- **Doll kit section ("Watch their reactions... Make the dream come true")** sits in the middle of a handmade-gifts email and reads like a different campaign stitched in.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill "Open to find out more" from the subject entirely.** That phrase burns 26 characters to say nothing. Lead with the inspiration hook + hint at the discount.
- 2. **Pick one offer tier and hero it clearly** — 25% off with a single, scannable expiration date. The 40% and 30% can live lower in the email but should be clearly scoped to specific categories.
- 3. **Add a deadline.** Even "this week only" tied to Father's Day (June 15 was the day of send in 2026) would give the inspiration content a time peg.
- 4. **Cut or relocate the doll kit section.** It breaks the handmade/craft narrative.
- 5. **Tighten to 3 sections max.** Embroidery → fiber arts → patterns is a tight, coherent story. The current six-section scroll loses most readers before they hit the 40% offer.
- **Subject Alt A:** `💝 Make a gift they'll actually keep — 25% off all weekend`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Handmade gifts are having a moment. Here's 25% off to make yours.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Embroidery, fiber arts, pattern kits — everything to give handmade this Father's Day`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `25% off craft supplies + ideas for gifts worth keeping`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Michaels "Gilded Gifting" Email (2026-06-15)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is sent via Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC) and uses IBM Coremetrics for click tracking, Thunderhead (SAP) for personalization pixels, and igoDigital for behavioral tracking. Two structural HTML defects (misplaced DOCTYPE, invalid tags) and missing RFC 8058 compliance headers are the highest-priority issues; mixed HTTP/HTTPS image content compounds deliverability risk.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**22 click-tracking links use a redirect domain (`click.emdeals.michaels.com`)** — all were skipped by the HTTP probe. No broken destination links confirmed, but cannot be verified.

**PII exposed in URL query string** — the `View in Browser` link and at least one nav CTA carry `customerEmail=bWlyYS1nbGl0dGVyLW1pY2hhZWxzLWZhcDZlQGV0ZWxsLmFwcA==`. Base64 is encoding, not encryption; the raw email address is trivially recoverable and will appear in server access logs and browser history.

**Coremetrics vs. GA4** — all click URLs use `cm_mmc=` (IBM Coremetrics) rather than standard `utm_*` params. This is intentional for Coremetrics analytics but means GA4 / Google Analytics will not attribute sessions unless the landing pages translate `cm_mmc` to `utm_*` on arrival.

**Tracking pixel URL truncation** — the Thunderhead pixel (`https://na5.thunderhead.com/one/rt/track/...`) includes `emailaddress%3DMIR` — the email address appears truncated in the captured source. If this is a capture artifact the pixel may still fire correctly, but worth verifying the full render against a non-truncated source.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Misplaced DOCTYPE** — `<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN" ...>` appears mid-document, after a `<table>` block containing the Coremetrics slot markup. Anything before a DOCTYPE is treated as quirks-mode content; the wrapping table may render inconsistently in strict-mode clients (Apple Mail, modern Outlook).

**Invalid `</custom>` tags** — two `</custom>` closing tags appear immediately after the open-pixel `<img>` with no corresponding open tags. These are non-standard elements and will be silently dropped by most parsers, but they indicate a templating artifact that should be cleaned up.

**Four images missing `alt` text** (QA confirmed):
| Image | src |
|---|---|
| Content image | `http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/.../e323ae89...jpg` |
| Thunderhead pixel | `https://na5.thunderhead.com/one/rt/track/...` |
| igoDigital pixel | `https://nova.collect.igodigital.com/c2/100010552/track_event?...` |
| Silverpop open pixel | `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847...` |

Tracking pixels conventionally carry `alt=""` to suppress screen-reader noise and suppress broken-image icons when blocked.

**Two images served over HTTP** (QA confirmed — may be blocked by Gmail/Outlook.com image proxies):
- `http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/.../e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` — content image; blocked = broken layout
- `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847...` — Silverpop open-tracking pixel; blocked = open rate undercount

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens (e.g., `%%FIRST_NAME%%`, `{{name}}`) visible in the truncated source. The `sfmc_Sub` and `emId` subscriber identifiers are present and appear populated.

No issues found beyond the Thunderhead pixel truncation noted in §2.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent** (QA: WARN) — required by CAN-SPAM for bulk senders and by Google/Yahoo's February 2024 bulk sender policy for lists >5k/day. Absence will suppress Gmail's native unsubscribe UI and increases spam complaint risk.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent** (QA: WARN, RFC 8058) — one-click unsubscribe (`POST` method) is mandatory under the same Google/Yahoo policy. Without it, Gmail cannot offer the one-click path and may route to spam for non-engaged users.

**`Authentication-Results` header not captured** — SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail is unknown through the AgentMail relay. If DKIM is not signing from `emdeals.michaels.com`, inbox placement is at risk regardless of the missing headers above.

**Plain-text body is 83% URLs by character count** (30,954 / 37,269 chars, QA: WARN) — spam filters (SpamAssassin, Proofpoint) score heavy URL density negatively. This is likely generated by SFMC auto-converting click-wrapped links into the plain-text part; SFMC has a "Remove tracking from text part" setting that should be evaluated.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The one nav CTA visible in the truncated source uses:
```
cm_mmc=EMAIL-_-Brand_Story-_-20260615_S1_TryThisNextGildedGifting_Story_FF_US-_-HEADER_HOME
```
Coremetrics attribution is intact for Coremetrics reporting. As noted in §2, GA4 will not see this session as email-attributed unless `michaels.com` runs a `cm_mmc` → `utm_*` translation on-site (common with Coremetrics implementations but not universal).

No UTM params (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) are present on the visible CTA URLs. Confirm whether the Michaels analytics stack relies entirely on Coremetrics or also reports in GA4.
