# The Party Shop at Michaels is bursting with NEW party supplies!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- This is a category-launch email for Michaels' "Party Shop" — a deliberate pivot away from the Memorial Day sale cadence that dominated the inbox for the past two weeks. The brand is clearly trying to establish a new vertical, and the visual identity earns it: the pink-and-balloon color palette is immediately readable, the 25% off headline is visible in the hero, and the product grid below makes the category breadth obvious. This is a competent category-awareness email.
- The weak link is the subject line. "Bursting with NEW party supplies" describes the email, not a reason to open it. There's a real offer inside — 25% off, a 99¢ price point, Rewards perks — but none of that surfaces until you're already in the body. For a party supply shopper on a deadline, that's a wasted opportunity.
- Overall verdict: a solid mid-tier promotional send that markets the department rather than the deal. The right move after the Memorial Day fatigue, but it's leaving conversion on the table by hiding the discount behind a generic headline.

## What's working

- **25% off is visible in the hero** — the discount is front and center without needing to scroll, anchoring the value proposition immediately.
- **Cohesive party aesthetic** — the pink/coral palette and balloon imagery signal the category instantly; no confusion about what's being sold.
- **99¢ price point** — a hard dollar anchor mid-scroll gives impulse buyers a concrete low-barrier entry.
- **Michaels Rewards callout at the bottom** — the "Earn $5" / 20% new-member offer adds a loyalty dimension that heavier shoppers will notice.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum visible anywhere in the scroll.

## What's weak

- **Subject buries the offer** — "bursting with NEW party supplies" is a descriptor, not a hook; the 25% off never appears in subject or preview.
- **No deadline on the 25% off** — without urgency, the offer feels ambient rather than actionable.
- **Subject line is over 50 chars** (~62 chars) — clips on most mobile clients at the worst possible point, after "bursting."
- **Party Shop framing may alienate crafters** — Michaels' core subscriber base skews DIY/craft; a party shop sub-brand launch with no craft angle risks low relevance for most of the list.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the subject with the offer** — the 25% off should be in the subject, not buried. Most recipients see subject + preview and decide; right now both are offer-free.
- 2. **Add a deadline** — even "this week only" on the 25% off would sharpen the click impulse materially.
- 3. **Tie party supplies back to crafting** — one sentence ("Make your own decorations or grab ours — both 25% off") bridges the Party Shop to Michaels' DIY identity and widens relevance.
- 4. **Trim subject to under 50 chars** — "25% off the Party Shop — new arrivals" is 37 chars and does more work.
- **Subject Alt A:** `25% off the Party Shop — new arrivals inside`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New: Party Shop at Michaels — 25% off this week`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Balloons, tableware, cake decor + a 99¢ find you'll want to grab`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your next birthday party starts here — 25% off through [date]`

## Technical Audit — Michaels "Party Shop Newness" (2026-05-27)

### 1. Technical Summary

The email renders functional but has a structural malformation (misplaced `<!DOCTYPE>` declaration), two HTTP image sources that modern clients will block, and missing email-level unsubscribe headers that create compliance risk with Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender requirements.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Redirect domain — all 22 CTAs unverifiable**
All click links route through `click.emdeals.michaels.com` (e.g., `https://click.emdeals.michaels.com/?qs=ABB7...`). The automated probe correctly skipped these — destination URLs and UTM integrity cannot be confirmed without live redirect resolution.

**Multiple competing tracking pixels**
Four distinct open-tracking systems are firing simultaneously:
- Coremetrics (`click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx?RGVSPLPBKTNERPSI7...`)
- Thunderhead ONE (`na5.thunderhead.com/one/rt/track/ONE-PFSLHYI824-2685/pixel`)
- iGoDigital/SFMC (`nova.collect.igodigital.com/c2/100010552/track_event`)
- SilverVerity (`svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847`)

Redundant pixels inflate open-rate counts and increase load. The iGoDigital pixel passes a JSON payload in a query string including `"email":"mira-glitter-micha..."` — PII in a GET request that will appear in server access logs.

**Plain-text body is 81.6% URLs**
QA reports 27,791 of 34,067 characters in the plain-text part are URLs. This ratio is a recognized spam signal and will increase the likelihood of bulk/spam folder placement.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<!DOCTYPE>` declaration is misplaced**
The document opens with a `<table>` block (the Coremetrics content-lookup wrapper) before the `<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN">` declaration. Browsers and email clients that encounter content before `<!DOCTYPE>` enter quirks mode. This is a template-stitching defect.

**Stray `</custom>` tags**
Two `</custom>` closing tags appear near the open-tracking pixel with no matching opening elements. These are invalid HTML and will produce parse warnings in strict renderers:
```html
<img src="https://click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx?...">
</custom>
</custom>
```

**Invalid CSS: `max-height: auto`**
Mobile stylesheet contains `max-height: auto !important` — `auto` is not a valid value for `max-height`. The correct value to remove a max-height constraint is `max-height: none`. Current behavior is undefined across clients.

**Two product images served over HTTP**
```
http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/.../e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg
http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true...
```
Apple Mail, Gmail (web), and Outlook 2016+ block mixed-content images. These will render as broken images or placeholder boxes in most modern clients.

**5 images missing `alt` text**
Confirmed by QA scan:
- `a69df4e5-1588-44d2-9192-4a8f1b92a8ab.jpg` (product image — content unknown without resolution)
- `e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` (product image, also HTTP)
- Thunderhead ONE pixel
- iGoDigital `track_event` pixel
- SilverVerity `svopn` pixel

Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` (empty string) explicitly; product images need descriptive alt text for screen readers and image-off rendering.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent** (QA confirmed)
Gmail and Yahoo both require `List-Unsubscribe` headers for bulk senders (>5k/day). Without it, recipients can only use the in-body unsubscribe link, and senders lose the "Unsubscribe" button that Gmail surfaces in the three-dot menu. This increases spam complaints.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent** (QA confirmed)
RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy. Its absence means Gmail cannot offer automatic one-click unsubscribe, which is a policy violation risk for high-volume sends.

**`Authentication-Results` header not captured** (QA confirmed)
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown for this message. The AgentMail relay did not surface these headers. Without confirmed DKIM alignment on `emdeals.michaels.com`, inbox placement is at risk.

CAN-SPAM physical address and opt-out mechanism appear to be present based on the template structure (footer block visible in the wider template), but could not be fully confirmed from the truncated source provided.
