# SALE! 🛒 Fill your cart for less with limited-time savings.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-02**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean reversion to the stacked-discount formula Michaels ran five times in a row last week, the exact pattern that preceded June 1's creative pivot. After two back-to-back sends on June 1 — the "Screen-free summer fun for kids" thematic morning piece and the "Unplug. Get outside. Get creative." lifestyle evening companion — this email resets the clock and delivers the same cascade of percentage-off blocks the subscriber just spent a week conditioning themselves to ignore.
- The single most egregious miss: the 20% off all regular-price purchases offer — a blanket discount that applies to anything a subscriber wants to buy — is buried four or five sections deep, treated as just another card in the stack. That's the strongest offer in the email and it should be the hero.
- The email is competently executed and renders cleanly, but it squanders the brand goodwill built over the last two days. A subscriber who opened both June 1 sends and now sees this in their inbox will feel the whiplash.

## What's working

- The 25% off hero banner is clean and immediately legible — red/white color blocking signals "sale" at a glance without confusion
- "You'll love these Summer deals" subhead adds a tonal hook that at least gestures toward seasonal context rather than raw percentage stacking
- The 20% off all regular-price purchases block is the email's most actionable, subscriber-friendly offer when you eventually reach it
- Michaels Rewards "Earn $5" section keeps loyalty members in the value conversation
- No visible render bugs — images load, text is legible, no overlap or broken layout

## What's weak

- Resets to the stacked-discount formula the day after two creative sends; for subscribers on daily Michaels email, this is the seventh or eighth identical-feeling promo in five days
- "SALE!" in ALL CAPS in the subject line reads as shouted desperation and trips spam heuristics — a step back from the more confident brand voice of June 1
- Subject is approximately 58 characters, likely truncating on mobile around "limited-time sav—"
- The blanket 20% off offer — objectively the most compelling deal — is buried behind three other sections and never gets the hero treatment it deserves
- No hard deadline date is visible anywhere in the body; "limited-time" is not urgency

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the 20% off all regular-price the hero.** A blanket discount beats a conditional one-item mechanic for casual browsers who don't arrive with a specific product in mind.
- 2. **Remove "SALE!" from the subject.** It adds spam risk and removes sophistication — the cart metaphor in the second half is already doing the work.
- 3. **Add a visible hard deadline in the hero.** "Ends Sunday 6/8" turns "limited-time" into actionable urgency.
- 4. **Collapse to one primary CTA.** Choose the 20% blanket offer, give it one button, demote everything else to secondary.
- 5. **Don't abandon the June 1 creative track this fast.** Two days of stronger brand storytelling builds equity; returning to discount stacking on day three spends it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `20% off everything regular price — today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your summer cart just got cheaper — 20% off regular price`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus up to 60% off sale items and $5 Rewards on every order`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Fill your cart before this weekend deal disappears`

## 1. Overview

This is a clean reversion to the stacked-discount formula Michaels ran five times in a row last week, the exact pattern that preceded June 1's creative pivot. ## Technical Audit — Michaels "Fill your cart for less" (2026-06-02)

### 1. Technical Summary

The email runs on Salesforce Marketing Cloud (ExactTarget) with Coremetrics click tracking and three separate open/analytics pixels. Two non-HTTPS image sources, absent authentication headers, and a plain-text body that is >80% URLs are the primary technical risks; one product image also ships without alt text.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**22 click links unverifiable** — all route through `click.emdeals.michaels.com` (Coremetrics/IBM click redirect). Final destinations and UTM integrity could not be confirmed by the HTTP probe; treat downstream UTM issues as unverified.

**Three open-tracking pixels in use simultaneously:**
| Pixel | Domain | Protocol |
|---|---|---|
| Primary open pixel | `click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx` | HTTPS |
| Thunderhead ONE | `na5.thunderhead.com/one/rt/track/ONE-PFSLHYI824-2685/pixel` | HTTPS |
| iGoDigital/SFMC Nova | `nova.collect.igodigital.com/c2/100010552/track_event` | HTTPS |
| SilverPop | `svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847` | **HTTP** |

The SilverPop pixel uses plain HTTP — it will be blocked by Gmail, Apple Mail (iOS 15+), and Outlook.com image proxies, making open attribution from this pixel unreliable.

**`cm_mmca2` parameter is empty** in the visible logo href:
```
cm_mmc=EMAIL-_-DCE-_-20260602_S2_US_TempA_DCE_FF_US-_-HEADER_HOME&cm_mmca2=
```
An unpopulated Coremetrics attribute parameter may break campaign attribution in IBM Digital Analytics.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP product image** — will be blocked by HTTPS-enforcing clients (Gmail image proxy, iOS Mail):
```
http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/lib/fe4015707564057d701173/m/1/e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg
```

**Missing alt text on that same image** — double failure: blocked on some clients, and no fallback text for screen readers or failed-load state.

**Invalid CSS value** in the `@media (max-width: 480px)` block:
```css
max-height: auto !important;   /* 'auto' is not a valid max-height value */
```
Should be `max-height: none !important`. Currently ignored by compliant renderers; harmless but technically invalid.

**Duplicate CSS rule:**
```css
p { margin: 1em 0; }   /* declared twice in <head> */
```
No functional impact, but indicates template hygiene issues.

**Non-standard `<custom>` tags** appear immediately after the open pixel `<div>`:
```html
<img src="https://click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx?..." ...>
</custom>
</custom>
```
These are invalid HTML and will generate parse errors. Most clients recover gracefully, but they indicate broken template cleanup.

**Doctype** is `HTML 4.01 Transitional` — technically acceptable for email, but the malformed comment preceding it (`<!-- <!-- -->`) may confuse some parsers.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge token placeholders (e.g., `%%FIRST_NAME%%`) visible in the truncated source — good.

**Subscriber identity is passed in plain base64** (not hashed) in the `customerEmail` URL parameter:
```
customerEmail=bWlyYS1nbGl0dGVyLW1pY2hhZWxzLWZhcDZlQGV0ZWxsLmFwcA==
```
Decoded: `mira-glitter-michaels-fap6e@etell.app` — the test/persona inbox address. This is expected for a seeded QA capture, but confirms that production sends expose the subscriber's email address in cleartext (base64 is not encryption) in every link's query string. Anyone who inspects a forwarded email or link preview can extract the recipient's address.

The `sfmc_Sub` and `mi_u` parameters carry the SFMC subscriber key as a SHA-256 hex hash — appropriate.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header unconfirmed** — not captured by the AgentMail relay. CAN-SPAM does not mandate this header, but Google and Yahoo's 2024 bulk sender requirements require it for senders exceeding 5,000 messages/day to Gmail. If Michaels meets that threshold (near-certain), absence would risk inbox placement.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent** — RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is not confirmed. Required by Google/Yahoo bulk sender policy alongside `List-Unsubscribe`.

**`Authentication-Results` header not found** — SPF/DKIM pass/fail status is unknown due to AgentMail relay not forwarding the header. Cannot confirm DMARC alignment. This is a relay capture artifact, not necessarily a sending-side failure, but it should be verified against raw SMTP logs.

**Plain-text body is 80.8% URLs** (28,056 of 34,729 chars). This is a strong spam signal for content filters and is almost certainly an auto-generated plain-text dump of the HTML. Spam filters including SpamAssassin penalize this pattern heavily.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 22 links route through `click.emdeals.michaels.com` before redirecting to `michaels.com`. UTM parameters on the visible logo link follow Coremetrics convention (`cm_mmc`, `cm_mmca2`) rather than standard `utm_source/medium/campaign` — this is consistent with IBM Digital Analytics usage but means Google Analytics / GA4 will not attribute these sessions without a cross-domain or import mapping.

`cm_mmca2` is empty (noted above) — one Coremetrics dimension will be blank for all sessions from this send.

Final landing page UTM alignment cannot be verified without resolving the click-redirect chain.
