# Up to 50% off! It’s time to stop scrolling and start creating. ➡️
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-20**
## Executive summary

- This is Michaels' sixth "stop scrolling / Screen Free Summer / up to 50% off" email in seven days — and it shows. The creative team tried to freshen the angle: "It's time to stop scrolling and start creating" is a self-aware callback to the Screen Free Summer theme that's been running since June 14, and it almost lands as clever. Almost. Because the payload inside is the exact same discount stack — 50% off, clearance 70% off, 40% off floral, 40% off peel & stick — that subscribers have been absorbing on near-daily repeat since mid-June.
- What saves this send from being pure noise is the content density: the email actually packs in eight distinct offer sections — regular price discount, Screen Free Summer 50%, party deals, floral, peel & stick, Father's Day gifting, Mega Clearance 70%, and Rewards — which gives it genuine utility for a subscriber who wants to cross-shop. The Father's Day "Thank outside the box" section is the most seasonally fresh element here, with product price callouts ($4.99, $1.99 visible) that feel curated.
- But eight sections is also the problem. There is no single hero this email is asking you to act on. The 30% off regular price card sits at top, but it immediately loses the visual argument to the 50% and 70% sections lower in the scroll. A subscriber scanning on mobile has to work too hard to find the one deal that matters to them. This is a catalog, not a campaign.

## What's working

- The "stop scrolling / start creating" subject flip is the most charming copywriting in this six-email run — it earns a second read even if the offer is familiar.
- The Mega Clearance 70% off section remains the strongest value signal in the recent cycle and anchors the email's bottom half.
- Father's Day gifting section with visible price points ($4.99, $1.99) adds seasonal relevance that the Screen Free Summer beats have lacked.
- Michaels Rewards "Earn $5" section gives loyalty members a concrete stacking incentive — visible and legible.
- No render bugs visible: images load cleanly, text isn't overlapping, layout holds.

## What's weak

- Eight stacked offer sections with no dominant visual hierarchy — the eye bounces rather than lands.
- The Screen Free Summer USA section (50% off) is visually competing with the 30% off hero instead of supporting it; two top-of-email banners fight each other.
- No visible deadline or expiry on any offer — "up to 50% off" with no "ends Sunday" anchor kills urgency.
- The subject line at ~64 characters is too long for clean mobile truncation; the clever "start creating" payoff risks being cut before it lands.
- Cadence has crossed the fatigue threshold: this is the sixth near-identical campaign email since June 14; the brand history shows the June 19 send was already flagged as a "fifth" fire — one more dilutes any individual send's impact.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one hero and commit to it.** The 70% Mega Clearance is the single strongest offer in this email — it should be the top section, not buried below three other discount blocks. Lead with the biggest number.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline.** "Ends Sunday" or "Through June 22" on the top offer would convert passive browsers to active buyers. None of the sections show one.
- 3. **Collapse to 4 sections maximum.** Mega Clearance hero → one curated category deal → Father's Day gifting (seasonal) → Rewards. The rest should be archived or linked from a "see all deals" footer CTA.
- 4. **Subject line trim.** Cut everything after "creating" or replace the back half with the deadline.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Stop scrolling — 50% off starts now (ends Sunday) ➡️`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 70% off. Your Screen Free Summer starts here.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Clearance, party deals, floral — grab what you need before the weekend`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Father's Day gifts from $1.99 + Mega Clearance up to 70% off`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Michaels Email (2026-06-20)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email renders on a legacy HTML 4.01 Transitional stack with functional responsive CSS, but contains invalid HTML artifacts from template rendering, two mixed-content HTTP image sources, six images without alt text, and missing RFC-compliant unsubscribe headers. Four separate third-party tracking systems fire on open, and subscriber PII is passed in plaintext URL parameters.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**22 click-tracking links unverifiable** — all pass through `click.emdeals.michaels.com` redirect domain; destination URLs and HTTP status codes could not be probed.

**Plain-text part is 81.7% URLs** — 31,547 of 38,606 chars. High URL density in the plain-text part is a known spam-filter signal (flagged by QA deliverability check).

**Four independent open-tracking systems fire simultaneously:**
| Vendor | Endpoint |
|---|---|
| Salesforce/ExactTarget | `click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx?...` |
| Thunderhead ONE | `na5.thunderhead.com/one/rt/track/...` |
| iGoDigital (Nova) | `nova.collect.igodigital.com/c2/100010552/track_event` |
| SailThru | `svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847` |

Redundant pixel firing increases page weight and creates multiple cross-vendor subscriber identity linkages per open event.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Malformed HTML comment at document top:**
```html
<!-- <!-- -->
```
The nested comment opener is invalid in HTML 4 and will cause parse errors in strict renderers.

**Invalid `<custom>` tags in body:**
```html
</custom>
</custom>
```
These are unrecognized elements left from a template system near the open-tracking pixel. Not HTML 4.01 spec; some parsers will emit warnings or drop surrounding content.

**`max-height: auto` in responsive CSS** — `auto` is not a valid value for `max-height`; valid values are `none` or a length. Applied to `.fluid` and `.photo img` via the 480px media query. Behavior is undefined and varies by client.

**Six images missing `alt` text** (QA confirmed):

| Image | URL |
|---|---|
| Content image | `...31528c47-4196-49bb-a540-b78900f21a07.jpg` |
| Content image | `...e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` |
| Thunderhead pixel | `na5.thunderhead.com/one/rt/track/.../pixel` |
| iGoDigital pixel | `nova.collect.igodigital.com/.../track_event` |
| SailThru pixel | `svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847...` |
| SFMC open pixel | `click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx?...` |

Content images (first two) must have descriptive alt text for screen readers and for image-blocked clients. Tracking pixels should have `alt=""` (empty string) to suppress screen-reader announcement.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unfired merge tokens (`{{`, `%%`, `[[`) detected in the visible HTML.

**PII in URL parameters — two confirmed instances:**

1. `customerEmail=bWlyYS1nbGl0dGVyLW1pY2hhZWxzLWZhcDZlQGV0ZWxsLmFwcA==` — this is base64-encoded and decodes to the subscriber's email address. It is appended to the `michaels.com` homepage URL and will appear in server logs, browser history, and Coremetrics analytics payloads.

2. Thunderhead pixel: `emailaddress%3DMIR...` — the QA capture shows the subscriber email being passed to `na5.thunderhead.com` as a URL parameter, transmitted to a third-party vendor in plaintext (URL-encoded, not encrypted).

Both patterns expose subscriber email addresses to third-party systems and web server logs in violation of current data minimization norms.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent** — QA confirmed not present. Gmail and Yahoo require this header for bulk senders (>5k/day) for inbox placement. Its absence also means email clients cannot surface a native unsubscribe button.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent** — RFC 8058 one-click machine unsubscribe is not supported. Required by Gmail's 2024 bulk sender policy for >5k/day senders.

**`Authentication-Results` header not found** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown. QA notes this may be an AgentMail relay artifact (headers stripped in transit), but it cannot be confirmed from available data.

**CAN-SPAM physical address** — not visible in the truncated HTML source; present in full email (assumed — could not verify from truncated input).

**Unsubscribe link** — a footer unsubscribe link is expected in the full email but was not captured in the truncated source. Cannot confirm CAN-SPAM §7704(a)(3) compliance from available data.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

UTM parameters are present and internally consistent:

```
cm_mmc=EMAIL-_-DCE-_-20260620_S2_US_TempD_DCE_FF_US-_-HEADER_HOME
```

Campaign date (`20260620`) matches send date. Channel (`EMAIL`), medium (`DCE`), and campaign name are populated. No empty or placeholder UTM values detected.

`mi_u` and `sfmc_Sub` subscriber identifiers are appended to landing page URLs — these are hashed/opaque values and do not themselves expose PII, unlike `customerEmail` (see §4).

Landing page destination (`michaels.com`) is reachable via HTTPS; no redirect loops or 404s are detectable from the truncated link data given the tracking wrapper on all 22 links.
