# THOUSANDS of items are on sale! 😲
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-11**
## Executive summary

- This is Michaels' sixteenth-ish promotional email in roughly four days — yet another "Ultimate Sale" blast that's essentially interchangeable with the June 10 "restock your craft stash" send (scored 7/10) and the June 9 "Up to 60% off 1,000s of items" send (scored 6/10). The creative strategy here is: stack as many discount badges as possible and hope something catches the eye. There are at least six distinct offer tiers visible (40% off, 60% off, 70% off, 30% off, BOGO, 50% off) crowded into a single scroll, creating decision fatigue rather than urgency.
- The deepest miss is strategic, not executional: Father's Day is four days away, and the June 10 Father's Day-themed send ("Balloons? Check. DIY gifts? Check.") scored 8/10 for exactly the reason this one fails — it had a reason to exist. This email has no hook. The "😲" subject emoji and all-caps "THOUSANDS" are compensating for a message that has nothing differentiated to say.
- The Michaels Rewards "Earn $5" module near the bottom is the only element that adds value not already present in the last three sends. Everything else is a copy-paste of a campaign that is visibly fatiguing the list.

## What's working

- **Michaels Rewards module is prominent.** "Earn $5" with rewards branding gives members a tangible reason to engage that goes beyond the generic sale stack.
- **The 40% off hero badge reads immediately** — it's the first thing the eye hits above the fold.
- **Colorful product photography** is consistent, on-brand, and gives the email visual energy even if the layout is crowded.
- **"Shop the sale →" CTA** is present, clearly labeled, and uses a directional arrow — it's unambiguous.

## What's weak

- **Father's Day is completely absent.** It's June 11. Father's Day is June 15. The June 10 Father's Day send scored 8/10. This send throws away that momentum entirely.
- **Subject line has two spam signals**: "THOUSANDS" in all caps + "😲" sensational emoji. Combined with the "Michaels SALE" sender name, this looks like a promo sub-account rather than the main brand.
- **Six competing discount tiers with no hierarchy.** 40% / 60% / 70% / 30% / BOGO / 50% — when everything is on sale, nothing is on sale. The eye can't land anywhere meaningful below the fold.
- **No deadline anywhere visible in the hero.** The sale is urgent in subject line tone but the body communicates zero time pressure.
- **Sender name is "Michaels SALE"** — a spammy modifier that erodes trust compared to their standard sender identity.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor to Father's Day immediately.** The June 10 Father's Day send proved the audience responds. A hero swap — "Still need a Dad's Day gift?" + one curated craft-kit feature — instantly differentiates this from the last three sends and matches the calendar moment.
- 2. **Kill five of the six discount tiers.** Pick the single strongest offer (60% off is the most compelling) and build the email around it. Let the other tiers live as secondary modules, not co-equals.
- 3. **Fix the sender name.** Drop the "SALE" modifier. Use "Michaels" or "Michaels Stores" — the same identity the brand history's higher-scoring sends used.
- 4. **Add a hard deadline to the hero.** "Sale ends Sunday" or "Through Father's Day only" creates real urgency. The subject implies scarcity; the body doesn't deliver it.
- 5. **Move Michaels Rewards above the fold.** The $5 earn module is buried. For members, this is the most differentiated element in the email — front-load it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Dad's Day is Sunday — up to 60% off at Michaels`
- **Subject Alt B:** `60% off The Ultimate Sale — ends Father's Day`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Grab craft kits, frames, and décor before the weekend rush`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Sale ends Sunday. Earn $5 in Rewards while you shop.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Michaels "THOUSANDS of items are on sale" (2026-06-11)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has a malformed document structure (content precedes `<!DOCTYPE>`), two HTTP-sourced assets that will be blocked or proxied by major clients, and base64-encoded PII (subscriber email) embedded in click-through URLs. Authentication headers could not be verified through the relay.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**22 click links unresolvable.** All clicks route through `click.emdeals.michaels.com` redirect domain; destination validity and redirect chain depth cannot be confirmed without a live probe.

**Four tracking pixels present:**

| Pixel | Protocol | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx?...` | HTTPS | OK |
| `na5.thunderhead.com/one/rt/track/...` | HTTPS | OK |
| `nova.collect.igodigital.com/c2/...` | HTTPS | OK |
| `svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847...` | **HTTP** | Will be blocked/proxied |

The `svopn.e.michaels.com` pixel (appears to be a Selligent/Marigold open tracker) is served over plain HTTP. Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail all proxy or block mixed-content images — open-rate data from this pixel is likely being under-counted or dropped entirely.

**Plain-text version is 76.5% URLs** (20,709 / 27,069 chars). This crosses the threshold at which spam filters treat the plain-text part as a URL dump and apply score penalties.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Malformed document structure.** `<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN">` appears mid-document, after a `<table>` block and platform injection code. Browsers and email clients enter quirks mode when doctype is not the first token; Outlook desktop is particularly sensitive to this.

**Orphaned `</custom>` tags.** Two `</custom>` closing tags appear inside the open-pixel `<div>` with no matching open tag. This is invalid HTML and could confuse parser-based prefetch and accessibility tools.

**Four images missing `alt` attribute entirely** (not `alt=""` — the attribute is absent):

- `e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` — content image (also HTTP, see §2)
- Thunderhead pixel (`/pixel?uri=outbound%3A%2F%2Femailpixel%2F`)
- iGoDigital pixel (`/track_event?payload=...`)
- Selligent open pixel (`svopn.e.michaels.com`)

Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` to suppress screen-reader announcement; the content image should have descriptive alt text.

**One content image served over HTTP:**
```
http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/lib/fe4015707564057d701173/m/1/e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg
```
Gmail proxies HTTP images through its own cache, which can cause load failures on first open and strips original referrer data. Outlook.com and Apple Mail may display a broken-image placeholder.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge token syntax (e.g. `%%FIRSTNAME%%`, `{{name}}`) detected in the visible source — tokens appear to have rendered.

**PII in query parameters — two locations:**

1. `customerEmail=bWlyYS1nbGl0dGVyLW1pY2hhZWxzLWZhcDZlQGV0ZWxsLmFwcA==` — base64-encoded subscriber email appended to the homepage link and visible click URLs. Base64 is not encryption; the value decodes trivially. This value will appear in web server access logs, browser history, and `Referer` headers on any subsequent navigation.

2. iGoDigital pixel payload: `%22email%22:%22mira-glitter-micha...` — subscriber email passed as a plain URL parameter in a tracking pixel `src`, visible in network logs.

These are not merge-token failures, but are a PII hygiene issue that may conflict with CCPA/GDPR data-minimization requirements.

---

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header: not captured.** The QA relay did not see this header. Google and Yahoo's February 2024 bulk-sender requirements mandate `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058 one-click) for senders above 5,000/day. If these headers are genuinely absent (vs. stripped by the relay), Michaels is out of compliance with both providers' policies and risks inbox demotion.

**Authentication-Results header: not captured.** SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status cannot be confirmed. The sending domain `emdeals.michaels.com` is a subdomain; alignment with the `michaels.com` DMARC policy (`p=reject` or `p=quarantine`) should be verified directly against raw headers from a test send.

**CAN-SPAM physical address:** HTML is truncated — presence of a physical mailing address in the footer cannot be confirmed from available source.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Coremetrics (`cm_mmc`) tracking, not standard UTM.** Click URLs use IBM Digital Analytics parameter format:
```
cm_mmc=EMAIL-_-Other_Promo/Value-_-20260611_S1_Circular_UltimateSale_OtherPromo_FF_US-_-HEADER_HOME
```
This is expected for a Coremetrics/SFMC shop, but confirms that standard `utm_source/utm_medium/utm_campaign` will not be populated in GA4 unless a custom parameter mapping is configured there.

**Destination pages unverifiable.** All 22 click links pass through `click.emdeals.michaels.com` before redirecting. Landing page availability, UTM passthrough on final destination, and consistent sale messaging on the receiving pages cannot be confirmed.

**`customerEmail` PII passthrough to michaels.com.** The decoded email address reaches the Michaels.com domain in the query string on every click, not just the confirmation pixel. This extends the PII exposure surface to the full Michaels analytics stack and any third-party tags firing on landing pages.
