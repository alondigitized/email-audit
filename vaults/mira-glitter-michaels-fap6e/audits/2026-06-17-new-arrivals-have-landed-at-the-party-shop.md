# 🎉 NEW arrivals have landed at The Party Shop!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-17**
## Executive summary

- This is Michaels announcing new party supply arrivals — a genuine content pivot after a week of heavy discount stacking (the Ultimate Sale, Screen Free Summer, and 50% off cycles that dominated June 14–17). The Party Shop new arrivals angle gives Michaels something to say beyond "here's another coupon," and the visual execution delivers: the hero is bright, vibrant, and immediately communicates party supplies before a word is read.
- That said, "new arrivals have landed" is a well-worn retail hook. Unlike the June 16 Jonathan Adler x Michaels exclusive — which earned its 8/10 by having singular, scarce new news — this send doesn't anchor to anything that feels must-have. The long, multi-section format works for discovery shoppers but bleeds intent for the focused buyer well before the scroll ends.
- The email earns its place in the cadence and is meaningfully different from what preceded it. But without a single standout hero product or a named character in the subject, it's a well-executed genre email, not a memorable one.

## What's working

- Vibrant, on-brand party imagery fills the hero — pinks, purples, teals signal "party supplies" before any copy is read.
- The 30% offer in the hero gives subscribers a reason to click beyond curiosity — price validation without making discount the whole story.
- Character-licensed party items section adds specificity and purchase intent for subscribers planning a themed party.
- Cadence contrast works for this email: after six consecutive sale-heavy sends, a discovery-focused new arrivals email reads as fresh.
- Michaels Rewards / Earn $5 at the bottom reinforces member value without hijacking the main message.

## What's weak

- "NEW arrivals have landed" is generic — no specific product, character, or collection is called out in the subject to make it feel unmissable.
- Two separate discount levels (30% in the hero, 40% buried lower) appear without clear explanation of scope, which creates friction instead of clarity.
- No time-bound urgency signal anywhere — "new arrivals" implies freshness but gives subscribers no reason to act today vs. next week.
- The long multi-section scroll dilutes conversion focus; most mobile readers will bounce long before the 40% offer in the lower half.
- No single hero product is named or spotlighted as the marquee new arrival, leaving nothing for the email to be remembered by.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Name a hero item or licensed character in the subject.** "NEW arrivals" is forgettable; calling out Bluey, Disney, or a trending party theme converts generic curiosity into specific intent.
- 2. **Consolidate discount messaging.** Lead with one clear offer (30% or 40%, not both) and explain scope upfront — two numbers without context reads as confusion.
- 3. **Trim the scroll to 3–4 hero products.** Curated signals confidence; six-plus grid sections signal "we didn't pick a winner."
- 4. **Add a scarcity or time signal on top items.** "Selling fast" or "just dropped — limited stock" flips new arrivals from passive browse to active purchase.
- 5. **Preheader should tease what's new, specifically** — not echo "Party Shop" already in the subject.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Bluey, Disney & more: new party arrivals are here 🎉`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The Party Shop just restocked — shop NEW arrivals`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Balloons, tableware, décor & licensed faves — all just dropped`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `30% off new party supplies. See what just landed.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**DOCTYPE misplacement — high severity.**
The `<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN">` declaration appears *after* a `<table>` block and two invalid `</custom>` closing tags. Quirks-mode rendering will be triggered in standards-compliant clients.

```html
<!-- CONTENT APPEARS BEFORE DOCTYPE -->
<table ...><tr><td ...><!--MASTER_Coremetrics_SLPH_Content--></td></tr></table>
</custom>
</custom>
<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN" ...>
<html>
```

**Invalid tags:**
Two `</custom>` closing elements appear in the tracking pixel `<div>` with no matching open tag — invalid in HTML 4.01 and will trigger parser error recovery differently across clients.

**HTTP image sources — will be blocked in Gmail, Apple Mail, Outlook.com:**
- `http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/lib/fe4015707564057d701173/m/1/e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` — content image, not a tracking pixel
- `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true&...` — SilverPop open-tracking pixel

**Missing alt text on content image:**
`e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` has no `alt` attribute — images-off rendering shows blank space with no fallback label.

*(Tracking pixels at thunderhead.com, igodigital.com, and svopn.e.michaels.com also lack alt text — acceptable for 1×1 pixels, but `alt=""` should be set explicitly.)*

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

UTM-equivalent tracking uses **Coremetrics `cm_mmc` format**, not standard GA4 `utm_*` parameters:

```
cm_mmc=EMAIL-_-Brand_Story-_-20260617_S3_PartyShopNewness_Story_FF_US-_-HEADER_HOME
```

This is consistent internally but will not populate GA4 session source/medium unless Michaels has a `cm_mmc` → `utm_*` mapping layer on the landing page. No `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, or `utm_campaign` parameters are present on the visible URL.

### 7. Recommendations

1. **Fix DOCTYPE placement** — move `<!DOCTYPE>` to line 1, before any markup. Remove or replace the two `</custom>` tags.
2. **Upgrade both HTTP image URLs to HTTPS** — the content image (`e323ae89...jpg`) and the SilverPop open pixel (`svopn.e.michaels.com`) are both served over HTTP and will be blocked or cause mixed-content warnings in secure clients.
3. **Add `alt=""` to all tracking pixels** — thunderhead.com, igodigital.com, and svopn.e.michaels.com pixels are missing explicit empty alt attributes.
4. **Add `alt` text to the content image** (`e323ae89...jpg`) — required for images-off accessibility.
5. **Confirm List-Unsubscribe headers are set at the SFMC send level** — the AgentMail relay is not capturing them; verify they are present in raw headers at the MTA, not just the relay snapshot.
6. **Remove or hash `customerEmail` from landing page query strings** — base64 is not encryption; a plaintext email address in the URL will appear in referrer logs, CDN logs, and third-party analytics on the destination page.
7. **Verify GA4 receives attribution** — confirm whether `cm_mmc` is remapped to `utm_*` on `michaels.com`; if not, email-driven sessions will be attributed as direct/none in GA4.
8. **Reduce plain-text URL density** — consider generating a human-readable plain-text version rather than a URL dump; current 83% URL ratio is above typical spam-filter thresholds.
