# 👀Check out these deals and score a cart full of savings.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-03**
## Executive summary

- This is Michaels on full autopilot — the deal-stack formula that appeared on June 2 ("Fill your cart for less") is back verbatim, with the same cascading grid of 25%-off-coupon + multiple 50%-off category blocks + a loyalty footer. The only structural change is that the Party Shop LIVE invite from yesterday's send has been stitched onto the bottom, which reads like an afterthought rather than a coherent campaign arc.
- The subject is the weakest Michaels has written in this entire run. "Check out these deals and score a cart full of savings" has no percentage, no product, no urgency, and no seasonal hook. It is 10 words of filler. Compare it to the same-day send "Up to 60% off everything you need to celebrate summer! ☀️" — that email led with a number and a season. This one leads with an eye emoji.
- The body itself isn't broken — it renders cleanly, the discounts are legible, the "Shop Now" buttons are visible — but it's hard to care about an email when the subject gives you no reason to open it.

## What's working

- **Coupon mechanics are front and center.** The 25%-off hero coupon and 50%-off section headers are immediately legible; no hunting required.
- **Michaels Rewards "Earn $5" block** at the bottom is a clean loyalty reminder — actionable for members.
- **No render bugs.** The layout is clean: no overlapping text, no broken images, no placeholder copy visible.
- **Party Shop LIVE CTA** adds a non-transactional element to an otherwise all-discount email.

## What's weak

- **Subject is vague to the point of being invisible.** "Check out these deals" is the weakest hook in six weeks of Michaels sends — no number, no category, no urgency.
- **Too many disconnected offer blocks.** At least five separate discount grids (25% coupon, 50% jewelry, 50% décor, 20% section, 25% section) with no editorial thread connecting them — the eye has nowhere to rest.
- **The Party Shop LIVE block feels recycled.** Subscribers who got yesterday's dedicated Party Shop send are now seeing the same invite appended at the bottom of an unrelated deals dump.
- **No seasonal narrative.** The June 3rd same-day sibling email built a summer + Pride Month story; this one could have been sent in October with a palette swap.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the subject with a specific number.** "Check out these deals" is the weakest signal possible. The body already has 50%-off blocks — use that.
- **Subject Alt A:** `50% off picks you'll actually use — shop now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your 25%-off coupon + 50% deals inside`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Jewelry, décor, and crafts at half price — plus a free Party Shop invite.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `One coupon. Five categories. Cart-filling weather.`
- 2. **Collapse the grid to 2–3 curated picks.** Five stacked discount blocks dilute urgency. Pick the highest-margin/highest-relevance category and make it the hero; move the rest to "also on sale" secondary rows.
- 3. **Either commit to Party Shop LIVE or drop it.** If the goal is driving live event registrations, it deserves its own send (which it already got). Buried at the bottom of a deals email it converts nothing and muddles the message.
- 4. **Add a visible deadline to the coupon.** The 25%-off hero shows no expiry. A "valid through Sunday" line would sharpen urgency without changing the offer.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Michaels Promotional Email (2026-06-03)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email renders with functional personalization and correct ESP substitution, but has three significant compliance gaps (missing List-Unsubscribe headers, unauthenticated relay), a PII-in-URL leak via base64-encoded subscriber email, and two content/tracking images served over plain HTTP.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Tracking pixel proliferation — 4 separate systems firing:**
| Pixel | Domain | Protocol |
|---|---|---|
| Open pixel (Coremetrics) | `click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx` | HTTPS |
| Thunderhead ONE | `na5.thunderhead.com/one/rt/track/...` | HTTPS |
| iGoDigital / SFMC collect | `nova.collect.igodigital.com/c2/...` | HTTPS |
| SilverPop open | `svopn.e.michaels.com` | **HTTP** |

The SilverPop pixel fires over HTTP — modern email clients (Gmail, Apple Mail) will block mixed-content images, silently dropping the open event.

**22 click-redirect links skipped by QA** — all routed through `click.emdeals.michaels.com`. Destinations cannot be verified without following redirects. If any redirect chain breaks (DNS, cert expiry on the sending subdomain), all CTAs go dead simultaneously.

**PII leak in URL parameters:** The header logo href passes the subscriber email as a base64 query parameter:
```
customerEmail=bWlyYS1nbGl0dGVyLW1pY2hhZWxzLWZhcDZlQGV0ZWxsLmFwcA==
```
This decodes to the subscriber's address. Base64 is not encryption — it's visible in server logs, browser history, and referrer headers sent to any third-party scripts on the michaels.com landing page. The same value is also repeated in `sfmc_Sub` and `mi_u` parameters as a hashed ID (acceptable), but the raw base64 email in `customerEmail` should be removed or replaced with an opaque token.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text (content image):**
```
src: http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/lib/fe4015707564057d701173/m/1/e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg
```
This is a content (not tracking) image with no `alt` attribute. Screen readers will skip it; if images are blocked, the block renders blank with no fallback text.

**HTTP image source (content image):**
Same `e323ae89-...jpg` above is served over HTTP. Gmail and Apple Mail enforce HTTPS for images — this image may fail to load for a substantial portion of recipients.

**Invalid CSS — `max-height: auto`:**
```css
img { max-height: auto !important; }
```
`auto` is not a valid value for `max-height`. Browsers ignore this declaration silently; intended behavior is undefined.

**Malformed HTML — stray `</custom>` tags:**
```html
<img src="...open.aspx..." width="1" height="1" alt="">
</custom>
</custom>
```
Non-standard closing tags adjacent to the open-pixel. Likely an ESP template artifact. Technically benign in most parsers but invalid markup.

**`<style>` block injected into `<body>`:**
```html
<div class="preheader">...<style type="text/css"> div.preheader { display: none !important; } </style>
```
A `<style>` block is placed inside a `<div>` in `<body>`. Gmail strips `<style>` from `<body>` in its webmail renderer — the preheader suppression rule will not apply, potentially exposing the preheader text as visible body copy in Gmail.

**Tracking pixel alt text:** Three tracking pixels (Thunderhead, iGoDigital, SilverPop) lack `alt=""`. Low severity for 1×1 pixels but generates screen reader noise and QA flags.

- `cm_mmc` campaign string: `20260603_S3_US_TempD_DCE_FF_US` — date-stamped, appears intentional

No issues found with merge token firing.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent** (QA WARN): Google's bulk sender policy (enforced since 2024) requires a `List-Unsubscribe` header for senders exceeding 5,000 messages/day to Gmail. Absence risks Gmail placing the email in spam or suppressing delivery for the domain.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` absent** (QA WARN, RFC 8058): One-click unsubscribe via POST is required for Gmail and Yahoo bulk senders. Without it, Gmail's unsubscribe UI falls back to a click-through flow, increasing friction and spam complaints.

**`Authentication-Results` header not found** (QA WARN): SPF and DKIM pass/fail status is unknown — the AgentMail relay did not surface auth headers. Cannot confirm the sending domain `emdeals.michaels.com` is passing DMARC alignment. If DKIM is misaligned, inbox placement degrades significantly.

**Plain-text body is 83.6% URLs** (34,164 / 40,874 chars): Spam filters that score plain-text parts will treat this as a high-risk signal. The plain-text part is likely auto-generated from the HTML with no cleanup pass applied.

CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe link are not visible in the truncated HTML, but the unsubscribe mechanism's presence in headers is unconfirmed (see above).

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Coremetrics tagging, not standard UTM:** All landing page links use `cm_mmc` / `cm_mmca2` parameter format (IBM Coremetrics / Watson Campaign Automation), not `utm_source` / `utm_medium`. If the site analytics stack has migrated away from Coremetrics, these parameters will be invisible in reporting.

**UTM coverage unverified:** All 22 click links are wrapped in `click.emdeals.michaels.com` redirects and were skipped by QA probes. It is unknown whether the redirects append or preserve UTM/cm_mmc parameters on the final destination URL or strip them on redirect.

**PII forwarded to michaels.com:** As noted in §2, the base64 subscriber email in `customerEmail` will appear in the `Referer` header sent to any third-party scripts (analytics, ad pixels) loaded on the michaels.com landing page. This is a GDPR/CCPA data minimization concern.
