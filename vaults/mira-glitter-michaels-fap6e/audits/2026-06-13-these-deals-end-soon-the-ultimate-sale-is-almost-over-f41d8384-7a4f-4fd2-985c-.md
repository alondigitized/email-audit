# These deals END SOON: The Ultimate Sale is almost over... ⏰
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-13**
## Executive summary

- This is Michaels' urgency-close play for The Ultimate Sale — the subject line screams "last call" with "END SOON" in caps and a clock emoji, and the email delivers exactly what you'd expect: a dense stack of overlapping discounts (25% hero, 60% off below, Buy 1 Get 1, 50% off, $10/$15/$25 price ladders) jammed into a single long scroll. The creative logic is to overwhelm the subscriber into clicking *something* before the sale ends. That's a coherent strategy, but it creates a real problem: the 25% off hero is actually one of the weakest offers in the email, and the better deals (60%, BOGO) are buried below the fold.
- There's also an uncomfortable context issue. Per the brand history, this is the second Michaels promotional email on June 13 alone — and roughly the nineteenth-ish in about four days. The urgency framing ("almost over") has diminishing returns when every email for a week has implied the same thing. Subscribers who've been trained to ignore the drumbeat won't suddenly re-engage because the subject line has a clock emoji.
- The Michaels Rewards "Earn $5" block near the bottom is a genuine differentiator that deserves to be higher. That's the one loyalty hook that could move a fence-sitter, and it's practically invisible. Net judgement: technically competent execution of a fatigued strategy.

## What's working

- **Urgency framing is legible and appropriate** — "END SOON" + "almost over" + the ⏰ emoji all reinforce the same message without feeling incoherent.
- **Offer density covers the subscriber's entire budget range** — from BOGO to $10 price points to 60% off, there's something for multiple spending intents.
- **Michaels Rewards "Earn $5" block is present** — loyalty hook is visible for members, even if buried.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds across the full email.
- **Red/white palette is instantly brand-identified** — no confusion about who sent this.

## What's weak

- **25% off hero undersells the email** — 60% off appears below the fold, making the headline offer the weakest thing in the send. The eye anchors on 25% and immediately lower-values the email.
- **Offer clutter creates decision paralysis** — five or six discrete offer modules with no clear hierarchy; no single "reason to click *now*."
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is send #2 on the same day (June 13) after a relentless four-day blast cycle; urgency claims have no credibility left.
- **Rewards block is too far down** — the "Earn $5" loyalty hook that could actually move a member is buried below multiple competing modules.
- **No specific product or category call-out** — every offer is generic; nothing says "here's the one thing you've been meaning to buy."

## Recommendations

- 1. **Flip the hero to the strongest offer (60% off), not the softest (25%)** — open with the best number in the email; the current hero actively undersells what's inside.
- 2. **Collapse to a single CTA module** — pick the one offer most likely to convert the most subscribers and build the entire email around it; the five-module approach dilutes every individual CTA.
- 3. **Move Michaels Rewards "Earn $5" to the secondary hero slot** — it's the only loyalty differentiator in the email and the one thing a member would click for.
- 4. **Add one specific product image with a linked name** — a single "trending right now" item gives the eye somewhere to land and the finger something specific to tap.
- 5. **Rethink the urgency copy** — when the sale has been "ending soon" for four days, the subject line stops working. Name a specific deadline (tonight at midnight, ends Sunday) or the claim has no teeth.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Sale ends tonight — 60% off is your last shot`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Final hours: up to 60% off The Ultimate Sale`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Earn $5 in Rewards on your last-chance order.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `60% off + BOGO deals expire soon — shop before they're gone.`

**PII exposed in pixel GET parameters:**
- iGo Digital payload includes `"email":"mira-glitter-micha...` (truncated in QA output but plaintext JSON in URL)
- Thunderhead pixel appends `emailaddress%3DMIR...` — again truncated but email is in the query string
- Transmitting email addresses in unencrypted HTTP GET requests (Selligent pixel) violates basic data hygiene and may conflict with GDPR/CCPA depending on subscriber geography

**Open pixel is HTTPS and benign** (`click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx?...`). No issue.

Two `</custom>` closing tags are invalid HTML with no matching opening element. Most email clients will silently discard them, but they indicate template corruption.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources — blocked by major clients:**
- `http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/lib/fe4015707564057d701173/m/1/e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` — content image, served over HTTP
- `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true&...` — Selligent tracking pixel over HTTP

Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook (365) all proxy or block non-HTTPS image sources. The content image (`e323ae89...jpg`) will fail to render for a significant share of recipients.

**Missing alt text on content image:**
```
e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg
```
This is not a tracking pixel — it is a content image with no `alt` attribute. Blind users and image-off environments get no fallback.

**Tracking pixels missing alt text** (acceptable for 1×1 pixels, but `alt=""` should be explicit):
- Thunderhead pixel
- iGo Digital pixel
- Selligent open pixel

All three omit `alt=""` entirely rather than setting it to empty string — minor but technically noncompliant with HTML 4.01.

**Invalid CSS property value:**
```css
img { max-height: auto !important; }
```
`max-height: auto` is not a valid value; the property accepts `none`, length, or percentage. Browsers treat it as `none`, so behavior is fine in practice, but it signals template tooling generating invalid CSS.

**Commented-out logo block in live send:**
```html

```
A header logo block is commented out. Whether intentional (replaced elsewhere) or an accidental omission cannot be determined from the truncated source, but it warrants verification against the final rendered output.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header: NOT FOUND** (QA confirmed)
CAN-SPAM does not require this header, but Gmail and Yahoo's 2024 bulk sender requirements mandate it for senders above 5k/day. Its absence will hurt inbox placement with both providers.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post header: NOT FOUND** (RFC 8058)
One-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy since February 2024. Missing this header means Gmail will use its own unsubscribe UI rather than the sender's, which can suppress unsubscribe confirmations back to the ESP.

**Authentication-Results header: NOT FOUND**
The AgentMail relay did not capture SPF/DKIM/DMARC results. The sending domain is `emdeals.michaels.com` (subdomain of `michaels.com`). Whether alignment passes cannot be confirmed from available data. This is a relay capture gap, not necessarily a real authentication failure — but it means the pipeline has no signal on deliverability auth for this message.

**CAN-SPAM physical address:** Not visible in the truncated source. Must be present in the footer — requires manual verification of the full render.

**Unsubscribe link:** A link is present in the body (standard ESP footer pattern is expected). Cannot confirm from truncated source — verify in full render.
