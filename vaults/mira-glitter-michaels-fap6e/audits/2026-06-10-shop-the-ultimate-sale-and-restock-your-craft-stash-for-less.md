# 👋 Shop The Ultimate Sale and restock your craft stash for less!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-10**
## Executive summary

- This is Michaels' fifteenth-ish promotional email in roughly four days, and it's indistinguishable from the previous three "Ultimate Sale" sends. The subject swaps in a friendly wave emoji and a "restock your craft stash" hook, but the body is the same stacked-discount cathedral: 30% coupon up top, 60% off banner, two BOGO FREE sections, another 60% block, another 30% block, a lifestyle editorial row, and a Rewards footer. At some point the offers stop competing for attention and just cancel each other out — the subscriber's eye has nowhere to land.
- The one genuine bright spot is the Rewards module near the bottom, which offers a concrete $5 earn. That's the most actionable thing in the email and it's buried six sections deep. The "Creativity is officially in season" lifestyle strip is also a welcome change of register — but it's sandwiched between offer blocks and gets none of the visual breathing room it needs to actually work editorially.
- The honest judgment: this email would have been a solid 7 if it ran once. Sent back-to-back with the June 7 and June 9 "Ultimate Sale" sends, it reads like a brand running out of ideas and turning the volume up. Subscribers who haven't bought yet have already said no twice.

## What's working

- **30% coupon is front-and-center** — immediately visible above the fold without ambiguity.
- **BOGO FREE frames offer** — "Buy One Get One FREE" is a high-perceived-value mechanic that stands out from the generic percentage-off language elsewhere.
- **Michaels Rewards $5 earn** — the loyalty module gives members a concrete reason to act that's separate from the sale.
- **Lifestyle/editorial break** — "Creativity is officially in season" adds a moment of brand warmth before the footer and differentiates this send slightly from pure coupon dumps.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no obvious layout failures.

## What's weak

- **Five competing offer tiers stacked vertically** — 30% off, 60% off, BOGO x2, 60% again, 30% again. The subscriber can't tell what the actual lead offer is.
- **Zero urgency signal** — no end date, no countdown, no "today only." There is no cost to waiting, which is the kiss of death for a sale email.
- **Subject is over 60 characters and entirely generic** — "Shop The Ultimate Sale" has appeared in subject lines for three consecutive days; it carries no new information.
- **Campaign fatigue is real** — this is the third near-identical "Ultimate Sale" execution since June 6. The "restock your craft stash" angle is the only differentiation, and it's in the subject only — the body doesn't deliver on it.
- **No single hero product or anchor SKU** — every section links to a category, never a specific item; there's nothing to grab onto.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill four of the five offer blocks and commit to one** — pick the BOGO frames (highest perceived value), give it the full email, and let it breathe. The dilution of stacking 30%/60%/BOGO/60%/30% destroys each offer's power individually.
- 2. **Add a deadline** — even "This weekend only" changes the conversion calculus entirely. No deadline = no urgency = no reason to click now vs. later.
- 3. **Rewrite the subject around the BOGO or deadline, not the sale name** — the "Ultimate Sale" label is burned; lead with the mechanic.
- 4. **Promote the Rewards $5 earn to the subject or preheader** — members who see a concrete loyalty payout open and click at meaningfully higher rates.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Buy 1, Get 1 FREE on frames – this weekend only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `BOGO free frames + earn $5 in rewards today`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus 60% off thousands of craft supplies — sale ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Restock your stash before the sale ends — frames, floral, and more.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Michaels "Ultimate Sale" Email (2026-06-10)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email sends correctly but has two HTTP (non-HTTPS) asset sources, orphaned non-standard HTML tags, invalid CSS, and undetected authentication/unsubscribe headers at the relay layer. Plain-text body composition is a deliverability risk.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Multiple redundant open-tracking pixels (4 systems firing simultaneously):**
- Coremetrics/ExactTarget: `https://click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx?HY222AR...`
- Thunderhead ONE: `https://na5.thunderhead.com/one/rt/track/ONE-PFSLHYI824-2685/pixel?...`
- igodigital/SFMC: `https://nova.collect.igodigital.com/c2/100010552/track_event?...`
- SilverPop/svopn: `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true...` **(HTTP — not HTTPS)**

22 click links all redirect through `click.emdeals.michaels.com` — standard, but QA correctly skipped probing destinations.

**PII exposed in URL parameters:** The `customerEmail` query param contains a base64-encoded recipient address (`bWlyYS1nbGl0dGVyLW1pY2hhZWxzLWZhcDZlQGV0ZWxsLmFwcA==` decodes to a real address). This appears in at least the header logo link. PII in URL params is logged by proxies, CDNs, and server access logs at the destination.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Malformed HTML — double comment open at document root:**
```html
<!-- <!-- -->
```
This is syntactically invalid and may cause parser ambiguity in strict renderers.

**Orphaned non-standard closing tags** immediately after the open-tracking pixel block:
```html
</custom>
</custom>
```
No corresponding `<custom>` opening tags exist. These are undefined elements that will be ignored by most clients but are a template artifact indicating template assembly issues.

**Invalid CSS — `max-height: auto` in mobile media query:**
```css
img { max-height: auto !important; }
```
`auto` is not a valid value for `max-height` (valid values are length, percentage, `none`, or `max-content`/`min-content`). This declaration is silently ignored; intended behavior is undefined.

**Missing alt text on content image (QA confirmed):**
```
http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/lib/fe4015707564057d701173/m/1/e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg
```
This is a product/content image (not a tracking pixel) with no `alt` attribute — fails WCAG 2.1 Success Criterion 1.1.1 and will render as a broken image icon in image-blocked clients.

Three tracking pixels are also missing `alt=""` (Thunderhead, igodigital, svopn). These should have `alt=""` (empty string) explicitly set; missing `alt` is not the same as `alt=""` for screen reader behavior.

**Legacy DOCTYPE:**
```html
<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN" "http://www.w3.org/TR/html4/loose.dtd">
```
HTML 4.01 Transitional is expected in email for legacy Outlook compatibility — not a defect, noted for context.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unfired merge tokens (e.g., `%%FIRSTNAME%%`, `[FIRST_NAME]`) visible in the truncated source. Token substitution appears to have fired correctly for subscriber ID fields (`sfmc_Sub`, `mi_u`, `emId`, `customerEmail`).

**Empty UTM dimension — `cm_mmca2=` has no value:**
```
cm_mmca2=&mi_u=6a0b108...
```
This parameter is present but blank in the header logo link. Either an intentionally unused dimension or an unfired template variable. Coremetrics will record this as an empty segment, polluting attribution data.

---

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header not detected (QA):**
The AgentMail relay did not surface a `List-Unsubscribe` header. If the header is present at SMTP level but stripped by the relay, this is a relay configuration issue. If absent, this violates Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender requirements (effective Feb 2024) for senders at >5K/day volume.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post not present (QA — RFC 8058):**
One-click unsubscribe (the `POST` mechanism required by Gmail for bulk senders) is not detected. Gmail will show its own unsubscribe prompt instead of honoring the sender's, and non-compliance risks deliverability penalties.

**Authentication-Results header absent (QA):**
SPF and DKIM pass/fail status cannot be confirmed. This may be a relay artifact, but it means automated inbox placement checks cannot verify authentication posture for this send.

**CAN-SPAM physical address:** Not visible in the truncated source, but the preheader and footer are cut off — assume present unless full source confirms otherwise.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

UTM structure uses Coremetrics format (`cm_mmc`, `cm_mmca2`, `mi_ecmp`) rather than standard GA4 `utm_source/medium/campaign`. This is consistent with Michaels' Coremetrics/Acoustic stack but means Google Analytics will not attribute these sessions without cross-domain mapping.

**`cm_mmca2` is empty** (see §4) — one attribution dimension is missing for all clicks.

**`customerEmail` base64 in landing page URLs** (evidence: `customerEmail=bWlyYS1nbGl0dGVyLW1pY2hhZWxzLWZhcDZlQGV0ZWxsLmFwcA==`): This enables site-side identity stitching but exposes the recipient's email in server logs and browser history on the destination domain. The destination URLs use HTTPS, which prevents transit interception, but server-side log exposure remains.
