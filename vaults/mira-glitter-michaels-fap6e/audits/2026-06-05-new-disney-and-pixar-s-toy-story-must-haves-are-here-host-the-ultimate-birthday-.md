# NEW ✨ Disney and Pixar’s Toy Story must-haves are here! Host the ultimate birthday now 🎉 --->
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-05**
## Executive summary

- This is Michaels' seventh promotional email in three days, and the first to lead with genuine licensed IP: Disney and Pixar's Toy Story party collection. On paper that's a differentiated hook — a specific, culturally resonant property tied to a clear use case (birthday party hosting). But the email immediately undermines itself by leading with the same 30% off coupon block that has appeared in every send since June 2nd. The Toy Story content — the thing the subject literally promises — doesn't appear until you scroll past the coupon and into the middle of a long multi-section email. That's a fatal mismatch between promise and delivery.
- The body itself is competent by Michaels' usual standards: color-coded sections, clearly branded Toy Story imagery, a logical party-supply-to-craft-activity flow. The Earn $5 Rewards footer and the 30% coupon are functional trust signals. But this email arrives after six near-identical promotional sends, and even a genuinely fresh creative angle is drowning in subscriber fatigue.
- The verdict: the Toy Story IP is the strongest brief Michaels has sent in this entire run, and it's being squandered by a layout that buries the lead.

## What's working

- **Licensed IP is differentiated**: Toy Story is emotionally specific — it gives parents a concrete reason to engage beyond another generic percentage-off send.
- **Clear use case stated**: "Host the ultimate birthday" is functional framing — it tells you what to *do* with the products, not just that they exist.
- **30% off coupon is prominent and legible**: Even when misplaced, a clean, usable discount at the top is always a functional signal.
- **Earn $5 Rewards reinforcement at the footer**: Consistent loyalty nudge for members.
- **Clean render throughout**: No broken images, no overlapping text, no layout breaks visible.

## What's weak

- **Coupon upstages the promised hero**: Subject says Toy Story "must-haves are here" — the first thing rendered is the same generic coupon from the June 2, 3, 3, 4, 4, and 5 sends. The IP content is buried below the fold.
- **Subject has a trailing "--->" artifact**: Looks like a template formatting leak; reads as spam noise in an inbox and wastes precious subject-line characters.
- **Subject is approximately 96 characters**: Far exceeds the ~50-char mobile threshold — almost certainly truncated before "Host the ultimate birthday now" on the majority of opens.
- **No visible expiry on any offer**: The 30% off and the Toy Story promotion have no deadline in the body, removing urgency.
- **Cadence fatigue is severe**: Email #7 in three days. Even good creative can't fully overcome that frequency.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the Toy Story hero, not the coupon**: Flip the layout — open with the Toy Story party imagery and a specific headline ("Buzz, Woody & friends are ready for the party"), then surface the 30% off as the supporting action driver. Right now the coupon cannibalizes the IP hook before the reader even sees it.
- 2. **Cut the subject and kill the "--->" artifact**: The current 96-char subject is being truncated on mobile and the trailing arrow reads as junk. Trim to the actual hook.
- **Subject Alt A:** `NEW: Toy Story birthday party supplies are here 🎉`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Host a Toy Story birthday — new at Michaels`
- 3. **Add a deadline to the offer**: "30% off — valid through Sunday" outperforms open-ended discounts. There's zero urgency in the current body.
- 4. **Write a real preheader that teases the product, not more subject filler**:
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plates, balloons, decor + a birthday coupon inside.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Woody plates, Buzz balloons + 30% off — all here.`
- 5. **Let this send breathe**: This Toy Story angle is strong enough to carry a 48-hour pause before the next promo. Another email tomorrow will undo whatever goodwill this creative builds.

## Technical Audit — Michaels "Toy Story Launch" Email (2026-06-05)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is built on a legacy HTML 4.01 Transitional template with a critical structural defect (DOCTYPE declared mid-document, after live markup) and two HTTP-sourced assets that will be blocked by modern clients. Compliance headers were not captured by the relay, leaving List-Unsubscribe and authentication status unverified; a raw PII exposure also exists in a third-party tracking pixel's GET parameters.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Redirect chain — all 21 click links unverified**
All links route through `click.emdeals.michaels.com`. QA skipped HTTP probing on all 21. Final destination URLs and UTM integrity on individual links cannot be confirmed without a live probe or redirect-chain walk.

**Four open-tracking systems fire simultaneously**
- Coremetrics — placeholder comment `<!--MASTER_Coremetrics_SLPH_Content-->` (status of actual tag unclear; may be empty)
- Thunderhead ONE — `https://na5.thunderhead.com/one/rt/track/ONE-PFSLHYI824-2685/pixel?uri=outbound%3A%2F%2Femailpixel%2F...`
- iGoDigital — `https://nova.collect.igodigital.com/c2/100010552/track_event?payload=...`
- Silverpop — `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true...` *(HTTP — see §3)*

Multiple simultaneous open-tracking pixels increases the chance of a false-open event and adds latency on load.

**Malformed `</custom>` tags in pixel wrapper**
```html
<img src="https://click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx?..." width="1" height="1" alt="">
</custom>
</custom>
```
Two `</custom>` closing tags appear here with no matching opening tags. These are invalid markup and may cause DOM parse errors in strict clients.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**DOCTYPE declared mid-document — triggers quirks mode**
The `<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN" ...>` declaration appears *after* an already-opened `<table>` wrapper. Per HTML spec, DOCTYPE must be the first token in the document. Clients encountering this may fall back to quirks rendering mode, breaking layout.

**Two HTTP image sources — will be blocked**
Gmail, Apple Mail, and most modern clients enforce HTTPS for embedded content:
- `http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/lib/fe4015707564057d701173/m/1/e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` — product/content image
- `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true&sv_svemi=...` — Silverpop open pixel

**Four images missing `alt` text**
| Asset | URL pattern |
|---|---|
| Product image | `e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` |
| Thunderhead pixel | `na5.thunderhead.com/.../pixel` |
| iGoDigital pixel | `nova.collect.igodigital.com/.../track_event` |
| Silverpop pixel | `svopn.e.michaels.com/...` |

Tracking pixels conventionally use `alt=""` to suppress screen-reader noise; product images require descriptive alt text for accessibility compliance.

**Invalid CSS value: `max-height: auto`**
```css
img { max-width: 100% !important; height: auto !important; max-height: auto !important; }
```
`auto` is not a valid value for `max-height`. Strict CSS parsers will discard this declaration; behavior in image-heavy clients (Outlook for Mac, iOS Mail) is undefined.

**Duplicate CSS rule**
`p { margin: 1em 0; }` is declared twice in the same `<style>` block — no functional impact but indicates template hygiene issues.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`customerEmail` base64-encoded in landing page URLs**
The header "View on Michaels.com" link includes:
```
customerEmail=bWlyYS1nbGl0dGVyLW1pY2hhZWxzLWZhcDZlQGV0ZWxsLmFwcA==
```
Base64 decodes to the subscriber email address. This is transmitted in plaintext in the URL, visible to proxy logs, CDN access logs, and browser history. If used for site-side personalization, a hashed or tokenized identifier is preferable.

**iGoDigital pixel exposes raw email in GET parameter**
```
https://nova.collect.igodigital.com/c2/100010552/track_event?payload={"user_info":{"email":"mira-glitter-micha...
```
Raw email address as a JSON value in a GET query string — logged by any intermediary. Should be hashed (SHA-256 or similar) before inclusion in pixel parameters.

**`sfmc_Sub`** present in header link — consistent with SFMC deployment; hashed subscriber ID, no issue.

No un-rendered merge tokens (e.g., `%%firstName%%`) visible in the truncated source.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not captured**
QA found no `List-Unsubscribe` or `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) headers via the AgentMail relay. Gmail and Yahoo require both for bulk senders; absence risks the sending domain being classified as non-compliant and increases spam folder placement. Needs verification against raw SMTP headers — SFMC typically injects these, so this may be a relay capture gap rather than a true absence.

**`Authentication-Results` header absent**
SPF and DKIM pass/fail status cannot be confirmed from the relay capture. Deliverability is unverifiable without an authenticated send test (e.g., mail-tester.com or a direct inbox capture).

**Plain-text body is 82.5% URLs**
29,719 of 36,027 characters in the plain-text alternative are URL strings. This is above typical spam-filter thresholds and will degrade the plain-text experience. CAN-SPAM does not mandate a text alternative, but major ISPs use text/HTML ratio as a signal.

**Physical address and unsubscribe link** — not visible in the truncated source. CAN-SPAM requires both in every commercial email. Assumed present in the full footer; needs confirmation.
