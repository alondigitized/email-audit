# 😎 Looking for family fun? These outdoor essentials get a stamp of approval. --->
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- This is Michaels' post-Memorial Day pivot email — after sixteen consecutive sale blasts in roughly two weeks, the brand is making a genuine content shift toward summer outdoor/family activity. The hero "Summer days mean backyard play" lands the season correctly, and the 25% off banner plus repeated 50% badges mean there's real value inside. The email earns its open on brand recognition alone, not on the subject line.
- The problem is the subject itself: "stamp of approval" is soft brand-speak with zero offer signal, and the trailing "--->" is a gimmick that reads as the inbox equivalent of waving your arms. After the punchy urgency of the Memorial Day series, this feels like the campaign team exhaled and shipped something comfortable instead of something sharp. The cadence concern is real — this subscriber has gotten roughly one Michaels email per day for two weeks, and this one doesn't give a strong reason to open over any of the others.
- The body, once opened, is reasonably well-executed: multiple product sections with clear 50% and $15 price callouts, a Rewards section with a $5 earn offer, and a clean summer color palette. But the multi-section layout diffuses focus — there's no single hero product or hero CTA, just a scroll of categories.

## What's working

- **25% off banner anchors the hero immediately** — the discount is above the fold and unambiguous.
- **Summer seasonal pivot feels earned** — "backyard play" is the right message for the week after Memorial Day; timing is defensible.
- **Rewards section pulls the loyalty lever** — the "$5 earn" and "20% off" signals near the bottom reward subscribers who scroll.
- **No render bugs visible** — clean layout, no overlapping text or broken images throughout the scroll.
- **Consistent brand palette** — bright summer colors match the Jonathan Adler Summer Sorbet campaign launched two days ago, creating visual continuity.

## What's weak

- **Subject line has no offer and no urgency** — "stamp of approval" is vague marketing language; a subscriber scanning 50 emails sees nothing actionable.
- **The "--->" trail is gimmicky and cheap** — it reads as desperation for attention, not confidence.
- **Too many competing CTAs in the body** — every product section has its own shop link; there's no hierarchy telling the eye where to go first.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is approximately email sixteen from Michaels in two weeks; "looking for family fun?" doesn't clear a high enough bar to justify another open from an already-taxed subscriber.
- **No time boundary anywhere** — none of the offers have a visible end date, removing the one lever that most reliably drives clicks.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the offer in the subject.** The 25% off exists in the body — lead with it. "25% off outdoor + art supplies this week" beats "stamp of approval" by a wide margin.
- 2. **Add a deadline to the hero.** Even "this week only" underneath the 25% banner gives the scroll a clock; right now there's no reason to act today vs. tomorrow.
- 3. **Cut the "--->" from the subject.** It's inbox noise, not intrigue.
- 4. **Pick one hero CTA.** Lead with the highest-margin or most-clicked summer category (likely outdoor paint or party supplies) and make that the single dominant action above the fold. Let the secondary sections be discovery, not competition.
- 5. **Rest the cadence.** Skip or delay a send — after sixteen emails in two weeks, a gap increases open rate on the next one more than any subject line tweak.
- **Subject Alt A:** `25% off all weekend — backyard crafts & outdoor fun`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your backyard deserves 50% off. Shop summer essentials.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Art supplies, party gear, and outdoor kits — all on sale this week.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Earn $5 in Rewards when you shop summer must-haves.`

## Technical Audit — Michaels "Family Fun / Outdoor Essentials" (2026-05-27)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has two structural defects (misplaced DOCTYPE, invalid `<custom>` tags) that can break rendering in strict parsers, and two compliance gaps (missing `List-Unsubscribe` headers) that create deliverability and regulatory risk. Four images lack alt text and two images serve over HTTP.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**DOCTYPE placed mid-document**
The `<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN">` declaration appears *after* an opening `<table>` block. Strict rendering engines will ignore the DOCTYPE entirely or enter quirks mode prematurely.

**Invalid `<custom>` tags**
Two bare `</custom>` closing tags appear after the open-pixel `<img>` with no corresponding open tag. These are likely template placeholder artifacts that weren't stripped before send:
```html
<img src="https://click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx?...">
</custom>
</custom>
```

**22 tracking/click-redirect links skipped (QA)**
All outbound links route through `click.emdeals.michaels.com`. Destination URL resolution was not verified — broken deep links would not surface until manual click-through testing.

**PII exposed in UTM-style parameter**
A base64-encoded subscriber email is appended as a query parameter on tracked links:
```
customerEmail=bWlyYS1nbGl0dGVyLW1pY2hhZWxzLWZhcDZlQGV0ZWxsLmFwcA==
```
This decodes to a plaintext email address and is visible in server logs, referrer headers, and any intermediate proxy — a data exposure risk depending on applicable privacy policy and CCPA/GDPR obligations.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Four images missing `alt` text (QA confirmed)**

Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` to suppress screen-reader announcement. The product image missing alt text directly harms accessibility when images are blocked.

**Two images served over HTTP (QA confirmed)**
```

```
Gmail, Outlook.com, and Apple Mail proxy/block non-HTTPS content. The HTTP product image may not render; the HTTP Silverpop pixel will silently fail to fire.

**`max-height: auto` (non-standard CSS)**
```css

```
`auto` is not a valid value for `max-height`. This is a no-op in all browsers but indicates a template code quality issue.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent (QA — WARN)**
RFC 2369 `List-Unsubscribe` is not present in captured headers. Gmail and Yahoo require this header for bulk senders; its absence can suppress the native unsubscribe UI and increase spam complaints.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent (QA — WARN)**
RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is missing. Yahoo and Google's bulk sender requirements (enforced February 2024) mandate one-click unsubscribe for senders over 5k/day. Absence is a compliance gap for a sender at this volume.

**Note:** Both headers may be present on the actual SMTP envelope but not captured by the AgentMail relay. This should be verified against raw headers from a seed inbox (e.g., Mail-Tester or GlockApps).

**`Authentication-Results` header not found (QA — WARN)**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status is unconfirmed. Sending domain `emdeals.michaels.com` should be verified as aligned with the `From:` domain for DMARC.

**CAN-SPAM physical address**
Physical mailing address and unsubscribe mechanism were not visible in the truncated HTML. These must be present to satisfy CAN-SPAM §7(d). Could not confirm from available source.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Coremetrics tagging used instead of standard UTM**
Links use `cm_mmc` (IBM Coremetrics / Watson Campaign Automation) rather than `utm_source/utm_medium/utm_campaign`. Example from source:
```
cm_mmc=EMAIL-_-Brand_Story-_-20260527_S1_SummerBackyard_CrossCategory_Story_FF_US-_-HEADER_HOME
```
If the destination site has migrated analytics to GA4 or another platform that does not parse `cm_mmc`, click attribution will be lost. No `utm_*` parameters were observed as a fallback.

**`sfmc_Sub` subscriber key appended to all links**
```
sfmc_Sub=6a0b108e8b75a227d95acdb55aac28a1c4325c544a88543985a0a0aa55b1b862
```
This is a hashed subscriber identifier used by SFMC for journey tracking. It is expected, but combined with the base64 email noted above, the full subscriber PII set is reconstructible from any logged URL.
