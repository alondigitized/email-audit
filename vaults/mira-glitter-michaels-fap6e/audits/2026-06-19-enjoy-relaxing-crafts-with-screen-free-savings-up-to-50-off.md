# Enjoy relaxing crafts with screen-free savings – up to 50% off! ☀️
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- This is the fifth "Screen Free Summer / up to 50% off" email Michaels has fired in five days. The June 14 original was fresh; the June 16 near-carbon-copy scored 9/10 in this trail on execution alone; by the June 18 "Phone off, savings on" variant it was already showing fatigue at 7/10. This send doesn't disguise the repetition — "screen-free savings" is right in the subject, which is the exact same phrasing that's been in every prior send. The only meaningful new element is the 30% off any-one-item coupon surfaced as a hero, and patriotic 4th-of-July theming that at least ties the summer discount to a seasonal peg.
- The underlying email is competent Michaels fare: multiple stacked offers, a loyalty rewards reminder, and clean rendering. But the information density is punishing — the scroll never ends, there's no single dominant product story, and after five near-identical sends the subject line now reads as more noise than signal. The most impactful single fix isn't creative — it's sending fewer emails.
- Judged purely as a standalone artifact for a craft subscriber, it still checks most relevance boxes. Judged in context of the subscriber relationship, it's doing real damage to the list.

## What's working

- **30% off any one item coupon lands in the hero**, giving subscribers a clear, immediately-actionable take-away above the fold.
- **Patriotic theming is timely** — the red/white/blue USA section ties the discount to the upcoming 4th of July, giving the send at least one seasonal hook.
- **Michaels Rewards section is prominently placed** near the bottom, reinforcing the loyalty program value without burying it in footer boilerplate.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, overlapping text, or layout breakage visible throughout the long scroll.

## What's weak

- **Fifth Screen Free Summer email in five days** — the subject line leads with "screen-free savings" yet again, which telegraphs staleness before the email is even opened.
- **No dominant visual hierarchy** — the email is a long cascade of offer tiles with no single product story or hero that the eye naturally lands on; it reads like a category page dump.
- **Subject line is 65+ characters with an emoji** — truncates badly on mobile, and the phrasing "Enjoy relaxing crafts" is soft/generic rather than action-driving.
- **Zero specific products linked** — every CTA points to categories or homepages; there's no "shop this item" moment for high-intent shoppers.
- **No deadline on the hero offer** — the 30% coupon has no visible expiration, which undercuts urgency at the one point where it matters most.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rest the Screen Free Summer concept for at least 3 days** — after five identical sends, this one is burning list equity faster than any creative tweak can recover. Silence is the highest-impact change.
- 2. **Give the 30% coupon a deadline in the subject and hero** — "Today only" or a specific date would add urgency that the current send entirely lacks.
- 3. **Collapse the offer tiles to two, not eight** — pick the 30% coupon + one seasonal category (4th of July party supplies, given the patriotic creative), and let white space do the work.
- 4. **Lead the patriotic angle as the fresh hook** — this is the only genuinely new creative element vs. the prior four sends; the current email buries it mid-scroll.
- 5. **Add an expiry to the loyalty callout** — "Earn $5 back — offer ends Sunday" turns a passive awareness moment into a click trigger.
- **Subject Alt A:** `30% off anything — our 4th of July gift to you ☀️`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your summer coupon: 30% off one item, today only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus up to 50% off party supplies, frames, and more — through Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Grab your coupon before it expires — valid in store and online`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Invalid CSS: `max-height: auto`** — the mobile media query sets:
```css
img { max-height: auto !important; }
.header img { max-height: auto !important; }
```
`auto` is not a valid `max-height` value (valid: `none` or a length). Browsers treat it as `none`, so it happens to work — but it's a spec violation that could behave unexpectedly in Outlook WebView or older Android clients.

**Malformed HTML: `</custom>` tags** — two stray closing tags appear immediately after the open pixel `<img>`:
```html
<img src="https://click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx?…" width="1" height="1" alt="">
</custom>
</custom>
```
`<custom>` is not a valid HTML element. These are likely an ESP template artifact. Most parsers ignore them, but they produce invalid DOM and can confuse accessibility trees.

**Nested/malformed comment at document top:**
```html
<!-- <!-- -->
```
This is a valid-but-degenerate comment that closes on the first `-->`. The second `<!--` becomes visible text in some older parsers.

**Legacy DOCTYPE** — `HTML 4.01 Transitional` is used instead of `<!DOCTYPE html>`. Not a rendering defect in practice, but it prevents the full benefit of modern Outlook/WebKit rendering modes.

**Six images missing `alt` text** (QA confirmed):

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent** (QA confirmed) — Gmail and Apple Mail use this header to surface the unsubscribe button in the UI. Its absence does not prevent unsubscribe within the email body, but Gmail will demote the sender in certain promotion-tab filtering logic and may show a less prominent unsubscribe path.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent** — RFC 8058 one-click machine unsubscribe is not supported. Google's February 2024 bulk-sender requirements mandate this for senders at ≥5k/day volume to Gmail. If Michaels meets that threshold (very likely), this is a deliverability compliance gap.

**Authentication-Results header not captured** — the AgentMail relay did not surface SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status. The sending domain `emdeals.michaels.com` should align with a published DMARC policy, but this cannot be confirmed from the captured headers. Needs spot-check against raw relay headers.

**CAN-SPAM physical address** — not visible in the truncated source but assumed present in the footer (standard Michaels practice). Cannot confirm from the excerpt provided.
