# Our sale is still going on with up to 50% off deals! 🛍️
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-05**
## Executive summary

- This is the sixth Michaels promotional email in three days (June 3–5), and the fatigue is audible in the subject line itself — "our sale is *still* going on" is defensive positioning, not a hook. The body is the same cascading deal-stack that appeared on June 3 ("Check out these deals"), June 4 ("Heeyyy! Your personalized deals"), and again today: a 30% hero block at the top, then cascading 40%/50%/60% category tiles, three BOGO offers, and a Michael Rewards "Earn $5" footer. Structurally clean, no render bugs, but creatively indistinguishable from its recent predecessors.
- The one genuinely new element is a mid-email Party Shop LIVE invite card — the same concept introduced in the June 2 exclusive-invite email and the June 4 party-people follow-up. It's been used enough times now that it no longer feels exclusive. The real problem here is cadence: sending effectively the same discount grid six times in 72 hours trains subscribers to ignore all of them.
- The email is competent but utterly blended into the noise. Nothing in the render gives a subscriber a reason to act today rather than wait for the inevitable next send.

## What's working

- **Multiple concrete discounts are visible** — 30%, 40%, 50%, 60% off blocks are all clearly labeled; a subscriber scanning quickly knows what's on offer.
- **Michael Rewards "Earn $5" block** is surfaced near the bottom — loyalty hook is visible without having to dig.
- **No render bugs** — layout is clean, images load, text doesn't overlap.
- **BOGO offers (×3) add variety** to what is otherwise a straight-percentage grid.

## What's weak

- **"Still going on" signals extension, not event** — it's the verbal equivalent of a clearance rack sign left up too long; it tells the subscriber this sale wasn't urgent enough for people to act on.
- **No single hero product or category** — the cascade of percentage blocks flattens everything; the eye has nowhere to stop.
- **The Party Shop LIVE invite is buried mid-email** — it was the freshest concept on June 2; now it's noise because it's been repeated three times.
- **Cadence is broken** — six near-identical emails in 72 hours; the open-rate and engagement signals from prior sends are being ignored.
- **Subject is 56 characters and still manages to be generic** — "deals" is the weakest possible noun to end on.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the send or pivot the creative entirely.** Another deal-stack on day 6 of the same sale is unambiguously worse than nothing. If the sale genuinely has a hard end, lead with that ("48 hours left on 50% off frames"). If it doesn't, don't send.
- 2. **Pick one category and go deep** — instead of 8 percentage blocks, feature one product category that's seasonally resonant (e.g., summer hosting, Pride Month crafts) with a specific product image and a single CTA. One hero beats eight tiles.
- 3. **Retire the Party Shop LIVE teaser** unless there's a new episode announcement with a specific date/time; it's appeared in at least three consecutive emails and no longer creates any anticipation.
- 4. **Rewrite the subject to name a deadline or a category**, not just restate that a sale exists.
- **Subject Alt A:** `50% off frames, yarn & more — ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last call: 30–60% off sitewide`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus earn $5 in Rewards on your next order — members only`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Grab it before the sale wraps — BOGO deals included`

## Technical Audit — Michaels "Sale Still Going On" Email
**Date:** 2026-06-05 | **Sender domain:** emdeals.michaels.com

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has a malformed document structure (DOCTYPE misplacement, orphaned `</custom>` tags) and carries multiple compliance gaps — missing RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe headers and unknown SPF/DKIM status. Plain-text URL density at 81% is a deliverability liability.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**22 click-redirect links skipped** — all route through `click.emdeals.michaels.com` (Salesforce Marketing Cloud click-tracker). Final destination URLs were not probed; broken landing pages cannot be confirmed or ruled out.

**Four distinct tracking pixel systems are active simultaneously:**
| Pixel | Domain | Protocol |
|---|---|---|
| Open pixel (SFMC) | `click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx` | HTTPS |
| Thunderhead/Interaction Studio | `na5.thunderhead.com/one/rt/track/ONE-PFSLHYI824-2685/pixel` | HTTPS |
| iGoDigital collect | `nova.collect.igodigital.com/c2/100010552/track_event` | HTTPS |
| SilverPop/Acoustic open | `svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847` | **HTTP** |

The SilverPop pixel (`svopn.e.michaels.com`) uses HTTP and will be blocked by Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook image proxying — open counts from that system are unreliable.

**PII exposed in tracking pixel URLs:** The iGoDigital pixel embeds a partially visible plaintext email address in the `payload` query parameter (`"email":"mira-glitter-micha…`). The Thunderhead pixel appends `?emailaddress%3DMIR` as a query param. Both are plaintext PII in a GET request, logged by any proxy or CDN in the path.

**`customerEmail` passed as base64 in UTM link (commented-out header `<a>`):**
```
customerEmail=bWlyYS1nbGl0dGVyLW1pY2hhZWxzLWZhcDZlQGV0ZWxsLmFwcA==
```
Base64-decodes to the subscriber's full email address. While the link is currently commented out, the pattern is present in other live links and represents recipient email exposure in referrer headers.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**DOCTYPE misplacement — hard bug:** The `<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN">` declaration appears after ESP wrapper `<table>` markup, not as the document's first node. Browsers and email clients will enter quirks mode for the entire document.

**Orphaned closing tags:** Two `</custom>` tags appear immediately after the SFMC open pixel with no matching opening tag:
```html
<img src="https://click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx?..." width="1" height="1" alt="">
</custom>
</custom>
```
These are invalid HTML and may cause parser tree corruption in strict renderers.

**Four images missing `alt` text (QA confirmed):**
- `e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` — likely a content image, not a pixel; missing alt is an accessibility failure
- Thunderhead pixel (`/pixel?uri=outbound…`)
- iGoDigital pixel (`track_event?payload=…`)
- SilverPop open pixel (`svopn.e.michaels.com/…`)

Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` explicitly; content images need descriptive alt text.

**Two images served over HTTP (will be blocked/proxied):**
- `http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/…/e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` — content image
- `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true&…` — SilverPop pixel

Gmail and Outlook proxy all images; HTTP sources break the proxy chain and result in broken images or blocked loads.

**Commented-out header navigation:** The primary home-page `<a>` link inside the header `<td>` is wrapped in an HTML comment (`<!-- <td><a href="https://www.michaels.com?cm_mmc=...">`) and will not render. If this is intentional, no issue; if it was meant to be live, it is a broken CTA.

The subscriber email is being injected via hashed values (`sfmc_Sub`, `emId`) and base64 encoding in URL parameters rather than rendered into body copy — no rendering failures detected here, but see PII concerns in §2.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not found (QA confirmed).** This header is required by Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender policies (enforced since Feb 2024 for >5K/day senders). Absence may trigger deliverability penalties or inbox suppression.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not found (RFC 8058, QA confirmed).** Without this, Gmail's one-click unsubscribe UI falls back to redirect-based unsub or suppresses the button entirely. Required for Gmail bulk sender compliance.

**`Authentication-Results` header not captured** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown via the AgentMail relay. Cannot confirm whether `emdeals.michaels.com` is properly authenticated. This should be verified against the raw SMTP headers of a delivered copy.

**Plain-text body is 81% URLs** (27,235 of 33,583 chars). Spam filters (SpamAssassin, Google, Proofpoint) score heavily against this pattern. The plain-text part is almost certainly auto-generated from HTML with no human-readable fallback content.

**CAN-SPAM:** An unsubscribe mechanism and physical mailing address are expected in the body; neither is visible in the truncated source. Cannot confirm compliance without the full footer.
