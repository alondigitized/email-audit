# 🔥 Score SIZZLING savings: Up to 60% off 1,000s of items at The Ultimate Sale!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-09**
## Executive summary

- This is Michaels' thirteenth-ish promotional email in roughly four days, and it's another shot at "The Ultimate Sale" — a campaign the brand has already deployed in at least three near-identical forms (June 6 online-only 60% off, June 7 in-store expansion, June 7 party creative). The subject leans on an emoji fire and the word "SIZZLING" in all-caps, signaling the creative team felt the offer needed louder packaging rather than sharper targeting. The body delivers on the headline — 60% off is front and center — but the email is dense with competing callouts: a 30% block, multiple Buy 1 Get 1 panels, a 60% section, another 30% section, and a Rewards earn banner. That's at least five distinct offer types fighting for eye space on a single scroll.
- The honest read: the mechanics are competent but the cadence has fully burned the list by now. A subscriber who has received a near-identical "60% off Ultimate Sale" email every day since June 6 has already decided whether to shop. This send adds noise without adding news. The Rewards "Earn $5" callout near the footer is the one differentiated element — but it's buried below the fold.
- Judgement: functional promotional blast, nothing to write home about. Would perform modestly against a fatigued list.

## What's working

- **60% headline is the real number** — not a "up to" tease buried in fine print; it's the most prominent typographic element in the hero.
- **Multiple offer types give something for everyone** — BOGO 50%, BOGO free, 60% clearance, 30% off — a subscriber who missed prior sends can find an entry point.
- **Michaels Rewards "Earn $5" callout** at the bottom is the one bit of personalization/loyalty acknowledgement in the send; it's a retention hook that prior sends in this run largely omitted.
- **No visible render bugs** — images load, layout holds, text doesn't overlap.

## What's weak

- **Five competing offer types create a cluttered hierarchy** — the eye doesn't know where to land after the 60% hero; the 30% block, three BOGO panels, and a second 60% section all compete simultaneously.
- **"SIZZLING" in all-caps plus 🔥 reads as spam bait** — nudges subject-line spam filters and signals desperation rather than confidence.
- **Subject is 78 characters** — truncates on most mobile clients before "The Ultimate Sale" resolves, so the anchor brand campaign name gets cut.
- **No deadline or time-bound language visible in the body** — every panel is an evergreen discount frame with no "ends Sunday" or "48 hours only" to drive action now.
- **Cadence fatigue** — this is effectively the fourth "60% Ultimate Sale" email in four days; the prior June 7 send (scored 9/10 here) already was the peak execution of this exact offer.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Retire "The Ultimate Sale" subject frame — it's spent.** The June 7 in-store expansion was the natural climax of this campaign arc. Every subsequent send using the same subject erodes open rates. Switch to a product-category angle or a closing-hours urgency frame.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline** — "Ends tonight" or "Final 24 hours" somewhere in the first scroll would be the single biggest lift available. Without it, every offer reads as optional and deferrable.
- 3. **Collapse offer types to two maximum** — pick the 60% clearance angle and one BOGO, cut everything else. Fewer choices = faster click.
- 4. **Move the Rewards callout above the fold** — "Earn $5 on this order" next to the primary CTA is a conversion driver; it's currently buried below three scrolls of product panels.
- 5. **Strip the all-caps and fire emoji from the subject** — the offer itself (60% off 1,000s of items) is strong enough without theatrical packaging.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Final hours: Up to 60% off at The Ultimate Sale`
- **Subject Alt B:** `60% off ends soon — your Michaels Ultimate Sale picks`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Earn $5 in Rewards on your next order. Shop the deals before they're gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `BOGO deals + 60% off clearance — in-store and online today only.`

## Technical Audit — Michaels "Ultimate Sale" Email (2026-06-09)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a legacy HTML 4.01 Transitional doctype with table-based layout and Coremetrics/SFMC tracking infrastructure. Core rendering and responsive structure are functional, but the send carries three confirmed infrastructure issues: mixed-content HTTP image sources, missing authentication header capture at the relay, and unresolved compliance header gaps.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**22 tracking links skipped by probe** — all click-redirect links route through `click.emdeals.michaels.com`. Destination URL integrity cannot be confirmed without following redirects; this is expected behavior but means broken destinations would go undetected in automated QA.

**Open-tracking pixel uses HTTP** — `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true&sv_svemi=...` is a plain HTTP open pixel. Gmail and Apple Mail both suppress mixed-content resources; this pixel will silently fail to fire in those clients, skewing open-rate metrics.

**Pixel URL exposes subscriber ID in plaintext** — `sv_svemi=6a0b108e8b75a227d95acdb55aac28a1c4325c544a88543...` is appended directly to an HTTP URL, transmitting a subscriber identifier without TLS.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Mixed-content image** — `http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/lib/fe4015707564057d701173/m/1/e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` is served over HTTP. Modern clients enforce HTTPS; this image will be blocked or show a broken image placeholder in Gmail (web), iOS Mail, and Outlook 2019+.

**Missing alt text on 4 images:**
- `e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` (content image, HTTP source)
- Thunderhead pixel — `https://na5.thunderhead.com/one/rt/track/ONE-PFSLHYI824-2685/pixel?...`
- iGoDigital pixel — `https://nova.collect.igodigital.com/c2/100010552/track_event?...`
- Swiftpage open pixel — `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/...`

Tracking pixels legitimately use `alt=""`, but the content image at `e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` should carry descriptive alt text for screen readers and image-blocked clients.

**`max-height: auto`** is not valid CSS (`auto` is not a valid value for `max-height`). Rule appears in the 480px media query: `.fluid { max-height: auto !important; }`. Most clients will ignore it, but it is a spec violation that could cause layout bugs in strict parsers.

**Malformed HTML comment** — The opening block contains `<!-- <!-- -->` (nested/unclosed comment). Some parsers, particularly older Outlook and Lotus Notes engines, may render content within the malformed comment as visible text.

**`<custom>` tags** — Two `</custom>` closing tags appear after the open-tracking pixel `<img>`. This is non-standard HTML that will be flagged by strict parsers; benign in most clients but indicates template hygiene issues.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`customerEmail` parameter is base64-encoded subscriber address** — `customerEmail=bWlyYS1nbGl0dGVyLW1pY2hhZWxzLWZhcDZlQGV0ZWxsLmFwcA==` decodes to a subscriber email address appended to click-through URLs as a query parameter. This exposes PII in server access logs, referrer headers, and any analytics intermediary that processes the URL.

**No unresolved merge tokens detected** — no `{{`, `%%`, or `[%` placeholders visible in the truncated source. Cannot fully confirm without the complete HTML.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not captured** — QA relay did not surface this header. CAN-SPAM requires a functional unsubscribe mechanism; if the header is absent at delivery (rather than just absent from the relay capture), large mailbox providers (Gmail, Yahoo) will suppress the native unsubscribe button, increasing spam complaints.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found** — One-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail and Yahoo for bulk senders (>5k/day) since February 2024. Absence means recipients must navigate to a landing page to unsubscribe, which directly elevates complaint rates.

**`Authentication-Results` header not captured by AgentMail relay** — SPF and DKIM pass/fail status is unknown. Cannot confirm the send domain `emdeals.michaels.com` is properly authenticated, though the established sender reputation makes a hard failure unlikely. Should be verified against SFMC send logs.

**Plain-text part is 83% URLs** (31,676 / 38,376 chars) — This is a strong spam signal for content-based filters. The plain-text version should be a human-readable summary of the email, not a serialization of all tracking URLs.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM parameters present and consistent** — click-through URLs carry `cm_mmc=EMAIL-_-DCE-_-20260609_S1_US_TempA_DCE_FF_US-_-HEADER_HOME` and `mi_ecmp=DCEmail20260609`. Campaign tagging is in place.

**`cm_mmca2` parameter is empty** — `&cm_mmca2=&` appears in at least the header logo link. If this is a required Coremetrics segment dimension, the empty value will produce gaps in attribution reporting.

**22 click-redirect destinations unverified** — no HTTP probe could confirm final landing pages are live and UTM-tagged end-to-end. Manual spot-check of key CTA destinations is recommended before deployment analysis.
