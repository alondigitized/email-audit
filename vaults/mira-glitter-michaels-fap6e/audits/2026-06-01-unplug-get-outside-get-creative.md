# 🍃 👩‍🎨 Unplug. 👏 Get outside. 👏 Get creative. 👏
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-01**
## Executive summary

- This is the second Michaels email to land on June 1 — following this morning's "Screen-free summer fun for kids is here!" send — and it reads like an adult companion piece to that one. Where the morning send targeted parents with kids' activities, this one makes the same "unplug/get creative" cultural pitch to the maker/crafter persona directly. The concept is sound: the email leans into lifestyle rather than leading with a screaming percentage, which is a visible creative shift from the May 29–31 sale stack.
- The execution is mixed. The hero opens cleanly with a 25% section and a lifestyle painting vibe, but the email quickly collapses into the same multi-section discount scroll Michaels has been running all week — paint-by-numbers, categories grid, outdoor summer, terrarium, in-store section, 20% members block. The "unplug" brand promise evaporates about three scrolls in when it becomes indistinguishable from the May 30 send.
- The subject kills open rate before any of this matters. Four emojis including three consecutive clapping hands, no offer, no urgency, no number. It reads like an inspirational Instagram caption, not a reason to open an inbox message. For a subscriber who already received a Michaels email this morning, this gets archived on sight.

## What's working

- **25% hero leads clearly.** The pink/red hero with 25% prominent is the first thing the eye hits — the one spot in the email where offer and visual hierarchy are aligned.
- **Lifestyle angle is genuinely differentiated.** "Unplug, get outside, get creative" is a real cultural hook for summer 2026, and the paint-by-numbers section ("Pick a painting and we'll do the rest") executes it well — it's action-oriented, not just badge-printing a seasonal word.
- **Loyalty section is present and specific.** The 20% member discount block and the Michaels Rewards "Earn $5" section are both visible without hunting — good for retention of the rewards base.
- **Render is clean.** No broken images, no layout breaks, no lorem ipsum. Given how much is crammed in here, that's worth noting.

## What's weak

- **Second email of the same day.** This email and "Screen-free summer fun for kids" both landed June 1. A Michaels subscriber who opened the morning send has already spent their goodwill quota for the day. The cadence is objectively fatiguing.
- **Subject has no offer and emoji-stacks.** Three consecutive 👏 emojis with no number, no product, no urgency — spam-signal territory in any modern inbox filter, and nothing to click through on even if it passes the filter.
- **The lifestyle promise collapses mid-scroll.** Below the paint section, the email reverts to the same multi-category icon grid and stacked discount blocks that have appeared in every send since May 29. "Unplug" stops being a real editorial direction and becomes a decorative headline.
- **CTAs are all generic "Shop Now."** Seven or eight "Shop Now" buttons and no CTA is differentiated — no "Start my painting," no "Shop summer kits," nothing that carries the "get creative" promise through to the action layer.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Consolidate to one offer, one CTA.** The 25% hero is strong — build the whole email around it. Drop the multi-section category scroll and run one featured product family (paint-by-numbers kits or outdoor crafts) with one specific CTA. This email is trying to be three campaigns at once.
- 2. **Replace "Shop Now" throughout with action-specific copy.** "Start my summer painting" or "Get the kit" carries the lifestyle angle into the action moment.
- 3. **Do not send twice in one day.** The morning send and this one are cannibalizing each other. One cohesive "summer creativity" email would outperform both.
- 4. **Drop two of the three 👏 emojis from the subject.** One clap reads as emphasis; three reads as clutter. The leaf and artist emojis are doing real work — the clapping wall is not.
- **Subject Alt A:** `25% off — your summer creativity kit starts here`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Unplug this summer with 25% off art & craft picks`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Paint-by-numbers, outdoor kits, and new color drops — all on sale now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your 25% off is waiting. Pick a project and make something this weekend.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Michaels "Unplug. Get Outside. Get Creative." (2026-06-01)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is served via Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC/iGoDigital) with a Coremetrics overlay and three separate pixel-based open-trackers. The primary technical concerns are: a misplaced DOCTYPE producing malformed document structure, two HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources, PII exposure in click and pixel URLs, and unresolvable authentication header state due to the AgentMail relay.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Multiple overlapping tracking systems** — three separate open-tracking pixels fire on load:
- `https://click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx?LMETJSAV4BFE3JIH3FYQ4YWSYQ...` (Coremetrics)
- `https://na5.thunderhead.com/one/rt/track/ONE-PFSLHYI824-2685/pixel?...` (Thunderhead ONE)
- `https://nova.collect.igodigital.com/c2/100010552/track_event?payload=...` (iGoDigital/SFMC)
- `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true...` (Silverpop — HTTP)

**22 click-redirect links skipped** by QA probe (all via `click.emdeals.michaels.com`) — destination URLs and redirect chain health unverified.

**PII exposed in tracking URLs:**
- Click links include `customerEmail=bWlyYS1nbGl0dGVyLW1pY2hhZWxzLWZhcDZlQGV0ZWxsLmFwcA==` — base64-encoded subscriber email, not encrypted. Base64 is trivially reversible.
- iGoDigital pixel payload contains plaintext email: `"email":"mira-glitter-micha...` (truncated in source but clearly a raw address in a GET request URL).

**Plain-text body is 79% URLs** (23,787 of 30,072 chars) — exceeds the 70% threshold flagged by QA. This is a spam scoring risk with Spamhaus and Gmail's bulk sender filters.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Malformed document structure — DOCTYPE is mid-document.** Content begins with a `<table>` block (Coremetrics SLPH wrapper) before the `<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN" ...>` declaration. The DOCTYPE should be the very first token; placing it mid-stream causes browsers and some email clients to enter quirks mode for the entire render.

**Unclosed/orphaned `</custom>` tags** appear in the `<body>` after the open-pixel `<img>`:
```html
<img src="https://click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx?..." width="1" height="1" alt="">
</custom>
</custom>
```
These are invalid HTML — likely leftover SFMC AMPscript block delimiters that were not stripped at render time.

**`max-height: auto !important` in mobile CSS** — `max-height` does not accept `auto` as a valid value per CSS2/CSS3 spec. Most clients silently ignore it, but it is a spec violation that could produce unexpected layout behavior on strict renderers.

**Missing alt text on a content image:**
- `e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` (`http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/lib/fe4015707564057d701173/m/1/...`) — this appears to be a content/product image, not a pixel. Missing `alt` fails WCAG 2.1 §1.1.1 and will produce a broken screen-reader experience.

**Tracking pixels missing `alt=""`:**
- Thunderhead pixel (`na5.thunderhead.com`)
- iGoDigital pixel (`nova.collect.igodigital.com`)
- Silverpop beacon (`svopn.e.michaels.com`)

All three have no `alt` attribute at all. Pixels should carry `alt=""` (empty string) explicitly — absence can trigger accessibility lint failures and produce tooltip artifacts in some clients.

**Two HTTP image sources** will be blocked by clients enforcing mixed-content policy (including Gmail web):
- `http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/lib/.../e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg`
- `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true&...`

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens (e.g., `%%FirstName%%`, `{{email}}`) are visible in the truncated source — render appears clean on that front.

However, the `sfmc_Sub` and `emId` parameters embedded in click URLs are subscriber-specific hashed identifiers passed in plaintext query strings:
- `sfmc_Sub=6a0b108e8b75a227d95acdb55aac28a1c4325c544a88543985a0a0aa55b1b862`
- `emId=c258532227e6840b4eee7a4c0dffb3d5660996c95a1dca0c0011637917ca154f`

These are standard SFMC tracking params, but combined with the base64 `customerEmail`, they create a fully de-anonymizable subscriber fingerprint in every click URL — a concern under GDPR/CCPA if logs are retained by intermediate proxies.
