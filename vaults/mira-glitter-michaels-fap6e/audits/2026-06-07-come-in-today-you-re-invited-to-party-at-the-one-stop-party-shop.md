# Come in TODAY! You’re invited to party at the one-stop-party-shop 🥳 🎉
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-07**
## Executive summary

- This is Michaels' eleventh promotional email in roughly four days — and it leans on the same "Party Shop LIVE" creative hook that the June 4th send already burned. The June 4th email was a genuine event invite for a same-day/next-day livestream; using the same party balloon hero art to push in-store foot traffic on June 7th makes the creative feel like a leftover. The "Come in TODAY!" opener in the subject is urgent on paper, but the body never gives you a deadline or a reason TODAY is different from yesterday.
- That said, the core mechanics work: two concrete 30% off coupons are prominent, Michaels Rewards is visible, and the party theme is coherent. This is a competent in-store-traffic email buried under nearly two weeks of sale fatigue.
- The frames coupon is the most jarring element — it appears as a secondary offer in a Party Shop email, which reads as a generic evergreen bolt-on. Someone deep into party planning has zero reason to care about frames in this context.

## What's working

- Two 30% off coupons are large, legible, and clearly positioned — easy to screenshot before walking in.
- Party theme is visually consistent: balloons, confetti, celebratory palette throughout.
- Michaels Rewards "Earn $5" section is present and scannable — reinforces loyalty value.
- "Come in TODAY!" creates genuine urgency in the subject line, even if the body doesn't back it up.
- No render bugs — all images loaded, text is clean, layout is intact.

## What's weak

- Recycled Party Shop LIVE creative from the June 4th send; that was an event invite, this is a foot-traffic push — they look identical to the subscriber.
- The 30% off frames coupon is a non-sequitur in a party-focused email; it dilutes the theme and signals template reuse.
- "Come in TODAY!" urgency has no body-level expiration — no "offer ends at 9PM," no "today only" badge on the coupon. The urgency evaporates on open.
- Eleventh email in ~four days; the cumulative weight of this cadence makes any individual send feel like noise.
- Category icon section ("Décor, Tableware, Supplies, Favors") is thumbnail-sized and low-impact — does no selling work.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the frames coupon or replace it with a party-specific offer** (e.g., 30% off all balloon décor) — the incongruence breaks the theme and signals laziness.
- 2. **Give the TODAY urgency teeth in the body**: add a banner stamp — "In-store offer valid today only, June 7" — directly on the coupon tile.
- 3. **Swap the hero from the June 4 event invite art to in-store imagery** — a photo of an actual party aisle or a styled party table would signal "come in and see this" rather than "watch the stream."
- 4. **Tighten the category section** — replace small icons with one bold product shot (balloon arch, party kit, etc.) that links directly to a shoppable page.
- 5. **Rest cadence**: this send probably should have been merged with or replaced one of the June 6–7 sale-generic sends rather than added on top.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Today only: 30% off your party supplies at Michaels`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Balloons, tableware, décor — grab your coupon before you head in.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Show this for 30% off any regular price item. Offer ends today.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Michaels "Party Shop" Email (2026-06-07)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is rendered on Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC) with Acoustic/Coremetrics attribution and a secondary Thunderhead/iGoDigital pixel stack. Two HTTP-only assets will be blocked by modern clients, and a misplaced `<!DOCTYPE>` declaration produces a structurally invalid document. No authentication headers were captured at the relay.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**HTTP tracking pixels (will be blocked by iOS Mail, Gmail, Outlook)**

Both of these fire over plain HTTP and will be silently dropped by clients that enforce HTTPS:

- `http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/lib/fe4015707564057d701173/m/1/e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg`
- `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true&sv_svemi=...`

**PII in landing page query strings**

The `customerEmail` parameter in at least one CTA URL contains a base64-encoded email address (`bWlyYS1nbGl0dGVyLW1pY2hhZWxzLWZhcDZlQGV0ZWxsLmFwcA==`) appended directly to the `michaels.com` hostname. This value is logged in web server access logs, forwarded in `Referer` headers to third-party scripts, and ingested by analytics platforms — a PII-in-URL leak.

**21 click-redirect links unresolvable**

All CTA links route through `click.emdeals.michaels.com`. The QA prober skipped HTTP probing on all 21 because the redirect domain is a tracking intermediary. Final destination URLs and their HTTP status codes are unverified.

**Plain-text body is 76% URLs**

QA measured 15,164 of 19,890 plain-text characters as URLs. This is a strong spam-filter signal for providers that inspect the plain-text alternative part.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Misplaced `<!DOCTYPE>` declaration**

The `<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN">` appears mid-document, after a `<table>` wrapper and two `</custom>` closing tags have already been emitted. A DOCTYPE must be the first token in the document. All content preceding it is outside any valid document context; Outlook's Word-based renderer will re-parse and may produce layout drift.

**Non-standard `</custom>` tags**

Two bare `</custom>` elements appear immediately after the open-pixel `<img>`. These are unrecognized in all HTML specifications and will trigger quirks-mode parsing in some clients.

**Duplicate CSS rule**

`p { margin: 1em 0; }` is declared twice in the `<head>` stylesheet with identical values — harmless but indicative of template concatenation without deduplication.

**Invalid CSS value**

`max-height: auto` appears in the responsive image rule. `auto` is not a valid value for `max-height`; the computed value falls back to `none`, which may break constrained image layouts in edge cases.

**Missing alt text on content image**

The main content image `e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` has no `alt` attribute. Screen readers will announce the full URL or skip the image silently. CAN-SPAM does not require alt text, but WCAG 2.1 Success Criterion 1.1.1 does.

**Tracking pixels without `alt=""`**

Three tracking pixel `<img>` elements — Thunderhead (`na5.thunderhead.com`), iGoDigital (`nova.collect.igodigital.com`), and Sitecore Send (`svopn.e.michaels.com`) — are missing even an empty `alt=""`. Screen readers will attempt to read the full URL string for each.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unfired merge tokens (e.g., `%%FIRST_NAME%%`, `{{subscriber.FirstName}}`) are visible in the truncated source. SFMC substitution appears to have completed normally — subscriber-specific values (`sfmc_Sub`, `emId`, `mi_u`) are present as resolved hashes, not raw token placeholders.

No issues found beyond the PII-in-URL concern noted in §2.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header not captured**

The AgentMail relay did not surface a `List-Unsubscribe` header. If the header is genuinely absent (vs. stripped at relay), Gmail and Yahoo will not render their native unsubscribe UI for this sender domain. Both providers made this mandatory for bulk senders in 2024.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post not found (RFC 8058)**

One-click machine-readable unsubscribe is absent. This is required for Gmail bulk sender compliance (≥5K messages/day to Gmail addresses). Without it, Yahoo and Google may demote deliverability.

**Authentication-Results not captured**

SPF and DKIM pass/fail status are unknown — the relay did not forward `Authentication-Results` headers. Cannot confirm DMARC alignment. Given the sending domain is `emdeals.michaels.com` (subdomain) and the `From` header reads `michaels.com`, DMARC alignment depends on whether `emdeals.michaels.com` is covered by the parent domain's DMARC policy.

**CAN-SPAM physical address**

Not visible in the truncated source; a physical mailing address is required. Assumed present in the footer (outside truncation window) — should be verified against the full source.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Acoustic/Coremetrics attribution, not UTM**

All CTA links use `cm_mmc=EMAIL-_-AdHoc/Other-_-20260607_S3_PartyShopEventInviteReminder_AdHoc_FF_US-_-HEADER_HOME` (Acoustic native tracking). If the destination site is instrumented with Google Analytics 4, these visits will land in GA4 as direct/unattributed traffic — UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) are absent. This is expected if Acoustic is the canonical analytics source, but creates a gap if GA4 is used for reporting.

**`cm_mmca2=67369038`**

Coremetrics segment ID is hard-coded in link parameters. If this segment ID is shared across multiple sends with different targeting criteria, post-click segment attribution will be unreliable.

**customerEmail in click-through URLs**

As noted in §2, the base64-encoded subscriber email is appended to destination URLs. If the landing page passes these parameters to GA4 or any tag manager, the PII will propagate into those systems' event streams.
