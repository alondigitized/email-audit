# 🤯 Wait till you see your deals!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-29**
## Executive summary

- This is a six-tier stacked-discount scroll — Michaels' default mode when the calendar says "sale." The hero opens with a bold "30% off" graphic, then immediately fragments into a gauntlet of competing sections: 50% off plants, another 50% block, 40% off, a BOGO, 25% off, and a Rewards earn layer buried at the bottom. The visual weight is identical across every section; there is no escalation, no anchor.
- The critical context: **this is the second Michaels sale email today.** The earlier 5/29 send ("Score up to 50% off during our sale!") already walked through the discount stack. Sending "🤯 Wait till you see your deals!" hours later, with nearly identical creative, makes the 🤯 face read as hollow — the subscriber already saw it. The intrigue hook requires the recipient to not have already received your sale email, and that condition is not met.
- Bottom line: the email has plenty of deals, but the format has become noise. The most differentiating element — the Michaels Rewards "Earn $5" moment — is in the footer, where open-and-done shoppers will never reach it.

## What's working

- **30% hero is dominant above the fold** — the large sale banner registers immediately before any scroll.
- **BOGO offer breaks the percentage monotony** — "Buy 1, Get 1 Free" is a visually distinct format that stands out in the tier stack.
- **Real product photography throughout** — plants, decor, and craft items are rendered cleanly with no broken images.
- **Michaels Rewards "Earn $5" section** — members get an incremental reason to click; the earn mechanic is a genuine differentiator from a raw-discount offer.
- **Multiple category sections** — a shopper scanning for one specific department can find their discount tier relatively quickly.

## What's weak

- **Subject is a tease with no payoff** — "Wait till you see your deals!" promises surprise but delivers the same stacked-sale format subscribers already received hours earlier; the earlier 5/29 subject ("Score up to 50% off") was actually more informative.
- **No deadline anywhere in the body** — six discount tiers with zero "ends Sunday" or "today only" language gives subscribers no reason to act now over later.
- **Visual hierarchy collapses after the hero** — every section below the fold is identical in size, color weight, and spacing, creating scroll fatigue rather than a buying journey.
- **Rewards moment is buried in the footer** — the one loyalty differentiator is treated as fine print after six promotional blocks.
- **Two sends in one day** — cadence fatigue is real; the second identical-format email in a single day trains subscribers to ignore rather than engage.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Don't send a second same-format sale email the same day.** If this send must exist, differentiate it completely — clearance only, a single hero BOGO, or a "your category" segmented version. Repeating the stacked-discount format within hours destroys the urgency lever Michaels has been building all month.
- 2. **Lead the subject with the actual offer.** The BOGO is the most emotionally compelling and visually differentiated deal in the email — put it in the subject line where it can pull opens.
- 3. **Add a hard deadline to the hero.** "Sale ends Sunday" or even "today only" alongside the 30% banner would provide the conversion urgency that is currently completely absent from this email.
- 4. **Move the Rewards earn block to position two** — right after the hero — so members see the incremental incentive before they've already decided whether to click.
- 5. **Collapse to three offer tiers max.** Six competing sections don't multiply intent; they dilute it. Fewer, bigger offers convert better than a discount menu.
- **Subject Alt A:** `BOGO + 50% off plants — sale ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Earn $5 Rewards today (plus your biggest deals)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `30%, 50%, and a BOGO — pick your best deal before it's gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Earn Rewards while you shop — $5 waiting for you inside.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Functional email with no broken merge tokens or dead landing domains, but three issues warrant fixes before the next send: two HTTP-served images that will be blocked by Gmail/Outlook secure-image enforcement, stray malformed tags in the HTML source, and unconfirmed SPF/DKIM authentication due to missing relay headers.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text body is 83% URLs (33,552 / 40,249 chars)**
QA flag. Spam filters score plain-text ratio; a body that is almost entirely redirect URLs is a deliverability risk. The plain-text alternate should contain readable content, not just link dumps.

**22 click-tracking links skipped**
All route through `click.emdeals.michaels.com`. QA cannot verify destination health. No broken URLs were confirmed, but redirect chain depth is unverifiable without probing.

**Four separate tracking pixel vendors active simultaneously**
- Coremetrics/IBM (`click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx`)
- Thunderhead ONE (`na5.thunderhead.com/one/rt/track/ONE-PFSLHYI824-2685/pixel`)
- iGoDigital/Nova Collect (`nova.collect.igodigital.com/c2/100010552/track_event`)
- SilverPop (`svopn.e.michaels.com`)

No functional issue, but parallel open-tracking pixels add load time and increase the mixed-content surface (see §3).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources — will be blocked in secure clients**

Two assets served over plain HTTP:

| Asset | URL |
|---|---|
| Content image (product/promo) | `http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/...e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` |
| SilverPop open-tracker | `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true&...` |

Gmail and Outlook both proxy/block HTTP image loads. The content image will render broken for a significant portion of recipients.

**Content image missing `alt` text**

`e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` has no `alt` attribute. When the image fails to load (or images are disabled), recipients see a blank. Tracking pixels missing `alt=""` are lower priority but contribute to accessibility warnings.

**Stray `</custom>` closing tags**

Immediately after the open-pixel `<img>`, the source contains:
```html
<img src="https://click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx?..." width="1" height="1" alt="">
</custom>
</custom>
```
These are invalid closing tags with no matching open element. Most clients will silently ignore them, but strict XML parsers and some accessibility tools will flag them as malformed. They should be removed.

**Malformed HTML comment in preamble**

The document opens with `<!-- <!-- -->` — a nested comment-open sequence. This is technically invalid HTML and may confuse parsers that do not implement comment parsing leniently.

**Invalid CSS value: `max-height: auto`**

In the `@media (max-width: 480px)` block:
```css
img { max-width: 100% !important; height: auto !important; max-height: auto !important; }
```
`auto` is not a valid value for `max-height` (valid values are `none`, a length, or a percentage). This is ignored by all current engines but should be `none` to be correct.

**DOCTYPE is HTML 4.01 Transitional**

Not a bug — this is the standard for broad email client compatibility — but worth noting as a known constraint if HTML5 features are considered in future templates.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header absent** (QA WARN)
Header not captured by the AgentMail relay. This may be a relay artifact rather than a sender omission — SFMC typically injects this header. Confirm by examining raw MIME headers from a direct inbox capture. If genuinely absent, this violates Gmail/Yahoo 2024 bulk-sender requirements.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post header absent** (QA WARN)
RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is not confirmed. Same caveat as above — verify at the raw-header level.

**Authentication-Results header not found** (QA WARN)
SPF and DKIM pass/fail status is unknown. The sending domain `emdeals.michaels.com` is a dedicated ESP subdomain and almost certainly has DKIM configured, but this cannot be confirmed from available data. A direct inbox capture (not via relay) is needed to verify.

**Physical address / CAN-SPAM footer**
HTML is truncated; footer content not auditable. Assumed present based on Michaels' standard templates.
