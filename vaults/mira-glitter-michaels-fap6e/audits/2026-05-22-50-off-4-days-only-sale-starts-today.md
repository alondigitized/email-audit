# 50% off 📣 4 days only 📣 Sale starts TODAY!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- This is Michaels' third promotional email in three days and the ninth (or close to it) in eight days — a cadence that has now crossed from aggressive into inbox-fatigue territory. The send is a dense, multi-block sale email with at least six distinct offer tiers stacked vertically: a 25% off hero at the top, followed by wave after wave of 50% off and 30% off category blocks, capped by a Rewards module. If this looks familiar, it's because the May 20 "Big Summer Sale" email ran almost the same playbook — stacked percentage blocks, same Rewards footer, similar product thumbnails.
- The most glaring problem is a subject-body disconnect: the subject screams "50% off" but the hero that greets you on open is a 25% off banner. The subscriber who opened expecting the bigger deal sees a smaller one first. That's a trust chip, however minor. Structurally, the email also suffers from having no dominant CTA — every block has its own shop button, so nothing is actually primary.
- **Judgment:** A solid machine-generated sale email that produces reliable results by volume, not by craft. It will drive clicks from motivated deal-hunters, but it is burning list goodwill at a high rate and leaving personalization and hierarchy wins on the table.

## What's working

- **Urgency framing lands** — "4 days only" in the subject, paired with the visible sale start date, creates a credible countdown without resorting to fake timer gimmicks.
- **Offer density is real** — Multiple distinct percentage tiers (25%, 30%, 50%) give different shoppers something to latch on to; this isn't a single thin offer.
- **Michaels Rewards module appears** — The "Earn $5" loyalty section at the bottom adds member-level value and serves as a secondary reason to click for enrolled members.
- **Clean render** — No broken images, overlapping text, or layout gaps visible anywhere in the email.
- **Patriotic/seasonal visual hook** — The flag imagery in the hero anchors the email to the Memorial Day window cleanly.

## What's weak

- **Subject-hero disconnect** — Subject promises 50% off; the first thing you see on open is 25% off. Even half a second of confusion erodes trust.
- **No dominant CTA** — Every offer block has its own "Shop" button, which means there's no primary action — the email reads as a catalog, not a campaign.
- **Cadence is now a liability** — This is email #9-ish in eight days. The May 20 "Big Summer Sale" already ran a nearly identical stacked-blocks format. Repetition kills urgency.
- **Hero product is generic** — The patriotic banner does seasonal work but doesn't lead with one aspirational product; there's no "wow, I want that" visual anchor.
- **No social proof anywhere** — For a brand this large, not a single review snippet, star rating, or UGC reference appears across a long email.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the subject-hero alignment** — If 50% off is the hook, the hero image and headline must reflect 50% off, not 25%. Either reorder the offer blocks so the deepest discount opens the email, or rewrite the subject to match what the hero actually says.
- 2. **Pick one hero product and feature it above the fold** — A single beautifully photographed "50% off" item with a specific name and a single "Shop This" button will outperform six simultaneous category blocks competing for attention.
- 3. **Reduce email frequency or segment harder** — Three emails in three days using near-identical creative is a list-churn accelerator. Segment Memorial Day deals to buyers who engaged with the May 20 sale; suppress recent openers who already converted.
- 4. **Add a real countdown or deadline date in the body** — "4 days only" is in the subject but if that deadline isn't reinforced visibly inside the email (a date, a timer, or even bold text), it dissipates by the second scroll.
- 5. **Pull one review or bestseller badge into a product tile** — A "Top-Rated" or "#1 in Yarn" tag on a single featured product adds credibility without redesigning the whole layout.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your 50% off Memorial Day sale is here`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop art supplies, frames, floral, and more — sale ends Monday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `25% and 50% off across the store. Plus earn Rewards on every order.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email renders from a structurally malformed document — the `<!DOCTYPE>` declaration appears mid-body after table markup, indicating broken template assembly. Four separate open-tracking pixels are fired on open, two of which use unencrypted HTTP, and compliance headers are absent from the relay-captured copy.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**DOCTYPE displacement / template assembly failure**
The `<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN">` declaration appears *inside* body content, after an opening `<table>` and `<td>`. This means the document has no valid doctype from the client's perspective. The pre-DOCTYPE block also contains two bare `</custom>` closing tags with no matching opening tags — artifacts of a failed dynamic content injection block (`<!--MASTER_Coremetrics_SLPH_Content-->`).

**Four concurrent open-tracking pixels**
| Pixel | Protocol | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx?P5PNNBXOE4...` | HTTPS | Salesforce/ExactTarget open pixel |
| `na5.thunderhead.com/one/rt/track/ONE-PFSLHYI824-2685/pixel` | HTTPS | Thunderhead ONE behavioral pixel |
| `nova.collect.igodigital.com/c2/100010552/track_event` | HTTPS | iGoDigital/SFMC collect pixel |
| `svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true` | **HTTP** | Silverpop pixel — unencrypted |

Firing four open pixels is redundant infrastructure that increases payload weight, slows render, and raises spam-filter suspicion. Two pixels have no `alt` text.

**22 click-tracking links unprobed**
All CTAs route through `click.emdeals.michaels.com` — destination URLs are opaque until redirected. QA flagged all 22 as skipped. Broken destination URLs cannot be detected programmatically.

**Plain-text body is 81% URLs**
28,748 of 35,626 characters in the plain-text alternative are URLs. Spam filters (SpamAssassin, Google Postmaster) score heavily against this ratio. The plain-text version likely consists of unrendered tracking URLs rather than human-readable fallback content.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image sources — will be blocked**
```
src: http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/lib/fe4015707564057d701173/m/1/e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg
src: http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true&...
```
Gmail blocks mixed-content images by default. Outlook 365 proxies HTTP images through a Microsoft cache, potentially altering dimensions. The content image (`e323ae89...`) will show as broken in Gmail if images are enabled but HTTP is blocked.

**Invalid CSS: `max-height: auto`**
```css
img { max-height: auto !important; }
```
`auto` is not a valid value for `max-height`. Browsers and email clients will ignore this declaration. Intended value is likely `none` or a pixel value. This appears in the `@media (max-width: 480px)` block, so mobile image scaling may be affected.

**Duplicate `p { margin: 1em 0; }` rule**
Declared twice in the `<head>` `<style>` block — harmless but indicates copy-paste template hygiene issues.

**Missing alt text — 4 images**
All four flagged images have empty or absent `alt` attributes:
- `e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` (content image)
- Thunderhead pixel
- iGoDigital pixel
- Silverpop pixel

Tracking pixels intentionally omit alt text (acceptable), but the content image (`e323ae89...`) missing alt text is an accessibility and deliverability issue.

**Deprecated `text="#000000"` body attribute**
Not a rendering blocker in current clients, but signals an aged template baseline.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent from relay capture**
QA reports neither `List-Unsubscribe` nor `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) headers were captured. Gmail and Yahoo now require one-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058) for bulk senders. If these headers are present on the SMTP envelope but stripped by the AgentMail relay, this is a relay configuration issue, not a sender issue — but it must be confirmed. If genuinely absent, Michaels is out of compliance with Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender requirements effective February 2024.

**Authentication-Results absent**
SPF and DKIM pass/fail status cannot be confirmed from the relay-captured copy. `emdeals.michaels.com` is a dedicated ESP subdomain — DKIM alignment should be verified against the sending domain's published records directly.

**CAN-SPAM physical address**
Not visible in the truncated HTML but required. Assumed present in the email footer (standard for Michaels sends); cannot confirm from available source.

**Preheader/subject mismatch**
- Subject: `50% off 📣 4 days only 📣 Sale starts TODAY!`
- Preheader: `Save on patriotic faves, frames, floral, décor & more — Memorial Day Sale is on!`

The preheader adds useful context not in the subject — no compliance issue, but the subject implies a generic sale while the preheader correctly identifies it as Memorial Day. Not a CAN-SPAM issue.
