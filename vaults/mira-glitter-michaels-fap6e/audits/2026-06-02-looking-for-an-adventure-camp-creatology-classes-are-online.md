# 🏝️ Looking for an adventure? Camp Creatology classes are ONLINE.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-02**
## Executive summary

- This is the third Camp Creatology-adjacent send in roughly 36 hours — following June 1's "Screen-free summer fun for kids" (7/10) and the adult companion creative that scored 9/10. Where those sent were inspirational, this one is executional: online class announcement, Week 2 specificity (Ice Cream Party), supply shopping, and a 25% off hero. The program-marketing structure is a genuine strength — "Week 2" implies a series, which gives subscribers a reason to return beyond a one-time discount hit.
- The email earns its keep mostly through contrast. After May's discount-fatigue parade and even today's earlier "Fill your cart for less" send, something with actual editorial structure (a camp, a theme, a week number) reads as refreshing. The 25% off hook is clearly visible in the hero, the visual hierarchy is clean, and the Michaels Rewards section at the bottom adds a secondary incentive layer.
- The weak link is the subject line. "Looking for an adventure?" is a generic opener that buries the real news — "Camp Creatology is online now" — in the back half of an already-long string. And landing three summer-camp emails in two days compresses whatever halo effect each individual send builds. Solid execution; questionable cadence decision.

## What's working

- **25% off hero is prominent and immediately legible** — the blue summer sky with bold discount treatment makes the offer land on first scroll.
- **"Week 2: Ice Cream Party" specificity** creates a program narrative; subscribers feel like they're joining something, not just being sold something.
- **Visual variety** — the email mixes class-promotion, supply shopping, and a kit CTA ($49 visible) without feeling cluttered.
- **Michaels Rewards "Earn $5" footer** reinforces loyalty value for existing members and adds a second reason to click.
- **Seasonal hook is well-timed** — June 2 is peak "what are we doing this summer?" parental anxiety, and this email shows up exactly on cue.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is too long and front-loads the weak hook** — "Looking for an adventure?" wastes 27 characters before naming the actual program; on mobile this is mostly what recipients read.
- **Demographic split creates segmentation risk** — the email is heavily kids/parent-oriented, which alienates the large adult-only crafter cohort in Michaels' subscriber base.
- **Two competing discount signals** (25% off in the hero, 20% off in a lower section) dilute each other; neither feels like the definitive offer.
- **No visible deadline or urgency cue** — "classes are ONLINE" implies permanence rather than a limited enrollment window; nothing pushes action today versus next week.
- **Third summer-camp send in <48 hours** from Michaels — cadence is compressing the creative investment.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Shorten and invert the subject** — lead with the program name and the news, not a question. "Camp Creatology is online — Week 2 starts now" does more work in fewer characters.
- 2. **Add a deadline or enrollment signal** — even "Spots fill fast" or "Week 2 starts June 7" gives the open a reason to happen today rather than whenever.
- 3. **Pick one discount and commit** — either 25% off is the hero and 20% is the footer-level member benefit, or consolidate. Two separate percentage-offs create decision paralysis.
- 4. **Segment the send** — parents/families get this camp-forward version; adult crafters get the 25% off supply angle without the kids-camp wrapper.
- 5. **Surface Week 2 theme earlier** — "Ice Cream Party" is a charming, clickable detail that's buried below the fold; it should be in the subject or preheader.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Camp Creatology Week 2: Ice Cream Party is live`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your summer camp starts online — 25% off supplies`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Week 2 theme drops now — grab your supplies before spots fill`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Ice Cream Party crafts + 25% off everything you need`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has two structural defects that affect rendering fidelity (misplaced DOCTYPE, unclosed custom tags), five accessibility violations, two HTTP mixed-content image sources, and missing RFC-required unsubscribe headers that expose the sender to deliverability penalties from Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules enforced since February 2024.

**Multiple open/tracking pixels**
Four separate pixel systems are firing:

- `na5.thunderhead.com/one/rt/track/ONE-PFSLHYI824-2685/pixel` — Thunderhead CDP (HTTPS, no alt)

**PII in pixel payloads**

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**DOCTYPE placement (critical)**
`<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN">` appears mid-document, after a `<table>` block and two `</custom>` closing tags. Any content before the DOCTYPE triggers quirks-mode parsing in standards-compliant clients; Outlook and WebKit-based clients will handle this differently, risking layout breakage.

**Unclosed/unknown `<custom>` tags**
Two `</custom>` closing elements appear with no corresponding opening tag in the visible source — likely unresolved ESP template fragments. These are invalid in HTML 4.01 Transitional and will be silently ignored by most parsers, but create parse-tree anomalies.

**Invalid CSS value**
`max-height: auto` appears in the mobile media query (`img` rule). `auto` is not a valid value for `max-height`; the correct keyword is `none`. Some clients will discard the entire rule.

**Five images missing `alt` text** (confirmed by QA):
| Image | Source |
|---|---|
| `736b2b4b...gif` | `image.emdeals.michaels.com` |
| `e323ae89...jpg` | `image.emdeals.michaels.com` (also HTTP) |
| Thunderhead pixel | `na5.thunderhead.com` |
| iGoDigital pixel | `nova.collect.igodigital.com` |
| Siteverse pixel | `svopn.e.michaels.com` (also HTTP) |

Screen readers will announce these as unlabeled. Decorative tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` explicitly.

**Two HTTP image sources** (will be blocked or proxied):
- `http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/lib/fe4015707564057d701173/m/1/e323ae89-...jpg`
- `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true&...`

Gmail and Outlook proxy all images through HTTPS; HTTP sources may result in broken images in non-proxying clients (Apple Mail with image blocking, some mobile clients).

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent** (QA confirmed)
Gmail and Yahoo have required this header for bulk senders (>5k/day) since February 2024. Its absence will suppress the native unsubscribe UI in Gmail and may trigger spam classification.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent** (QA confirmed, RFC 8058)
One-click machine unsubscribe (required by the same Gmail/Yahoo policy) is not present. This is a deliverability risk for high-volume sends to those providers.

**`Authentication-Results` header not captured**
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status are unknown due to AgentMail relay not surfacing this header. Cannot confirm alignment between `emdeals.michaels.com` envelope domain and any published DMARC policy. Should be verified at the MTA level.

**CAN-SPAM physical address**
Not verifiable from the truncated HTML; standard for Michaels emails but should be confirmed in full source.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

Links carry `cm_mmc=EMAIL-_-Brand_Story-_-20260602_S3_VariableCampCreatology_Story_FF_US-_-[PLACEMENT]` and `cm_mmca2=3393863`. Attribution chain is intact for IBM/Coremetrics analytics.

**Attribution fragmentation**
Four tracking systems fire simultaneously (ESP, Thunderhead, iGoDigital, Siteverse). If these systems are not deduplicated server-side, open and click counts will be overcounted across platforms. Not a code defect per se, but a data hygiene risk.
