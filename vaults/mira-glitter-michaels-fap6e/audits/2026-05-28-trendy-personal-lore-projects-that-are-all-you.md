# Trendy personal lore projects that are all YOU ‼️
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-28**
## Executive summary

- Michaels is tapping the "personal lore" aesthetic — a genuine 2025/2026 social-media trend around self-expression crafts, journaling, and identity collage — as the editorial spine of this product email. The concept is smart and culturally legible: it's the same move as the Grandmacore send on 5/26 (8/10), using a named cultural moment to make a product catalog feel intentional rather than promotional. The 25% banner anchors value immediately above the fold, and the personalization category (sublimation, photo printing, custom mixed-media kits) maps cleanly to the subject.
- The execution loses the thread halfway through. What starts as a cohesive identity piece fractures into four distinct sub-theme sections — "Be you your way," "So unique it's printed," "Designer, but make it DIY," "Your story, your way" — each with its own header and product grid. By the third section, the personal-lore mood has dissolved into a standard catalog scroll. A subscriber primed for self-expression content gets a warehouse.
- This is also Michaels' second email today — the "BRB: Our Sale is ON" blast went out earlier on 5/28. Two emails in one day is a meaningful friction point, and this one asks for attention without offering the same urgency hook.

## What's working

- **Trend anchor is sharp.** "Personal lore" is specific and culturally current; naming it in the subject positions Michaels as aware, not just discounting.
- **25% offer above the fold.** The discount banner is the first thing the eye lands on — value prop established before the first scroll.
- **Category breadth is coherent.** Sublimation, photo products, cutting/Cricut-style kits, and mixed media all live naturally under the personalization umbrella.
- **MichaelsRewards "Earn $5" block** at the bottom keeps loyalty members from feeling the email isn't for them.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, overlapping text, or layout artifacts in the screenshot.

## What's weak

- **No deadline, no urgency.** A 25% offer with no end date floats without consequence — "shop whenever" is not a call to action.
- **Four sub-themes dilute the lead.** Each new section header re-explains the concept instead of deepening it; the personal-lore identity hook is gone by section two.
- **No single hero product.** The email bets on everything, which is the same as betting on nothing — no one product is positioned as the must-have this week.
- **‼️ double exclamation in subject** is a mild spam signal and looks slightly anxious next to an otherwise editorial concept.
- **Second email today.** Subscribers who opened the "BRB: Our Sale is ON" send earlier this morning are being asked for attention again inside the same session.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one hero product and frame the entire email around it.** Choose the best-margin or highest-velocity personalization SKU (custom photo mug, sublimation starter kit, the Cricut bundle) and make it the emotional anchor. The other products can appear below as supporting cast.
- 2. **Add a deadline to the 25% offer.** "Ends Sunday night" is enough. The offer is invisible without a close date.
- 3. **Collapse the sub-theme headers into one through-line.** One emotional arc — "this is how your personal lore takes a physical form" — sustains the mood the subject promises.
- 4. **Drop the ‼️ from the subject.** The concept earns the open on its own.

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has two structural defects that affect rendering and deliverability: a misplaced `DOCTYPE` declaration embedded mid-document inside a `<table>`, and two HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources that will be blocked by modern clients. Compliance headers are absent from what the relay captured, which is a separate but material risk.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Misplaced DOCTYPE** — The `<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN">` declaration appears inside an already-open `<table><tr><td>` structure, after a `</custom>` tag (itself invalid HTML). A DOCTYPE mid-document is ignored by parsers; the email renders in quirks mode regardless. Evidence: HTML lines ~5–6 of source.

**22 tracking links not probed** — All click links route through `click.emdeals.michaels.com`. The HTTP probe skipped them by design, so redirect-chain integrity (final landing page, HTTPS termination) is unverified this cycle.

**Four distinct tracking stacks active simultaneously:**
- Coremetrics (`cm_mmc=` params) — legacy IBM platform; comment `<!--MASTER_Coremetrics_SLPH_Content-->` suggests this template predates the Salesforce migration
- Salesforce Marketing Cloud (`sfmc_Sub=`, `emId=`, `nova.collect.igodigital.com`)
- Thunderhead ONE (`na5.thunderhead.com/one/rt/track/ONE-PFSLHYI824-2685/pixel`)
- SailThru (`svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847`)

Running four tracking pixels multiplies load time and creates redundant subscriber identity signals. Not a hard defect, but worth flagging for template hygiene.

**Thunderhead pixel — truncated email parameter** — The pixel src ends with `emailaddress%3DMIR`, cutting off mid-value. If the full subscriber email is not being sent, Thunderhead open-event attribution for this send will be incomplete.
Evidence: `src="https://na5.thunderhead.com/one/rt/track/ONE-PFSLHYI824-2685/pixel?uri=outbound%3A%2F%2Femailpixel%2F?emailaddress%3DMIR"`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Two HTTP image sources** — Will be blocked or downgraded by Gmail, Outlook.com, Apple Mail, and Yahoo (all enforce HTTPS for remote content since 2023–2024):
- `http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/lib/fe4015707564057d701173/m/1/e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` — appears to be a content image (not just a pixel), so blocking causes a visible broken image
- `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true&...` — SailThru open-tracking pixel

**Four images missing `alt` text:**
| Image | Issue |
|---|---|
| `e323ae89-...jpg` (content image) | No `alt`; also HTTP — double failure |
| `na5.thunderhead.com` pixel | No `alt=""` on tracking pixel |
| `nova.collect.igodigital.com/track_event` | No `alt=""` on tracking pixel |
| `svopn.e.michaels.com` open pixel | No `alt=""`; also HTTP |

Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` (empty, not absent) to pass accessibility linting and avoid screen readers announcing a broken image label.

**Duplicate CSS rule** — `p { margin: 1em 0; }` is declared twice in the same `<style>` block. No rendering impact in practice, but indicates template was assembled by concatenation without deduplication.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent** — Not captured by the AgentMail relay. Google and Yahoo's 2024 bulk-sender requirements mandate this header for lists over 5K recipients/day. Without it, Gmail will suppress the native unsubscribe UI and may route to spam.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent** — RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe not implemented (or not relayed). Required by the same Google/Yahoo policy for bulk senders.

**`Authentication-Results` header absent** — SPF and DKIM pass/fail status unknown for this send. The sending domain is `emdeals.michaels.com`; if DMARC alignment is misconfigured at the relay level, deliverability impact is significant. This is likely a relay capture gap, but it cannot be confirmed from available data.

**CAN-SPAM physical address** — Not visible in the truncated source. Assumed present in footer (standard for Michaels), but cannot confirm.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

UTM parameters are present on at least one link: `cm_mmc=EMAIL-_-Brand_Story-_-20260528_S3_TryThisNextTrendReportMainCharacter_Story_FF_US-_-HEADER_HOME`. The `cm_mmc` format is Coremetrics (now IBM Digital Analytics), not standard `utm_source/utm_medium/utm_campaign`. If the Michaels site has migrated analytics to GA4 or another platform, these parameters will not be attributed correctly.

The 22 click links were not probed, so landing-page HTTP→HTTPS redirect chains and UTM preservation at final destination are unverified.
