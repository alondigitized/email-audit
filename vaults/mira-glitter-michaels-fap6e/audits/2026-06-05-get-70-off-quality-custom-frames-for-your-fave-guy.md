# 🥰 Get 70% off quality custom frames for your fave guy.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-05**
## Executive summary

- This is Michaels Custom Framing's Father's Day push, arriving about 10 days before the holiday — perfectly timed. The hero line "If you frame it, Dad will hang it" is the strongest thing in the email: compact, emotional, and on-brief. The 70% off badge is large and visible immediately, so the value prop lands without scrolling.
- The problem is déjà vu. The Memorial Day send on May 24 ran the same 70% off + $10 in-store offer with essentially the same creative skeleton. Two weeks later, swapping the occasion but keeping the exact same discount erodes credibility — it reads less like a genuine promotion and more like a permanent sale with rotating holiday copy. The email also runs very long, stacking four or five distinct modules (coupon, how-it-works, 10-minute service, 60% section, employee photo) that dilute attention past the fold.
- The unexplained Arabic text block near the bottom footer is either a localization bug or a mis-targeted audience segment — either way it breaks the polish of the send and warrants a fix before the next deployment.

## What's working

- **Hero headline lands** — "If you frame it, Dad will hang it." is warm, specific, and gift-occasion-perfect.
- **70% badge is immediately legible** — prominent placement, high contrast, no ambiguity about what the offer is.
- **Layered offer structure** — 70% off online + $10 off in-store rewards both shopping modes without conflict.
- **Father's Day timing is right** — 10 days out is the sweet spot for a considered gift purchase like custom framing.
- **Process visualization (Upload → Customize → Preview → Pick Up)** removes the main anxiety about custom framing being complicated or slow.

## What's weak

- **Offer fatigue** — same 70% + $10 combo as the May 24 Memorial Day send; the deal is starting to feel permanent rather than urgent.
- **Arabic text block at the bottom** — visible, unexplained, and looks like a segmentation or template bug; undermines brand finish.
- **Too many modules** — the email stacks a coupon, a how-it-works explainer, a $29 section, a 60% offer, and an employee spotlight; each dilutes the next.
- **No deadline visible in the hero** — "Limited-Time Offer" appears only mid-scroll on the coupon banner; the hero doesn't anchor the urgency.
- **Subject emoji (🥰) feels slightly off** — the blushing-heart face skews greeting-card; a gift emoji or frame emoji would stay more on-brand with their usual tone.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add an expiry date to the hero** — "Offer ends June 13" or "Order by June 12 for Father's Day delivery" creates honest urgency and gives a clear reason to act now vs. next week.
- 2. **Differentiate the offer from May 24** — same 70%+$10 back-to-back reads as a permanent discount. Add a Father's Day exclusive (free liner upgrade, personalized engraving, gift wrap) to justify the occasion.
- 3. **Cut to two modules max** — hero + offer, then one how-it-works strip. Remove the 60% section; it conflicts with the 70% lead and confuses which deal to claim.
- 4. **Fix or remove the Arabic text block** — if it's not intentional segmentation, pull it before the next send.
- 5. **Sharpen the CTA copy** — generic button labels ("Shop Now") should become something like "Frame a Gift for Dad" to stay occasion-specific.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Dad's gift, framed. 70% off — order by June 12.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `70% off custom frames — Father's Day deadline is close`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Frame it before June 12 and he'll hang it before Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free gift wrap with every Father's Day frame order.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[FAIL] DOCTYPE misplaced inside `<body>`**
The outer template emits a `<table>` wrapper before the `<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN">` declaration. The DOCTYPE appears mid-document, which is invalid HTML. Most email clients will still render it, but it triggers quirks-mode in any standards-aware parser and signals a broken template composition layer.

**[FAIL] CSS syntax errors**

| Location | Error |
|---|---|
| `a:visted` | Typo — should be `a:visited`. Visited link color override silently fails. |
| `@-ms-viewport { width: device-width; s }` | Trailing `s` is invalid CSS. Entire rule likely discarded by IE/Outlook. |
| `img { max-height: auto !important; }` (mobile MQ) | `max-height: auto` is not a valid value. Has no effect but indicates copy-paste error. |

**[WARN] Three tracking pixels missing `alt` text**
- `https://na5.thunderhead.com/one/rt/track/...` (Medallia/Thunderhead journey pixel)
- `https://nova.collect.igodigital.com/c2/100010552/track_event?...` (SFMC iGoDigital personalization pixel)
- `http://svopn.aaronbrothers.com/...` (Aaron Brothers open-track pixel)

Screen readers will announce the full URL string. Standard fix is `alt=""` on decorative/tracking pixels.
