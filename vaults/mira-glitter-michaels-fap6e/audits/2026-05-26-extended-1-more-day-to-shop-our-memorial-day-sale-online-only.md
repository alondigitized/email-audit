# EXTENDED! 1 more day to shop our Memorial Day Sale - online only.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-26**
## Executive summary

- This is email fifteen (or close to it) in Michaels' Memorial Day series, and the "EXTENDED! 1 more day" hook is a classic retail shelf-life extension — the sale was supposed to end, now it hasn't. That framing can work once; at this cadence, subscribers who've been getting daily 50%-off blasts since May 12 will recognize the manufactured urgency immediately. The "online only" qualifier is the one genuinely useful differentiator here: it gives lapsed in-store shoppers a real reason to re-engage digitally, and it channels scarcity to a specific format rather than crying wolf again.
- The email itself is structurally identical to the prior dozen sends — dense multi-category scroll, competing discount callouts from 20% to 60%, a BOGO buried mid-scroll, and the Rewards footer. Nothing in the creative signals that this send is different from May 22 or May 23. After yesterday's genuinely smart Grandmacore trend email and the Jonathan Adler collab launch, dropping back into a generic percentage-soup feels like a creative regression.
- The bottom line: the "online only + 1 more day" message is legitimate but it's wrapped in a template that has fully exhausted its persuasive credibility with this list.

## What's working

- **"Online only" is a real channel signal** — it distinguishes this send from the in-store countdowns and gives digital-first shoppers a specific reason to act.
- **Hero discount is immediately visible** — the 50% off callout hits above the fold with no scrolling required.
- **Michaels Rewards "Earn $5" footer** — loyalty benefit is surfaced, which is the right thing to do on a high-intent sale send.
- **Render is clean** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no layout breakage across the visible scroll.

## What's weak

- **Cadence has destroyed urgency credibility** — this is roughly the fifteenth Memorial Day email; "1 more day" reads as the same lie subscribers heard on May 23 and May 24.
- **Too many competing discount callouts** — 25%, 40%, 50%, 60%, $2.99, BOGO, 20% are all visible in the scroll with no clear visual hierarchy; the eye has nowhere to land.
- **"EXTENDED!" ALL CAPS** — spam-signal territory and slightly adversarial in tone; it reads as defensive rather than exciting.
- **No creative differentiation** — this template is byte-for-byte identical to the prior sends; there is nothing in the visual treatment that signals "online only extension" versus another generic sale blast.
- **"Shop all deals" CTA is maximally generic** — after a week of identical CTAs, this copy has zero pull.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the "online only" angle as the hero, not a discount percentage.** A dedicated banner that says "In-store sale is over — but you can still shop online through midnight" gives subscribers a real news hook. The extension stops feeling like a lie and starts feeling like a benefit.
- 2. **Cut to one category, not fifteen.** Pick the highest-converting category from the prior two weeks of sale data (presumably yarn/floral/seasonal) and make this email about one thing. The scatter-shot format has had two weeks to prove itself; it hasn't earned another send.
- 3. **Replace "Shop all deals" with something time-anchored** — "Shop by midnight tonight" or "Last chance online" anchors the click to the deadline.
- 4. **Drop the ALL CAPS "EXTENDED!"** — it reads as panic. "Extended through tonight" in title case is calmer and more credible.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last chance online: sale ends tonight at midnight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `In-store sale is over — still 50% off online`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stock from the Memorial Day sale is still available online. Shop before it's gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `One more day to grab what sold out in stores.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Michaels Memorial Day Sale Extension

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has no critical blockers but carries multiple HTTP-over-HTTPS image issues and unverifiable authentication state due to relay header stripping. The plain-text part is URL-dominated, which is a deliverability signal.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**22 tracking links skipped** — all click-redirects route through `click.emdeals.michaels.com`. Live resolution could not be confirmed by the QA probe; destination URLs and redirect chain health are unverified.

**Pixel tracking over HTTP** — two open-tracking pixels use plain HTTP:
- `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true&sv_svemi=6a0b1…` (SilverPop/Acoustic tracker)
- `http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/…/e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg`

Modern clients (Gmail, Apple Mail) block mixed-content resources. These pixels will silently fail to fire in any client enforcing HTTPS-only image loading, undermining open-rate accuracy.

**Multiple overlapping tracking systems** — the email fires pixels to at least four separate platforms: Coremetrics (IBM), Thunderhead ONE (`na5.thunderhead.com`), Salesforce IGO/Collect (`nova.collect.igodigital.com`), and SilverPop (`svopn.e.michaels.com`). This is an infrastructure observation, not a defect, but any one of these endpoints going stale silently degrades analytics.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Four images missing `alt` text** (confirmed by QA):
| Image | src (truncated) |
|---|---|
| `e323ae89…jpg` | `http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/…/e323ae89…jpg` |
| Thunderhead pixel | `https://na5.thunderhead.com/one/rt/track/…` |
| IGO collect pixel | `https://nova.collect.igodigital.com/c2/…/track_event` |
| SilverPop open pixel | `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847…` |

Tracking pixels with non-empty `alt` attributes will expose raw pixel URLs as visible text in screen readers. They should use `alt=""` explicitly — not omit the attribute.

**`max-height: auto` is not valid CSS** — the mobile media query contains `max-height: auto !important` on `img`. `auto` is not a valid value for `max-height`; this rule is silently ignored but is noise that could mask future overrides.

**HTML comment nesting malformation** — the `<head>` opens with `<!-- <!-- -->` (nested comment start). This is invalid HTML and can cause some parsers to mis-tokenize the comment boundary, though in practice most email clients recover.

**Malformed closing tags** — `</custom>` appears twice after the open-tracking pixel `<img>`. `<custom>` is not a standard HTML element; these stray closing tags are benign but indicate a templating artifact left in production HTML.

**`DOCTYPE` is HTML 4.01 Transitional** — not a defect per se (HTML 4.01 is widely used in email), but worth noting that the `@-ms-viewport` rule in the stylesheet is an IE10-era reset that is no longer relevant.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Encoded email address in URLs** — `customerEmail=bWlyYS1nbGl0dGVyLW1pY2hhZWxzLWZhcDZlQGV0ZWxsLmFwcA==` is a Base64-encoded email address passed as a query parameter on the logo `href`. This is the etell test address (`mira-glitter-michaels-fap6e@etell.app`), confirming the send was to a test/monitored inbox. Personalization substitution is working.

**`mi_ecmp` parameter is empty** — `mi_ecmp=DCEmail20260526` is present (correct), but `cm_mmca2=` is an empty-value UTM-adjacent parameter on the header logo link. If this is a required Coremetrics field, it should either be populated or removed; empty parameters add noise to analytics without providing signal.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not detected** — the QA relay did not capture this header. This is a hard requirement for bulk senders under Gmail/Yahoo 2024 sender policy. If the header is being set by the ESP before relay but stripped by the AgentMail capture layer, this is a monitoring gap, not a send-time gap — but it cannot be confirmed either way from this data.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not detected** — same caveat as above. Without this header, one-click unsubscribe (as required for Gmail bulk senders >5k/day) cannot be confirmed. Should be verified directly against raw SMTP headers from the ESP, not through the relay.

**`Authentication-Results` header absent** — SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status are unverifiable from this capture. The sending domain (`emdeals.michaels.com`) is a subdomain; alignment with the `From:` header domain must be confirmed at the ESP level.

**CAN-SPAM physical address** — not verifiable from the truncated HTML provided. Full footer must include a valid postal address; this should be confirmed in the full source.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM parameters present and consistent** — the header logo URL contains:
```
cm_mmc=EMAIL-_-DCE-_-20260526_S2_US_TempD_DCE_FF_US-_-HEADER_HOME
```
Coremetrics tagging is present. Standard UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) are not used; the site appears to rely on Coremetrics/IBM tagging exclusively. If GA4 or any non-Coremetrics analytics tool is on the landing page, these clicks will appear as direct traffic.

**Landing page alignment** — the 22 click-tracking links were not probed, so destination URL and landing page state (correct sale pricing, no 404s) cannot be confirmed from this audit.
