# Screen-free summer fun for kids is here!: 🏖️ 🫧 🖍️ ✂️
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-01**
## Executive summary

- After six consecutive Michaels sale emails hammering the same 30-50% off structure, this send finally shows some creative ambition. "Screen-free summer fun for kids" is a real cultural hook — parents in 2026 are actively seeking offline activities for kids, and Michaels has genuine products for that need. The "School's out, creativity is in!" hero copy lands that moment cleanly.
- That said, the editorial wrapper quickly dissolves into the same stacked-discount scroll format every recent Michaels email uses: 30% off hero, then 50% off section, then BOGO, then 25% off, then Rewards. The thematic freshness of the subject doesn't survive past the first scroll. If you came here for curated summer kids activities, you get a department-store wall of competing discounts instead.
- The subject line is a meaningful break from the sale-a-thon, but the body fails to honor the premise. A sender that has trained its list to expect "another sale email" needed this email to be structurally different — not just differently labeled. As a standalone send this is above average for Michaels' recent output; against the "screen-free summer" promise it underdelivers.

## What's working

- **Thematic hook is timely and genuine.** "Screen-free summer" names a real parental anxiety at exactly the right calendar moment (June 1, school-out season).
- **Hero copy is tighter than recent sends.** "School's out, creativity is in!" is the best Michaels one-liner in this audit window — punchy, occasion-specific, and on-brand.
- **Subject line emoji selection is smart.** 🫧🖍️✂️ encode the product promise visually; the bubbles emoji is a tiny delight that connects to actual summer play.
- **Rewards/loyalty block is visible.** The "Earn $5" Rewards call-out at the bottom adds a retention layer absent from recent pure-sale sends.
- **Trending on social section.** Acknowledging social proof (however generic) gives the email a currency signal that prior sends lacked.

## What's weak

- **Subject line punctuation is broken.** "is here!:" — an exclamation mark followed immediately by a colon — looks like a formatting error and signals sloppiness right at the inbox.
- **The body reverts to the same stacked-discount format.** Four separate discount tiers (30%, 50%, BOGO, 25%) collapse the kids-activities editorial into a generic sale scroll. The thematic promise evaporates after the first section.
- **No single hero product or activity.** "Screen-free summer" needs one irresistible anchor — a $12 tie-dye kit, a paint-your-own-pot set — not a content carousel. Shoppers can't hold a carousel in their head.
- **No credible deadline.** "Summer fun is here" implies the offer lasts all summer. Without a date or scarcity signal there's no reason to act now.
- **Subject is too long for mobile.** At ~53 visible characters (including emojis) it will likely truncate on most phones, cutting off some of the emoji charm.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the subject punctuation and tighten the line.** "Screen-free summer fun for kids is here!:" is awkward and long. Drop the colon.
- 2. **Pick one hero product/activity and build the whole email around it.** Lead with a $10-$15 activity kit at a specific price, then upsell related items. This is what the "personal lore" send from May 28 attempted — apply the same editorial discipline here.
- 3. **Add a hard deadline.** Even "this week only" beats "summer is here." Parents respond to scarcity when they're planning activities.
- 4. **Collapse the discount tiers.** Four competing percentage-off blocks undercut the credibility of each one. Pick the strongest and lead with it.
- 5. **Align preheader to the editorial hook**, not to a generic offer.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Screen-free summer starts with this 🖍️`
- **Subject Alt B:** `School's out — here's 30% off the good stuff`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Tie-dye, sand art, bubble kits — all on sale this week only`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Kids need a summer project. We've got 30% off to make it happen.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email renders from a malformed document structure (misplaced DOCTYPE) and carries three separate open/click tracking stacks with mixed HTTP/HTTPS assets. Two compliance headers are missing from the relay capture, and a base64-encoded recipient email address is exposed in plain text across multiple URLs.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Misplaced DOCTYPE**
The `<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN">` declaration appears *after* a `<table>` block and two `</custom>` closing tags. Doctype must be the document's first token; in its current position it is ignored by parsers, which may trigger quirks-mode rendering in Outlook and some webmail clients.

**Unclosed / non-standard tags**
Two `</custom>` closing tags appear immediately after the open-tracking `<img>` with no corresponding opening tag. These are non-standard and will generate parse errors.

**Three concurrent tracking stacks**
| Stack | Domain | Protocol |
|---|---|---|
| SFMC open pixel | `click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx` | HTTPS |
| Thunderhead behavior | `na5.thunderhead.com` | HTTPS |
| Selligent open pixel | `svopn.e.michaels.com` | **HTTP** |
| iGoDigital collect | `nova.collect.igodigital.com` | HTTPS |

Running four independent open-tracking pixels inflates open counts and fires four third-party network requests per open event.

**21 click-redirect links skipped by QA probe** — all route through `click.emdeals.michaels.com`; destination URLs and HTTP status codes unverifiable from this audit.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**HTTP image assets (2)**
- `http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/…/e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` — content image served over plain HTTP; will be blocked or show a broken-image warning in Gmail, Apple Mail, and any client enforcing mixed-content rules.
- `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true&…` — tracking pixel also on HTTP.

**Missing alt text (4 images)**
QA flagged the following `<img>` elements with empty or absent `alt`:
1. `e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` (content image — should carry descriptive alt)
2. Thunderhead pixel (`/pixel?uri=outbound…`)
3. iGoDigital collect pixel (`/track_event?payload=…`)
4. Selligent open pixel (`svopn.e.michaels.com`)

Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` explicitly to satisfy screen-reader pass-through; content images need descriptive alt text.

**`max-height: auto` is invalid CSS**
The mobile media query contains `.photo img { max-height: auto !important; }`. `auto` is not a valid value for `max-height`; the declaration is silently ignored. No functional breakage expected, but it indicates template hygiene issues.
