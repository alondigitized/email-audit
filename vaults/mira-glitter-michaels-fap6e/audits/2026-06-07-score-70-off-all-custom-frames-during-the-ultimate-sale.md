# Score 70% off all custom frames during The Ultimate Sale. ⚡
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-07**
## Executive summary

- Two days after sending "Get 70% off quality custom frames for your fave guy" (June 5), Michaels Custom Framing is back with the exact same headline discount wearing a new outfit. "The Ultimate Sale" is meant to signal a crescendo moment — the big one before Father's Day — but without a visible deadline or a differentiated offer, it reads as confirmation that 70% off is just always on the table. Subscribers who noticed the June 5th send will feel this, and it weakens the urgency the ⚡ emoji is trying to manufacture.
- Set the cadence problem aside and the email is solidly built. The hierarchy is clean: 70% off hero at the top, a $29 entry-price anchor mid-email, multiple use-case proof points ("Custom Frame anything, really"), and a closing "Custom Frame a Book" hook that broadens perceived utility. The multiple offer tiers (70% off → $29 floor → secondary 30% section) could create confusion about what the primary offer is, but the hero remains dominant enough that the email doesn't collapse into chaos.
- Judgment: competent execution, corroded by repetition. The brand is trading long-term urgency credibility for short-term coverage of the Father's Day window. One more identical send before June 15 and "70% off" stops meaning anything to this list.

## What's working

- **Clear hero hierarchy** — "70% off" dominates the top fold; no competing claims steal the opening beat.
- **$29 price anchor** — A specific floor price mid-email reduces the "custom framing is expensive" objection and gives fence-sitters a mental entry point.
- **"Custom Frame anything, really" section** — Broadens perceived use-case beyond gift-giving; good persuasion work for anyone who hasn't framed before.
- **No visible render bugs** — Layout holds cleanly across the full scroll; imagery loads, text doesn't overlap.
- **Father's Day timing** — The seasonal angle is still live and relevant at 8 days out.

## What's weak

- **Offer fatigue is real** — Identical 70% off pitch two days after the June 5 "fave guy" send. The "Ultimate Sale" framing doesn't compensate for the repeated discount.
- **No visible deadline** — "Ultimate Sale" implies scarcity but there's no "ends Sunday" or countdown visible; the urgency is asserted, not earned.
- **Multiple competing offers dilute focus** — 70% off at the top, $29 mid-email, a 30% off badge further down — subscribers have to do mental math about what the actual deal is.
- **Subject line is over mobile threshold** — 58 characters; will truncate on most mobile inbox previews right at "The Ultimate Sale," cutting the ⚡ entirely.
- **No social proof** — No review count, star rating, or "X frames ordered this week" signal anywhere in the scroll.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard deadline in the subject and hero** — "Ends Sunday" or "48 hours left" transforms "Ultimate Sale" from marketing copy into a real reason to act now. This is the single highest-leverage change.
- 2. **Lead the mid-email with Father's Day delivery guarantee** — "Order by [date], arrives before Father's Day" is more motivating than a secondary price callout; it removes the biggest objection for a gift buyer.
- 3. **Consolidate the offer narrative** — Pick one frame: either "70% off everything" OR "$29 entry + escalating value." Running both dilutes each. The 30% off section further down especially competes with the 70% off hero.
- 4. **Add social proof** — A single line ("Over 2M custom frames created") above the CTA button would do meaningful work given there's nothing there now.
- 5. **Shorten the subject by 8–10 characters** to survive mobile truncation.
- **Subject Alt A:** `70% off custom frames — Father's Day sale ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Ultimate Sale: 70% off frames, starting at $29`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Order by June 12 and it arrives in time. Free design help included.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `From $29. Frame photos, jerseys, diplomas — anything.`

## Technical Audit — Michaels Custom Framing Email (2026-06-07)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Two Salesforce Marketing Cloud (ExactTarget) URLs return HTTP 500 errors, which likely breaks the preference center and/or unsubscribe flow — a CAN-SPAM risk. The email also has zero plain-text fallback, an HTTP-only tracking pixel, and several CSS/HTML structural defects.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Critical — 500 server errors on ExactTarget endpoints:**

```
https://pub.s10.exacttarget.com/roevnimqczg/l5ia4u01aia?email_ID=...&mid=100010554
https://pub.s10.exacttarget.com/roevnimqczg/o520eeoyv3r?email_ID=...&mid=100010554
```

Both return HTTP 500. These URL patterns (`/l5ia4u01aia`, `/o520eeoyv3r`) are consistent with Salesforce Marketing Cloud preference center and profile center endpoints. If either resolves the unsubscribe path, the 500 constitutes an inoperable opt-out mechanism — a CAN-SPAM violation.

**HTTP (non-HTTPS) tracking pixel:**

```
src="http://svopn.aaronbrothers.com/?sv_cid=0415_04228&sv_emopen=true&sv_svemi=..."
```

Mixed-content; will be silently blocked by Gmail, Outlook web, and most modern clients. Also leaks subscriber identifier over plain HTTP. The Aaron Brothers cross-brand pixel is likely intentional (Michaels subsidiary), but the HTTP scheme is not.

**21 click-tracking links skipped** — these are redirect-domain links through `click.em.michaelscustomframing.com` and could not be probed; no pass/fail available for destination URL health.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**DOCTYPE placement error — critical for Outlook/legacy clients:**

The `<!DOCTYPE>` declaration appears _inside_ a `<table>` element, not at the document root:

```html
<table ... role="presentation" ...><tr><td ...><!--MASTER_Coremetrics_SLPH_Content--></td></tr></table>
<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN" ...>
<html>
```

This causes Outlook and strict-mode parsers to enter quirks mode for the entire document. The ESP appears to be injecting a wrapper table before the actual `<html>` tag — this is a template assembly defect.

**CSS defects:**

| Location | Issue |
|---|---|
| `a:visted` | Typo — should be `a:visited`; rule never fires |
| `@-ms-viewport { width: device-width; s }` | Stray `s` token; rule is malformed and will be dropped by IE/Edge parsers |
| `max-height: auto !important` (mobile img rule) | Invalid value; `auto` is not accepted for `max-height` — should be `none` |

**Tracking pixels missing `alt` text (3 instances):**

```
https://na5.thunderhead.com/one/rt/track/.../pixel?...
https://nova.collect.igodigital.com/c2/100010552/track_event?...
http://svopn.aaronbrothers.com/?sv_cid=0415_04228&sv_emopen=...
```

Screen readers will announce the `src` URL as image text. Convention is `alt=""` on all tracking pixels.
