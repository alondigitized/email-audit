# It’s outdoor party time! Embrace cabana chic with our NEW limited-edition Jonathan Adler x Michaels collection.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-16**
## Executive summary

- This is Michaels doing something genuinely interesting for the first time in a week: launching a real exclusive. The Jonathan Adler x Michaels collection gives the brand actual new news to lead with — a bold-patterned, cabana-chic hero that looks nothing like the endless "Ultimate Sale" grid emails that dominated June 13-16. - But Michaels can't help itself. After a strong hero, the email sprawls into generic summer product grids, yard games, crafting supplies, and a tacked-on 30%-off coupon — diluting the premium collab signal into the same chaotic merchandise dump they always run. The JA collab deserves its own clean send; instead it's been asked to carry five different shopping intents at once.

## What's working

- The Jonathan Adler hero pattern is visually arresting — bold, graphic, and immediately distinct from Michaels' usual stock-photo look.
- Seasonal relevance is perfect: outdoor entertaining in mid-June is exactly when this creative should land.
- "Shop All Outdoor Party" as the primary CTA is clear, direct, and specific — no "Discover More" vagueness.
- Loyalty/Rewards section at the bottom rewards members with visible earn language, a genuine engagement signal.
- This email breaks the monotony of the June 13-16 sale barrage in a meaningful way, similar to the Lemax Spooky Town launch (June 15, 8/10) — exclusive collections outperform generic discount drums.

## What's weak

- The subject line is ~110 characters and will truncate to almost nothing on mobile — most recipients see only "It's outdoor party time! Embrace cab..."

- No urgency mechanics for a "limited-edition" claim — no quantity signal, no countdown, no deadline. "Limited" means nothing without a constraint.
- The 30%-off coupon buried near the footer clashes with the premium collab positioning opened at the top.
- Five distinct shopping categories (outdoor party, summer items, outdoor games, crafting, rewards) compete for attention with no clear priority after the hero.

- **Subject Alt A:** `Jonathan Adler x Michaels: new outdoor collection is here`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Cabana chic is in — shop the Jonathan Adler x Michaels drop`
- 2. **Write a preheader that earns its slot.** The subject names the collab; the preheader should sell why it matters — the aesthetic, the newness, the exclusivity.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Bold patterns, outdoor entertaining picks — limited-edition and new this week.`

- 4. **Add a scarcity or deadline signal.** "Limited-edition" needs an anchor — "Only available while supplies last" with a visible collection count, or a hard end date.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Michaels / Jonathan Adler Party Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is built on a legacy IBM/Acoustic ESP stack with secondary tracking layers from Thunderhead ONE, iGo Digital, and Silverpop firing in parallel. Two HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources and a misplaced `<!DOCTYPE>` declaration introduce rendering and deliverability risk; missing `List-Unsubscribe` headers are a compliance gap against current Gmail/Yahoo requirements.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Misplaced DOCTYPE / pre-DOCTYPE content block**
The `<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC ...>` declaration appears *after* a fully rendered `<table>` block containing `<!--MASTER_Coremetrics_SLPH_Content-->`. Content before `<!DOCTYPE>` is outside the HTML document and may be parsed inconsistently.

```html
<table ... class="stylingblock-content-wrapper">...</table>
<!-- -->
<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN" ...>
```

**21 click-redirect links unverifiable**
All CTAs route through `click.emdeals.michaels.com` (Acoustic/IBM redirect service). The QA probe skipped all 21 — destination URLs and UTM integrity are unconfirmed.

**Quadruple tracking pixel stack**
Four separate open/event beacons fire on render. Two use HTTP, not HTTPS (see §3):

| Pixel | Domain | Protocol |
|---|---|---|
| Coremetrics open | `click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx` | HTTPS |
| Thunderhead ONE | `na5.thunderhead.com/one/rt/track/...` | HTTPS |
| iGo Digital | `nova.collect.igodigital.com/c2/100010552/track_event` | HTTPS |
| Silverpop | `svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847` | **HTTP** |

**Plain-text body is 82% URLs**
QA reports 28,682 of 35,004 plain-text characters are URLs. This is a significant spam-filter signal and degrades deliverability on text-only clients.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Invalid CSS: `max-height: auto`**
`auto` is not a valid value for `max-height` (valid values: `none`, length, percentage). Appears twice in the mobile media query:

```css
img { max-height: auto !important; }
.header img { max-height: auto !important; }
```

**Non-standard `<custom>` tags**
Two unclosed `</custom>` tags appear immediately after the Coremetrics open pixel `<img>`. These are not valid HTML and may confuse parsers:

```html
<img src="https://click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx?..." width="1" height="1" alt="">
</custom>
</custom>
```

**HTTP image source — may be blocked**
Two image sources use plain HTTP, which is blocked by default in Gmail, Apple Mail (iOS 15+), and Outlook on Mac:

- `http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/lib/fe4015707564057d701173/m/1/e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` — content image, no alt text
- `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true&sv_svemi=...` — Silverpop open pixel

**Missing alt text on content image**
`e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` has no `alt` attribute. If the HTTP block fires, subscribers see a broken image with no fallback text.

**Missing alt text on tracking pixels**
Three tracking pixels (`track_event`, Thunderhead pixel, Silverpop pixel) lack `alt=""`. Screen readers may attempt to announce the raw URL. Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` explicitly.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**PII in query parameters — base64 email**
The `customerEmail` parameter in the header home link passes a base64-encoded subscriber email:

```
customerEmail=bWlyYS1nbGl0dGVyLW1pY2hhZWxzLWZhcDZlQGV0ZWxsLmFwcA==
```

This decodes to a subscriber address. Base64 is not encryption — it is trivially reversible. This value will appear in web-server access logs, browser history, and referrer headers on the destination site.

**iGo Digital payload truncated**
The `nova.collect.igodigital.com` pixel URL contains a JSON payload with `"email":"mira-glitter-micha...` that is truncated in the captured source. Verify the full value is properly URL-encoded and does not exceed the GET request limit; truncated pixels silently fail.

**SFMC subscriber hash exposed**
`sfmc_Sub=6a0b108e8b75a227d95acdb55aac28a1c4325c544a88543985a0a0aa55b1b862` is appended to click-through URLs. This is a hashed identifier but still constitutes linkable subscriber metadata landing in destination-site logs.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent**
QA confirmed the header is not present (or not relayed by AgentMail). Gmail and Yahoo both require `List-Unsubscribe` for bulk senders (≥5k/day); absence increases spam folder placement and blocks one-click unsubscribe in Gmail UI.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent (RFC 8058)**
Without this header, Gmail's one-click unsubscribe button cannot fire a POST to the ESP. The subscriber must click through to an unsubscribe page instead. This is a current Yahoo/Google bulk sender requirement (enforced since February 2024).

**Authentication-Results not captured**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown due to the AgentMail relay not forwarding the `Authentication-Results` header. Sending domain `emdeals.michaels.com` should be verified to have a DMARC policy; without it, spoofing risk exists regardless of DKIM signing.

**CAN-SPAM physical address / unsubscribe link**
Not visible in the truncated source, but QA did not flag their absence — assume present in footer. Confirm physical mailing address and functioning unsubscribe link are present in the full render.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Coremetrics tagging, not standard UTM**
Click links use `cm_mmc` (IBM Coremetrics / Acoustic attribution format), not `utm_source` / `utm_medium` / `utm_campaign`. If Michaels has migrated any analytics to GA4 or a non-Coremetrics stack, these parameters will not be attributed:

```
cm_mmc=EMAIL-_-Other_Promo/Value-_-20260616_S4_Variable_JonathanAdlerParty_OtherPromo_FF_US-_-HEADER_HOME
```

**21 links unprobed — landing page UTM integrity unconfirmed**
Because all CTAs are behind the `click.emdeals.michaels.com` redirect, it is unknown whether final destination URLs carry attribution parameters or strip them on redirect.
