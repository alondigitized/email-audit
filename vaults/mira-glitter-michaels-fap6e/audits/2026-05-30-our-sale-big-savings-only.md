# Our Sale = Big. Savings. Only!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-30**
## Executive summary

- This is the third Michaels sale email in as many days with essentially the same structure: a 30% hero over regular-priced items, followed by a cascade of 60%/50%/25% percentage-off blocks stacked top to bottom. The creative is nearly indistinguishable from the 5/30 "Shop our sale for up to 50% off!" send and the 5/29 "Score up to 50% off during our sale!" — same stacked-discount architecture, same roughly-60% ceiling, same Rewards footer anchoring the bottom. Michaels is in a daily sale loop, and the subject line is the only meaningful variable changing between sends.
- The subject "Our Sale = Big. Savings. Only!" uses period breaks as dramatic pauses — a stylistic move that reads as trying hard without delivering anything. No discount number, no deadline, no category. A subscriber who opened either of the last two Michaels emails already knows what's inside; this subject neither confirms that expectation usefully nor surprises them compellingly.
- Bottom line: a structurally competent sale email undone by cadence fatigue and a subject that swings for personality and misses.

## What's working

- **30% off hero lands immediately** — the top section announces the offer without burying it; no mystery about what you're here for.
- **Pricing specificity in product tiles** — $29.99 and $9.99 price points are visible inline, giving shoppers a concrete sense of spend before clicking.
- **Michaels Rewards "Earn $5" section** — the loyalty touchpoint is cleanly placed at the bottom without cluttering the main offer flow.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, overlapping text, or visible layout breaks anywhere in the scroll.
- **"NEW! Youpi! IG Balloons" section** — the party product launch breaks the pure-discount cadence with a novelty signal, consistent with the 5/27 Party Shop launch build.

## What's weak

- **Subject communicates zero specifics** — "Our Sale = Big. Savings. Only!" contains no percentage, no product, no deadline; it's punchy language with no information payload.
- **Stacked-discount fatigue is at peak** — this is structurally the same email as 5/30 and 5/29; a subscriber who opened either has already processed this creative.
- **No visual hierarchy below the hero** — six or more competing discount tiers fight for attention simultaneously; the eye has no single resting point after the 30% opener.
- **No sale deadline visible anywhere** — without an end date, this trains subscribers to wait for the next send rather than act now.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a specific sale end date in the hero** — "Ends Sunday" or a countdown badge would be the single highest-leverage change. Right now, the email teaches subscribers that waiting is fine because the sale perpetually continues.
- 2. **Collapse to one headline discount** — pick the ceiling (60% off) and make it the dominant message; subordinate or remove all competing tiers. Six competing percentages produce decision fatigue and scroll-and-close behavior.
- 3. **Rewrite the subject to lead with the number** — the discount is the news.
- **Subject Alt A:** `60% off starts now — but not forever`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 60% off this weekend only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `30% off regular prices + members save even more`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Florals, home decor, party supplies — all on sale now`
- 4. **Rest the format for one send** — an editorial or category-focus email (like the 5/28 "personal lore projects" send, the highest-scoring recent Michaels email) would reset attention and make the next sale land harder by contrast.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Michaels "Our Sale = Big. Savings. Only!" (2026-05-30)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email sends and renders adequately but carries four infrastructure-level problems worth addressing: two HTTP-served assets that will be blocked in modern clients, unencrypted PII leaked into click-through URLs, and authentication headers that are opaque to the relay pipeline. Malformed HTML artifacts are also present but low-risk.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**PII in click-through URL parameters (high severity)**
The commented-out header logo link (visible in source) passes the recipient's email address base64-encoded — not encrypted — as `customerEmail=bWlyYS1nbGl0dGVyLW1pY2hhZWxzLWZhcDZlQGV0ZWxsLmFwcA==` in the destination URL. Base64 is trivially reversible (`mira-glitter-michaels-fap6e@etell.app`). This value will appear in CDN access logs, browser history, and any intermediary proxy on the click path. The same address is also partially present in the Thunderhead pixel querystring (`emailaddress%3DMIR...`) and in plaintext JSON inside the iGoDigital pixel payload.

**22 tracking links skipped by QA probe**
All click links route through `click.emdeals.michaels.com` redirect chains. Final destination URLs could not be validated. Broken redirects would produce silent 404s with no QA signal.

**Empty UTM parameter**
`cm_mmca2=` is present with no value in the header logo destination URL. Likely a template substitution that failed; any downstream attribution query on this dimension will return null.

**Four distinct open-tracking pixels**
The email fires four separate open-tracking beacons on load:
- `click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx` (SFMC)
- `na5.thunderhead.com` (Salesforce Interaction Studio)
- `nova.collect.igodigital.com` (iGoDigital personalization)
- `svopn.e.michaels.com` (Silverpop/Watson)

Two of these are served over HTTP and will be blocked (see §3). Multiple concurrent pixel fires can inflate open-rate metrics across disconnected systems.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Two images served over HTTP — will be blocked**

| Asset | URL |
|---|---|
| Content image | `http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/.../e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` |
| Silverpop tracking pixel | `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true&...` |

Gmail, Outlook 365, and Apple Mail all proxy or block mixed-content HTTP images. The content image will show as a broken placeholder for a material share of recipients.

**Missing alt text on content image**
`e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` has no `alt` attribute. If the image is blocked or a screen reader is used, the content area is silent. The three tracking pixels also lack alt text, which is lower severity but contributes to the QA warning count.

**Malformed HTML artifacts**

Two `</custom>` closing tags appear immediately after the open pixel `<img>`, with no corresponding opening tag:
```html
<img src="https://click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx?..." width="1" height="1" alt="">
</custom>
</custom>
```
These are likely unflushed ESP template processor tokens. No current major client crashes on unknown closing tags, but they are invalid per the declared HTML 4.01 Transitional DOCTYPE and may interfere with strict parsers or linting pipelines.

The file also opens with `<!-- <!-- -->` — a doubly-nested comment open that is technically malformed. Most parsers will recover, but it is a code hygiene issue.

**`p { margin: 1em 0; }` declared twice** in the `<head>` stylesheet. Redundant, no functional impact.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header absent from relay capture**
The QA pipeline did not find a `List-Unsubscribe` header. This header is required for Gmail's bulk sender policy (enforced February 2024 for >5,000/day senders) and strongly expected by Yahoo Mail. Its absence — even if present at the MTA level and stripped by the AgentMail relay — means this pipeline cannot confirm compliance. Needs verification at the MTA or SFMC header log level.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) absent**
One-click unsubscribe (the machine-readable POST variant) is not confirmed. Gmail will demote senders without it for bulk campaigns.

**Authentication-Results header absent**
SPF and DKIM pass/fail status is unknown because the `Authentication-Results` header was not captured by the relay. This is likely a relay stripping issue rather than an actual auth failure, but it cannot be confirmed from available data. The sending domain `emdeals.michaels.com` should be independently verified against SPF/DKIM records.

**CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe mechanism**
Not verifiable from the truncated HTML. Assumed present in footer; full source required to confirm.
