# Up to 50% off your summer faves during The Big Summer Sale!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- This is a classic "kitchen-sink summer sale" email — stacked discount blocks from top to bottom, with at least six distinct offer tiers (25%, 50%, 60% off, Buy 1 Get 1, Buy 2 deals, 20% off, plus a Rewards module). The sheer volume of deals is both the strength and the problem: there's no single hero offer for the eye to commit to, and by the time you reach the Rewards section at the bottom you've already forgotten the 25% at the top.
- The bigger context issue: this is Michaels' **sixth email in five days** to a subscriber who just got an Anniversary Sale 50%-off pitch in the welcome email on May 15. "The Big Summer Sale" now sounds redundant — it's the same "up to 50% off" claim rephrased. Offer inflation is setting in.
- That said, the email is clean — no obvious render bugs — and the Rewards nudge ("Earn $5") at the bottom is smart cross-sell. The execution is competent. The strategy is exhausted.

## What's working

- **Strong top-of-fold numbers**: 25% and 50% are both visible in the hero without scrolling, giving the inbox a clear value signal.
- **Seasonal alignment**: "Summer Sale" framing and the product imagery (floral, bright palettes) map to the calendar correctly.
- **Rewards module**: Visible loyalty CTA at the bottom keeps the membership pitch alive without dominating the main body.
- **No render bugs**: All image blocks loaded, text is readable, layout is intact.
- **Breadth of deals**: Six distinct discount tiers means almost any category shopper finds something relevant.

## What's weak

- **No single hero offer**: Six+ competing discount blocks mean the eye never commits — 60% off should be the headline, but it's buried mid-email behind 25%.
- **Cadence fatigue**: The welcome on May 15 already led with "up to 50% off." This subject is functionally identical. A subscriber who's been opening every Michaels send for five days has no reason to believe this is new.
- **No deadline visible**: "Big Summer Sale" implies urgency but nothing in the visible render shows an end date or countdown — the urgency is hollow.
- **Too long to scan on mobile**: The stacked block layout requires heavy scrolling; the most compelling offer (60% off) is not in the first viewport.
- **CTA copy appears generic**: The call-to-action buttons visible in the product blocks look like "Shop" — no verb specificity pulling toward a category.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with 60% off** — flip the block order so the deepest discount hits the hero. "Up to 60% off summer faves" is a harder punch than "up to 50%."
- 2. **Add a visible deadline** — "Sale ends Sunday" or a date stamp gives the urgency the subject line promises but the body doesn't deliver.
- 3. **Collapse to 3 offer blocks max** — consolidate the six tiers into three clear lanes (highest % off / BOGO / Rewards). Fewer choices = more clicks.
- 4. **Differentiate from the May 15 welcome** — that email opened with "Anniversary Sale — Up to 50% off." Calling this "The Big Summer Sale" with the same % feels like a copy-paste. At minimum rename it internally so subscribers perceive progression, not repetition.
- 5. **Rewrite the subject to lead with the deepest number:**
- **Subject Alt A:** `60% off summer faves — Big Summer Sale is on`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your summer wishlist: up to 60% off right now`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Florals, outdoor décor, and more — ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop the sale before it's gone — Rewards members earn double`

## Technical Audit — Michaels "Big Summer Sale" Email

**Sent:** 2026-05-20 | **From:** michaels@emdeals.michaels.com

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound for a legacy ESP-built HTML email, but carries two medium-severity deliverability risks (HTTP image assets, missing authentication headers) and a structural anomaly — `<!DOCTYPE>` is declared mid-body, not at document root.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**22 click-redirect links skipped by HTTP probe** — all route through `click.emdeals.michaels.com`. No broken links detected among resolvable URLs, but destination URLs could not be verified.

**Pixel tracking mixed-provider sprawl** — three separate open-tracking pixels fired:
- `https://click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx?I3V4URSXWMSU5O2QK4FBVNWED4...` (Acoustic/Watson Campaign)
- `https://na5.thunderhead.com/one/rt/track/ONE-PFSLHYI824-2685/pixel` (Thunderhead ONE)
- `https://nova.collect.igodigital.com/c2/100010552/track_event` (Salesforce iGo Digital)
- `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true` (Selligent, HTTP)

Multiple redundant open-tracking vendors is normal for large retail stacks but increases page weight and pixel-blocking surface area.

**`customerEmail` param carries Base64-encoded address in plain URLs** — visible in `cm_mmc` links:
```
customerEmail=bWlyYS1nbGl0dGVyLW1pY2hhZWxzLWZhcDZlQGV0ZWxsLmFwcA==
```
Base64 is not encryption. This PII is transmitted in GET params, logged in CDN/server access logs, and visible in referrer headers if the user clicks through to a third-party page.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<!DOCTYPE>` declared mid-document.** The DOCTYPE appears after an opening `<table>` and two `<custom>` tags — it is not the first element in the document. Parsers will ignore or misplace it, falling back to quirks mode in some clients.

**`max-height: auto` is invalid CSS** — appears in `.fluid` mobile media query:
```css
max-height: auto !important;
```
`auto` is not a valid value for `max-height`. This is likely a copy-paste artifact; browsers will ignore it but it flags in validators.

**4 images missing `alt` text** (QA confirmed):
| Image | Risk |
|---|---|
| `e323ae89-...f33897013cd1.jpg` (product/hero image) | Content invisible in image-off clients |
| Thunderhead pixel | Minor — tracking pixel |
| iGoDigital `track_event` pixel | Minor — tracking pixel |
| Selligent `svopn` pixel | Minor — tracking pixel |

The hero/product image missing `alt` is the meaningful one; tracking pixel `alt=""` would be the correct fix (empty string, not omitted).

**Two image sources served over HTTP, not HTTPS:**
- `http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/...e323ae89-...f33897013cd1.jpg` — content image; will be blocked by Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook 365 in strict mixed-content mode
- `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847...` — Selligent open pixel; blocked open-tracking = silent open inflation

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens detected in the truncated source. The `sfmc_Sub` and `emId` params in click URLs are populated (hashed values present), indicating subscriber context resolved correctly at send time.

`customerEmail` Base64 value decodes to a valid address — merge fired correctly, though transmission in URL params is a concern (see §2).

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not captured** — QA confirms the header was not present in the relayed message. This is likely an AgentMail relay stripping issue rather than a sender omission (Acoustic/Salesforce Marketing Cloud both inject this header), but it cannot be confirmed from available data. If absent at the MTA level, this violates RFC 2369 and Gmail/Yahoo 2024 bulk sender requirements.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not captured** — same caveat. Without this, Gmail's one-click unsubscribe UI falls back to mailto, increasing friction and spam complaint rate.

**CAN-SPAM physical address** — not visible in truncated source; assumed present in footer (standard for Michaels). Cannot confirm without full source.

**Unsubscribe link** — present via click-redirect; not independently verifiable due to tracking domain skip.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

UTM parameters are present on all confirmed `michaels.com` links using the `cm_mmc` convention (Coremetrics/IBM format), e.g.:
```
cm_mmc=EMAIL-_-Other_Promo/Value-_-20260520_S2_BigSummerSale_Promo_OtherPromo_FF_US-_-HEADER_HOME
```

This is a legacy analytics format (Coremetrics, not GA4). If the site has migrated to GA4, `utm_source` / `utm_medium` / `utm_campaign` params are absent, meaning this traffic will be unattributed in GA4 reports. The HTML includes a `<!--MASTER_Coremetrics_SLPH_Content-->` comment confirming Coremetrics is the intended attribution system.
