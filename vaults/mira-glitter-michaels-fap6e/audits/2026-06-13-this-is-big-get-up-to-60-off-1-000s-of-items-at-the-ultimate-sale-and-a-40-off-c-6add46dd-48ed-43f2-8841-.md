# 🤩 This. Is. Big! Get up to 60% off 1,000s of items at The Ultimate Sale and a 40% off coupon.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-13**
## Executive summary

- This is Michaels' 18th-or-so promotional email in roughly four days, and the honest read is that it's a near-carbon-copy of the June 12 "🤯 40% off coupon + 70% off summer decor" send that scored 7/10. The hero delivers the same 40% coupon badge flanked by a 60%-off banner; the product grid below it — frames, summer decor, craft supplies, BOGO tiles — is structurally identical to every "Ultimate Sale" send this week. The subject line swapped "BEST. WEEKEND. EVER." for "This. Is. Big!" and added a different face emoji, but the information content is the same.
- Inside, the email isn't broken. The offers are real, the loyalty "Earn $5" Plum Rewards block is a quiet differentiator, the yellow floral imagery adds some seasonal freshness to the hero, and multiple discount tiers (70% off, 30% off, BOGO) give deal-seekers several entry points. But the density of competing sections destroys any single dominant CTA, and there is no urgency signal anywhere in the body — no deadline, no countdown, no "ends Sunday." Without a hard close date, the sale feels permanent and shoppers can comfortably defer.
- The cadence is now the biggest strategic problem. At 18 sends in four days, even a highly motivated Michaels shopper has learned to treat these emails as visual noise — scan for the coupon code, close. That inbox behavior is trained, not accidental.

## What's working

- Hero badge clarity: 40% coupon + 60% off land in the first viewport without any scrolling
- Offer stacking across multiple discount tiers (70% off, 30% off, BOGO) gives multiple price-sensitive entry points
- Yellow tropical floral imagery in the hero breaks the all-red pattern of recent sends and is seasonally appropriate
- Plum Rewards "Earn $5" block is a loyalty hook that makes this send more than just a pure discount blast
- Email renders cleanly — no broken images, no text overlap, layout intact across the full length

## What's weak

- "🤩 This. Is. Big!" as a hook is indistinguishable from yesterday's "🤯 BEST. WEEKEND. EVER." — the breathless-punctuation signal is being trained out by repetition
- Zero urgency anywhere in the body — no sale-end date, no countdown, no credible deadline; the offer feels evergreen
- Grid density is extreme: the email has so many competing sections that there's no single dominant CTA for the eye to land on
- No segmentation visible — the product tiles are a store-wide dump with no indication Michaels knows anything specific about this subscriber
- Cadence fatigue is structural at this point: 18 emails in four days means the coupon itself carries less perceived value even when the percentage is unchanged

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard deadline in the hero.** "Sale ends Sunday, 11:59 PM" in the hero is the single highest-lift change available. Nothing else about this email will move conversion more.
- 2. **Cut the grid to three sections.** Pick the three highest-performing categories by click rate (historically frames and summer decor), give them room to breathe, and remove everything else. The current density is self-defeating.
- 3. **Move the Plum Rewards hook into the hero block.** "Earn $5 in rewards while you shop" is structurally different from a coupon blast — it should be part of the value proposition that opens the email, not a footnote near the footer.
- 4. **Replace generic "Shop Now" CTAs with category-specific copy.** "Shop frames →" or "See all summer decor →" will outperform "Shop Now" because they signal intent at the click.
- 5. Rewrite the subject and preheader:
- **Subject Alt A:** `40% coupon + 60% off — sale ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your 40% off coupon is inside (+ 70% off summer decor)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Earn $5 in Plum Rewards while you shop The Ultimate Sale.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Frames, summer decor, craft supplies — up to 70% off today only.`

## Full review
## 1. Overview

This is Michaels' 18th-or-so promotional email in roughly four days, and the honest read is that it's a near-carbon-copy of the June 12 "🤯 40% off coupon + 70% off summer decor" send that scored 7/10. The hero delivers the same 40% coupon badge flanked by a 60%-off banner; the product grid below it — frames, summer decor, craft supplies, BOGO tiles — is structurally identical to every "Ultimate Sale" send this week. The subject line swapped "BEST. WEEKEND. EVER." for "This. Is. Big!" and added a different face emoji, but the information content is the same.

Inside, the email isn't broken. The offers are real, the loyalty "Earn $5" Plum Rewards block is a quiet differentiator, the yellow floral imagery adds some seasonal freshness to the hero, and multiple discount tiers (70% off, 30% off, BOGO) give deal-seekers several entry points. But the density of competing sections destroys any single dominant CTA, and there is no urgency signal anywhere in the body — no deadline, no countdown, no "ends Sunday." Without a hard close date, the sale feels permanent and shoppers can comfortably defer.

The cadence is now the biggest strategic problem. At 18 sends in four days, even a highly motivated Michaels shopper has learned to treat these emails as visual noise — scan for the coupon code, close. That inbox behavior is trained, not accidental.

## 2. What worked

- Hero badge clarity: 40% coupon + 60% off land in the first viewport without any scrolling
- Offer stacking across multiple discount tiers (70% off, 30% off, BOGO) gives multiple price-sensitive entry points
- Yellow tropical floral imagery in the hero breaks the all-red pattern of recent sends and is seasonally appropriate
- Plum Rewards "Earn $5" block is a loyalty hook that makes this send more than just a pure discount blast
- Email renders cleanly — no broken images, no text overlap, layout intact across the full length

## 3. What didn't

- "🤩 This. Is. Big!" as a hook is indistinguishable from yesterday's "🤯 BEST. WEEKEND. EVER." — the breathless-punctuation signal is being trained out by repetition
- Zero urgency anywhere in the body — no sale-end date, no countdown, no credible deadline; the offer feels evergreen
- Grid density is extreme: the email has so many competing sections that there's no single dominant CTA for the eye to land on
- No segmentation visible — the product tiles are a store-wide dump with no indication Michaels knows anything specific about this subscriber
- Cadence fatigue is structural at this point: 18 emails in four days means the coupon itself carries less perceived value even when the percentage is unchanged

## 4. What I'd change

1. **Add a hard deadline in the hero.** "Sale ends Sunday, 11:59 PM" in the hero is the single highest-lift change available. Nothing else about this email will move conversion more.
2. **Cut the grid to three sections.** Pick the three highest-performing categories by click rate (historically frames and summer decor), give them room to breathe, and remove everything else. The current density is self-defeating.
3. **Move the Plum Rewards hook into the hero block.** "Earn $5 in rewards while you shop" is structurally different from a coupon blast — it should be part of the value proposition that opens the email, not a footnote near the footer.
4. **Replace generic "Shop Now" CTAs with category-specific copy.** "Shop frames →" or "See all summer decor →" will outperform "Shop Now" because they signal intent at the click.
5. Rewrite the subject and preheader:
   - **Subject Alt A:** `40% coupon + 60% off — sale ends Sunday`
   - **Subject Alt B:** `Your 40% off coupon is inside (+ 70% off summer decor)`
   - **Preheader Alt A:** `Earn $5 in Plum Rewards while you shop The Ultimate Sale.`
   - **Preheader Alt B:** `Frames, summer decor, craft supplies — up to 70% off today only.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Michaels "Ultimate Sale" Email (2026-06-13)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has a malformed document structure (DOCTYPE placed mid-document inside a table), two content/tracking pixels served over plain HTTP, and four images missing `alt` attributes. Compliance headers (List-Unsubscribe, Authentication-Results) were not captured by the relay, creating deliverability risk.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**22 click-redirect links unverified**
All outbound links route through `click.emdeals.michaels.com` with opaque encoded query strings. The QA probe skipped all 22 — destination URL validity and redirect health are unconfirmed for this send.

**Plain-text body is 81% URLs**
QA measured 27,792 / 34,129 plain-text characters as URLs. This ratio significantly exceeds the typical spam-filter threshold (~70%). Aggressive URL density in the plain-text part increases the probability of bulk/spam classification by receiving MTAs.

**Open pixel uses non-standard parameter format**
`https://click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx?WAWA7HXYAFXEJDMPVCFHTVPZSI.100217&d=100217&bmt=0` — the first query parameter has no `=` sign. This is likely intentional (ESP proprietary format) but warrants confirming the pixel is firing correctly in reporting.

**Header anchor link is commented out**
The `<a href="https://www.michaels.com?cm_mmc=EMAIL-_-...HEADER_HOME...">` element is wrapped in `<!-- ... -->`. If this was the intended header click target, it is currently a dead zone — clicks on the header image will not be tracked or may not navigate.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**DOCTYPE placed mid-document (structural defect)**
The `<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN" ...>` declaration appears inside the `<body>` of an already-open document, after a wrapping `<table>`. It will be ignored by all email clients and browsers. The effective rendering mode is undefined/quirks for that inner block.

**Stray `</custom>` tags**
Two unclosed `</custom>` tags appear immediately after the open-tracking `<img>` element. These are unrendered ESP template artifacts and produce invalid HTML:
```html
<img src="https://click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx?..." width="1" height="1" alt="">
</custom>
</custom>
```

**`max-height: auto` is not a valid CSS value**
The mobile media query contains:
```css
img { max-width: 100% !important; height: auto !important; max-height: auto !important; }
```
`auto` is not a valid value for `max-height` (valid values: `none`, length, or percentage). Most clients will ignore this declaration; it has no effect but indicates a CSS authoring error.

**4 images missing `alt` text**

| Image | src (truncated) |
|---|---|
| Content image | `http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/.../e323ae89-...f33897013cd1.jpg` |
| Thunderhead pixel | `https://na5.thunderhead.com/one/rt/track/ONE-PFSLHYI824-2685/pixel?...` |
| igoDigital event pixel | `https://nova.collect.igodigital.com/c2/100010552/track_event?...` |
| SilverPop open pixel | `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true&...` |

The content image (`e323ae89`) is a visible product/promo image — missing `alt` text is an accessibility failure and will render a broken-image placeholder in image-blocked clients.

**2 images served over HTTP (not HTTPS)**

- `http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/lib/fe4015707564057d701173/m/1/e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` — content image, will be blocked by Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook (modern) under mixed-content policies
- `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true&sv_svemi=6a0b108...` — SilverPop open-tracking pixel over HTTP; open-rate data from this pixel will be undercounted in clients that block HTTP resources

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header absent**
QA found no `List-Unsubscribe` header captured at the relay. Gmail and Yahoo (as of February 2024 bulk sender requirements) require this header for senders above 5,000/day. If absent at the MTA level (not just relay capture artifact), this is a compliance gap.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post absent (RFC 8058)**
One-click unsubscribe via POST is not supported or not captured. This is required for Gmail bulk senders and is expected alongside `List-Unsubscribe`.

**Authentication-Results not captured**
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown — the AgentMail relay did not surface an `Authentication-Results` header. The sending domain `emdeals.michaels.com` should have valid SPF and DKIM alignment with the `From:` header domain. This cannot be confirmed from available data.

**CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe link**
An unsubscribe mechanism appears to be present in the email body (standard for Michaels commercial email), but the truncated HTML prevents full confirmation. No issues flagged from visible source — further review of footer HTML required.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Tracking parameter scheme: Coremetrics (IBM DA), not Google UTM**
Links use `cm_mmc`, `cm_mmca2` (IBM Coremetrics / Digital Analytics) rather than `utm_source/utm_medium/utm_campaign`. This is consistent with Michaels' platform but means Google Analytics will not attribute these sessions without a parallel UTM parameter or cross-domain import. If GA4 is the reporting system, session attribution from this email will appear as `(direct)`.

**`cm_mmc` value structure confirmed**
`cm_mmc=EMAIL-_-Circular/EOW/Promo-_-20260613_S1_Promo_UltimateSale_WKND_FF_US-_-HEADER_HOME` — date-stamped and campaign-labeled correctly for the send date (2026-06-13).

**Header click target is commented out**
As noted in §2, the header `<a>` tag is inside an HTML comment. If the header image is meant to be clickable and tracked, this link is broken.
