# Balloons? Check. DIY gifts? Check. Best Dad ever?✅ ✅
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-10**
## Executive summary

- This is the best Michaels send in the recent audit window — and that's a meaningful statement given how relentlessly they've hammered "The Ultimate Sale" for the past four days. Father's Day gives the creative team a genuine reason to exist: the checklist subject line is playful and distinct, the hero "DAD" spelled in craft supplies is on-brand and seasonally locked, and the email parcels out genuinely different gift categories (balloons, DIY crafts, photo gifts, MakerPlace sessions) rather than dumping one generic discount carousel.
- The execution is clean: no broken images, clear section hierarchy, multiple visible price points ($9.95, $19, $24.95, $29), and a Michaels Rewards offer tucked at the bottom to give members a reason to care beyond the headline discount. The 30% off and 60% off figures are both visible above the scroll, so the value signal lands immediately.
- The main weakness is what they've earned through volume: this subscriber has now received fifteen-plus emails in roughly four days. Even a well-crafted send is fighting acute list fatigue. The other miss is deadline paralysis — Father's Day is June 15, five days away, and nothing in the email says "order by Thursday for guaranteed delivery." That's a conversion fumble at exactly the moment urgency would be most credible.

## What's working

- **Subject line breaks the pattern.** The checklist format ("Balloons? Check. DIY gifts? Check.") is the first playful, non-blunt subject in this run and immediately signals this isn't another "60% off everything" blast.
- **Hero creative earns its theme.** "Put the 'Art' in Father's Day" with "DAD" spelled in colorful craft supplies is a genuine Michaels-specific execution — no other retailer could run this.
- **Multiple distinct price points are visible.** $9.95, $19, $24.95, $29 give the eye something concrete to land on in each section rather than one vague "up to X%" promise.
- **Section variety is real.** Balloons, DIY gifts, photo/personalized items, and MakerPlace experiences are four genuinely different gift angles — not four reskins of the same product grid.
- **Michaels Rewards integration.** The 30% member offer at the bottom gives loyalty customers a separate reason to click beyond the general sale.

## What's weak

- **No delivery/order deadline anywhere visible.** "Best Dad ever" is June 15 — five days away. "Order by [date] for in-store pickup" or a ship-by cutoff would convert browsers into buyers right now.
- **CTAs are all "Shop Now."** Every section uses the same generic verb. "Build His Gift," "Grab Balloons," "Make It Personal" would each carry the section's specific promise into the button.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** This is email fifteen-plus in four days; even this strong a send is discounted in the inbox before it's opened.
- **60% off section is ambiguous.** The discount is visible but what's actually 60% off (specific categories? clearance?) isn't immediately readable without squinting, which creates hesitation.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a countdown or deadline line.** A single line — "Order by Thursday for in-store pickup in time for Sunday" — above or below the hero would be the highest-leverage change. Father's Day has a hard stop; not using it is leaving urgency on the table.
- 2. **Rewrite CTAs to match their section.** "Shop Dad's Balloons," "Build His Gift Box," "Make It Personal" are each one edit and immediately pull the reader toward a click with intent.
- 3. **Clarify the 60% off scope.** Add a small descriptor: "60% off select Father's Day décor" removes the asterisk anxiety and makes the offer feel more honest.
- 4. **Throttle the list.** Suppressing this cadence from 15+ sends in four days to 4-5 would make every send — including this well-executed one — perform materially better.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Father's Day is Sunday — check everything off the list`
- **Subject Alt B:** `DAD deserves DIY: gifts, balloons & more under $30`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Order by Thursday for in-store pickup — balloons, DIY kits, and personalized gifts in one stop.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Up to 60% off Father's Day essentials. Don't let Sunday sneak up on you.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Michaels Father's Day Email (2026-06-10)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has a misplaced DOCTYPE, two mixed-content (HTTP) image sources, missing alt text on content images, unresolved List-Unsubscribe header visibility through the AgentMail relay, and PII (subscriber email address) embedded in plain-text click URLs and third-party tracking pixel query strings.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**DOCTYPE out of order**
The `<!DOCTYPE HTML>` declaration appears mid-document, after a `<table>` block containing `<!--MASTER_Coremetrics_SLPH_Content-->`. This causes browsers and some email clients to enter quirks mode for the initial block, potentially breaking layout on that section.

**Invalid `</custom>` tags**
Two `</custom>` closing tags appear immediately after the open-pixel `<img>` with no matching opening elements. These are invalid HTML and may confuse renderers or spam filters.

**21 click-tracking links skipped**
All links route through `click.emdeals.michaels.com`. The HTTP probe could not validate destinations. If that redirect domain goes down or is blocklisted, all CTAs in the email go dark simultaneously with no fallback.

**Four separate tracking vendors active**
- `click.emdeals.michaels.com` — click tracking
- `na5.thunderhead.com` — Thunderhead ONE behavioral pixel
- `nova.collect.igodigital.com` — Salesforce/iGoDigital behavioral pixel
- `svopn.e.michaels.com` — SilverPop open pixel

Four independent vendor pixels add load time, increase surface area for mixed-content blocks (see below), and signal high URL density to spam filters.

**Plain-text body is 83% URLs**
QA measured 31,499 of 37,803 plain-text characters as URLs. This ratio is a known deliverability trigger for Gmail and Microsoft filtering. Plain-text alternative should be prose, not a dump of all redirect URLs.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Two HTTP (non-HTTPS) image sources — may be blocked**
```
http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/lib/fe4015707564057d701173/m/1/e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg
http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true&sv_svemi=…
```
Apple Mail (iOS 15+), Gmail, and Outlook Web enforce HTTPS for embedded images. These will show broken-image placeholders or be silently blocked. The SilverPop open pixel on HTTP also means open-tracking fires may be lost.

**Two content images missing `alt` text**
```
a69df4e5-1588-44d2-9192-4a8f1b92a8ab.jpg
e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg
```
Content images (non-pixel, non-tracking) require descriptive `alt` text for screen readers and for clients that block images by default. Tracking pixels missing `alt=""` is lower priority but still technically non-conformant.

**`max-height: auto` is invalid CSS**
```css
max-height: auto !important;  /* appears twice in mobile media queries */
```
`auto` is not a valid value for `max-height`. Intended value is likely `none`. Some email clients will ignore the rule entirely; others may apply unexpected behavior.

**Duplicate `p { margin: 1em 0; }` declaration**
The rule appears twice in `<head>` styles. No functional breakage, but indicates the template was assembled from multiple partials without deduplication.

**Legacy hacks present but harmless**
`ReadMsgBody`, `.ExternalClass`, `@-ms-viewport` — these target Outlook 2003 and IE. No impact on current clients but add dead weight.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Subscriber email encoded in click URLs (PII exposure)**
Every click URL contains:
```
customerEmail=bWlyYS1nbGl0dGVyLW1pY2hhZWxzLWZhcDZlQGV0ZWxsLmFwcA==
```
This is a Base64-encoded email address. It will appear in:
- Server access logs at `click.emdeals.michaels.com`
- Browser history and referrer headers on redirect to `michaels.com`
- Any analytics platform that logs full URLs

Standard practice is to use an opaque subscriber ID, not the encoded address.

**Email address in third-party pixel query strings**
```
https://na5.thunderhead.com/…?…emailaddress%3DMIR…
https://nova.collect.igodigital.com/…"email":"mira-glitter-micha…
```
Both pixels appear to pass the subscriber's email address as a query parameter to external vendors. Depending on CCPA/GDPR consent scope, transmitting PII to third parties in this manner may require explicit disclosure.

**No unresolved merge token artifacts detected** in the visible HTML. Subscriber-specific values appear to be correctly populated.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not confirmed present**
QA: `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header not found — may not be captured by AgentMail relay`
This header is required by Google and Yahoo for bulk senders (>5,000/day) since February 2024. If the header exists upstream but is stripped by the relay, that is a relay configuration issue to resolve. If it is genuinely absent, this is a compliance failure.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not confirmed present**
QA: `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found`
Required alongside `List-Unsubscribe` for one-click unsubscribe compliance with Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy. Absence means users must navigate to a landing page to unsubscribe, which increases spam complaints.

**`Authentication-Results` header not captured**
QA: `[WARN] Authentication-Results header not found`
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status cannot be confirmed from the captured message. Given the sending domain is `emdeals.michaels.com` (subdomain), DKIM alignment with `michaels.com` should be verified directly against the MTA.

**CAN-SPAM physical address**
Not visible in the truncated HTML, but must be present. Assumed present — cannot confirm from available source.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Coremetrics (`cm_mmc`) parameters present, not standard UTM**
```
cm_mmc=EMAIL-_-Brand_Story-_-20260610_S3_PartyShopFathersDay_Story_FF_US-_-HEADER_HOME
```
If the analytics stack has migrated away from Coremetrics/IBM to GA4 or another tool, these parameters will not be captured. Verify the Michaels analytics layer still processes `cm_mmc` and maps it correctly to campaign attribution.

**No `utm_source` / `utm_medium` / `utm_campaign` parameters observed** in the visible link sample. If GA4 is the canonical analytics tool, UTM params should be added alongside or instead of `cm_mmc`.

**`sfmc_Sub` and `emId` appended to landing page URLs**
```
sfmc_Sub=6a0b108e8b75a227d95acdb55aac28a1c4325c544a…
emId=c258532227e6840b4eee7a4c0dffb3d5660996c95a1…
```
These are SFMC-internal subscriber identifiers. They pass to the destination domain, appear in referrer headers, and are stored in browser history. Confirm this is intentional and within the disclosed data-sharing scope with `michaels.com`.
