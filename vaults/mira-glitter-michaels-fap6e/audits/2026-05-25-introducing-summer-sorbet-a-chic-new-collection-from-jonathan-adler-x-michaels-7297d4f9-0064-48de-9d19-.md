# 😎 🏖️ Introducing Summer Sorbet, a chic NEW collection from Jonathan Adler x Michaels.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- This is a collection-launch editorial email for the Jonathan Adler x Michaels "Summer Sorbet" line — a creative hard pivot from the fourteen Memorial Day "50% off" sale blasts that preceded it. The visual identity earns attention: a tight pastel sorbet palette runs top to bottom, lifestyle photography is clean and aspirational, and the Jonathan Adler name carries real design credibility that Michaels doesn't usually have to work with. The 25% off offer in the hero ties commerce to the editorial tone without making it feel purely transactional.
- The problem is timing and context. This email arrived on May 25 — the same inbox session as the "DON'T MISS: 50% off Memorial Day Sale ends today" send. After a fourteen-email deluge of red "50% off" banners, even a genuinely well-made product launch struggles to land. The subject line is also doing this email no favors: 85+ characters with two emojis up front is optimized for brand expression, not inbox performance.
- The net read: a legitimately better email than anything Michaels sent in the prior two weeks, buried under audience fatigue and a subject line that will truncate badly on every mobile client.

## What's working

- **Jonathan Adler name in the subject** — the designer collaboration is a real signal; it differentiates this from generic Michaels promo and earns a second look from home decor subscribers.
- **Cohesive palette throughout** — the sorbet pastel color story holds from hero to footer; the creative team clearly art-directed this as a unified editorial shoot.
- **25% off anchor in the hero** — threading a discount into a launch email is smart; it gives readers a reason to act, not just admire.
- **Variety of product categories shown** — totes, barware, serving pieces, table decor — the email communicates range without feeling like a catalogue dump.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no obvious layout failures visible.

## What's weak

- **Subject line length** — "😎 🏖️ Introducing Summer Sorbet, a chic NEW collection from Jonathan Adler x Michaels." is ~85 characters; it will be hard-truncated on most mobile clients, cutting off the designer's name entirely.
- **Memorial Day sale remnants in the footer** — "50% / 70% off" callouts at the bottom clash directly with the aspirational editorial framing; it undercuts the premium Jonathan Adler positioning.
- **Sent the same day as the sale-ends-today email** — two emails in one inbox session after a two-week flood compounds fatigue precisely when this launch needs headroom.
- **No single hero product** — the scroll through multiple equal-weight categories dilutes click intent; there's no "the one thing to shop first" anchor.
- **No urgency or deadline signal in the body** — the 25% off has no visible end date, which removes the friction-reducer that would push a browser to a buyer.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the subject to under 50 characters and lead with the designer name.** "Jonathan Adler x Michaels is here" or "New: Jonathan Adler Summer Sorbet" surfaces the credibility signal before truncation hits.
- 2. **Strip the Memorial Day sale callouts from the footer** — or replace with a softer "25% off collection, today only" bar that reinforces the launch offer without the jarring red "50% 70% off" legacy blocks.
- 3. **Pick one hero product** — lead with the most photogenic, most giftable piece from the line; let the rest of the email be discovery.
- 4. **Add a visible offer deadline** — "25% off through Sunday" or similar converts the launch discount from a passive fact to an action trigger.
- 5. **Hold this email for the next morning** — sending a collection launch the same session as a finale sale email is a self-inflicted cannibalization; this deserved its own send day.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Michaels "Summer Sorbet / Jonathan Adler" (2026-05-25)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email sends cleanly from a Salesforce Marketing Cloud stack with Coremetrics attribution intact, but has a structural HTML defect (content precedes the DOCTYPE), two HTTP-only image sources that will be blocked by modern clients, and compliance headers that cannot be verified through the AgentMail relay.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**22 click-redirect links unverifiable**
All CTAs route through `click.emdeals.michaels.com`, which returns a redirect that the QA prober cannot follow. Destination URL health is unconfirmed for this send.

**Three separate open/impression trackers active simultaneously**
- `click.emdeals.michaels.com/open.aspx` — primary SFMC open pixel
- `nova.collect.igodigital.com/c2/100010552/track_event` — iGoDigital behavioral tracker
- `na5.thunderhead.com/one/rt/track/ONE-PFSLHYI824-2685/pixel` — Thunderhead/ONE journey tracker
- `svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847` — Acoustic (SilverPop) legacy open tracker (HTTP only — see §3)

Running four tracking systems in a single email increases header weight and introduces redundant data pipelines. Not a deliverability blocker, but worth auditing for consolidation.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**DOCTYPE appears mid-document (critical structural defect)**
The Coremetrics lookup block is injected *before* the DOCTYPE declaration:
```html
<table ... class="stylingblock-content-wrapper">
  <!-- MASTER_Coremetrics_SLPH_Content -->
</table>
<!DOCTYPE HTML PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD HTML 4.01 Transitional//EN" ...>
<html>
```
Content preceding the DOCTYPE forces all browsers/email clients into quirks mode for the entire parse tree. This is a template assembly bug — the content lookup wrapper must be moved inside `<body>` or rendered as a comment-only block outside the document shell.

**`</custom>` orphan tags**
Two `</custom>` closing tags appear after the open-pixel `<img>` with no corresponding opening tag. These are likely unresolved SFMC AMPscript template artifacts and will cause validation warnings in strict parsers.

**Invalid CSS: `max-height: auto`**
In the `@media (max-width: 480px)` block:
```css
img { max-height: auto !important; }
```
`auto` is not a valid value for `max-height` (valid values: `none`, `<length>`, `<percentage>`). Renders as if the declaration is absent; no visual breakage expected, but should be corrected to `max-height: none`.

**HTTP image sources (will be blocked)**
Two sources use `http://` rather than `https://`:
- `http://image.emdeals.michaels.com/lib/fe4015707564057d701173/m/1/e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` — product image, missing alt text
- `http://svopn.e.michaels.com/?sv_cid=0415_03847&sv_emopen=true&...` — Acoustic open pixel

Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook all proxy or block mixed-content HTTP image sources. The product image (`e323ae89...`) will render as a broken image for a significant portion of recipients.

**Missing alt text on 4 images**
- `e323ae89-6f89-4b47-8f9a-f33897013cd1.jpg` — product/content image (highest impact; should describe the visual)
- Thunderhead pixel — acceptable as `alt=""`
- iGoDigital track_event pixel — acceptable as `alt=""`
- Acoustic svopn pixel — acceptable as `alt=""`

Tracking pixels should have `alt=""` (empty, not absent) to satisfy accessibility linters and avoid screen readers announcing the src URL.

No unresolved merge tokens or `%%variable%%` literals detected in the truncated source.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe headers not captured**
The AgentMail relay did not surface `List-Unsubscribe` or `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) headers. These are likely present at the MTA level but stripped or not forwarded by the relay. Gmail and Yahoo's 2024 bulk sender requirements mandate both headers for senders at this volume tier. **Verify against raw SMTP headers from a direct inbox capture**, not the relay.

**SPF/DKIM/DMARC status unknown**
`Authentication-Results` not captured. The sending domain `emdeals.michaels.com` is a subdomain ESP envelope — confirm DMARC alignment (SPF + DKIM both aligning to `michaels.com` or `emdeals.michaels.com`) via MXToolbox or a direct Gmail header view.

**Plain-text body is 79% URLs**
23,646 of 29,891 plain-text characters are URLs. This strongly suggests the plain-text part is auto-generated from the HTML without scrubbing tracking links or injecting human-readable fallback content. Spam filters (SpamAssassin, Proofpoint) weight this pattern negatively.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

CTA links carry Coremetrics attribution (`cm_mmc`, `cm_mmca2`) rather than standard GA4 UTM parameters:
```
cm_mmc=EMAIL-_-Brand_Story-_-20260525_S2_Seasonal_JonathanAdler_Story_FF_US-_-HEADER_HOME
cm_mmca2=27801775
```
This is consistent with Michaels' IBM Coremetrics stack. No issues if the site's analytics layer is configured to parse `cm_mmc`. If Michaels has migrated any properties to GA4, these parameters will not attribute correctly — verify the landing page analytics tag.
