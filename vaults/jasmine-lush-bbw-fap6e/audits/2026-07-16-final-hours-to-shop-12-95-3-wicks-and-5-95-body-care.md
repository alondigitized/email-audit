# final hours ⏰ to shop $12.95 3-wicks and $5.95 body care.
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- This is B&BW's fourth email in the $12.95/$5.95 sales cycle — teaser (7/15), launch (7/15), morning closer "don't miss it" (this morning, 8.00/10), and now this evening "final hours" send. The email itself is technically clean and priced correctly: the Fall Favorites hero leads with both prices front and center, the "FINAL HOURS! ONLINE ONLY!" badge lands immediately, and the Wallflowers section delivers the cycle's strongest creative — a fortune-teller carnival scene that's genuinely unexpected compared to standard product photography. The "Picked just for you!" carousel with named scents is a nice personalization layer.
- The problem isn't the email — it's that the subscriber who opened "don't miss it: $12.95 3-wicks and $5.95 body care is ending" this morning is now getting the same message repackaged as "final hours ⏰ to shop $12.95 3-wicks and $5.95 body care." Two closing emails in one day for one sale, with nearly identical copy and prices, is the B&BW cadence tic in its most glaring form. Compare to July 14's two-send closer on the $7.99 candle deal — same pattern, same problem.
- The 75% Summer Sale offer is buried below three other modules. A 75% off deal deserves a hero slot, not a footnote.

## What's working

- **Hero prices are the first readable text after the header** — $5.95 and $12.95 are unavoidable, which is exactly right for a closing email.
- **"FINAL HOURS! ONLINE ONLY!" badge** reads immediately and removes ambiguity about when this ends.
- **Wallflowers fortune-teller creative** is the most visually distinctive section of the entire campaign cycle — the dark carnival aesthetic stands apart from every other send this week.
- **Named scents in the "Picked just for you!" carousel** (A Thousand Wishes, Into the Night, Champagne Toast) earn the personalization claim; individual shop links help.

## What's weak

- **Second closing send of the day on the same exact prices** — the morning "don't miss it" email already rang the bell; this one rings it again in a quieter room.
- **Every CTA says "SHOP"** — no specificity; "Shop 3-Wicks" / "Shop Body Care" / "Shop Wallflowers Refills" would each drive to the right destination and perform better.
- **Preheader leaks the shipping-threshold bar** — "FREE SHIPPING WITH ANY $50 PURCHASE" is what inbox clients show, completely disconnected from the "final hours" urgency the subject establishes.
- **Subject line is ~57 characters** — gets truncated before "body care" on most mobile clients, dropping half the offer from the inbox preview.
- **75% Summer Sale buried below three modules** — the deepest discount in the email is functionally invisible to anyone who doesn't scroll past Wallflowers and the personalization carousel.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Consolidate the two closing emails into one, sent at 5–6 PM with a concrete countdown.** "Final hours" loses authority when there was a "don't miss it" closer this morning. One strong PM send outperforms two weak ones.
- 2. **Name the deadline explicitly in hero and subject** — "Ends at midnight" beats "final hours" every time; it's a specific clock, not vague urgency.
- 3. **Rewrite every "SHOP" button to category-specific copy** — "Shop 3-Wick Candles," "Shop $5.95 Body Care," "Shop Wallflowers Refills" — each button should know where it's going.
- 4. **Write a real preheader** that mirrors the subject's urgency instead of letting the shipping bar bleed into the preview slot.
- 5. **Promote the 75% Summer Sale** — pull it above the Wallflowers module or give it its own send; buried at row 4 it will massively underperform its potential.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Fall Favorites ends tonight — 3-wicks $12.95, body care $5.95`
- **Subject Alt B:** `⏰ Midnight deadline: $12.95 candles. $5.95 body care.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Both deals close at midnight — lock in your price before they're gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The $12.95 and $5.95 prices disappear tonight. Here's what's left.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is functionally sound for a large ESP send (Salesforce Marketing Cloud via `e2.bathandbodyworks.com`), but has three notable issues: unstripped template artifacts in the HTML source, multiple images missing `alt` text, and unconfirmed compliance headers due to relay capture limitations.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Open pixel** present and correctly implemented:
```
https://click.e2.bathandbodyworks.com/open.aspx?ZWO3SFOJUNWEJOZRMRK7XQVFLI.70247&d=70247&bmt=0
```
Served as a 1×1 `<img>` in a hidden 0×0 table — standard, no issues.

**No issues** with link structure itself; untestable redirect chain is the only gap.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing `alt` text on 5 images (confirmed by QA):**

| Image | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Movable Ink personalized image | `a04fef77a8e834c2.png` — no `alt` attribute |
| Static asset | `418d0db7-5a65-43cb-b5c9-9c6c142e4fd2.png` — no `alt` |
| Everest rendering pixel | `https://bbwus.everestengagement.com/ea/fw0q51vctP/?e=200511831233&c=071626_EM_SU_BAU_PROMO_0_NRDM_12953W595BC_FH_NTH_G6M` — no `alt` |
| Two images with **empty `src`** | No `src` and no `alt` — broken image references in source |

The two broken-`src` images are the most concerning: they will render as broken image icons in non-blocking clients and indicate a template assembly issue.

**Duplicate Google Fonts `@import`** — once in `<head><style>` and once inline in a body `<table>`:
```css
@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Source+Sans+Pro&display=swap');
@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Source+Sans+Pro:wght@400;700&display=swap');
```
These fire two separate requests for the same font family. The second import also includes `wght@400;700` while the first omits it — inconsistent loading, potential FOUT in webmail clients that honor `@import`.

**`<meta charset>` placement** — `Content-Type` meta tag is at the bottom of `<head>` rather than first. Browsers/clients that pre-scan for charset before full parse may default to a fallback encoding before reaching it. Low risk for UTF-8/ASCII-heavy content but non-conformant.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Unresolved template artifacts leaked into sent HTML (two instances):**
```html
<!-- `date "+ET%m.%y"` -->
```
This is a literal shell command string that was not evaluated or stripped before send. It appears twice in the source. Not user-visible but exposes build tooling details to anyone inspecting source.

**Commented-out slot definition not stripped:**
```html
<!-- <div data-type="slot" data-key="gvhu7dvhmx9" data-label="Preheader"></div> -->
```
Platform-internal block metadata left in the delivered HTML.

**Internal campaign logic exposed in comments:**
```html
<!--Adds daily APT set via ampscript. Adds preset APT for emails flagged NTF-->
<!--Set APT for NTF campaigns, otherwise respect email-defined APT-->
<!--If @NTF_Flag is "N", @APT should be set in the email; no default fallback here-->
```
These expose SFMC AMPscript variable names (`@NTF_Flag`, `@APT`) and segmentation logic (NTF = New To File flag handling) to any recipient who views source. Not a security vulnerability but an information leak about internal audience logic.

No unresolved merge token strings (e.g., `%%variable%%` or `{{field}}`) found in visible source.
