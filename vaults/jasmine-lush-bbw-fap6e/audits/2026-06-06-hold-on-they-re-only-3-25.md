# hold on. ✋ they're only $3.25?! 😲
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-06**
## Executive summary

- Bath & Body Works breaks its recent single-arc discipline with this one. Every send from June 1–5 committed to one hero product and drove it hard — today's email stacks three separate "LIMITED TIME ONLY" sections (Wallflowers at $3.25, body mists at $5.95, 3-wick candles with a countdown timer) plus a product grid and a BOPIS module. The $3.25 Wallflowers hero is genuinely punchy — the lowest price point in the recent arc — and the "TODAY ONLY!" red banner executes cleanly. But by the time your eye reaches the third offer, the urgency has dissipated.
- The subject line lands the hook: "hold on. ✋ they're only $3.25?!" earns an open. The render is clean, the members-exclusive badge is present, and the beach creative ("It's a Shore Thing") is seasonally tight for early June. What hurts is the carry-over $5.95 body care section — that was yesterday's arc (June 5), and recycling it here without a "final hours" close frame makes it feel like filler rather than a deliberate two-arc play.
- Verdict: a solid promotional send that would have scored higher with one offer and one CTA. The multi-stack approach trades conversion focus for catalog breadth, and B&BW's own recent history proves the focused approach wins.

## What's working

- **$3.25 price anchors immediately** — lowest price in the recent six-send arc; visually prominent and credible.
- **"TODAY ONLY!" red banner + "Members EXCLUSIVE" badge** — urgency and loyalty validation in the same block, tight execution.
- **"SHOP WALLFLOWERS" CTA** — verb + noun, not "Discover"; one of the cleaner CTAs in this audit history.
- **"It's a Shore Thing" seasonal creative** — beach backdrop and colorful bottles are on-brand and seasonally correct for June.
- **Subject line curiosity hook** — "hold on." as an opener stops the scroll; $3.25 is specific enough to drive the open.

## What's weak

- **Three competing "LIMITED TIME ONLY" sections** — Wallflowers, body mists, and 3-wick candles all fight for the click; the singular focus that powered June 1–5's 9/10 run is gone.
- **$5.95 body mists feel like a leftover** — yesterday's arc lead (June 5) now appears as a secondary section with no "final hours" framing; subscribers who opened yesterday will recognize the repeat.
- **No visible preheader/preview text** — the email jumps straight to the logo and banner; any inbox preview copy appears to be missing or leaking hidden boilerplate, wasting the subject line's momentum.
- **Product grid ("TOP OFFERS") adds length without CTA focus** — four small-image category tiles near the bottom scatter attention after three already-competing sections.
- **Barcode/in-store scan element in the hero** — the "use phone to shoot & redeem" instruction adds visual noise directly below the $3.25 badge.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to one arc.** Strip the $5.95 and candle sections entirely. The Wallflowers $3.25 story is strong enough to carry a full send — B&BW's own June 1 ($10 single wicks) and June 4 ($3.50 room sprays) prove single-offer sends outscore multi-stacks.
- 2. **Add a "FINAL HOURS" close for $5.95 body care** — if the mists must appear, frame it as a closing bell (the pattern B&BW used June 4 PM to great effect), not a passive second section.
- 3. **Write a real preheader.** Something like "Members price: every Wallflowers refill, all day." Don't let the inbox preview default to hidden HTML.
- 4. **Move the barcode below the CTA button** — the in-store scan instruction is useful but interrupts the price-to-button flow.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$3.25 Wallflowers — members, today only.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `your $3.25 Wallflowers window is now open 🪟`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Every fragrance refill, all day. Members price.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stock up: Wallflowers refills just $3.25 today.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Broken image src attributes (2 instances):**
```
[WARN] Image missing alt text: : src: 
[WARN] Image missing alt text: : src: 
```
Two `<img>` tags with empty `src=""` are present. These render as broken image boxes in clients that don't suppress empty-src images (Outlook, some mobile clients).

**Missing alt text on content image:**
```
src: https://image.e2.bathandbodyworks.com/lib/fe9213737561047a72/m/1/418d0db7-5a65-43cb-b5c9-9c6c142e4fd2.png
```
No `alt` attribute on what appears to be a content/hero image. Fails WCAG 2.1 AA (non-decorative images require descriptive alt).

**Everest pixel alt attribute set to a URL fragment** instead of `alt=""`:
```
alt text value: "https://bbwus.everestengagement.com/ea/fw0q51vctP/?e=2005118"
```
Tracking pixels must carry `alt=""` — a partial URL string as alt text surfaces as visible text if images are blocked.

**`@import` for Google Fonts stripped by major clients:**
```css
@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Source+Sans+Pro&display=swap');
```
Gmail, Outlook, and Yahoo strip `@import` rules. Silent fallback to system fonts occurs — the import is dead weight in email context.

**Duplicate preheader containers:** A `div.preheader` with the preview text (`wall-to-wall summer, let's goooooo. 😎`) exists in a 600px-wide header table *above* `<body>`, and a second invisible-character padding block appears inside the body. While this is a common pattern to push preheader text off the preview window, having the text container outside `<body>` is non-standard and may cause some clients to ignore it entirely or concatenate unexpectedly.
