# this $1.95 deal is almost on empty. ⛽️
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-17**
## Executive summary

- This send is scattered — three different offers ($1.95 Car Refills, $2.95 Wallflowers Refills "lowest price of the season," and $2.95 Wallflowers Diffusers) crammed into one email, and none of them matches the subject line. The subject promises "this $1.95 deal is almost on empty" with a gas-tank emoji, but there's zero urgency copy or countdown visible in the render — no "almost gone," no stock indicator, nothing that pays off the subject's premise. It reads like a bait subject slapped onto a generic Fall Wallflowers/Car Refill bundle email.
- Compared to the brand's own recent top performer (09-17's "$2.95 Wallflowers - lowest price of the season* starts today" at 8.00/10), this send buries that exact same $2.95 hero deal under a *second* competing hero (the $1.95 Car Refill/Diffuser combo) above it, splitting attention instead of reinforcing one clear offer. The barcode/in-store redemption block is a nice practical touch, but the overall hierarchy is muddled.
- Judgement: a middling, over-stuffed send that dilutes a genuinely good price point with subject/body mismatch and competing CTAs.

## What's working

- Two concrete, specific prices are visible immediately ($1.95 Car Fragrance Refills, $2.95 Hanging Diffusers) with a clear promo code (ROADTRIP).
- The "Fall Wallflowers Event" section restates the $2.95 lowest-price-of-season offer with a clean honey-toned hero and "up to 30 days of room-filling fragrance" benefit copy.
- In-store barcode + online code are both shown, removing friction for either shopping path.
- "We think you'll love" product carousel gives a light personalization/cross-sell touch.
- No visible render bugs — images, buttons, and text all display cleanly.

## What's weak

- Subject says "$1.95 deal is almost on empty" but nothing in the body signals scarcity, low stock, or a ticking clock — the promise isn't paid off visually.
- Two hero offers ($1.95/$2.95 Car+Diffuser bundle, then $2.95 Wallflowers Refills) compete for the same visual real estate, with no single dominant CTA.
- "Final hours" banner sits atop the first hero, while "Starts today" sits atop the second — contradictory urgency signals stacked in the same scroll.
- Multiple near-identical Wallflowers $2.95 sends in three days (per brand history) risks fatigue; this one adds a third variant without escalating the offer.
- No loyalty/member-specific benefit visible despite the brand's recent Member Days cadence — a missed reinforcement opportunity.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one hero and make the subject match it exactly — either lead with "Final hours: $1.95 Car Refills" and show a real countdown/low-stock cue, or lead with the Wallflowers $2.95 deal and drop the car-refill subject entirely. Mismatched subject-to-hero is the single biggest drag here.
- 2. Cut the email to one primary offer above the fold; move the second offer ($2.95 Diffusers or Wallflowers) below as a secondary block, not a competing hero.
- 3. Add a visible urgency device (e.g., "Ends tonight at 11:59pm ET" or a stock-style progress bar) since the subject already promises scarcity — right now it's an empty threat.
- 4. Resolve the "Final hours" vs. "Starts today" banner contradiction — these can't both be true in the same email.

- **Subject Alt B:** `$1.95 Car Refills — most sizes almost sold out`
