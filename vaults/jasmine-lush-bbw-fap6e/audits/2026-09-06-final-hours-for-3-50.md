# final hours for $3.50. 💸
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-06**
## Executive summary

- Same "$3.50, TODAY/FINAL HOURS" Wallflowers refill hero this brand has run repeatedly this week — this is basically the 2026-09-06 pumpkin Wallflowers send but relabeled "FINAL HOURS!" and stacked with two more offers below (Santal Sage body care at $5.95, 3-Wick Candles at $13.95). The pumpkin plug-in shot is genuinely nice and on-season, and the barcode-for-in-store block is a clear, actionable CTA for anyone near a store.
- The problem is the same one flagged on 2026-09-04's "$1.95 is still here" send: stacking three "LIMITED TIME ONLY" / "FINAL HOURS" banners under one email dilutes the urgency of all three. Only the top offer is actually framed as ending soon in the subject; the candle and body-care blocks reuse "LIMITED TIME ONLY" banners that read as generic rather than tied to the same clock. It's a competent, on-brand execution of copy the subscriber has now seen multiple times this week.

## What's working

- Hero pumpkin Wallflowers plug-in shot is warm, seasonal, and immediately legible as the offer.
- Clear price ($3.50), unit clarity ("up to 24 per customer"), and a promo code (HARVEST) all visible without digging.
- Barcode block for in-store redemption is a nice touch for a "final hours" push — reduces friction for a same-day trip.
- Three distinct product CTAs (Wallflowers, body care, candles) each have their own "SHOP" button, so no ambiguity about what to click for what.
- Dermatologist-approved badge on the body care block adds a small trust signal.

## What's weak

- "FINAL HOURS!" urgency is only substantiated for the top offer — the two "LIMITED TIME ONLY" blocks below don't say when they end, undercutting the panic the subject line is selling.
- This is at least the third "$3.50 Wallflowers, act now" framing this brand has sent this week (2026-09-06 "bring the harvest home," 2026-09-05 "just for today"), so a daily subscriber is seeing near-identical creative on repeat.
- Three separate offers in one email splits attention — no single strong hierarchy after the hero, just three roughly equal shop blocks.
- "We think you'll love" recommendation row feels like filler bolted onto an already offer-dense email.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this to one hero offer with one deadline. If it's truly final hours, sending three unrelated products with three vague urgency banners contradicts the premise — pick the Wallflowers deal and drop or defer the other two to a non-urgency send.
- 2. Put an actual countdown or explicit end time ("Ends tonight at 11:59pm ET") on every "LIMITED TIME" banner, not just the subject line.
- 3. Vary the hero creative — this pumpkin/plug-in shot is close enough to the last two days' sends that a daily opener may tune it out; rotate to the Santal or candle imagery as hero instead.
- 4. Move "We think you'll love" out of an urgency-themed email — it dilutes the final-hours framing.

- **Subject Alt B:** `ends tonight — $3.50 pumpkin Wallflowers`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Use code HARVEST in-store or online before midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus $5.95 body care and $13.95 candles while they last.`
