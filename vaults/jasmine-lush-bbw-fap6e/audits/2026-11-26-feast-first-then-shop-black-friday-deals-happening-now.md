# feast first. then shop. Black Friday deals happening now. 🖤
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-26**
## Executive summary

- This is a competent Thanksgiving-day Black Friday send, but it's the fourth or fifth message in two days pushing the same offer. The hero is hard to miss: "ENTIRE STORE BUY 3, GET 4 FREE" in giant white type on a red block, under a gift-bag photo. "feast first. then shop." is a fresh, seasonal angle after "we NEVER do this" on 11/26 and "you buy 3. you get 4 FREE*" on 11/25. The email itself is not new, though. It's the same hero with a second offer stacked underneath.
- The second offer is the real addition. It's a "FIRST TIME EVER" bundle: spend $40 and pick 4 gifts plus a free tote, with an "Over $110 value" badge. It's strong, but it sits below the fold and competes with the hero. The page then runs long: a product strip, brand-values block, rewards and texts links, and a wall of fine print. Nothing is visibly broken.
- Verdict: solid execution, tired campaign. Fatigue is the main risk, not creative quality.

## What's working

- The "BUY 3, GET 4 FREE" block is unmissable and reads in a second. Four category buttons (Body Care, Candles, Wallflowers, Hand Soaps) give clear next steps.
- "feast first. then shop." ties the send to Thanksgiving and gives permission to open later. It's a new hook after days of the same line.
- The "Online only | In stores tomorrow" ribbon and the "Online only today (stores closed for Thanksgiving)" banner explain the timing honestly.
- The "FIRST TIME EVER" gift-with-purchase module has a concrete $40 threshold and an "Over $110 value" badge, which gives a second reason to click.
- The Rewards banner and the free-shipping-over-$50 bar at the top keep loyalty and shipping benefits visible.

## What's weak

- The two offers compete. A shopper sees "buy 3 get 4 free" and then "$40 gets you 4 gifts and a tote" with no guidance on which one to take.
- The "Free items are the 4 lowest priced items in each set of 7 eligible items*" line is small and light, and it's the condition that determines value. The asterisk and the long block of fine print at the bottom make the headline feel less clean.
- The category buttons are noun-only labels ("Body Care", "Candles"). They aren't verbs, and none of them links to a specific hero product.
- The "You might like" strip shows three items (Champagne Toast hand sanitizer, Winter Candy Apple soap, Mahogany Teakwood candle) with no prices, reviews or CTA. It reads as filler.
- The "Our feel good formula" block, "More to love" and "Find a store" all sit below the offers and dilute the close. "Find a store" is odd on a day the stores are closed.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one hero for the send.** Lead with the single offer that matters today and demote the other to a one-line banner. Today that's the tote and gift offer, since the buy-3 message has already run all week.
- 2. **Put the math in the hero.** A line like "Mix any 7, pay for 3" is clearer than the asterisked fine print.
- 3. **Make the buttons verbs plus product** ("Shop hand soaps", "Shop 3-wick candles"). Add prices or "from $" to the product strip, plus a star rating.
- 4. **Cut the footer weight.** Drop the brand-values block and "Find a store" on a store-closed day. Move the fine print to a collapsed or linked terms line.
- 5. **Respect cadence.** Send less often, or change the creative each time instead of repeating the same hero.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Thanksgiving only: 4 free gifts + a tote with $40 🦃`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Pay for 3, get 4 free. Online today only.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus a first-ever gift set worth $110+. Stores open early Friday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Soaps, candles, wallflowers. Mix any 7, pay for 3.`
