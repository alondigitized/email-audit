# spoiler alert: tomorrow's deal is worth it. 👀
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-17**
## Executive summary

- This is the clearest, most concrete send in the recent Bath & Body Works run: "$2.95 Wallflowers Fragrance Refills," "Lowest price of the season," and a limit of 24 per customer, all stacked right under the hero image. Unlike the 09-13 "click to reveal" flash sale that hid its own discount, this one leads with the number — no game, no guessing.
- The subject line plays coy ("spoiler alert: tomorrow's deal is worth it") while the email itself immediately un-coys it, which is a small internal contradiction but not a fatal one — the "Starts tomorrow" banner makes clear this is a preview/heads-up send, which is a distinct and fine tactic (build anticipation, get the date on the calendar) rather than a same-day close. Compared to the Member Days blitz (5 sends in 3 days stacking free shipping + BOGO + points + sweepstakes), this is refreshingly single-minded: one product line, one price, one CTA.
- Solid, on-brand, single-offer send — score it a clean mid-to-upper range for a pre-announcement email.

## What's working

- The price is the hero: "$2.95" in large white type is the first thing the eye hits after the headline.
- Single, unambiguous CTA — "Start your list" — ties directly to the "up to 24 per customer" stockpiling angle.
- "Lowest price of the season" gives a credible, dated (Sept 17–21) reason to act now rather than vague urgency.
- Clean visual hierarchy: banner → hero headline → price → CTA → supporting product shot, no competing offers crammed in.
- New scent ("Whipped Honey & Vanilla") is called out on the bottle itself, reinforcing "new" without needing separate copy.

## What's weak

- Subject line doesn't match the hero: "spoiler alert" implies mystery, but the email reveals the deal instantly — the tension is undercut by its own body copy.
- "Starts tomorrow" banner is small, dark, and easy to miss if a reader skims straight to the big price — someone could click "Start your list" expecting to buy today and hit friction.
- "We think you'll love" cross-sell row (Pocketbac, holder, body cream) is generic filler, unrelated to Wallflowers, diluting the single-offer focus that's otherwise this email's strength.
- Fine print is genuinely dense — the asterisked date window (Sept 17 6am–Sept 21 5:59am) lives only in the tiny legal footer, not in the visible hero copy.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the date window in the hero, not just the footer — e.g. "Sept 17–21 only" directly under "Lowest price of the season" so urgency is visible without scrolling to legal text.
- 2. Align subject and body: either make the subject literally reveal the price ("$2.95 Wallflowers — starts tomorrow") or make the body hold back a beat (e.g., blur/tease the price) so the "spoiler" framing pays off.
- 3. Swap the generic "We think you'll love" cross-sell block for Wallflowers-adjacent add-ons (plugs, scent boosters) to reinforce rather than dilute the single-offer message.
- 4. Make "Starts tomorrow" visually louder — larger type or a contrasting color — since it's the one thing that prevents a mismatched-expectations click today.
