# 75% off body care. 🙌 plus, new price drops start today! ⬇️
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- This is Bath & Body Works' July 19 send — at least the seventh email in a five-day Semi-Annual Sale blitz — and it's doing two things simultaneously: riding the "75% off body care" freight train and introducing a Father's Day urgency lane with a "Last Call" men's module. The "NEW PRICE DROPS!" banner is the intended differentiator, but it runs into a credibility problem: candles are still priced at $7.99–$12.99, the identical range from the June 17 email scored 9/10 here. When a "new drop" looks exactly like the old price, the headline reads as noise.
- The strongest thing going for this send is the Father's Day timing — June 21 is two days out, the pickup deadline is visible in the preheader, and the men's body care module at $6.95 earns its place. The scarcity line ("Once they're gone, they're gone") is credible when backed by the Father's Day clock. That real deadline saves the email from feeling like pure re-skin.
- Overall: a functionally competent promotional email hobbled by five-day blitz fatigue and a "new price drops" claim that underdelivers on the evidence visible in the render. Solid mid-range execution, nothing to write home about.

## What's working

- **Father's Day urgency is legitimate and time-stamped** — preheader surfaces the June 21 in-store pickup deadline, and the "Last Call" men's module at $6.95 earns the scarcity framing.
- **Five offer modules with explicit prices** — candles, body care, men's, hand soaps, Wallflowers all carry a dollar figure; the subscriber knows what they'd be clicking toward.
- **"Once they're gone, they're gone"** — scarcity copy is credible here because a real deadline (Father's Day) backs it up.
- **Clean render, no visible bugs** — layout is tight, images load, no broken elements or overlapping text.

## What's weak

- **"NEW PRICE DROPS!" banner betrayed by candle pricing** — $7.99–$12.99 candles appeared in the June 17 email; presenting the same price range under a "new drop" banner is a trust-eroding move for engaged subscribers.
- **"SHOP" CTAs are maximally generic** — five identical "SHOP" buttons with no object noun means a subscriber can't orient from a quick scan; "Shop Father's Gifts" or "Shop Candles" would differentiate the sections.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is the seventh-or-eighth BBW send in five days; no loyalty call-out, no segmentation signal, nothing to feel like it's speaking specifically to this subscriber versus blasting the full list.
- **75% off body care is no longer fresh** — the June 16 email ("🤩is this for real?! ➡️75% off select body care") already owned this headline with a 9/10 score. Repeating the hero claim without a meaningfully new hook feels like list exhaustion.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the Father's Day deadline, not the sale badge** — this send has a real clock (June 21, two days out). Make the clock the hero: "2 days to Father's Day — gifts under $7" outranks generic sale copy.
- 2. **Differentiate the "price drops" specifically** — if Wallflowers and men's body care are genuinely new drops, say so in the subject. Vague "price drops" next to unchanged candle pricing damages credibility.
- 3. **Replace "SHOP" CTAs with object-specific labels** — "Shop Gifts for Dad" on the men's module and "Shop Candles" on the candle section lets eyes navigate faster on mobile.
- 4. **Add a scarcity signal tied to inventory, not just the sale** — "Low stock" or "selling fast" on the Father's Day module would sharpen urgency. The sale badge alone doesn't do it after seven emails.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last call: Father's Day gifts under $7 ⏰`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New price drops — gifts for Dad, plus 75% off body care`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Order by tonight for Father's Day pickup — men's body care from $6.95`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Fresh price drops on Wallflowers, hand soaps & men's gifts`

### 1. Technical Summary

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Unprobed tracking links:** 23 click-redirect URLs via `click.e2.bathandbodyworks.com` were skipped by the HTTP probe. Destination UTM params and redirect integrity are unverified.

**Empty merge token in Moveable Ink URLs (9 instances):** The `mi_u=` subscriber identifier parameter is blank on all `mi.bathandbodyworks.com` image URLs:
```
https://mi.bathandbodyworks.com/p/rp/ddecf1d4739b65f5.png?mi_u=&mi_ecmp=061926_NRDM...
```
An empty `mi_u` means Moveable Ink cannot personalize or target content to the correct subscriber. This affects at least `ddecf1d4739b65f5.png` (served twice), `e186cf66af258bea.png`, `9f2e8147e80eb2f8.png`, and `5eb8119936914a02.png`.

**Empty `e=` in Everest engagement URL:**
```

```
The `e=` subscriber/email parameter is unfilled, which will break any subscriber-level attribution in Everest.

**Two images with empty `src`:** QA flagged two images with blank `src` attributes (`src: `). These will generate broken image renders or spurious HTTP requests depending on the client.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Google Fonts `@import` (stripped by major clients):**
```css
@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Source+Sans+Pro&display=swap');
```
Gmail, Yahoo Mail, and Outlook strip `<style>` blocks or `@import` directives. `Source Sans Pro` will silently fall back to system fonts (`Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif` per inline styles). Not a crash, but design fidelity degrades across the majority of email clients.

**Alt text missing on 9 images:** All five Moveable Ink images, the `418d0db7` hero image, the two blank-`src` images, and the Everest image have no `alt` attribute. Screen readers will skip these entirely; in image-off rendering (common in corporate environments), content is invisible.

**Pixel tracker `alt=""` present but content is empty:** The open-pixel `<img>` at `click.e2.bathandbodyworks.com/open.aspx?...` has `alt=""` which is correct for a tracking pixel — no issue here.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Unresolved shell template variable (appears twice):**
```html
<!-- `date "+ET%m.%y"` -->
```
This literal Unix shell command appears in the HTML source twice — it was never substituted during build. It should have resolved to something like `ET06.26`. This is a build/template pipeline failure. While it's inside HTML comments and invisible to subscribers, it indicates the template rendering step did not complete correctly, raising questions about whether other dynamic substitutions (e.g., `mi_u=`, `e=`) also failed.

**AMPscript variable scaffolding present, outcome unknown:** The HTML contains AMPscript comments referencing `@NTF_Flag` and `@APT` variable logic (Salesforce Marketing Cloud). The truncated source doesn't confirm whether these resolved to values or were stripped — but the pattern of other empty merge params (`mi_u=`, `e=`) suggests the personalization pass may have been incomplete.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header not found:**
One-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo for bulk senders since Feb 2024) depends on `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) headers being present. Both are missing per QA. This may be a relay capture gap (AgentMail may not forward these headers), but if they are genuinely absent at the SMTP layer, the email is non-compliant with Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender requirements.

**Authentication-Results header not found:**
SPF and DKIM status is unknown — the AgentMail relay did not surface this header. Sending domain is `e2.bathandbodyworks.com` (ExactTarget/SFMC subdomain). Without confirmed DMARC pass, deliverability risk exists for ISPs enforcing strict alignment.

**CAN-SPAM physical address:** Not verifiable from truncated HTML — full source review needed to confirm presence of postal address in footer.

**Preheader text present:** `"Semi-Annual Sale just keeps getting better..."` — hidden div with `display:none` and `max-height:0px` is correctly implemented.
