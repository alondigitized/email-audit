# happening now: buy 1 candle, get 1 free. 🤩
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-07**
## Executive summary

- This is another entry in Bath & Body Works' three-offer-stacking playbook — same structure as the last several sends: a hero deal, a second unrelated deal immediately below it, then a grid of category tiles. The hero itself is genuinely nice: "First Flicker of Fall" with a fall-leaf-and-candle beauty shot lands the seasonal mood, and "Buy 1, Get 1 Free" on all candles is a real, clear, high-value offer with a visible SHOP button.
- Where it falls apart is the immediate second pitch: "First dibs on fall" pushes men's body care and cologne, which has nothing to do with the candle hero above it and buries the fall theme under a different product category and a different gender target within two scrolls. That's the same fatigue pattern flagged in the 2026-08-03 "buy 3, get 3 free" send (8/10) — except here the two offers don't even share a throughline, they just happen to both be "fall."
- Solid hero, undermined by tacking on a mismatched second offer instead of letting the BOGO candle deal breathe. Worth shipping as-is for the candle fans, but the men's cologne insert is dead weight for most of the list.

## What's working

- Hero offer is unmistakable: "BUY 1, GET 1 FREE" in large bold type with a SHOP button directly beneath it.
- Seasonal styling (maple leaf, warm amber tones, "First Flicker of Fall") is cohesive and on-brand for fall launch season.
- The candle product shot is clean, well-lit, and clearly the star of the frame.
- "Limit 24" and "Lowest-priced item is free" fine print is visible but not hidden or buried — sits right under the offer.
- Category tile grid (Fragrance, Body Care, Candles, Wallflowers, etc.) gives a low-friction second path to browse without derailing the hero message.

## What's weak

- The men's body care/cologne block directly below the candle hero is a jarring category and gender switch — no visual or thematic bridge connects it to "First Flicker of Fall."
- Two offers before any category browsing means the eye has to reset twice in the first two screens — classic promo fatigue from this brand's recent cadence.
- No product name for the featured candle in the hero (just "Pumpkin Pecan Waffle" visible on the jar label itself, not called out in headline copy) — a shopper has to zoom in to know what candle is being sold.
- "STARTS TODAY!" banner is used for the second offer while "LIMITED TIME ONLY!" tops the first — competing urgency banners in the same email dilute both.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the men's body care/cologne block from this send entirely, or move it to a separate email — let the candle BOGO stand alone as the hero message, which is strong enough to carry the whole email on its own.
- 2. Name the candle in the headline copy (e.g., "Pumpkin Pecan Waffle") so the product is identifiable without reading the tiny label text.
- 3. Pick one urgency banner style and reuse it — having "LIMITED TIME ONLY!" and "STARTS TODAY!" stacked in the same scroll undercuts both.
- 4. If the second offer must stay, reframe it as complementary (e.g., a "fall gifting" category umbrella) rather than a hard category jump from candles to men's cologne.
- **Subject Alt A:** `first flicker of fall: buy 1 candle, get 1 free 🕯️`
- **Subject Alt B:** `today only: all candles buy 1, get 1 free`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Pumpkin Pecan Waffle and more — lowest-priced candle is free.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Fall's favorite candles, BOGO through Aug 10.`

# Technical Audit: Bath & Body Works — "buy 1 candle, get 1 free" (2026-08-07)

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- Open-tracking pixel confirmed: `https://click.e2.bathandbodyworks.com/open.aspx?OOBVYAMNOTQE3MM6ERTUXLKTPU.70251&d=70251&bmt=0` (1x1, `alt=""` — correctly marked decorative).
- QA skipped HTTP probing on **32 tracking/click-redirect links** (SFMC `click.e2.bathandbodyworks.com` domain) — destination URLs cannot be verified as live/non-404 from this audit; recommend a manual click-through pass before send.
- One image reference resolves to a third-party engagement domain rather than the standard SFMC image host: `src="https://bbwus.everestengagement.com/ea/fw0q51vctP/?e=&c=080726_EM_SU_BAU_PROMO_0_NRDM_B1G1ALLCDL_AM_BAL_FULL_V1"`. This is a tracking/engagement pixel, not a static asset — flagged below under Rendering since its `alt` attribute also appears to contain a raw URL rather than descriptive text.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 4 images fail alt-text checks per QA:
  - `418d0db7-5a65-43cb-b5c9-9c6c142e4fd2.png` (`src: https://image.e2.bathandbodyworks.com/lib/fe9213737561047a72/m/1/418d0db7-5a65-43cb-b5c9-9c6c142e4fd2.png`) — no `alt` attribute.
  - Two images with **empty `src` and empty alt** (`src: ` / name: blank) — these are broken `<img>` tags with no source, not just an accessibility gap; will render as broken-image icons in clients that don't suppress them.
  - The `everestengagement.com` pixel above has a URL string in place of alt text (`alt="https://bbwus.everestengagement.com/ea/fw0q51vctP/?e=&c=0807..."`) rather than empty or descriptive alt — likely a template/merge artifact rather than intentional markup.
- Head uses `@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Source+Sans+Pro...')` (declared twice, once in `<head>` and again inline in a `stylingblock-content-wrapper`) — `@import` in email `<style>` is not honored by Outlook/most webmail clients; no fallback web-safe font is declared alongside it in the affected blocks, so those clients will fall back to browser/client default rather than a controlled fallback.
- Markup otherwise follows standard SFMC/email conventions (HTML 4.01 Transitional doctype, MSO conditional comments for Outlook DPI, table-based layout, hidden zero-width-character preheader padding) — these are expected patterns for cross-client email rendering, not defects.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

These indicate the email relies on an `@APT`/`@NTF_Flag` AMPscript branch with **no default fallback** if `@APT` is left unset in a non-NTF send — a risk if this template is reused without setting that variable, but not an observed failure in this instance.
