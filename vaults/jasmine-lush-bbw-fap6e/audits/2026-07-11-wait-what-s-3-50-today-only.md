# wait, what's $3.50 today only? 👀
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-11**
## Executive summary

- This is the third $2.95–$3.50 mini deal B&BW has sent in two days. Yesterday's "final hours" email closed out the $2.95 window; today they've nudged the price up $0.55 and reopened with a "today only" hook. The subject teases the price without naming the product, which works as a curiosity driver but undercuts clarity. The hero — "Serving Up Cozy Vibes" with a fall-coded candle visual — is a soft-pivot to fall that matches what was started on July 8 with the Wallflowers plug send.
- The real problem is what's below the fold. B&BW has stacked at least five distinct offer blocks (the $3.50 hero, a BOGO "First Flicker of Fall," a "Coffee Order" collection, a BUY 3 GET 3 skin care deal, a "Hot Scents Cool Prices $3.50" block, and a Summer Sale 75%-off banner) into a single send. That's not a promotional email — it's a catalog page. The dilution is severe and consistent with this brand's recent pattern: every send since July 6 has suffered from the same multi-offer sprawl.
- Verdict: mildly better subject execution than the July 10 "final hours" send, but structurally the same mistake. B&BW is training subscribers to wait for the next offer rather than act on this one.

## What's working

- The "wait, what's $3.50?" subject creates a genuine curiosity gap — the withheld product name earns the open.
- Hero visual is warm and seasonally coherent (cozy/fall palette, candle prop), consistent with the early-fall pivot begun July 8.
- "TODAY ONLY" urgency stamp appears in the hero and is reiterated in the "Hot Scents" block, so it registers twice without feeling desperate.
- Summer Sale 75%-off banner near the bottom is a legitimate high-value anchor for deal-seekers who scroll.

## What's weak

- Five-plus competing offer blocks completely neutralize the hero. The $3.50 mini deal, the BOGO, the Coffee Order collection, the BUY 3 GET 3, and the 75%-off Summer Sale all fight for attention — none wins.
- The $3.50 price nudge from yesterday's $2.95 is never explained or positioned as an upgrade; it just reads as a different mini deal, eroding the "TODAY ONLY" story.
- No CTA button is visible on the hero unit — the "SHOP" link is text-small and easy to miss, especially at mobile widths.
- Demographic/model signals are absent from the hero; the candle-and-product flatlay is generic, not aspirational.
- This is the third consecutive same-format mini-deal email; cadence fatigue is real for anyone who opened the last two.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill four of the five offer blocks.** Run one message: the $3.50 mini. Everything else goes in its own send. This single change would lift CTR materially.
- 2. **Give the hero a real CTA button**, not a text link — "Shop $3.50 Minis" in a high-contrast pill button, above the fold.
- 3. **Name the product in the subject.** "wait, what's $3.50? 🕯️ your cozy fall mini" beats pure mystery when subscribers are promo-fatigued.
- 4. **Acknowledge the cadence** — "We changed our minds: the mini's back at $3.50" would be honest and charming instead of hoping subscribers didn't notice yesterday.
- 5. **Preheader is wasted.** Replace it with the product name and scent family to complement the teaser subject.
- **Subject Alt A:** `your $3.50 fall mini is here. today only. 🕯️`
- **Subject Alt B:** `we couldn't stop at $2.95 — $3.50 cozy minis, today only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cozy fall scents in pocket-sized form — grab yours before midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New mini drop: warm, fall-ready, and $3.50 today only.`
