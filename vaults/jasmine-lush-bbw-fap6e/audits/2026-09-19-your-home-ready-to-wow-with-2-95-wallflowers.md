# your home = ready to wow with $2.95 Wallflowers. 😍
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-19**
## Executive summary

- This is a scattergun email dressed up as a single offer — the subject promises "$2.95 Wallflowers" but the body stacks four distinct promotions (Wallflowers Fragrance Refills, "New scents just dropped," a 50%-off Halloween code, and $14.95 3-Wick Candles) with no clear priority between them. The hero section itself is clean and well-executed — pumpkin pecan waffles imagery, price, and "up to 24 per customer" all land together — but by the time a reader scrolls past the second and third full-width promo blocks, the $2.95 hook from the subject line is old news.
- Against the brand history, this is a step backward from the tight 09-17 "spoiler alert" and "$2.95 lowest price" sends (both 8/10) that led with one offer and stuck to it, and closer to the messy 09-17 "$1.95 deal" send (6/10) that crammed three offers into one email. The in-store barcode block is a nice functional touch, but visually it's buried mid-scroll where most readers on a promo email won't linger.
- Verdict: good hero, undisciplined body — this needs to be split into two sends or trimmed to one offer per email.

## What's working

- Hero section is crisp: "$2.95 Wallflowers Fragrance Refills," "Lowest price of the season," and "Up to 24 per customer" are all visible without hunting.
- Product photography (waffle scent, candle/orchard scene) is seasonally on-brand and high quality, no broken images.
- "Shop now" CTA buttons are consistent, high-contrast, and repeated at each offer block so scanning readers always have a next action.
- The 50% off Halloween block adds real urgency ("2-day offer") with a visible promo code, which is concrete and actionable.

## What's weak

- Four separate offers (Wallflowers $2.95, "new scents," 50% Halloween, $14.95 candles) compete for attention with no single hero — visual hierarchy collapses after the first screen.
- Subject line promises Wallflowers, but visually the boldest, most saturated block in the email is the purple/orange Halloween 50%-off banner, not the Wallflowers offer.
- "We think you'll love" recommendation carousel is generic filler that adds scroll length without a matching offer.
- The barcode/promo code block reads flat and could be missed — it's not visually tied to the Halloween banner above it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this to one offer. Ship the $2.95 Wallflowers deal alone (as the strong 09-17 sends did) and move the Halloween 50%-off and candle offer into separate sends — stacking kills conversion focus.
- 2. If multiple offers must stay in one email, re-order so the highest-value/most-urgent offer (arguably the 2-day Halloween 50% off) leads, with Wallflowers second — align subject line to whatever leads visually.
- 3. Drop or shrink the "We think you'll love" and "Our feel good formula" filler blocks — they add length without incremental offer value.
- 4. Tie the barcode visually to the Halloween banner (shared background/border) so in-store shoppers don't scroll past it.

- **Subject Alt B:** `your home, wowed: $2.95 Wallflowers refills`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Lowest price of the season, up to 24 per customer`

## Full review
## 1. Overview
