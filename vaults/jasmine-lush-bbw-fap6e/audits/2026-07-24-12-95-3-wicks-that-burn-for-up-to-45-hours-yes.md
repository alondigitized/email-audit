# 🌟 $12.95 3-wicks that burn for up to 45 hours? yes!
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- This is the third B&BW touchpoint on the $12.95 3-Wick Candle event in two days — yesterday's teaser ("set your alarms"), yesterday's launch ("get glowing"), and now this retention send. The smart play here is the "45 hours burn" feature angle: instead of just rebroadcasting "deal is still live," it gives the subscriber a new, specific reason to engage. That's better than most follow-up sends.
- The hero executes cleanly — "Fall for the Glow / $12.95 / LIMITED TIME ONLY / SHOP" is a complete, readable value proposition above the fold. But then the email immediately pivots to a "Today Only! Daily Essentials in New Scents / Men's Body Care / $3.5" section, which competes with the candle hero and splits attention. The Summer Sale 75% block arrives a third, making this a three-headline email with one CTA theme.
- The real weakness is the subject-to-body coherence. The subject introduces "burns for up to 45 hours" as the hook, but the body doesn't lean into that claim with any depth — no burn chart, no comparison, no dramatized lifestyle moment. The feature is mentioned once in small print under the hero and never reinforced. That's a missed opportunity to justify this third send.

## What's working

- Hero price ($12.95) is large and immediately readable without any guesswork.
- "Fall for the Glow" creative direction is consistent with yesterday's launch — no whiplash for subscribers who opened both.
- "Enjoy up to 45 hours to burn" is the right differentiating hook for a second-day send; something new was needed and this delivers it.
- No render bugs — images load, text is clean, layout holds.
- "TODAY ONLY!" badge on the Men's Body Care block does add urgency to a secondary offer without spilling onto the primary.

## What's weak

- Three distinct headline offers (candle event, men's body care, Summer Sale 75%) create click-decision fatigue — the candle hero never fully owns the send.
- The "45 hours" claim introduced in the subject is not dramatized anywhere in the body — it surfaces once in small copy and disappears.
- Every CTA is labeled "SHOP" — undifferentiated and generic; with three offer tracks, buttons need to say which shop.
- "Fall for the Glow" is a fall-seasonality frame in late July — the slight seasonal dissonance is minor but real.
- This is the third email on this deal in 36 hours; subscribers primed by the teaser opened yesterday's launch, which scored 9/10. Today's send has to justify itself with new information, and "45 hours" is the right angle — but it's not leaned into enough to fully earn the send.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make "45 hours" the body's hero, not a footnote.** The subject promises a specific, impressive claim. The body should deliver it visually — a simple graphic ("45 hours of fragrance. That's less than $0.29/hour.") would turn a generic candle-sale email into a value-proposition story. This is the single change with the highest lift.
- 2. **Collapse the competing offers.** Men's Body Care "Today Only!" is strong enough to stand alone as a secondary module — but not wedged between the candle hero and the "Why Pick Our 3-Wicks?" explainer. Move it below the 3-wick rationale block so the top half of the email stays unified on candles.
- 3. **Differentiate the CTAs.** "Shop 3-Wick Candles," "Shop Men's Care," "Shop Summer Sale" — three buttons, three tracks, zero confusion.
- 4. **Add credible urgency to the subject.** The deal has a finite window; the subject doesn't signal it.

- **Subject Alt B:** `🕯️ 45 hours of glow for $12.95. Today only.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `That's less than $0.29 per hour of fragrance — shop before it's gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `High-fragrance 3-Wicks, limited window. Your top scents are here.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Duplicate Google Fonts import — double HTTP request:**
`@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Source+Sans+Pro&display=swap')` appears in `<head>` and again as an inline `<style>` block inside the email body (`family=Source+Sans+Pro:wght@400;700&display=swap`). These are two separate requests; the second also requests weights 400 and 700 that the first omits. Consolidate to one import with all required weights.

**`<meta charset>` placement:**
`<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8">` is placed after the `<style>` block and MSO conditionals in `<head>`. Per HTML spec, charset declaration should be the first child of `<head>` to ensure correct encoding detection before any content is parsed. Low risk in practice for UTF-8/ASCII emails, but technically non-compliant ordering.
