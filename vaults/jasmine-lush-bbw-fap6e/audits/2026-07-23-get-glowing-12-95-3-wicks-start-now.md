# get glowing: $12.95 3-wicks start now. ✨
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-23**
## Executive summary

- This is the payoff email for the most effective B&BW tease of the recent run — yesterday's "⏰ set your alarms, you don't want to miss this $12.95 deal tomorrow" setup was a clean 8/10 precisely because it held back the offer and created a reason to open today. This delivery does not squander that setup: the hero is immediately "Fall for the Glow / $12.95 / All 3-Wick Candles / STARTS TODAY!" — unambiguous, bold, and above the fold. Subject line matches the hero. The promise is kept.
- What undermines it is what B&BW does almost every time: they couldn't leave it at one punch. Below the hero there are three more distinct offer blocks — a mid-email "Your Go-to Coffee Orders, Bottled Up" lifestyle module, a BUY 3 GET 3 FREE on full-size body/skin/hair care, and a 75% Summer Sale grid. The coffee-orders block is the worst offender: it is tonally disconnected from "Fall for the Glow," it has its own lifestyle imagery and SHOP button, and it asks the eye to re-orient entirely in the middle of what should be a single-minded candle event.
- The verdict: this email earns its send. The teaser-to-delivery mechanic works. But the clutter below the hero costs clicks that should have been easy conversions on the $12.95 event.

## What's working

- **Teaser delivery is exact.** "Starts Today!" directly honors yesterday's "don't miss this deal tomorrow" promise — subscribers who saw both emails get a completion loop, which is rare for B&BW to execute cleanly.
- **Hero offer is above the fold and unambiguous.** $12.95 / All 3-Wick Candles / "Fall for the Glow" occupies the full-width hero. No hunting required.
- **Subject line is the best of recent B&BW sends.** "get glowing: $12.95 3-wicks start now. ✨" is concrete, under 50 chars, no spam signals, and has a soft urgency signal ("start now") without screaming.
- **"Why Pick Our 3-Wicks?" module is a useful trust block.** Calling out premium, lead-free wicks in a dedicated section right after the hero gives hesitant buyers a reason to click. It's rare to see B&BW invest real estate in product justification.
- **Render is clean.** No broken images, no lorem ipsum, no layout collapse visible anywhere in the email.

## What's weak

- **The coffee module ("Your Go-to Coffee Orders, Bottled Up") kills narrative momentum.** It lands immediately after the hero and asks the reader to pivot from fall candles to coffee-inspired body products. These may be autumn-adjacent scents, but the editorial framing is jarring and the SHOP button here competes directly with the candle CTA.
- **No deadline on the $12.95 offer.** "Starts Today!" is visible but there is no "ends Sunday" or "through 7/27 only" callout anywhere in the hero or below it. Without an end date, urgency erodes.
- **BUY 3 GET 3 FREE (body/skin/hair) is a stronger offer than the hero and it's buried.** For subscribers who care more about body care than candles, this deal would beat $12.95 candles. Positioning it below the fold as a secondary module means it's likely seen by fewer openers than it deserves.
- **"SHOP" CTA copy is generic throughout.** Every button in this email reads SHOP. A button that says "Get $12.95 Candles" or "Shop the Event" would capture more intent at a glance without any design lift.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill or re-sequence the coffee module.** Move it to a dedicated send or push it below the Summer Sale grid. The fall candle event deserves the entire above-fold and mid-email space without a beverage detour.
- 2. **Add an offer expiry to the hero.** Even "Valid through 7/27" under the $12.95 callout converts "starts today" urgency into something with a closing door. This is the single highest-lift change.
- 3. **Specifize the hero CTA.** Replace the generic "SHOP" button under the hero with "Shop $12.95 Candles" or "Fall into $12.95." Takes ten seconds to write, meaningfully increases intent clarity.
- 4. **Surface one specific scent in the hero image.** The current hero shows several candle jars in warm tones, which is pleasant but anonymous. Naming one signature fall scent (e.g., "Mahogany Teakwood," "Autumn," "Sweater Weather") in the hero gives loyalists a reason to click immediately rather than browsing the full grid.
- 5. **Elevate BUY 3 GET 3 FREE.** Consider a two-column layout that puts the candle event and the B3G3 body/hair offer side by side — both are strong; let subscribers self-select rather than forcing linear discovery.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary

The email is built on Salesforce Marketing Cloud (ExactTarget) infrastructure with Movable Ink personalization and Everest engagement tracking. Four categories of issues are confirmed by source inspection and automated QA: missing SPF/DKIM authentication visibility, unresolved merge tokens on the Movable Ink image, multiple images lacking `alt` attributes, and two `<img>` tags with empty `src` values.

---

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**24 click-tracking links skipped by QA probe** — all routed through `click.e2.bathandbodyworks.com`. This is expected ESP behavior (SFMC/ExactTarget click redirect). Cannot validate final destination URLs or UTM params without following redirects.

**Open pixel URL contains what appears to be an unresolved or raw subscriber token:**
```
https://click.e2.bathandbodyworks.com/open.aspx?FK4QEQZGE6AELP4FFS2XV4US2I.70253&d=70253&bmt=0
```
The `FK4QEQZGE6AELP4FFS2XV4US2I.70253` segment is not a standard SFMC substitution format — confirm this is intentional encoding and not a failed merge.

**Third-party engagement tracker present** (`bbwus.everestengagement.com`) — this is a reputation/engagement scoring pixel, separate from ESP tracking. No alt text (see Section 3).

**Movable Ink image URL has empty `mi_u=` parameter:**
```
https://mi.bathandbodyworks.com/p/rp/c20f77f5336a84c1.png?mi_u=&mi_ecmp=072326_EM_SU_BAU_PROMO_0_NRDM_12953W_AM_BAL-NTF_
```
`mi_u` is the subscriber identifier Movable Ink uses for personalization. If this is empty at send time, Movable Ink cannot serve subscriber-specific content and will fall back to a default. Verify the AMPscript variable populating `mi_u` is resolving correctly.

---

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Google Fonts imported twice via `@import`:**
- `<head>`: `Source+Sans+Pro&display=swap`
- Body `<style>` block: `Source+Sans+Pro:wght@400;700&display=swap`

The body-level import includes weight variants the head import omits. Gmail strips `<style>` blocks entirely; `@import` is unsupported in Outlook. The font will silently fall back to Arial/Helvetica in both clients. This is a pre-existing known email-client limitation, but the duplicate import creates unnecessary HTTP overhead for clients that do render it.

**Two `<img>` tags with empty `src` attributes confirmed in QA:**
```
[WARN] Image missing alt text: : src: 
[WARN] Image missing alt text: : src: 
```
Empty `src` causes browsers/email clients to either skip rendering or fire a request to the current page URL. These are likely unresolved template slots or spacer images where the `src` substitution failed.

**Five images missing `alt` text** (QA-confirmed). Affected images:
| Asset | Domain |
|---|---|
| `c20f77f5336a84c1.png` | `mi.bathandbodyworks.com` (Movable Ink) |
| `418d0db7-5a65-43cb-b5c9-9c6c142e4fd2.png` | `image.e2.bathandbodyworks.com` |
| Two images with empty `src` | — |
| Everest tracker image | `bbwus.everestengagement.com` |

Missing `alt` on the Movable Ink and ESP-hosted images is an accessibility failure (WCAG 2.1 1.1.1). The Everest tracker pixel can use `alt=""` (explicit empty) — that is acceptable and intentional for decorative/tracking pixels.

**Shell command fragment left in HTML source as comment (appears twice):**
```html
<!-- `date "+ET%m.%y"` -->
```
This appears to be a Bash date-formatting expression that was inserted as a comment rather than executed. It's non-functional but leaks template tooling conventions in plain HTML source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`mi_u` empty at render time** (confirmed above) — primary personalization risk.

**AMPscript variable names exposed in HTML comments:**
```html
<!--Adds daily APT set via ampscript. Adds preset APT for emails flagged NTF-->
<!--Set APT for NTF campaigns, otherwise respect email-defined APT-->
```

Comments are stripped by most clients but remain visible in raw source. The `@NTF_Flag` reference also documents that NTF (New-To-File) campaigns have no APT fallback — if a subscriber falls through this logic incorrectly, send-time behavior is undefined.

## 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent** (QA-confirmed). This is a required header for bulk senders under Google/Yahoo's 2024 bulk sender requirements. The QA caveat ("may not be captured by AgentMail relay") means this could be a capture gap rather than a true absence — but must be verified at the raw SMTP level.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent** (QA-confirmed). RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is required for >5k/day senders to Gmail/Yahoo. If these headers are genuinely missing (not just uncaptured), this is a deliverability risk.

**SPF/DKIM status unknown** — `Authentication-Results` header not captured by relay. Sending domain is `e2.bathandbodyworks.com`. Cannot confirm alignment without raw header inspection.

**Unsubscribe link** — not visible in the truncated source, but expected to be present further in the email footer. No finding here without full source.
