# 🤲 time to clean up: clean hands deals starting at $1.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-17**
## Executive summary

- B&BW delivers on yesterday's teaser ("🫧 lather up, a fresh deal starts tomorrow," 8/10) with a clean launch email built around The Clean Hands Event. The hero does exactly what was promised — "$1/$2" price anchors on hand soaps are bold, the "STARTS TODAY!" badge closes the loop, and the subject line is among the strongest in this brand's recent run: price-first, category-named, no spam noise.
- The problem is everything below the fold. A "TODAY ONLY!" deal module, a "Cast a Spell on Your Senses" $10 Off barcode coupon, a "Picked just for you!" body-spray grid, a "Summer Sale — up to 75% off" module, and a "TOP OFFERS" horizontal scroll all compete for attention in what should be a tight hand-care launch. Compared to the July 16 "final hours" send (9/10) — which was laser-focused on two prices and one CTA — this feels like a template that absorbed every live promotion before it shipped.
- The subject is doing heavier lifting than the email deserves. A subscriber who opens for $1 soaps has to mentally block out four unrelated modules to find the click.

## What's working

- **"$1" price anchor in the subject** is unusually low and specific for B&BW — it earns opens in a crowded inbox.
- **Hero section delivers on the teaser** — "STARTS TODAY!" and $1/$2 pricing confirm what yesterday's "lather up" email promised.
- **Emoji choice is on-brand** — 🤲 is thematically relevant and non-gimmicky, a cut above their usual 🤑 closers.
- **"Picked just for you!" personalization module** gives the email a tailored feel even if it's likely behavioral segmentation.

## What's weak

- **Four competing promotional modules below the fold** fragment the Clean Hands narrative — the Summer Sale 75% off module alone could be its own email.
- **Generic "SHOP" CTA** across every section — "Shop $1 Soaps" or "Shop the Event" would have driven click intent.
- **No visible end date for the Clean Hands Event** in the body despite "STARTS TODAY!" — the urgency loop never closes.
- **The "Cast a Spell" coupon barcode** is jarring mid-email — thematically mismatched to the clean/fresh hand-care moment.
- **Today Only! module immediately undercuts the hero** — a different deal, different products, different CTA, two sentences below the $1 price anchor.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut to two sections:** Hero (Clean Hands Event) + one supporting CTA (the personalized body spray grid). Drop or defer the Summer Sale, Today Only!, and Cast a Spell modules to separate sends — they each deserve their own moment rather than cannibalizing each other here.
- 2. **Add end date to the hero:** "Shop $1 soaps — through Sunday" turns "STARTS TODAY" into a real countdown.
- 3. **Rewrite the CTA to mirror the subject:** "Start at $1" or "Shop $1 Hand Soaps" > "SHOP".
- 4. **Move the $10 Off coupon to a loyalty-specific followup**, not mid-launch email — it's a separate conversion mechanic that dilutes the price anchor.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$1 hand soaps are here: The Clean Hands Event starts today`
- **Subject Alt B:** `clean hands event: $1 soaps, $2 foaming wash — starts now.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Hand soaps from $1, foaming wash from $2 — but only while the event lasts.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `You saw the teaser. Here's the deal: soaps from $1, today only.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound for a table-based HTML email, but has two notable compliance gaps (missing `List-Unsubscribe` headers) and several rendering/accessibility issues, including broken image references and leaked build artifacts in the HTML comments.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**24 click-tracking links skipped** — all route through `click.e2.bathandbodyworks.com` (Salesforce Marketing Cloud). Destination URLs and UTM parameters could not be verified by the probe since the redirect domain was skipped.

**Open pixel** — `https://click.e2.bathandbodyworks.com/open.aspx?U6CPLJC5G2AUDEFYJVYUPT3G2A.70248&d=70248&bmt=0` is rendered as a 1×1 `<img>` with `alt=""`. Alt is blank, which is acceptable for a tracking pixel, but it is placed inside a `<table>` that is hidden with `mso-hide:all` — correct Outlook suppression pattern.

**No issues with the pixel domain itself**, but UTM parameter coverage on destination URLs is unverified (see §6).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Two images with empty `src` attributes:**
```
[WARN] Image missing alt text: : src: (×2)
```
Empty `src` triggers an HTTP request to the page's own URL in some clients (legacy behavior). These are broken image references — either dynamic slots that failed to populate or stripped template variables.

**Three content images missing `alt` text:**
- `https://mi.bathandbodyworks.com/p/rp/68e911b8f2d75c07.png?mi_u=200511831233&mi_ecmp=071726_EM_SU_BAU_PROMO_0_NRDM_CLEANH` — Movable Ink personalized image, no `alt`
- `https://image.e2.bathandbodyworks.com/lib/fe9213737561047a72/m/1/418d0db7-5a65-43cb-b5c9-9c6c142e4fd2.png` — static asset, no `alt`
- `https://bbwus.everestengagement.com/ea/fw0q51vctP/?e=200511831233&c=071726_EM_SU_BAU_PROMO_0_NRDM_CLEANHANDS_AM_NTH_G6M_` — Everest engagement pixel, no `alt` (acceptable for a tracking pixel but worth flagging)

Screen readers will announce unlabeled images as the filename or URL, degrading accessibility for visually impaired recipients.

**Duplicate `<style>` block** — the Google Fonts `@import` for `Source+Sans+Pro` appears twice: once in `<head>` (weight 400 only) and again inside a `<td>` (weights 400 and 700). The in-body `<style>` tag is stripped by several clients (Gmail, some Android clients). This means bold weight may silently fall back to system fonts in those clients.

**`<meta charset>` placement** — the `Content-Type` meta tag appears after the `<style>` block in `<head>`. Per HTML spec, the charset declaration should be the first element in `<head>` to guarantee correct parsing before any style rules are applied. Low risk in practice for UTF-8 email, but technically incorrect.

This shell command template appears twice verbatim — once before `<body>` and once before the preheader table. It was not evaluated; the backtick syntax was passed through raw. Visible to anyone viewing source; no functional impact, but indicates a broken template pipeline step.

These are internal campaign configuration notes left in the sent HTML. Not a security risk, but they expose ESP configuration logic to recipients who view source.

**Unpopulated CMS slot:**

This slot block rendered empty while the actual preheader text lives in a separate `<div class="preheader">`. The CMS slot was not used. No functional impact — the preheader text is present — but the empty slot div is dead markup.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not found** — flagged by QA as potentially not captured by the AgentMail relay. If absent from the actual delivered message headers, this violates Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender requirements (mandatory since February 2024 for senders >5k/day). Must be confirmed against raw message headers, not relay capture.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not found** — RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe header absent. Required by Gmail for bulk senders; without it, Gmail's unsubscribe button triggers a redirect flow rather than a POST, increasing friction and complaint risk.

**`Authentication-Results` header not found** — SPF and DKIM pass/fail status unknown due to relay capture gap. Cannot confirm authentication posture from available data. Sender domain `e2.bathandbodyworks.com` is a dedicated ESP subdomain (SFMC), which typically has SPF/DKIM configured, but this is unverified from this capture.

**CAN-SPAM:** An unsubscribe mechanism and physical address are expected in the body; neither is visible in the truncated source provided. Assumed present in the footer (not in scope from the truncated HTML), but cannot confirm.

The QA probe skipped these, so whether UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) are appended to final landing page URLs is unknown. This is the highest-priority manual verification step.

**Campaign code inconsistency between tracking pixels:**

The Movable Ink campaign code is truncated. If that field is used for attribution reporting, the MI impression data will not join correctly to the Everest/SFMC send data under the same campaign key.
