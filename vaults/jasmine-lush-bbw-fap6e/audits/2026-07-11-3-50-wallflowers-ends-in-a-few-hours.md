# ⏰ $3.50 Wallflowers ends in a few hours.
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-11**
## Executive summary

- This is the "final hours" closer on the $3.50 Wallflowers deal that B&BW launched this morning — the exact same playbook they ran on July 10 ($2.95 "today only" in the morning, "final hours" in the evening, scored 6/10). The subject line is the best thing about this email: specific, time-stamped, under 50 characters, no spam smell. But the moment you open it, B&BW buries the headline in a five-block avalanche of competing offers — a BOGO "First Flicker of Fall," a B3G3 body care deal, a second "FINAL HOURS: ONLINE ONLY!" block (the hot scents $3.50, which may or may not be the Wallflowers), and a Summer Sale 75%-off teaser. There's so much noise that by mid-scroll you've forgotten why you opened.
- The phrase "FINAL HOURS: ONLINE ONLY!" appears at least twice in the body, applied to what appear to be different offers. That redundancy drains the credibility of the urgency framing entirely — if everything is final hours, nothing is final hours.
- The send is competent template execution, not marketing. The subject line earns the open; the email doesn't earn the click.

## What's working

- **Subject is the strongest in this recent string.** "⏰ $3.50 Wallflowers ends in a few hours." is specific (named product, exact price, time signal) and clean — no exclamation stacking.
- **$3.50 price anchor appears in the hero**, so price-motivated openers get confirmation in the first scroll position.
- **No visible render bugs** — images load, text is legible, layout holds.
- **"First Flicker of Fall" BOGO block is seasonally timely** — a credible early-fall tease alongside a summer deal is smart shelf-shifting, even if it fights for attention here.

## What's weak

- **"FINAL HOURS: ONLINE ONLY!" appears on at least two separate offer blocks** — the phrase has lost meaning by the second use.
- **Five distinct offer blocks below the hero** (BOGO, B3G3, Hot Scents $3.50, Summer Sale 75%) turn a focused urgency send into a catalog dump; the Wallflowers deal promised in the subject can't be located at a glance.
- **All CTA buttons read "SHOP"** — generic across every block; no copy specificity to distinguish which deal you're clicking into.
- **This is the fifth B&BW email in three days** running some variant of the mini/Wallflowers price hook; cadence fatigue is real and each send dilutes the next.
- **The July 10 "final hours" email already ran this script on $2.95 minis** — any subscriber who noticed will recognize this as a template reset, not genuine urgency.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Strip the email to ONE offer.** The Wallflowers deal is the subject — make it the only body block. Remove the BOGO, B3G3, and Summer Sale sections entirely for this send. Urgency emails that try to carry a full catalog fail twice: they undermine urgency and depress click-through by paralysis.
- 2. **Name the scent in the hero copy.** "Serving Up Cozy Vibes" is vague lifestyle language. Swap to a specific scent name — "Pumpkin Pecan Waffles Wallflowers, $3.50" — so the subscriber knows exactly what they're buying before they click.
- 3. **Fix the CTA buttons.** Replace "SHOP" with "Grab Wallflowers for $3.50" on the primary. Every secondary block should disappear, so there's no secondary CTA to fix.
- 4. **Break the "today only / final hours" same-day cadence.** Running a morning "today only" and an evening "final hours" on back-to-back days for slightly different prices trains subscribers to wait — they learn the deal re-opens. Consider consolidating to a single send per deal day.
- 5. **Use the preheader to add specificity the subject couldn't hold.** If the scent isn't in the subject, it belongs here.
- **Subject Alt A:** `⏰ Pumpkin Pecan Waffles Wallflowers — just $3.50 today`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last call: $3.50 Wallflowers. Tonight only.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `One scent. One price. Gone tonight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your cozy fall plug-in for less than a coffee.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC/ExactTarget) send with standard table-based layout and click-redirect tracking. Three infrastructure concerns stand out: unresolved template artifacts in source comments, missing alt text on at least one non-decorative image, and compliance headers not observed by the monitoring relay.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Dual tracking pixels** — two separate open/engagement pixels fire on load:
- SFMC open pixel: `https://click.e2.bathandbodyworks.com/open.aspx?ETDAKVGKMH5E3OW2C6CBPE5EQ4.70252`
- Everest Engagement (Validity) pixel: `https://bbwus.everestengagement.com/ea/fw0q51vctP/?e=200511831233&c=071126_EM_...`

The Everest pixel renders via a malformed `<img>` with a blank `src` attribute (the QA scan shows `src: ` empty, actual URL in the `href`-adjacent field). Confirm this pixel is firing correctly — if `src` is truly blank in the delivered HTML, the pixel is broken.

**24 click-redirect links** all route through `click.e2.bathandbodyworks.com`. QA skipped HTTP probing on these (expected). No raw destination URLs are visible in the truncated source to verify UTM passthrough — see Section 6.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text (3 images flagged by QA):**
- `418d0db7-5a65-43cb-b5c9-9c6c142e4fd2.png` — product/hero image, no `alt` attribute. Screen readers will skip or announce the filename.
- Two additional images with empty `src` — likely spacers; empty `alt=""` would be correct here to mark them as decorative, but absence of the attribute is technically noncompliant with WCAG 2.1 SC 1.1.1.

**Google Fonts imported twice:**
```html
<!-- In <head> -->
@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Source+Sans+Pro&display=swap');

<!-- Inside a <style> block inside a <table> cell -->
@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Source+Sans+Pro:wght@400;700&display=swap');
```
The second import (in-body `<style>`) requests weight variants the first omits. Redundant round-trips; consolidate into one `<head>` import. Note: most email clients block external fonts anyway — the fallback stack (`Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif`) is what most subscribers see.

**Preheader structure is correct** — hidden div + zero-width non-joiner spacer padding both present. No issue.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Unresolved shell command artifacts** — the following comment appears twice in the source:
```html
<!-- `date "+ET%m.%y"` -->
```
This is a bash substitution that was never executed. It appears to be a build-time timestamp meant to encode the send month/year (e.g., `ET07.26`) for internal version tracking. It rendered as a literal string in the delivered HTML, leaking a template authoring artifact. Not a recipient-visible issue, but indicates a broken pre-send substitution step.

**Exposed AMPscript logic comments:**
```html
<!--Adds daily APT set via ampscript. Adds preset APT for emails flagged NTF-->
<!--If @NTF_Flag is "N", @APT should be set in the email; no default fallback here-->
```
These comments document internal personalization logic (APT = Adaptive Personalization Token, NTF = New-to-File flag). They are not visible to end users but are inspectable via View Source. Low risk, but constitutes unnecessary implementation exposure.

**No unresolved `%%Variable%%` merge tokens** observed in the truncated source. Preheader text is static ("no time to waste, grab your faves.") — no first-name or dynamic content visible.

### 5. Compliance

This header is required by Google and Yahoo bulk sender guidelines (enforced since Feb 2024) for senders >5k/day. The QA note attributes this to AgentMail relay capture limitations — the header may exist in actual SMTP delivery. **Must be verified against raw SMTP headers**, not just the relay capture.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) — not observed:**
> `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found`

One-click unsubscribe (the `POST` method variant) is required by Gmail's bulk sender policy. Same caveat applies — verify via raw headers.

Sending domain is `e2.bathandbodyworks.com`. Authentication status not captured by relay. Unverified — not a confirmed failure.
