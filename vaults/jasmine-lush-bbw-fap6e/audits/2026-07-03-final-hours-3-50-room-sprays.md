# final hours! $3.50 room sprays. 🤩
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-03**
## Executive summary

- This is the "final hours" close on the $3.50 All Concentrated Room Spray deal introduced in this morning's send — the same one-two pattern Bath & Body Works ran on July 2 with the $1 PocketBac (launch → urgency close). The formula works: "FINAL HOURS!" is planted in the top banner, the $3.50 hero price is the first thing the eye lands on, and the visual hierarchy is clean. Nothing is broken.
- Where it loses points is focus. Rather than letting the room spray urgency own the email, the brand stacks a 3-Wick Candle pod and a New Summer-Ready Soaps pod below — each with their own "LIMITED TIME ONLY" tags. That diffuses the "you're about to miss this" tension. The candles and soaps could be their own send; here they make the room spray CTA feel less urgent, not more.
- The "LOOK IN-STORE" callout beneath the $3.50 hero is the email's single weakest moment. For an online subscriber clicking to shop, it reads as a caveat — and caveats under the hero price are the fastest way to stall a click.

## What's working

- **"FINAL HOURS!" banner is unmissable** — occupies the first element in the scroll, sets urgency before the eye reaches the offer.
- **$3.50 price is front and center** in the hero pod — no ambiguity about what the deal is.
- **Subject line is disciplined** — concrete price, concrete product, "final hours" urgency, all under 37 chars. One of the cleaner lines in this blitz.
- **Summer product tie-in** — the Foaming Hand Soaps pod is legitimately new creative, not just recycled sale padding.
- **No render bugs** — clean layout, readable prices, no broken images.

## What's weak

- **"LOOK IN-STORE" under the $3.50 hero** creates doubt for online shoppers — it implies the deal is either in-store only or better in-store, which undercuts the click.
- **Three urgency pods, three "LIMITED TIME ONLY" tags** — when everything is final hours, nothing is.
- **"SHOP" CTA is too generic** — appears on every pod, indistinguishable from any other send; doesn't reinforce the specific deal.
- **No hard deadline** — "final hours" without a time ("ends at 11:59 PM ET") is weaker than it could be, especially on mobile where the clock matters.
- **Second email today** — this blitz now has at minimum two sends on July 3; at ~22+ emails in the campaign, list fatigue is a real risk for anyone who isn't already converted.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard deadline time to the hero pod** — "Ends tonight at 11:59 PM" turns "final hours" from a vague tease into a credible deadline. This is the single highest-leverage change.
- 2. **Remove or push the candle/soap pods below the fold** — let the room spray own the urgency. Stack the other offers in the "TOP OFFERS" grid rather than giving them full-pod treatment.
- 3. **Rewrite "LOOK IN-STORE" to "Also in stores"** or drop it entirely from the online email — don't give the subscriber a reason to hesitate.
- 4. **Sharpen the CTA copy** — "Shop $3.50 Sprays" instead of "SHOP" on the hero button; save the generic "SHOP" for secondary pods.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `last call: $3.50 room sprays end tonight`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `$3.50 room sprays: hours left. stock up.`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `Deal closes at midnight — grab All Concentrated Room Sprays now.`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `Stock up before $3.50 room sprays disappear tonight.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`<meta charset>` placed after conditional comments**
The `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8">` tag appears after the `<!--[if gte mso 9]>` block. Charset declaration should be the first element inside `<head>` to prevent pre-parse encoding errors in some Outlook versions.

**Two images with empty `src` attributes**
QA flagged two images with no `src` value:
```
[WARN] Image missing alt text: : src: 
[WARN] Image missing alt text: : src: 
```
An empty `src` causes some email clients to fire a GET request to the current page URL, generating noise in server logs and potentially broken image icons in the rendered email.

**Three content images missing `alt` text**
```
src: .../861e990bb83170bc.png        (likely hero/promo image)
src: .../418d0db7-5a65-43cb-b5c9-9c6c142e4fd2.png
src: https://bbwus.everestengagement.com/...  (tracking pixel — alt="" is acceptable here)
```
The two content images lack `alt` attributes entirely. Screen readers will read the filename or skip the image; image-off rendering (common in Outlook) will show nothing.

**Duplicate Google Fonts `@import`**
`Source+Sans+Pro` is imported twice: once in the `<head>` `<style>` block (weight 400 only) and again in a second `<style>` block inline (weights 400 and 700). The first import is redundant and adds an extra render-blocking network request in clients that support web fonts.
