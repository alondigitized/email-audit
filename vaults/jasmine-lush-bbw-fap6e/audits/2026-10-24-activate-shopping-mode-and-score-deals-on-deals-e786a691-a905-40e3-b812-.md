# activate shopping mode and score deals on deals. 🤑
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-24**
## Executive summary

- This is the same $5.95 mists-and-body-spray hero from the 10/22 "don't sleep on these gift-worthy deals" and 10/23 "ready, set, gifts for everyone" sends. The hero is clean. "Save on perfect gifts" sits over a big **$5.95**, with a dark "Limited time" bar and an outlined "Shop now" button, and the eye goes to the price first. The Ocean and Snowflakes & Cashmere bottles against a pink, sparkly backdrop look festive and on-season.
- Nothing here is new, though. This is the sixth send in about five days, and the subject "activate shopping mode and score deals on deals" is vague and repeats the word "deals." The urgency bar says "Limited time" with no deadline in the creative. The real end date (Oct 26, 5:59 AM ET) is only in the fine print, and earlier sends gave stronger cues like "Ends today" and "Final hours." Below the hero, the "You might like" row shows different products from the hero (hand soap, mist, wallflower) with no prices. The long block of legal copy makes the email feel heavy.
- Overall it's a competent, repetitive send. It's fine as one of many, but a subscriber would likely start skipping it.

## What's working

- The **$5.95** price is the clear focal point of the hero.
- A single offer and a single "Shop now" button keep the message simple.
- The product photography is festive and fits the gifting season.
- The product tiles have a clean layout with scent names, and the Snowflakes & Cashmere tile echoes the hero bottle.
- The header nav (New arrivals / Top offers / Gifts) and the free-shipping-over-$50 bar add a second reason to buy without competing with the hero.

## What's weak

- The subject is vague and repeats "deals." It names no product, price or deadline, and "activate shopping mode" is filler.
- The "Limited time" bar gives no date or hour, so the urgency isn't credible.
- It's the sixth send in five days with the same $5.95 mists offer, so fatigue is likely.
- The "You might like" tiles have no prices and no link back to the $5.95 deal, so it's unclear which items qualify.
- The "Shop now" button is a thin outline that sits low in the hero and is easy to miss. The long fine-print block also makes the offer feel hedged.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the deadline in the hero, for example "Ends Sun 10/25," and show the date or a countdown instead of "Limited time."
- 2. Rewrite the subject to name the product and price, and add a reason to open that differs from the last five sends.
- 3. Fill the "You might like" tiles with $5.95 qualifying items and show prices, so each tile supports the offer.
- 4. Make the CTA a solid filled button with specific copy such as "Shop $5.95 mists."
- 5. Cut the fine print down to a short summary with a link.
- 6. Space out sends of this offer, or vary the creative (a different scent or a gift-set angle).

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **Tracking pixel:** `https://bbwus.everestengagement.com/ea/fw0q51vctP/?e=200511831233&c=102426_EM_FA_BAU_PROMO_0_NRDM_BIGDAY_AM_BAL-NTF-OPTD` is flagged as an image with no alt text. It is a third-party (Everest) open/engagement pixel. The `e=` value looks like a per-recipient ID.

- No broken or malformed links are confirmed in the visible source.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text (5 images and the pixel):**
  - `4851c40c-9928-4c38-88b8-02583dd793aa.png`
  - `d6327aef-bba4-4c90-8be7-77b3d8306641.png`
  - `00a9ac12-8940-4f37-9570-9b23c6fb7b9c.png`
  - `7b7c94ef-aaa3-42f7-8531-38bf7f901ae6.png`
  - `bbebdf75-1fc0-48a3-96d4-ad8652de93f7.png`
  - The check doesn't distinguish an absent `alt` attribute from an empty one. If absent, screen readers may read the UUID filenames, and the images give no fallback when blocked. The pixel should carry `alt=""`.
- **Invalid document structure:** In the visible source, a `<table class="stylingblock-content-wrapper">` and the MSO conditional blocks appear after the `<meta>` tags, before any visible `</head>` or `<body>`. If that holds in the full source, block content sits inside `<head>`. Clients differ in how they recover from that.
- **Font import:** `@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Helvetica+Neue&display=swap')` likely points to a family that Google Fonts doesn't host. Gmail and Outlook ignore `@import` in any case. The stack falls back to `Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif`, so the import only adds a wasted request in the clients that honor it.
- **Duplicate Outlook settings:** `<o:OfficeDocumentSettings>` appears in both `<!--[if mso]>` and `<!--[if gte mso 9]>`. That is redundant, and both blocks apply in Outlook 2007+.
- **Duplicate charset declarations:** Both `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=UTF-8"/>` and `<meta charset="utf-8">` are present. They are consistent, so this is minor.
- **Generic title:** `<title>Bath &amp; Body Works </title>` has a trailing space and doesn't match the subject.
- **Dark mode:** Support is present (`color-scheme`, `prefers-color-scheme`, `[data-ogsc]`/`[data-ogsb]`). It uses `!important` on `.darkmode-dark` and `.darkmode-light` display toggles. Those toggles weren't verifiable in the truncated source.
