# don't forget to shop the lowest price of the season* - $2.95. 😉
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-18**
## Executive summary

- This is the fourth "$2.95 Wallflowers, lowest price of the season" send in the recent Bath & Body Works run (09-17 hero, 09-17 spoiler-alert teaser, 09-17 scattered $1.95/$2.95 mashup), and it's a reversion to the clean version that scored well before — honey-toned hero, big "$2.95" price, "Lowest price of the season," "up to 24 per customer," a clear "Shop now" button. But it doesn't stop there: the email immediately stacks a second offer ($6.95 select full-size body care with code STARS), then a third (Limited-batch 3-Wick candles at $14.95), then a "we think you'll love" product grid, a brand-values module, and a "sign up for texts" pitch — six distinct asks in one scroll.
- The subject line — "don't forget to shop" — is a reminder/urgency framing for an offer that's now been the subject of at least three prior emails this week, which risks feeling like fatigue rather than urgency to a subscriber who's already seen this exact price point twice.
- The hero itself is well-executed in isolation, but the cumulative effect of the email is the same problem flagged on 09-17: too many offers, diluted hierarchy, and now added cadence fatigue since this is a repeat of an already-audited hero.

## What's working

- Hero band is clean: "$2.95," "Wallflowers Fragrance Refills," "Lowest price of the season," "Up to 24 per customer," and a visible "Shop now" button all stack in one glance.
- Honey/amber product photography ties visually to the "Whipped Honey & Vanilla" scent being promoted.
- "Limited time" and "Today only" banners give each section a clear timing cue.
- No render bugs — images, barcode, and text all display cleanly.
- Secondary sections (3-Wick, body care, "we think you'll love") are visually distinct with their own clear CTAs.

## What's weak

- Three competing offers ($2.95 Wallflowers, $6.95 body care, $14.95 candles) in one email dilutes which one the subject line is even about.
- Subject says "don't forget," but this is at minimum the second or third email this week leading with this identical $2.95 price — reads as nagging rather than reminding.
- In-store barcode/"use code STARS" section is a jarring visual break (plain white background, small barcode) sandwiched between two glossy product heroes.
- No urgency deadline visible anywhere in the rendered body (no "ends [date]") despite "lowest price of the season" language implying scarcity.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email to one offer — lead with $2.95 Wallflowers only, move body care and candles to a follow-up send; this is the single highest-leverage fix given the repeated "too many offers" note across this whole email flight.
- 2. Add an explicit end date/countdown near the $2.95 price so "lowest price of the season" carries real urgency instead of vague scarcity.
- 3. Since this is a repeat hero from earlier sends, differentiate the subject/preheader instead of reusing "lowest price" language a third time — signal it's closing soon, not just reminding.
- 4. Move the in-store barcode module to the very end (with the other "more to love" utility content) so it doesn't interrupt the two premium product heroes.
- **Subject Alt A:** `last call: $2.95 Wallflowers refills, up to 24`
- **Subject Alt B:** `still $2.95 — Wallflowers refills won't stay this low`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stock your favorite scents before this price is gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `24 refills, one low price — today's the day to grab them.`
