# 🍎 $11.95 for NEW fall favorites. it's too good to miss.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-11**
## Executive summary

- This is basically a rerun of the "Fall Candle Event" hero from the 2026-09-10 send that scored 9/10 for its clean live-fire launch — same $11.95 3-wick candle, same "Lowest price of the season" copy, same product shot. The subject line tries to reframe it as "NEW fall favorites," but the hero image and offer are identical to yesterday's, so anyone who opened that email will feel a flicker of "didn't I already see this?"
- Below the fold the email actually does a lot of work: a Buy 3 Get 3 Free body-care offer, a Rewards "Member Days" teaser starting 9/14, and a "We think you'll love" product row. That's a genuinely dense, well-organized email — the problem is the top third is recycled creative wearing a new subject line rather than a fresh "new arrivals" story the subject promises.
- Verdict: solid execution, weak differentiation. It'll perform fine because the $11.95 candle offer is real and proven, but it's cannibalizing yesterday's stronger, cleaner send instead of adding something new.

## What's working

- Clear, unmissable hero: "$11.95 All 3-Wick Candles" with a high-quality lit-candle photo and a single "Shop now" CTA — no ambiguity about the deal.
- Strong secondary offer (Buy 3 Get 3 Free body care) gives a second concrete reason to click if candles aren't the reader's thing.
- Member Days teaser (starts 9/14) is well-placed and creates forward-looking anticipation without derailing the main offer.
- "We think you'll love" product grid gives three specific, shoppable SKUs with individual Shop buttons — good for browsers who skip the hero.
- Free shipping banner at $50 is visible immediately below the logo, reinforcing value before the reader even reaches the offer.

## What's weak

- The hero is a near-exact repeat of yesterday's "Fall Candle Event" creative — same product, same price, same "Lowest price of the season" line — while the subject claims "NEW fall favorites," which is misleading.
- "Up to 24 per customer" sits awkwardly under the candle photo with no visual treatment — reads like a legal afterthought rather than intentional copy.
- No countdown or explicit end date on the $11.95 offer despite "Limited time" label — after two straight days of this exact deal, "limited time" is losing credibility.
- Visual pacing is heavy: four distinct promo blocks (candle, ingredients/BOGO, Rewards, product grid) stacked with no breathing room, so the eye doesn't clearly land on one dominant offer after the fold.

## Recommendations

- 1. Swap the hero for an actual new-arrival product (the subject promises it) — if the candle deal must stay, demote it to the second block and lead with something the reader hasn't seen in the last 24 hours.
- 2. Add a real deadline to the $11.95 offer ("ends 9/13" or similar) — two consecutive sends with no expiration erodes urgency.
- 3. Tighten the body of the email — merge the "ingredients for slow living" block and the BOGO offer into one section; right now they compete for attention.
- 4. Move "Up to 24 per customer" into smaller, clearly fine-print styling so it doesn't interrupt the hero's visual flow.
