# 🙋‍♂️ heads up guys, final hours for $5.95 picks.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- This is the most deliberately male-targeted B&BW email in this entire audit run. "🙋‍♂️ heads up guys" isn't just a subject line — it's a genuine segmentation signal, and the Football Season Men's Body Care hero at $5.95 delivers on that promise. For a male subscriber who's been watching B&BW's deal cadence this week, this email has real pull: right price, right hook, right timing.
- The problem is that B&BW can't resist stuffing a second (and third) promo under the hero. The $12.95 3-Wick Candle section — which already had a dedicated retention send earlier today ("🌟 $12.95 3-wicks that burn for up to 45 hours?") — reappears here like an uninvited guest. This is the fourth B&BW touchpoint for the candle deal in two days, and it lands in what was supposed to be a men's email. The Summer Sale 75% off block below it piles on further. By the time a reader scrolls past the hero, the "heads up guys" throughline has completely evaporated.
- If B&BW had committed to the bit — a clean, masculine email around the $5.95 Football Season picks — this could have been the best-executed send of the week. Instead it's a good idea diluted into a grab-bag.

## What's working

- The subject line is the strongest B&BW subject in this audit run: concrete price, explicit demographic target, credible urgency, and the right tone in under 50 characters.
- "Football Season" branding gives men's body care a cultural anchor that transcends generic fragrance copy — it's timely (preseason is approaching) and works visually in the hero.
- "Use code: HANDSOME" is smart: brand-voice-consistent, memorable, and likely to generate a smile at checkout — the kind of detail that builds goodwill.
- The dual-price hero ($5.95 body care anchored against $35 cologne) gives male subscribers a clear price ladder without crowding the layout.
- "FINAL HOURS" + "Limit 30" create genuine scarcity — the limit number especially makes the urgency feel earned rather than manufactured.

## What's weak

- The $12.95 3-Wick Candle section has no business being in a men's email — it was already its own send earlier today, and its presence here signals that this email was assembled from template blocks rather than designed around a concept.
- Three competing promo blocks (men's, candles, summer sale 75% off) destroy the visual hierarchy the subject line sets up — the eye doesn't know where to go after the hero.
- Every CTA reads "SHOP" — a complete failure to carry the "guys" framing through to the action moment.
- The Summer Sale block is the worst offender: generic clearance language with no connection to the men's focus; it reads like a footer that migrated into the body.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Strip the candle and summer sale sections entirely.** The men's angle is genuinely differentiated — don't suffocate it with two recycled promos that belong in other sends. One hero, one CTA, one email.
- 2. **Rewrite every "SHOP" button.** "Get $5.95 Picks" or "Shop Football Season" keeps the masculine framing alive through to the click. Generic verbs cost nothing to fix and are one of the highest-leverage edits on any email.
- 3. **Make "HANDSOME" visible at hero-level.** The code is there but visually buried under the product copy. Treat it as a badge or bold callout — it's a fun, brand-relevant moment that deserves top-of-email visibility.
- 4. **Replace "FINAL HOURS" with a hard timestamp.** "Ends tonight at 11:59 PM ET" gives the urgency a credible anchor. "Final hours" is vague; a real deadline is not.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$5.95 Football Season picks — tonight only. 🏈`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your $5.95 men's picks are almost gone, guys.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Full-size Football Season scents at $5.95. Code HANDSOME — offer ends at midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Grab the $5.95 deal before it's gone. Code HANDSOME at checkout.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Table-based HTML 4.01 Transitional layout sent via Salesforce Marketing Cloud (e2.bathandbodyworks.com). Two structural errors are present — a table rendered outside `<body>` and images with empty `src` attributes — alongside unresolved header warnings that require verification against the upstream sending record.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**25 click links unprobed.** All CTAs route through `click.e2.bathandbodyworks.com` (SFMC click-redirect). QA skipped HTTP probing for these domains, so destination URL health and UTM parameter presence are unverified.

**Third-party engagement pixel exposed.** An `<img>` tag pointing to `https://bbwus.everestengagement.com/ea/fw0q51vctP/?e=200511831233&c=072426_EM_SU_BAU_PROMO_0_RDM_595MENS35CEE_FH_BAL_G6M` is present. The `c=` parameter encodes the internal campaign code (`072426_EM_SU_BAU_PROMO_0_RDM_595MENS35CEE_FH_BAL_G6M`) in plaintext, exposing naming conventions in rendered source. This is a Validity/Everest deliverability tracker; it also lacks `alt` text (confirmed by QA).

**Open pixel alt text is empty.** `https://click.e2.bathandbodyworks.com/open.aspx?RYNLFIVUQSXEVPDE457DT2FSNI.70251...` uses `alt=""` — acceptable for a 1×1 tracking pixel, no action needed.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[Critical] Table rendered outside `<body>`.**
The HTML structure is:
```
</head>
<!-- comment -->
<table ...>  ← preheader wrapper, outside <body>
  <td>...</td>
</table>
<body style="...">  ← body opens here
```
The first `<table>` (containing the preheader `<div>`) and the open-tracking pixel table are both between `</head>` and `<body>`. Most modern email clients will attempt to repair this via implicit body insertion, but behavior is undefined and Gmail in particular may strip or mishandle content placed in this position.

**[High] Two images with empty `src` attributes.**
QA flagged:
```
[WARN] Image missing alt text: : src:
[WARN] Image missing alt text: : src:
```
Both `src` and `alt` are empty strings. Empty `src` causes some clients (notably older iOS Mail) to issue a GET to the email's base URL or render a broken image indicator. These need to be either populated or removed.

**[Medium] Content image missing alt text.**
`https://image.e2.bathandbodyworks.com/lib/fe9213737561047a72/m/1/418d0db7-5a65-43cb-b5c9-9c6c142e4fd2.png` has no `alt` attribute. If images are blocked, this element renders silently with no fallback text — likely a hero or product image where alt text is load-bearing for conversion.

**[Medium] Duplicate Google Fonts import.**
`@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Source+Sans+Pro&display=swap')` appears twice: once in the `<head>` `<style>` block (correct), and again inside a `<style>` tag injected within a table cell in the body. The in-body `<style>` tag has inconsistent support across clients (Outlook strips it; Gmail supports it but may de-duplicate unpredictably). The second import is redundant and should be removed.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**[Medium] Unexpanded build artifact present twice.**
```html
<!-- `date "+ET%m.%y"` -->
```
This string appears at two points in the HTML. It is a shell command that was never substituted — either a templating pipeline failure or a stale comment from a build process. It is inert in the rendered email but visible in source and indicates the template pre-processing did not complete cleanly.

**[Low] AMPscript debug comments left in production output.**
Three internal logic comments are visible in delivered HTML:
```html
<!--Adds daily APT set via ampscript. Adds preset APT for emails flagged NTF-->
<!--Set APT for NTF campaigns, otherwise respect email-defined APT-->
<!--If @NTF_Flag is "N", @APT should be set in the email; no default fallback here-->
```
These expose variable names (`@NTF_Flag`, `@APT`) and internal logic. Strip before send.

**[Low] Commented-out slot definition visible.**
```html
<!-- <div data-type="slot" data-key="gvhu7dvhmx9" data-label="Preheader"></div> -->
```
Template scaffolding artifact. No functional impact but pollutes source.

**No unrendered merge tokens detected** in the visible HTML. No `%%variable%%` or `{{token}}` patterns found unfilled.

---

### 5. Compliance

**[Warn] List-Unsubscribe header unconfirmed.**
QA could not detect `List-Unsubscribe` or `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) headers. The QA system notes these may not be captured through the AgentMail relay — this is likely a relay artifact, not a true absence — but must be verified against the raw SMTP headers from the actual delivered message. Gmail and Apple Mail use `List-Unsubscribe-Post` for one-click unsubscribe compliance; absence would affect inbox placement under Google's bulk sender requirements (enforced since February 2024 for >5k/day senders).

**[Warn] SPF/DKIM authentication unconfirmed.**
`Authentication-Results` header not captured by relay. Verify against raw headers that SFMC's DKIM signing on `e2.bathandbodyworks.com` is passing. A failed DKIM check at this send volume is a significant deliverability risk.

**Unsubscribe link in body**: Not visible in the truncated HTML provided; assumed present in footer (standard SFMC template behavior). Confirm it resolves and is not behind a redirect that itself is broken.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM parameters unverifiable.** All 25 CTAs were skipped by QA because they route through `click.e2.bathandbodyworks.com`. There is no way to confirm that destination URLs carry UTM parameters without either (a) following the redirects manually or (b) probing via a redirect-follower that exempts click-tracking domains. This is a gap in QA coverage, not a confirmed problem — but it should be closed.

**Campaign code in Everest pixel** (`c=072426_EM_SU_BAU_PROMO_0_RDM_595MENS35CEE_FH_BAL_G6M`) suggests the send date is `072426` (July 24, 2026) and segment is `MENS35CEE`. If UTMs on site links use a different campaign date or identifier, attribution will be fragmented in analytics.
