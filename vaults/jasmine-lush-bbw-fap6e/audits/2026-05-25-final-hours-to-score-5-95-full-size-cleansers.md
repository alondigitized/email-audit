# final hours to score $5.95 full-size cleansers. 🛁
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- This is Bath & Body Works' third email for the $5.95 Full Size Cleansers deal — a closing bell following the May 24 opener and this morning's "ends today" send. The formula mirrors what B&BW executed well on May 22 ("last chance $2.95 minis," 8/10) and May 23 ("final hours $3.50 soaps," 8/10): a "FINAL HOURS!" hero banner, the price badge, a secondary BOGO offer, and a product grid below the fold. Executional craft is solid — clean layout, dual urgency banners, no render bugs.
- The structural problem is deal fatigue. This is the sixth B&BW email in 48 hours, third on this specific deal. The hero creative is the same blue-gradient lifestyle shot that ran this morning, so the re-open looks like a double-send error, not a deliberate final push. Subscribers who are engaged already decided; subscribers who are disengaged won't be woken up by the same image twice. Compared to the May 23 hand soap send — which had a promo code front and center, tight subject-to-hero copy match, and a single CTA — this one diffuses with "TOP OFFERS" and "MORE TO LOVE" sections that compete with the primary deal.
- Judgment: a competent closing bell undermined by cadence abuse and identical creative. It'll convert fence-sitters who opened and forgot; it won't recover anyone who tuned out.

## What's working

- Subject line earns its keep — price, category, and "final hours" urgency packed under 50 characters, with the bathtub emoji adding just enough personality.
- Dual "FINAL HOURS!" banners on both the cleansers hero and the BOGO candle section leave no ambiguity about the time constraint.
- BOGO candle secondary offer is a legitimate cross-sell, not a filler module.
- No render bugs — images load, text doesn't overlap, layout holds.
- "TOP OFFERS" product grid below fold gives browsers a reason to scroll even if the hero deal doesn't land.

## What's weak

- Same blue-gradient creative as this morning's send — at a glance it reads as a duplicate, not a final call.
- No promo code visible in the render; the May 23 soap send had code B3050 printed in the hero, which made the discount actionable and saveable.
- "SHOP" CTAs are fully generic — identical copy on different offers, different categories, no specificity.
- "TOP OFFERS" and "MORE TO LOVE" sections dilute the single-deal urgency this email needs to close with.
- Three emails on one deal in under 24 hours trains subscribers that "final hours" doesn't actually mean final.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse to one closing email per deal.** The morning "ends today" and this afternoon's "final hours" cancel each other's urgency — pick one, not both.
- 2. **Print a promo code in the hero.** Replicating the May 23 soap playbook (code + "use it before midnight") gives subscribers a concrete action and a reason to save the email.
- 3. **Differentiate CTAs by offer.** Change the cleansers button to "Shop $5.95 Cleansers" and the candle button to "Shop BOGO Candles" — specificity drops hesitation.
- 4. **Strip "TOP OFFERS" and "MORE TO LOVE" from closing-bell sends.** One deal, one exit. These sections belong in browse emails, not deal finales.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Images with empty `src` attributes (2 instances — QA flagged):**
```
src: [empty]
```
Empty `src` causes some clients to issue a GET request to the email's base URL or render a broken image placeholder. These must be populated or the `<img>` tags removed.

**Images missing `alt` text (4 total — QA flagged):**
- `a75a2ad2250a2430.png` (`mi.bathandbodyworks.com/p/rp/...`) — likely promotional hero image; no alt.
- `418d0db7-5a65-43cb-b5c9-9c6c142e4fd2.png` (`image.e2.bathandbodyworks.com/...`) — no alt.
- Two images with empty `src` also have no alt.
- Everest Engagement pixel — no alt (acceptable for tracking pixels if `alt=""` is explicitly set; here it is absent).

**Google Fonts `@import`:**
```css
@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Source+Sans+Pro&display=swap');
```
Stripped by Gmail and Outlook. Falls back to `Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif` declared inline — no visual breakage, but the import is wasted overhead.

**`role="presentation"` inconsistency:** Applied to some content-wrapper tables but absent on several structural tables (the centering wrappers). Not a breaking issue but inconsistent with the accessibility pattern already in use.

**`lang="en"` on `<html>`:** Present — correct.

**Mobile breakpoint:** `@media (max-width:599px)` is correctly set just below the 600px fixed-width container — no breakpoint gap issue.
