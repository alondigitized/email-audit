# sensitive skin? treat yourself with buy 3 get 3 FREE. 🥰
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-28**
## Executive summary

- This send buries its own headline offer. The subject promises "buy 3 get 3 FREE" but the hero — a full-screen shower/bath lifestyle shot with "Gentle care for skin is here" — never mentions the deal at all; it's pure product-launch copy for the new Essentials collection. A shopper skimming on mobile has to scroll past the entire hero before finding anything transactional, and even then the offer only appears in the fine-print footer, not in a real banner or button.
- The middle of the email (fragrance plug-in, cross-sell tiles, "feel good formula" brand copy) is pleasant but generic, and it pushes the actual promo even further out of view. This reads like two emails mashed together — a new-launch announcement and a buy-3-get-3 promo — and neither wins. Bath & Body Works has been inconsistent lately: compare this to 2026-09-25's "$10 off $40... dreamers" send, which led with offer + hero in one glance and scored an 8. This one regresses toward the buried-offer pattern from 2026-09-27's Flash Sale email (5/10).

## What's working

- Clean, on-brand hero photography (warm bathroom lighting, new Essentials packaging clearly shown).
- Clear visual hierarchy within each module — headlines, subcopy, and product shots are well-spaced with no overlapping or broken elements.
- "New!" badge is a legible, simple way to flag the product launch.
- Secondary "We think you'll love" product row gives concrete, named products (Mahogany Teakwood, Eucalyptus Spearmint, Into The Night) with real images.
- Footer utility links (Rewards, texts, store locator) are tidy and functional-looking.

## What's weak

- The buy-3-get-3-free offer from the subject line is nowhere in the visible body copy — no banner, no button, no headline mentions it.
- No visible CTA button anywhere in the screenshot tied to the sale; "Learn more" and "Find a store" are the only buttons, neither transactional.
- No price, percentage, or discount graphic anywhere above the fold — the hero reads as a product launch, not a sale.
- Sensitive-skin targeting from the subject ("sensitive skin? treat yourself") isn't reinforced anywhere visually — no hypoallergenic/dermatologist callout beyond one small subhead line.
- The email is very long with a lot of dead space (large scenery/plug-in module) before reaching anything shoppable.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the buy-3-get-3-free offer in the hero itself, as a real banner/badge with a "Shop the sale" button — right now the subject's best asset is invisible in the body.
- 2. Add a sensitive-skin/hypoallergenic visual callout (icon or badge) near the hero to pay off the subject line's targeting.
- 3. Move the "We think you'll love" product tiles up, directly under the hero, so there's a shoppable CTA within the first screen.
- 4. Trim or move the fragrance plug-in "fresh change of scenery" module lower — it's pretty but adds scroll before any offer appears.
- 5. Add a distinct CTA button under the hero headline (e.g., "Shop Essentials — Buy 3, Get 3 Free") instead of relying on users to reach the footer fine print.
