# today's the last hurrah for $10 single wicks...
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-03**
## Executive summary

- This is the closing bell on B&BW's $10 single wick arc that launched June 1 (9/10 in this audit history). The "ENDS TODAY!" red banner and "Coastal Mood Lighting" headline are essentially the same creative as the launch email — they've swapped "STARTS TODAY!" for "ENDS TODAY!" and called it done. That's not inherently wrong; the mechanics of a closing-bell email are well understood, and B&BW executes them cleanly.
- The structural problem is creative fatigue stacked on arc fatigue. A subscriber who opened June 1 has now seen this layout in identical form, and the May 28–31 3-wick run (which closed on the same "last day" pattern) is fresh memory too. The secondary soaps section (5/$28) below the hero is a clean arc-pivot tease, but on a closing-bell send it dilutes the urgency case.
- Verdict: functional and competent, not inspired. The deal is real, the urgency signal is honest, but the execution is on autopilot.

## What's working

- "ENDS TODAY!" red banner is high-contrast and reads as urgency before any copy is processed.
- "$10 All Single Wick Candles" price badge in the hero is clear and unambiguous.
- The Summer-Ready Soaps 5/$28 module is visually separated and offers a genuine second click reason.
- Lowercase subject line ("today's the last hurrah...") maintains B&BW's conversational voice, avoids spam flags.
- Clean render — no broken images, no layout breakage across the entire email.

## What's weak

- Creative is near-identical to the June 1 launch: same hero, same product shots, same price badge, same "Coastal Mood Lighting" headline. Day 3 creative recycling signals autopilot.
- Both CTAs say "SHOP" — generic copy on a day-of-deadline email misses the urgency lever that button copy can carry.
- No scent names or specific products called out in the hero — a closing-bell email is precisely where you showcase the top-sellers to drive FOMO by name.
- The full-width "TOP OFFERS" navigation grid (Fragrance, Bath Care, Candles, Wallflowers…) creates click scatter on a send that should funnel toward a single expiring deal.
- The soaps secondary offer, while useful as an arc pivot, softens the urgency close; it should be a footer whisper, not a co-feature.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Call out 2–3 specific scent names in the hero.** "Blue Waves, Saltwater Taffy, Ocean Air — all $10, ENDS TONIGHT." Urgency without specificity is just pressure; specificity creates real FOMO.
- 2. **Rewrite the CTA buttons.** "Shop $10 Candles" and "Shop 5/$28 Soaps" over generic "SHOP" — six characters of work for a meaningful lift.
- 3. **Demote the soaps section to a footer tease.** Let the expiring deal own the email; preview the June 4 arc in two lines at the bottom, not a full-bleed module.
- 4. **Strip the navigation grid.** "TOP OFFERS" category tiles belong in a browse email, not a deadline close. Remove or push below the legal copy.
- 5. **Punch up the subject line.** "Last hurrah" has voice but reads soft against a hard deadline.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$10 single wicks end tonight—shop now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `last day for $10 candles. don't miss it.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Coastal Mood Lighting is on—until midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop $10 single wicks before this deal disappears.`

The two images with empty `src` attributes are the most critical — they will generate HTTP requests to the page's own URL in some clients, and may cause rendering errors.

**`<meta charset>` placement** — the `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8">` appears *after* `</head>` and inside the body-level comment block. It should be the first child of `<head>`. Late charset declaration can cause character misinterpretation in legacy clients before the tag is parsed.

**Preheader duplication** — two separate preheader blocks exist: one in a `<table>` before `<body>`, and one inside `<body>` as a hidden `<div>`. Some clients may surface both, producing duplicate or conflicting preview text.

**Google Fonts `@import`** — `Source Sans Pro` is imported but Gmail and most corporate clients strip `<style>` blocks or block external CSS fetches. This is a known fallback gap; not a bug if Arial fallback is acceptable.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**AMPscript / dynamic template references present but unresolved in source:**
- Comments reference `@NTF_Flag` and `@APT` AMPscript variables — these are server-side and should be resolved before send. Presence in HTML comments is expected but confirms the template is SFMC-based.
- `mi_u=200511831233` in image URLs is a static test/seed value. Confirm this is replaced per-recipient at send time; a hardcoded subscriber ID would mean all open tracking maps to one record.

**`data-key="gvhu7dvhmx9"` slot** in the preheader table is empty — the dynamic slot was not populated, leaving the containing `<td>` with no content. No visible rendering impact, but the slot system is partially broken.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header — NOT detected** (QA WARN). RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail/Yahoo for bulk senders (>5k/day). Absence would cause deliverability penalties.
