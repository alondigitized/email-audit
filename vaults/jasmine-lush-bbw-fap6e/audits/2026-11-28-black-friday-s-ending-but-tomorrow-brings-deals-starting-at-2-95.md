# Black Friday’s ending, but tomorrow brings deals starting at $2.95. 🤑
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-28**
## Executive summary

- This is a "Black Friday is over, here's what's next" send. It's a decent pivot, but it doesn't deliver on the subject line. The subject promises "deals starting at $2.95." The render shows $4.95 for the hero bundle and $24.95 for the Mini Fine Fragrance Mist Sampler. I can't find $2.95 anywhere, so the hook is missing from the body.
- The hero is strong. "Big Gifting Weekend" sits next to a giant green "$4.95" and a full-size mist, body spray and body cream photo, with a "Starts tomorrow | In-store & online" strip above it. The eye lands on the price first. Below that, the Doorbuster banner is cropped in its marquee ("ORBUSTER DEAL") and a big blank white gap follows the sampler. After the three small product tiles, the email ends in a long empty void with a few stray underlines. It looks half-rendered, which hurts trust.
- Compared with the 11/27 "FINAL HOURS - buy 3 get 4 free" and "LAST DAY" sends, this one at least changes the story to a new offer, which breaks the sameness. But it's still one of several sends in two days, and the render bugs undercut it. Verdict: right idea, sloppy execution, and a subject-to-body mismatch.

## What's working

- The "$4.95" hero price is huge and unmistakable, so the offer is the first thing you see.
- The "Starts tomorrow | In-store & online" strip gives a credible, time-bounded reason to come back.
- Pivoting from the "buy 3 get 4 free" run to a new offer, the gifting weekend and doorbusters, breaks the repetition in the brand history.
- The $24.95 sampler is a concrete, giftable product with named scents (Winter Candy Apple, Vanilla Bean Noel and others).
- The Rewards and free-shipping strips at the top add member and threshold value without cluttering the hero.

## What's weak

- The subject's "$2.95" never appears in the render, which is a bait-and-switch feel.
- The Doorbuster marquee is cut off at both edges, so "ORBUSTER DEAL" reads as a rendering bug.
- A large blank white gap sits between the sampler and the product tiles.
- A long empty stretch with stray underlined fragments sits at the bottom, with missing footer content and no readable legal or unsubscribe text.
- The product tiles have tiny captions and no prices or CTAs.
- "Start your list" is a vague CTA for a sale that hasn't started yet.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the $2.95 item in the email as a visible tile, or fix the subject to match the $4.95 and $24.95 offers. This is the biggest trust fix.
- 2. Fix the render: remove the blank gaps, repair the footer so the legal and unsubscribe text show, and make the Doorbuster banner fit its container.
- 3. Replace "Start your list" with a specific CTA like "Shop tomorrow's deals" or "Set my reminder." Add prices and a "Shop" link to each product tile.
- 4. Cut the three tiny product tiles or enlarge them. They currently do nothing.
- 5. Reduce cadence. Skip the weekend send if there's no new offer.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a Salesforce Marketing Cloud-style build with all 43 links behind click-tracking redirects. The QA run found no hard failures. It found 6 images without alt text and could not verify the headers (List-Unsubscribe, List-Unsubscribe-Post, Authentication-Results) because they weren't captured. The HTML is truncated, so the footer and body links are unchecked.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified destinations:** All 43 links go through tracking or click-redirect domains, so the HTTP probe was skipped. The landing pages, redirect chains and 404s are untested.
- **Third-party tracking host:** Click and pixel traffic uses `bbwus.everestengagement.com/ea/fw0q51vctP/?e=200511831233&c=112726_EM_FA_BAU_PROMO_0_NRDM_BIGDAY_PREV_BAL_G6M_V1`. This is a different domain from the sending domain (`e2.bathandbodyworks.com`) and the image domain (`image.e2.bathandbodyworks.com`). The mismatch can hurt reputation alignment and tracking-link blocklisting.
- **Identifiers in the query string:** `e=200511831233` looks like a per-recipient ID. The `c=` value encodes the segment: `NRDM_BIGDAY_PREV_BAL_G6M` (the NRDM_/G6M parts suggest a non-redeemer, 6-month-lookback segment). Both are exposed in every forwarded or shared URL.
- **Google Fonts `@import`:** `@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Helvetica+Neue&display=swap')`. Helvetica Neue isn't a Google Fonts family, so the request likely fails, and I haven't confirmed that. Many clients strip `@import` anyway, so it adds a third-party request for no benefit. The `font-main` fallback stack still works.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text (6 images):**

  - `d6327aef-bba4-4c90-8be7-77b3d8306641.png`

  - The `everestengagement.com/ea/fw0q51vctP` image, which is probably the open-tracking pixel. For a pixel, `alt=""` is the correct fix.
- **Missing alt on the content images:** These are hosted at `image.e2.bathandbodyworks.com/lib/fe9213737561047a72/m/1/`. If they carry the offer or CTA text, the message is lost with images off or in a screen reader.
- **Markup structure:** In the visible source, the `<table class="stylingblock-content-wrapper">` follows the `<meta>` tags with no visible `</head><body>`. If that holds in the full HTML, it is invalid, though SFMC output often renders fine anyway. I can't confirm it from the truncated source.
- **Redundant markup:** There are two `<meta ... charset>` declarations (`http-equiv="Content-Type"` and `<meta charset="utf-8">`). There are also duplicate `<o:OfficeDocumentSettings>` blocks under `<!--[if mso]>` and `<!--[if gte mso 9]>`. These are harmless but redundant.
- **`<title>`:** `Bath &amp; Body Works ` has a trailing space and is generic.
- **Dark mode handling:** The email includes `prefers-color-scheme` and `[data-ogsc]` rules, with `color-scheme` and `supported-color-schemes` metas. No issues found.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved merge tokens (such as `%%...%%`, `{{...}}` or `[FIRSTNAME]`) appear in the visible source or the QA data. The truncated portion is unchecked.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058):** Both are reported absent. The QA note attributes this to the AgentMail relay, so it is unconfirmed rather than a sender defect. If the headers are really absent, Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules are not met.
- **Authentication-Results:** Not present, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment are unknown. The QA note again points to relay stripping.
- **Footer:** The unsubscribe link and postal address are not in the truncated HTML, so they can't be checked. The `footer-disclaimer` class suggests a footer exists.
- **Sender domain:** The From domain is `e2.bathandbodyworks.com`, a dedicated subdomain. That is good practice, but its authentication can't be verified here.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **UTM parameters:** None are visible in the exposed URLs. Attribution relies on the Everest `c=` campaign code (`..._BIGDAY_PREV_BAL_G6M_V1`) rather than standard `utm_*` params. Final landing-page URLs were not probed, so I can't tell whether UTMs are appended after the redirect.
- **Landing page alignment:** Can't be assessed, because the redirect targets were not resolved.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add `alt` text to the 5 content images, and `alt=""` to the tracking pixel.
2. Re-run QA against the raw `.eml` or a direct SMTP capture to check `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results`. Don't treat the current warnings as sender defects until then.
3. Resolve the 43 redirect chains with a follow-redirect probe (HEAD or GET) to confirm final URLs, status codes and any UTMs.
4. Remove the Google Fonts `@import` for Helvetica Neue and rely on the system font stack.
5. Confirm the full HTML has `</head><body>` and a visible unsubscribe link and address in the footer.
6. Consider a first-party tracking domain such as `click.e2.bathandbodyworks.com` to match the sending domain.
7. Fix the QA tooling: "57% pass, 0 issues, 3 warnings" doesn't add up against the 11 warnings listed, so the scoring logic needs checking.
