# 75% off fall goodness - while it lasts.
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-08**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, single-offer send. The hero is a huge "Up to 75% off" in dark type over a fall-leaf photo, with a "Shop now" button. The eye lands on the number first, which is the right call. It's the same fall-sale creative as the 10/05 "rake in the savings" send, and it reads as a fresh, honest recap of the sale.
- Below the hero it loses focus. The Platinum tile is a pretty product shot, but it advertises a redesign, not a deal. It has no price, no button and no clear link. The "You might like" row shows three soaps and lotions with no prices, so none of them tie back to the 75% claim. The "Our feel good formula" and "More to love" blocks are brand filler. The brand's strongest recent sends (10/07 "final hours for the good stuff") led with concrete prices like $3.50 soaps and $12.95 candles. This one leads with a vague "up to" and never shows a single discounted product.
- Judgement: a competent reminder with a strong hero and a weak body. It's a solid mid-tier send, but it won't beat the price-tile sends from 10/06–10/07.

## What's working

- The "Up to 75% off" hero is unmissable and sits above the fold.
- The fall-leaf photo fits the season and the "Fall sale" label.
- The "Shop now" button is visible and obvious.
- The Rewards banner at the top gives member context without crowding the hero.
- The legal fine print is complete and not buried in the main body.

## What's weak

- The Platinum tile has no price, no CTA button and no tie to the sale. It dilutes the offer.
- The "You might like" products have no prices or sale badges, so the 75% claim is never shown on a real item.
- "Select items" and "Up to" hide which products qualify.
- "While it lasts" has no deadline in the visible body. The only end date, October 26, is in the fine print.
- The bottom half is generic brand content (feel good formula, rewards, texts, store pickup) that adds length without adding reasons to click.
- It's the 5th-plus sale email in four days. The creative is the same as 10/05, and the cadence is heavy.

## Recommendations

- 1. Replace the Platinum and "You might like" blocks with 3–4 priced sale tiles, in the style of the 10/07 send. For example, show "$3.50 foaming hand soaps" and "$12.95 3-wick candles" with sale-price badges.
- 2. Name the deadline in the hero, such as "Ends Oct 26", so the urgency is credible.
- 3. Make "Shop now" a filled, high-contrast button and add a second CTA mid-email.
- 4. Put the sale price on every product card, and add "Shop soaps" style category links.
- 5. Cut the filler blocks (feel good formula, More to love) down to a single rewards line.

## Technical Audit

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified links:** 38 tracking links on click-redirect domains were skipped by the HTTP probe. Their destinations and redirect health are unconfirmed.
- **Open pixel:** `https://click.e2.bathandbodyworks.com/open.aspx?OW2EVZEJDZWEBMW57IUOUCGLOE.70268&d=70268&bmt=0` is a 1x1 image with `alt=""` inside a `width="0" height="0"` table with `mso-hide:all`. It is first-party and on the sending subdomain, so this is fine.
- **Third-party tracker:** `https://bbwus.everestengagement.com/ea/fw0q51vctP/?e=200511831233&c=100826_EM_FA_BAU_PROMO_0_NRDM_75OFF_AM_BAL_G6M_V1` is flagged as an image. The `e=` value looks like a per-recipient identifier. It is a separate tracking domain from the first-party `e2.` subdomain, so it is more likely to be blocked and it adds a privacy surface.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Invalid document structure:** `<table class="stylingblock-content-wrapper">` elements, with `<style>` and Outlook conditional blocks inside them, sit between `<meta>` tags and `</head>`. More wrapper tables sit between `</head>` and `<body>` (the `<!-- Begin variables -->` and `<!-- Begin Preheader and Header -->` sections). Tables aren't allowed in `<head>`, so parsers close the head early and move that content into the body. The `<style>` rules and the Outlook `<xml>` conditionals therefore land in unpredictable positions.
- **Font import:** `@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Helvetica+Neue&display=swap')` requests a family that, as far as I know, Google Fonts doesn't serve, so it will probably return an error. Gmail and Outlook also strip `@import`, so it can't help in those clients.
- **Second import:** `@import ...Source+Sans+Pro...` is also in a `<style>` block, and the `font-main` class uses `"Helvetica Neue"`. The imported Source Sans Pro isn't referenced in the visible CSS.
- **Fixed width:** the layout tables use `style="width: 600px;"`. The responsive rules target `.email-container`, `.mobile-padding` and `.logo-image`. None of the visible tables carry those classes, so the mobile rules may not apply, and the email could render at a fixed 600px on small screens. I can't confirm this for the truncated remainder.
- **Missing alt text (5 images):**
  - `.../m/1/4851c40c-9928-4c38-88b8-02583dd793aa.png`
  - `.../m/1/d6327aef-bba4-4c90-8be7-77b3d8306641.png`
  - `.../m/1/00a9ac12-8940-4f37-9570-9b23c6fb7b9c.png`

  All are on `image.e2.bathandbodyworks.com/lib/fe9213737561047a72/`. If these images carry offer content, images-off clients and screen readers get nothing.
- **Document metadata:** the `<title>` is `Bath & Body Works ` (generic, with a trailing space). Both `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type">` and `<meta charset>` are declared, which is redundant but harmless. The HTML 4.01 doctype is mixed with `role="presentation"` and modern meta tags, which is inconsistent but renders.
- **Dark mode:** `color-scheme` meta tags are present. The dark-mode overrides in the visible source only force white text on iOS, via `@supports (-webkit-touch-callout: none)` and `(-webkit-hyphens: none)`. I can't judge their coverage from the truncated source.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to the five flagged `image.e2.bathandbodyworks.com` images, and a decorative `alt=""` where appropriate. Check the Everest image too.
2. Move the `<style>` and `<xml>` blocks into a single valid `<head>`, and keep all tables inside `<body>`.
3. Remove the invalid `Helvetica+Neue` Google Fonts `@import`, or replace it with a real web font and a `<link>`. Keep the system font stack.
4. Add the responsive classes (`email-container` and others) to the 600px tables, or use `max-width:600px; width:100%` so the mobile rules have an effect.
5. Verify `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and SPF/DKIM/DMARC from the original headers. Capture them in a direct inbox, not through the relay.
6. Resolve the 38 tracking redirects with a headless follow, so the final URLs and UTMs can be audited.
7. Review whether the third-party `everestengagement.com` pixel is needed, since it carries a recipient identifier.
8. Set a meaningful `<title>`, for example one matching the subject line.
