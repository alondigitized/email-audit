# final hours: ⏳ give $3.50 room sprays a spritz. ✨
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-14**
## Executive summary

- This is a competent "final hours" send for $3.50 room sprays, and it is close to a copy of the 2026-11-14 "$3.50 room sprays today only. 🎉" email. The hero is the same: "Instantly make every room merry," a big **$3.50**, and a "Shop now" button, now under a "Final hours" bar. A subscriber who got this morning's version gets the same creative again with a new banner.
- The body is where it loses focus. Below the hero there are three more offers: $5.95 body care, a $9.95 moisturizing body wash, and a $40 Festive Reset Gift Set. All of them have equal weight and their own "Shop now" button. The $5.95 body care Doorbuster was already pushed three times from 2026-11-12 to 11-13. A reader who came for room sprays has plenty of reasons to wander, and no single reason to commit.
- The offer is concrete and the urgency is credible. The email is cluttered, repetitive, and pairs a $3.50 price with a promo code and "up to 24 per customer" fine print. It's a solid send for the category, but not a sharp one.

## What's working

- The hero price is huge: **$3.50** sits next to "All Concentrated Room Sprays." It's the first thing the eye lands on.
- The "Final hours" bar above the hero adds time pressure without shouting.
- The in-store barcode and the "JUMPFORJOY" online code give both channels a clear way to redeem.
- The product shots (red candle-style sprays against a pink backdrop) feel seasonal and festive.
- Every module has its own visible "Shop now" button, so the next step is always obvious.

## What's weak

- This repeats this morning's "$3.50 room sprays today only" send almost exactly. It is the second near-identical room spray email in one day.
- The three secondary modules ($5.95, $9.95, $40) compete with the hero and dilute the single room spray message.
- The "Up to 24 per customer" and "with code* JUMPFORJOY" lines under the hero are small, and a wall of dense legal text fills the footer. The offer feels a bit buried.
- The hero CTA is a thin outlined "Shop now" button on a busy image, so it is weaker than the lower buttons.
- Every CTA says "Shop now." None names a product or an outcome.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the body down to the hero plus one secondary offer. Drop the $9.95 wash or the $40 set, since both are separate promos that thin out the urgency.
- 2. Make the hero button solid and high-contrast, and put the code on the button or right next to it ("Shop $3.50 sprays — code JUMPFORJOY").
- 3. Don't resend identical creative twice in a day. If you do resend, change the hero, for example a countdown timer or a "last chance" crop of the top scents.
- 4. Give the buttons specific copy: "Grab $3.50 sprays," "Shop body care $5.95."
- 5. Add social proof near the hero, such as star ratings or "best-selling scent" tags.

- **Subject Alt B:** `$3.50 room sprays, gone at midnight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Use code JUMPFORJOY. Up to 24 per customer, in stores and online.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Fresh up every room for less before the deal ends.`
