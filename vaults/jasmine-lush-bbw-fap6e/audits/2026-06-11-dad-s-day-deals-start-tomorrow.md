# dad's day deals start tomorrow...
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-11**
## Executive summary

- This is Bath & Body Works' day-before teaser for the Father's Day event — sent June 11, event kicking off June 12, holiday landing June 15. It's the logical next step after the strong June 10 MVP send (9/10 in this audit), pivoting from gift inspiration to the transactional preview. The price anchors are real and competitive — $5.50 full-size men's body care and $35 colognes with a "LOWEST PRICE OF THE SEASON" badge — and the hero image (a bourbon-style body wash with a dramatic liquid pour) nails the masculine premium aesthetic. Execution is clean.
- The strategic problem is embedded in the premise: "deals start tomorrow" is a self-defeating send. It tells subscribers there is no reason to act today. The CTA "START YOUR LIST" compounds this by directing toward passive list-building behavior rather than purchase intent. For a subscriber who just saw the inspirational June 10 send, this email functions as a speed bump between desire and transaction.
- Clean email, wrong job. It should either drive preview clicks that build real purchase intent or it shouldn't exist as a separate send.

## What's working

- **Price anchors are specific and prominent**: $5.50 full-size men's body care and $35 colognes are visually prominent, backed by a "LOWEST PRICE OF THE SEASON" badge — real signals that this is a genuine deal.
- **Hero image earns its space**: The bourbon-themed body wash with a dark, dramatic pour shot reads masculine premium — exactly right for Father's Day gifting.
- **Shipping deadline in the banner**: "Order by June 12 at 11:59 p.m. ET for standard shipping" is a useful, credible logistics anchor that creates a soft real deadline.
- **Category grid (TOP OFFERS / TODAY'S DEALS)**: Gives gift-buyers a clear browse path — fragrance, bath care, candles, wall flowers — useful for someone still deciding what to buy for dad.

## What's weak

- **"Start tomorrow" kills same-day urgency**: Announcing that deals don't exist yet hands subscribers permission to close the email and return later — most won't.
- **"START YOUR LIST" is a weak CTA**: List-building is a passive micro-commitment; "Shop Men's," "Preview the Event," or even "See All Deals" all drive stronger click intent.
- **No specific product SKU to trigger desire**: The June 10 send was built around product inspiration; this one has prices but no "here's the exact Bourbon body wash for $5.50" moment that closes the loop.
- **"LOWEST PRICE OF THE SEASON" claim has no deadline**: The badge implies scarcity/value but gives no reason to act before the event ends — weakens its punch.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill "start tomorrow" framing or give it a job**: Either make this a genuine preview with clickable products at the discounted price (pre-shop now, confirm at checkout tomorrow) or flip the subject to make the shipping cutoff the urgency hook instead.
- 2. **Swap "START YOUR LIST" for "Preview Dad's Day Deals"** — same list-page destination if needed, but copy that reads as action toward purchase.
- 3. **Feature one specific SKU in the hero with an in-email reason to want it now**: "Bourbon Body Wash — $5.50 starting tomorrow, shipping cutoff tonight at midnight" turns this into a specific product email, not a vague teaser.
- 4. **Move the shipping deadline into the hero area**: The June 12 11:59 p.m. cutoff is buried in the top banner — bring it below the headline where it competes with the price anchors.

- **Subject Alt B:** `$5.50 men's body care — dad's day preview`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `$5.50 full-size men's body care, $35 colognes — lowest prices of the season`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `order tonight to guarantee Father's Day delivery — event starts at midnight`

The two images with empty `src` values are the most actionable concern: empty `src` may trigger a browser/client request to the page's own URL or throw a console error in some clients, and suggests unfilled template slots.

**`<meta charset>` placement:** The `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type">` tag appears *after* the `<style>` block in `<head>`, contrary to best practice (should be first child of `<head>`). Low-severity in practice but technically incorrect per HTML spec.

**Preheader implemented twice:** A standard hidden preheader div exists in the `<body>` table *and* there is a separate `<div>` slot in the `<head>` area (`data-key="gvhu7dvhmx9"`). The head-area slot appears to be a CMS placeholder that was not removed. This creates a risk of double-preheader rendering in some clients if the slot resolves.

These are developer comments that were not stripped before send. They expose internal logic about `@NTF_Flag` and `@APT` variables. No rendered merge tokens are unresolved in the visible truncated source, but the empty-`src` images suggest at least two template variables did not resolve.

Appears twice. This is a shell command that was intended to be evaluated (likely for template versioning) but was committed as a raw literal comment. Not a functional failure, but indicates a broken pre-send scripting step.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not detected** — flagged by QA relay. Without this header, one-click unsubscribe (Gmail/Yahoo requirement for bulk senders since 2024) cannot function. If the header is present at the MTA level and simply not captured by the AgentMail relay, this is a relay instrumentation gap. If genuinely absent, this is a deliverability and compliance risk.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not detected** — required for RFC 8058 one-click compliance. Same caveat as above.

**`Authentication-Results` header not found** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status unknown from this capture. The sending domain `e2.bathandbodyworks.com` is a dedicated ESP subdomain likely with valid alignment, but this cannot be confirmed from available data.

**CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe link:** Not visible in the truncated HTML. Assumed present in the footer (not captured in the excerpt). Flag for manual verification.

All click-through links route through `click.e2.bathandbodyworks.com` — UTM parameter presence on final destination URLs cannot be confirmed without resolving the redirect chains (skipped by QA probe).
