# something to feel good about starts tomorrow. 🤗
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-04**
## Executive summary

- This is a legitimate departure from the Buy 3 Get 3 Free / apple-orchard treadmill this brand has been stuck on since August 30 — a genuinely new "Feel-Good Fall Event" hero with a Santal Sage body wash bottle shot, a specific $5.95 price point, and a "TOMORROW ONLY!" hand cream stack underneath at $1.95. The bones are clean: real product photography, water droplets, actual price callouts, a barcode-and-code combo for the hand cream deal, and a "300+ items to choose from" reassurance line.
- The problem is the framing, not the offer. "Starts tomorrow" as the entire hook is a soft ask — it's telling the reader to do nothing today, which works against open/click urgency rather than for it. Compare this to 2026-09-02's $4.95 hand soap send (7.00/10), which led with an ends-today deadline and got real lift from that immediacy. Here the immediacy is inverted: two separate "wait until tomorrow" offers stacked in one email, which reads more like a preview/teaser than a call to action right now.
- Solid creative, undercut by a subject and hero that ask for patience instead of action.

## What's working

- Real, well-lit product photography (the Santal Sage bottle with water droplets, the hand cream trio with apple slices) — no stock-photo or lorem-ipsum feel.
- Concrete, specific pricing in both hero sections ($5.95 body care, $1.95 hand cream) rather than vague "% off" language.
- Barcode + promo code (MAINSQUEEZE) presented together, with clear phone/print instructions — low friction for in-store redemption.
- "Made without sulfates, parabens & dyes" callout box adds a credible product-detail signal absent from most of this brand's recent sends.
- "We think you'll love" product grid (candle, wallflowers refill, foaming soap) gives a secondary path to purchase beyond the two hero offers.

## What's weak

- Both hero offers are gated behind "tomorrow" / "TOMORROW ONLY!" — there's no action available today, which weakens the entire email's reason to click right now.
- Two competing offers (body care $5.95, hand cream $1.95) stacked top-to-bottom dilute the single clear CTA the rubric rewards — a reader has to choose which list to start.
- "Enjoy up to 24 per customer" and "Up to 24 per customer with code" repeated twice reads as legalese creeping into the hero rather than staying in the fine print.
- Subject emoji (🤗) plus vague "something to feel good about" undersells the actual news (specific $5.95 and $1.95 price points) that's sitting right in the hero.

## Recommendations

- 1. Flip the framing from "starts tomorrow" to day-of urgency once the event is live — this brand's own history shows "ends today" (09-02, 7.00/10) outperforms "starts tomorrow" framing. If this must go out a day early, lead subject/preheader with the actual date the event runs, not just "tomorrow."
- 2. Pick one hero offer, not two. Either lead with the $5.95 body care event and demote the hand cream deal to the product grid, or vice versa — stacking two "wait for it" CTAs splits attention.
- 3. Surface the $5.95 and $1.95 numbers in the subject line itself — they're the strongest, most concrete assets in the email and currently only live in the hero image.
- 4. Move "Enjoy up to 24 per customer" language out of the hero into the fine print at the bottom where the other legal disclaimers already live.
- **Subject Alt A:** `fall event: $5.95 body care starts Sept 4`
- **Subject Alt B:** `tomorrow only: $1.95 hand cream + $5.95 body care`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Santal Sage, Champagne Apple & Honey, and 300+ more — one day only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Two deals, one day: $5.95 body care and $1.95 hand cream with code MAINSQUEEZE.`
