# join Rewards, get a 3-wick + mist for $19.95. ($45 value!) ✨
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- This is the product-offer payoff email for Bath & Body Works' Member Days campaign — the 5/15 send teased the event and recruited members, today's send reveals the actual deal: a 3-wick candle plus a mist for $19.95 (claimed $45 value). That sequencing logic makes sense. The creative is clean and the hero price point lands immediately, which is what matters most.
- The problem is the stacking. By the time you count the $19.95 bundle, the 3x Points activation, the 100 Bonus Points, and the free Summer Tote with $75 purchase, you have four distinct value propositions fighting for one email. None of them get enough real estate to breathe. The hero "$19.95" reads, but everything beneath it dilutes rather than reinforces. A subscriber scanning on mobile will struggle to identify what the one action is.
- This is also the fourth BBW email in five days. The creative is better than the 5/13 app-push and more product-specific than the 5/15 Rewards-recruitment send, but the cadence is wearing thin. That burns open likelihood even when the subject line is genuinely strong.

## What's working

- **The hero price reads instantly.** "$19.95" dominates the above-fold section; the "($45 value)" parenthetical makes the saving explicit without requiring math.
- **"3 DAYS ONLY!" urgency tag is visible.** The May 16–18 window is anchored in the header badge, giving the deadline credibility rather than vagueness.
- **The subject line is the best in the recent run.** "join Rewards, get a 3-wick + mist for $19.95. ($45 value!) ✨" is specific, priced, and value-stated — a genuine step up from "join Rewards now to unlock exclusive Member Days deals 🤑" on 5/15.
- **Loyalty stacking is clear.** The 3x Points and 100 Bonus Points sections are visually distinct; a member can see multiple reasons to act.
- **No render bugs.** Layout is clean, images load, no overlapping text, no empty placeholders.

## What's weak

- **Four competing offers, one email.** The $19.95 bundle, 3x Points activation, 100 Bonus Points, and free tote with $75 each demand a different action. None gets committed support.
- **"FIND A STORE" is the hero CTA for the $19.95 deal.** If this is in-store-only, that should be front-and-center in the subject and hero copy — many subscribers will not realize they can't redeem online until they click through (or don't).
- **No product specificity.** No scent names, no collection label, no "choose from X scents" — for BBW's core candle audience, this is the first thing they want to know, and it's absent.
- **Cadence damage.** Fourth email in five days. Even a strong deal faces skepticism when the inbox looks like this, and a portion of the list will have tuned out entirely by now.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the in-store/online distinction explicitly.** If the $19.95 deal works online, say "shop online or in-store." If it's in-store only, say so in the subject and hero — that's the real reason to go to a store, and hiding it erodes trust.
- 2. **Choose one primary CTA per email.** The $19.95 bundle is the hero; everything else should be a secondary strip below, not a full competing section. The 3x Points and free tote can be footnote rows.
- 3. **Name a scent in the hero.** Even "starting at $19.95 — choose from 50+ scents" or featuring one iconic scent (Mahogany Teakwood, Vanilla Bean Noel) would make the deal tangible and improve click intent.
- 4. **Tighten the subject under 50 characters.** The current subject clocks at ~60 characters and will truncate on many mobile clients.
- 5. **Write a dedicated preheader.** The current version appears to be leaking header nav text rather than serving as a true preheader.
- **Subject Alt A:** `3-wick + mist: $19.95 for members. Ends Sunday.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `$19.95 gets you 2 products ($45 value) — 3 days only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop the deal in-store or online before May 18.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus 3x Points and a free tote with $75 — this weekend only.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Bath & Body Works Rewards Recruitment Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

HTML 4.01 Transitional table-based layout sent via Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC) on the `e2.bathandbodyworks.com` infrastructure. Three confirmed rendering/personalization issues and two missing compliance headers detected; authentication status is unverifiable through the relay.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Empty merge token in Everest engagement pixel**
The Everest engagement tracking image contains an empty `e=` parameter:
```
https://bbwus.everestengagement.com/ea/fw0q51vctP/?e=&c=051626_NRDM_XCAT_1995LOYUPICK_FS50_BALPROSOPTDOWNS_V2
```
In Everest/SFMC engagement tracking, `e=` typically carries a subscriber identifier. An empty value indicates a failed merge substitution, which will corrupt engagement attribution for this send.

**25 click-redirect links skipped**
All click links route through `click.e2.bathandbodyworks.com` (SFMC click tracking). UTM parameter pass-through to the landing page could not be verified — see section 6.

**Open pixel present, no alt text**
```
https://click.e2.bathandbodyworks.com/open.aspx?PSTWQUIWFLSELCSOAURLGKU7I4.70251&d=70251&bmt=0
```
Standard 1×1 open tracker. No alt attribute — acceptable for tracking pixels but should carry `alt=""` explicitly to suppress screen reader announcements.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Two images with empty `src` attributes**
QA flagged two `<img>` tags where both `src` and `alt` are empty strings. Empty `src` triggers a broken-image icon in most email clients (Outlook, Apple Mail, Gmail) and an unnecessary HTTP request to the current page URL in some older clients.

**Product image missing alt text**
```
src: https://image.e2.bathandbodyworks.com/lib/fe9213737561047a72/m/1/418d0db7-5a65-43cb-b5c9-9c6c142e4fd2.png
```
No `alt` attribute on what appears to be the primary product image. Subscribers with images disabled (a significant share of B2C email openers) receive no text fallback for the offer visual.

**Google Fonts import will silently fail**
```css
@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Source+Sans+Pro&display=swap');
```
Gmail, Outlook, and Samsung Mail all strip or block external CSS imports. `Source Sans Pro` will not load in the majority of email clients; the fallback stack defaults to whatever the client provides. This is a known email platform limitation — flag to the template owner if the brand requires the custom typeface.

**Shell command artifact in HTML comments**
```html
<!-- `date "+ET%m.%y"` -->
```
This appears twice. The backtick shell command was never executed — it was committed as a literal comment string. It exposes internal build tooling conventions and should be stripped from production output.

**Preheader padding characters present and correct**
The zero-width space / ghost character padding block after the visible preheader is properly hidden (`display: none; max-height: 0px; overflow: hidden`). No issue.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Suspicious literal `(Member)` in preheader**
```html
want brighter (Member) Days? ☀️ sunny steals are waiting...
```
`(Member)` with parentheses is not a standard SFMC AMPscript or Personalization String syntax (`%%First_Name%%`, `%%=v(@var)=%%`). This reads either as an unresolved placeholder that should have been substituted (e.g., a first name or tier label) or an intentional copywriting choice. If it was meant to dynamically inject a subscriber attribute, the token failed silently and every recipient sees the literal string `(Member)`.

**AMPscript business logic exposed in HTML comments**
```html
<!--Set APT for NTF campaigns, otherwise respect email-defined APT-->
<!--If @NTF_Flag is "N", @APT should be set in the email; no default fallback here-->
```
AMPscript variable logic and campaign-flag names are visible in the rendered HTML source. While not a security vulnerability, it exposes campaign segmentation logic to any recipient who views source.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent** (QA: WARN)
The header is not present as captured by the AgentMail relay. Gmail and Yahoo now require `List-Unsubscribe` for bulk senders (>5K/day); its absence risks inbox demotion or spam classification for subsequent sends.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent** (QA: WARN, RFC 8058)
One-click unsubscribe (required by Google/Yahoo bulk sender policy since February 2024) requires both `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`. Neither header is present.

**Note:** Both headers may be injected by SFMC at send time and simply not captured by the relay. Verify against a raw header dump from a delivered copy (e.g., Gmail "Show original") before treating these as confirmed failures.

**Authentication-Results absent** (QA: WARN)
SPF and DKIM pass/fail status cannot be confirmed from the relay capture. Verify via Gmail "Show original" → `Authentication-Results` header that `dkim=pass` and `spf=pass` for `e2.bathandbodyworks.com`.

**CAN-SPAM physical address**
Not visible in the truncated HTML. Confirm the footer contains a valid USPS-deliverable postal address — required for all commercial email to US recipients.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM pass-through unverifiable**
All 25 CTA links were skipped by the HTTP probe (click-redirect domain). It cannot be confirmed that UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, `utm_content`) survive the SFMC redirect chain and arrive on the `bathandbodyworks.com` landing pages. Manual spot-check of one decoded click URL is recommended.

**Campaign code present in Everest pixel**
```
c=051626_NRDM_XCAT_1995LOYUPICK_FS50_BALPROSOPTDOWNS_V2
```
Campaign naming convention is intact in the engagement pixel. If the `e=` parameter (see §2) is correctly populated in production, Everest attribution should be resolvable via the `c=` parameter alone.
