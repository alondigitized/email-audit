# final hours ⏰ to score sanitizer steals under $5.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-30**
## Executive summary

- This is the closing bell on B&BW's same-day hand sanitizer deal — a mirror of the structural pattern they've run all month (May 27 on Wallflowers, May 25 on cleansers). The "FINAL HOURS!" red banner is unmissable and the two price points ($2 PocketBac, $4.95 sprays) anchor the hero cleanly. So far, so familiar.
- The problem is what comes after: this email crams in two more distinct deals — the $12.95 3-Wick candles (a week-long arc in its own right) and a new "BUY 3, GET 3 FREE" full-size body care offer with its own "LIMITED TIME ONLY / 48 hours" timer. Three concurrent promotions, two of which carry independent urgency signals, turn a sharp closing bell into a catalog blast. The "final hours" in the subject only applies to the hero; the body copy undermines it immediately.
- One more flag: the morning send ("today only—starting at $1") set a floor of $1 for sanitizers. This email's "under $5" framing technically covers $2 and $4.95 but will read as a walk-back to anyone who saw both sends today. B&BW's best recent work (May 25, May 28) kept the offer frame consistent across a deal's lifecycle.

## What's working

- **"FINAL HOURS!" banner** is red, full-width, above the fold — urgency lands before the scroll.
- **Price badges are bold and specific** — "$2" and "$4.95" are clearly readable in the hero without needing to squint.
- **"Fresh Sanis for Summer"** is a tight seasonal hook; summer prep = hand sanitizer makes intuitive sense in late May.
- **Promo code is visible** in the hero section, giving deal-seekers a concrete activation path.

## What's weak

- **Three competing deals kill focus** — sanitizers (hero), $12.95 candles, and Buy 3 Get 3 Free body care are all fighting for attention; the closing-bell urgency of the hero bleeds away by the second scroll.
- **"Under $5" subject vs. "$1 starting" this morning** — subscribers who opened the AM send will notice the floor crept up; feels like inconsistent framing within the same deal window.
- **"SHOP" is a weak CTA** — used twice for two different deal sections with no differentiation; "Shop $2 Sanis" and "Shop Candles" would prevent click confusion.
- **The Buy 3 Get 3 Free section has its own "48 hours" countdown**, which is confusing alongside the hero's "FINAL HOURS!" — two urgency timers in one email read as noise, not tension.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Strip to one hero, one CTA.** Drop the candle module entirely (it's been running since May 28 and has its own email arc) and subordinate the Buy 3 Get 3 Free to a smaller strip below the fold, no separate urgency timer. Closing-bell emails should feel like a sprint, not a clearance rack.
- 2. **Reconcile the price floor with the morning send.** If the $1 item sold out, say so: "Deals from $2 — while they last." That turns the walk-back into a scarcity story.
- 3. **Sharpen the CTA copy.** "Shop $2 Sanis →" beats "SHOP" every time.
- 4. **Use the promo code as urgency anchor in the subject.** Code-in-subject works well for B&BW's audience.
- **Subject Alt A:** `final hours: $2 sanis + code inside ⏰`
- **Subject Alt B:** `your $2 sanitizers expire tonight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `PocketBac $2, sprays $4.95 — ends tonight. Use code CLEAN10.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Last call on Fresh Sanis for Summer — $2 while they last.`
