# 🙋 heads up - lowest price of the season* for Wallflowers ends today.
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-20**
## Executive summary

- The subject promised one thing — Wallflowers ending today — and the email delivers four: $2.95 Wallflowers, "New scents just dropped," 50% off Halloween items, and $14.95 3-Wick Candles. This is the exact same scattergun pattern flagged in the 09-17 "$1.95 deal is almost on empty" send and the 09-19 Wallflowers audit — a single-offer subject stapled onto a four-deal body. The Wallflowers hero itself is clean and well-built: price, scent name, "up to 24 per customer," and a 30-day scent-throw callout all in one clear block. But by the time a reader scrolls to the Halloween or candle sections, "ends today" has already been said twice and starts to feel like wallpaper rather than urgency.
- This brand keeps producing genuinely strong individual hero modules (this one included) and then burying them under three more asks in the same send. Against the 09-17 8/10 "spoiler alert" and "$2.95 Wallflowers" sends — which stayed disciplined to one offer — this is a step back toward the kitchen-sink format that scored 6/10 twice already this month.

## What's working

- The Wallflowers hero is unambiguous: $2.95, product name, "Shop now" button, purchase limit, and a 30-day fragrance duration claim all visible without digging.
- "Ends today" is used as a real banner element at the top of each of the three time-bound sections (Wallflowers, Halloween, 3-Wicks), reinforcing urgency visually.
- The Halloween module has a legible barcode + promo code (BOO) for in-store use, a nice cross-channel touch.
- Product photography is on-brand and seasonally appropriate (honey/vanilla styling, Halloween treats, fall bakery imagery for candles).
- "We think you'll love" personalized product row adds a lightweight recommendation layer.

## What's weak

- Subject says "Wallflowers ends today" but the body leads with Wallflowers then immediately stacks Halloween and 3-Wick offers — a bait-and-switch structure repeated from 09-17 and 09-19 sends.
- Three separate "Ends today" urgency banners in one email dilutes the urgency of all three; nothing distinguishes which deal is actually most time-sensitive.
- No unifying value prop ties the four offers together (fragrance refill vs. Halloween gift sets vs. candles are different shopping missions), so the email reads like three flyers taped together.
- The "New scents just dropped" module has no price or code — it's a soft browse CTA competing for attention against three hard offers with prices.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this to a single-offer send matching the subject — Wallflowers only, drop Halloween and 3-Wicks into a separate email. This is what made the 09-17 "spoiler alert" and "$2.95 Wallflowers" sends score 8/10 vs. this one's likely mid-range score.
- 2. If multi-offer is a business requirement, rank by actual urgency and cut "Ends today" down to the one deal that's truly expiring, not three.
- 3. Move "New scents just dropped" out of a $-lead flow entirely — it has no price, so it reads as noise between two priced offers.
- 4. Add the purchase-limit and scent-duration language to the Halloween and 3-Wick modules too, not just Wallflowers, for consistency.
- **Subject Alt A:** `last call: $2.95 Wallflowers, today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Wallflowers drop to $2.95 — gone after today`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus 50% off Halloween favorites, while they last.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stock up before the price resets tomorrow.`
