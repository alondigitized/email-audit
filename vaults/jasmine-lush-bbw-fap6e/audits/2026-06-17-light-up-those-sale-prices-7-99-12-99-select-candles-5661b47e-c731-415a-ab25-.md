# light up ✨ those Sale prices: $7.99-$12.99 select candles.
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-17**
## Executive summary

- This is Bath & Body Works' candle-specific chapter of a Semi-Annual Sale cadence that has now fired roughly seven emails in three days. The pivot to a product-specific subject ("$7.99–$12.99 select candles") is a smart attempt to keep the sale feeling fresh — but the email body immediately betrays that intent by stacking three more equal-weight offers (75% body care, 50% hand soaps, $3.50 refills) before the first scroll. The candle focus that the subject sets up evaporates mid-page.
- What saves this email is execution quality. The hero price range is visible before a single pixel of scroll, the "Glow for It" pun is on-brand and earns its place, and the Father's Day pickup callout (June 21, 4 days out) gives the email a credible, finite clock. The render is clean — no broken images, no layout breakage.
- The real risk here is cadence, not creative. After the 9/10 body-care send on June 16 and two additional emails that same day, this send is fighting for attention in a saturated inbox. A subscriber who already knows the sale is live and hasn't acted yet needs a new angle — the candle price range is that angle, but the email doesn't commit to it.

## What's working

- **Price-first hero** — "$7.99–$12.99" appears in both the subject and the first content block; the number does the job before copy has to.
- **"Glow for It" pun** — ties directly to candles, light, and the sale concept; feels earned, not forced.
- **Father's Day pickup callout** — specific date (June 21), specific mechanic (in-store pickup after 9 a.m. ET), makes the urgency credible.
- **Multi-tier price ladder** — $3.50 refills → $7.99–$12.99 candles → 75% off body care creates multiple entry points for different budgets.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no text overlap, no lorem ipsum visible.

## What's weak

- **Subject promises candles; email delivers a catalog** — body care at 75% off takes the second content slot and is visually as prominent as the candle hero.
- **Every CTA says "SHOP"** — four identical "SHOP" buttons across four different categories is a missed chance to anchor each click ("Shop Candles," "Shop Body Care").
- **No scent or product specifics** — the candle images are visible but unnamed; naming one hero scent drives the FOMO that justifies a click.
- **Father's Day angle is buried mid-email** — the best urgency signal in the send lives below three product blocks, where a significant share of mobile openers never reach.
- **Cadence** — this is send #7+ from BBW in ~72 hours; the prior June 16 "🤩is this for real?!" send already scored 9/10 with a stronger single-offer story; this one arrives with diminishing novelty.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to candles for the full above-fold experience.** The subject earns a candle-first layout. Push body care and hand soaps below the fold. One hero, one price, one click.
- 2. **Replace generic "SHOP" CTAs with category-specific copy.** "Shop Candles," "Shop Body Care," "Shop Refills" — each button should say what it links to.
- 3. **Name a hero scent in the candle block.** Pick the most-clicked scent from prior sends and give it a line ("Think: Fresh Strawberry Daiquiri" or whatever is pictured) — specificity converts browsers.
- 4. **Move the Father's Day callout to the hero.** "Gift it by June 21 — free in-store pickup" in a sub-headline under the price range doubles urgency without adding a new section.
- 5. **Consolidate sends.** Candles + body care + hand soaps + refills could live in one well-structured send; breaking them into daily emails trains subscribers to ignore the signal.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop select candles before they sell out — in-store pickup by June 21`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**10 images missing `alt` text** (confirmed by QA):

- 1× static asset (`image.e2.bathandbodyworks.com/lib/fe9213737561047a72/m/1/418d0db7-5a65-43cb-b5c9-9c6c142e4fd2.png`)
- 1× Everest engagement pixel (alt acceptable as empty for tracking pixels, but flagged)
- **2× images with empty `src` attribute** — these will render as broken image icons in Outlook and some mobile clients

**Google Fonts import will not render in most clients:**
```css
@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Source+Sans+Pro&display=swap');
```
Gmail, Outlook, and Yahoo strip external `@import`. The declared fallback (`Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif`) handles this correctly — no visual break, but the import is dead weight and adds a DNS lookup.

**`<meta charset>` is placed after the `<style>` block** — the charset declaration appears below the `<style>` tag rather than as the first `<head>` element. Best practice is charset-first to prevent pre-parse encoding issues in older clients.

**Missing `<meta name="viewport">`** — no viewport meta tag present. Most modern email clients handle this, but absence can cause suboptimal rendering in some mobile webview environments.
