# starts today: big price drop. big. HUGE. 💁‍♀️
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-26**
## Executive summary

- Bath & Body Works fires its sixteenth-plus email of the Semi-Annual Sale blitz on June 26, this time with a "Pretty Woman" cultural riff and a "Biggest Price Drop Yet" headline. The creative is legitimately fresh — beach imagery, a playful rubber duck banner, and a three-category breakdown that gives the reader a clear shopping menu instead of a firehose of SKUs. The visual execution is the best of the recent run.
- The problem is arithmetic. Yesterday's (June 25) hero was $2.99 Wallflowers. Today's Wallflowers hero is $3.50 — fifty-one cents higher. That undercuts the "Biggest Price Drop Yet" claim for anyone who opened yesterday's double-send. A subscriber carrying that price anchor will feel the mixed signal, even if the 75%-off body care headline is genuinely new.
- Bottom line: great creative direction, credible escalation story on body care, but the Wallflowers pricing regression is a self-inflicted wound on the single most-drummed SKU of the blitz.

## What's working

- **"Biggest Price Drop Yet" delivers on the subject's promise.** The subject teases scale; the hero confirms it with "75% off" above the fold.
- **Three-category grid is the clearest layout of the blitz.** "Glow / Clean / Home" with distinct price callouts reduces friction versus the brand's earlier wall-of-product formats.
- **Beach/summer imagery is seasonally on-point for June 26.** The creative finally matches the calendar instead of recycling evergreen studio shots.
- **The Pretty Woman riff ("big. HUGE. 💁‍♀️") is distinctive.** It earned a second look in the subject line without feeling spammy or using !!!.
- **BOPIS and "Find A Store" anchored at the bottom.** Practical utility for subscribers near a physical location — smart inclusion for a same-day "starts today" send.

## What's weak

- **Wallflowers pricing regressed from $2.99 (June 25) to $3.50 (June 26).** Subscribers who opened yesterday will notice; it directly contradicts the "biggest price drop yet" frame.
- **"SHOP" is a generic CTA.** "Shop Body Care Deals" or "Shop 75% Off" would convert better than a single verb with no object.
- **"Starts today" without an end date.** The urgency is front-loaded but open-ended — no deadline to drive same-session action.
- **The "TOP OFFERS / TODAY'S DEALS" carousel is too small to read.** Product thumbnails are thumbnail-sized, not scannable; they add scroll weight without decision value.
- **No loyalty or My Bath & Body Works member callout.** At minimum the fifteenth email of a blitz is the right moment to reward members visibly — and none appears.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the Wallflowers price contradiction before send.** If $3.50 is the real price, don't run "Biggest Price Drop Yet" in the hero in the same week you headlined $2.99. Either hold the $2.99 or reframe the hero around the body care 75% without touching Wallflowers pricing.
- 2. **Replace "SHOP" CTAs with category-specific copy.** "Shop 75% Off Glow Picks" / "Shop 50% Off Soaps" — specificity lifts click rate.
- 3. **Add a sale end date.** Even "through July 4th" would convert fence-sitters who need a reason to act now.
- 4. **Collapse or remove the TOP OFFERS grid.** It adds visual noise but no decision signal at that size; use the space to surface one aspirational hero product.
- 5. **Add a member-exclusive tier.** "My Bath & Body Works members save an extra 10%" inserted between sections gives the loyalty block its own moment.
- **Subject Alt A:** `75% off body care starts today. yes, really.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `the real price drop dropped. 75% off.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Biggest discount of the Semi-Annual Sale — body care, hand soap, Wallflowers all hit new lows.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `75% off select body care + $3.50 Wallflowers refills, one day only.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Bath & Body Works "Big Price Drop" (062626)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email renders on a standard Salesforce Marketing Cloud / ExactTarget stack and passes basic structure checks, but contains two leaked unprocessed template artifacts and nine images missing alt text. RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe compliance is unconfirmed, which is a deliverability risk for Google/Yahoo bulk sender thresholds.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**23 click-tracking links** all route through `click.e2.bathandbodyworks.com` — HTTP probes were skipped for all of them. Landing page UTM coverage cannot be confirmed from this audit.

**Open-pixel** (`click.e2.bathandbodyworks.com/open.aspx?XAB3WDRANGJUXNXCLFJYURIXSA.70256`) is correctly hidden in a 1×1 table with `mso-hide:all` — no issue there.

**Two `<img>` tags have empty `src` attributes** (confirmed by QA: `src: ` with blank value). Empty-src images cause an unnecessary HTTP request to the page's own URL in some clients, and will render as broken image icons in others.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**DOCTYPE is HTML 4.01 Transitional** — the legacy standard. Not a rendering blocker given inbox client normalization, but signals template age.

**Google Fonts loaded via `@import`** in `<style>`:
```css
@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Source+Sans+Pro&display=swap');
```
`@import` inside `<style>` blocks is stripped by Gmail and unsupported in Outlook. Source Sans Pro will not render in major clients; the fallback (Arial/Helvetica in inline styles) carries the load — acceptable, but the import is wasted overhead.

**9 images missing alt text** (QA-confirmed), including what appear to be content images, not just tracking pixels:
- `ab904853f6e026db.png`, `b3603e51d3bdf040.png`, `af1a58be4c9bb740.png`, `a7e63b1df5d8c5d9.png`, `7fc62fadc161fd8d.png` — all via `mi.bathandbodyworks.com` (Movable Ink personalized content)
- `418d0db7-5a65-43cb-b5c9-9c6c142e4fd2.png` — hosted on `image.e2.bathandbodyworks.com`
- `bbwus.everestengagement.com/ea/fw0q51vctP/` — missing alt on what appears to be a behavioral tracking image

Missing alt on Movable Ink content images is the most impactful: images-off users and screen readers get no context for the primary visual content.

`.applelinks a` CSS class is defined to suppress Apple Mail auto-linking — functional only if the class is applied to a wrapper element in the body (not verifiable from truncated source, but pattern is present).

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Two unprocessed template artifacts** found in HTML comments:
```html
<!-- `date "+ET%m.%y"` -->
```
This appears **twice** — once before `<body>` and once inside the body. This looks like a shell-style template variable that was never substituted. It does not surface to end users (it's inside an HTML comment), but it signals a broken template pre-processing step and will appear in source-view tools.

**AMPscript logic comments leaked into rendered HTML:**
```html
<!--Adds daily APT set via ampscript. Adds preset APT for emails flagged NTF-->
<!--If @NTF_Flag is "N", @APT should be set in the email; no default fallback here-->
```
These are not stripped before send. They expose internal campaign logic (APT = Audience Personalization Token, NTF = New-to-File) in the raw source. No security risk, but it leaks deployment detail.

**Commented-out slot definition visible in source:**
```html
<!-- <div data-type="slot" data-key="gvhu7dvhmx9" data-label="Preheader"></div> -->
```
Dead scaffolding from the template builder.

**Subscriber ID present in Movable Ink image URLs** (`mi_u=200511831233`) — this is expected behavior for personalized content; not an issue.

**RFC 8058 absence is the priority compliance item.** Google and Yahoo have enforced one-click unsubscribe (`List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`) for bulk senders (>5k/day to Gmail) since February 2024. BBW is almost certainly above that threshold. If the header is genuinely absent (not just a relay capture gap), this is an active deliverability risk.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM coverage is unverifiable** — all 23 CTA links route through `click.e2.bathandbodyworks.com` redirects. The campaign code `062626_EM_JUNSAS_BAU_PROMO_0_NRDM_75` is present in Movable Ink image parameters but that is an internal SFMC tracking code, not a standard UTM param.

Without resolving the redirect chain, there is no way to confirm that `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, and `utm_content` are appended to destination URLs on `bathandbodyworks.com`. This should be spot-checked against 2-3 resolved redirect targets.

---

### 7. Recommendations

**P1 — Confirm RFC 8058 compliance:** Verify `List-Unsubscribe-Post` is set at the SFMC sending domain level. If absent, add it — this is a Google/Yahoo bulk sender requirement.

**P2 — Fix empty `src` image tags:** Two `<img>` elements have blank `src` values. Identify and remove or correct them to prevent spurious requests and broken image indicators.

**P3 — Add alt text to Movable Ink content images:** The five `mi.bathandbodyworks.com` personalized images have no alt text. These are likely the primary hero/product visuals; images-off users see nothing.

**P4 — Strip template artifacts before send:** The two `<!-- \`date "+ET%m.%y"\` -->` comments should be resolved or removed. Audit the template pre-processing pipeline for why these shell-style substitutions were not evaluated.

**P5 — Purge internal AMPscript comments from production HTML:** Comments referencing NTF flags, APT logic, and slot keys add source noise and expose deployment internals. Remove before send.

**P6 — Verify UTM params on redirect destinations:** Spot-resolve 3–5 `click.e2.bathandbodyworks.com` links and confirm UTM params are present on `bathandbodyworks.com` landing pages.

**P7 (low) — Remove dead `@import` for Google Fonts:** Source Sans Pro renders in <5% of email clients via this mechanism. Removing the import eliminates a wasted external request with no rendering downside.
