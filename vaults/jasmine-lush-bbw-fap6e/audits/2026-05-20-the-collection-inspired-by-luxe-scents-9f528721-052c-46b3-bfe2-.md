# the collection inspired by luxe scents. ✨😉
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- Bath & Body Works pivots again — less than 24 hours after the $1.95 car freshener "final hours" close (5/19), we're now in premium-fragrance-launch territory with a new collection called "Inspired by Luxe Scents," anchored by three sub-lines: Strawberry Flirt, Imperfect Beauty, and All Eyes on Her. The photography is polished, the spring color palette is seasonally right, and the BUY 3, GET 1 FREE banner lands immediately after the hero. On paper, it's a decent new-launch email.
- The problem is the subject line does nothing. "The collection inspired by luxe scents. ✨😉" could have been written by any fragrance brand on any Tuesday. After a week of App installs, Member Days recruitment, a $19.95 candle deal, and two consecutive car-freshener flash-deal emails, this subscriber has been pinged six times in nine days — and today's subject gives them zero reason to tap open that wouldn't also apply to yesterday, the day before, or a month from now.
- The email itself suffers from the classic "three campaigns duct-taped together" problem: a time-boxed offer badge (48 hours) competes with a collection narrative that wants to build desire slowly. Pick one job per send.

## What's working

- BUY 3, GET 1 FREE is surfaced in a dedicated banner immediately below the hero — no digging for the deal.
- The 48-hour timer badge on the hero adds credible urgency that the subject line completely abandons.
- "SHOP THE COLLECTION" is a clean, specific CTA — verb + object, not the generic "Discover."
- Product names (Strawberry Flirt, All Eyes on Her) signal a real, named collection, not a generic clearance push.
- Spring/summer color palette and product photography feel current and seasonally earned.

## What's weak

- Subject line is entirely generic — "luxe scents" describes roughly 80% of the fragrance category and gives the inbox zero reason to choose this over the next unread email.
- Three product heroes compete for the same real estate; none gets enough room to build actual desire before the next one crowds it out.
- Zero price anchoring on the new collection — what does Strawberry Flirt cost? The only number visible is the promotional mechanic, not the product itself.
- "If you know, you know" is insider-speak that actively excludes new or lapsed subscribers — the exact people a collection launch should be recruiting.
- The register whiplash is real: yesterday was a $1.95 budget flash deal, today is "Rich. Layered. Delectable." aspirational luxury. One of these is lying about who Bath & Body Works is.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject to lead with a product name** — "Strawberry Flirt" or "All Eyes on Her" is a specific, evocative hook that earns a tap; "luxe scents" earns a scroll-past.
- 2. **Pick one hero product for this send.** Give Strawberry Flirt the full email, tease the other two as "and more in the collection →". Save Imperfect Beauty and All Eyes on Her for days 2 and 3 of the launch sequence — you now have two more sends banked.
- 3. **Surface the 48-hour deadline in the subject line** — the badge is doing real work in the body; the inbox line throws it away entirely.
- 4. **Add a price anchor** — even "starting at $17.50" or the BUY 3 GET 1 FREE math spelled out ("4 for the price of 3, ~$13 each") moves the reader from browse to buy intent.
- 5. **Retire "if you know, you know"** — replace with a one-line origin story: "inspired by the scents you've been seeing everywhere — now at B&BW prices."
- **Subject Alt A:** `Strawberry Flirt is here — your summer scent. 48 hrs only. 🍓`
- **Subject Alt B:** `3 new scents. Rich, layered, and BUY 3 GET 1 FREE.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Inspired by the world's most-coveted fragrances — now yours for a lot less.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Strawberry Flirt, Imperfect Beauty + All Eyes on Her drop today.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is a standard Salesforce Marketing Cloud / ExactTarget send using legacy HTML4 Transitional markup. No critical blockers found; primary concerns are missing authentication header visibility through the relay, absent `List-Unsubscribe` headers in captured metadata, and multiple images with no `alt` attributes.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Tracking pixel domain mismatch — two separate systems in use:**
- Click tracking: `click.e2.bathandbodyworks.com` (ExactTarget/SFMC)
- Open tracking: `https://mi.bathandbodyworks.com/p/rp/25b6733181620ab1.png?mi_u=...&mi_ecmp=052026_NRDM_TBC_TBCB3G1_NS_BAL_V1` — this is a **Movable Ink** personalization pixel, not SFMC native

Both are expected for a Movable Ink + SFMC stack, but the QA relay skipped all 25 click-redirect links (`click.e2.bathandbodyworks.com`), so broken destination URLs cannot be confirmed from this audit alone. Manual spot-check of click-through destinations is required.

**Open pixel `alt` is empty string:**

Empty `alt=""` is technically correct for a decorative/tracking pixel; no issue here.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing `alt` text on 6 content images (confirmed by QA):**

| Image | src |
|---|---|
| `25b6733181620ab1.png` | `https://mi.bathandbodyworks.com/p/rp/25b6733181620ab1.png` (Movable Ink) |
| `418d0db7-5a65-43cb-b5c9-9c6c142e4fd2.png` | `https://image.e2.bathandbodyworks.com/lib/fe9213737561047a72/m/1/418d0db7...` |
| Everest engagement pixel | `https://bbwus.everestengagement.com/ea/fw0q51vctP/?e=200511831233...` |
| 2× blank `src` images | `src=""` — likely conditional/unfilled template slots |

Two images with `src=""` indicate **unfilled template slots** that should have been suppressed or conditionally rendered. If they reach any client that requests empty `src`, the browser may issue a spurious request to the base URL of the page.

**Outlook DPI comment has misplaced comment terminator:**
```html
<!-- This code helps render code in 120dpi versions of Outlook */ -->
```
The `*/` inside an HTML comment is harmless but indicates a copy-paste artifact from a CSS comment — minor code hygiene issue.

**`<meta charset>` placement:**
```html
<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="text/html; charset=utf-8">
```
This appears *after* `</head>` content including `<style>` blocks in the truncated source. If the `<meta>` is genuinely out-of-order, some parsers may misinterpret character encoding before the declaration is reached. Should be the first element inside `<head>`.

**Double preheader pattern:** The email contains two separate preheader constructs — one in a `<table>` before `<body>` and one as a hidden `<div>` inside `<body>`. Only one preheader text renders in inbox preview. The outer one (`<td>` before body) is structurally invalid placement; the inner one is the canonical pattern. Risk: some clients may surface the wrong preheader text.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header — not captured:**
> `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header not found` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found (RFC 8058)`

The AgentMail relay did not surface these headers. SFMC typically injects `List-Unsubscribe` at the MTA layer; the most likely explanation is relay stripping, not true absence. However, if headers are genuinely absent:
- Gmail and Yahoo (as of Feb 2024 bulk sender requirements) **require** `List-Unsubscribe` + `List-Unsubscribe-Post` for bulk senders. Absence will suppress the one-click unsubscribe UI and may affect deliverability scoring.
- Needs verification against raw SMTP headers from a seed inbox (Gmail/Yahoo), not just the relay capture.

**SPF/DKIM status unknown:**
> `[WARN] Authentication-Results header not found`

Sending domain is `e2.bathandbodyworks.com`. Cannot confirm SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass from relay data alone. Verify via seed send to Gmail — `Authentication-Results` header in received message should show `dkim=pass` and `spf=pass`.

**CAN-SPAM physical address:** Not verifiable from truncated HTML, but the full email must include a valid postal address. Confirm it appears in the footer of the untruncated source.
