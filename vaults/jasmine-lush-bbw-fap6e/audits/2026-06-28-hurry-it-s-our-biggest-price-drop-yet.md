# ⚠️ hurry, it's our biggest price drop yet. 🏃‍♀️💨
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-28**
## Executive summary

- Bath & Body Works fires its seventeenth-or-so email of the Semi-Annual Sale blitz on June 28 — and it has a structural problem the creative team apparently didn't catch: the hero headline **"Biggest Price Drop Yet"** is word-for-word identical to June 26's send (scored 9/10 here). A subscriber who opened that email two days ago will feel like they've already read this one. The $7.99 candle price was also the lead offer in yesterday's send. Recycling the marquee claim and the lead price point on back-to-back-to-back days is how you teach your list to stop opening.
- Inside the email the execution is clean: three well-separated offer blocks with legible prices, summer beach imagery, and functional SHOP buttons. The 50% off hand soap / $3.50 fragrance refill blocks are the freshest elements here. But the creative arrives wrapped in a ⚠️ subject-line emoji — a mild spam signal — and "hurry" urgency with no concrete deadline visible anywhere in the body.
- **Verdict:** A competent, clean send torpedoed by creative déjà vu and catastrophic send cadence. The email itself would score higher in isolation; as the third send in three days with the same headline, it earns a penalty it didn't technically deserve.

- **Three distinct price-point offers** are clearly separated and scannable — candles ($7.99–$10.99), hand soap (50% off), fragrance refills ($3.50).
- **Visual hierarchy is solid** — 75% off badge anchors the hero before any scrolling.
- **Summer beach creative** fits the season without being heavy-handed.
- **"WE JUST WANT YOU TO LOVE IT! 100% guaranteed"** tagline at the bottom adds a trust anchor that's easy to miss but earns points.
- **No render bugs** — layout is clean, images load, text doesn't overlap.

## What's weak

- **"Biggest Price Drop Yet" is the June 26 headline verbatim** — subscribers who opened that send (scored 9/10) will feel no reason to engage.
- **$7.99 candles anchored yesterday's send** — leading with the same price today signals nothing has changed.
- **"Hurry" in the subject with no deadline in the body** — urgency that can't be backed up reads as noise.
- **⚠️ warning emoji in subject** is a mild spam-filter signal and feels desperate at send #17.
- **"SHOP" CTA copy is inert** — three buttons all say the same one word; none tells you what you're shopping for.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Retire "Biggest Price Drop Yet" immediately** — it fired its shot on June 26. The headline today should be offer-specific: "Candles from $7.99. Refills at $3.50. Today Only." anchors something new.
- 2. **Add a visible deadline to the body** — "Ends tonight at midnight" under each offer block converts "hurry" into a real reason to click now.
- 3. **Make SHOP CTAs specific**: "Shop Candles," "Shop Hand Soaps," "Shop Refills" — each button earns the click it's asking for.
- 4. **Drop the ⚠️ emoji** — swap for a price-forward subject that doesn't rely on alarm imagery.
- 5. **Cadence break** — skip a day. The list needs a reset before they tune out entirely; a missed day buys more opens on the next send than a repeated creative costs.

## Technical Audit — Bath & Body Works "Biggest Price Drop" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

Well-formed HTML4 Transitional email with Salesforce Marketing Cloud infrastructure. No broken links or merge-token leakage detected, but three compliance gaps and nine missing alt attributes require attention before volume sends.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**23 tracking links skipped** — all click-throughs route through `click.e2.bathandbodyworks.com`, which is expected for SFMC. No HTTP probing was possible, so destination URL integrity (404s, expired landing pages) cannot be confirmed from this audit.

**Open pixel** uses an unsigned `open.aspx` beacon:
```
https://click.e2.bathandbodyworks.com/open.aspx?2I65APMJ7UIUHAC5B7PFM274GU.70254&d=70254&bmt=0
```
The `bmt=0` flag is present; no structural issues, but the token string appears static and not subscriber-personalized beyond what SFMC appends at send time — acceptable if SFMC is injecting the subscriber key dynamically.

**No issues with dead links** confirmed from what was probeable.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

The two images with **empty `src` attributes** are the most problematic — these will produce broken image requests and may trigger spam filters in some clients (Outlook, ProtonMail).

**Google Fonts `@import`** (`Source+Sans+Pro`) will be stripped silently in Gmail, Outlook, and Apple Mail with image-blocking on. This is expected behavior but means fallback stacks (`Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif`) carry the full load — confirm that's acceptable.

**Fixed 600px width** on the outer table — mobile styles apply via `@media (max-width:599px)`, which is correct, but Gmail's app on Android ignores `!important` in media queries. Test in Gmail Android before send.

**Preheader padding technique** uses both a hidden `<div>` in the `<td>` and a separate invisible Unicode-padding block — functional but redundant. Not a bug.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**No unresolved merge tokens** visible in the truncated source. AMPscript comments reference `@NTF_Flag` and `@APT` variables, suggesting conditional logic for new-to-file subscribers. These appear structurally intact (comments, not rendered output).

**`mi_u=200511831233`** appears hardcoded in multiple image URLs on `mi.bathandbodyworks.com`. If this is a subscriber ID, it should be a dynamic token — a static value here means every recipient's image impressions map to the same profile, breaking personalization analytics.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not found** — captured as a QA warning. This is a relay capture issue (AgentMail may not forward these headers), but if absent at actual delivery, Gmail and Yahoo will suppress the native unsubscribe UI and may route to spam. **High priority to verify** on a raw received header.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found** — one-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo for bulk senders since Feb 2024) may not be supported. If this sender exceeds 5,000 msgs/day to Gmail, this is a deliverability violation.

**`Authentication-Results` header not found** — SPF/DKIM status unverifiable through the relay. The sending domain `e2.bathandbodyworks.com` is a subdomain consistent with SFMC's DKIM setup, but needs raw-header confirmation that DKIM passes.

**CAN-SPAM physical address** — not visible in the truncated source; assumed present in footer (not auditable from provided excerpt).

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**`mi_ecmp=062826_EM_JUNSAS_BAU_PROMO_0_NRDM_79`** appears on image impression URLs — this is a campaign code, not a UTM parameter. Whether this same campaign code propagates through click-redirect URLs to the landing page as `utm_campaign` is not verifiable without probing the 23 skipped tracking links.

**No `utm_*` parameters confirmed** in the HTML source. If SFMC is not appending UTMs at redirect resolution, site analytics will attribute traffic to `(direct)` rather than `email`. Verify that `click.e2.bathandbodyworks.com` redirects append UTM params before the final destination.
