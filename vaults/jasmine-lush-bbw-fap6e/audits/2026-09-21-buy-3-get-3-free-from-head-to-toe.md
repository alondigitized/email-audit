# buy 3 get 3 FREE 💎 from head to toe. 💁‍♀️ ✨
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-21**
## Executive summary

- The subject promises "buy 3 get 3 FREE from head to toe" but the visual hero is a "New Everyday Luxuries" fragrance launch — the actual buy-3-get-3 offer is buried as small text and a modest outline button two-thirds down the fold, well below a full-bleed product photo of a mist and body cream. Whoever wrote the subject line and whoever built the hero were not talking to each other.
- That said, execution quality on the render itself is clean — no broken images, no overlapping text, a legible clean layout, and the "New Everyday Luxuries" product shot is genuinely well-composed (orchids, pink glass bottles, soft styling). The problem isn't craft, it's alignment: against the brand's recent run (09-20 mystery-discount flash sale, 09-19/09-18 scattergun Wallflowers stacking), this is at least a single coherent product story visually — but it's the wrong story for the subject line that got someone to open it.
- Net: a subscriber who opens for "buy 3 get 3 FREE" scrolls past a fragrance-launch pitch before finding the deal, which is a bait-and-switch on hierarchy even if not on substance.

## What's working

- Clean, bug-free render — no overlapping text, no broken assets, no lorem ipsum.
- The "New Everyday Luxuries" hero photography is high quality and on-brand (orchids, soft pink tones, product-forward).
- "Starts today" adds credible urgency to the buy-3-get-3 callout once you reach it.
- Secondary content ("We think you'll love," seasonal candle, store finder) is organized and scannable, not cluttered like recent scattergun sends.
- Free shipping banner at top is a real, simple offer with a visible "Shop" link.

## What's weak

- The subject's entire hook — buy 3 get 3 FREE — is not the hero. It's a small text block below the fold, competing with a fragrance-launch pitch for attention.
- No discount percentage or dollar amount is visible anywhere near the hero; a shopper skimming the top of the email sees a new-product ad, not the promised deal.
- "Shop now" CTA button is a plain white/outline box with no visual urgency — it doesn't match the size of the offer being advertised in the subject.
- The offer terms ("All full-size body care") aren't shown until small print at the very bottom — someone deciding whether the deal applies to what they want has to scroll past everything to check exclusions.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the buy-3-get-3 callout into the hero itself — either replace "New Everyday Luxuries" as the top visual or run it as a banner directly above the fold so subject and hero match. This is the single biggest lift available here.
- 2. Make the CTA button solid-filled and larger — "Shop now" as an outline box undersells a buy-3-get-3 offer.
- 3. State the category ("body care") next to the offer in the hero, not just in fine print — shoppers want to know if lotions/creams/mists qualify before clicking.
- 4. Keep the fragrance-launch content, but demote it to the "We think you'll love" section rather than leading with it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `buy 3, get 3 free — all full-size body care, today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `your body care haul just got 2x cheaper (buy 3 get 3 free)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Mix & match lotions, creams, mists — no code needed.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Ends 9/23 at 6am ET. Shop before it's gone.`
