# ⌚️final hours! $2.99 ends soon...don't miss it!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-25**
## Executive summary

- This is Bath & Body Works' second June 25 send — and at minimum the fifteenth email in a nine-day Semi-Annual Sale blitz — deploying "final hours" urgency on the same $2.99 Wallflowers price that anchored the morning email ("bring this $2.99 deal home. 🏡 today only!"). The creative is well-structured: a red FINAL HOURS banner, an unmissable price, and a logical cascade of secondary offers. In isolation, this would be a strong send. In context, it is a diminishing-returns double-tap that asks subscribers to feel urgency twice in the same day for the exact same offer.
- The layout does the right things. The $2.99 number is the first place the eye goes, the SHOP CTA sits directly below it, and the offer cascade — Wallflowers → Candles → Body Care — covers different purchase motivations without needing to scroll far. "Fresh Deals on the House" lands cleanly as a home-fragrance pun. The "Select" qualifier and "Limit 24" note are both visible, which keeps the framing honest.
- The structural problem isn't execution — it's exhaustion. BBW has now sent "final hours" language at least twice (June 20 at $3.99, today at $2.99), and a subscriber who opened the morning email already saw this price framed as "today only." Sending it again as "final hours" trains the list to wait for the actual last email — whichever one that turns out to be.

## What's working

- **$2.99 dominates the fold**: Price is the first thing the eye lands on; no hunting required.
- **"FINAL HOURS" banner reads instantly**: The red-on-red stripe communicates urgency before any copy is processed.
- **Offer cascade is logically sequenced**: Wallflowers (hero) → Candles ($7.99–$12.99) → Body Care (75% off) gives different need states a clear path.
- **"Once they're gone, they're gone."** is the sharpest scarcity copy in the email; it works because it sounds different from the surrounding promo language.
- **BOPIS callout** (Buy Online, Pickup in Store) is a genuine friction reducer for same-day shoppers responding to urgency.

## What's weak

- **Same-day double-tap on the identical price**: The morning send already used "today only" framing at $2.99; this "final hours" email adds no new information and risks feeling like a brand that cried wolf.
- **No specific deadline time**: "Final hours" without a clock or "ends at midnight ET" leaves urgency vague and unverifiable — a subscriber can't act with precision.
- **"SHOP" CTA is generic across all three offers**: Every section uses the same dead-weight verb; no button distinguishes what you're actually clicking into.
- **Candle price ($7.99–$12.99) is psychologically expensive next to the $2.99 hero**: Anchoring this high after the lead price makes the candle deal feel less like a sale.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put a specific cutoff time in the subject and the hero** ("ends at midnight ET" or a live countdown timer). This is the single highest-lift change — it makes "final hours" actually mean something actionable.
- 2. **Lead the evening send with a different product hero** than the morning send. If morning was $2.99 Wallflowers, evening should lead with candles or body care so the second email doesn't read as a copy-paste with a new subject line.
- 3. **Rename every CTA button** to match its section: "Shop Wallflowers," "Shop Candles," "Shop Body Care." Trivial copy lift with meaningful specificity gain.
- 4. **Cut or collapse the TOP OFFERS thumbnail grid**. After three full-offer panels, eight more small tiles scatter attention rather than driving any one of them to convert.
- **Subject Alt A:** `⌚️ ends at midnight: $2.99 Wallflowers — last call`
- **Subject Alt B:** `the $2.99 deal closes tonight. no more "final hours" after this.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus candles from $7.99 and 75% off body care — all wrapping up tonight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Grab your $2.99 faves before the Semi-Annual Sale ends for real.`

Images-off rendering (default in Outlook, common elsewhere) will show broken image icons with no fallback text for all of these.

**Two images have empty `src` attributes** — these will render as broken image icons in all clients regardless of image-loading settings.

**Google Fonts `@import` in `<head>`:**
```css
@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Source+Sans+Pro&display=swap');
```
Blocked by Gmail, Outlook (all versions), and Apple Mail in some configurations. The email will silently fall back to `Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif` (declared inline). Not a defect but confirms the font import has no effect for the majority of the audience.

**Missing `<meta name="viewport">` tag** — only `Content-Type` meta is present. Mobile media queries (`@media (max-width:599px)`) are present in the CSS and will apply in Gmail app and other clients, but the absence of a viewport meta can cause suboptimal scaling in some mobile webmail views.

**DOCTYPE is HTML 4.01 Transitional** — standard for email and expected for Outlook MSO compatibility. Not an issue.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not detected** (QA: `[WARN]`) — AgentMail relay may not be forwarding this header. At the MTA level, `List-Unsubscribe` is required by Gmail and Yahoo for bulk senders (>5k/day) under their 2024 sender requirements. If the header is absent at delivery, Gmail will surface its own unsubscribe UI rather than the sender's — loss of control over the unsubscribe flow.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not detected** (QA: `[WARN]`) — One-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy) relies on this header alongside `List-Unsubscribe`. Its absence means Gmail's one-click unsubscribe button may not function as intended.

**`Authentication-Results` header not found** (QA: `[WARN]`) — SPF and DKIM pass/fail status cannot be confirmed from available data. Sending domain is `e2.bathandbodyworks.com`; DKIM alignment with `bathandbodyworks.com` should be verified at the MTA. Cannot assess from HTML alone.

**CAN-SPAM physical address** — not visible in truncated source, but expected to be present in the footer. Cannot confirm or deny.

**Unsubscribe link in body** — not visible in truncated source. Assumed present given B&BW's compliance posture, but cannot confirm from available HTML.

Click URLs go through `click.e2.bathandbodyworks.com` and likely append UTM params on redirect — but this cannot be confirmed because all 24 click links were skipped in the HTTP probe. Recommend spot-checking at least one click URL outside the QA pipeline to confirm UTM appending is live.
