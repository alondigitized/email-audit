# $6.95 skin-loving body care. 🧖‍♀️today only!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-09**
## Executive summary

- Bath & Body Works opens a new $6.95 body care arc here, the day after the Nautical B3G3 send (June 8) and two days after the $5.95 closing bell (June 7). The template formula executes cleanly — "TODAY ONLY!" banner anchors urgency, the price badge registers before any copy, and the hero images are polished. The bigger problem isn't creative; it's calendar math. A subscriber who just saw "$5.95 ends today" three days ago will feel this as a price step-up, not a deal. That framing problem never gets addressed.
- The secondary BUY 3, GET 3 FREE block on Fine Fragrance is a recycled beat from June 8's Nautical send, now running alongside the $6.95 hero. Two unrelated offers competing for one email means neither owns it — the eye lands on "TODAY ONLY!" and then has to stop and figure out which offer is worth acting on.
- Verdict: disciplined formula, executed cleanly, undermined by a counterintuitive price escalation and a split-offer structure that halves the urgency of both.

## What's working

- **"TODAY ONLY!" banner is immediate** — no ambiguity about urgency, and it visually locks in the frame before any body copy.
- **"$6.95" price badge is the clear visual center** of the hero — the number reads before the headline does.
- **"Breathe In. Chill Out." headline** fits the products and the June mood; it's short, sensory, and season-right.
- **No render bugs** — layout is clean, images load, no broken or overlapping elements visible.
- **"TOP OFFERS" grid** gives additional entry points below the hero fold, hedging against the single-offer risk.

## What's weak

- **Price step-up from the still-recent $5.95 arc undercuts the deal framing** — a subscriber paying attention is buying the same category of product for $1 more this week, and no copy addresses why.
- **Dual-offer structure splits attention** — $6.95 body care AND B3G3 Fine Fragrance are separate deals fighting for the same email, and neither gets enough space to build desire.
- **"SHOP" is the weakest possible CTA copy** — it communicates nothing about what you'd land on, what category you'd see, or what makes clicking now better than closing the tab.
- **No specific product is named or shown prominently** — "skin-loving body care" is a category description, not a reason to buy anything in particular.
- **"Rich. Layered. Delectable." copy** reads as aspirational flavor text but doesn't actually surface the B3G3 offer clearly — a skim reader could miss the deal entirely.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the dual-offer structure.** Either lead with $6.95 and drop B3G3 to a line in the footer, or lead with B3G3 and treat $6.95 as the per-item context. Right now neither offer has room to convert.
- 2. **Address the price step-up directly in the hero copy.** "New summer scents, now at $6.95" or "Our newest arrivals start at $6.95" reframes the number as a category entry point rather than a repeat sale at a higher price.
- 3. **Name the hero product or scent family** — even one product name (e.g., "Waterfall Chill Body Mist") gives subscribers something specific to want before clicking.
- 4. **Replace "SHOP" with a specific CTA** — "Shop $6.95 Body Care", "See All Scents", or "Get $6.95 Mists" all outperform a bare verb.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New summer scents at $6.95 — today only, no code needed`
- **Subject Alt B:** `$6.95 body care drops today — meets tonight's deadline`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh summer mists and lotions at $6.95 — offer ends at midnight tonight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New arrivals priced at $6.95 each — grab yours before the clock runs out.`

## Technical Audit — Bath & Body Works "$6.95 Body Care" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard HTML4 Transitional table-based layout sent via Salesforce Marketing Cloud (`e2.bathandbodyworks.com`). Six accessibility violations, two structural HTML defects, a truncated tracking parameter, and unverifiable List-Unsubscribe header state represent the primary technical risk surface.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Tracking links unverifiable (25):** All click links route through `click.e2.bathandbodyworks.com` redirects. The QA relay cannot unwrap these, so UTM parameters and destination URL integrity are unconfirmed.

**Truncated campaign parameter:** The image tracking URL contains a malformed `mi_ecmp` value:
```
https://mi.bathandbodyworks.com/p/rp/5114c4a06bd61e83.png
  ?mi_u=200511831233
  &mi_ecmp=060926_RDM_TBC_695WELLNESSEE_NS_BAL_
```
The value ends with a trailing underscore and no terminal segment — the full campaign code appears to have been truncated. This will cause attribution mismatches in whatever system consumes `mi_ecmp`.

**Open pixel:** `https://click.e2.bathandbodyworks.com/open.aspx?ZOXEL4PRC2HUJCGFAIUQUETCNQ.70252&d=70252&bmt=0` — correctly implemented as a 0×0/1×1 hidden table cell.

**2 images with empty `src` attributes:** These will render as broken image icons in clients that don't suppress them. Likely vestigial template slots left unfilled.

**HTML structure defect:** The preheader `<table>` and open-pixel `<table>` both appear before the `<body>` tag in the source order. Content outside `<body>` is technically invalid and behavior is parser-dependent.

**Google Fonts `@import`:** `Source+Sans+Pro` is loaded via `@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?...')`. External font loading is blocked by Gmail, Outlook (all versions), and Apple Mail in some configurations. The stylesheet does not declare a CSS fallback stack — inline styles use `Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif`, which is the de-facto fallback, but only because it's specified separately on individual elements, not as a CSS fallback on the `@import` rule itself.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Exposed template comments:** The following shell-expansion comment appears twice verbatim in the HTML:
```html
<!-- `date "+ET%m.%y"` -->
```
This is a build-time comment that should be stripped pre-send. If the ESP is not removing HTML comments during send, this leaks internal template versioning logic.

**No unresolved merge tokens** visible in the truncated source. Subscriber ID `200511831233` appears consistently across `mi_u` and `e=` parameters, indicating correct personalization merge.

**AMPscript logic blocks** are present as HTML comments (NTF flag, APT logic). These are comments only — no raw AMPscript syntax is visible in the output, so they did not leak as unexecuted code.

---

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header — status unknown:**
The QA relay did not capture a `List-Unsubscribe` header. Since February 2024, Gmail and Yahoo require this header for bulk senders (>5k/day). Absence will trigger deliverability penalties. This may be a relay capture gap rather than a true absence — but it must be confirmed against raw SMTP headers.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) — status unknown:**
One-click unsubscribe support is not confirmed. Same caveat as above — verify against raw headers, not relay output.

**Authentication-Results — not captured:**
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status is unknown through the relay. `e2.bathandbodyworks.com` is an Epsilon/Conversant sending domain; authentication is likely configured at the ESP level, but cannot be confirmed from this capture.

**Physical address / footer unsubscribe:** Cannot verify from truncated HTML. Expected in footer — no flag raised, but confirm manually.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Unverifiable UTM state:** 25 click-redirect links could not be probed. Cannot confirm whether destination URLs carry `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, or `utm_content` parameters. This is the highest-priority gap for attribution.

**`mi_ecmp` truncation (see §2):** If this parameter feeds a campaign attribution system, the truncated value `060926_RDM_TBC_695WELLNESSEE_NS_BAL_` will not match expected campaign codes, breaking any downstream report joining on this field.
