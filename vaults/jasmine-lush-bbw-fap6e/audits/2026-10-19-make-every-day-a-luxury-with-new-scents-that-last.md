# make every day a luxury with new scents that last. ✨

## Executive summary

- This is a brand-launch email, not a promo. "Everyday Luxuries" is the hero, with new eau de parfums, a fine fragrance mist and an "Orchid Effect" scent. The imagery is polished and the pink and lilac palette is cohesive. The problem is that there is no offer anywhere in the render. - The copy also gets in its own way. White headlines such as "EVERYDAY LUXURIES" and "Fine fragrance mist" sit on pale backgrounds and are hard to read. - The product photography is lush and consistent, and the pink bottles read as one new line.
- The hero's group of smiling models gives it energy and a premium feel.
- The "High concentration" and "Medium concentration" tags and the "2X the concentration" claim give a concrete product benefit.
- The "You might like" row and the Rewards, texts and pickup footer give useful next steps.

## What's weak

- No price, percent off, code or deadline is visible, so the email lacks urgency. It's a marked step down from the "$6.95" and "BOGO" hooks earlier in the week.
- White text on light imagery hurts legibility in the hero, in "Fine fragrance mist" and in "Our feel good formula".
- The hero wordmark "EVERYDAY LUXURIES" is clipped by the model photo and the brand logo.
- A large blank white gap sits below the last block and near the footer, and the final footer block looks broken or empty.
- The CTAs are small outlined buttons ("Explore the collection", "Shop the collection") with generic copy, and there are too many different CTAs.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The sampled markup is a dark-mode-aware table layout sent through Everest (`bbwus.everestengagement.com`). The QA run found 0 hard failures and 3 warnings (57% pass rate). Compliance and authentication could not be verified, because the headers weren't captured. The HTML source was truncated, so footer and body links are unchecked.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Destinations unverified.** 43 tracking links were skipped by the HTTP probe because they are click-redirect domains. Final destinations, redirect chains and dead links are unconfirmed.
- **Image with a tracking URL and no alt.** QA flagged `https://bbwus.everestengagement.com/ea/fw0q51vctP/?e=200511831233&c=101926_EM_FA_BAU_PROMO_0_NRDM_EDLEDIT_AM_BAL_FULL_V1` as an `<img>` with no alt. It is probably an open-tracking pixel, which is low severity. If it is a content image, it is an accessibility gap.
- **Malformed `src`.** The `1cc306ef-4e4e-4c74-a632-97aabb8c4023.png` image has a leading space (`src:  https://image.e2...`). Browsers trim it, but strict parsers and some clients may not.
- **Font import.** `@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Helvetica+Neue&display=swap')` requests a family I don't believe Google Fonts hosts. If so, the request fails and falls back to Helvetica/Arial. `@import` is also stripped by Gmail and several other clients. It is a wasted request with no functional harm, since the `font-main` stack has fallbacks.
