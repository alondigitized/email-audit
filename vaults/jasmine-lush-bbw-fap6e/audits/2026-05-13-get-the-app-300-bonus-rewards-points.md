# get the app + 300 bonus Rewards points. 🙌
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-13**
## Executive summary

- This is a standard app-install push from Bath & Body Works, dressed up with a loyalty reward hook (300 Rewards points) to justify the ask. The hero is clean — the 300-point badge reads immediately, the "DOWNLOAD THE APP" CTA is unambiguous, and the asterisk handling for existing users ("your bonus points have been added") is a smart friction-reducer. Below the fold, a personalized product shelf and a category grid pad the email into something resembling a full send.
- The problem is the primary action (download an app) is a high-friction ask relative to the reward, and nothing in this email creates urgency. There's no expiry on the 300 points, no countdown, no deadline language. The email is pleasant and on-brand, but it's asking for behavioral change — install an app — without any reason to do it *today*.
- The dense fine print wall at the bottom undermines the "100% guaranteed" badge sitting just above it. That section reads as boilerplate padding. Net assessment: a competent mid-tier promotional send that will convert existing members who were already on the fence about the app, and convert almost nobody else.

## What's working

- **300-point starburst badge** reads instantly in the hero — the value prop is clear before a single word is read.
- **Asterisk handling for existing users** ("already have the app? your bonus points have been added") is thoughtful — removes the "this doesn't apply to me" skip reflex.
- **"DOWNLOAD THE APP" CTA** is a specific, unambiguous verb+noun — no vague "Discover More" nonsense.
- **"Picked just for you!" product shelf** adds a personalization layer and gives a second reason to engage beyond the app ask.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping layout, consistent brand visual system throughout.

## What's weak

- **Zero urgency on the offer** — 300 points with no expiry date means there's no reason to act now vs. next week vs. never.
- **The primary CTA competes with the personalized product section** — the email can't decide if it's an app-install email or a shopping email; neither wins fully.
- **"TOP OFFERS / TODAY'S DEALS" category grid has no actual deals visible** — it's a navigation bar dressed as an offer, which feels like a bait signal.
- **Dense fine print at the bottom actively undermines the "100% guaranteed" badge** sitting directly above it — the juxtaposition is trust-negative.
- **No social proof anywhere** — no reviews, no star ratings, no "X million members" — missed for an app-install push where trust in the new surface matters.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a deadline to the 300-point offer** — even "earn by May 31" would create urgency that's currently absent. This single change would lift conversions more than anything else.
- 2. **Pick a lane: app-install email OR shopping email** — either run a tight 3-section app-install send (hero → benefit bullets → CTA) or fold the app mention into a shopping email as a secondary strip. The hybrid dilutes both.
- 3. **Replace the category navigation grid with a live deal** — if it says "TODAY'S DEALS," show a deal (e.g., "3-wick candles 2 for $30 — in-app only") that makes downloading the app the path to unlock.
- 4. **Cut the "100% guaranteed" badge or move it to the product section** — it's meaningless at the footer level and looks defensive next to the fine print.
- 5. **Add a single app store rating callout** — "4.8 stars, 500K+ reviews" in the app feature section would directly address hesitation about the install.
- **Subject Alt A:** `300 Rewards points are waiting in the app`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your 300 bonus points expire soon — grab the app`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Easier shopping, your wallet in one place, and exclusive app-only deals.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop, earn, and redeem — all from your phone. Points added automatically.`
