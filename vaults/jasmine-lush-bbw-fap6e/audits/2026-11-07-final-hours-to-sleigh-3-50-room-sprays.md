# final hours to sleigh $3.50 room sprays. 🛷
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-07**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, price-led closer for the room-spray promo. The "Final hours" bar sits above "Festive feels in two spritzes" and a large **$3.50**, with a "Shop now" button next to the price. The offer lands fast. It is also the second send today on the same deal, after this morning's "✨ALL room sprays are $3.50, just for today.✨", so this reads as a deadline nudge, not news.
- Below the hero the email turns into a catalog. A "Lowest price of the year" Wallflowers block at **$2.75** follows, then a $14.95 3-wick candle block, then a three-tile "You might like" row. The hero is strong, but the secondary blocks pull attention away from the one deal the subject promises. The legal fine print is a dense wall at the bottom.
- My judgement: a solid, effective urgency send that executes the brand's usual price-forward formula, but it's busy and repetitive.

## What's working

- The hero price **$3.50** is huge and reads instantly, with "All Concentrated Room Sprays" directly beneath it.
- The "Final hours" bar at the top gives credible urgency that matches the subject.
- The in-store barcode and online code **ROOMSPRAY** are shown together, so the offer works in both channels.
- The hero product shot (festive bottles among ornaments) fits the holiday season.
- The top banner shows Rewards and free shipping over $50, and the footer has "Become a Rewards member" and "Sign up for texts."

## What's weak

- The email has three competing offers: $3.50 sprays, $2.75 Wallflowers and $14.95 candles. That dilutes the "final hours" focus.
- All three CTAs are the generic "Shop now" or "Shop now" variants, with no verb plus noun.
- The "Lowest price of the year" Wallflowers block repeats yesterday's "$2.75 Wallflowers start today" send. A subscriber has seen it already.
- The fine print is very long and tiny. It runs about as tall as the offer content and makes a simple deal feel conditional ("up to 24 per customer," code required).
- The "You might like" row is small and low-contrast. Its products (Fresh Balsam, Laundry Day, Winter Candy Apple) don't connect to the room-spray offer.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email to the single room-spray offer and a countdown element. Remove the Wallflowers and candle blocks, or push them well below a second room-spray CTA. The subject promises one thing, and the body should deliver only that.
- 2. Replace the generic "Shop now" with specific CTA copy such as "Shop $3.50 room sprays."
- 3. Show a few named scents at $3.50 in the hero, instead of only the three bottles.
- 4. Collapse the legal copy behind a "Details" link, or shorten it, so it doesn't take up a third of the email.
- 5. Don't re-run the same Wallflowers creative on consecutive days. Rotate in something new for the remaining slots.
