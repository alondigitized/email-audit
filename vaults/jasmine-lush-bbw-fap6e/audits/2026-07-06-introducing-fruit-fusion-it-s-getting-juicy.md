# introducing Fruit Fusion: it's getting juicy. 🍉🫐 🍊🍌
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- This is a product launch email introducing Bath & Body Works' new "Fruit Fusion" line. The creative leans into the name — bright, saturated editorial photography of fruit-themed body care products, models in summer-ready looks, and a color palette that screams "July." The brand is doing exactly what you'd expect after a week of deal-driven sends (the Semi-Annual Sale close, the $1 PocketBac, the $3.50 room spray): reset the narrative and introduce newness to recapture attention from the deal-fatigued segment.
- The structural problem is that this email is trying to do too much. It opens with a product-launch hero, pivots to a "Top Offers" product grid, then adds a "More to Love" row, and closes with a "Buy Online, Pickup in Store" callout — four distinct jobs in one send. None of them get enough visual real estate to dominate. The launch moment gets diluted before it lands.
- The email is clean, renders correctly, and the Fruit Fusion imagery is legitimately appealing. But the call to action is diffuse: "Shop Now" buttons appear against the hero, "Shop Offers" appears below, and a second "Shop Now" closes the pickup section. You exit without a clear mandate.

## What's working

- **Hero photography is strong.** The bold fruit-and-product styling communicates the new line's personality in a single glance — vibrant, summery, feminine without being exclusionary.
- **Product storytelling copy.** The trio of benefit callouts ("Pour fruity scents, Pack juicy benefits, Indulge in every drop") gives the line a real brand voice rather than generic filler.
- **Seasonal relevance is locked in.** A fruit-themed body care launch landing in July is perfectly timed to the season — no cognitive dissonance.
- **No render bugs.** Images load cleanly, text is legible, layout holds at thumbnail size.
- **"Top Offers" section adds a deal hook.** After a week of pure deal emails, leading with newness but backing it with a savings row is smart — it gives price-sensitive subscribers a reason to click even if they don't care about new products.

## What's weak

- **No concrete offer in the hero.** The launch section has zero pricing, no discount, no urgency — unusual after a week of "TODAY ONLY: $X" sends that trained subscribers to expect a number.
- **CTA copy is generic.** "Shop Now" tells you nothing. "Shop Fruit Fusion" or "Try the New Drop" would be vastly more specific and drive the launch narrative.
- **Too many modules compete for attention.** The Top Offers grid, the More to Love row, and the BOPIS callout all appear below the fold, fracturing attention rather than funneling it.
- **"Welcome to Watermelon" subhead is promising but tiny.** That's actually a great in-line headline — it just gets lost in the middle of the email at small size.
- **Subject line emoji overload diminishes clarity.** Four emojis in the subject line (🍉🫐🍊🍌) looks fun on desktop but reads as visual noise on mobile lock screens where truncation is brutal.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor the hero with a price or a launch incentive.** "New Fruit Fusion — 2 for $30" or "New arrivals: Buy 3, get 1 Free" would activate the deal-trained audience from the last week without cheapening the launch. The current all-imagery, no-price hero is a weaker choice after the brand spent July 2–5 conditioning subscribers to expect a number up front.
- 2. **Replace generic "Shop Now" CTA with launch-specific copy.** "Shop Fruit Fusion" or "Get Juicy" earns the subject line's tone. The current button is invisible.
- 3. **Cut the email to two modules.** Launch hero + Top Offers. Drop More to Love and the BOPIS module into next week's send — they're clutter here.
- 4. **Promote the "Welcome to Watermelon" callout.** That line has enough personality to be a subheading or a secondary card. Watermelon is a hero trend scent right now; give it room to breathe.
- 5. **Tighten the subject line to one or two emojis.** Four is too many.

- Two additional `<img>` tags with **empty `src`** and no `alt` — these are broken image references that will render as broken icons in most clients

**Preheader duplication** — the slot-based preheader (`data-key="gvhu7dvhmx9"`) is commented out entirely. A second hidden `<div class="preheader">` with different copy ("feat. Hilary Duff...") is the live preheader. The slot comment is dead weight; the commented-out slot suggests a template management mismatch.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Unresolved shell template tokens** — the string `` <!-- `date "+ET%m.%y"` --> `` appears **twice** in the HTML. This is a backtick shell substitution that was never evaluated; it should resolve to a month/year stamp (e.g., `ET07.26`) but is rendering as a literal comment. Harmless to recipients but indicates a broken build step.

**AMPScript logic exposed in comments:**
```html
<!--Adds daily APT set via ampscript. Adds preset APT for emails flagged NTF-->
<!--Set APT for NTF campaigns, otherwise respect email-defined APT-->
<!--If @NTF_Flag is "N", @APT should be set in the email; no default fallback here-->
```
These are visible in view-source. Not a deliverability risk, but they leak internal segmentation logic (APT = Audience Profile Tag, NTF = New To File). Standard practice is to strip or minify these before send.

### 7. Recommendations

1. **Fix the two broken `<img src="">` tags** — identify the missing image assets and populate or remove the tags.
