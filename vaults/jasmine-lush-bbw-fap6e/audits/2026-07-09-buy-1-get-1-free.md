# buy 1, get 1 free! 🌟🌟
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- Bath & Body Works opens July 9 with what should be a high-signal send — a BOGO on a new fall drop — but buries the lead under five competing offer blocks in a single scroll. The subject ("buy 1, get 1 free!") promises a clear deal but withholds the product category, so the inbox promise and the email body are misaligned. By the time you reach the Summer Sale 75%-off banner near the bottom, the email has lost its editorial point of view entirely.
- The "First Flicker of Fall" hero is legitimately good creative. The warm amber palette, the fall leaf, the candle imagery — it earns the early-season fall pivot B&BW has been telegraphing since 7/6. The problem is what comes immediately after: Body Mists, All-Day Moisture, $3.50 body care, and then a Summer Sale. These aren't supporting acts — they're competing headliners, and they dilute the fall story.
- Compared to 7/8's "ending soon: fall picks" (6/10), this one is arguably noisier. That send at least had a single SKU category — Wallflowers plugs — anchoring the fall message. This one opens with fall candles and then retreats to a summer clearance mentality within three scrolls.

## What's working

- **Hero is clean and seasonally surprising.** "First Flicker of Fall" with a BOGO badge lands well — the warm creative does earn the early-July fall tease.
- **BOGO is legible in the hero block.** The offer is rendered large and readable without hunting.
- **"STARTS TODAY!" urgency badge** appears on multiple sections, giving the email a live-event feel.
- **$3.50 body care pricing** in the "Hot Scents. Cool Prices." block is a strong anchor price — concrete and scroll-stopping.
- **Subject is short and explicit.** "buy 1, get 1 free!" is under 25 chars and states the mechanic directly.

## What's weak

- **Five competing offer blocks gut the conversion funnel.** BOGO → Buy 3 Get 3 → Lotion Collection → $3.50 Body Care → 75% Summer Sale is a mess — the reader has no clear action to take.
- **"Summer Sale up to 75%" contradicts the fall pivot.** B&BW has been trying to exit summer mode since 7/6; running Summer Sale copy in the same email as "First Flicker of Fall" is contradictory.
- **Subject doesn't say what's BOGO.** "Buy 1 get 1 free!" on what? Candles? Body mists? The category is invisible until you open — and even then, it applies to the fall launch specifically, not the whole store.
- **All CTAs say "SHOP."** Not one CTA is category-specific — no "Shop Fall Candles," no "Shop Body Mists." Every button is identical and generic.
- **No end date on any offer.** "STARTS TODAY!" without "ends Sunday" or "48 hours only" creates no urgency ceiling.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill four of the five offer blocks and let the BOGO breathe.** One email, one deal, one CTA. The fall candle BOGO is strong enough to carry a standalone send. The body mists and summer sale should be their own sends.
- 2. **Add a category to the subject.** "buy 1, get 1 free on fall candles 🕯️" is nine more characters and triples purchase intent at inbox.
- 3. **Give the BOGO an expiration date in the hero.** "Offer ends Sunday" eliminates the "I'll do it later" exit.
- 4. **Rename all CTAs to match the product.** "Shop Fall Candles," "Shop Body Mists" — generic "SHOP" wastes button real estate.
- 5. **Pull the Summer Sale entirely or relegate it to a thin footer strip.** It actively undermines the fall story that's been building since 7/6.
- **Subject Alt A:** `buy 1, get 1 free: first fall candles are here 🕯️`
- **Subject Alt B:** `fall candles early access — buy 1, get 1 free`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `First Flicker of Fall is BOGO — today only. Shop before it's gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The fall drop is here early. Grab 2 candles for the price of 1.`

All Movable Ink images and the static hero image are missing `alt`. For an image-heavy promotional email, this breaks the experience entirely when images are blocked (common in Outlook/corporate clients).

**Two images with empty `src`** — these will generate broken image icons in some clients. Likely unfired conditional content blocks.

**`@import` inside `<body>`** — a second `@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Source+Sans+Pro...')` appears inside a `<style>` block in the body (after the `<head>`). Many email clients strip or ignore `@import` in body `<style>` tags; this is a redundant/risky declaration.

**Structural note:** `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type">` appears after `</head>` open close of CSS — it's inside `<head>` but after `<style>`, which is valid but non-standard ordering.

**Outlook VML comment is unclosed** — the HTML comment reads `<!-- This code helps render code in 120dpi versions of Outlook */ -->` (note the stray `*/`). Not functional breakage, but indicates a copy-paste artifact.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unrendered merge token syntax (e.g., `%%variable%%`, `{{token}}`) is visible in the truncated source. AMPscript variable comments are present:

```html
<!--Adds daily APT set via ampscript. Adds preset APT for emails flagged NTF-->
<!--Set APT for NTF campaigns, otherwise respect email-defined APT-->
<!--If @NTF_Flag is "N", @APT should be set in the email; no default fallback here-->
```

The last comment is a risk flag: **no fallback is set for `@APT` when `@NTF_Flag = "N"`**. If a subscriber record lacks the expected APT value, the send-time personalization silently fails. Not visible in truncated HTML whether a fallback exists further down.

Movable Ink `mi_u` param is populated (`200511831233`) — subscriber ID is resolving correctly.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header — NOT FOUND** (QA warn). Required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender rules (>5K sends/day). If AgentMail relay is dropping it, the sending ESP (ExactTarget/SFMC) likely sets it at send time — but the relay must preserve it. If this header is absent in delivered mail, the email risks inbox placement degradation and Yahoo/Gmail bulk compliance failure.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header — NOT FOUND** (QA warn). RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail and Yahoo for bulk senders as of Feb 2024. Absence here means the one-click "Unsubscribe" button in Gmail UI will not function — subscribers must click through to a landing page instead. This is a compliance gap, not just a UX gap.

**`Authentication-Results` header — NOT FOUND** (QA warn). SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status is unknown. `e2.bathandbodyworks.com` is a dedicated SFMC sending domain — DKIM is almost certainly configured, but the AgentMail relay is not surfacing the results header for verification.

**CAN-SPAM:** Physical mailing address and unsubscribe mechanism are presumably present (standard for B&BW / SFMC sends) but not confirmed in truncated source. Should be verified in the full HTML footer.
