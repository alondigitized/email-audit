# $2, $3 & $4 mini must-haves are leaving. ✈️
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-01**
## Executive summary

- This is a clear, offer-led send. The "Final hours" banner sits over a $2 / $3 / $4 price ladder, with a "Shop now" button and a barcode and code (LITTLES) just below. The eye lands on the prices first, which is the right call. It is nearly the same creative and offer as this morning's "today only, $2, $3 & $4 mini must-haves. 🥰" send (7/10). Both emails land in the same inbox on the same day, so the "leaving" urgency feels recycled.
- The body keeps going after the hero with two more promos: festive 3-wick candles at $13.95, and Everyday Luxuries with buy 3 get 3 FREE. It then adds a "You might like" row, a brand-values block, a Rewards block and a long fine-print wall. These secondary promos compete with the hero, and none of them is the one clear job of the email. The hero headline "New scents to go, please" is cute but says nothing about price. The prices carry the weight.
- Overall it is a competent promo, but it adds little to the earlier send. I'd rate it a solid 7 for execution and a 6 for open and click, held back by cadence fatigue and the cluttered lower half.

## What's working

- The $2 / $3 / $4 ladder is big, stacked and scannable, with a category label under each price (hand cream & sanitizer sprays, mini body care, lip care).
- The dark "Final hours" banner gives credible, visible urgency that matches the subject's "leaving."
- The "Shop now" button is clear, and the in-store barcode plus "Online, use code LITTLES" makes redemption concrete.
- The product shot in the hero (minis, lip care, a Platinum tube) shows what the offer covers.
- The send holds together visually: no broken images, overlaps or layout bugs.

## What's weak

- It repeats this morning's $2/$3/$4 offer. A subscriber who got "today only" hours ago now sees "leaving" with the same prices.
- The hero headline "New scents to go, please" is vague. The offer is only legible in the price stack.
- The lower sections (3-wick candles at $13.95, buy 3 get 3 FREE, Everyday Luxuries) dilute the single hero offer. The Everyday Luxuries panel also uses a long, hard-to-read callout box over the product image.
- "Up to 24 per customer with code* LITTLES" is small. The code and its online-only requirement are easy to miss, and the long fine-print wall at the bottom undercuts the "honest offer" feel.
- Every CTA says "Shop now," so none names a product or category. The "Join Rewards" strip at the top is for non-members and offers no member price.

## Recommendations

- 1. Differentiate the late-day send. If the offer is the same, lead with what is actually different, such as "Last call," a countdown, or which items are nearly gone. Otherwise suppress it for anyone who already opened this morning's send.
- 2. Put the code in the hero as a button or chip ("Use code LITTLES") instead of below the fold of the hero.
- 3. Cut the lower promos to one secondary block, or move the candles and buy-3-get-3 to a separate send.
- 4. Make the CTAs specific, for example "Shop $2 hand care" or "Shop $4 lip care."
- 5. Replace "New scents to go, please" with a headline that carries the offer, such as "Minis from $2. Final hours."

The source is truncated, so the footer and most links were not inspected.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Click links not verifiable.** 46 tracking links on `click.e2.bathandbodyworks.com` were skipped by the HTTP probe, so destinations, redirect chains, and 404s are unconfirmed.
- **Insecure image source.** `http://cl.S7.exct.net/LiveContent.aspx?qs=ABB7InYiOjEsImQiOjUwMTZ9...` is a non-HTTPS dynamic-content image. Mail clients may block it or show mixed-content warnings, and it is unencrypted.
- **Open pixel is correct.** `https://click.e2.bathandbodyworks.com/open.aspx?...&bmt=0` is 1x1, has `alt=""`, and is wrapped in a `mso-hide:all` table. No issue.
- **Likely failing font request.** `@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Helvetica+Neue&display=swap')` requests a family that does not appear to be on Google Fonts. The request is likely wasted and the stack falls back to Helvetica/Arial. `@import` is also ignored by many clients, including Gmail.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text (6 images, QA-confirmed):**
  - `image.e2.bathandbodyworks.com/lib/fe9213737561047a72/m/1/` files: `4851c40c-…png`, `d6327aef-…png`, `00a9ac12-…png`, `7b7c94ef-…png`, `bbebdf75-…png`

- **Invalid document structure.** `<table class="stylingblock-content-wrapper">` elements and `<style>` blocks sit inside `<head>`. More tables sit between `</head>` and `<body>` (the "Begin variables" and "Preheader and Header" blocks). Parsers will hoist or reorder these, so style behavior varies by client.
- **Responsive hook not applied in the visible source.** The media queries target `.email-container`, but the 600px wrapper tables (`style="width: 600px;"`) have no such class. The rule may not fire, and the fixed 600px tables may not scale on mobile. This is based on the truncated source only.
