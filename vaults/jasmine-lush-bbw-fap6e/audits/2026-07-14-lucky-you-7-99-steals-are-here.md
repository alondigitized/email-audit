# lucky you: $7.99 steals are here. 🤗
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-14**
## Executive summary

- This is a kitchen-sink promo — B&BW stacks four separate offers into one scroll: $7.99 candles, Fruit Fusion BOGO 1 Free, All-Day Moisture "Shop the Collection," and a Two Pumps soap deal. The hero ($7.99 Select Candles) is the named offer from the subject, and it lands clearly at the top. But below that, the email immediately pivots into a product-feature mode that dilutes the urgency the subject line tried to build.
- The "Picked just for you!" personalization section and "Summer offer 75% off" badge give the middle of the email some energy, but the creative below that gets repetitive fast — "Top Offers," "More to Love," and footnote-heavy fine print all crowd the bottom third. This is a high-volume house doing what high-volume houses do: max surface area per send. It works for subscribers who scroll, but it punishes anyone who bounces after the first screen.
- Taken in context of the last six days of B&BW sends — $2.95 minis, $3.50 minis, BOGO candles, BOGO Fruit Fusion, Flash Sale mystery deals — this email feels like the fifth gear of an already-revved promo engine. The $7.99 candle price is new today, which is the one thing that saves it from feeling like a pure repeat.

## What's working

- **Hero is clean and on-brief.** "$7.99" is rendered large, the candle visual is warm and recognizable, and "Shop Today" CTA follows immediately — the offer is understood in under two seconds.
- **Subject-to-hero alignment.** The "$7.99 steals" promise in the subject is fulfilled by the first screen; there's no bait-and-switch on landing.
- **"Picked just for you!" section** injects a personalization signal mid-scroll — product thumbnails with names make the mid-email feel less generic.
- **Summer 75% off badge** adds a separate value anchor that catches the eye on scroll and layers in urgency beyond the candle deal.
- **Fruit Fusion BOGO carries over** from yesterday's scored send (July 13, 7/10) — if you missed it, you get a second shot; if you didn't buy, B&BW is betting recency works in their favor.

## What's weak

- **Four offers = no single offer.** Candles, Fruit Fusion, All-Day Moisture, and Two Pumps soap all compete for attention; the email has no editorial spine.
- **"All-Day Moisture" block is vague.** No price, no discount — just "Shop the Collection." It breaks the discount-anchor pattern the rest of the email establishes.
- **Bottom-half density is punishing.** "Top Offers," "More to Love," and "Favorites" panels stack in rapid succession with tiny thumbnails — on mobile, this reads as visual noise, not curated selection.
- **No urgency signal on the $7.99 deal.** The subject says "steals are here" but there's no end date, no countdown, no "today only" — the urgency is entirely manufactured by tone, not mechanics.
- **Brand history fatigue is real.** B&BW has sent candle promos on July 11, 12, and now 14. A subscriber paying attention has seen this creative framing three times in four days.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to one offer per email.** The $7.99 candle is a clean hero — let it breathe. Fruit Fusion already had its own send yesterday (July 13). Pulling it out of this email would sharpen the message and reduce scroll abandonment.
- 2. **Add a deadline to the $7.99 deal.** "Today only," "through Sunday," or even "while supplies last" gives the subscriber a reason to act now vs. saving it for later.
- 3. **Replace the "All-Day Moisture" block** with a single product at a visible price — or cut it entirely. A CTA with no offer is a dead zone in a promo email.
- 4. **Collapse "Top Offers" + "More to Love" + "Favorites"** into a single tighter grid. Three editorial carousels back-to-back reads as a template dump.
- 5. **Preheader:** Leverage the missing urgency in the body — put a deadline or quantity signal there.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$7.99 candles: today only. 🕯️`
- **Subject Alt B:** `your candle deal is waiting — $7.99 steals inside`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop Select Candles at $7.99 — offer ends tonight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Grab a $7.99 candle before they're gone — plus BOGO Fruit Fusion.`

**Duplicate Google Fonts import:** `Source Sans Pro` is loaded twice — once in `<head>` (weight 400 only) and once inline (weights 400 and 700). The first import is redundant and adds an unnecessary network request.

```html
<!-- In <head> -->
@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Source+Sans+Pro&display=swap');

<!-- Inline, later -->
@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Source+Sans+Pro:wght@400;700&display=swap');
```

`@import` inside `<style>` tags is blocked by Outlook and some older Gmail renderers — fallback to Arial/Helvetica is defined, so degradation is handled, but the duplicate import is unnecessary overhead.

**`<meta charset>` placement:** Declared after the MSO conditional comment block rather than as the first child of `<head>`. Not a breaking issue but best practice is to declare charset first.
