# starts tomorrow: $5.95…you don't want to ✨mist✨ this.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-31**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, no-gimmicks flash-sale email and it works because it doesn't try to be clever. "Fall starts with a spritz" plus a $5.95 hero shot of three full-size mists is exactly the kind of single, legible offer this brand nails when it's at its best — same energy as the 7/28 "$4.95 fan faves" send that scored a 9. The "STARTS TOMORROW" banner creates real anticipation rather than fake urgency, and the "Dermatologist approved" callout is a nice trust nudge that most fragrance mist ads skip.
- Where it loses points against that 7/28 benchmark: the CTA button says "START YOUR LIST" instead of something that drives an actual click to the sale, and the "Limit 24" detail is an odd thing to lead with — it reads like a restriction before the offer even lands. It's a solid, on-brand send, but it's a notch below the tightest version of this same playbook.

## What's working

- Single clear hero offer — $5.95 full-size mists — with no competing message fighting for attention.
- "STARTS TOMORROW!" banner sets credible, specific timing rather than vague urgency.
- Product photography is well-lit and on-theme (fall scents), consistent with brand voice.
- "Dermatologist approved" trust badge is a differentiator not seen in this brand's typical mist promos.
- Secondary nav (Top Offers grid: Fragrance, Body Care, Candles, etc.) gives a clean fallback path if the hero doesn't convert.

## What's weak

- Primary CTA "START YOUR LIST" is vague — it doesn't say "shop" or "buy," so it undersells the actual action.
- "Limit 24" is oddly prominent placement right under the price, which reads as a caveat instead of a hook.
- Because it "starts tomorrow," there's no way to act on this email today — it's a pure anticipation play with no immediate click path to the sale itself.
- Man in the "Football Season" body spray bottle breaks the "fall spritz" florals/fragrance framing a bit — three very different product types (fragrance mist, body spray) crammed into one hero shot dilutes the visual story.

## Recommendations

- 1. Swap the CTA copy from "START YOUR LIST" to something transactional and urgency-matched, e.g. "SHOP $5.95 MISTS" — right now the button undersells the actual offer sitting right above it.
- 2. Move "Limit 24" into smaller fine print near the terms at the bottom instead of directly under the price — it currently reads as a restriction, not a perk.
- 3. Since the sale doesn't start until tomorrow, add a one-line reminder mechanism ("we'll remind you" or a calendar-add link) or a "browse now, buy tomorrow" wishlist CTA to actually justify "START YOUR LIST" as the button text.
- 4. Simplify the hero shot to fragrance mists only, and give the deodorizing body spray its own row in the grid — the mixed product types blur the "spritz" narrative.
- **Subject Alt A:** `tomorrow only: $5.95 full-size mists (limit 24)`
- **Subject Alt B:** `set your alarm: $5.95 mists drop tomorrow`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stock up before they're gone — fall's best scents, $5.95 each.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Dermatologist-approved mists, full-size, starting at $5.95.`

Standard Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC) transactional/promotional template with table-based layout, MSO conditional comments, and an SFMC open-tracking pixel; QA scan returned a 57% pass rate with 0 confirmed issues and 3 warnings, mostly related to header visibility and missing alt text.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- Open tracking pixel present and correctly formed: `https://click.e2.bathandbodyworks.com/open.aspx?VCQUFXDZJW4UXHBMR2VAX6MUNA.70250&d=70250&bmt=0` (1x1, inside a `mso-hide:all` table).

- One flagged "image missing alt" is actually a third-party engagement/deliverability tracking pixel, not content: `https://bbwus.everestengagement.com/ea/fw0q51vctP/?e=200511831233&c=073026_EM_SU_BAU_PROMO_0_NRDM_595TBCMISTS_PREV_BAL_G`. - Two `<img>` tags have both empty `src` and no `alt` attribute (QA: `Image missing alt text: : src: ` ×2). Empty `src` can trigger spurious requests to the current page in some clients — worth locating and removing/fixing in the full source.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- Google Fonts `@import` for `Source+Sans+Pro` is declared twice — once in `<head>` (`family=Source+Sans+Pro`) and again inside a `stylingblock-content-wrapper` (`family=Source+Sans+Pro:wght@400;700`). Redundant, and moot in Outlook/Gmail, which strip `@import` entirely.
- Main promotional image `418d0db7-5a65-43cb-b5c9-9c6c142e4fd2.png` (served from `image.e2.bathandbodyworks.com`) has no `alt` text — confirmed by QA. With images blocked by default (Outlook, many Gmail configs), this renders as blank space with no fallback context.
- Layout tables correctly use `role="presentation"`, which is good practice for screen-reader table skipping.
- `<html lang="en">` is set correctly.
- Preheader is implemented via the standard hidden-div pattern (`display:none!important; mso-hide:all; font-size:1px`) plus a large block of zero-width padding characters (`&#847; &zwnj; &nbsp; &#8199; &shy;`) to suppress Gmail/Outlook auto-pulled preview text — correct technique, no issue. Note there's also a leftover empty AMPscript comment placeholder (`data-type="slot" data-key="gvhu7dvhmx9" data-label="Preheader"`) sitting unused above the real preheader — dead markup, not a rendering bug.
