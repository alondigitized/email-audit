# The CHANEL Métiers d’art 2026 Collection
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-10**
## Executive summary

- This is a brand-partner editorial push — CHANEL's Métiers d'art 2026 collection launch — and it reads exactly like one. NM handed the send over to the brand: cinematic hero photograph, two paragraphs of heritage copy explaining the Maisons d'art tradition, and three category CTAs. There is no offer, no promo code, no price, and no deadline. That's a deliberate choice, not a miss — CHANEL doesn't discount, and NM knows it.
- After a relentless promotional sprint (gift cards June 3–7, Beauty Event GWP, Designer Sale at 50% off), this editorial break is strategically sound. It signals curation over discounting and keeps the NM luxury positioning intact. But it comes at a cost: the subscriber who opened expecting a deal will bounce quickly.
- The execution is clean and on-brand. The photograph is genuinely beautiful — a model in leopard-print CHANEL in an ornate palatial hall — and the minimal black-and-white typography lets the image carry the email. For a luxury fashion subscriber this is a welcome pause from the promo noise. For anyone else, it's a pass.

## What's working

- Hero photograph is exceptional — palatial setting, full editorial quality, immediately signals luxury and newness.
- Typography hierarchy is disciplined: CHANEL in large bold, collection title smaller, body copy restrained. Eye travels correctly.
- Three distinct category CTAs ("SEE MORE READY-TO-WEAR," "SEE MORE ACCESSORIES," "SEE MORE SHOES") let the subscriber self-sort without landing on a generic homepage.
- Subject line is precise and non-spammy — brand + collection + year, under 40 characters.
- Clean render: no broken images, no overlapping text, no layout failure.

## What's weak

- Zero offer mechanic means click motivation relies entirely on editorial pull — it will convert only subscribers who were already looking for this collection.
- Body copy is brand boilerplate ("declaration of love to the artisans…") with no new information for anyone who follows CHANEL. It fills space without earning attention.
- "IN BOUTIQUES" is a weak value signal — it suggests in-store only, which could actively suppress clicks from subscribers who shop exclusively online.
- No product detail visible: no specific pieces called out, no price range, no styling context — the CTAs feel abstract.
- Preview text not confirmed visible in the render; if it leaks technical copy, the open rate is undercut before the editorial image can land.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one hero product with a price floor.** "CHANEL Métiers d'art — pieces from $X, in boutiques and online." You can be aspirational and still give the subscriber a reason to click. The current copy gives them zero purchase signal.
- 2. **Rewrite "IN BOUTIQUES" to "In Boutiques & Online."** If NM.com carries any Métiers d'art pieces, bury "boutiques only" is leaving online conversion on the table.
- 3. **Cut the second body paragraph.** The heritage copy is a brand advertorial; subscribers are here to shop. One sentence of context is enough; the second paragraph repeats the same idea and loses momentum before the CTAs.
- 4. **Make one CTA product-specific.** "SEE THE BAGS" beats "SEE MORE ACCESSORIES" for a subscriber who shops luxury accessories. Give her one specific anchor.
- 5. **Preheader should not repeat the subject.** If it's mirroring the subject line, replace it with a product hook.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Ready-to-wear, bags, and shoes from the new collection`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is built on Salesforce Marketing Cloud (AMPscript) with a table-based layout and multi-breakpoint responsive CSS. No rendering-blocking bugs were found in the truncated source, but the QA pass rate of 43% flags meaningful deliverability and compliance gaps — primarily around unsubscribe headers and authentication signal visibility through the AgentMail relay.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**34 tracking links skipped by QA probe.**
All click-redirect domains were excluded from HTTP probing, so destination URL validity (404s, redirect chains, expired landing pages) is **unverified**. Any broken links would be invisible in this report.

**Plain-text body is 70%+ URLs** (3,520 of 4,025 chars).
This is a near-certain indicator that the plain-text part was auto-generated from the HTML — common in SFMC but technically problematic. Spam filters (SpamAssassin, Google Postmaster) penalize plain-text versions that are URL-dense with no readable prose. This increases spam folder risk.

No issues found with link syntax in the truncated HTML portion.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Commented-out viewport meta tags** (3 alternates in `<head>`):
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
These are inert but indicate iterative debugging was left in production source. No rendering impact, but increases payload size and signals poor build hygiene.

**`maximum-scale=1` on active viewport tag** prevents pinch-to-zoom on iOS — a marginal accessibility concern for users with low vision, though common practice in email.

**`-webkit-animation: bugfix` iOS 9 hack** is present in the mobile media query. iOS 9 is a ~10-year-old OS; this adds dead code to every render.

**`<title>` has a trailing space:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` — minor, no functional impact.

**`mso-table-lspace/rspace: 0pt`** correctly applied on all tables — no Outlook ghost-spacing issues expected.

No missing `alt` attributes are visible in the truncated source, but the full image inventory is unaudited.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**AMPscript variable block is in an HTML comment**, not a `%%[ ]%%` code block:
```html
<!-- @user Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)) -->
```
If this is the actual production template (not a sanitized export), variable declarations inside HTML comments are **not executed** by SFMC. Variables would resolve as empty strings. The correct form is:
```
%%[
SET @user = Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)
]%%
```
This needs verification against the live template — if this is the rendered/sent output rather than the source template, the comment block is already a no-op and downstream personalization likely failed.

**`@user` override note is also commented out:**
```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_SHA256 */-->
```
The "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW" instruction suggests manual scenario selection was required pre-send. A missed toggle here would silently send the wrong identifier variant.

**`@uEm` and `@cmdid`** are declared in comments — same risk. If these drive any suppression logic or CRM event triggers, they may have fired against empty values.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not detected** (QA WARN).
CAN-SPAM does not strictly require this header, but Gmail and Yahoo Mail's 2024 bulk sender requirements mandate it for senders over 5,000 msgs/day. Neiman Marcus almost certainly crosses that threshold. Missing this header risks inbox demotion on both platforms.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not detected** (QA WARN).
One-click unsubscribe (the `POST` variant) is required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy. Without it, Gmail renders a slower multi-step unsubscribe flow and may suppress future sends.

**Root cause caveat:** Both warnings note the header "may not be captured by AgentMail relay" — these headers may exist on the actual SMTP envelope and simply not be forwarded through the monitoring relay. This should be verified against raw headers from a seed-address inbox test (Gmail, Outlook) before treating as a confirmed defect.

**Authentication-Results header not found** (QA WARN).
SPF and DKIM pass/fail status is unknown. The sending domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` is a dedicated ESP subdomain — DKIM should be provisioned there. Unverifiable via this relay capture.
