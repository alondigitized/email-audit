# Homepage snapshot · www.neimanmarcus.com · 2026-05-20
**Score:** 2/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- I opened Neiman Marcus on my phone and instead of the homepage I got a security block — "Access is temporarily restricted" — with a list of reasons why I might be a bot. I am a 51-year-old InCircle member with a black card who has been shopping here for twenty years, and the site just called me suspicious. The brand looks paranoid, not premium.

## What's working

- The Neiman Marcus wordmark loads cleanly at the top — I at least know I'm in the right place
- "Submit feedback" link is present, so there is technically an escape hatch

## What's weak

- The entire page is a bot-detection wall — an InCircle member on her personal iPhone hitting the NM homepage should never see this
- The copy is clinical and accusatory: "unusual activity," "rapid taps or clicks," "automated (bot) activity" — this reads like a network error page, not a luxury brand
- No InCircle session recognition — if I were logged in, this should not be happening
- Zero brand warmth; the olive/gray background peeking around the modal looks like a cached fragment, not intentional design
- There is no auto-retry, no redirect to a CAPTCHA I can solve, no path forward except a feedback link that goes nowhere obvious

## Recommendations

- **Whitelist logged-in InCircle accounts from bot-detection hard blocks** — a paying member with an active session should never hit this wall; show a soft CAPTCHA at worst
- **Replace the clinical error copy with brand-appropriate language** — something like "We're verifying your connection — hang tight" with the NM brand voice, not a firewall readout
- **Add a direct phone/chat support link** — "Call your stylist" or "Chat with us" so a blocked high-value customer has an immediate human path instead of a feedback form
- **Log and flag these friction events for InCircle accounts** — if a top-tier member bounces here, the stylist program should know and follow up
