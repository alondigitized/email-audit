# Lafayette 148 New York celebrates 30 years
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-08**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, expensive-looking editorial send with no offer. A tall hero shows a model in a long black leather shirtdress with fringe, under a serif "Luxe Leather" headline. Two outlined buttons sit below it, then a 2x3 grid of Lafayette 148 pieces. It looks like Neiman Marcus and renders without bugs.
- The subject promises "celebrates 30 years," but the body never mentions an anniversary. The email is really a leather and fall outerwear pitch. Someone who opens for the milestone finds no story, no date and no reason to care about the 30 years. After the Nov 7 "Things We Love" send (5/10, also offer-free) and the Nov 8 "Limited time: up to $650 off" email (7/10), this one gives a subscriber no price or deadline to act on.
- Judgement: pretty, but it is a lookbook with a mismatched subject line. It will get brand-loyal opens and few clicks.

## What's working

- The hero is large and the black leather reads instantly as fall luxury, so the eye lands on it first.
- "Luxe Leather" is a clear headline and fits the November season.
- The two stacked outlined buttons are legible, and "Shop Lafayette 148 New York" is a clear verb-plus-noun CTA.
- The 2x3 product grid has consistent grey tiles, and the mix of leather, knit, plaid skirt and shearling coat gives range.
- Nothing is broken: no overlapping text, no missing images, and the Neiman Marcus wordmark and nav are crisp.

## What's weak

- The subject's "30 years" hook is not in the body, so the email doesn't pay off the subject.
- There is no offer, no price, no gift-with-purchase and no urgency. Nothing says why to click today.
- Every grid caption reads "Lafayette 148 New York," with no product name, price or detail, so the six tiles are indistinguishable by text.
- On mobile the CTAs sit below a very tall hero, so the buttons are likely below the fold.
- The second button, "Shop Designer Collections," is generic and pulls attention from the brand the email is about.
- The footer's "View in a Web Browser" link sits at the very bottom, and the add-to-address-book line is likely what the inbox shows as preview text.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the subject and body tell the same story. Either add a "30 years of Lafayette 148" strip with a short line of heritage, or drop the anniversary from the subject and lead with leather.
- 2. Add product names and prices under every grid tile, such as "Shearling coat, $2,xxx," and link each tile to its product page.
- 3. Put one CTA directly under the headline and move the hero's buttons up, so they appear without scrolling on mobile. Cut "Shop Designer Collections."
- 4. Add a hook such as new arrivals, a complimentary tailoring or styling offer, or early access, to give a reason to click now.
- 5. Set a real preheader instead of letting footer text leak.

# Technical Audit: "Lafayette 148 New York celebrates 30 years" (Neiman Marcus)

**Scope note:** The HTML source was truncated after the `<style>` blocks. The body, links, alt text, footer and unsubscribe markup were not available. Anything I couldn't see is marked "not verifiable" rather than flagged.

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible markup is a standard table-based ESP template. The two real findings are an ESP template comment left in the delivered HTML and a viewport tag that blocks zoom. The automated QA's four warnings are inconclusive, and several look like artifacts of the AgentMail relay.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **URL-heavy plain-text part.** `[WARN] Plain-text is >70% URLs: 2840/3325 chars are URLs`. This is about 85% URLs. It suggests the text alternative is mostly raw tracking URLs, which can add to spam-filter scoring. Because the HTML source is truncated, I can't tell whether the HTML link count is what drives it.
- No broken links were confirmed.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom blocked.** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` prevents pinch-zoom on clients that honor it. This conflicts with WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
- **Checkbox hamburger nav.** The CSS uses a checkbox-hack hamburger menu (`.navbar-checkbox:checked~.hamburger`, `.menu`) inside `@media (max-width: 600px)`. Gmail and several Outlook clients don't support this pattern. The no-support fallback is not visible in the truncated source.
- **Fragile selectors.** The mobile rules use exact-match attribute selectors such as `*[class=contenttable]` and `*[class=hide]`. They stop matching if an element carries more than one class.
- **Not verifiable:** image alt text, the `lang` attribute, link text, and the plain-text/HTML parity.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **ESP template comment shipped in the delivered HTML.** The source opens with `<!--REQUIRED variables: @email ... @base_refid ... Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", ...) ... Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm", ...) CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW --> <!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->`.
  - This is developer scaffolding that wasn't stripped at send time.
  - It exposes internal data-extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`) and field names (`uEm`, `cmdid`).
  - It also adds avoidable bytes toward clipping thresholds.
  - It doesn't render, and the variables sit inside comments, so nothing is visibly unresolved.
- I saw no unresolved tokens in the visible markup. The body, where tokens would appear, is truncated.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication)
- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post.** Both headers are reported as not found, so one-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058) can't be confirmed. The QA note says the relay may not capture these headers, so this is **inconclusive, not a confirmed defect**. Check the original message at the ESP.
- **Authentication-Results.** The header is absent, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. This is an expected relay artifact. The `From` domain is `e.neimanmarcus.com`, a dedicated sending subdomain.
- **Footer.** The visible unsubscribe link and the physical postal address sit in the truncated footer and are not verifiable.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
Not verifiable. The 15 tracking links were not resolved, and no UTM or `refid` parameters or landing-page URLs are visible in the truncated source. The comment block references `@refid`/`@base_refid`, so campaign IDs are likely carried in the tracking URLs. That is unconfirmed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip the `<!--REQUIRED variables ... -->` and `<!--/* SET @user ... */ -->` comments at send time. This removes the internal DE names and saves bytes.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag, or at least avoid `user-scalable=no`.
3. Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and the SPF/DKIM/DMARC results from the original ESP message or a seed-inbox test, since the relay stripped them here.
4. Re-run the audit on the full HTML. That would let you resolve the 15 tracking links, check alt text and the footer, and see whether UTM or `refid` parameters reach the landing pages.
5. Shorten the plain-text part, or make sure its link text is meaningful, instead of mostly raw tracking URLs.
6. Test the hamburger nav in Gmail and Outlook, and confirm the no-support fallback keeps navigation usable.
