# Shop Black Friday now: up to $1,500 off your purchase (includes beauty & fragrance)
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-26**
## Executive summary

- This is the cleanest offer Neiman Marcus has sent this week. The black hero with the red frame puts "UP TO $1,500 OFF" in huge serif type. A spend-and-save table sits right under the SHOP NOW button, and the code THANKFUL is stated up front. You know the deal in two seconds. The "Includes Beauty & Fragrance" line is the only new beat compared with the 11/24 "Shop early & get up to $1,500 off your purchase" send, which used the same creative.
- It is also the fourth Black Friday send in three days. The 11/25 "Want up to $1,500 off? Plus, extra 25% off & The Designer Sale is on now" email already stacked these same three offers. Below the hero, this one repeats them: a nine-product grid, six category buttons, a red Designer Sale block, and a 25%-off block. Three offers in one email dilutes the lead message, and a subscriber has seen this layout several times already.
- Verdict: strong, readable execution, but it is repetitive and the subject is bloated. The mid-email grid has no prices, so nothing moves a shopper to click a specific product.

## What's working

- The hero is unmissable. The huge "$1,500 OFF" on black with a red frame wins the eye first.
- The spend/save table (spend $250 save $50, up to spend $5,000 save $1,500) makes the offer concrete and rewards bigger baskets.
- The promo code "THANKFUL" is in the hero, so there is no hunting for it.
- "Includes Beauty & Fragrance" is a useful callout, since beauty is often excluded from these events.
- The six category buttons (Beauty, Women's Clothing, Women's Shoes, Handbags, Men's, Men's Shoes) give easy routing. The layout has no visible render bugs.

## What's weak

- The subject is 83 characters and will truncate on mobile. "(includes beauty & fragrance)" is the part most likely to get cut off.
- The email stacks three offers: $1,500 off, Designer Sale up to 60% off, and extra 25% off for up to 70% off. The extra 25% block is almost identical to the 11/25 "Black Friday alert" send, so the message gets muddy.
- The product grid has no prices and no product names, only brand labels. Nothing shows what the discount does for a specific item.
- The real deadline (ends 11/28 at 10 PM CT) is buried in tiny fine print. The hero only says "Limited Time," which is weak urgency.
- The tiny exclusions text is a long block, and "Exclusions Apply" appears three times. That undercuts how honest the offer feels.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the deadline in the hero: "Ends Friday 11/28, 10 PM CT." It is the cheapest lift available.
- 2. Cut the email to one offer. Move the Designer Sale and extra-25% blocks to a separate send, or collapse them into one slim footer strip.
- 3. Add prices and "after savings" examples to the product tiles. For example: "Spend $1,000, save $250," shown on a hero product.
- 4. Tighten the subject to under 50 characters and keep "beauty" if it is the segment hook.
- 5. Vary the creative from the 11/24 and 11/25 sends. Add something that changes, such as a countdown or a "last days" treatment.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Black Friday: save $250 when you spend $1,000`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Use code THANKFUL. Beauty & fragrance count. Ends Friday at 10 PM CT.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Spend $500, save $125. Spend $5,000, save $1,500. Code THANKFUL.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible HTML source is a table-based XHTML Transitional template with a hamburger-menu checkbox hack. It has an internal AMPscript/data-extension comment leaking into the delivered markup and a zoom-blocking viewport tag. The 4 QA warnings are all header or relay-capture limitations, not confirmed sending defects. The source was truncated, so the footer, body links and plain-text part were not available for inspection.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified links:** 31 tracking links were skipped for HTTP probing because they are click-redirect domains. Destination status, redirect chain depth and final landing URLs are unverified.
- **Plain-text part:** 5862 of 7785 characters (~75%) are URLs, over the 70% threshold. This is typical of long tracking URLs in a multipart alternative, and it can hurt spam scoring.
- **No broken or malformed links confirmed** in the visible source.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom blocked:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` prevents pinch-zoom on mobile, which fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
- **Missing language:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang` attribute, so screen readers can't pick a pronunciation profile.
- **Title:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has a trailing space and is generic.
- **Fragile media queries:** exact-match attribute selectors such as `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=mobile]` and `*[class=hide]` only match elements whose entire `class` attribute equals that value. Any element with multiple classes will silently skip the mobile rules.
- **Global paragraph rule:** `p { margin: 0 auto; text-align: center; }` forces centered text for every `<p>`, unless each one is overridden inline.
- **Hamburger menu:** the nav depends on `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu`. This is unsupported in Gmail and some other clients, and the visible source shows no fallback.
- **Legacy hacks:** `.ReadMsgBody` and `-webkit-animation: bugfix` are harmless but dead weight.
- **Not assessable:** image `alt` text, since it fell outside the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Leaked internal data:** the delivered HTML begins with a comment containing the ESP's internal logic: `@email`, `@base_refid`, `Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", ...)`, `Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm"/"cmdid", ...)`, and `SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256`. This exposes data-extension names, field names and the hashing approach to every recipient. It should be stripped at send time or moved into server-side AMPscript.
- **Comment before DOCTYPE:** this can trigger quirks mode in some legacy renderers.
- **Unresolved tokens:** none seen in the visible portion, but the `@uEm`, `@cmdid` and `@user` usage can't be confirmed resolved in link parameters because the body was truncated.

## 5. Compliance
- **[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header not found.** The QA note attributes this to the AgentMail relay possibly not capturing it. This is not confirmed missing at the sender.
- **[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) not found.** One-click unsubscribe support is unconfirmed. Gmail and Yahoo require it for bulk senders.
- **[WARN] Authentication-Results not found.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. The sender is `NeimanMarcus@e.neimanmarcus.com`, a subdomain, so alignment should be checked on the original message.
- **Not assessable:** the footer's visible unsubscribe link and physical postal address were not in the truncated source.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
- **Not verifiable:** no UTM parameters were visible in the truncated source. The 31 links were not probed, so the redirect destination, parameter preservation and landing-page alignment with the "up to $1,500 off" offer are unconfirmed.
- **Likely `@refid` mechanism:** the header comment suggests `@refid` (derived from `@base_refid`) is used for campaign attribution instead of standard UTMs. This is inference, not confirmed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Strip the AMPscript comment** (the leaked `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated` and `NM_CRM_Feed` lookups) from the rendered output, and keep the DOCTYPE as the first line.
2. **Remove `maximum-scale=1`** from the viewport meta tag.
3. **Add `lang="en"`** to `<html>` and tighten the `<title>`.
4. **Switch to `[class~=...]` or class selectors** so multi-class elements match the mobile rules. Provide a non-checkbox fallback for the hamburger nav.
5. **Verify headers on the original message** (not the relay copy): `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, and SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment.
6. **Re-run the QA** with the full HTML and resolved click-tracking redirects. That will cover the footer, `alt` text, UTM or `@refid` propagation and landing-page status.
7. **Shorten the plain-text part's URLs** (or reduce link count) to bring URL density under 70%.
