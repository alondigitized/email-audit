# Wedding-guest elegance
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-25**
## Executive summary

- This is a pure editorial wedding-season send with no offer. A black strapless gown in a moody ballroom hero sits over a thin serif headline, "Take Your Wows Seriously." The image is gorgeous and the brand voice is confident. It's also a different kind of email from the last week of Neiman Marcus promos. The Oct 24 gift-card send and the Oct 23 and Oct 25 Fall Style Sale emails all had a number to react to. This one has none.
- The weakness is commercial. The headline is clever but vague, and the two buttons, "Shop Wedding" and "Shop Dresses," are thin outlined boxes with light gray text. No product is named, no price is shown and no deadline is given. The men's section is a nice inclusive touch, but its link is a small underlined text line. A wedding guest who needs an outfit in a few weeks gets mood, not a reason to act today.
- Verdict: a polished brand-building email that won't drive much click volume. It reads like a lookbook rather than a send with a job to do.

## What's working

- The hero image is striking and on-theme. The black gown with the white sweetheart neckline is memorable.
- The layout is clean and uncluttered. The eye goes from logo to hero to headline to buttons in order.
- The send covers both women and men with a men's tuxedo-jacket section, which widens the audience.
- The footer utilities (personal style advisor, app download, find a store) add some service value.
- The serif headline matches the luxury positioning.

## What's weak

- There is no offer, price or incentive anywhere. Nothing is time-bound.
- The CTA buttons are low-contrast outlined boxes with pale gray text, so they look weak and easy to miss.
- No product detail is visible. There's no designer name, no price and no product callout on either hero.
- "Take Your Wows Seriously" is clever but says nothing about what's inside.
- The men's CTA is a small underlined text link, and the copy "Jilt the fashion mainstays" is oddly worded.
- The send gives no sense of urgency, which matters for wedding season.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a concrete hook, such as an offer, a free-alterations or free-shipping perk, or a "Order by [date] to arrive in time" deadline. Without a hook, wedding intent has no reason to convert now.
- 2. Make the buttons solid black with white text and more specific copy, such as "Shop Wedding-Guest Dresses" and "Shop Black-Tie Menswear."
- 3. Name the hero look with a designer and price, and link it directly to that product.
- 4. Split the women's section into two or three shoppable pieces, such as dress, shoe and clutch, so there's something concrete to click.
- 5. Rewrite the men's copy to be plainer and give the link button treatment.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- No `href` values appear in the truncated source, so I can't confirm link structure or UTM presence.
- The plain-text part is 3216 of 3722 characters URLs (about 86%), against the QA threshold of 70%. That is consistent with long tracking URLs in the text alternative. It can raise spam-filter scoring and makes the text version hard to read.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom blocked:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` disables pinch-zoom on mobile. This fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
- **Comment before DOCTYPE:** the template comment sits before `<!DOCTYPE html PUBLIC ...>`. Content ahead of the doctype can trigger quirks mode in legacy IE-based renderers.
- **Hamburger nav relies on a checkbox hack:** `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu` and `.mobileCont:checked~table` need `:checked` sibling support. Gmail and some other clients don't support it, and the fallback can't be seen in the truncated source. Treat it as a risk, not a confirmed defect.
- **Brittle class selectors:** the rules use exact-match selectors such as `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=mobile]` and `*[class=hide]`. They fail if any element carries a second class.
- **Generic title:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` is generic and has a trailing space.
- **Alt text and tap targets:** I can't assess these because the body markup is truncated.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unresolved merge tokens appear in rendered content in the visible source.
- The leaked comment shows the personalization inputs. These include `@email`, `@emailid`, `@uEm`, `@cmdid` and a `SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256` scenario. Whether hashed identifiers (`uEm`, `cmdid`) end up in link query strings can't be confirmed from the truncated source.
- The comment exposes internal Marketing Cloud data extension names, "NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated" and "NM_CRM_Feed", along with their lookup fields. This is information leakage and a hygiene issue.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe not found:** QA flagged it as possibly not captured by the AgentMail relay. It is unverified, not confirmed absent.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) not found:** QA flagged this too, with the same relay caveat. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require one-click unsubscribe, so it should be checked at the source.
- **Authentication-Results not found:** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. The sending domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` can be checked directly via DNS and original headers.
- **Footer not visible:** the physical address and in-body unsubscribe link are in the truncated portion, so I can't confirm them.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)

- The comment mentions `@refid` and `@base_refid` (campaign-calendar IDs). These suggest a `refid`-style tracking parameter instead of standard UTMs, but that is unconfirmed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip the template and developer comment (the REQUIRED variables block and its DE lookups) from the production send. Put the doctype first.
2. Change the viewport to `width=device-width, initial-scale=1` and drop `maximum-scale=1`.
3. Verify `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` in the raw headers from the sending platform, not the relay. Confirm SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment for `e.neimanmarcus.com`.
4. Shorten the URLs in the plain-text alternative or reduce the number of links in it, to bring the URL ratio under 70%.
5. Test the hamburger and checkbox navigation in Gmail and Outlook to confirm it degrades to a usable fallback. Use class-contains selectors (`[class~=...]`) instead of exact-match ones.
6. Re-run the audit with the full, untruncated HTML. Probe the 17 redirect links through to their final landing pages to check parameters and continuity.
