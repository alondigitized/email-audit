# Summer-ready styles are going fast!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- This is the **fifth-plus Designer Sale email from Neiman Marcus in less than a week** — the sale launched around 5/17, NM followed with urgency on 5/18, spotlighted Givenchy/Ferragamo on 5/19, added new designers on 5/21, and now comes back with this send. The creative team clearly felt the "Designer Sale" subject line was spent, so they pivoted to "Summer-ready styles are going fast!" — a deliberate refresh move. The problem: the email body is *entirely* The Designer Sale, so the subject and hero are living in different campaigns. A subscriber who opens expecting summer newness gets a sale banner.
- Inside the email, the execution is solid NM boilerplate. The red hero lands the offer cleanly — "UP TO 50% OFF / LIMITED TIME / SHOP SALE" is impossible to miss. The nine-product grid (Zimmermann, Loewe, Valentino Garavani, Rag & Bone, Amiri) gives the sale visual credibility. The six category CTAs below are exhaustive but not elegant.
- The verdict: a competent, low-risk send that will convert its loyal Designer Sale shoppers on autopilot, but it's misfiring on acquisition and wasting subject-line real estate that could have extended reach to fence-sitters.

## What's working

- **Hero is unambiguous.** Red field, bold all-caps "THE DESIGNER SALE / UP TO 50% OFF," "SHOP SALE" button — offer lands in under two seconds.
- **Product grid does the selling.** Nine branded items (Zimmermann skirt, Loewe denim jacket, Valentino heels) give the sale credibility without relying on copy.
- **Category CTAs are comprehensive.** Six buttons (women's clothing, shoes, handbags, dresses, men's, men's shoes) reduce navigation friction for subscribers who know what they want.
- **Clean render, no bugs.** Layout holds, images load, no overlapping text or broken gaps.
- **"LIMITED TIME" + "before they're gone" scarcity is double-reinforced** — hero badge plus body subhead, consistent.

## What's weak

- **Subject/body mismatch.** "Summer-ready styles" primes expectations of newness; opening to the same Designer Sale banner a repeat subscriber has seen four times this week is a small betrayal.
- **No individual price callouts on products.** A 50% off sale email with nine product images showing zero prices or original/sale price pairs leaves money on the table — "Zimmermann skirt, was $595, now $297" would drive far more urgency.
- **No InCircle/loyalty hook.** NM's highest-LTV customers are InCircle members; zero member-exclusive framing (early access, extra points) on a fifth-send sale email is a missed retention opportunity.
- **App and "Watch with a personal style advisor" sections feel like footer filler.** They appear immediately below the primary content and dilute the urgency the hero worked hard to establish.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** This is email five of the Designer Sale arc — without a meaningfully new hook (new brands, deeper discount, last-chance deadline), the open and click curves have been declining since the 5/19 peak.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Align the subject to a real news hook — or be honest about urgency.** The "going fast" angle works, but only if the body delivers scarcity evidence: "X% of sale styles sold through" or "final 48 hours." Right now the body copy doesn't support the subject's implied scarcity.
- 2. **Add sale prices to at least 2-3 hero products.** "Zimmermann • $349 (was $625)" beneath the product image turns browsing into buying intent.
- 3. **Lead with a last-chance or deadline date.** "Sale ends Sunday" in the hero subhead gives "going fast" real teeth.
- 4. **Drop the app section to footer.** It interrupts the purchase flow.
- 5. **Add an InCircle callout for members.** A single line — "InCircle members: earn triple points on sale styles through Sunday" — raises retention value for zero incremental inventory.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Up to 50% off — prices drop further as inventory clears`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Loewe, Valentino, Rag & Bone — shop before sizes sell out`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Summer-ready styles are going fast!"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a Salesforce Marketing Cloud AMPscript setup with standard XHTML table layout. Four QA warnings were raised: missing `List-Unsubscribe` headers, unknown SPF/DKIM authentication status, 26 unprobed tracking links, and a plain-text part that is 87% URLs — a meaningful deliverability risk.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 tracking links skipped (click-redirect domain)**
All CTAs route through a click-redirect domain (Neiman's ESP redirect service). QA probing was skipped for these, so broken destinations, redirect loops, or UTM misconfiguration cannot be confirmed without manual spot-checks.

**Plain-text part is 87% URLs (5,095 of 5,865 chars)**
The plain-text alternative is nearly all raw URLs with little surrounding prose. Spam filters treat text-heavy-with-URLs plain-text parts as a strong spam signal. Plain-text should mirror readable content from the HTML version.

**`@refid` concatenation is a no-op**
```
@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid, "")
```
The second argument is an empty string — `@refid` equals `@base_refid` exactly. If this was intended to append a suffix (e.g., a deployment variant tag), the value is missing. Any link that uses `@refid` for attribution will silently attribute to the wrong campaign identifier if `@base_refid` itself is wrong.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**No `lang` attribute on `<html>`**
```html
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">
```
Missing `lang="en"`. Screen readers default to system language; WCAG 3.1.1 requires page language to be declared.

**`maximum-scale=1` disables user zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">
```
Prevents users from pinching to zoom on mobile. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) violation. Use `minimum-scale=1` instead, or drop the `maximum-scale` constraint entirely.

**Multiple commented-out viewport tags**
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
Dead code. Not breaking, but indicates stale template management. The `user-scalable=no` variant is also a WCAG violation if ever re-enabled.

**iOS 9 webkit animation hack**
```css
body { -webkit-animation: bugfix infinite 1s; }
@-webkit-keyframes bugfix { from { padding: 0; } to { padding: 0; } }
```
iOS 9 is over a decade old. This fires an infinite animation on every render. No practical effect today but adds unnecessary noise to paint cycles on modern clients.

**`<title>` has trailing whitespace**
```html
<title>Neiman Marcus </title>
```
Minor. Some clients expose this in tab labels or accessibility trees.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Ambiguous `@user` variable — commented-out scenario not resolved**
The template comment says "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW" and then immediately contains:
```
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */-->
```
followed by the Lookup-based assignment. It is unclear which scenario is active. If the SHA256 branch should be live but remains commented out, `@user` resolves to `EMAIL_ID` (a numeric DB key), not a hash — this would corrupt any identity-based targeting or pixel/personalization that expects a hashed value downstream.

**Silent Lookup failures**
Four Lookup calls (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed` ×2) will return empty string on miss, not an error. If `@uEm`, `@cmdid`, or `@emailid` are used in links or personalization blocks without null-guards (`IIF(EMPTY(@uEm), ...)`) they will inject blank values into URLs silently.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent**
QA found no `List-Unsubscribe` header captured at the relay. Gmail and Yahoo (since February 2024 bulk sender requirements) require this header for senders over 5,000/day. Its absence risks messages being routed to spam or the unsubscribe button being suppressed in Gmail UI.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) absent**
One-click unsubscribe (the POST mechanism required by Gmail/Yahoo 2024 policy) is not present. This is now a hard requirement for bulk senders. Without it, Gmail falls back to redirect-based unsubscribe, which is slower and not compliant with current policy.

**SPF/DKIM status unknown**
```
Authentication-Results header not found: Expected via AgentMail relay — SPF/DKIM status unknown
```
The relay did not forward or expose authentication results. Cannot confirm DMARC alignment. The sending domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` should have a valid DKIM signature and SPF record; this should be verified against raw headers from an inbox delivery, not the relay capture.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Cannot verify UTM parameters on 26 tracking links**
All outbound links were skipped by the probe. UTM completeness (source, medium, campaign, content) and landing page alignment cannot be confirmed without spot-checking decoded redirect destinations. Given the `@refid` no-op issue above, campaign attribution is suspect.

**`@uEm` / `@cmdid` in link parameters — null injection risk**
If these CRM Feed values are appended to landing page URLs (common for personalization or A/B logic) and either Lookup returns empty, the destination URL will contain a dangling `?uEm=&cmdid=` which breaks analytics segmentation.
