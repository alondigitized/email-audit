# Just in: The Holiday Wishlist
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-03**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, on-brand holiday gift guide with no offer. The hero is strong: red condensed "THE HOLIDAY WISHLIST" over a model stepping out of a black convertible with red gift bags on the back. It looks like Neiman Marcus and says "holiday" immediately. The structure is simple, a her block and a him block, each with three designer products and one link.
- The weakness is commercial. After the Nov 1 and Nov 3 "25% off one item" sends, this reads as a soft pivot back to editorial. Nothing here gives a reason to click today. The links are small underlined text ("Shop Gifts for Her", "Shop Gifts for Him", "Shop Our Favorite Gifts"), not buttons, and the product tiles show no prices. The large lifestyle photo in the middle takes up a lot of space and does no selling.
- Verdict: it is polished and seasonally right, but low-urgency and low-conversion. It would work as a brand-warming send. It would not drive revenue next to the 25% off emails.

## What's working

- The hero is on season and eye-catching. The red headline against the neutral backdrop and the red gift bags tie together well.
- The six named designers (Saint Laurent, Bottega Veneta, Christian Louboutin, Gucci, David Yurman, Wolf x WM Brown) signal luxury and give recognizable entry points.
- The her/him split is easy to scan. Each block has one headline and one link.
- The layout renders cleanly, with no broken images or overlapping text.
- The footer is tidy, with app download, style advisor, store finder and account links.

## What's weak

- There is no offer, price or deadline. The email gives no reason to act now, especially after the 25% off sends on Nov 1–3.
- The CTAs are small underlined text links. "Shop Gifts for Her" and "Shop Gifts for Him" are easy to miss, and no button is visible.
- The large mid-email lifestyle photo takes up about a screen of scroll and is not shoppable.
- The product tiles show only the designer name, with no product names or prices, so there is little detail to act on.
- The hero's secondary copy and link ("The gifts they're dreaming of…") are small and sit in the top-right corner, away from the headline.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a concrete hook, such as a gift-card or shipping-deadline callout, or tie the send to the 25% offer. At minimum, add a "Order by Dec 15 for holiday delivery" line under the hero.
- 2. Turn the text links into real buttons, such as "Shop Gifts for Her" and "Shop Gifts for Him", and make the hero CTA a button.
- 3. Cut or shrink the middle lifestyle image and replace it with a second row of products that have prices.
- 4. Make each product tile clickable with a name and price (for example, "Saint Laurent card case, $295").
- 5. Add a gifting-by-price row (under $100, $250, $500) to widen the set of buyers.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Designer picks for everyone on your list, wrapped and ready.`

# Technical Audit: Neiman Marcus, "Just in: The Holiday Wishlist"

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible HTML is a table-based XHTML Transitional template with a checkbox-hack mobile nav. It has two confirmed code issues: leaked SFMC/AMPscript documentation and a zoom-blocking viewport tag. The four QA warnings are mostly capture or relay limitations, not confirmed defects. The HTML source was truncated, so anything past the `<style>` blocks is unverified.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified links:** 16 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the HTTP probe (QA INFO). Their destinations, redirect chains and status codes are unverified. This is not evidence of breakage.
- **URL-heavy plain text:** QA reports the plain-text part is 3012/3501 characters of URLs (about 86%). This points to long redirect-wrapped URLs in the text alternative. It can hurt text-part spam scoring and readability, but the cause is not confirmed from the truncated source.
- No other link issues can be confirmed from the available data.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom on mobile. This fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
- **Leaked internal comment:** The HTML opens with an un-stripped developer comment: `<!--REQUIRED variables: @email ... Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", ...) ... "NM_CRM_Feed" ... CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW -->`. It exposes internal data extension names and lookup fields in the delivered markup. It also adds weight and shows the template was not cleaned before send. Three dead commented-out `<meta name="viewport">` variants remain as well.
- **Checkbox-hack nav:** The hamburger menu uses `.navbar-checkbox:checked ~ .menu` inside `@media (max-width: 600px)`. This is unsupported in Gmail (app and web) and in several Outlook clients. The source shows no fallback, so navigation may be unreachable there. I can't confirm this because the body is truncated.
- **Fragile class selectors:** Exact-match selectors like `*[class=contenttable]` and `*[class=mobile]` fail if an element carries more than one class. This is a minor risk.
- **Document metadata:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has trailing whitespace. The truncated source shows no `lang` attribute on `<html xmlns=...>`, which is an accessibility gap for screen readers. Alt text and role attributes can't be assessed.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- The only tokens visible (`@email`, `@refid`, `@user`, `@emailid`, `@uEm`, `@cmdid`) are inside the leaked comment. They are documentation, not rendered content.
- No unresolved merge tokens are confirmed in the visible markup. The body is truncated, so body-level tokens can't be checked.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post** (RFC 8058): Both are absent from the captured headers. QA notes the AgentMail relay may strip them, so this is **unconfirmed**. Gmail and Yahoo require both for bulk senders (about 5,000+ per day). Verify against the original message, not the relayed copy.
- **Authentication-Results:** Not found, which is expected via the relay. SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is **unknown**. The From domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` is a branded subdomain, which is a good sign but not proof.
- **Footer unsubscribe link and physical address:** Not visible in the truncated source, so unverified.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- The provided data contains no resolvable destination URLs or UTM parameters, because all 16 tracking links were skipped. UTM presence, landing-page match and redirect behavior can't be assessed.
- The `@refid`/`@base_refid` variables in the leaked comment suggest campaign IDs are appended through the click-tracking layer rather than as visible UTMs. This is an inference only.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip the AMPscript/variable documentation comment and the dead viewport comments from the production template. Do this at the SFMC build step so internal DE names don't ship.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta (keep `width=device-width, initial-scale=1`).
3. Add `lang` to `<html>` and trim the `<title>`.
4. Confirm the nav has a non-checkbox fallback for Gmail and Outlook, such as visible links or a "view in browser" path.
5. Re-run the QA against the original message, not the relayed copy. Check `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results`, and probe the 16 tracking links with a redirect-following check that reports final URLs and UTMs.
6. Shorten or simplify the plain-text part (fewer wrapped URLs) to bring URL density down from about 86%.
