# New. Sale. Arrivals. (& they’re up to 40% off)
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-11**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, product-forward "New Sale Arrivals" send built on a big oversized script "S" hero — genuinely striking as a hero graphic, distinctive without leaning on a discount slab or countdown clock. The 3x3 product grid underneath is the real strength: nine named designer pieces (Castañer espadrilles, Tory Burch quilted denim bag, Jimmy Choo bucket bag, Ungaro emerald gown) that give a much clearer sense of "what's actually on sale" than most of the recent Last Call sends in this brand's history, which just show a generic red slab and a single SHOP SALE button.
- Compared to the 8/9 "Ends tonight: Extra 40% off" and 8/10 "Last Call" sends, this one earns points for genuine merchandising — real named brands, real product photography — but loses points versus the 8/8 and 8/9 no-discount editorial sends ("1,000+ new arrivals," "Fendi eyewear") on visual ambition; the layout here is straightforward grid-and-buttons rather than art direction. Net: a solid, honest, mid-tier promotional email — better than the recycled Last Call creative, not as memorable as the editorial full-price sends.
- Judgement: ship-worthy as-is, but the "up to 40%" claim and "Exclusions Apply" microcopy undercut trust a little, and the CLAIM YOUR 15% OFF text-signup block competing for attention near the bottom dilutes the single sale CTA.

## What's working

- Nine real, named products (Castañer, Tory Burch, Jimmy Choo, Ungaro, Alice + Olivia, etc.) give concrete texture instead of a generic sale banner.
- The oversized script "S" hero is a distinctive, on-brand piece of art direction that stands out from the recycled red Last Call slab used in prior sends.
- Clear black "SHOP SALE" CTA button is unambiguous and well-placed directly under the offer copy.
- Secondary category buttons (Shop Women's Clothing, Shop Dresses, Shop Handbags, etc.) give multiple entry points without cluttering the hero.
- No render bugs — clean grid, no overlapping text, no broken images.

## What's weak

- "Up to 40% off" plus small "Exclusions Apply" text is a classic vague-offer pattern — no floor discount, no sense of what's actually included.
- The nine products in the grid have no visible prices or discount percentages, so a shopper can't tell what they're actually saving.
- The "CLAIM YOUR 15% OFF" text-signup block sits right below the shopping CTAs and competes with the sale offer for attention — two asks in one email.
- Cadence concern: this is at least the third sale-themed Neiman Marcus send in the recent history alongside 8/9's "Ends tonight" and 8/10's "Last Call," all pushing markdowns.
- Product grid has no prices, which a shopper would want before clicking through on a sale email.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add prices (and struck-through original prices or % off per item) directly under each of the nine product photos — that's the single biggest lift, since shoppers open sale emails wanting concrete numbers, not just imagery.
- 2. Move or drop the "CLAIM YOUR 15% OFF" text-signup block to a separate send — it dilutes the primary sale CTA and is a different ask entirely.
- 3. Replace "Exclusions Apply" with a one-line summary of what IS included (e.g., "Dresses, shoes & bags now included") to reduce bait-and-switch feel.
- 4. Tighten the subject line — "New. Sale. Arrivals." reads gimmicky with the staccato periods; a cleaner phrasing would perform better.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New markdowns: Tory Burch, Jimmy Choo & more, up to 40% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Fresh sale arrivals — up to 40% off, just added`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Tory Burch, Jimmy Choo, Ungaro and more just hit the sale rack.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New markdowns across dresses, bags and shoes — shop before sizes sell out.`
