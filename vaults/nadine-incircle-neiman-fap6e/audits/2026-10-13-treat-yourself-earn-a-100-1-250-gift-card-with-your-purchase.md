# Treat yourself & earn a $100–$1,250 gift card with your purchase
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-13**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, confident gift-card-with-purchase send, and it is the first departure from the NMCIRCLE "$50–$200 off" run of Oct 6–12. A deep plum hero says "EARN A $100–$1,250 GIFT CARD", with a spend-and-earn ladder ($500→$100 up to $5,000+→$1,250) and a code, OURGIFT. The offer reads at a glance, and the 3x3 designer grid (Moncler, Burberry, Jimmy Choo, Valentino) fits the luxury positioning.
- The weak points are friction and honesty. The offer is a delayed reward, not a discount, and the "Exclusions Apply" line is tiny and low-contrast under the hero. The real terms sit in a long block of fine print at the bottom. The gift card arrives within 8 weeks. Sale items don't count toward the spend. The reward can be clawed back on returns. The hero's "Includes Beauty & Fragrance" is a nice hook, but it is not matched by any beauty product in the grid.
- Overall it is a solid, better-differentiated send than the recent repetitive ones, but it is generic and offer-led, with no personalization.

## What's working

- The hero offer is huge and legible: "EARN A $100–$1,250 GIFT CARD" in large white type on plum.
- The spend/earn table makes the tiers instantly scannable, with a clear ladder from $500 to $5,000+.
- The code OURGIFT and "Limited Time" sit right under the headline, so there is no hunting for how to redeem.
- The 3x3 grid has consistent, clean product shots and designer names, which suits the luxury positioning.
- The category buttons (Women's Clothing, Shoes, Handbags, Home, etc.) give clear paths below the grid.

## What's weak

- "Exclusions Apply" is tiny and low-contrast beneath the table, while the actual terms (sale exclusion, 8-week delivery, returns forfeiting the reward) are buried in the footer.
- The deadline (10/20/26 at 10:00 PM CT) appears only in the fine print, not the hero, so the urgency is vague ("Limited Time").
- The grid items have no prices or product-specific links, and the grid doesn't reflect the "Beauty & Fragrance" call-out.
- The hero's "SHOP NOW" is generic. The grid is also mostly women's, with men's relegated to one small button.
- The gift card is a deferred reward, not instant savings. That is weaker than the $50–$200 off sends from Oct 6–12.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the deadline in the hero ("Ends 10/20") and add a one-line honest qualifier such as "Regular-price items only," so the bold promise matches the terms.
- 2. Change the CTA to "Earn Your Gift Card" or "Shop Gift Card Styles".
- 3. Add a beauty/fragrance tile to the grid to match the hero copy, and a men's row.
- 4. Add one worked example, e.g. "Spend $1,500, get $375 back."
- 5. Cut the fine print to 4–5 bullets with a "Full details" link.

The QA run found 0 hard failures and 3 warnings. All three warnings are visibility gaps from the AgentMail relay, not confirmed defects. The HTML was truncated, so footer, link, and image checks are incomplete.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **Link inspection not possible:** the truncated source doesn't show the link `href` values. I can't confirm whether links are consistently wrapped or whether any are unwrapped or malformed.
- No broken-link evidence was found.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom on some clients. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **Exact-match class selectors:** the mobile rules use `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=hide]`, `*[class=mobile]`, and similar. They match only when `class` is exactly that string. Any element with a second class silently loses its responsive rule.
- **Checkbox-hack hamburger nav:** the rules `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu` and `.hamburger` depend on CSS `:checked` support. Gmail (including GANGA accounts) and most Outlook builds don't support it. The `*[class].menu` rule sets `display:none; visibility:hidden`, so the nav may be unreachable in those clients. A fallback can't be confirmed from the truncated source.
- **Dead `bugfix` animation:** the `body { -webkit-animation: bugfix infinite 1s }` rule is a hack for old iOS 9.
- **Leftover commented-out viewport tags:** three alternative `<meta name="viewport">` tags remain in the source, including `width=1024`. This adds clutter but has no runtime effect.
- **Title:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has a trailing space and is generic.
- **Unverified:** image alt text, `role="presentation"` on layout tables, and the preheader weren't visible in the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Template comment leaked into delivered HTML:** the first comment contains ESP build instructions. They include internal data extension names and field names: `Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)`, `Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm", ...)`, and `Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "cmdid", ...)`. It also includes `CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW` and `SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256`. This is a production template-hygiene issue and a minor information disclosure. It also shows the `@user` and `@uEm` variables are tied to an email identifier or hash.
- **Unrendered tokens:** none are visible in the supplied portion. The rest of the body wasn't checked.

- **List-Unsubscribe-Post not found:** WARN. RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is unverified. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require it, so check it in the original MIME headers.
- **Authentication-Results not found:** SPF, DKIM, and DMARC status is unknown. The sending domain is `e.neimanmarcus.com`, so alignment with the From domain couldn't be verified. This is a relay limitation.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Strip the template comment:** remove the ESP variable documentation comment (the `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated` and `NM_CRM_Feed` lookups) in the send-time render. Also remove the commented-out viewport tags.
