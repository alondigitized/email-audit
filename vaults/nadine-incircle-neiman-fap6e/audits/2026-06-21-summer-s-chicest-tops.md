# Summer’s chicest tops
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-21**
## Executive summary

- This is a soft editorial from Neiman Marcus built around a peach/blush color story — a high-fashion model shot leading into a "Peachy Keen" theme with four product thumbnails below. The creative is tasteful and on-brand, but it's a brand-building play with almost no purchase urgency. There's no price anywhere in the hero, no discount in the editorial section, and the product grid is too small to read on mobile.
- The email then pivots awkwardly in the lower third to plug the Designer Sale "Private Event — Up to 40% Off," which we've already seen twice this week (June 18 and June 21 launches). That module feels bolted on, not integrated. The result is a split-personality send: a mood-board editorial on top, a sale reminder on the bottom, with neither half fully earning the open.
- **Verdict:** A competent but low-conviction send. The editorial concept is clear, the render is clean, but there's no hook strong enough to drive a click on a Saturday morning.

## What's working

- **Hero image is striking and cohesive** — the blush/peach palette ties the model shot and the product thumbnails into a clear color story.
- **"Peachy Keen" headline is clever and specific** — better than generic editorial copy; it gives the email a point of view.
- **No render bugs** — clean layout, no broken images, no overlapping text throughout.
- **Designer Sale module is visually distinct** — the dark banner separates it cleanly so it reads as a bonus, not a distraction from the hero.

## What's weak

- **No price, no discount in the editorial section** — four product shots with no price or "shop" anchor make this feel like a lookbook, not a transaction.
- **Product thumbnails are too small** — the four peach-top grid images are thumbnail-sized; product detail (silhouette, brand name, material) is illegible.
- **CTA copy is weak** — "Shop Tops" and "Shop Designer Collections" are generic. Neither is tied to the color/editorial story.
- **Designer Sale module repeats prior creative** — this is the third Designer Sale banner in four days (June 18, June 20, June 21); subscriber fatigue is real.
- **No time limit on the sale** — "Up to 40% off" with no end date or urgency signal reduces any push to act now.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a price anchor or "starting at" callout on at least one product** — even a single "$X" in the editorial section transforms this from a mood-board into a shoppable send.
- 2. **Enlarge the product grid and add brand/price captions** — four tiny thumbnails with no labels waste the real estate; go 2×2 with name + price below each.
- 3. **Make the CTA specific to the editorial** — "Shop Peachy Tops" beats "Shop Tops"; ties the click back to the promise.
- 4. **Drop or consolidate the Designer Sale module** — subscribers have seen it three times. Either remove it here or replace it with a "last chance" end-date message to create genuine urgency.

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a Salesforce Marketing Cloud (AMPscript) template with multiple unresolved variable conflicts and commented-out dead code indicating template drift. Compliance posture is weak: both `List-Unsubscribe` headers are absent, and SPF/DKIM authentication status is unverifiable via the relay.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text part is 78.8% URLs (3,311 / 4,200 chars)**
QA flag: `[WARN] Plain-text is >70% URLs`. A machine-generated plain-text part consisting almost entirely of redirect URLs is a strong spam-filter signal. Gmail's bulk sender heuristics and SpamAssassin both penalize this pattern.

**35 tracking/redirect links skipped**
All click-tracking links passed through a redirect domain; destinations and UTM integrity could not be verified. This is a blind spot — broken destination links or missing UTM params would not surface in this audit.

**`@refid` is a no-op concatenation**
```
@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")
```
Concatenating `@base_refid` with an empty string produces the same value. If a campaign suffix (e.g., a variant code) was intended, it was never populated. Any downstream reporting keyed on `@refid` will be indistinguishable from `@base_refid`.

**`@uEm` and `@cmdid` variable assignments lack `SET` keyword in comment definitions**

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent**
QA: `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header not found`. RFC 2369 requires this for bulk senders. Google and Yahoo's February 2024 bulk sender policy mandates `List-Unsubscribe` for senders of >5,000 msgs/day to Gmail/Yahoo addresses. Its absence increases the probability of Gmail placing this in the Spam folder or suppressing inbox delivery.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent**
QA: `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found (RFC 8058)`. One-click unsubscribe (the `POST` variant) is required by the same Google/Yahoo 2024 policy. Without it, list-managed unsubscribes via the Gmail UI will not work, leading to higher spam complaint rates.

**Both headers may be present upstream but stripped by the AgentMail relay**
QA notes: "Header may not be captured by AgentMail relay." This should be verified by inspecting raw headers directly from an inbox (e.g., Gmail "Show original"), not the relay capture. If NM's ESP (Salesforce MC) is injecting these headers correctly and the relay is stripping them, this is a monitoring gap, not a sending defect.

**Authentication-Results header not found**
QA: `[WARN] Authentication-Results header not found`. SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status for `e.neimanmarcus.com` is unknown in this capture. Same relay caveat applies — verify at the inbox level.

**Unsubscribe link in body**
CAN-SPAM requires a functional opt-out mechanism in the body. The source was truncated before the footer; presence of a working unsubscribe link cannot be confirmed from the provided HTML.
