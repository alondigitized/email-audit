# The Designer Sale: Now up to 60% off with new styles added
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-24**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, single-offer reminder for the Designer Sale, and it works because it does one thing. A big red block framed in black says "THE DESIGNER SALE, UP TO 60% OFF," with a black "SHOP THE EVENT" button under it. The eye lands on the offer first. The 3x3 grid of named designers (Etro, Manolo Blahnik, Khaite, Thom Browne, Ferragamo) backs up the "designer" claim.
- It is also the Nth variation of the same Black Friday-early campaign. The Nov 23 "3 ways to save" send, the Nov 22 "Last day" send and the Nov 22 "40% off" reminder all pushed similar offers. The "New styles added" kicker is the only new news here, and it is a small line of text at the top. The grid items have no prices, no sale markers and no individual links I can see. The six red category buttons look weak because their text is low-contrast red-on-red.
- Verdict: a solid, honest, on-brand promo, but a repeat with little to differentiate it. It earns a mid-range score.

## What's working

- The hero is unmistakable: "UP TO 60% OFF" is the largest element, and it is the offer a skimmer should see.
- "SHOP THE EVENT" is a clear black button that stands out against the red.
- The designer names under each product (Manolo Blahnik, Thom Browne, Ferragamo) signal luxury and credibility.
- The grid mixes dresses, shoes, bags and menswear, so it covers several shopper types.
- The "Exclusions Apply" line and the footnote are disclosed rather than hidden, which helps offer honesty.

## What's weak

- "NEW STYLES ADDED" is the only news in the email, and it is set in small, widely spaced text at the top of the hero.
- The category buttons use dull red text on a red background, so they are hard to read and look disabled.
- The grid shows no prices, no "was/now" and no discount badges, so the 60% claim isn't tied to any product.
- The "'Tis the season" line is generic filler.
- This is another near-duplicate of the recent sale sends, which risks fatigue. See the Nov 22 "Last day: Shop Black Friday early with up to 60% off designer styles" send.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the news the hero. Lead with "New styles added" in a larger size, and show a few of those new items with sale prices in place of the generic grid.
- 2. Fix the category buttons: use white or black text on red, or invert them to a black fill with white text.
- 3. Add "$X now, was $Y" or "40% off" tags to the grid products and link each one to its product page.
- 4. Add a real deadline, such as "Ends Sunday", to give the urgency a credible end.
- 5. Vary the creative across the cadence so it doesn't repeat the prior sends.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh styles from Manolo Blahnik, Thom Browne and Ferragamo, now marked down.`

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Pinch-zoom disabled.** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` sets `maximum-scale=1`, which blocks zoom on iOS and Android. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) concern.
- **No document language.** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang` or `xml:lang`, so screen readers can't pick the right pronunciation.
- **Generic title.** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has a trailing space and doesn't reflect the campaign. This is minor.
- **Checkbox-hack mobile nav.** The `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu` and `.hamburger` / `.close` rules depend on `:checked` support, which Gmail and several other clients don't provide. Without it the nav is probably unreachable or hidden in those clients. The truncated source doesn't show whether a fallback exists, so I can't confirm.
- **Dead code in the head.** Three commented-out `<meta viewport>` variants remain. This is harmless but adds weight.
- **Comment before the DOCTYPE.** The large comment precedes `<!DOCTYPE ...>`. Some clients and webviews go into quirks mode when anything other than whitespace comes before the doctype. Verify in Outlook and legacy webmail.
