# 25% off select styles + 20% off jewelry starts now
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-17**
## Executive summary

- This is Neiman Marcus's Friends & Family event — a real, storewide markdown event (25% off select styles, 20% off jewelry) rather than another vague gift-card-tier or single-category catalog send, which puts it a notch above most of this brand's recent sends. The hero is clean and on-brand: deep green, serif "Friends & Family" wordmark, clear percentage-off callout, and a single unambiguous "Shop the Event" button above the fold.
- Where it falls down is the merchandising grid underneath — nine wildly disparate items (a blue gown, jeans, a printed dress, two handbags, red mules, boots, a men's blazer, loafers) with no organizing logic, spanning women's and men's with no labels distinguishing them. It reads as "here's some stuff on sale" rather than a curated edit, and there's no visible product pricing or discount-per-item, so the click-through decision is entirely deferred to the site.
- Net: solid, recognizable, honest offer with real urgency (ends 9/26), let down by a generic, unpriced product wall. This will perform above the brand's editorial/catalog sends but isn't reaching its ceiling.

## What's working

- Clear, singular offer in the hero: "25% off select styles + 20% off jewelry" with no ambiguity about what the event is.
- One unmistakable CTA button ("Shop the Event") placed immediately under the offer, above the fold.
- InCircle Double Points callout adds a loyalty layer for members without cluttering the hero.
- "Exclusions Apply" is disclosed right in the hero, not buried in fine print only — transparent framing.
- Clean brand execution — consistent green/cream/black palette, legible typography, no render bugs.

## What's weak

- Product grid mixes categories (dresses, handbags, shoes, menswear) with zero segmentation or "shop by category" framing before it — feels like a random sale bin.
- No prices or per-item discount shown on any of the nine products — user has to click through blind to see if 25% off a Brunello Cucinelli blazer is even in reach.
- Designer name is the only label under each product (no product name/description), which undercuts the "product detail you care about" signal for anyone deciding whether to click.
- The category button grid (Shop Women's, Shop Men's, etc.) duplicates navigation already at the top of the email — wasted real estate that could've reinforced the offer instead.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add visible price + strikethrough/discounted price under each of the nine products — this is the single biggest lever, since right now every click is a blind bet on relevance and affordability.
- 2. Group the grid into two labeled rows ("Women's Picks" / "Men's & Accessories") instead of one undifferentiated nine-up block, so the eye can skip to a relevant category fast.
- 3. Cut the redundant category-button block (Shop Women's/Men's/Shoes/Handbags/Home) since the same links already live in the top nav — replace with a second curated product row or a countdown/urgency banner ("Ends Wednesday 9/26").
- 4. Move "Ends 9/26" into the hero itself, not just buried in the fine print at the bottom — visible deadlines lift urgency-driven opens and clicks.
