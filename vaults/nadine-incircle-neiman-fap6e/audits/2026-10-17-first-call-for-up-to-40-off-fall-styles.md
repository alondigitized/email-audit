# First Call for up to 40% off fall styles
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-17**
## Executive summary

- This is a loud, clear markdown send, and it works as one. The red "FIRST CALL® UP TO 40% OFF" hero in a heavy black frame sits above the fold, and the black "SHOP NOW" button is right under it. There's no ambiguity about what's on offer. But it is nearly a repeat of the Oct 14 "First Call: Up to 40% off starts now" and the Oct 16 "Up to 40% off: Shop new markdowns on fall styles". That's a third send of the same hero in four days.
- The product grid is the new part. It has nine designer pieces (Alice + Olivia, A.L.C., AG, Acler, Ksubi, Veronica Beard, Polo Ralph Lauren, Chiara Boni, Theory), all women's. That gives a subscriber something to react to. But there are no prices, no discount badges and no per-item links I can tell are specific, so "up to 40%" never lands on a real product. The six category buttons at the bottom are all the same red with the same weight, so the click paths are flat.
- Verdict: a competent, readable, repetitive markdown email. It earns a middling score because of the fatigue, not because of execution. The best fix is to differentiate it from the last two sends.

## What's working

- The hero is unmissable. Red on black with "UP TO 40% OFF" in large type puts the offer first.
- The "SHOP NOW" button is clear and directly under the offer.
- The nine-item grid is clean, evenly spaced and labeled by designer name, which suits a luxury shopper.
- The category buttons at the bottom (women's apparel, women's shoes, kids', men's, handbags, accessories) give easy routes by category.
- The "Exclusions Apply" note and the footnote about "25-40% off" are shown openly and are not buried.

## What's weak

- It repeats the Oct 14 and Oct 16 First Call sends almost exactly, with the same hero and the same offer. That's the 3rd send in 4 days.
- The grid shows no prices, no "was/now" and no percent-off tags, so the discount is abstract.
- The grid is women's only, but the nav and buttons imply men's, kids' and shoes. The men's shopper gets nothing.
- The footnote says "25-40% off", which is a softer floor than the "up to 40%" headline implies.
- The six same-weight red buttons have no hierarchy, and the sale content below the grid (app, style advisor) is generic filler.

## Recommendations

- 1. Show real markdown prices on the grid (for example "$498 $299") with a "40% off" tag on the best item. This turns an abstract offer into a concrete one.
- 2. Change the creative between sends. Rotate the hero by category (outerwear, denim, dresses), so Oct 14, 16 and 17 don't look identical.
- 3. Add a deadline or a stock cue ("sizes selling fast", "ends Sunday") so there's a reason to act today.
- 4. Make the first button category-specific (e.g. "Shop women's dresses up to 40% off") instead of a bare "SHOP NOW".
- 5. Cut the cadence. Send one First Call email per week, with a different angle each time.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Dresses, denim & coats: up to 40% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Alice + Olivia, AG, Theory: now up to 40% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New markdowns on fall designer pieces, ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Fresh markdowns just added. Online and in stores.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible HTML is a table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional template with a CSS-checkbox mobile nav. It leaks internal template notes in an HTML comment and blocks pinch-zoom with the viewport meta. The source is truncated and the QA run had no header data, so links, footer, UTM parameters and authentication could not be verified.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Not verifiable:** QA skipped HTTP probes on 30 tracking/click-redirect links, so destination status, redirect chains and UTM preservation are unconfirmed.
- **Plain-text bloat:** 5,675 of 6,647 plain-text characters (about 85%) are URLs. This points to long, unshortened tracking URLs in the text part, and the QA check flags it as a deliverability warning.
- No broken or malformed links are visible in the truncated source.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Pinch-zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` prevents user scaling, which fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). The commented-out alternatives show this was an intentional choice.
- **Missing language:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang` attribute, so screen readers can't pick the right pronunciation.
- **Comment before DOCTYPE:** a large comment block precedes `<!DOCTYPE>`. Some older clients (legacy Outlook/IE-based) treat anything before the doctype as quirks mode.
- **Exact-match class selectors:** rules like `*[class=contenttable]` and `*[class=mobile]` only match elements whose class attribute is exactly that value. Any element with a second class silently loses the responsive rule.
- **Checkbox-hack mobile nav:** the `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu` and `.hamburger`/`.close` rules need `:checked` support. Gmail and Outlook don't support it, so the menu won't open there. Whether a fallback exists can't be confirmed from the truncated source.
- **Generic title:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has a trailing space and doesn't describe the email.
- **Duplicated declaration:** `.show` sets `width` twice (`auto !important` and `278px`), so the second is dead code.
- **Alt text and heading structure:** not verifiable in the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Internal template notes shipped in the live HTML:** the opening comment is a developer note: `REQUIRED variables: @email … @base_refid … Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email) … Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm"…) … @cmdid … CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW`. It was not stripped before send. It exposes internal data-extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`) and field names (`EMAIL_ID`, `uEm`, `cmdid`), and the "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO" instruction suggests the scenario selection was left unresolved. A second comment, `<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->`, is also left in.
- **Rendered tokens:** no unresolved merge tokens appear in visible content in the truncated portion.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)
- **List-Unsubscribe not found (WARN):** the QA note says it may not be captured by the AgentMail relay, so this is unconfirmed rather than a confirmed defect.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) not found (WARN):** this is also possibly a relay artifact. If it is truly absent, Gmail and Yahoo one-click unsubscribe requirements for bulk senders aren't met.
- **Authentication-Results not found (WARN):** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. The sender is `e.neimanmarcus.com`, a subdomain, but alignment can't be assessed.
- **Footer unsubscribe link and postal address:** not visible in the truncated source, so unverified.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
Not assessable. The link targets, UTM parameters and landing pages were not visible in the truncated HTML, and the tracking redirects were not probed. The template does reference a `@refid` / `@base_refid` campaign identifier, which suggests tracking is handled through redirect parameters rather than visible UTMs. That is an inference, not a confirmed finding.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Strip the developer comments** (`REQUIRED variables…`, `SET @user…`) in the send pipeline. They expose internal DE and field names.
2. **Remove `maximum-scale=1`** from the viewport meta, or add `user-scalable=yes`.
3. **Add `lang="en"`** to `<html>`. Move any comments below the DOCTYPE, and give `<title>` a descriptive value.
4. **Verify headers on a direct-from-ESP copy** (not the AgentMail relay): `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, and SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment.
5. **Shorten the plain-text URLs** (for example, with a short-link domain), or reduce the number of links in the text part.
6. **Confirm a non-`:checked` fallback** for the hamburger nav in Gmail and Outlook.
7. **Re-run the audit on the full, untruncated HTML**, and probe the 30 tracking links to confirm destinations and UTM/refid carry-through.
