# Your exclusive offer ends today: 25% off one item
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-22**
## Executive summary

- This is the "last call" for the 25% off one item offer Neiman Marcus sent yesterday ("Your exclusive offer: 25% off one item"). The creative is nearly identical: a mauve hero with "25% OFF" in a thin serif, a black "TREAT YOURSELF" button, and a black promo-code bar. The only real change is the subject line, which adds "ends today". That is a credible urgency lever, and the fine print confirms the deadline: 10/22/26 at 10:00 PM CT.
- The body does its job. A 3×3 grid of designer product images (Burberry, Chloe, Manolo Blahnik, Givenchy, Amiri) sits above six category buttons. But the urgency lives only in the subject and the fine print. Nothing in the hero or the code bar says "today" or shows a time. A reader who opens this email sees the same hero as yesterday's, with no sign the deadline has arrived.
- Verdict: a competent reminder with a good subject line and a weak execution. It is the second send of the same offer in two days, so it needs a visible deadline in the hero to justify itself. It also follows the gift-card promo that ran four times in eight days, so cadence fatigue is real.

## What's working

- "Ends today" in the subject adds real urgency to a repeat offer, backed by a specific 10:00 PM CT deadline in the fine print.
- The hero is clear: "25% OFF" is the largest element, and the single black CTA stands out against the mauve.
- The promo code sits in a high-contrast black bar right under the hero, so there is no hunting for it.
- The 3×3 grid of recognizable designer names, with clean product shots and brand labels, fits the luxury positioning.
- Six category buttons give shoppers a way into their own area.

## What's weak

- The hero never mentions the deadline. "For a limited time" is the same vague copy as yesterday's send, so the subject's urgency is not reinforced in the body.
- "Exclusions apply" is small, low-contrast grey text. The fine print then lists dozens of excluded brands (Chanel, Saint Laurent, Moncler, Dior and more), which makes the offer feel less honest than the hero suggests.
- The product grid has no prices, no "25% off" tag and no individual links that I can see. The images show what you could buy but not what you'd save.
- The six category buttons are a muted mauve with grey text, so they look disabled and carry low contrast.
- "TREAT YOURSELF" is a soft, generic verb with no category or action in it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the deadline in the hero: "Ends tonight at 10 PM CT" in place of "for a limited time". This is the single biggest lift, because it makes the email feel different from yesterday's.
- 2. Add a "was / now" price or a "25% off" tag under each product, and cut the grid to 6 items so each one gets more attention.
- 3. Change the CTA to "SHOP 25% OFF NOW" or "USE MY CODE", and darken the category buttons to improve contrast.
- 4. Move a short exclusions summary next to the hero, for example "Excludes select brands, see full list below". That is more honest than "Exclusions apply".
- 5. If you send a reminder, vary the creative so it doesn't read as a duplicate of the previous day's email.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last call: your 25% off ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Tonight only: 25% off one item`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Use your code before 10 PM CT. One regular-price item, any eligible brand.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your personal code expires tonight. Here's what's worth grabbing.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible part of the source is a standard Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC) table-based template. It has two real code-level problems: an internal ESP comment leaks into the delivered HTML, and the head blocks zoom and sets no `lang`. The compliance and authentication warnings come from the AgentMail relay and can't be confirmed as sender defects. The HTML was truncated, so links, footer and body markup were not reviewed.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 30 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped from HTTP probing (`INFO [WARN]`). Their validity, redirect chains and final destinations are **unverified**, not confirmed broken.
- The plain-text part is more than 70% URLs (5,675 of 8,001 chars). This is likely long tracking URLs in the text alternative. It is a spam-scoring and readability risk, but it is a warning, not a confirmed failure.
- No individual broken links are evidenced.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing `lang`:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang` attribute, so screen readers can't pick the correct language.
- **Zoom blocked:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` disables pinch-zoom, which conflicts with WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
- **Weak `<title>`:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` is brand-only with trailing whitespace. It doesn't describe the message.
- **Comment before DOCTYPE:** the ESP comment sits ahead of `<!DOCTYPE>`. Some clients and webmail parsers can fall back to quirks mode when content precedes the DOCTYPE. This is a risk, not a confirmed rendering failure.
- **Fragile mobile selectors:** `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=hide]` and similar are exact-match attribute selectors. They fail on any element that carries more than one class. The `*[class].menu` hamburger uses the checkbox hack, which clients such as Gmail don't support. I can't confirm from the truncated source whether a fallback exists.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unresolved merge tokens appear in the visible portion.
- The leftover template comment (`@email`, `@base_refid`, `@refid`, `@user`, `@emailid`, `@uEm`, `@cmdid`) shows the template depends on lookups against `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated` and `NM_CRM_Feed`. Whether those resolved in the body can't be confirmed from the truncated source.

## 5. Compliance
- **Internal template comment leaked** (confirmed in source): the delivered HTML begins with `<!--REQUIRED variables: ... Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email) ... Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm", ...) ... CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW -->` and `<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->`. It exposes internal data extension names, field names (`uEm`, `cmdid`) and an authoring instruction ("CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW"). The comment is also stray authoring text that should have been removed before send. It is an information-disclosure and hygiene issue.
- **List-Unsubscribe not found** (`[WARN]`): the check notes the relay may strip the header, so this is unconfirmed.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) not found** (`[WARN]`): the same caveat applies. One-click unsubscribe support is unverified. Gmail and Yahoo require it for bulk senders.
- **Authentication-Results not found** (`[WARN]`): SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown because of the relay. The From domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` is a dedicated subdomain, which is a good sign, but alignment is unverified.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip the SFMC authoring comments, including the `REQUIRED variables` block, from the rendered output, for example by using AMPscript-side comments (`%%[ /* */ ]%%`) instead of HTML comments. Where possible, put the DOCTYPE first.

3. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta.
4. Make the `<title>` descriptive and remove the trailing space.
5. Check the raw headers from the original send (not the relay copy) for `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, and SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment.
6. Re-run the audit with the full HTML and a probe that follows the tracking redirects, so UTMs, destinations and footer compliance can be verified.
