# The Roundup: Heel Appeal
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-29**
## Executive summary

- This is Neiman Marcus running its most polished "no-discount, all-merchandise" send of the recent stretch — a single hero shoe (a raffia polka-dot mule set on a garden step, doing double duty as the visual thesis for "Garden Party Heels"), two clean CTAs, and a genuinely well-organized six-shoe grid spanning Valentino Garavani, Roger Vivier, Christian Louboutin, Alexandre Birman, Manolo Blahnik, and Malone Souliers. Every shoe is labeled with its designer, nothing is broken, and the whole thing renders cleanly top to bottom.
- The catch is the same one that's dogged every editorial reset since 7/23: there's no price, no discount, no urgency, and no reason to act *today* versus next week. Compared to 7/28's "Make a splash in summer looks" (5/10, one hero shot and one CTA), this is a stronger build — two CTAs instead of one, and a real merchandising grid instead of an app-download tile doing the heavy lifting. But it's still a browse invitation, not a sales pitch, and it lands in an inbox that's already seen this "editorial no-price reset" beat run at least five times in eight days (7/23, 7/24, 7/26, 7/27, 7/28).
- Solid execution, thin motivation to click today. This is a keep-the-relationship-warm send, not a revenue send.

## What's working

- The hero image is well-composed and does real work — a distinctive polka-dot raffia mule on a garden step directly sets up the "Garden Party Heels" CTA below it, so image and copy reinforce each other.
- Two CTAs ("Shop Heels" / "Shop Garden Party Heels") give the reader a general and a specific path, which is more useful than a single generic button.
- The six-shoe grid is genuinely curated — recognizable luxury names (Louboutin, Manolo Blahnik, Roger Vivier) clearly labeled under each product, giving the browse real substance.
- Clean render throughout: no broken images, no overlapping text, no placeholder copy.
- Nav bar and footer utility tiles (app download, personal stylist, store finder) are all present and legible.

## What's weak

- Zero price or discount signal anywhere in the email — for a "roundup" of six specific products, not showing even one price is a missed chance to let the reader self-select by budget.
- No urgency or time-boundedness at all, consistent with the brand's recent editorial cadence, which makes the email easy to skim past.
- The subject "Heel Appeal" is a mild pun but tells the reader nothing concrete — no product count, no occasion cue, no price cue — so it competes poorly against the "Extra 25% off" and "$500 off" subject lines this same brand has run this week.
- This is at least the sixth thematic/editorial no-discount send since 7/23 ("Your new faves," "Prada's sneakers," "Transitional style," "1,000+ new arrivals," "Make a splash," now this) — the cadence risks training subscribers to skip these opens entirely.
- No social proof, reviews, or "why now" hook on any of the six featured shoes — it's a lookbook, not a sales page.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add at least a starting price ("Starting at $X") under one or two hero pieces — even without a discount, a price anchors intent and is the single biggest lever missing here.
- 2. Tie the send to an actual occasion or deadline ("Wedding season heels — shop before [date]") rather than the generic "Roundup" framing, to differentiate it from the prior five editorial sends this month.
- 3. Trim the grid to 4 shoes and let one "Editor's Pick" get a larger callout with price + a one-line styling note — six equal-weight thumbnails ask a lot of a skimming reader.
- 4. Alternate this beat with an actual promo-ladder send sooner — the brand's own history shows "Extra 25% off" sends outperform (7.0/10) the editorial resets (5.0/10 average), so back-to-back editorial cadence is underusing a proven lever.

- `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` — `maximum-scale=1` disables pinch-to-zoom, which fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) for low-vision recipients. Recommend removing `maximum-scale=1`.
- `<meta name="x-apple-disable-message-reformatting">` and the `.ExternalClass` / `.ReadMsgBody` resets are correctly applied for Apple Mail and Outlook.com rendering consistency — no issue.
- Image `alt` attributes and color-contrast values are not visible in the truncated source, so accessibility of body content (beyond the viewport tag) cannot be confirmed.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- The HTML ships with a **live, unrendered AMPscript documentation comment** at the very top of the source:
  ```

  ```
  This is internal build/QA scaffolding (Data Extension names `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated` and `NM_CRM_Feed`, lookup key logic, and personalization variable scaffolding) that leaked into the production send. - Because it's a comment block listing multiple "scenarios" ("CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW"), it's worth confirming the correct AMPscript block was actually selected/activated for this send — an unresolved scenario selection could cause `@user`/`@emailid` to resolve incorrectly downstream (e.g., in personalized links or the `@user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256` line).
