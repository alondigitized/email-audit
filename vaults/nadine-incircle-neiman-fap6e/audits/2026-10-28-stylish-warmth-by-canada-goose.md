# Stylish warmth by Canada Goose

- This is a clean, editorial Canada Goose send with no offer. A woman in a dark olive bomber and white trousers fills the hero against a pale studio backdrop. ## What's working

- The hero image is strong. One model, one jacket and a clean backdrop make it instantly clear this is outerwear.
- The Canada Goose name in the subject and the "Shop Canada Goose" button give the click a clear destination.
- The layout is uncluttered and the header nav (Women, Men, Shoes, Sale) is easy to read.
- The timing fits late October, when people start shopping for cold-weather outerwear.
- The two buttons split intent: shop the brand, or browse all outerwear.

## What's weak

- There's no offer, price, shipping perk or deadline, so there's no urgency to click now.
- "Summit Style" is vague. It names neither the product nor the benefit.
- The buttons are thin outlines with light grey text, so they're weak against the large image. They feel like secondary links, not a primary CTA.
- The whole hero is one image, with no product name, price or detail on the jacket. A reader can't tell which style is being featured.
- The app and style-advisor modules below the buttons add clutter and pull attention away from the single CTA.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a concrete hook under the headline, such as a price on the featured parka, a "new arrivals" label, or a delivery or availability note. The email needs one reason to click today.
- 2. Make the primary button solid black with white text and a specific verb, such as "Shop Canada Goose Parkas." Keep "Shop Outerwear" as a lighter secondary link.
- 3. Show three to four linked products with names and prices beneath the hero, so the click lands on a specific item.
- 4. Replace "Summit Style" with a headline that names the benefit, such as "Built for the first real cold snap."
- 5. Drop the app and stylist block, or move it below a product row.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom. This fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
- **Comment before DOCTYPE:** a large `<!--REQUIRED variables…-->` block precedes `<!DOCTYPE …>`. Some legacy engines can drop into quirks mode when anything but whitespace comes before the DOCTYPE.
- **Fragile mobile selectors:** `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=mobile]`, `*[class=hide]` and similar are exact-match attribute selectors. They stop matching if an element carries more than one class, so the mobile overrides fail silently.
- **Hamburger menu:** the nav depends on the checkbox hack (`.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu`). Gmail and Outlook don't support it, so those clients need a fallback.
- **Generic title:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` is the same for every send and has a trailing space.
- **Alt text and `lang`:** not assessable because the source is truncated.
