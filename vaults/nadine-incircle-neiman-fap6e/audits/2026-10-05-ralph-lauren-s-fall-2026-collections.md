# Ralph Lauren’s Fall 2026 Collections
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-05**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, image-led brand-story send for Ralph Lauren's Fall 2026 collections, and it has no offer. The hero is a woman in a shearling-collared suede coat against a weathered tree trunk. "Rugged Meets Refined" sits under it, with an outlined "Shop Ralph Lauren Collection" button. A second image shows a man in a grey tweed suit and Fair Isle knit lounging in the woods, followed by a short line about menswear and a "Shop Ralph Lauren Purple Label" text link.
- The photography is strong and the mood is cohesive and seasonal. The commercial side is thin: no price, no named product, no reason to click today. It is the third no-discount editorial send in three days, after Chloé on Oct 3 and Veronica Beard on Oct 4, and the same thing as "Things We Love" earlier today. It reads as a brand billboard and will land in the same 4-6 range as those.
- Judgement: well made but low-urgency. The pairing of a women's hero with a menswear second panel and copy is slightly muddled, and the two CTAs go to two different places.

## What's working

- The hero image is cinematic and on-season. The shearling coat and woodland setting sell fall without any copy.
- The visual hierarchy is clean. The eye goes image, headline, button, then the second image.
- "Rugged Meets Refined" is a short, confident headline that fits the brand's voice.
- The layout is uncluttered and every image rendered correctly. The nav (Women, Men, Shoes, Sale) and the footer service links (app, style advisor, find a store) are tidy.

## What's weak

- There is no offer, no price, no named product and no deadline, so nothing gives a reason to click now.
- Both CTAs go to brand-level collections ("Ralph Lauren Collection", "Purple Label"), not specific pieces. The "Purple Label" link is small, grey and easy to miss.
- The woman's hero paired with menswear-only copy and a menswear second image blurs who the email is for.
- The outlined button is low contrast and understated for the only primary action.
- The footer is cluttered with an address-book prompt, address, privacy link, unsubscribe and "View in a Web Browser" stacked in small type.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the hero shoppable. Credit or price the shearling coat and link the button straight to that piece or a "Shearling & Suede" edit. Specific products beat "Collection".
- 2. Split the creative by audience, or segment it. Send the women's hero to women's shoppers and the Purple Label panel to men's shoppers, and give each its own labelled CTA.
- 3. Add one reason to act now, such as "New arrivals", "Free shipping" or an Neiman Marcus loyalty line.
- 4. Use a solid filled button instead of the thin outline, and make the second CTA a real button.
- 5. Space these brand-story sends out. Three editorial sends in three days is a lot for a no-offer program.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Ralph Lauren Fall: shearling, tweed & suede`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The Ralph Lauren coat everyone will want`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shearling, Fair Isle and Purple Label tailoring, new for fall.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop the season's heritage outerwear before sizes go.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The truncated source shows a conventional table-based email with a leaked Salesforce Marketing Cloud template comment, no `lang` attribute, and a viewport tag that blocks zoom. The compliance and authentication checks were inconclusive because of the relay, and click-through behavior could not be verified.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **15 tracking links were not probed.** QA skipped them as click-redirect domains, so destination, redirect chain and final status are unverified. This is a coverage gap, not a confirmed defect.
- **The plain-text part is mostly URLs.** QA reports 2840 of 3325 characters (~85%) are URLs, which can raise spam-filter scores. This probably comes from long wrapped tracking URLs in the text alternative.
- The truncated HTML shows no broken or malformed `href` values.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom is disabled.** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom, which fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
- **No `lang` attribute.** The tag is `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">`, so screen readers can't select a pronunciation profile.
- **Generic `<title>`.** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has a trailing space and doesn't identify the campaign.
- **Fragile mobile selectors.** The rules use exact-match attribute selectors such as `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=mobile]` and `*[class=hide]`. They fail silently on any element with more than one class.
- **Checkbox-hack hamburger nav.** The `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu` rules depend on `:checked` support. Gmail and several other clients strip this, so the menu's behavior there is unknown, and a CSS-only toggle gives poor keyboard and screen-reader access.
- **Legacy CSS.** `.ReadMsgBody` and `.ExternalClass` are harmless dead weight.
- I couldn't check image `alt` attributes or the `role="presentation"` layout tables because the source was truncated before the body.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Template scaffolding is in the delivered HTML.** An HTML comment before `<!DOCTYPE>` exposes internal Marketing Cloud data:
  - Data extension names: `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`.
  - Field names: `EMAIL_ID`, `EMAIL_ADDRESS`, `uEm`, `cmdid`.
  - Leftover instructions: `CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW`, `SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256`.
- This is an information leak and a template-hygiene problem. A comment before the DOCTYPE can also push some older clients into quirks mode.
- No unresolved tokens such as `%%…%%` or `{{…}}` appear in the visible portion.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **No `List-Unsubscribe` header** was found.
- **No `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header** (RFC 8058) was found.
- **No `Authentication-Results` header** was found, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown.
- QA attributes all three to the AgentMail relay stripping headers, so they are **unconfirmed**, not proven failures. The sender (`e.neimanmarcus.com`) is a major retailer that likely sends these headers. Verify them against raw original headers.
- The visible-footer unsubscribe link and postal address are outside the truncated source, so they could not be checked.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **No issues found.** UTM parameters and landing-page alignment can't be assessed because every tracked link was skipped and the redirect targets were not resolved.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip the pre-DOCTYPE SFMC comment block, and any other developer comments, from the rendered output. This removes the DE and field-name leak and the quirks-mode risk.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag.
3. Add `lang="en"` to `<html>`, and use a descriptive `<title>` without the trailing space.
4. Replace `*[class=x]` selectors with `.x` or `[class~=x]`, and provide a non-checkbox fallback for the nav.
5. Shorten or strip URLs in the plain-text part, or add more descriptive text, to bring the URL ratio below 70%.
6. Re-run the audit on the raw message source, outside the relay, to confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and SPF/DKIM/DMARC. Follow the tracking redirects to verify destinations and UTM parameters.
