# The Roundup: Happy Heels
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- This is a pure editorial / category-spotlight email — no discount, no deadline, no price. NM is surfacing a curated heel edit ("The Roundup: Happy Heels") with six product shots arranged in a clean two-column grid. The hero is a styled flat-lay of pink mules that reads beautifully on mobile. Two CTAs bookend the grid — "Shop Heels" and "Shop Must-Have Shoes" at the top, "Shop New Arrivals" at the bottom — all pointing at category pages rather than individual products.
- The creative execution is solid. Shoes are well-lit, product names are visible (Staud Sparrow, Louboutin-style red, Gucci, Black platform, and others), and the layout has no visible bugs. But the email makes no commercial case beyond aesthetic appeal. There is zero price reduction, no inventory signal, no personalization hook, and no time pressure. After the 50%-off Designer Sale blitz this week (three consecutive sends 5/17–5/19), arriving at a no-offer roundup will feel like a cool-down lap.
- Verdict: competent brand content that will move units for already-motivated buyers, but a missed opportunity to close anyone on the fence.

## What's working

- **Hero image is strong** — the pink mule flat-lay is aspirational, clean, and immediately communicates the theme.
- **Product grid is well-curated** — six distinct heel silhouettes covering stilettos, mules, slingbacks, and platforms; something for multiple taste profiles.
- **Layout has no render bugs** — product names, images, and CTAs all display correctly at this render width.
- **Dual opening CTAs** ("Shop Heels" / "Shop Must-Have Shoes") give two entry points without feeling cluttered.

## What's weak

- **No offer, no price, no urgency** — pure editorial after three high-intensity sale emails; the drop in value exchange is jarring.
- **No product prices or sale callouts visible** — a shopper can't tell if these are in the Designer Sale or full price, which creates friction.
- **CTA copy is generic** — "Shop Heels" and "Shop New Arrivals" are interchangeable with any other NM email; neither is specific to this edit.
- **Subject line "The Roundup: Happy Heels" is soft** — no verb, no offer, no urgency; reads as internal category shorthand rather than consumer copy.
- **No social proof** — no reviews, "bestseller" badges, or "low stock" signals on any of the six products.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a price anchor or sale tie-in** — at minimum, badge any of the six products that are in the ongoing Designer Sale with "On Sale" so shoppers riding the sale momentum can immediately act.
- 2. **Rewrite CTAs from category to specific** — replace "Shop New Arrivals" with "Shop These 6 Styles" or "See All Heels Under $X" to create a reason to click this email rather than just navigating directly.
- 3. **Strengthen the subject line** — the current line buries the value entirely.
- **Subject Alt A:** `6 heels worth clicking on right now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Heels we're obsessed with this week`
- 4. **Write a real preheader** — the current one either bleeds junk or is invisible; it should carry the payoff the subject teases.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `From Louboutin mules to crystal slingbacks — shop the edit.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Six standout styles, all landing in your cart today.`
- 5. **Link individual products**, not category pages — let each shoe in the grid click directly to its PDP to reduce browse friction.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "The Roundup: Happy Heels"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is built on Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC) with AMPscript personalization and a CSS-only hamburger nav. No critical rendering blockers detected, but four deliverability/compliance warnings from QA require attention before future sends — particularly the missing `List-Unsubscribe` headers and unverifiable authentication status.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**34 tracking links skipped by QA probe.** All click-redirect URLs routed through a tracking domain (consistent with SFMC `click.e.neimanmarcus.com` or similar) — the probe correctly skipped these rather than flagging false positives. Cannot confirm destination URLs or UTM hygiene without resolving the redirect chain.

**Plain-text body is 87% URLs** (3,520 of 4,042 chars). This is a spam-filter signal. The plain-text alternative is almost certainly auto-generated from the HTML with no human-readable copy — a pattern flagged by Gmail and SpamAssassin rules. The plain-text version should be a hand-crafted summary of the email content.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Hamburger nav uses CSS checkbox hack** (`*[class].navbar-checkbox:checked~*[class].menu`). This pattern works in Apple Mail but silently degrades in Gmail, Outlook, and most webmail clients — users on those clients never see the nav. No fallback is present in the source.

**Multiple commented-out `<meta name="viewport">` alternatives** remain in `<head>`:
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
Stale dead code; no rendering impact, but indicates template debt.

**`-webkit-animation: bugfix` loop on `body`** targets an iOS 9 hamburger menu bug that no longer exists on supported iOS versions. Benign but unnecessary.

**No `alt` text visible in the truncated source** for product images — cannot confirm coverage. Image-heavy heels roundup emails are high-risk for blocked-image clients if `alt` is absent.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")` is a no-op.** Concatenating with an empty string string literal produces the same value as `@base_refid` alone. This is almost certainly a template placeholder (`""` should be a module-suffix string like `"_A"` or `"_hero"`). If `@refid` is used in tracking parameters, all modules will share an identical refid, breaking module-level click attribution.

**Dual-scenario user-identity logic is partially commented out:**
```amscript
<!-- CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW -->
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */-->
```
The SHA-256 path is commented out but the comment block containing it is still present as plain HTML comment — meaning the instruction text ("CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW") is visible in View Source to any recipient who inspects the email. The live path appears to be the `Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", ...)` lookup. Confirm only one scenario is active and strip the other from production sends.

**Three CRM lookups per send** (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed` twice) will add SFMC query overhead at send time. If the sendable DE already carries `EMAIL_ID`, `uEm`, and `cmdid`, the lookups should be replaced with DE column references.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not detected** (QA WARN). This header is required by Gmail and Yahoo for bulk senders (>5k/day) under their 2024 sender requirements. Its absence from the relay-captured headers suggests either the AgentMail relay is stripping it or SFMC is not injecting it. Verify SFMC "SafeUnsubscribe" or custom header injection is configured on this send definition.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not detected** (QA WARN). Without this header, one-click unsubscribe (the Gmail/Yahoo requirement) is not supported. Gmail will show a manual unsubscribe flow instead, increasing spam complaint probability.

**Authentication-Results header absent** (QA WARN). SPF and DKIM pass/fail status cannot be confirmed from the captured headers — this is likely an AgentMail relay artifact, not a real authentication failure. Confirm SPF alignment for `e.neimanmarcus.com` and DKIM signing is active on the SFMC sending domain. DMARC policy for `neimanmarcus.com` should also be `p=quarantine` or `p=reject`.

**CAN-SPAM physical address** — not visible in the truncated HTML; assumed present in the footer. Confirm.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

Cannot resolve destination URLs from tracking redirects without unwinding the click chain. Key risk: if `@refid` is broken (see §4), UTM `campaign` or `content` parameters derived from it will be wrong or uniform across all links, collapsing module-level attribution in analytics.

No UTM parameter structure is visible in the source — confirm that SFMC link wrapping or AMPscript URL construction appends `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, and `utm_content` before the tracking redirect layer.
