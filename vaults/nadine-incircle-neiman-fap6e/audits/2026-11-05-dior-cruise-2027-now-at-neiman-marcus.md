# Dior Cruise 2027, now at Neiman Marcus
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-05**
## Executive summary

- This is a pure brand-prestige send with no offer, and it looks the part. A three-model group shot in purple, black and yellow pleated Dior dresses sits against a dark plum wall and a Monet-style water-lily painting. It reads like a gallery spread. The only copy is a serif "Explore Dior" and a short paragraph about Jonathan Anderson's first Cruise collection.
- The weakness is that it gives a subscriber no reason to act today. There is no price, no deadline, no named product and no member benefit. The button, "Discover the Collection," is the vaguest CTA the brand could have used. Compared with the earlier sends on Nov 5 ("New sale arrivals: up to 40% off Amiri, Vince & more" and "The Beauty Event: free train case with your $225 beauty purchase"), this one asks the least of the reader and offers the least. It is the third send in a day, so it competes with two stronger, offer-led emails.
- Judgement: a lovely, well-executed awareness piece that will get admired more than clicked. It is a decent 6–7 for brand equity and a weak performer for revenue.

## What's working

- The hero image is strong. The three models share one color story and the painting adds a luxury, editorial feel.
- The visual hierarchy is clean: logo, nav, hero, headline, copy, one button. The eye never wanders.
- The "Explore Dior" serif headline and the copy about golden-era Hollywood and California poppies give the collection a real story.
- The render is clean, with no broken images, no overlap and generous white space.
- The header nav (Women, Men, Shoes, Sale) keeps a path to the rest of the site open.

## What's weak

- The CTA "Discover the Collection" is a thin outline button with light grey text. It is easy to miss and the copy is generic.
- There is no price, no product name and no deadline, so nothing creates urgency.
- The hero and button link to a whole collection, not a specific piece. The reader has to browse before anything is visible.
- The lower modules (Download the app, Match with a personal style advisor, Find a store, Contact us, My account) are boilerplate and dilute the single message. They also add dead space before the footer.
- This is the third Nov 5 send, so cadence fatigue is a risk.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the CTA do the work. Use a solid black filled button with white text and specific copy such as "Shop Dior Cruise 2027." The current outline button is the single biggest lift available.
- 2. Name and price something. Add a three-product row under the hero (the purple pleated dress, the yellow dress, the flower-trim bag) with names and "from $X" so the reader sees what they could buy.
- 3. Add a reason to come now: "First to shop," "Now in select stores," or a style-advisor appointment tied to the launch.
- 4. Cut the app, advisor and store/contact boilerplate. Pick one secondary action, such as booking the personal style advisor, and let it sit directly under the CTA.
- 5. Space out prestige sends from promo sends. Don't put a no-offer editorial in the same day as two offer-led emails.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Just landed: Dior Cruise 2027`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Golden-era Hollywood meets California poppies. Shop the new pleated dresses and bags.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Jonathan Anderson's debut Cruise collection, now in store and online.`

## Technical Audit

The HTML is truncated and the relay stripped the headers, so compliance, link and continuity checks can't be confirmed either way. The QA run shows 0 failures and 4 warnings, all of which are relay or measurement limits rather than confirmed defects.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **14 tracking links not probed.** QA skipped them as click-redirect domains, so their destinations and status codes are unverified.
- **Plain-text part is mostly URLs.** QA reports 2652 of 3133 characters (about 85%) are URLs, which points to long redirect URLs in the text alternative. This is a mild spam-filter and readability risk, not a confirmed bug.
- **Unresolved tokens in hrefs can't be ruled out.** The truncated source doesn't show the link bodies, so I can't check for unresolved `@refid`, `@uEm` or `@cmdid` values.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **No `lang` attribute.** The tag is `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">`, so screen readers have no declared language.
- **Zoom blocked.** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` prevents pinch-zoom, which fails WCAG 1.4.4 on clients that honor it.
- **CSS hamburger menu.** It uses `.navbar-checkbox:checked ~ .menu` inside the `@media (max-width: 600px)` block. The `:checked` technique is unsupported in Gmail and most Outlook clients, and the fallback isn't visible in the truncated source.
- **Attribute selectors that break on multiple classes.** `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=hide]` and `*[class=mobile]` are exact-match selectors, so any element with a second class won't get the mobile override.
- **Invalid font stack.** `.disclaimerText` uses `font-family: Arial, Helvetica, 'sans-serif'`. Quoting `sans-serif` turns it into a font name rather than the generic fallback.
- **Alt text and table roles.** I can't assess these because the body is truncated.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **No rendered merge tokens visible.** Nothing in the visible portion is an unresolved `%%…%%` or `{{…}}` token.
- **Authoring comment left in the delivered HTML.** It reads `<!--REQUIRED variables: @email … Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", …) … Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm"…) CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW --> <!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->`.
  - It exposes internal data extension names and lookup fields (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`, `uEm`, `cmdid`).
  - "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO" suggests the template wasn't fully cleaned before send.
  - The comment also sits before the DOCTYPE.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **No List-Unsubscribe or List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) header found.** QA notes the AgentMail relay may not capture it, so this is unconfirmed. If the headers are truly absent, Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender requirements aren't met.
- **No Authentication-Results header.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. The sender is `NeimanMarcus@e.neimanmarcus.com`, which is a subdomain of `neimanmarcus.com`, so alignment can't be verified here.
- **Footer not visible.** The in-body unsubscribe link and physical postal address are in the truncated section, so I can't confirm them.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)

- **Possible tracking scheme.** The template references `@refid` and `@base_refid`, so the campaign may use a `refid`/`cmdid` scheme rather than UTMs. That is only inferred from the comment.

1. Strip the AMPscript documentation comment before send, and move the DOCTYPE to the first line.
2. Add `lang="en"` (and `xml:lang`) to `<html>`.
3. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag.
4. Change `'sans-serif'` to the unquoted generic `sans-serif`, and use `[class~=…]` or `[class*=…]` for the mobile overrides.
5. Provide a non-checkbox fallback for the mobile nav in Gmail and Outlook.
