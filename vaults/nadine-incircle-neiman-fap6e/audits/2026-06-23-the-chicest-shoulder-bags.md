# The chicest shoulder bags
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-23**
## Executive summary

- This is a pure editorial brand play from Neiman Marcus — a single-hero product shot of a woven shoulder bag under the headline "Woven Wonder," with two CTA buttons (Shop Shoulder Bags / Shop Must-Have Bags) and almost nothing else. There is no price, no sale, no discount, no urgency. It's a mood board, not a marketing email.
- Coming right after the June 22 "Last chance for up to $200 off" (scored 9/10) and the June 23 designer sale email, this send represents a full gear-shift from transactional to pure editorial. The problem is the editorial isn't strong enough to carry the email alone — the hero image is small and compressed-looking, the product isn't featured prominently or linked to a specific SKU, and the "Woven Wonder" headline reads like a catalog section header rather than a compelling hook. Neiman Marcus can pull off minimalist creative when the photography has room to breathe (the June 22 dark-hero new arrivals was excellent), but this render is too tight and too vague to be memorable.
- Judgment: this is a filler send — it exists to cover a calendar date and softly keep bags top-of-mind after a heavy promotion week. It will not drive meaningful clicks.

## What's working

- The woven bag photography is on-brand and the neutral/cream palette fits the NM luxury aesthetic.
- Dual CTA structure (Shoulder Bags vs. Must-Have Bags) gives the user a light navigation choice without overwhelming them.
- Subject line "The chicest shoulder bags" is clean, short, and category-specific — passes the mobile-length test.
- Footer is clean and complete — app download, personal stylist link, store finder, legal copy all visible.

## What's weak

- Zero offer — no price, no sale percentage, nothing to justify a click over simply visiting the site organically.
- The hero image is small and cramped; the bag doesn't command the screen — NM's June 22 dark hero was far more visual.
- "Woven Wonder" is vague copy that signals category but not product or value — it's a magazine subhead, not an email hook.
- No product detail visible (price, material, brand attribution, size) — NM's June 22 new-arrivals email showed brand labels per item and did this better.
- Single product focus without a product grid means zero browse signal — one click destination or nothing.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add at least one price anchor or brand attribution in the hero** — even "From $X" under the bag image gives the reader a reason to click vs. browse later. This is the single highest-leverage change.
- 2. **Open up the hero image** — the woven bag deserves full-width treatment. Compress the nav bar and let the product breathe the way the June 22 new-arrivals hero did.
- 3. **Rewrite the hero headline to include a product signal** — "Woven Wonder" says nothing actionable. Make it "The Bag of the Season" + brand name, or anchor it to summer/occasion.
- 4. **Add a 3–4 item product grid below the hero** — shoulder bags at different price tiers. One click destination is a dead end for browse-mode users.
- 5. **Add a time or context hook** — "Summer's most-carried bag" or "Styled for the weekend" would connect this to season and the current campaign cadence.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "The chicest shoulder bags"

**Sender:** NeimanMarcus@e.neimanmarcus.com | **ESP:** Salesforce Marketing Cloud (inferred from AMPscript, link redirect pattern)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has two confirmed compliance gaps (missing List-Unsubscribe headers, unresolved SPF/DKIM status) and a high-risk AMPscript issue where the `@user` (SHA-256 hashed email) assignment appears inside an HTML comment and will not execute. Deliverability risk is elevated by a plain-text part that is 85% URLs.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**34 click-redirect links — not probed**
All 34 links route through a click-tracking redirect domain. HTTP status, final destination, and UTM payload could not be verified. This is expected for SFMC but means broken-link detection is blind.

**Plain-text body is 85% URLs (2,965 / 3,479 chars)**
QA finding. Spam filters (SpamAssassin `MIME_HTML_MOSTLY_LINKS`, Gmail heuristics) penalize plain-text parts composed almost entirely of URLs. This inflates spam scores. The plain-text alternative should be a human-readable synopsis, not a URL dump.

**`@refid` is a no-op concatenation**
In the AMPscript header block:
```
@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")
```
The second argument is an empty string. If a campaign suffix or segment code was intended here, it was never added. Downstream analytics using `@refid` as a campaign tag will only capture the base ID.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom**
Active viewport declaration:
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">
```
This prevents users from zooming on mobile, violating WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Three alternative viewport tags are commented out immediately below — the active choice should be reviewed against accessibility requirements.

**CSS-checkbox interactive nav will silently fail in most clients**
The template includes a full hamburger navigation implemented via `:checked` pseudo-class:
```css
.mobileCont:checked~table *[class=contentInteractive] { display: block !important; }
```
This pattern has near-zero support outside Apple Mail. In Gmail, Outlook, and Yahoo the menu will either be permanently hidden or permanently visible depending on the fallback state. No graceful degradation is present.

**`position: absolute` and CSS `transition` in email**
Mobile styles include:
```css
*[class].abs { position: absolute !important; right: 0 !important; }
*[class].menu { transition: opacity .25s ease-in-out; position: absolute !important; }
```
Both properties are unsupported in email rendering engines. They will be silently ignored, potentially breaking layout on clients that do parse the rule partially (older Android Gmail app).

**Three commented-out viewport tags indicate unresolved template debt**
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
Dead code. Remove to reduce parse weight and avoid confusion during future template edits.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`@user` (SHA-256 hash) is assigned inside an HTML comment — will not execute**

The only `SET` statement visible for `@user` is:
```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->
```
AMPscript inside an HTML comment is not parsed by the SFMC rendering engine. If `@user` is referenced in link parameters (e.g., for audience matching or analytics), every subscriber will receive an empty or null value. This silently corrupts any downstream identity resolution.

**`@emailid` has ambiguous sourcing**
The comment block documents two resolution paths — from the sendable DE directly, or via a `Lookup()` against `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated` — but the chosen path is not visible in the truncated source. If the Lookup path is active and a subscriber's email is absent from that DE, `@emailid` returns empty with no error.

**`@uEm` and `@cmdid` depend on `NM_CRM_Feed` join**
Both variables require a successful `Lookup()` against `NM_CRM_Feed`. Subscribers not present in the CRM feed (e.g., new opt-ins not yet synced) will silently receive empty values. No fallback is documented.

---

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header not detected**
QA finding. Gmail and Yahoo require a `List-Unsubscribe` header for bulk senders (>5,000/day). Its absence — even if attributable to AgentMail relay stripping — means the mailbox-provider-level unsubscribe button may not appear, increasing spam complaints.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) not detected**
QA finding. One-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy since February 2024) depends on this header alongside `List-Unsubscribe`. Without it, compliance with current bulk sender requirements is not confirmed.

**SPF/DKIM authentication status unknown**
QA finding: `Authentication-Results` header absent from captured message. Sending domain is `e.neimanmarcus.com` (SFMC subdomain). SPF and DKIM alignment cannot be verified from available data. Unauthenticated mail from a high-volume sender risks DMARC-based filtering.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM parameters unverifiable**
All 34 links are behind click-redirect URLs; final destination URLs and query strings could not be inspected. It is unknown whether UTM tags (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) are appended before or after redirect resolution, or whether `@refid`/`@emailid` are correctly threaded into link parameters given the tokenization issues above.

**`@refid` no-op risks campaign attribution gap**
If `@refid` is used to populate a `cm_mmc` or custom analytics parameter, the missing suffix (see §2) means campaign segmentation data will be incomplete in site analytics.
