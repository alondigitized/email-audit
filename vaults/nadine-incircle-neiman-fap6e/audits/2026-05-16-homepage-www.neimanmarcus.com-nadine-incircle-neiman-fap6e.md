# Homepage snapshot · www.neimanmarcus.com · 2026-05-16
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- I opened Neiman Marcus on my phone and instead of the homepage I got a locked door — a bot-detection wall telling me my device looks suspicious. There is no hero, no campaign, no merchandise. The brand I've shopped for twenty years is greeting me with an error message and an IP address. This is not a homepage review; this is a damage-control situation.

## What's working

- The Neiman Marcus wordmark renders correctly at the top, so brand identity is at least intact at a glance
- The "Submit feedback" link is present, which is at minimum a lifeline if a real customer lands here

## What's weak

- The entire homepage is replaced by a bot-detection interstitial — a paying InCircle member sees zero product, zero campaign, zero loyalty acknowledgment
- The error copy lists "rapid taps or clicks" as a possible cause, which is exactly how I'd browse on a phone; it makes me feel accused rather than helped
- Listing a raw IP address (`75.140.9.0`) and a UUID (`d4028...`) is meaningless to me and feels cold and technical, not premium
- No guidance on how to get through — "submit feedback" is not a solution when I want to shop
- The blurred background teases a lifestyle image I can't see, which is more frustrating than a blank screen

## Recommendations

- **Whitelist mobile browsing patterns** — fast taps and swipes are normal phone behavior; the challenge threshold is too aggressive for legitimate shoppers
- **If a gate must exist, brand it**: replace the plain modal with NM-designed copy and tone ("We want to make sure it's you — one moment") rather than a technical error dump
- **Add an authenticated bypass**: InCircle members who are logged in should never hit this wall; session tokens should signal legitimate user
- **Remove the IP/UUID from the consumer-facing view** — that belongs in a backend log, not on my phone screen
