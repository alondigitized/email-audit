# Things We Love
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-05**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, image-led editorial send with no offer. Neiman Marcus uses the Loewe Whisker bag as the hero, then a row of contemporary jackets, two designer pairs (Marc Jacobs and Gianvito Rossi shoes, Creed and Mind Games fragrance), and an app and stylist footer. It looks expensive and the hero is strong. But it is a catalog with a dozen small "Shop Now" links and no reason to click today.
- It also lands in a heavy week. Veronica Beard's Fall 2026 collection, the Chloé collection, "The Roundup: Chic Shoulder Bags" and the 15% beauty promos all went out from this sender in the last few days. A Loewe shoulder bag is close to a repeat of the Oct 2 bag roundup. A subscriber has seen this move already.
- One visible bug: the middle jacket in the contemporary row is labeled "BRAND NAME", a placeholder that was never filled in. On a luxury send that reads sloppy. Judgment: handsome but low-urgency, a 5 out of 10 send.

## What's working

- The Loewe Whisker bag hero is large and sharp, and the eye lands on it first.
- Small, quiet captions ("THE WHISKER BAG" plus one line of copy) keep the luxury tone without shouting.
- The mix of categories (bags, jackets, shoes, fragrance) gives most shoppers something relevant.
- The fall styling fits the season, and nothing in the fine print feels sneaky.
- The footer (app, personal stylist, store finder) is tidy and useful.

## What's weak

- The middle jacket in the "Brand New Styles Just Landed" row is captioned "BRAND NAME", a visible placeholder.
- There is no offer, deadline or loyalty hook, so there is no reason to click today.
- Every CTA is a small underlined "Shop Now" text link. There are no buttons, and the links don't say what they lead to.
- The three-jacket row has no prices or product names, and its single "Shop Contemporary" link is generic.
- The Loewe bag hero echoes the Oct 2 shoulder-bag roundup, and the sender's cadence is heavy.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the "BRAND NAME" caption and name the real designer. It's a quick fix, and it's the only visible error.
- 2. Replace the text links with real buttons and specific copy, such as "Shop the Whisker Bag" or "Shop Loewe", and put a price on the hero.
- 3. Add one reason to act now, such as a "just landed" or low-stock cue, or a member perk.
- 4. Cut the grid to the hero plus three or four products with prices, so the send stops feeling like a catalog.
- 5. Space out the editorial sends, or vary the theme, so the Loewe bag doesn't read as a repeat of the bag roundup.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- No broken links confirmed. The QA run skipped 20 tracking/click-redirect URLs ("skipped HTTP probe"), so redirect health, final destinations and expiry are **unverified**.
- The truncated source contains no `href` values, so I can't comment on UTM or link structure.
- **Plain-text part:** 3460 of 3979 characters (about 87%) are URLs, against a 70% threshold. This is consistent with long tracking URLs written out in full in the text alternative. It raises spam-filter risk and makes the text version hard to read.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom blocked:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">`. `maximum-scale=1` disables pinch-zoom on some mobile clients, which conflicts with WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
- **No language declared:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang` attribute, so screen readers can't select a pronunciation profile.
- **Weak `<title>`:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has a trailing space and no descriptive content. Some clients and screen readers use it.
- **Dead code in `<head>`:** three commented-out viewport variants remain (`user-scalable=no`, `width=320, initial-scale=0.29`, `width=1024`). They are harmless but clutter the source.
- **CSS-only hamburger nav:** The CSS uses the `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu` pattern. Gmail and several Outlook builds don't support `:checked`. If there is no fallback markup, the nav could be unreachable in those clients. This is inferred from CSS only and is not confirmed, because the body is truncated.
- **Images/alt text:** not assessable from the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

## 5. Compliance
- **`List-Unsubscribe` not found** (QA WARN). The tool notes the header may not be captured by the AgentMail relay, so absence is **unconfirmed**.
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` not found** (RFC 8058) (QA WARN). Same caveat. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender requirements expect one-click unsubscribe.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Strip the template comment** (the `REQUIRED variables…CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO` block and the `SET @user` line) in the send pipeline, or move it out of the deliverable. It leaks DE and field names.
2. **Remove `maximum-scale=1`** from the viewport meta (use `width=device-width, initial-scale=1`).
3. **Add `lang="en"`** to `<html>` and give the `<title>` descriptive text (for example the subject line).
4. **Check the hamburger nav in Gmail and Outlook.** Confirm a non-`:checked` fallback exists, or that the nav links are in the footer.
5. **Shorten the plain-text part** by using short or branded link wrappers, or by trimming repeated product URLs, to get below the 70% URL ratio.
6. **Verify the headers from the raw `.eml` at the source** (a seed inbox such as Gmail's "Show original"). That would confirm `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and SPF/DKIM/DMARC, which the relay prevents here.
7. **Re-run QA on the full HTML**, not the truncated excerpt. Then check the following items:
   - Link and UTM consistency.
   - Image alt text.
   - The unsubscribe link and footer address.
   - Tracking-link resolution.
