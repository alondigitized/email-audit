# 1,000+ styles just arrived
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-19**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean new-arrivals catalog send with no offer behind it. A black hero reads "BRAND NEW STYLES JUST LANDED." Below it are two 3×2 product grids, one for women and one for men, each followed by an outline "Shop All … New Arrivals" button. The designer labels at Cult Gaia, Saint Laurent, Thom Browne, Santoni, Burberry, Cartier and Moncler are what carry the email.
- It looks premium but gives the reader little reason to act now. There is no price, no discount, no deadline and no loyalty hook. After four First Call markdown sends in a week, the Oct 18 gift-card push and the Oct 18 Tom Ford editorial, this is the quiet, low-pressure send in the rotation. It suits browsers and does little for anyone who needs an incentive.
- The hero is also weak. It is a text-only black block with no imagery. Its two CTAs are small underlined text links ("Shop Women's," "Shop Men's"), not buttons. The product grids are the real hero and sit below it. Overall: a tidy, honest send that is under-powered. The subject promises "1,000+ styles," but the render never shows that number.

## What's working

- The layout is clean and easy to scan: two grids, labeled by designer, with generous white space and no visible render bugs.
- The women's and men's split is clear, and each half has its own "Shop All" button.
- The designer names (Saint Laurent, Cartier, Moncler, Burberry) signal luxury credibility.
- The products show variety, from sandals, a bomber and a tank to a bag, jeans, a shoe and eyewear.
- The black hero is on-brand and the type is legible.

## What's weak

- There is no offer, price, member benefit or deadline, so nothing creates urgency.
- The hero is a text block with two small underlined links where it needs buttons, and it has no product image.
- The "1,000+" claim from the subject never appears in the body.
- No individual product shows a price, and the tiles don't appear to link to specific items.
- The "Shop All" outline buttons are low-contrast and easy to miss.
- It's a lot of near-identical tiles with no hero product or editorial angle, so nothing stands out.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the subject's hook in the hero. Show "1,000+ new styles" with a lead image, add a visible filled "Shop New Arrivals" button, and surface one hook such as new-in-this-week or a member benefit.
- 2. Show prices or "from $" on the tiles, and make each tile link to its product page.
- 3. Turn the hero text links into filled buttons.
- 4. Segment by gender or category affinity so recipients get the women's or men's grid first, rather than a combined catalog.
- 5. Cut the grid to two or three curated picks with a short styling hook, so it doesn't read as a dump of 12 tiles.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New this week: Saint Laurent, Cartier & more`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Just landed: fall's newest designer arrivals`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop 1,000+ new styles from Cult Gaia, Moncler, Burberry & more.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible HTML is a table-based XHTML Transitional template with a mobile media-query layer. Two confirmed issues are the internal ESP variable and data-extension comment left in the delivered source, and the `maximum-scale=1` viewport. Compliance and authentication could not be verified, because the headers weren't captured and the source is truncated before the footer.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **30 tracking/click-redirect links were not probed** (QA INFO). Destinations, redirect chains and status codes are unverified.
- **Plain-text part is more than 70% URLs** (5726 of 6531 chars). This is consistent with long tracking URLs in the text alternative. It is a spam-filter and readability risk.
- Individual `href` values fall outside the truncated source, so no broken or malformed links can be confirmed.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **`<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">`** restricts pinch-zoom in clients that honor it (WCAG 1.4.4).
- **Exact-match class selectors** such as `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=mobile]` and `*[class=hide]` only match elements whose entire `class` value is that token. Any element given a second class silently loses its mobile override.
- **Checkbox-hack hamburger nav** (`.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu`, `.hamburger`, `.close`) depends on `:checked` support, which Gmail and most Outlook versions lack. The fallback is not visible in the truncated source, so I can't confirm whether the nav degrades gracefully.
- **Global `p { margin: 0 auto; text-align: center; }`** affects every `<p>` in the template, so any paragraph meant to be left-aligned needs an inline override. I can't confirm this causes a defect.
- **`<title>Neiman Marcus </title>`** has a trailing space. This is cosmetic.
- `alt` text, `role="presentation"` on layout tables and image dimensions fall outside the truncated source and can't be assessed.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **The delivered HTML opens with a developer comment** containing ESP variable logic and internal data-extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`) and field names (`EMAIL_ID`, `uEm`, `cmdid`). It also contains an unbalanced-parenthesis `@user Lookup(...))` line and the instruction "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW". This is internal template scaffolding that should be stripped at send time. It exposes infrastructure details to any recipient who views source, and it adds payload size.
- No unresolved tokens (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`, `@var`) appear in the rendered portion that was visible.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` header not found** (QA WARN). The QA note says this may be an AgentMail relay artifact, so it is unconfirmed.
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found** (QA WARN). One-click unsubscribe support is unconfirmed. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require it.
- **`Authentication-Results` not found** (QA WARN). SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. The QA note attributes this to the relay.
- The visible unsubscribe link and physical postal address are in the truncated footer and can't be verified.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not assessable. Link targets fall outside the truncated source, and the tracking redirects were not resolved. The only evidence is the comment's `@refid`/`@base_refid` variables, which suggests campaign IDs are appended to links. That is unconfirmed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Strip the leading ESP variable comment** (and the other commented-out viewport meta tags) from the sent output.
2. **Remove `maximum-scale=1`** from the viewport meta tag to allow zoom.
3. **Check the raw MIME headers** from the origin, not the relay, for `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment. Treat the three WARNs as unverified until then.
4. **Shorten or reduce the URLs in the plain-text part**, or make it a purpose-built text version with fewer tracked links.
5. **Resolve the 30 tracking links** with a redirect-following probe to confirm final destinations, status codes and UTM/refid preservation.
6. **Replace exact-match `[class=…]` selectors** with `[class~=…]` or `.class`, and confirm the hamburger nav has a non-`:checked` fallback in Gmail and Outlook.
7. **Re-run the audit on the full, untruncated HTML** to cover the footer, unsubscribe link, alt text and link parameters.
