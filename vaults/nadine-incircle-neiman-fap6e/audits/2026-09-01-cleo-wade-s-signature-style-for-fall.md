# Cleo Wade's signature style for fall
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-01**
## Executive summary

- This is a total tonal departure from Neiman Marcus's run of $250-off gift-card blasts and product-grid roundups (8/26–9/1) — a celebrity/creator editorial built around poet-artist Cleo Wade, styled in three lifestyle shots with a "shop the look" product callout at the bottom. It's the most premium-feeling creative in the recent stretch: warm blush background, elegant serif type, a real narrative voice ("I don't put on anything if it doesn't make me smile"). It reads like a Vogue editorial, not a retail blast.
- But that's also the problem — there is zero discount, zero urgency, and zero price anywhere in this email, at a moment when every other Neiman Marcus send this week has been training subscribers to wait for the next $250-off cycle. A brand storytelling piece can work, but it needs to convert somewhere concrete, and this one asks the reader to "Shop the Story" or "Shop the Look" with no idea what anything costs.
- Judgement: beautifully executed brand content, weak as a commerce email. It'll perform on brand-lift and dwell time, not on click-to-cart.

## What's working

- Strong, distinctive visual identity — hand-lettered "Fall 2026," blush palette, and editorial photography that doesn't look like a template.
- A named creator (Cleo Wade) with a real quote gives the email a point of view other NM sends this week lacked.
- The "Shop the Look" product flat-lay at the bottom clearly ties the editorial back to purchasable items (Proenza Schouler, Carolina Herrera, Christian Louboutin, Emily P. Wheeler) with brand names visible.
- Clean render — no overlapping text, broken images, or layout breaks.

## What's weak

- No price, discount, or offer anywhere in the email — a hard miss against the persona focus of Skechers/value-shopper, and out of step with the $250-off cadence NM has run all week.
- Subject "Cleo Wade's signature style for fall" assumes reader familiarity with a specific poet/influencer; if you don't recognize the name, there's no hook.
- Two full screens of mood photography before any product appears — heavy scroll burden on mobile before the CTA that actually matters.
- "Shop the Story" and "Shop the Look" are both vague, generic CTA copy — neither tells you what you're buying or why.
- No loyalty/InCircle member benefit surfaced, despite NM having a rewards program worth calling out.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a concrete price or perk to the "Shop the Look" set (e.g., "InCircle members save 15% on this look") — right now there's nothing to click for beyond curiosity.
- 2. Move a product thumbnail or price tag higher, before the second lifestyle photo, so mobile readers aren't scrolling blind.
- 3. Rewrite the CTAs to name the actual product or category instead of "Shop the Story" / "Shop the Look."
- 4. Give Cleo Wade a one-line credibility tag in the subject or hero (e.g., "bestselling author") for readers who don't already know her.
