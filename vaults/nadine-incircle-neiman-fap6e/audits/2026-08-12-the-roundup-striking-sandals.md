# The Roundup: Striking Sandals
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-12**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, editorial designer-sandal roundup — a hero shot of a zebra-print pony-hair Stuart Weitzman mule up top, then a tidy 3x2 grid of six sandals from Givenchy, Jacquemus, Christian Louboutin, Stuart Weitzman, Bottega Veneta, and Versace, each simply labeled by brand with no price shown. It's the most product-focused, least discount-driven send in this recent Neiman batch — no countdown clock, no "EXTRA 25% OFF" slab, no last-call urgency, just curated taste-making. That's a real point of differentiation next to 8/10's Last Call reskin and 8/12's dusty-blue Sale hero.
- The tradeoff is that "taste-making" without prices or a discount is a much softer ask than the sale sends — this is a browse invitation, not a conversion push, and it reads that way. It's well-executed for what it is, but it's a mid-funnel content email, not a revenue email, and the subject undersells even that.

## What's working

- Hero image is striking and on-theme — the zebra-print mule justifies "Striking Sandals" literally and visually.
- Clean, scannable 3x2 grid with consistent product photography angles and clear brand labels.
- Single unambiguous CTA ("Shop Sandals") placed right under the headline, easy to spot.
- No render bugs — every thumbnail loaded, spacing is even, nothing overlaps.
- Genuine variety in silhouette (stiletto, flat mule, kitten heel, block heel) gives the "roundup" concept real substance.

## What's weak

- No prices anywhere — for $700-1200 designer sandals, that's a real click deterrent; shoppers want to gut-check spend before tapping through.
- No individual product links implied per item — six named products but one generic "Shop Sandals" CTA means no direct path to the Louboutin or Bottega piece someone actually wants.
- Zero urgency or offer — fine for a content play, but nothing nudges a browse into a purchase this week.
- Subject line "The Roundup: Striking Sandals" is vague trend-editorial language, easy to skip past for someone scanning for an offer.
- No loyalty/InCircle callout despite this being a full-price, high-consideration purchase where member perks (points, early access) would be a natural add.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add prices (or "from $X") under each product name — this is the single biggest lift; high-consideration purchases need cost visible before the click.
- 2. Make each product image/name individually clickable to its own PDP, not just one blanket "Shop Sandals" button.
- 3. Add a one-line InCircle hook ("Earn 2X points on Shoes this week") to give a full-price purchase an extra reason to convert now.
- 4. Sharpen the subject to name-check a recognizable brand or trend detail for curiosity + specificity.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `From kitten heels to statement mules — shop the edit`
