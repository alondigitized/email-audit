# Up to $500 off your purchase starts now
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-25**
## Executive summary

- Neiman Marcus swaps the personalized 25%-off ladder and the Last Call blowout for a blunt, sitewide "Up to $500 off" sitewide-discount play — a straightforward spend/save tier table (spend $200, save $50 … spend $2,000+, save $500) sitting under a clean blue hero. It's tidy and legible, with a well-organized nine-item product grid spanning Mac Duggal gowns to Jacquemus sneakers, so there's something for nearly every shopper browsing the email.
- But it's also the least distinctive send in this stretch. After the "Tick. Tock." Last Call Sale (7/23, 9/10), the Extra 25% stack (7/24, 9/10), and the "1,000+ new arrivals" reset (7/23, 8/10), this is just another generic dollars-off-your-purchase email with a promo code, no product story, and no urgency beyond a buried "Ends 7/27" in the fine print. It reads like a mid-tier filler send between the sharper urgency and editorial plays NM has been running.
- The tiered discount math is a genuinely strong mechanic — savvy shoppers can see exactly what they get at their basket size — but the subject line completely wastes it by staying vague ("your purchase") instead of leading with the tier structure itself.

## What's working

- The spend/save table is a clean, scannable value visual — five tiers laid out with dollar precision, no math required.
- Hero hierarchy is textbook: "LIMITED TIME" → "UP TO $500 OFF" → subhead → code → CTA, all in the first screen.
- The nine-product grid is well-curated across categories (dresses, denim, shoes, menswear) and renders with no broken images or layout issues.
- Secondary nav grid (Shop Women's Clothing, Shop Men's, Shop Handbags, etc.) gives multiple exit ramps for shoppers not drawn to the hero grid.
- "Exclusions Apply" is disclosed directly under the offer table rather than hidden only in the footer.

## What's weak

- No hero imagery or models anywhere — it's flat-lay/product-only, so there's zero aspirational or seasonal visual pull compared to the Prada editorial send (7/24, 7/10).
- Subject and hero copy never say what the discount excludes; the real fine print (exclusives, beauty, fragrance, gift cards, Bergdorf Goodman, Saks Global employees) is dense and buried at the very bottom.
- CTA copy is the generic "SHOP NOW" repeated with no category or product hook — a missed chance to point to the strongest grid item (the Mac Duggal gown or Jimmy Choo slingbacks).
- This is the fourth-plus promo-flavored send in six days from this brand; nothing here differentiates it from the Last Call or Extra 25% blasts already sent.
- The actual expiration date ("Ends 7/27/26 at 10:00 PM CT") is hidden in body-copy fine print instead of being surfaced in the hero or subject, wasting real urgency.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the tier mechanic in the subject line — it's the most concrete, differentiating thing about this send and it's currently invisible until open.
- 2. Surface the real deadline ("Ends Monday 7/27") in the hero, not buried in six-point legal copy — that's the urgency lever this email is missing.
- 3. Swap "SHOP NOW" for a category-specific CTA under the hero (e.g., "Shop Women's" / "Shop New Arrivals") to route intent instead of dumping everyone on the homepage.
- 4. Add one lifestyle/model image near the top — every product shot is a flat lay, which flattens the whole email next to NM's editorial sends.
- 5. Trim the exclusion list down to the two or three that actually matter to most shoppers (beauty/fragrance, gift cards) and drop the rest to a linked terms page.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Spend $200, save $50 — up to $500 off ends Monday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your $500 discount is live (for a limited time)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The more you spend, the more you save — see your tier inside.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New arrivals from Jimmy Choo, Rag & Bone, and Jacquemus, all discount-eligible.`
