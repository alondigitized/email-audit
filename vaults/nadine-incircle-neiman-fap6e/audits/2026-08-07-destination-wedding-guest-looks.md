# Destination wedding-guest looks
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-07**
## Executive summary

- This is the strongest editorial send from Neiman Marcus I've reviewed in this batch — a hero image of a model in a wind-swept pale-yellow gown that actually earns the "Dreamy Days Are Here to Stay" copy, followed by a clean "Shop the Look" outfit build (Oscar de la Renta gown, Lana earrings, Judith Leiber Couture clutch, Jimmy Choo sandals). It's a real departure from the discount-mechanic sends that have dominated the brand history — 8/6's "Extra 40% Off" escalation, 8/5's gift-card repeat, 8/4's beach editorial — and lands closer in spirit to 8/1's "Field Notes" horse-and-pantsuit send.
- The problem is the subject line doesn't match the email. "Destination wedding-guest looks" promises a curated multi-look guest edit; what's actually inside is a single bridal/formal-gown hero plus one outfit-of-the-day styled around an Oscar de la Renta gown that reads more "black-tie gala" than "wedding guest." There's no second or third guest look, no venue/dress-code framing (beach, garden, black-tie), and the "Shop Gowns / Shop Wedding" CTAs suggest this is really a bridal-adjacent push wearing a wedding-guest subject line for open rate.
- It's a beautiful, high-craft email let down by a subject-to-content mismatch and zero price/occasion signal — worth opening for the visual, but it won't convert someone actually shopping for a specific wedding.

## What's working

- Hero image is genuinely striking — the motion in the fabric and natural-light setting differentiate it from the brand's usual studio/lifestyle shots.
- Clear visual hierarchy: hero → headline → two CTA buttons → single curated look, no clutter.
- The "Shop the Look" module (dress + jewelry + bag + shoes) is a smart merchandising format that shows full outfit context, not just one SKU.
- No render bugs — clean layout, images load, nav and footer are intact.
- Two distinct CTAs ("Shop Gowns," "Shop Wedding") give the reader a fork depending on intent.

## What's weak

- Subject says "wedding-guest looks" (plural) but the email delivers one gown-focused hero and one outfit — not a "looks" roundup by dress code or venue.
- Zero price visibility anywhere — no starting-at price, no "from $X," nothing to anchor expectation before clicking into what's likely a $2K+ gown.
- No occasion segmentation (beach wedding vs. black-tie vs. garden) despite the subject implying exactly that kind of practical guidance.
- The featured look (a coral one-shoulder gown + statement clutch) reads more like a gala outfit than "wedding guest" — a real guest rarely wants to out-dress the bride.
- Preview text is missing/junk (address-book copy leaks through), wasting valuable inbox real estate right under a strong subject.

## Recommendations

- 1. Rewrite the subject to match what's actually inside, or add a second look to match the subject's promise — pick one. Given the asset (one gown hero + one outfit), the subject should narrow to what's shown rather than promise a roundup.
- 2. Fix the preview text — right now it's leaking "add to your address book" copy instead of real preview copy that complements the subject.
- 3. Add a starting price or "from $X" near the "Shop the Look" module — a coral gown + Judith Leiber clutch outfit without any price anchor is a click-suppressor for anyone budget-conscious.
- 4. Add occasion sub-navigation under the hero (Beach / Garden / Black-Tie) if the subject is going to promise "looks" plural — this is the single highest-leverage fix for matching intent to content.
- 5. Swap the featured gown for something visibly less bridal-adjacent (the coral gown reads formal-gala, not guest-appropriate) or explicitly label it "Black-Tie Wedding" to set the right expectation.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `One gown, four ways to finish the look.`
