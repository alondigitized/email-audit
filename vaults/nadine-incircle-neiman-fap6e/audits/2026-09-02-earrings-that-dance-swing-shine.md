# Earrings that dance, swing & shine
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-02**
## Executive summary

- This is a total category swing from the gift-card churn Neiman Marcus has been running nearly daily since 8/26 (most recently 9/1's "Ends soon: up to $250 off," 5.00/10) — a clean, single-category earrings editorial with a striking hero portrait, a tight "All Ears" headline, and three designer product callouts (Lana, Bottega Veneta, Alexis Bittar). The model shot is genuinely well-composed and the earrings are the actual subject, not an afterthought.
- But it's a zero-discount, zero-urgency send — same fatal flaw that's dragged the last several category/roundup emails (8/30's crossbody roundup, 8/31's new-arrivals) to 6/10 territory. There's no price, no percentage off, no "new," no seasonal hook — just a product category with a pun for a headline. It's tasteful but it gives a browsing subscriber zero reason to act today versus next week.
- Solid creative, weak commercial mechanics — worth sending occasionally to break up the discount fatigue, but it won't move click-through on its own.

## What's working

- Hero image is sharp, well-lit, and puts the earrings front and center on the model — no render bugs.
- "All Ears" is a tight, on-category pun that's memorable without being cringey.
- Two clear CTAs ("Shop Earrings," "Shop Jewelry") give a direct next step.
- Three-item product grid names real designers (Lana, Bottega Veneta, Alexis Bittar) with a range of styles (statement, drop, hoop).
- Clean visual hierarchy — eye goes hero → headline → CTAs → products in order.

## What's weak

- No offer at all — no price, no % off, no member perk — in a brand history full of $250-off blasts, this reads like a non-sequitur rather than a deliberate full-price editorial moment.
- No urgency or seasonality signal — could run any week of the year.
- Subject promises earrings "that dance, swing & shine" but only one earring style (the hero's fringe drops) actually dances/swings; the Bottega and Alexis Bittar pieces are static drops/hoops — slight overpromise.
- No pricing shown on any of the three products, so there's no way to gauge fit against a personal budget before clicking.
- No social proof, no "new arrival" flag, no styling context (occasion, pairing) to justify the click beyond "these are earrings."

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a price or price range under each product (or at minimum "from $X") — right now there's zero information to decide if this is impulse-buy or splurge territory before clicking through.
- 2. Give the send a reason to exist today: "new in," a mini seasonal tie-in (fall dressing), or a modest loyalty perk — anything to differentiate it from a browse-anytime category page.
- 3. Tighten the subject-to-content match — either add a third dynamic/dangly piece to the grid or trim the subject to match what's actually shown (e.g., focus on "shine" and "swing" only).
- 4. Add one line of styling copy under "All Ears" (e.g., "Statement drops for day-to-night") to give the category emotional context instead of just a pun.
