# Want up to 70% off? Last Call starts now
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-08**
## Executive summary

- This is Neiman Marcus's Last Call launch — a red hero block announcing an extra 25% off select sale styles, stacking on top of up to 70% off total. The creative is clean and on-brand: recognizable NM typographic hierarchy, a tight product grid of seven designer items, and a six-button category strip below. The offer is the most aggressive this brand has sent in the last week, topping the 60% off Designer Sale from July 7.
- The problem is credibility damage. Subscribers have now watched this brand escalate from 40% → 60% → 70% in three days. "Last Call" as a label has real meaning — it's NM's markdown event brand — but landing here after a "Last day for up to 60% off" closer yesterday means the "last day" signal has been burned. The subscriber who opened yesterday's email and didn't buy got rewarded with a bigger discount today, which trains exactly the wrong behavior.
- The email itself executes well; the campaign strategy is what's hurting it.

## What's working

- **Hero offer is instant and legible.** Red block, "EXTRA 25% OFF," and "LAST CALL" — the eye lands in under a second.
- **Stacking math is visible.** "For up to 70% off" explains the mechanic immediately; no mental math required.
- **Product grid is strong.** Seven named designer items (Akris, Stella McCartney, L'Agence, Versace, FRAME, MOTHER, A.L.C., Oscar de la Renta, Joe's Jeans) across two rows — variety covers women's ready-to-wear, handbag, and denim shopper.
- **Category strip is functional.** Six shop buttons (Women's Clothing, Men's, Handbags, Shoes, Kids, Women's Dresses) give clear navigation paths beyond the grid.
- **15% loyalty bonus CTA** at the footer is a clean upsell for non-members — specific offer, not vague.

## What's weak

- **"Last Call" after "Last day" kills urgency.** Yesterday's email declared the Designer Sale's final hours. Today's email says a new sale "starts now." Real subscribers notice that math.
- **No visible deadline.** "Last Call starts now" implies scarcity, but there's no end date anywhere in the visible email — no "through Sunday," no countdown. The urgency is asserted, not proven.
- **Product images are tiny.** Two-row grid with small thumbnails at email width means fine details (fabric, color, silhouette) are hard to evaluate — particularly relevant for the $800+ category shopper.
- **"Exclusions Apply" is the only fine print visible** — fine, but paired with a stacking offer, subscribers will wonder what's actually excluded.
- **No personalization visible.** No "Hi [Name]," no "based on your browsing" — this reads as a blast to the full list, same as every other send this week.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor the deadline.** Add "Ends Sunday" or a visible date to the hero block. Without it, "Last Call" is a brand name, not urgency. This is the highest-leverage single change.
- 2. **Explain the stack mechanic more explicitly.** "Extra 25% off items already up to 65% off" is more compelling than "for up to 70% off" — show the before/after math or at least signal it's already-reduced merchandise.
- 3. **Lead with the product, not the discount.** Feature one hero product at the 70%-off price before the grid. A specific $1,200 dress marked to $360 is more motivating than a percentage claim.
- 4. **Acknowledge the cadence.** A one-liner like "Yes, we went bigger — this is the real Last Call" would actually land well with a subscriber who noticed yesterday's "last day" messaging, and it's honest.
- 5. **Test a personalized subject variant** segmented by category browse history.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last Call: extra 25% off an already-60%-off sale`
- **Subject Alt B:** `70% off starts now — this one ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your 15% member bonus stacks too. Shop before it closes.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Designer styles at their lowest price of the season — ends soon.`

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Last Call" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a Salesforce Marketing Cloud (AMPscript) template with standard table-based layout and responsive CSS. No critical rendering bugs are present, but two compliance gaps and one deliverability risk need attention before this template pattern is reused.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 tracking links skipped (not probed)**
All click-through URLs route through a redirect/tracking domain (likely `click.e.neimanmarcus.com` or similar). QA cannot confirm destination URLs resolve correctly or that UTM parameters are intact — these must be manually spot-checked or probed from a non-relay IP.

**AMPscript `@refid` is a no-op concatenation**
```
@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")
```
This concatenates with an empty string — functionally identical to just assigning `@base_refid`. If `@refid` was intended to append a suffix (e.g., campaign variant code), this is a silent bug. Confirm whether `@refid` differs from `@base_refid` in any downstream tracking logic.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Commented-out conflicting viewport meta tags**
Three additional `<meta name="viewport">` declarations are commented out but still present in the `<head>`:
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
These are dead code and suggest viewport sizing was iterated without cleanup. No active rendering impact, but increases parse weight and creates confusion during future edits.

**`<title>` has trailing whitespace**
```html
<title>Neiman Marcus </title>
```
Minor; some email clients surface the title as a tooltip or preview label.

**`-webkit-animation: bugfix` iOS 9 hack**
```css
body { -webkit-animation: bugfix infinite 1s; }
```
This is a known workaround for an iOS 9 hamburger nav CSS bug. iOS 9 is end-of-life (2016). This runs a perpetual animation on `body` in every render — remove it.

**No `alt` text visible in truncated source**
The HTML is truncated before image tags appear. Alt text coverage on product images cannot be confirmed — this must be verified in the full source. Missing alt text breaks accessibility and causes broken text display in image-off clients (common in enterprise/Outlook).

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Multiple lookup paths for the same identity fields**
```
@emailid — from sendable DE if available, otherwise Lookup to Subscriber_New
@user — Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", ...)
@uEm — Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", ...)
@cmdid — Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", ...)
```
`@emailid` has a conditional fallback; `@user` and `@emailid` both resolve `EMAIL_ID` from the same table for the same `EMAIL_ADDRESS` key — redundant lookups that add send-time latency. More critically, the comment block says `SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256` is commented out, meaning `@user` resolves to `EMAIL_ID` (a numeric internal ID), not a hashed email. If `@user` is passed to any ad-platform pixel or audience sync expecting SHA-256, this is a data contract violation.

**`@cmdid` unvalidated**
`@cmdid` is looked up from `NM_CRM_Feed` but there is no null-check shown. If a subscriber has no CRM record, `@cmdid` will be empty and any URL that interpolates it will produce a broken or bare parameter (e.g., `?cmdid=`).

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not detected**
```
[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header not found
[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found (RFC 8058)
```
Both headers are missing from what the AgentMail relay captured. Gmail and Yahoo (as of Feb 2024 bulk sender requirements) require `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` on all commercial sends above 5,000/day. Absence triggers suppressed unsubscribe UI and increases spam classification risk. This may be a relay capture gap — confirm against raw SMTP headers from the sending MTA before assuming the headers are truly absent.

**SPF/DKIM authentication status unknown**
```
[WARN] Authentication-Results header not found
```
The relay did not forward `Authentication-Results`. Sending domain is `e.neimanmarcus.com` (ESP subdomain). Cannot confirm SPF pass or DKIM signature validity from captured data alone. Verify via MX Toolbox or a direct SMTP header dump.

**CAN-SPAM physical address**
Not verifiable from the truncated source — confirm the footer contains a valid postal address. Standard for NM but must be present.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**26 tracked links cannot be probed**
UTM parameter coverage, correct destination URLs, and HTTPS validity are all unverifiable from the relay capture. At minimum, spot-check:
- Hero CTA link
- One product deep-link

**`@refid` as the campaign identifier**
If `@refid` is the value injected into UTM `utm_campaign` or similar, the no-op `CONCAT(@base_refid,"")` means there is no campaign variant differentiation in analytics unless `@base_refid` itself carries that suffix. Confirm the attribution chain end-to-end.
