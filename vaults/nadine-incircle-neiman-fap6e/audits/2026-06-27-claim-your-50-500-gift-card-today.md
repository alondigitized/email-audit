# Claim your $50–$500 gift card today
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-27**
## Executive summary

- This is NM's **third touch on the exact same gift card earn promotion** in four days — June 24 launched it ("Your $50–$500 gift card is here," scored 9/10), June 25 was a second touch ("Shop now & earn a $50–$500 gift card," scored 9/10), and now this June 27 send arrives with "Claim your $50–$500 gift card today." The promotion itself is still strong — the spend/earn table is legible, code NM.JUNE is prominent, and the "SHOP & EARN" CTA is clean. Swapping the subject opener from "Your..." to "Claim your..." adds a mild urgency verb, which is smart creative variation, but not enough to mask that the underlying email is structurally identical to the prior two.
- The secondary brand grid — Jimmy Choo, Ulla Johnson, Zimmermann, FRAME, Mac Dugal, Veronica Beard, and others — gives the email actual product content and justifies a browse session, which is a better choice than sending the promo mechanics alone for a third time. That said, the thumbnails are very small and the product images feel interchangeable at a glance.
- The bottom of the email has a dense wall of fine print text — promotional gift card details that occupy a large block — which visually undercuts the clean presentation above it. At cadence send #3, most subscribers have already decided whether they're acting on NM.JUNE. The only way this email earns its place is if it signals a deadline, and it doesn't.

## What's working

- Spend/earn table ($200→$50 / $300→$75 / $500→$125 / $1,000+→$500) is still the most scannable element in the email — clear, legible, and immediately actionable.
- NM.JUNE code is visually prominent and easy to locate.
- "SHOP & EARN" button is unambiguous — verb + action, dark button on white.
- Designer brand grid adds genuine product browse context, making this more than a promo-mechanics-only blast.
- Six category-level CTAs (Women's, Women's Clothing, Shoes, Men's Clothing, Handbags, Home) give different subscribers a clear entry point.

## What's weak

- Third email on the identical promotion in four days — cadence fatigue is real, and the creative hasn't changed enough to justify a standalone send.
- No deadline in the email body — "today" appears in the subject but there's no visible offer expiration in the hero or anywhere above the fine print, which drains the urgency.
- Dense fine print block at the bottom is a wall of small text — it creates visual noise and signals exclusion conditions even if subscribers don't read it.
- Product thumbnails are too small to create desire; the designers are named but the imagery doesn't pull you in.
- No InCircle tier callout or loyalty member differentiation — a subscriber who knows their tier is missing a reason to feel specifically rewarded.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a visible end date in the hero** — "Offer ends July 3" or a countdown would be the single highest-lift change, giving this third send a reason to exist and converting the implicit "today" urgency into a real one.
- 2. **Consolidate the fine print** — replace the dense block with a 2-sentence summary and a "Full terms" link; this would clean up the bottom third of the email significantly.
- 3. **Make one designer product the hero** — surface a specific Jimmy Choo sandal or FRAME denim piece with price, to show what $200+ of earning looks like in practice.
- 4. **Tier the subject by InCircle status** — members who are close to a new tier should see a different message than first-time earn prospects; this is the cadence differentiation that would make send #3 feel targeted rather than repeated.
- **Subject Alt A:** `NM.JUNE ends soon — earn up to $500 back`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Spend $200, earn $50: your gift card window closes`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Offer expires July 3 — use NM.JUNE at checkout on new arrivals`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Jimmy Choo, FRAME, Zimmermann + earn a gift card while you shop`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Multiple commented-out viewport meta tags**
Three conflicting `<meta name="viewport">` declarations are present in comments:
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
These are inert but indicate historical viewport thrashing. The active tag (`initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1`) is fine; the dead code should be cleaned up to avoid future confusion.

**Dual breakpoint media queries**
The template defines responsive rules at both `max-width: 600px` and `max-width/max-device-width: 480px/520px` in separate `<style>` blocks. This is not a bug but increases specificity conflict risk if the template is modified.

**`<title>` has a trailing space**
```html
<title>Neiman Marcus </title>
```
Minor; no rendering impact, but some spam filters flag unnecessary whitespace in headers.

**No issues found** with table-based layout, character encoding (`UTF-8` declared), or iOS auto-link suppression (`a[x-apple-data-detectors]` is correctly implemented).

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**AMPscript variable comment left in production source**
The required-variable block is present as an HTML comment in the live send:
```html
<!--REQUIRED variables: @email (EMAIL_ADDRESS from sendable DE) @base_refid ...-->
```
This is documentation-style commentary, not executable AMPscript, so it does not affect rendering. However, it exposes internal data extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`) and field names (`uEm`, `cmdid`, `EMAIL_ID`) to anyone who views source. This is a minor information-disclosure risk and should be stripped pre-send.

**Scenario selector comment present**
```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_SHA256_EMAIL */ -->
```
This is a dormant AMPscript block inside an HTML comment — it will not execute. If SHA-256 hashing of `@user` is required for any downstream personalization or tracking pixel, this block needs to be activated (remove the outer `<!-- -->`) or confirmed intentionally disabled.

**No unfired merge tokens detected** in the truncated source (no `%%variable%%` literals visible outside of AMPscript blocks).

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not captured** `[WARN]`
The QA relay did not surface this header. CAN-SPAM does not mandate `List-Unsubscribe`, but major inbox providers (Gmail, Yahoo) use it for one-click unsubscribe placement. If the header is set by the ESP at send time and simply not forwarded by the AgentMail relay, this is a relay gap — confirm against a raw `.eml` capture. If it is genuinely absent, it should be added.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not found** `[WARN]`
RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe (`List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`) is absent or not captured. Gmail and Yahoo have required this for bulk senders (>5k/day) since February 2024. This is a compliance gap if Neiman Marcus qualifies as a bulk sender — which, for a national promotional campaign, it almost certainly does.

**`Authentication-Results` header not captured** `[WARN]`
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status cannot be confirmed from this capture. The sending domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` is an ESP subdomain — verify that DKIM is signed for this subdomain and that the `neimanmarcus.com` DMARC policy covers it (or that `e.neimanmarcus.com` has its own aligned record).

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 26 links are wrapped in the ESP click-tracker and were not probed, so UTM parameter presence cannot be confirmed. The following should be verified against a decoded sample URL:

- `utm_source` set to the ESP/channel (e.g., `email`)
- `utm_medium=email`

The `@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid, "")` pattern in the AMPscript header suggests a campaign ID is being appended to links — confirm it is non-empty at send time and that the landing page accepts/passes it downstream.
