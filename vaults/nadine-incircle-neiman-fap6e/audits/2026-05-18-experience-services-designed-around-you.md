# Experience services designed around you
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- This is a brand-services awareness email — no discount, no deadline, no concrete value exchange. Neiman Marcus is using it to introduce (or remind subscribers of) three services: personal style advisors, click & collect, and in-store beauty consultations. The "Shopping Made Personal" headline is aspirational but generic, and the four separate CTAs ("Explore Our Services," "Find Your Match," "Learn More," "Learn More") scatter attention rather than driving toward one clear action.
- The bigger problem is cadence. This is the fourth Neiman Marcus email in three days. Yesterday's designer sale email ("Your exclusive access ends tonight!") was a 9/10 — urgent, specific, and well-executed. Following that with an offer-free services tour feels like a step backward that will accelerate unsubscribes. A subscriber who just acted (or decided not to act) on a hard-deadline sale is not primed to click through a corporate services brochure the very next morning.
- The email is clean, renders well, and the photography is on-brand. But without a hook — no exclusive perk, no sample offer surfaced prominently, no personalization — it reads as filler. It earns its place only if Neiman Marcus is deliberately running a nurture sequence; even then, the subject and CTA work are too soft to carry it.

## What's working

- **Clean layout, no render issues.** Three clearly separated service sections with consistent image/headline/CTA structure — easy to scan.
- **"YOUR STYLE SOULMATE" section is the strongest unit.** The red heel image is visually arresting, and "Find Your Match" is the most specific CTA in the email.
- **Hero copy is honest.** "We've designed thoughtful, innovative services" sets accurate expectations — no bait-and-switch.
- **Footer utility links are well-organized.** Store finder, contact, and account links are clean and accessible.

## What's weak

- **No offer.** Four days ago there was 15% off; yesterday there was a designer sale. Today: nothing. The drop-off in value will feel abrupt to anyone in the welcome sequence.
- **"Learn More" x2 is wasted CTA real estate.** Two consecutive sections ending in identical, vague button copy tell the reader nothing about what they're clicking into.
- **"Beauty Your Way" copy appears truncated.** "...let our Beauty Product Specialists do anything" reads like a sentence that lost its object — almost certainly "anything you need" or similar was cut off.
- **Subject is pure brand-speak.** "Experience services designed around you" could be from any luxury retailer; nothing about it makes you feel it was sent to *you* specifically.
- **Four emails in three days.** Even a good services email gets penalized at this send frequency.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one tangible hook to the hero.** The complimentary beauty session and free samples buried in section 3 belong in the hero. "Shop in person, leave with free samples — book your beauty session" is a concrete reason to engage that "Explore Our Services" is not.
- 2. **Collapse to a single primary CTA.** Pick the service with the highest click rate and make this a mono-focus email. Style advisor matchmaking is the most differentiated and most likely to drive engagement — cut "Click & Collect" (table-stakes retail) and move beauty to a secondary mention.
- 3. **Rewrite the "Learn More" CTAs.** "Book a Beauty Session" and "Choose Your Pickup" are specific, actionable, and tell the reader exactly what happens next.
- 4. **Fix the "Beauty Your Way" sentence.** Confirm and restore the missing object in "...do anything."
- 5. **Respect cadence.** This email would land better on day 5 or 6 of a welcome sequence, not 24 hours after a sale deadline.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Experience services designed around you"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC) build with a table-based layout and inline AMPscript. No hard errors were detected, but four warnings from automated QA indicate gaps in compliance header capture and authentication visibility that warrant investigation at the relay layer.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**34 tracking links skipped by HTTP probe.**
All outbound links pass through a click-redirect domain (likely `e.neimanmarcus.com` or a SFMC tracking subdomain). No probe could confirm destination URLs are live, redirect correctly, or carry consistent UTM parameters. This is expected behavior for ESP-tracked links but means broken-destination failures would not surface in pre-send QA.

**No issues found** with link syntax in the truncated source — `href` values appear well-formed.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Commented-out `<meta viewport>` variants (3 of them):**
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
Dead comment debt — no functional impact but signals the template has been patched repeatedly without cleanup. Risk of an editor accidentally uncommenting the wrong variant.

**`user-scalable=no` in a commented block** — if ever re-enabled, this would fail WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize text) and is prohibited by Apple App Store policy for WebViews.

**`-webkit-animation: bugfix` on `body`** — a known iOS 9 hamburger-menu hack. iOS 9 is EOL; this adds animation overhead on every render for zero benefit.

**`<title>` has a trailing space:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` — minor, inconsequential.

**`@media (max-device-width)` deprecated selectors:**
```css
@media only screen and (max-device-width: 480px), screen and (max-width: 480px)
```
`max-device-width` was removed from the CSS spec; some clients will silently ignore the rule. The `max-width` fallback in the same block saves this in practice, but it is fragile.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**AMPscript variables are declared but not confirmed resolved:**
```
@user = Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)
@emailid = Lookup(...)
```
The comment block includes `/* SET @user = HASHED_SHA256 */` commented out — indicating a SHA-256 hashed email path exists but is disabled. If the active path fails the `Lookup()` (e.g., subscriber not in `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`), `@user` and `@emailid` will render as empty strings with no visible fallback defined in the truncated source. This could silently break any personalized content blocks or downstream pixel/tracking calls that depend on these values.

**`@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")` is a no-op** — concatenating an empty string adds no value; likely a template stub that was never filled.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not detected** (QA WARN):
The AgentMail relay did not capture this header. If it is genuinely absent, this violates:
- CAN-SPAM best practice
- Google/Yahoo 2024 bulk sender requirements (mandatory for >5k/day senders)
- RFC 2369

This must be confirmed against the raw SMTP headers at the MTA — the header may have been stripped by the relay rather than absent at send time.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not detected** (QA WARN):
One-click unsubscribe (required by Google/Yahoo for bulk senders since Feb 2024) depends on this header pair. If absent at the MTA, Neiman Marcus is non-compliant with current inbox provider requirements.

**Authentication-Results header missing** (QA WARN):
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown due to relay capture gap. Given the sending domain is `e.neimanmarcus.com` (a dedicated ESP subdomain), DKIM alignment should be present — but cannot be confirmed from available data.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM parameters unverifiable** — all 34 links are behind click-redirect tracking and were not probed. Cannot confirm whether `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, or `utm_content` values are present and consistent on destination URLs.

**`@refid` / `@base_refid` are campaign-calendar variables** — if these are empty at send time, any analytics or personalization tied to the refid will silently fail. No fallback or error handling is visible in the source.

---

| Priority | Action |
|---|---|
| **High** | Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are present in raw SMTP headers — do not rely on relay capture alone. Non-compliance is a deliverability risk with Google/Yahoo. |

| **Medium** | Add a null-check fallback for `@user` and `@emailid` AMPscript lookups. An empty subscriber record should gracefully degrade, not silently break personalized blocks. |
