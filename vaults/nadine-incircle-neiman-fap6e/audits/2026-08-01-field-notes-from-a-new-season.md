# Field notes from a new season
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-01**
## Executive summary

- This is Neiman Marcus running its purest editorial-reset play yet — a full-bleed "Field Notes" lookbook with a woman in a white pantsuit flanked by two horses, no price, no percentage, no urgency, and only two shoppable links in the entire send. It's the same "no-discount, all-merchandise" format that showed up in 7/31's "Fashion Finds: Matching Sets" (5.00/10) and 7/30's "1,000+ new designer styles" (6.00/10), but this one leans even harder into mood over merchandising — three big lifestyle images, minimal copy, one CTA link per section.
- The visual execution is genuinely strong: the hero typography ("FIELD NOTES" split across the horse's back) is elegant and on-brand, and the styling is cohesive — linen stripes, a chunky knit, tailored white suiting, all shot in the same barn/countryside setting. But as a commerce email it's thin to the point of being closer to a brand-awareness postcard than a shopping prompt. There's no offer, no product grid, no price point, and the last section ("Earthy tones and organic textures lend a natural ease to every look") doesn't even have a link.
- This is a well-art-directed email that will do little to move product. Compared to the brand's Last Call sends, which reliably score 7 on clear offer + grid + CTA, this trades performance for brand image — fine occasionally, risky as a pattern.

## What's working

- The hero image is striking and on-brand — clean horizon light, confident model, and the "FIELD NOTES" wordmark integrated directly into the photo.
- Visual hierarchy is unmistakable: eye lands on the hero within the first screen, no competing elements.
- Styling is cohesive across all three images (linen stripe set, cream suit, chunky knit) — reads as a genuine trend story, not a random product dump.
- Render is completely clean — no broken images, no overlapping text, no layout breaks.
- Nav bar and footer (app download, personal stylist, store finder) are all present and functional-looking.

## What's weak

- Zero offer of any kind — no % off, no price, no loyalty tie-in — despite this being a promotional marketing email.
- Only two of three sections have a CTA at all; the final "Earthy tones and organic textures" block is copy with no link, a dead end after all that scrolling.
- CTAs are small underlined text links, not buttons — easy to miss on mobile, no visual weight to signal "click here."
- Only three products are effectively shown (all styled on models, none flat-laid or detailed) — no sizing, fabric, or price information anywhere.
- This is now the third or fourth product-thin editorial send in the recent stretch (7/31 Matching Sets, 7/30 1,000+ new styles) — the format is becoming repetitive rather than special.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add at least one real CTA button (not a text link) under the hero, and give the final "Earthy tones" section a CTA — right now it's the one place in the email where a reader who scrolled that far has nothing to click.
- 2. Introduce a light merchandising element — even a 3-4 product strip with prices under the lookbook would convert browsers who like the story but want to shop it directly, instead of forcing an intermediate click to a collection page.
- 3. Swap the underlined-text-link CTA style for a visible button (bordered or filled) to raise tap-ability on mobile.
- 4. Vary the CTA verbs — "Shop Countryside" and "Shop Designer Collections" are both generic; something like "Shop the Suit" or "Shop This Knit" ties the link to the specific piece shown.
- 5. Space out editorial-only sends — three product-thin lookbooks in under a week risks training subscribers to skip this brand's emails when they're not running a sale.

## Technical Audit — "Field notes from a new season" (Neiman Marcus)

### 1. Technical Summary
This is a standard Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC/AMPscript) email build with responsive/Outlook-safe boilerplate; the primary technical issues are unstripped AMPscript developer comments in the shipped HTML and missing unsubscribe/authentication headers flagged by automated QA.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 16 tracking/click-redirect links were present but skipped by the automated HTTP probe (by design, since redirect domains return non-final status codes) — link destinations were not independently verified and should be manually click-tested before/after send.
- No broken or malformed `href` values are visible in the truncated source provided (body/link markup was not included in the excerpt, so this cannot be fully assessed from source alone).

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- Standard defensive CSS is present and correctly implemented: `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting`, iOS blue-link override (`a[x-apple-data-detectors]`), Outlook table resets (`mso-table-lspace/rspace`), `.ExternalClass` line-height fixes for Outlook.com, and mobile breakpoints at 480px/520px/600px with a hamburger-nav checkbox pattern.
- No issues found in the visible `<head>`/style block.
- Image `alt` text, semantic structure, and color contrast in the body could not be assessed — the provided source is truncated before the `<body>` content.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- The HTML ships with an unstripped AMPscript developer comment block at the very top of the source, e.g.:
  `<!--REQUIRED variables: @email ... @user Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)) ... @uEm = Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm", ...) @cmdid Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "cmdid", ...) CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW -->`
  followed by `<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->`.
- This is a real code-quality/leakage issue: it exposes internal Data Extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`) and field names (`EMAIL_ID`, `uEm`, `cmdid`) in view-source, and leaves an ambiguous, unresolved "choose one scenario" toggle for how `@user` is set (raw email vs. `HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256`) in the production payload. Recipients/anyone viewing source can see this internal CRM/DE schema.
- Recommend stripping all AMPscript authoring comments from the compiled/sent HTML at deploy time.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- `List-Unsubscribe` header not found by automated check — may be a relay-capture limitation (AgentMail) rather than a true absence, but could not be confirmed present.
- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header (RFC 8058, required for one-click unsubscribe) not found — same caveat applies.
- `Authentication-Results` header not found, so SPF/DKIM pass/fail status could not be verified via this relay.
- No footer/unsubscribe link content was visible in the truncated source to confirm a CAN-SPAM-compliant physical address or unsubscribe link independent of the header check.
- **Recommendation**: re-verify these three header checks against the raw MTA-received headers (not the AgentMail-relayed copy) before concluding non-compliance, since all three warnings note the relay may simply not be capturing them.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- The AMPscript header references `@base_refid` (from Campaign Calendar) and `@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")`, indicating link tracking/refid construction is in place, but actual outbound URLs/UTM parameters were not present in the truncated source and could not be verified against landing pages.
- No issues found in the visible source; full assessment requires the untruncated body with actual `href` values.

### 7. Recommendations
1. Strip AMPscript/developer HTML comments (variable/DE/scenario notes) from the final compiled send — currently exposes internal SFMC schema (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`) in view-source.
2. Resolve the ambiguous `@user` assignment scenario (plaintext email vs. SHA256 hash) before send rather than leaving both options commented in the template — confirm which is actually active.
3. Re-check `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` / `Authentication-Results` against raw received headers at the MTA, not the AgentMail relay copy, to rule out a capture artifact vs. a genuine compliance gap.
4. Reduce plain-text URL density (currently 2,772/3,275 chars, ~85%, are URLs) — this is a common spam-filter signal; consider shortening or reducing redundant tracked links in the plain-text alternative.
5. Manually verify the 16 skipped tracking-redirect links resolve to correct final landing pages, since the automated probe could not confirm them.
