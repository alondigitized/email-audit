# Fashion Finds: Everyday Suede
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-24**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean editorial merchandising email with no commercial hook. A 3x3 grid of suede pieces (studded jacket, patchwork boot, shirtdress, corduroy-brown trouser, shoulder bag, loafer, shearling clog, mini skirt, belt) sits under a thin-serif "FASHION FINDS" header. It looks expensive and the suede story is coherent. But it asks the reader to browse, and it gives no price, no offer and no deadline.
- Compare it with this week's sends: the Oct 24 "$50 gift card for every $200 spent," the Oct 23 "Fall Style Sale: up to 30% off," and the Oct 22 "25% off one item." Those had a number your eye landed on. This one has nothing to land on, so it reads as a soft-sell filler send after a run of promotions. It's on-season and on-brand, but it will probably lose to the offer emails sitting next to it in the inbox.
- Judgement: a pretty catalog page that wastes its best asset, a strong seasonal trend with nine desirable products, by giving shoppers no reason to act today.

## What's working

- The suede theme is tight. Every product is suede, and the warm tan, rust and chocolate palette suits late October.
- The 3x3 grid is clean, evenly spaced and uniform. There are no broken images or overlapping text.
- The "FASHION FINDS" header with the "everyday suede" line sets the story in one glance. The bolded "everyday suede" does the work.
- The product mix spans clothing, shoes, bags and accessories, so there is something for most browsers.
- The brand credits (Veronica Beard, Zimmermann, Polo Ralph Lauren, Staud, Tanya Taylor, L'Agence) add designer credibility.

## What's weak

- There is no price, offer or urgency anywhere. Nothing tells the reader to click today.
- Eight outlined "Shop…" buttons compete (two at the top, six at the bottom). They're all the same weight, and "Shop Contemporary" appears twice.
- The brand credits are a run-on text line in tiny type. They aren't mapped to the products, so you can't tell which brand makes the boot and which makes the skirt. There are no product names or prices.
- The buttons are thin gray outlines with light text. They look like secondary links, not calls to action.
- The footer block (app download, style advisor, store, contact, account) adds clutter before the legal text. No loyalty or InCircle benefit shows, even though Neiman Marcus ran a points promotion on Oct 21.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add one concrete hook, such as a price anchor ("Suede from $195"), an InCircle points tie-in or a deadline. This is the single biggest lift. The email currently has no reason to click now.
- 2. Collapse the buttons to one filled, high-contrast primary CTA, "Shop Suede," under the header. Keep the rest as a small text-link row.
- 3. Caption each tile with brand and price, and link each tile to its own product page. Right now the brand line is detached from the images.
- 4. Lead with a hero. A larger image of the studded jacket or the boot, with the grid below it, would give the eye a first landing spot.
- 5. Drop or shrink the app and style-advisor block. If the advisor is the differentiator, give it one line under the primary CTA.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible HTML is a table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional layout with mobile media queries. The source has a leaked ESP variable-definition comment and a viewport setting that blocks pinch-zoom. The four QA warnings (missing unsubscribe and authentication headers, URL-heavy plain text, skipped tracking links) are mostly relay-capture limits and can't be confirmed as defects. The source was truncated, so body-level checks were limited.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Click-tracking redirects not verified.** 22 tracking links were skipped because they are click-redirect domains. Final destinations, redirect chains, and link health are unverified.
- **Plain text is 89% URLs.** 4134 of 4657 chars are URLs, which is above the 70% threshold. This is likely caused by long encoded tracking URLs in the text part. It raises the spam-filter risk and makes the plain-text part hard to read.
- The truncated source didn't show any broken, empty, or `href="#"` links.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Pinch-zoom is blocked.** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` limits zoom, which conflicts with WCAG 1.4.4 (resize text).
- **Comment sits before the DOCTYPE.** The leading `<!--REQUIRED variables…-->` block comes before `<!DOCTYPE>`. Some older clients (IE-based and some webmail) can drop into quirks mode when anything precedes the doctype.
- **Dead code is shipped.** There are three commented-out `<meta name="viewport">` variants, including `width=1024` and `width=320, initial-scale=0.29`. They add bytes and are a regression risk if someone uncomments one.
- **Generic `<title>`.** It reads `Neiman Marcus ` with a trailing space and no subject context.
- **Hamburger nav uses the checkbox hack.** The CSS relies on `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu`, plus `display:none` and `visibility:hidden` toggles, which are unsupported in Gmail and Outlook. The body markup was truncated, so I couldn't check the fallback or the ARIA/keyboard behavior.
- `*[class=…]` attribute selectors and the `@-webkit-keyframes bugfix` animation on `body` are legacy workarounds. They are harmless but add maintenance weight.
- Alt text, image dimensions, and `role="presentation"` on layout tables are not verifiable from the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Internal data structures leak in the delivered HTML.** The top comment exposes Data Extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`), field names (`EMAIL_ID`, `EMAIL_ADDRESS`, `uEm`, `cmdid`), the variables `@email`, `@base_refid`, `@refid`, `@user`, `@emailid`, and the `HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256` option. It is invisible to recipients but readable in view-source or forwarded copies.
- **The comment text is malformed.** It has unbalanced parentheses in `Lookup(…, @email))`, and the reference to "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW" has no scenario below it. This suggests the template was only partly cleaned up.
- No unresolved visible tokens (`%%…%%`, `{{…}}`) appear in the truncated portion.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) were not found.** The QA tool itself says the relay may not capture them, so this is **unconfirmed**. Verify against the raw message at the origin. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require both.
- **`Authentication-Results` was not found.** SPF, DKIM, and DMARC status are unknown because of the relay. The From domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` is a subdomain, so check alignment at the source.
- The footer, physical address, and in-body unsubscribe link were not visible in the truncated source, so I can't confirm them either way.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not assessable. All 22 tracked links were skipped, and the visible source doesn't show final URLs. The `@refid`/`@base_refid` variables suggest a refid-based attribution scheme rather than UTM parameters. Confirm that the refid is passed through to the landing page.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip the `<!--REQUIRED variables…-->` block, and any other internal comments, from the production render. Do it in the ESP's AMPscript/SSJS pre-send step, or move the comments to template documentation.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag and delete the commented-out viewport variants.
3. Put the DOCTYPE first, before any comments.
4. Shorten the plain-text part. Use `alias=` or shorter redirect URLs, or hand-write a plain-text version, to bring URL share under 70%.
5. Check the raw source message for `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, and SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment. Don't act on the relay-based warnings alone.
6. Confirm the checkbox-hamburger nav degrades to a visible link list in Gmail and Outlook.
7. Re-run the audit with the full, untruncated HTML. That would let a probe of the 22 redirect destinations, and checks on alt text and the footer, finish the job.
