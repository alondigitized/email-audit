# Fall Style Sale: up to 30% off starts now
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-23**
## Executive summary

- This is the broad "Fall Style Sale" send. It's clean and easy to read, with a mauve hero, a big red "30% OFF" and a black SHOP NOW button. The eye lands on the offer first, and the hierarchy is the best thing about it. It reads as a proper sale email, which this week's Neiman Marcus sends haven't been. The Oct 22 "1,000+ new arrivals" email had no commercial hook, and the 25% off one item offer was a personal, single-item deal. This one gives a storewide discount.
- The body undercuts the hero. There's a 3x3 grid of designer product tiles with brand names but no prices, no sale prices and no "was/now". That makes it hard to feel the "up to 30%" in practice. "Up to" plus "select regular-price items" plus "Exclusions Apply" is a lot of hedging for a discount that tops out at 30%. The Saint Laurent triple-points block near the bottom repeats the Oct 21 send almost word for word. It competes with the sale and adds a second, differently dated deadline (10/25 vs 10/29).
- Overall it's a solid, competent sale announcement and a step up from the new-arrivals email. It's still generic, and it relies on a cadence of near-identical offers. Neiman Marcus has now sent a discount, a last-call, a sale and a points promo in three days.

## What's working

- The hero hierarchy is strong: "FALL STYLE SALE" and a large red "30% OFF" dominate, with a high-contrast black SHOP NOW button.
- The offer is concrete and sits above the fold, with the percentage visible immediately.
- The 3x3 grid of recognizable luxury brands (Dolce&Gabbana, Balmain, Polo Ralph Lauren, Stuart Weitzman) gives clean, scannable product variety.
- The six category buttons (Women's Clothing, Men's, Home, Handbags, Jewelry, Shoes) let people jump to their own area.
- The render is clean, with no broken images or layout problems, and the typography is consistent with the brand.

## What's weak

- The product grid shows no prices or sale prices, so the discount never gets proven.
- The hero hedges with "UP TO", "select regular-price items" and "Exclusions Apply". The real end date (10/29) appears only in the fine print, so the hero has no visible deadline beyond "limited-time".
- The Saint Laurent triple-points block repeats Oct 21 creative and splits attention from the sale. It also carries a different deadline.
- Every CTA is generic ("SHOP NOW", "SHOP WOMEN'S CLOTHING"), with no verb-plus-noun specificity.
- Product tiles are labeled by designer only, with no item name, and there is no social proof.
- The fine print is very long, and the exclusionary language weakens the feeling of an honest, simple offer.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a sale price or a "% off" tag on each product tile, for example "$495 $346". That would do more for clicks than any other change.
- 2. State the deadline in the hero: "Ends 10/29". A real date is more credible than "limited-time".
- 3. Cut the Saint Laurent points block, or move it to a separate send, so the sale gets one clear message.
- 4. Replace the generic CTA with something specific, such as "Shop 30% off dresses", and make at least one hero product a named best-seller.
- 5. Cut the "up to" hedge, or say which categories hit 30%, so the offer feels real.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dresses, boots and bags from Balmain, Stuart Weitzman and more are marked down.`

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">`. `maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-zoom on some clients, which is a WCAG 1.4.4 resize-text concern.
- **Exact-match class selectors:** the mobile rules use `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=mobile]`, `*[class=hide]` and similar. They match only when the `class` attribute is exactly that value. Any element with a second class won't get the mobile override.
- **Checkbox-hack hamburger nav:** `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu` and `.mobileCont:checked~table` depend on `:checked` support, which is unreliable in Gmail and Outlook. I can't see the fallback markup in the truncated source, so I can't confirm the nav degrades gracefully.
- **Dead code:** three commented-out `<meta name="viewport">` variants, including `width=1024` and `width=320, initial-scale=0.29`, ship in the production head. They are harmless but are cruft.
- **Alt text and image sizing:** can't be assessed from the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Template documentation comment shipped in the delivered HTML:**
  `<!--REQUIRED variables: @email … @base_refid … @refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"") @user Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", …) @uEm = Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm", …) @cmdid … CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW -->`
- This exposes internal data-extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`), field names (`uEm`, `cmdid`, `EMAIL_ID`) and the `HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256` option to every recipient. It is also evidence of an unfinished template ("CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO") that wasn't stripped at send.
