# Earn a $50 gift card for every $200 you spend
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-18**
## Executive summary

- This is a gift-card-with-purchase event — earn up to $500 in gift cards for spending $2,000+, tiered starting at $200/$50. It's a genuinely different mechanic than the last five Neiman Marcus sends I've logged (Friends & Family markdown, denim catalog, sale arrivals, stylist editorial, "25% off one item"), so credit for variety. The maroon hero is clean and the offer headline is legible at a glance, but the tiers themselves are buried in a wall of fine print at the bottom rather than shown visually, so most readers will only register "up to $500" without understanding how to actually hit that number.
- The nine-item product grid underneath (Zimmermann, Veronica Beard, Akris punto, Chloe, Ferragamo, Miu Miu, Jimmy Choo, Amiri, Tom Ford) is a reasonable stock-up-for-fall showcase, but nothing in the grid signals price or how close it gets you to a gift-card tier — a $3,000 Zimmermann dress and a pair of shoes look identical in weight here. Solid concept, thin execution on making the mechanic easy to act on.

## What's working

- Clear, single, differentiated offer mechanic (gift-card-back tiers) not seen in the last several sends.
- Hero has strong visual hierarchy — offer headline, dollar amount, and code are all immediately visible without scrolling.
- Promo code (SEPTGC) is visible in the hero, not buried in body copy.
- Product grid spans a real range of categories and designers (dresses, bags, shoes, menswear), giving multiple entry points.
- Clean render — no overlapping text, broken images, or layout breaks.

## What's weak

- The tier ladder ($200→$50, $400→$100, $600→$150... up to $2,000+→$500) never appears visually — it's only in dense legal-style copy at the very bottom, so the hero's "up to $500" reads as an aspirational teaser rather than an achievable plan.
- No prices shown on any of the nine products, making it impossible to gauge how close a cart gets to a tier threshold.
- "Exclusions Apply" sits directly under the CTA with no elaboration, which reads as a caution flag right at the moment of intended click.
- Category CTA buttons repeat "SHOP WOMEN'S" and "SHOP WOMEN'S CLOTHING" as separate buttons — redundant and eats space that could've shown the tier table.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move a simplified 2-3 row tier table (e.g. "$200 → $50 | $600 → $150 | $2,000+ → $500") into the body just under the hero, in easy-to-scan format — this is the single biggest lever since the whole email's value prop currently requires scrolling to fine print to understand.
- 2. Add prices under each product in the grid so shoppers can mentally build a qualifying cart.
- 3. Move "Exclusions Apply" to sit near the legal copy instead of directly under the CTA, or soften it to "some exclusions apply" so it doesn't undercut the click moment.
- 4. Collapse "SHOP WOMEN'S" and "SHOP WOMEN'S CLOTHING" into one button, use the freed row for a "SHOP MEN'S CLOTHING" or "SHOP BAGS" pairing to broaden coverage.
- 5. Test tightening the deadline into the subject line for urgency, since the code expires 9/27/26 — one day out from typical send date.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to $500 in gift cards — ends tomorrow`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Spend $200, get $50 back in gift cards`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Tiers up to $2,000+ = a $500 gift card. Code SEPTGC, ends 9/27.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The more you spend, the more you earn back — see the tiers inside.`
