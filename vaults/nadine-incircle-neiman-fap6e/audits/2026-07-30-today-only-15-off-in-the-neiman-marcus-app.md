# Today only: 15% off in the Neiman Marcus app
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-30**
## Executive summary

- This is Neiman Marcus running a different play than anything in the recent stretch: not a discount ladder, not an editorial reset, but an app-acquisition push dressed up as a sale. The hero is clean and confident — giant "15% OFF" typography, a QR code, and an App Store badge — but the actual offer is locked behind downloading the app, scanning a code, and entering a promo code at checkout. That's a lot of friction between "opened the email" and "got the discount," and it's a step backward in usability compared to 7/27's "Last chance for up to $500 off," where the CTA was a straight click-to-shop.
- The merchandising grid below the fold is genuinely good — nine real, on-brand SKUs (Chloe, Manolo Blahnik, Burberry, Carolina Herrera, ZEGNA) spanning women's, shoes, and menswear, none of which land on the exclusion list buried in the fine print. That's a smarter curation choice than some recent sends, since a shopper who clicks through won't hit an "oh, that's excluded" wall on the specific items shown.
- The dealbreaker is the fine print: a sprawling exclusion list that knocks out Chanel, Dior, Gucci, Louis Vuitton, Prada, Saint Laurent, Valentino, Ralph Lauren (all lines), and more — essentially most of the marquee designers a Neiman Marcus shopper associates with the store. Combined with the app-only redemption, this reads less like a customer perk and more like a data-capture campaign wearing a discount as a costume. Solid design, honest merchandising, but the core mechanic works against the reader.

## What's working

- The hero typography (giant "15% OFF") creates instant, unmissable visual hierarchy — no scrolling needed to get the headline offer.
- The nine-product grid is real, diverse merchandise (bags, dresses, shoes, outerwear, menswear) with clear brand labels under each image.
- None of the specifically featured products (Chloe, Burberry, Manolo Blahnik, etc.) appear on the excluded-brands list, so the merchandising is honest relative to the fine print.
- A hard deadline ("Ends 7/30/26 at 10:00 PM CT") gives the "Today only" urgency actual teeth.
- Clean render — no overlapping text, broken images, or layout breaks anywhere in the send.

## What's weak

- The offer requires downloading an app AND scanning a QR code AND entering a promo code at checkout — three steps of friction before any discount is realized, with no simple "shop and save" path for someone who just wants to buy from the email.
- The exclusion list in the fine print knocks out nearly every top-tier designer name (Chanel, Dior, Gucci, LV, Prada, Saint Laurent, Valentino, all Ralph Lauren lines) — that's the kind of buried, extensive carve-out that erodes trust when a reader eventually notices.
- No loyalty/InCircle member benefit is surfaced, despite this being exactly the kind of send where a "plus double InCircle points" line would add real incentive.
- No demographic or lifestyle signal anywhere — it's flat-lay product photography only, no styling context, which makes the email feel transactional rather than aspirational (a step down from NM's usual editorial tone, e.g. 7/29's "Heel Appeal" hero).

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with a real "Shop Now" button that takes non-app users straight to the website with the 15% applied automatically, and treat the app/QR path as a secondary option — don't gate the entire discount behind app download.
- 2. Move the exclusion summary out of dense fine print and into one honest line near the offer ("Excludes select luxury designers — see app for details") so it doesn't read as a bait-and-switch when discovered at checkout.
- 3. Add an InCircle loyalty callout if this offer stacks with member points — right now there's no reason for a loyalty member to feel this is "for them" specifically.
- 4. Swap at least the hero visual for a styled lifestyle shot instead of pure product-on-white, to differentiate this from a flash-sale/clearance tone and keep it in NM's usual premium register.
- 5. A/B test the subject line to front-load urgency instead of the channel:
- **Subject Alt A:** `Today only: your 15% off code is in the app`
- **Subject Alt B:** `15% off ends tonight — NM app exclusive`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Scan, shop, save — new arrivals from Chloe, Burberry & Manolo Blahnik`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `One day only. Nine new pieces we think you'll want.`
