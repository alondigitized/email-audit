# Designer Sale upgrade: extra 20% off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-13**
## Executive summary

- This is Neiman Marcus's second bite at the Extra 20% Off apple in less than 24 hours — June 12 brought "Limited time: Enjoy an extra 20% off select designer styles" (8/10 in the brand history), and June 13 sends the same offer under the thinner cover of "Designer Sale upgrade." The creative execution is clean: a commanding red hero, nine product shots from legitimate names (FRAME, Jacquemus, Cinq à Sept, A.L.C., Rag & Bone), and six department CTAs that at least acknowledge men's, shoes, and handbags. There are zero render bugs, and the visual hierarchy is doing exactly what it should.
- The problem is the word "upgrade." It promises something new or better. The body delivers nothing new — same percentage, same vague "select styles" caveat, same category links. A subscriber who skipped June 12's send opens this expecting an escalation (bigger discount? Extended sizing? New brands added?) and finds the exact same offer framed as a fresh event. That's a trust erosion.
- At the seventh Designer Sale email in roughly seven days, NM is burning list equity faster than the sale is closing carts. The email is technically competent; the strategy behind it is not.

## What's working

- **Red hero stops the scroll.** "EXTRA 20% OFF" in white on deep red is unambiguous; the offer lands before the eye moves anywhere else.
- **Product selection is strong and credible.** FRAME, Veronica Beard, Jacquemus, A.L.C., Cinq à Sept, Jimmy Choo — this is a legitimate luxury grid, not filler brands.
- **Six department CTAs cover the bases.** Women's clothing, shoes, handbags, dresses, men's clothing, men's shoes — the navigation is genuinely useful for different entry intents.
- **"Exclusions Apply" is disclosed prominently** below the CTA, not buried in a footnote. That's honest framing.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no misaligned text, no layout collapse.

## What's weak

- **"Upgrade" is a broken promise.** The subject implies something has changed or improved; the body is word-for-word the same offer sent yesterday.
- **No deadline anywhere in the visible body.** June 12's "Limited time" framing at least gestured at urgency; this send removes it and adds nothing in its place.
- **Nine product shots, zero linked to individual products.** Every CTA goes to a category page. A $450 FRAME jacket or the Jacquemus bag could have pulled a click on its own; linking to category pages dilutes that intent.
- **No social proof, no popularity signal.** "Best seller," "selling fast," a count of items low in inventory — any of these would bridge the gap for undecided openers.
- **Cadence is actively hurting open rates.** Seven sends in seven days from the same sale, two of them with the same offer, trains subscribers to wait or unsubscribe rather than buy.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Honor the "upgrade" or kill it.** If NM actually added brands, extended sizes, or boosted the discount, say so specifically. If nothing changed, don't call it an upgrade — reframe it as a last-call re-target ("24 hours left" or "these are moving fast").
- 2. **Pick a single product and link directly to its PDP.** The Jacquemus white bag or the FRAME quilted jacket has enough desire-object appeal to drive a click on its own. Use it as the hero with a direct link.
- 3. **Add a visible deadline.** Any deadline — "Ends Sunday" in the hero, a countdown label, anything — converts the reactivation send from a browse into a decision.
- 4. **Cut to five emails total per sale event.** With seven sends so far, one more re-target without new content is a list-burn event, not a revenue driver.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Designer Sale upgrade: extra 20% off"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The template renders on a Salesforce Marketing Cloud (AMPscript) stack and passes basic structural checks, but exposes internal data-model details in rendered HTML comments, is missing required bulk-sender unsubscribe headers, and carries authentication gaps that are unverifiable through the relay.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 tracking links unprobed**
All click-through URLs pass through a redirect/tracking domain; QA skipped HTTP probes on all 26. Link-rot or destination errors would be silent until recipient click.

**Plain-text part is 87% URLs by character**
QA finding: 5,225 of 6,016 plain-text chars are URLs. Most spam filters treat a plain-text part that is >60–70% URLs as a signal. The plain-text alternative should contain human-readable copy mirroring the HTML body, with URLs as anchors, not the dominant content.

**UTM parameters not verifiable**
The HTML source is truncated before any `<a href>` tags are visible. UTM attribution on the 26 tracked links could not be confirmed. See §6.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom (accessibility)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom in mobile browsers. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA) requires content be resizable up to 200% without assistive technology. This is a confirmed violation.

**CSS-only hamburger menu has near-zero email client support**
The template implements a `:checked` checkbox hack for a mobile nav:
```css
*[class].navbar-checkbox:checked~*[class].menu { visibility: visible !important; ... }
```
This pattern is only reliably supported in Apple Mail. Gmail (all clients), Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo strip or ignore the `:checked` pseudo-class. Subscribers on those clients see a broken or missing nav element.

**Interactive tabbed content equally unsupported**
```css
.mobileCont:checked~table *[class=contentInteractive] { display: block !important; }
```
Same support problem as above. Content hidden behind `.contentInteractive` is invisible to the majority of the recipient base.

**Commented-out viewport debris**
Three dead viewport declarations remain in the `<head>` as HTML comments (widths 320, 1024, and a `user-scalable=no` variant). No active rendering impact, but indicates the template was never cleaned after iteration.

**`<title>` has a trailing space**
```html
<title>Neiman Marcus </title>
```
Minor; some preview pane renderers surface the title tag.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Internal data schema exposed in rendered HTML comments**
The comment block at the top of the document — present in the delivered HTML — discloses:
- Internal table names: `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`
- Column names: `EMAIL_ID`, `EMAIL_ADDRESS`, `uEm`, `cmdid`
- Full AMPscript lookup logic for subscriber resolution

This is a data-architecture disclosure. These comments should be stripped at build/send time, not left in deliverable HTML.

**Dual `@user` code paths require manual selection — error-prone**
```html
<!--REQUIRED variables: ... @user Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", ...) -->
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */-->
```
The template comment instructs the operator to "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW." One path sets `@user` to an internal `EMAIL_ID`; the other sets it to a SHA-256 hash. This manual toggle is an operational risk: selecting the wrong path would silently send the wrong identifier in tracking parameters on every link in the campaign. Neither branch has a runtime guard or fallback.

**`@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")` is a no-op concatenation**
The variable declaration `@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")` appends an empty string, producing an identical value to `@base_refid`. If the intent was to append a suffix (e.g., a variant code), the suffix is missing. If not, the CONCAT is dead code.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent (RFC 2369)**
QA confirmed the header was not captured through the AgentMail relay. CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) does not mandate this header, but Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender policies (enforced since February 2024) require it for senders exceeding 5,000 messages/day. Neiman Marcus is a high-volume sender; absence here is a deliverability policy violation.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent (RFC 8058)**
One-click unsubscribe is not present. Gmail requires this header for bulk senders to maintain inbox placement. Without it, Gmail may route to spam or display degraded sender reputation signals.

**SPF/DKIM/DMARC authentication unverifiable**
`Authentication-Results` header was not captured via relay. The sending domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` (an ESP subdomain) should have aligned SPF and DKIM. Status is unknown and must be verified directly against delivered message headers, not via relay capture.

**Physical address and unsubscribe link**
Not verifiable from the truncated HTML. Assumed present given Neiman Marcus's compliance posture, but must be confirmed in a full-source review.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM parameters not confirmed**
All 26 links were skipped by the QA prober (tracking redirect domains). UTM presence, correctness (`utm_campaign` matching `@base_refid`, `utm_medium=email`, etc.), and redirect-chain integrity cannot be assessed from the current data. A secondary probe against the final destination URLs is required.

**`@base_refid` as campaign key**
The template uses `@base_refid` as the primary campaign identifier threaded into `@refid`. If this value is misconfigured in the sendable data extension, all 26 links will carry a wrong or blank campaign parameter, breaking attribution silently.
