# Your $50-$1,250 gift card is waiting…
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-06**
## Executive summary

- This is NM's third gift card promotional send in four days — following the June 3 "STARTS NOW" (8/10) and the June 4 repeat (7/10). The creative strategy here is clearly a re-engagement blast aimed at non-clickers: the subject line shifts from "event starts now" to "your gift card is waiting," adding a soft possessive hook. The "ONLINE ONLY" badge is new to this iteration and is a genuine differentiator that justifies re-sending.
- The structure is clean and on-brand — olive hero, code JUN66C front and center, product grid below. But the product grid is the problem: eight items across six designers with no prices shown and no story connecting them to the gift card mechanic. It's a catalog dump tacked onto a promotion.
- The verdict: a competent third-send cadence hit that doesn't earn the subscriber's renewed attention beyond the subject line. It will convert the fence-sitters who just needed one more nudge, but it leaves meaningful personalization on the table for everyone else.

## What's working

- **"ONLINE ONLY" badge** is the one new creative element — it creates a channel-exclusive reason to act that the prior two sends lacked.
- **Code + CTA hierarchy** is solid: JUN66C is displayed large, and "SHOP THE SALE" button is visually prominent in the hero without scrolling.
- **Six-category shop links** (Women's, Shoes, Clothing, Handbags, Men's, Jewelry) give every subscriber a relevant entry point below the fold.
- **Subject line specificity** — the dollar range "$50-$1,250" makes the offer concrete and scannable at inbox.

## What's weak

- **No deadline visible anywhere in the render** — a gift card event without a closing date loses its urgency on its third appearance; "waiting" implies permanence, not scarcity.
- **Product grid has no prices** — for an email built around a spend-to-earn mechanic, showing $0 pricing makes it impossible for the subscriber to self-sort into a gift card tier.
- **"Get rewarded two ways" is unexplained** — the subhead promises two reward paths but the body never delivers the second one visibly; it reads like orphaned copy.
- **Eight products, zero story** — a mix of Oscar de la Renta, Jacquemus, Jimmy Choo, Cinq à Sept, Chloe, and Veronica Beard with no editorial thread or "complete the look" logic; it's a merchandise dump.
- **App section at the bottom feels misplaced** — "Download the app / Match with a personal style advisor" is a cold acquisition pitch inside a conversion email, misaligned with the send's goal.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a visible countdown or hard end date to the hero** — "Ends Sunday" or a timer graphic. This is the single highest-lift change: a gift card event in its third day needs urgency mechanics, not just possessive framing.
- 2. **Show prices on at least two hero products** — pick one item near the $500 tier threshold (to unlock the $50 gift card) and one near $1,000. Make the spend-earn math visible without a click.
- 3. **Resolve "rewarded two ways"** — either show both mechanics clearly or cut the line. If the two ways are (a) earn a gift card and (b) earn loyalty points, say that explicitly.
- 4. **Cut the app pitch** — move it to the footer or a dedicated nurture send. It dilutes conversion focus.
- 5. **Replace the eight-brand grid with a curated "spend $500, get $50" starter set** — three products, each under $600, each with price shown. Tie the editorial to the mechanic.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Gift card event ends soon — code JUN66C`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Spend $500, earn $50 — online only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Online only: four spend tiers, one code. Your gift card tier is still open.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Ends soon — shop new collections and earn up to $1,250 back.`

## 1. Overview

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is built on Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC) AMPscript with a table-based XHTML layout. Three compliance-level headers are unconfirmed at the relay layer, and a commented-out SHA-256 hashing block creates a data-handling ambiguity in the personalization layer.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 click-redirect links skipped by QA probe.** All links route through `e.neimanmarcus.com` (SFMC click-tracking domain). Because the probe could not follow redirects, UTM parameters and final destination URLs are unverified. This is a coverage gap, not a confirmed bug, but it means landing page alignment (§6) cannot be validated.

No issues confirmed within the HTML link markup itself — insufficient source to audit further.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in active viewport meta tag.**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">
```
Disabling pinch-to-zoom fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Three dead viewport meta tags left in source (commented out).**
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
Template hygiene issue; no functional impact but indicates accumulated copy-paste debt. The `user-scalable=no` variant would have the same WCAG 1.4.4 violation as above.

**iOS 9 webkit animation bugfix is present.**
```css
body { -webkit-animation: bugfix infinite 1s; }
@-webkit-keyframes bugfix { from { padding: 0; } to { padding: 0; } }
```
iOS 9 is end-of-life (2016). This adds parse overhead with no benefit on any current client. Safe to remove.

**`*[class=...]` attribute selectors used throughout mobile CSS.** This pattern (e.g., `*[class=contenttable]`) is a legacy hack for Gmail's pre-2016 class-stripping behavior. Modern Gmail supports standard class selectors. Not a breakage, but produces unnecessary specificity complexity.

**`<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has a trailing space.** Minor; no functional impact.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**SHA-256 hashing of `@user` is commented out.**
```amscript
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */-->
```
As deployed, `@user` is set via:
```amscript
@user Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)
```
This returns the raw `EMAIL_ID` integer, not a hashed email address. If `@user` is appended to any tracking URL or passed to an analytics endpoint, it exposes an internal subscriber identifier rather than the intended hashed value. **Confirm whether `@user` appears in any link parameters before the next send.**

**"CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW" requires manual pre-send selection.** The template comment indicates two deployment paths exist and one must be manually activated. This is a human-error risk point; no automated guard is visible in the source.

**`@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")` is a no-op.** Concatenating with an empty string serves no purpose; likely a copy-paste artifact from a template that previously appended a suffix.

**Four separate `Lookup()` calls to two data extensions** (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`) each querying by `EMAIL_ADDRESS`. At high send volume, batching `NM_CRM_Feed` lookups into a single `LookupRows()` call reduces query overhead, but this is a performance note, not a correctness issue.
