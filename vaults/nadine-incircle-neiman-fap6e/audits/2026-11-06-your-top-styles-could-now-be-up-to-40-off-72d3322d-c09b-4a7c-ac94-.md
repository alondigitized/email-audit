# Your top styles could now be up to 40% off
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-06**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, on-brand sale announcement, and it is nearly a repeat of yesterday's "New sale arrivals: up to 40% off Amiri, Vince & more." The sage-green hero is the same, with a giant black serif "S" and "UP TO 40% OFF". The eye lands on the offer first, and the black SHOP SALE button is unmistakable. Below it sits a tidy 3x3 grid of products with brand names: Cinq à Sept, Amiri, Theory, Polo Ralph Lauren, Vince, Stuart Weitzman, Schutz, Tory Burch and Chiara Boni La Petite Robe.
- The execution is fine, but the idea is weak. The subject promises "your top styles," which hints at personalization. The body is a generic sale grid with no prices, no sale badges and no "your" anything. Nothing in the render shows these are styles I browsed or saved. A subscriber who got the Nov 5 sale email will see this as the same send with new product photos.
- Verdict: competent and professional, but a low-effort re-send. It works as a reminder and doesn't give me a new reason to click.

## What's working

- The hero is bold and instantly legible. The "S" with "ale" is a distinctive brand moment, and "UP TO 40% OFF" is the biggest text after the logo.
- The SHOP SALE button is high-contrast black on sage and impossible to miss.
- The product grid is evenly spaced, with consistent grey backdrops and bold brand labels. It reads as a premium assortment across categories: dresses, outerwear, tailoring, knitwear, shoes and a bag.
- The six category buttons (women's clothing, men's, shoes, handbags, dresses, accessories) give useful secondary paths.
- No render problems. Images load, text is crisp and nothing overlaps.

## What's weak

- The subject says "Your top styles," but nothing in the body feels personalized. It's a generic grid with no "picked for you" framing.
- There are no prices, no percent-off tags and no markdowns on any product. A shopper can't tell whether the sweater is 15% or 40% off, so the grid has no price pull.
- The hero's "Exclusions Apply" is tiny. The footer adds "Savings of 25-40% off regular prices," so "up to 40%" is really 25-40% on select items.
- The creative and offer duplicate the Nov 5 "New sale arrivals" send. There is no escalation and no deadline, so it adds fatigue.
- All nine product tiles carry only brand names, and the nine tiles and six category buttons compete for attention with the single hero CTA.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make it match the subject or change the subject.** Either use real browse or wishlist data ("Back on sale: styles you viewed"), or drop the personal claim. This one change would lift the email most.
- 2. **Add the price to each tile.** Show a struck-through original price, the sale price and a "-35%" tag. That turns a lookbook into a shopping grid.
- 3. **Add a deadline or a reason.** Use something like "Ends Sunday" or "Extra markdowns just added." Without one, this is the same email as yesterday.
- 4. **Change the creative from the Nov 5 send.** Use a different hero, or lead with the one category the subscriber cares about.
- 5. **Trim the nine tiles and six buttons** to a smaller hero set with a clearer path.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Just marked down: Amiri, Vince & Theory`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Sale ends Sunday: up to 40% off your faves`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New markdowns on Polo Ralph Lauren, Tory Burch, Stuart Weitzman and more.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Savings of 25-40% on select styles, online and in stores.`

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Content before DOCTYPE:** A long HTML comment with SFMC variable documentation comes before `<!DOCTYPE html PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD XHTML 1.0 Transitional//EN" ...>`. Content before the DOCTYPE can put some legacy clients (IE-engine/older Outlook) into quirks mode.
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom on mobile, which conflicts with WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
- **No language attribute:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang`/`xml:lang`, so screen readers can't pick the right pronunciation.
- **Title:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has a trailing space and is generic. This is minor.
- **Exact-match class selectors:** The mobile rules use exact-match selectors such as `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=mobile]` and `*[class=hide]`. They silently fail on any element that has more than one class, so the mobile layout is fragile.
- **Checkbox-hack hamburger nav:** The nav relies on `.navbar-checkbox:checked~*[class].menu`. It isn't supported in Gmail or most Outlook clients, and it has no visible keyboard or ARIA handling. Whether a non-interactive fallback exists can't be seen in the truncated source.
- **Conflicting declarations:** `*[class].show` declares `width:auto !important` and later `width:278px`. The second is dead code.
- **Not verifiable:** Image `alt` text, image dimensions and total HTML size (Gmail clips near 102 KB) aren't visible in the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` not found (RFC 8058):** One-click unsubscribe can't be verified. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender requirements make it mandatory for high-volume senders.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip the pre-DOCTYPE SFMC comment block before send. This removes the quirks-mode risk and the exposure of internal DE names.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag.
3. Add `lang="en"` (and `xml:lang`) to `<html>`, and give the `<title>` a descriptive value without the trailing space.
4. Switch the mobile selectors to `[class~=...]` or plain class selectors, and remove the duplicate `width` declaration.
5. Confirm that the hamburger nav has a non-interactive fallback in Gmail and Outlook.
6. Check `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` against the original message, since the relay may have stripped them. Likewise check SPF/DKIM/DMARC in the raw headers.
7. Shorten the URLs in the plain-text part, or add more descriptive text around them.
8. Re-run the QA with the tracking links resolved, and with the full HTML including the footer. That would verify the final destinations, parameter hygiene, alt text, the unsubscribe link and the postal address.
