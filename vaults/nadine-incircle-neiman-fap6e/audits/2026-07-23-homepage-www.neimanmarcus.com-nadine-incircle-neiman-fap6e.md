# Homepage snapshot · www.neimanmarcus.com · 2026-07-23
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-23**
## Executive summary

- I open Neiman Marcus on my phone and instead of the homepage I get a wall of text telling me my device looks like a bot. The logo is there — elegant as always — but below it is a gray error screen accusing me of "rapid taps or clicks" and "automated activity." There is literally nothing to shop, no offers, no imagery, no navigation. This is not a luxury experience; this is a door slammed in a loyal customer's face.

## What's working

- **The Neiman Marcus wordmark** at the top is clean and recognizable — at least I know I'm in the right place.
- **"Submit feedback" link** exists, which is marginally better than a dead end with no recourse.
- A ghost image of what appears to be a model's feet and shoes peeks in at the very bottom of the frame — proof that a real homepage exists somewhere behind this wall.

## What's weak

- **The access block itself** is the entire above-the-fold experience. As an InCircle member who visits twice a season, being treated like a fraud attempt is humiliating and off-brand for a store that knows my name at the door.
- **The error copy is written for a systems administrator**, not a 51-year-old woman shopping on her phone during lunch. "Automated (bot) activity on your network (IP 47.229.200.33)" means nothing to me and reads as accusatory.
- **No graceful retry or alternative path** — there's no "try again," no phone number, no chat link to a stylist. The "Submit feedback" link is the only exit and it sounds like filing a complaint with HR.
- **The IP address is displayed publicly** — that's a strange, overly technical detail that adds to the clinical, cold feel.
- **Zero shopping content** is visible. No hero, no categories, no offers, no new arrivals. The page is a dead stop.

## Recommendations

- **Replace the generic bot-block with a soft challenge** — a simple "tap to confirm you're human" or a CAPTCHA that doesn't read like an arrest warrant. Keep the brand voice warm even in error states.
- **Add an InCircle/logged-in escape hatch** — if a customer is signed into their account, suppress or soft-gate the block entirely; loyal members should never see this wall.
- **Rewrite the error copy in brand voice** — something like "We're having trouble loading your experience — tap below to continue" rather than a bullet list of IP addresses and developer tools.
- **Surface a fallback CTA** — even in a restricted state, show the store phone number or a "chat with a stylist" link so a customer like me has somewhere to go.
