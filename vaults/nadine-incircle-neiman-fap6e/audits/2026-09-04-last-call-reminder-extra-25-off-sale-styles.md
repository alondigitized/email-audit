# Last Call Reminder: extra 25% off sale styles
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-04**
## Executive summary

- This is Neiman Marcus's Last Call sub-brand doing what it does best: a clean, unambiguous discount stack — extra 25% off on top of sale styles already marked to 70% off, deadline stamped right in the fine print (ends 9/7/26). Compared to the brand's recent run of $250-off gift-card blasts (8/26–9/2) and editorial detours (Cleo Wade, earrings), this reads as the most straightforwardly transactional send of the bunch, closer in spirit to the 9/3 "Surprise: 25% off" email but with a harder discount and a real end date instead of vague urgency language.
- The nine-item grid is genuinely well-curated — Zimmermann, Burberry, Versace, Staud, Gianvito Rossi — mixing women's, men's, and accessories in a way that gives almost any shopper something to land on. The red hero block is loud and legible with zero render bugs. This is a solid execution of a well-worn playbook; it won't surprise anyone, but it will convert better than the vague-urgency gift-card sends this brand has been leaning on.

## What's working

- Hero is unmissable: "EXTRA 25% OFF" in huge white type on red, with "UP TO 70% OFF" stacked right below it — the full stack of value is visible in one glance.
- A specific, credible deadline is present in the footer copy ("Ends 9/7/26 at 10:00 PM CT"), which the last several gift-card sends lacked.
- Product grid is diverse and desirable — three rows spanning dresses, handbags, shoes, and menswear, each clearly labeled with the designer name.
- Six category shop buttons (Women's, Men's, Handbags, Shoes, Kids, Dresses) give multiple paths to purchase beyond the single hero CTA.
- No layout bugs, no broken images, no lorem ipsum — clean render top to bottom.

## What's weak

- "SHOP SALE" is the only CTA inside the hero, and it's a small white button on a huge red block — it should be bigger or repeated below the product grid.
- "Exclusions Apply" sits directly under the CTA in the hero, which undercuts the offer's credibility right where the eye lands next.
- The text-signup black block ("Claim your 15% off") competes with the main 25%-off message and is visually the second-most prominent element in the email — a stronger hero-offer callback (e.g., a second CTA button) would use that space better.
- Subject line repeats "Last Call" and "25% off sale styles" almost verbatim in the hero — no surprise or added info once opened.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a second "SHOP SALE" button directly beneath the nine-product grid — right now the only CTA is 1,600+ pixels above the products, forcing a scroll back up to act.
- 2. Move or shrink "Exclusions Apply" — right now it sits right under the CTA in bold-adjacent placement; push it to the footer with the other legal copy.
- 3. Swap the SMS-signup black block for a countdown or "Ends 9/7" reminder — reinforces urgency instead of introducing a separate ask.
- 4. Test a subject line that leads with the compounding math (25% + 70%) rather than repeating "Last Call," which this brand already uses constantly as a sub-brand name.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Last Call ends 9/7: take an extra 25% off sale`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Zimmermann, Burberry, Versace and more — final markdowns before they're gone.`

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Bug:** Raw AMPscript scaffolding/documentation is exposed as an HTML comment at the top of the source (`<!--REQUIRED variables: @email (EMAIL_ADDRESS from sendable DE) @base_refid ... @user Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", ...) ... CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW -->`). This is developer/template documentation that should never ship in a live send — while it's inside a comment (won't render visually), it is a template-hygiene leak that exposes internal data-extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`), lookup field logic (`uEm`, `cmdid`, `emailid`), and hashing method (`SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256`) to anyone who views source.
- The presence of a "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO" instruction with no visible indication of which scenario was selected suggests a risk that the wrong personalization branch (or none) was compiled into this send — cannot confirm actual `@user`/`@refid` resolution from the truncated source.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header not found** — either absent or not captured by the relay; without it, mailbox providers can't surface a native unsubscribe action.
- **[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not found (RFC 8058)** — one-click unsubscribe is not supported, which is required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender rules for high-volume senders.
- **[WARN] `Authentication-Results` header not found** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status could not be verified for this message.
- Cannot confirm presence of a footer unsubscribe *link* (CAN-SPAM's actual legal requirement) since the HTML is truncated before the footer; the `.disclaimerText`/`.footerText` CSS classes exist, implying a footer is present, but its content is unverified.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- No link URLs are visible in the truncated source, so UTM parameter presence/consistency and landing-page alignment cannot be assessed in this pass.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip AMPscript documentation comments (variable lists, DE names, "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO" notes) from the production template before send — confirm the CI/build step that should remove dev comments didn't fail.
2. Add `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers to comply with 2024+ Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender requirements and enable one-click unsubscribe.
3. Investigate why `Authentication-Results` is absent — confirm SPF/DKIM/DMARC are actually passing at the sending MTA, not just missing from this relay's capture.
