# 1,000+ new arrivals for wardrobe building
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-31**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, well-organized "new arrivals" send — a black-and-white hero, twelve products split evenly across women's and men's, brand-name callouts (Versace, Prada, Dior, Kiton), and two clear category CTAs. But it's yet another zero-discount send in a week where Neiman Marcus has otherwise been running a legitimate $250-off mechanic (8/26–8/29) alongside a string of no-offer "lookbook" emails (Classic Crossbodies, Daytime Dresses, Tailored for any occasion). There's nothing wrong with this execution technically — it's tidy and shoppable — but it gives a subscriber zero reason to act today versus browsing whenever.
- The subject line "1,000+ new arrivals for wardrobe building" is honest but generic — it's a volume claim, not a hook, and it doesn't tell me anything about price, category priority, or urgency. This lands as a mid-tier catalog email: fine for someone actively browsing, forgettable for someone triaging an inbox.

## What's working

- Clear two-column structure (Women's block, then Men's block) each with 6 products and its own "Shop All" CTA — easy to scan.
- Product photography is consistent, well-lit, and correctly labeled with brand names (STAUD, Versace, Miu Miu, Prada, Dior, etc.).
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, text doesn't overlap, layout holds together top to bottom.
- Hero is simple and legible: "BRAND NEW STYLES JUST LANDED" with two immediate shop links (Women's/Men's) right under the fold.
- Footer utility (app download, personal stylist match, store finder) adds real value beyond just another unsubscribe block.

## What's weak

- Zero pricing anywhere — not one of the 12 products shows a price, let alone a discount, continuing the pattern flagged in "Fashion Finds: Daytime Dresses" (8/29) and "Tailored for any occasion" (8/28).
- No urgency, no personalization, no loyalty tie-in — this reads identical whether sent today or in three weeks.
- "1,000+ new arrivals" is a stat that isn't paid off in the email — only 12 items are shown, so the number feels like inbox bait rather than a real signal of scale.
- No mixed hero visual — unlike "The Roundup" (8/30) or "Poised for Perfection" (8/28), there's no lifestyle/model photography, just type on black, so nothing anchors the eye emotionally before the eye moves to product photography.
- Cadence risk: this is now the brand's fourth type of no-discount browse-y send in five days (Crossbodies, Dresses, Tailoring, now New Arrivals) — subscribers who want a deal have every reason to tune these out.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a price or "starting at $X" under at least the hero CTAs, or pair this send with the $250-off mechanic the brand has been running all week — right now this email and the discount campaign feel like two different brands.
- 2. Make "1,000+" mean something — link it to a landing page truly showing volume, or swap the number for a specific claim ("This week only") that ties to urgency.
- 3. Add a hero lifestyle image (a model in one look) above or beside the "BRAND NEW STYLES" type block to give the eye a human anchor before the grid, consistent with recent sends like "Poised for Perfection."
- 4. Cut the product count in the body from 12 to 6–8 curated hero pieces per category, and move the rest behind "Shop All" — the current grid is undifferentiated, no items stand out as "the ones to see."
- **Subject Alt A:** `New in: Versace, Prada, Dior, and more`
- **Subject Alt B:** `This week's new arrivals, picked for you`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh pieces from Staud, Chloe, Miu Miu, and Ralph Lauren just landed.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop new arrivals in women's and men's — no wait required.`

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- 30 tracking/click-redirect links were present but **skipped by the automated HTTP prober** (expected behavior for redirect-domain links; destinations not independently verified — flag as unverified, not broken).
- Plain-text part is **86.7% URLs** (5,289 of 6,098 chars) — QA WARN. This ratio is a known spam-filter heuristic trigger (Barracuda/Proofpoint/Cisco score plain-text URL density); the plain-text alternative should carry more prose relative to link count.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Finding:** The delivered HTML `<head>` contains a literal, un-rendered HTML comment documenting the AMPscript personalization contract — variable names and internal data-model references are exposed verbatim in the sent source:

  - `@user = Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)`

  - `@uEm = Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)`

- This indicates the actual AMPscript block (`%%[ ... ]%%`) that should populate/resolve these values isn't in the excerpt — either it was correctly stripped downstream and this comment is inert leftover documentation, or (if this comment ships as-is to inboxes) it discloses internal Data Extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`) and field names (`EMAIL_ID`, `uEm`, `cmdid`) to end recipients — a data-model disclosure risk, not a functional bug per se.
