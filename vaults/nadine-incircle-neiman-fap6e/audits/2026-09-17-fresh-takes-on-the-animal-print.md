# Fresh takes on the animal print
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-17**
## Executive summary

- This is Neiman Marcus's thinnest send in the recent history — one hero image, one headline, one CTA, no offer, no price, no product grid. Compared to yesterday's Friends & Family event (25% off select styles + 20% off jewelry, 7.00/10) or even the "New sale arrivals" catalog send (6.00/10), this is a pure brand/mood piece with zero commercial content visible.
- The visual is striking — a single model in a pink dress with a zebra-print bag, "Wild & Wonderful" headline, "Shop Animalia" button — but there's no price, no percentage off, no product name, no second option. It reads like a lookbook page, not a marketing email, and after two straight weeks of markdown events this brand has been running, a no-offer trend piece is a step backward in urgency and conversion potential.
- Judgement: a good lookbook/awareness send, a weak commercial email. Fine as an occasional brand-building beat, but it doesn't stand up next to this brand's discount-driven cadence.

## What's working

- Clean, uncluttered layout — the eye goes straight to the model and the zebra bag.
- The "Wild & Wonderful" headline and "Shop Animalia" CTA are thematically coherent and on-trend for fall animal print.
- No render bugs — image, type, and button all display cleanly.
- Clear top nav (Women/Men/Shoes/Sale) gives an easy fallback path if the hero doesn't land.

## What's weak

- No visible offer, price, or discount anywhere in the email — a pure trend/mood send with nothing transactional.
- Single product shown (the zebra bag) with no name, price, or "shop the look" breakdown of the dress.
- "Shop Animalia" is vague — doesn't tell the reader what they're clicking into (a collection page? one bag? a whole animal-print assortment?).
- No social proof, no urgency, no loyalty/member callout.
- Coming right after two straight sale-driven sends, this de-escalates momentum rather than building on it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a concrete offer or at least a starting price ("Bags from $X" or tie it into the ongoing Friends & Family 25% off) — right now there's nothing to click for besides curiosity.
- 2. Rename the CTA to something specific: "Shop Zebra Print Bags" or "Shop Animal Print" instead of "Shop Animalia," which is unclear and untested as brand language.
- 3. Add a second product tile below the hero (e.g., 2-3 more animal-print pieces — shoes, scarf, dress) so this isn't a single-SKU bet.
- 4. If this is meant to ride the Friends & Family event still running, say so — "25% off animal print, ends soon" would connect trend content to the active promo instead of running it in isolation.
