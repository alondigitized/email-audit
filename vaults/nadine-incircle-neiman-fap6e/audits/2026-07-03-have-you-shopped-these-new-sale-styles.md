# Have you shopped these new sale styles?
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-03**
## Executive summary

- This is Neiman Marcus's fifth sale-focused touchpoint in three days — following the blunt "Up to 60% off. Need we say more?" that landed yesterday at a 9/10. The creative pivots to a re-engagement angle ("Have you shopped these new sale styles?") paired with a curated nine-product editorial grid: Zimmermann, Dries Van Noten, Off White, Manolo Blahnik, Giorgio Armani, Ferragamo, Carolina Herrera. The curation is genuinely strong and the editorial photography reads premium.
- The core problem is that this email has nothing new to say. The "up to an extra 40% off" offer launched on July 1 and has now appeared in at least three consecutive sends with no price anchoring, no deadline, and no escalation. The question-format subject is the weakest hook in this entire series — it generates mild curiosity but no urgency, and it pulls backward from yesterday's more assertive "60% off" positioning.
- The email looks good and the brand selection is right. But it's a reskin of the same message, and subscribers who didn't convert in four days aren't persuaded by a polite re-engagement nudge. The missing sale prices and missing close date are the two levers that could have moved this, and both are absent.

## What's working

- **Luxury brand curation**: Nine distinct designer names across the grid (Zimmermann, Manolo Blahnik, Carolina Herrera, Giorgio Armani, Ferragamo) create real pull for the target shopper.
- **Hero typography**: The oversized decorative "Sale" treatment is aspirational rather than discount-bin — it communicates event without cheapening the brand.
- **Clean editorial layout**: No render bugs, consistent white-background product photography, clear grid structure.
- **Hero CTA placement**: "SHOP SALE" button sits directly below the offer copy with minimal visual noise between offer and action.
- **Multi-category CTA block**: Six category-level buttons at the bottom (Women's Dresses, Handbags, Men's Shoes, etc.) give shoppers with different intent a clear path.

## What's weak

- **Zero prices visible**: A sale email showing nine products without a single before/after price abandons the most powerful conversion signal. "Extra 40% off" means nothing without a price anchor.
- **No sale deadline**: "Up to an extra 40% off" has appeared since July 1 with no close date — killing urgency four emails deep into the same event.
- **Stale offer, weaker framing**: Yesterday's email led with "Up to 60% off." Today steps back to "Have you shopped?" — a regression in both urgency and specificity.
- **Products aren't individual CTAs**: The nine product tiles don't link to their product pages; conversion paths route through generic category buttons, adding friction and losing intent signal.
- **Question-format subject is the series' weakest hook**: It works as a CRM re-engagement tactic but competes poorly against its own recent siblings.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show a price.** Pick two or three of the nine products and display "Was $895 / Now $537." One real number does more than five emails of "up to 40% off."
- 2. **Add a hard close date.** "Sale ends Sunday midnight" or "Last 48 hours" transforms this from a passive restatement into a credible urgency signal.
- 3. **Make every product tile a direct link.** The Zimmermann dress, Manolo sandal, and Ferragamo bag should each go straight to the product PDP, not to the category browse.
- 4. **Escalate, don't re-announce.** On day three of a sale, the non-converters need a new signal — a deeper cut on a subset of styles, or a countdown — not the same discount restated.
- 5. **Subject rewrite:**
- **Subject Alt A:** `40% off Zimmermann, Ferragamo, Armani — sale ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last days: extra 40% off the sale. Manolo Blahnik, more.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Nine designer picks, prices dropping. See what's on sale now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Zimmermann, Carolina Herrera, Giorgio Armani — up to 40% off, limited time.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus: "Have you shopped these new sale styles?"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Table-based layout with AMPscript personalization (Salesforce Marketing Cloud), responsive CSS via media queries, and a CSS-only hamburger nav. QA returned 4 warnings and a 43% pass rate, driven by compliance header gaps and a malformed plain-text body.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text body is 87% raw URLs** — 5,225 of 6,027 characters are URLs per QA scan. This strongly suggests the plain-text alternative was auto-generated from HTML without sanitization, producing a near-unreadable body. Some spam filters penalize this ratio.

**26 click-redirect links skipped** — all tracking links point through a redirect domain (likely `click.e.neimanmarcus.com` or similar). The HTTP probe could not follow them to verify final destination URLs or UTM parameter presence.

No broken link errors were returned for the links that were probed, so the redirect infrastructure itself appears up.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Dead commented-out viewport meta tags left in production source:**
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
Three deprecated viewport overrides remain in the `<head>`. These are inert but add noise and suggest stale template debt.

**CSS-only hamburger nav** uses the checkbox-hack pattern (`navbar-checkbox:checked ~ .menu`). This is not supported in Outlook on Windows (any version) or Gmail's web client, meaning mobile nav is broken for those environments without a fallback.

**`*[class=...]` attribute selectors** — the responsive stylesheet targets elements via `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=mobile]`, etc. This pattern breaks if any element carries multiple classes, since it matches only exact single-class strings. No issues confirmed from source alone, but it's a latent fragility.

Cannot confirm `alt` text on images from the truncated source — recommend verifying all `<img>` tags carry non-empty `alt` attributes for screen readers and image-blocked clients.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**AMPscript variable block rendered into HTML comments:**
```html
<!--REQUIRED variables: @email ... @user Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", ...) 
    @uEm = Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm", ...) @cmdid Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "cmdid", ...) -->
```
This comment is delivered verbatim to every recipient's inbox. It exposes internal Salesforce data extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`) and field names (`uEm`, `cmdid`, `EMAIL_ID`). These are internal schema details that should not be in outbound HTML.

**Hashed email variable is commented out:**
```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */-->
```
The instruction block says "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW" but the SHA-256 hashed email assignment is inside a comment, not active AMPscript. If `@user` is used downstream in tracking pixels or link parameters, it will resolve to empty/null for all recipients in this send path.

---

### 5. Compliance

| Check | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| List-Unsubscribe header | **WARN** | Not captured by relay — may be stripped or absent |
| List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) | **WARN** | One-click unsubscribe not confirmed |
| SPF/DKIM/DMARC | **WARN** | `Authentication-Results` header absent from relay capture |

The missing `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers are a material issue. Gmail and Yahoo require both for bulk senders (>5k/day) under their 2024 enforcement rules. If these headers are genuinely absent (vs. stripped by the AgentMail relay), this send is out of compliance and risks deliverability throttling.

The missing `Authentication-Results` header means SPF/DKIM pass/fail status is unverified for this capture. Sending domain is `e.neimanmarcus.com` — authentication should be confirmed at the ESP level.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

Cannot verify UTM parameters on destination URLs — all 26 tracked links were skipped by the HTTP probe. Manual spot-check of a decoded click-redirect URL is needed to confirm:
- `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` are present
- `utm_content` or `utm_term` align with this specific send

The `@base_refid` / `@refid` AMPscript variables suggest a ref ID is appended to URLs. If `@user` (hashed email) is null due to the commented-out assignment noted above, any personalized URL parameter depending on it will silently drop.

---

### 7. Recommendations

1. **Remove the AMPscript comment block from the template.** Move variable documentation to an internal wiki or template notes field. Do not ship internal data extension names to recipient inboxes.

2. **Activate or remove the `@user` SHA-256 assignment.** Decide which personalization scenario is live and delete the dead branch. The current state leaves `@user` unset.

3. **Verify List-Unsubscribe headers at the ESP level.** Confirm SFMC is injecting `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` on outbound mail. If the AgentMail relay is stripping them, whitelist those headers.

4. **Regenerate or manually author the plain-text part.** The 87% URL ratio in plain-text is a spam signal and useless to plain-text readers. SFMC's plain-text editor should be used to write a human-readable summary.

5. **Audit `Authentication-Results` on a direct SMTP capture** (not via relay) to confirm SPF/DKIM are passing for `e.neimanmarcus.com`.

6. **Remove or replace the CSS hamburger nav** with a bulletproof fallback for Outlook/Gmail, or accept it degrades gracefully to a static nav in those clients and document that as intentional.

7. **Purge the three commented-out viewport meta tags** — they add no value and reflect unresolved historical experiments.
