# The Designer Sale: Shop Givenchy, Ferragamo & more
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- This is the third consecutive Designer Sale send from Neiman Marcus — following the 10/10 launch email on 5/18 ("It's here! The Designer Sale") and the 9/10 urgency email on 5/17 ("Your exclusive access ends tonight!"). By day three the architecture is identical and the cadence is becoming noise. The red hero still delivers cleanly — "THE DESIGNER SALE / UP TO 50% OFF" with a hard "SHOP SALE" button — and the nine-brand product grid is well-curated. But the strategic gap is real: yesterday's launch email nailed the reveal moment; today's follow-up needed new creative ammunition to justify another send.
- The most concrete problem: the subject line promises Givenchy and Ferragamo, but Ferragamo is nowhere in the product grid. That's a broken contract — the subscriber who opens expecting Ferragamo finds no Ferragamo. The five-day InCircle email (also sent today) also went out separately, and that loyalty framing would have given this email more reason to exist if woven in.
- Verdict: a technically clean email that loses points for being a recycled template at a moment when the campaign needed to evolve.

## What's working

- Red hero is fast and decisive — offer lands in three lines without scrolling.
- Six category CTA buttons at the bottom (Women's Clothing, Men's, Women's Shoes, Men's Shoes, Handbags, Dresses) cover multiple intent paths without cluttering the main funnel.
- Nine-brand product grid is well-curated and features recognizable luxury names (ALAIA, Burberry, Courreges, Givenchy, Polo Ralph Lauren, Jacquemus).
- "Exclusions Apply" and "LIMITED TIME" are both visible in or near the hero — at least the caveat is disclosed.
- Clean render, no broken images, no overlapping text.

## What's weak

- Subject names Ferragamo; Ferragamo is not in the visible product grid — the subject over-promises.
- "LIMITED TIME" with no deadline date is weaker than yesterday's "access ends tonight!" urgency hook; the campaign has regressed on urgency signaling.
- No InCircle / loyalty context — NM sent an InCircle pitch on the same day; a one-line loyalty callout ("InCircle members earn double points today") would have differentiated this send.
- Three near-identical Designer Sale emails in 48 hours is cadence fatigue territory; a subscriber who opened all three has seen the same red hero three times with diminishing returns.
- Product grid offers no price anchors — no original price, no "now $X" — so "UP TO 50% OFF" stays abstract.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show actual sale prices in the product grid.** Even one "Was $895 → Now $447" entry makes the 50%-off headline feel real. Right now the grid is pure editorial; the offer is only in the hero.
- 2. **Make the deadline concrete.** Replace "LIMITED TIME" with "Ends Sunday" or "Sale ends in 3 days." The 5/17 email did this well with "access ends tonight" — borrow that urgency.
- 3. **Fix the subject/body Ferragamo gap.** If Ferragamo is in the sale, put a Ferragamo product in the grid. If it isn't, swap it out for Courreges or Cult Gaia which are both visible.
- 4. **Fold in InCircle.** A single line — "InCircle members: double points on sale purchases through Sunday" — gives loyalty subscribers a reason to click that non-members don't have. Especially relevant given today's separate InCircle email.
- 5. **Vary the hero creative.** The red block is strong but has now appeared three sends in a row. A lifestyle image in the hero would reset visual fatigue while keeping the offer copy.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sale prices on 9 designer brands — see what's marked down`

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Designer Sale" Email

### 1. Technical Summary
Salesforce Marketing Cloud deployment using AMPscript with CRM lookup-based personalization. QA pass rate is 43%, driven by four infrastructure warnings — none are fatal rendering bugs, but two compliance headers are unconfirmed and the plain-text body is a deliverability risk.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 tracking links skipped — no HTTP probe results available.**
All outbound links route through a click-redirect domain. Zero broken-link confirmation is possible from this audit; a secondary probe against the redirect chain is required to verify destination URLs resolve and UTM parameters are intact.

**Plain-text body is 87% URLs by character count (5,140 / 5,910 chars).**
QA threshold is 70%; this exceeds it. A plain-text part composed almost entirely of raw redirect URLs is a strong spam-filter signal (SpamAssassin `URI_ONLY`, Barracuda heuristics). The plain-text version should mirror readable human copy, not dump link lists.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Three commented-out `<meta name="viewport">` tags remain in `<head>`.**
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
Dead code with no rendering impact, but indicates prior viewport thrashing. The active tag (`maximum-scale=1`) suppresses user zoom — an accessibility concern on its own.

**`max-device-width` used in media queries.**
```css
@media only screen and (max-device-width: 480px), screen and (max-width: 480px)
```
`max-device-width` is deprecated (CSS Media Queries Level 4 removed it). Modern clients ignore it; only the `max-width` arm fires. No breakage today, but a maintenance liability.

**CSS checkbox-hack hamburger nav relies on `:checked` pseudo-class.**
```css
*[class].navbar-checkbox:checked~*[class].menu { display: block; }
```
This technique fails in Gmail (strips `<input>` elements) and Outlook desktop entirely. If the hamburger is the only nav affordance on mobile and those clients represent significant audience share, it silently hides navigation.

**Image alt text not auditable** — HTML is truncated before `<body>` product content. A full-source audit should verify all `<img>` tags carry non-empty `alt` attributes per WCAG 2.1 AA and Yahoo/Gmail rendering-off fallback behavior.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`@user` assignment is commented out.**
The comment block reads:
```
/*  SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256  */
```
If `@user` is referenced downstream (e.g., in preference-center or tracking URLs), this will render as an empty string or AMPscript error token. Confirm whether `@user` appears in any `%%=v(@user)=%%` substitution in the full template.

**`@uEm` and `@cmdid` have no fallback.**
Both are single-lookup calls against `NM_CRM_Feed` with no `@default` argument:
```
@uEm  = Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm",   "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)
@cmdid = Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "cmdid", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)
```
If a subscriber is absent from that data extension, both variables return empty string. If either is appended to tracking URLs (common pattern), it silently degrades analytics for unmatched records. Add `IIF(Empty(@uEm), "unknown", @uEm)` guards or a default argument.

**`@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid, "")` is a no-op.**
This produces an identical string to `@base_refid`. Either a suffix was removed and the concat was not cleaned up, or a dynamic suffix is missing. Verify intent.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not confirmed present.**
QA: `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header not found`.
Gmail and Yahoo require this header for bulk senders (>5,000/day). Its absence — even if caused by AgentMail relay not forwarding it — will suppress the one-click unsubscribe button in Gmail UI and risks inbox placement downgrade. Verify the header is set at the MTA/ESP level and survives relay.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not confirmed present.**
QA: `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found`.
Required alongside `List-Unsubscribe` for Gmail's one-click POST mechanism. Without it, Gmail falls back to mailto-based unsubscribe or omits the button entirely. Both headers must be present and aligned.

**SPF/DKIM authentication status unknown.**
QA: `[WARN] Authentication-Results header not found`.
The AgentMail relay is not forwarding (or is not capturing) the `Authentication-Results` header. Sending domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` should have DKIM signed by SFMC and SPF aligned. Inability to confirm means DMARC pass/fail is unverifiable from this capture. Request a raw `.eml` with full headers from a direct inbox delivery to verify.
