# Starts now: Up to $500 off your next order
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-22**
## Executive summary

- This is a spend-to-save promo — a tiered discount mechanic that lets Neiman Marcus lead with the eye-catching "$500 OFF" headline while most subscribers will land well below that ceiling. The structure is straightforward: a centered hero, a clean spend/save table, a shoppable product grid across two designer rows, and a grid of category CTAs. The hierarchy works — the eye goes logo → offer → table → products without friction.
- The offer itself is the tension point. "UP TO $500 OFF" is technically honest but functionally misleading — the entry tier saves $50 on a $500 purchase, a 10% return that undersells the visual promise. After a punishing five-day run of Designer Sale emails, this at least feels like a format reset: new mechanic, new hero, new product mix. But without a hard end date, "Starts now" doesn't close the loop on urgency.
- The product grid is the email's quiet strength — nine designer names across two rows (Jimmy Choo, Jacquemus, Chiara Boni La Petite Robe, Cinq à Sept, Stuart Weitzman, LANA) give this editorial credibility without feeling like a clearance dump. Overall, a competent promotional send that would land better with a visible deadline and a more honest headline framing.

## What's working

- Tiered spend/save table is scannable at a glance — amounts are clearly laid out in columns with no ambiguity
- Designer roster in the product grid is strong and consistent with NM's positioning (Jacquemus, Jimmy Choo, Stuart Weitzman, Chiara Boni)
- "Starts now" creates forward momentum without exclamation-mark abuse
- Six-category CTA grid at the bottom (Women's Clothing, Men's, Shoes, Handbags, Jewelry) reduces dead ends for different shopper types
- Clean render — no overlapping elements, no broken images, tight layout throughout

## What's weak

- "UP TO $500 OFF" overpromises — the entry tier ($50 off a $500 spend) is a 10% savings ratio that feels thin once the table is read
- No end date visible anywhere — "Starts now" without a close date is half an urgency story
- Product grid shows no prices, so the discount is abstract until you click and land on a PDP
- Sixth NM email in roughly five days; even a format switch can't fully defuse send-frequency fatigue
- The app download block at the bottom dilutes focus in an already multi-section email

## Recommendations

- 1. **Reframe the headline around a relatable tier, not the max.** "Spend $500, save $50 — up to $500 off" anchors expectations and still lets the top tier shine. The current "$500 OFF" hero sends a lot of subscribers to the table feeling undersold.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline** — even a vague "through Sunday" sharpens the urgency that "Starts now" only implies.
- 3. **Show one hero product with a before/after price** so the discount feels real, not hypothetical.
- 4. **Remove or demote the app block** — it breaks the promotional momentum right when the CTAs should be closing the loop.
- 5. **Suppress or delay for recent openers of the Designer Sale sends** — the cadence this week has been relentless and a 24-hour suppression would protect deliverability goodwill.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Up to $500 off" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has no blocking rendering failures, but carries two compliance gaps that directly affect inbox placement under current Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender rules, and the AMPscript comment block exposes the full variable schema in production HTML. QA flagged four warnings; two are material.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 tracking links — destinations unverifiable**
All click-redirect URLs (domain: `e.neimanmarcus.com` click-tracker) were skipped by the HTTP probe. Cannot confirm final destinations resolve, UTM params are present, or landing pages are live.

**Plain-text body is 70%+ URLs (5,095 / 6,346 chars)**
The plain-text part is effectively not human-readable — it is a dump of raw tracking URLs. This pattern triggers spam filters that look for URL-to-text ratio in the text/plain MIME part and degrades the message's reputation score with aggressive filters.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta blocks pinch-to-zoom**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">
```
Prevents users from zooming on mobile. Accessibility failure (WCAG 1.4.4). Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Three commented-out viewport tags are dead production code**
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
Debugging artifacts left in production. No functional impact, but adds payload and reveals iteration history.

**Hamburger nav relies on CSS `:checked` hack**
The mobile nav uses `input[type=checkbox]:checked ~` selectors with `visibility`/`opacity` transitions. This is unsupported in Gmail web, Outlook.com, and Yahoo webmail. Users on those clients see a broken nav with no fallback. The `@-webkit-keyframes bugfix` workaround targets iOS 9 (EOL ~2017) and adds dead weight.

**`<title>` has a trailing space**
```html
<title>Neiman Marcus </title>
```
Minor; won't affect rendering but indicates template hygiene issues.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Full AMPscript variable schema exposed in HTML comments**
```
<!--REQUIRED variables: @email @base_refid @refid @user @emailid @uEm @cmdid ...-->
```
This comment ships verbatim in every recipient's inbox, documenting internal data extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`), lookup logic, and hashed field names. Strip all AMPscript scaffolding comments before send.

**`@user` scenario is ambiguous — "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW" is unresolved in visible source**
```
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->
```
The block comment around the SHA256 assignment means `@user` falls through to the other scenario (not visible in truncated source). If the wrong branch is active, `@user` could render as an empty string or raw token in links. Confirm active scenario in the template.

**`@refid` no-op concatenation**
```
@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")
```
Concatenating with an empty string is a no-op. If this was intended to append a suffix (e.g., campaign variant), it's silently broken. Verify intent; remove if vestigial.

**Silent Lookup() failures on missing records**
`@uEm` and `@cmdid` are fetched from `NM_CRM_Feed` with no default value set. If a subscriber is absent from that DE, both fields return empty string — any URL parameters built from them will silently pass blank values to the analytics pipeline.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not found — HIGH PRIORITY**
Gmail (Feb 2024) and Yahoo require `List-Unsubscribe` on all bulk sends. Its absence risks demotion to spam folder or a "Report spam" prompt replacing the unsubscribe link in Gmail UI. The QA relay did not capture this header; confirm it is set at the ESP level, not just in the HTML footer link.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found — HIGH PRIORITY**
One-click machine unsubscribe is required by Gmail/Yahoo for senders above 5k/day threshold. Without it, Gmail displays a manual unsubscribe flow, increasing spam complaints. Must be added as a header:
```
List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click
```

**`Authentication-Results` header not found**
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC alignment for `e.neimanmarcus.com` cannot be confirmed via the AgentMail relay. Sending domain authentication is a prerequisite for inbox placement — verify SPF includes the ESP's sending IPs, DKIM is signing with a valid selector, and DMARC is in at least `p=none` with reporting enabled.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

Cannot fully assess: all 26 tracking links were skipped by the HTTP probe. The following are unverified risks:

- `@refid` parameter used in tracking URLs — if the CONCAT no-op above represents a broken variant suffix, campaign attribution in analytics will be flat/incorrect.
- `@uEm` / `@cmdid` passed as URL params — silent empty-string failures (see §4) would corrupt per-recipient attribution.
- No direct-link probing means broken redirects, 404 landing pages, or missing UTM params are invisible until click volume is live.

**Recommendation:** Run a pre-send link validation pass against at least a sample of the 26 tracking URLs using a click-redirect resolver to confirm destination URLs and UTM parameter completeness.
