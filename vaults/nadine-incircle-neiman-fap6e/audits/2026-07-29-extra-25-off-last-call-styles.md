# Extra 25% off Last Call styles
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-29**
## Executive summary

- This is the most complete Last Call send Neiman Marcus has run in the recent stretch — a real hero (Extra 25% off, up to 70% off, clear "SHOP SALE" button), a genuine 9-product merchandising grid across categories, category CTA buttons, and a text-signup incentive. Compared to 7/28's "New Last Call styles added + Extra 25% off" (7/10), which had similar bones, this one leans harder into product density and gives the eye more to actually shop.
- The offer stack is easy to parse: 25% off on top of markdowns already at up to 70%, with an explicit end date (8/31/26) buried in the fine print — good that it exists, bad that it's not in the hero. The 3x3 product grid is the standout element: real designer names (Isabel Marant, Balmain, Manolo Blahnik, Theia) with clean product shots, no placeholder or broken images visible.
- Where it falls short of an 8+ is specificity and urgency at the top: no promo code visible, no countdown, and the hero copy is generic sale language rather than anything time-bound or scarcity-driven. Solid, shoppable send — a notch above the brand's editorial resets, held back from great by a flat, evergreen-feeling top section.

## What's working

- Clear, high-contrast hero: "EXTRA 25% OFF" is the largest element on the page and impossible to miss.
- Stacked offer messaging (25% extra + up to 70% off) gives a real sense of total value.
- 3x3 product grid with actual designer names and clean, non-broken product photography.
- Two-tier CTA structure — one hero "SHOP SALE" button, then eight category-specific buttons (Women's Clothing, Handbags, Shoes, Kids, etc.) for direct navigation.
- Text-club incentive (15% off) is a distinct, additive offer rather than a repeat of the email's own discount.

## What's weak

- No visible promo code or expiration date above the fold — the "Ends 8/31/26" detail is buried in dense legal copy at the very bottom.
- "SHOP SALE" is a generic CTA verb; nothing signals which category or bestseller it leads to.
- No social proof, reviews, or "selling fast" signals anywhere in the product grid.
- Product grid has no prices — for a value-driven "25% off" email, seeing $ amounts or strikethrough pricing would reinforce the offer instead of making the shopper do the math at checkout.
- Hero is entirely maroon/white/text — no lifestyle or model imagery until you scroll into the flat-lay grid, so there's nothing to signal season or who this is for.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the expiration date in the hero. "Ends 8/31" next to the 25% off callout would convert this from an evergreen-feeling sale into a time-bound one — the single biggest lever here since urgency is the weakest rubric dimension.
- 2. Add a promo code chip in the hero if one exists, or clarify "discount applied at checkout" — ambiguity here costs clicks from shoppers trained to look for codes.
- 3. Add prices (or "was $X, now $Y") under at least the hero product tiles so the value is visible without a click.
- 4. Swap "SHOP SALE" for a more specific CTA like "SHOP UP TO 70% OFF" to repeat the strongest number at the point of action.
- 5. Move the text-club 15%-off block higher or make clear it stacks with the 25% — as positioned, some readers will assume it's a redundant/competing offer and skip it.
