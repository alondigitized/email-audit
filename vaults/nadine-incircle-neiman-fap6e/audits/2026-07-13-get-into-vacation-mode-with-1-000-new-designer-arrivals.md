# Get into vacation mode with 1,000+ new designer arrivals
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-13**
## Executive summary

- This is Neiman Marcus's classic "new arrivals" broadcast — black hero block with bold white type, two gender-split CTAs ("Shop Women's / Shop Men's"), and a clean product grid showcasing twelve brands including Tom Ford, Alaia, Marni, Prada, Saint Laurent, Givenchy, Montblanc, Ferragamo, Amiri, and Bottega Veneta. It's a well-produced, brand-consistent send. The "1,000+ new designer arrivals" hook gives it real scale and specificity.
- The timing is smart. After two consecutive gift-card-mechanic emails (July 12 and 13) and an editorial bridal detour, pivoting to a fresh arrivals drop resets the register and gives loyalists a genuine reason to click. This is one of NM's cleaner editorial templates — no sale noise, no red blocks, just luxury product photography.
- The weakness is what's absent: no offer (no price, no member perk, no promo), no urgency, and a thin CTA structure. "Shop Women's New Arrivals" and "Shop Men's New Arrivals" are the only calls to action across twelve featured brands. For someone not already in a buying mindset, there's nothing here that compels a click beyond pure browsing curiosity.

## What's working

- **Hero is striking and unambiguous** — "BRAND NEW STYLES JUST LANDED" on a black ground reads instantly, no squinting required.
- **Scale of the claim lands well** — "1,000+" in the subject line creates genuine breadth; the 12-brand grid reinforces it visually.
- **Brand-name recognition is doing heavy lifting** — Tom Ford, Prada, Givenchy, Bottega Veneta, Saint Laurent in a single scroll is a strong luxury signal.
- **Gender split in the hero CTAs** — "Shop Women's / Shop Men's" immediately segments intent without requiring the subscriber to hunt.
- **Zero render bugs** — clean layout, images load, brand names label correctly under each product.

## What's weak

- **No offer, no price, no hook beyond newness** — pure editorial with nothing to tip a hesitant buyer.
- **Product grid is anonymous** — twelve unlabeled product shots with brand names only; no item names, no prices, no "new in" callouts to differentiate why *these* pieces matter.
- **CTA copy is generic** — "Shop All Women's New Arrivals" repeated twice is a catalog browse prompt, not a purchase trigger.
- **No urgency or inventory signal** — "1,000+ new arrivals" is actually abundance framing, which reduces FOMO rather than creating it.
- **Loyalty member benefit absent** — NM Circle members exist, and this send doesn't acknowledge them at all; no early access angle, no member-only preview hook.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one featured product with a visible price or "just dropped" callout** — a single hero item (e.g., the Alaia cutout dress or Tom Ford bag) with a price anchor gives the grid emotional weight and something to click vs. just browse.
- 2. **Reframe the CTA around scarcity, not catalog size** — swap "Shop All Women's New Arrivals" for "See what just arrived" or "Explore 1,000+ new styles" — keeps the scale signal while adding motion.
- 3. **Surface a NM Circle early-access angle in the hero** — "Members: shop before everyone else" costs nothing and is a strong loyalty retention beat given the cadence fatigue building across July.
- 4. **Trim the product grid to 6–8 items** — twelve brands with zero item context is visually noisy; curated fewer with item names would convert better.

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Vacation Mode / 1,000+ New Arrivals"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is built on Salesforce Marketing Cloud AMPscript with table-based layout and a CSS hamburger nav. The 43% QA pass rate is driven entirely by infrastructure/header gaps introduced at the AgentMail relay layer — the HTML itself is structurally sound but carries several dead-code artifacts and a high-risk merge token pattern.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

| Severity | Issue | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| WARN | 26 click-redirect links not probed | QA: `26 tracking link(s) skipped — Tracking/click-redirect domains` |
| WARN | Plain-text version is 87% URLs | QA: `5289/6108 chars are URLs` — significant spam-filter signal |
| INFO | Redirect domain not confirmed resolvable | All 26 links skipped; no HTTP 200/301 confirmed |

The plain-text body URL ratio (87%) is well above the 70% threshold that triggers spam filter penalties in Gmail and Outlook. Plain-text should contain human-readable content with links interspersed, not a dump of raw tracking URLs.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Dead code / commented-out markup** — three `<meta name="viewport">` variants left in `<head>` as HTML comments. This is debugging residue:
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
No rendering impact, but increases payload size and signals poor template hygiene.

**iOS 9 hamburger animation hack** — the `-webkit-animation: bugfix` keyframe loop is present in the mobile stylesheet. iOS 9 is effectively extinct (0.01% market share); this is dead weight that runs on every mobile render.

**`<title>` has a trailing space** — `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>`. Minor, but visible in browser tab previews for webmail clients.

**Hamburger nav via CSS `:checked`** — the interactive mobile nav relies on hidden `<input type="checkbox">` toggled by labels. This pattern has known failure modes in Outlook Mobile, Yahoo Mail app, and Samsung Mail. No fallback for clients that suppress `:checked` pseudo-class behavior is visible in the truncated source.

**Alt text** — cannot confirm from truncated source; recommend verifying all `<img>` tags carry descriptive `alt` attributes, particularly product images.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Critical: `@user` SHA256 hash is commented out.**

```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->
```

The comment block at the top instructs implementers to "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW" — but `@user` is commented out with `/* */` syntax inside an HTML comment, meaning it does not execute. If any downstream link or personalization block references `@user` for identity resolution or audience targeting (common in NM's behavioral retargeting links), it will render as an empty string or AMPscript error.

**`@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")` is a no-op.** Concatenating with an empty string literal is vestigial code from a prior template version where a suffix was appended. Should either be `SET @refid = @base_refid` or removed.

**`@uEm` lookup passes email address into URLs.** The `uEm` field from `NM_CRM_Feed` is typically the raw or encoded email address embedded in click URLs for identity stitching. This creates PII exposure via HTTP Referer headers when the subscriber navigates away from the landing page. Confirm the value is hashed/encoded before URL injection.

---

### 5. Compliance

| Severity | Header | Status |
|---|---|---|
| WARN | `List-Unsubscribe` | Not found — AgentMail relay not capturing |
| WARN | `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) | Not found — one-click unsubscribe unconfirmed |
| WARN | `Authentication-Results` | Not found — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status unknown |

The `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` warnings are likely a relay artifact — NM/SFMC virtually always injects these. However, **absence at the capture layer means the audit trail is incomplete.** Google and Yahoo's 2024 bulk sender requirements mandate both headers for >5k/day senders; if headers are genuinely absent at delivery, this is a deliverability compliance failure.

Authentication-Results absence prevents confirming DKIM signing by `e.neimanmarcus.com`. Given the sending domain is a dedicated ESP subdomain, DKIM should be verifiable — the gap is in relay capture, not necessarily in the email itself. Flag for relay configuration review.

Footer unsubscribe link is expected to be present (CAN-SPAM §5(a)(3)) but cannot be confirmed from truncated source.
