# Witness the big day in something elegant
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- Neiman Marcus sends a second email on July 6 — this time a zero-discount wedding editorial, just hours after the morning's "score up to 60% off" sale blast. The creative logic is recognizable: alternate hard-sell days with aspirational editorial to preserve brand equity. The execution is clean. The problem is the subscriber math: after 10+ touchpoints in five days, including an identical editorial reset on July 4 ("Fashion Your Escape") and another on July 5 ("In-demand accessories for right now"), the editorial valve has already been pulled twice this week. A third zero-discount send in two days reads as filler, not intentional cadence design.
- The email itself is well-composed. The hero shot is graceful — a woman in a white skirt against a coastal backdrop, with "Love is in the Air" in elegant serif type. Two flatlay sections follow: a women's "nuptial noir" LBD look with accessories, and a men's suit styling. Both sections have specific shop CTAs and coherent stories. But there is no price, no offer, no member benefit, and no urgency anywhere. For a brand that just told subscribers they could save 60%, asking them to shop at full price for a wedding they may or may not be attending requires more motivation than this email provides.
- The copy in the first flatlay section stumbles: "an unexpected LBD accented by pristine white looks wedding dressing an edge" is either a garbled line or an awkward word-order edit. That's a small but visible flaw in an otherwise polished send.

## What's working

- **Clean hero hierarchy** — "Love is in the Air" in serif over a lifestyle shot, followed immediately by two explicit CTAs ("Shop Wedding Guest" / "Shop Gowns") — the eye travels correctly.
- **Dual-gender editorial** — including a men's suit flatlay is smart for a wedding-dressing occasion; NM rarely does this visibly in a single send.
- **"Shop Wedding Guest" CTA copy** — more precise than most NM CTAs; it names the exact shopping occasion rather than the generic "Shop Now."
- **No render bugs** — layout is clean, images load, no overlapping text or broken blocks.

## What's weak

- **Zero offer on the heels of a 60%-off morning email** — same-day editorial sends in the middle of an active sale campaign create cognitive whiplash; the subscriber just learned they can save 60%, and now they're being shown full-price editorials.
- **Garbled copy in the first flatlay** — "pristine white looks wedding dressing an edge" reads as a truncated or badly edited sentence.
- **No urgency or specificity** — no price, no "limited styles," no wedding date anchor; there's nothing to act on beyond vague interest.
- **Same editorial beat, third time this week** — follows July 4 and July 5 editorial resets almost identically; the creative device has lost its surprise value.
- **Preheader not visible / likely weak** — subject line does all the heavy lifting with no complementary preview text reinforcing the occasion.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor the subject and hero to an actual occasion or deadline** — "Wedding in two weeks? Dress the part." or a specific "Summer wedding edits — final styles" creates the urgency that "Witness the big day" doesn't.
- 2. **Add at least one entry-level price point** — even "From $295" on the accessories flatlay gives the subscriber a number to hold; editorial-only sends on a discount-primed list almost never convert.
- 3. **Fix the garbled flatlay copy** — "an unexpected LBD accented by pristine white — edge-setting for any summer wedding" is cleaner and correct.
- 4. **Delay this send by 24 hours** — this email would land harder on July 7, after the sale noise has cleared, rather than the same afternoon as the 60%-off blast.
- 5. **Collapse to one editorial per week, not three in two days** — the brand equity argument for editorial only works when it's genuinely counterprogramming; three in two days turns it into white noise.
- **Subject Alt A:** `The chicest thing to wear to a wedding this summer`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Wedding guest dressing, done right — from $295`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `From nuptial noir to classic suiting — Neiman has the look.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Two looks. One big day. Shop wedding guest styles now.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Witness the big day in something elegant"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The template is a Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC) AMPscript deployment with a hamburger-nav responsive layout. The primary technical risks are a commented-out SHA-256 variable assignment that may silently break audience matching, missing one-click unsubscribe headers flagged by the relay, and unverifiable authentication chain (SPF/DKIM) due to the AgentMail relay not surfacing `Authentication-Results`.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**33 tracking links unprobed** — All click-redirect URLs were skipped by the HTTP prober (expected for ESP redirect domains like `e.neimanmarcus.com`). Destination URLs and HTTP response codes cannot be confirmed from this audit.

**Plain-text body is 87.5% URLs** — QA reports 3,632 of 4,150 plain-text characters are URLs. This ratio is a known spam-filter signal. The plain-text version appears to be auto-generated from link-heavy HTML without meaningful prose content. Threshold typically cited by major filters is >60–70%.

No broken or duplicate tracking parameters can be confirmed from the truncated source alone.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Commented-out viewport variants left in `<head>`** — Three alternate viewport tags remain in the HTML as comments. These are inert but indicate unresolved configuration decisions:
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
The active tag (`initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1`) suppresses user zoom, which is an accessibility regression on mobile.

**iOS 9 webkit animation hack present** — The `@-webkit-keyframes bugfix` hack for iOS 9 hamburger rendering is still in the stylesheet. iOS 9 reached end-of-life in 2019; this adds dead code weight.

**`max-device-width` deprecated** — Media queries mix `max-device-width` (removed in CSS Media Queries Level 4) with `max-width`. Functionally equivalent today but `max-device-width` is not forward-safe.

**Alt text not verifiable** — HTML is truncated before image tags; alt attributes cannot be audited.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`@user` SHA-256 assignment is commented out** — The template header documents two scenarios for setting `@user`, then the SHA-256 path is commented out:

```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */-->
```

If `@user` is referenced anywhere downstream in the template body (e.g., for audience pixel firing, personalization lookups, or UID pass-through in URLs), it will resolve to an empty string or throw an AMPscript runtime error silently. The "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW" comment suggests this was a deliberate toggle, but the active selection is not visible in the truncated source — the SHA-256 branch is confirmed inactive.

**Variable declarations are in HTML comments, not AMPscript blocks** — The `@email`, `@base_refid`, `@refid`, `@emailid`, `@uEm`, `@cmdid` declarations appear inside `<!-- -->` HTML comments at the top of the file. HTML comments are not executed by the SFMC AMPscript engine. If these are the only declarations (i.e., no `%%[ ]%%` block exists in the un-truncated body), none of these variables would be set and all personalization lookups would fail silently.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not detected** — QA warns the header was not captured by the AgentMail relay. RFC 2369 requires this header for bulk commercial email. Its absence (or relay-level stripping) increases Gmail/Yahoo complaint rates and disables the native unsubscribe button in Gmail UI.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not detected** — RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is not present or not surfaced. As of February 2024, Google and Yahoo require one-click unsubscribe for bulk senders (>5,000/day). Missing this header is a deliverability compliance gap.

**`Authentication-Results` header not captured** — SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status cannot be confirmed. The sending domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` (ESP subdomain) should have its own DKIM signature; whether alignment is achieved with the `From:` domain (`neimanmarcus.com`) is unverifiable from this capture.

No CAN-SPAM issues are inferrable from the truncated source (physical address and unsubscribe link presence cannot be confirmed but are standard for NM).

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM parameters unverifiable** — All 33 tracked links were skipped by the prober. Whether destination URLs carry `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, and `utm_content` parameters cannot be confirmed. Given the ESP redirect chain (`e.neimanmarcus.com` → final URL), UTM parameters must be appended before the redirect domain, not after.

**Landing page alignment** — Cannot audit without resolved destination URLs.
