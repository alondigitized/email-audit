# New arrivals to spend the summer in
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-29**
## Executive summary

- This is a competent, clean editorial email — strong hero image of a woman in a striped midi dress by the water, a decent "New Looks On the Horizon" headline pun, and an efficient two-segment layout (women's + men's) with a product flat-lay in between. Execution is fine. Context is the problem.
- This is NM's second email today. The first — "The Designer Sale favorites are selling fast!" — was the closing scarcity chapter of a two-week campaign and scored 8/10. Sending a cheerful new-arrivals editorial on the same day creates whiplash and dilutes both messages. Worse, the subject is the third variation on the "new arrivals + summer" theme in five days: May 25 had "Refresh your wardrobe with 1,000+ new signature styles," May 28 had "1,000+ stylish ways to embrace summer," and today is "New arrivals to spend the summer in." A subscriber with half-awake preview scanning will not distinguish this from the last two sends.
- Judgment: the email earns its existence but loses its impact entirely to cadence. NM is the clearest example in this audit of a brand systematically training its list to ignore it.

## What's working

- "New Looks On the Horizon" is a smart headline — the visual horizon + fashion-newness pun earns its place
- Hero image is genuinely strong: real outdoor setting, natural light, summer-appropriate editorial styling
- Product flat-lay mid-email (bag, fragrance, sandals, striped separates) shows coordination and gives a specific "shop the look" reason to click
- Two-segment structure covers women's + men's without feeling forced — the men's section actually earns its place with a specific editorial line ("The classic button-up and jeans combo")
- Clean render throughout — no broken images, no overlapping text, layout is solid

## What's weak

- Second send today, third "new summer arrivals" email this week — the subject has become invisible through repetition
- No offer, no urgency, no price anchoring anywhere — nothing creates a reason to click today versus next week
- "Shop New Arrivals" is the most generic CTA in e-commerce; it competes with last week's "Shop New Arrivals" button identically
- The flat-lay products have no labels, no prices, no names visible — the merchandising intent is there but the execution stops short
- No personalization signal at all — men's and women's served to everyone in one untargeted blast

## Recommendations

- 1. **One send per day, maximum.** Hold this email for May 30. Sending a cheerful new-arrivals editorial the same morning as a "selling fast" scarcity close makes both emails worse — the urgency of the first evaporates, the optimism of the second feels out of place.
- 2. **Name at least one specific product or designer in the hero.** "New arrivals" is not an editorial hook. "The Vince linen set everyone's asking about" is. This email would be sharply better with one hero product called out by name.
- 3. **Swap "Shop New Arrivals" for the flat-lay category.** "Shop the Look" is doing good work mid-email — push that logic up to the primary CTAs. "Shop Linen Sets" and "Shop Summer Shoes" would outperform anything generic.
- 4. **Segment or suppress.** Subscribers who just opened the Designer Sale closing email are in a different decision state than cold subscribers. Sending new arrivals to the same list the same day wastes both segments.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Our newest drops, styled together — shop the flat-lay`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Just landed: the pieces building every summer outfit right now`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "New arrivals to spend the summer in"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a standard Salesforce Marketing Cloud (AMPscript) send with a multi-lookup variable setup and a CSS-only hamburger nav. The primary technical risks are missing RFC-compliant unsubscribe headers and an unverifiable authentication chain through the AgentMail relay.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**34 tracking links not probed** — All click-redirect domains were skipped by the HTTP checker, so destination URL validity, redirect chain depth, and UTM parameter presence are unconfirmed.

**Plain-text body is 87% URLs** — QA measured 3,484 of 4,006 plain-text characters as URLs. This is well above the ~40–50% threshold commonly flagged by spam filters (SpamAssassin `MIME_HTML_MOSTLY`, Gmail content scoring). A plain-text part this URL-dense effectively functions as a URL dump rather than a readable fallback.

No issues found with the tracking domain itself from the available data.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Hamburger nav using CSS `:checked` hack** — The mobile nav relies on `input[type=checkbox]:checked ~ .menu` toggling `display:block`. This pattern is clipped or fully ignored in Gmail (web and app), Outlook 2016+, and Samsung Mail. Affected selectors: `.navbar-checkbox`, `.hamburger`, `.close`, `.menu`. Recipients on those clients see a broken nav affordance with no fallback.

**Commented-out viewport variants in `<head>`** — Three `<meta name="viewport">` tags are commented out alongside the active one (`width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1`). The `maximum-scale=1` value blocks user zoom, which is an accessibility failure (WCAG 1.4.4). The dead comments are noise that could cause issues if accidentally uncommented during template reuse.

**XHTML 1.0 Transitional DOCTYPE** — Functional and expected for legacy email clients, but the `xmlns` attribute on `<html>` conflicts with some modern rendering engines. No breaking issue, low priority.

**`alt` text** — Source is truncated; cannot confirm. Flag for content-side review.

**`role="presentation"` on layout tables** — Not visible in the available source excerpt; cannot confirm. Screen reader behavior on layout tables is unknown without the full source.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Scenario selector is a comment, not logic** — The block reads:
```
<!-- CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW -->
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->
```
The SHA256 assignment is double-wrapped (HTML comment + AMPscript comment), meaning it never executes. The active `@user` falls through to the `Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", ...)` path. If the intent was a conditional between hashed and looked-up identity, that branch logic is missing from the rendered template.

**`@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")` is a no-op** — Concatenating with an empty string produces an identical value. If a campaign suffix was intended (e.g., `CONCAT(@base_refid,"_NL")`), it was omitted.

**Four separate `Lookup()` calls against two DEs** — `@user`, `@emailid`, `@uEm`, and `@cmdid` each trigger individual data extension queries keyed on `EMAIL_ADDRESS`. Consolidating into a single `Lookup` per DE (or using a `LOOKUPROWS` + `ROW()` pattern) would reduce query overhead at send time.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not detected** — This header is required by Google and Yahoo for bulk senders (>5,000/day) as of February 2024. Its absence — even if caused by relay capture rather than omission — means one-click unsubscribe may not surface in Gmail's UI, increasing spam complaints.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not detected** — Required alongside `List-Unsubscribe` for the one-click POST mechanism. Same risk as above.

**`Authentication-Results` header absent** — SPF and DKIM pass/fail status is unknown through the AgentMail relay. If the relay is not preserving or re-signing these headers, downstream inbox providers cannot verify sender identity. The sending domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` should have a published SPF record and DKIM key; this cannot be confirmed from available data.

**CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe link** — Cannot verify from truncated source. Assumed present given sender sophistication; confirm in full source.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM parameters unverifiable** — All 34 links route through click-redirect domains that were not probed. Campaign attribution, UTM source/medium/campaign tagging, and final landing page alignment cannot be confirmed without resolving at least a sample of redirect chains manually.

**`@refid` is the primary tracking token** — The campaign ID flows through `@base_refid` → `@refid` into link parameters (pattern typical of NM templates). If the `CONCAT` no-op noted above was meant to append a variant tag, attribution data for this specific send may be miscategorized in analytics.
