# Homepage snapshot · www.neimanmarcus.com · 2026-05-15
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-15**
## Executive summary

- I opened the site expecting my usual Neiman Marcus welcome — maybe the new resort arrivals or a preview of the InCircle summer event — and instead I'm looking at a security wall that's calling me a bot. There's no hero, no product, no loyalty hook — just a white modal with a Neiman Marcus logo sitting on top of what looks like a blurred dark background. This is not a homepage. This is a door that slammed in my face.

## What's working

- The Neiman Marcus wordmark logo renders crisply at the top of the modal — at least I know whose wall I hit.
- The "Submit feedback" link is present and underlined, so there's technically an escape hatch if I want to report the problem.

## What's weak

- **The entire homepage is blocked.** I see "Access is temporarily restricted" — nothing behind it is accessible or viewable.
- **The error message accuses me.** Language like "unusual activity from your device," "rapid taps or clicks," and "bot activity" feels accusatory toward a loyal InCircle customer on her phone. That tone is completely off-brand for a luxury retailer.
- **No graceful fallback.** A brand at this price point should at minimum show me a styled error page with a phone number or stylist contact — not a plain white card that looks like a generic firewall notice.
- **No path forward for me.** The four bullet points listing causes are technical and alienating. "IP 75.140.9.0" and "developer tools" mean nothing to me and make me feel like I'm being interrogated.
- **The background is wasted.** I can faintly see what looks like a lifestyle or editorial image behind the modal — which is cruelly close to what I actually came for.

## Recommendations

- **Replace the generic bot-wall with a branded soft-block page.** A luxury house should never flash something that looks like a firewall error. Show the logo, a warm apology message ("Something went wrong on our end"), and a "Call your stylist" or "Visit a store" CTA.
- **Add a direct InCircle member help line to any error state.** If I'm a recognized member (which I am), the error page should surface my account tier and a direct concierge number — not a generic "Submit feedback" link.
- **Audit mobile bot-detection thresholds.** Whatever triggered this for a 51-year-old woman browsing on her phone in normal use is set way too aggressively. I'm going to lose trust and head to Saks.
- **Test this flow with real InCircle members.** A/B test or session-replay review would immediately surface how often high-LTV customers are hitting this wall and bouncing permanently.
