# Homepage snapshot · www.neimanmarcus.com · 2026-07-09
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- I opened the Neiman Marcus app on my phone and instead of the homepage I got a wall of text telling me my device is suspicious. The Neiman Marcus wordmark is there at the top, elegant as always, but everything below it is a security block — "Access is temporarily restricted" — with a bullet list of reasons I might be flagged as a bot. I can see two shoes peeking up from the bottom of the screen, which is the only hint this is a luxury retailer. This is not a homepage experience. This is a door slammed in my face.

## What's working

- The **Neiman Marcus wordmark** renders correctly and cleanly at the top — the brand identity at least survived the block.
- The **"Submit feedback" link** is present and underlined — at least there is some escape hatch, even if I'd never use it.

## What's weak

- **The entire homepage is inaccessible.** I am an InCircle member who has spent real money at this store, and the site is treating me like a scraper bot. That is an insulting first impression.
- **The error copy is cold and clinical.** "We detected unusual activity from your device or network" — that reads like a parking ticket, not a luxury brand voice. There is zero warmth, no apology, no invitation to try again.
- **The error ID** (`ID: 3c18c677-46a6-361e-d33f-6100990a5566`) is shown to the customer with no context. I have no idea what to do with that string.
- **The two floating shoes at the bottom** of the screen are a ghost of a homepage underneath — they make the block feel even more broken because I can tell something was supposed to render and didn't.
- **No phone number, no stylist contact, no alternative path.** If I cannot get into the website, there is nowhere for me to go. A brand at this tier should route blocked customers to a concierge option instantly.
- **JavaScript disabled listed as a possible cause** — I did nothing to disable JavaScript. This is the site's problem, not mine, and framing it as my fault is off-putting.

## Recommendations

- **Add a graceful fallback page with a luxury brand voice.** If the bot check triggers, the message should read something like "We're having trouble loading your experience — please try again or call your NM stylist at [number]." Not a security bulletin.
- **Surface the InCircle member phone line or chat immediately on this error state.** If you cannot serve a high-value customer through the web, hand them off to a human. Do not leave them stranded with an error ID.
- **Remove the raw error ID from customer-facing view** or at minimum explain what to do with it ("Use this reference number if you call us").
- **Investigate why a legitimate mobile browser is triggering the bot block.** If this is happening to real InCircle members on their phones, the brand is losing significant in-season revenue.
