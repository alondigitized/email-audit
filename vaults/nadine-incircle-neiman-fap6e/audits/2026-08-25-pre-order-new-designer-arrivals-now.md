# Pre-order new designer arrivals now!
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-25**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, image-forward "pre-order" send: a striking "BE FIRST" hero of a windswept blonde model at a wrought-iron gate, followed by a six-item grid of new-season pieces with brand names as the only label. It's visually the most cinematic Neiman Marcus email in this brand's recent run — better art direction than the flat black-panel arrivals send from 8/24 — but it asks for the biggest commitment (pre-order, no price, no delivery date) with the least information to justify it.
- The core problem is the same one this brand keeps having: gorgeous editorial photography, zero product substance. No prices, no estimated ship/arrival dates, no explanation of what "pre-order" actually means in terms of payment or wait time. A shopper who doesn't already trust Neiman Marcus completely has no reason to commit to buying something sight-unseen-in-detail with an unknown timeline.
- Judgement: a good-looking, low-information send that will pull opens on the strength of the visual but underperform on clicks because it doesn't answer the first question anyone has about pre-ordering — when will I get it and what does it cost.

## What's working

- The hero image is genuinely arresting — strong model styling, moody urban-gate setting, "BE FIRST" wordmark integrates well with the photo.
- "Pre-Order Now" CTA is a clear, clickable link directly on the hero.
- The subhead ("Get ahead of the season with early access to the latest collections") gives a clean, single-sentence value prop for why pre-order matters.
- Six-item grid spans multiple categories (dress, jacket, blouse, skirt, bag, boots) with recognizable designer names (Oscar de la Renta, Carolina Herrera, Jimmy Choo, Christian Louboutin), signaling breadth without overwhelming.
- No render bugs — images, type, and layout are all clean and properly aligned.

## What's weak

- No prices anywhere, on the hero or any of the six products — for a pre-order ask, this is the single biggest omission.
- No delivery/arrival timing given — "pre-order" implies a wait, and the email never says how long.
- Product tiles have only a brand name, no item description (dress? boots? — you can infer from the photo but nothing is stated) and no direct "shop this item" link cue.
- No urgency or scarcity signal (limited quantities, pre-order window closing) to justify acting now versus waiting for the item to be in stock.
- Repeats a pattern from 8/24's "1,000+ designer arrivals" send — full-price/no-info editorial catalog — making this the second such send in two days with no offer mechanic at all.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a price (or "from $X") directly under each product name in the grid — this is the highest-leverage fix, since pre-order without price is a non-starter for most shoppers.
- 2. State expected arrival/ship windows on the hero ("Ships in October" or similar) so "pre-order" has a concrete meaning instead of just sounding exclusive.
- 3. Make each product tile individually clickable to its own product page rather than relying on a single hero CTA — right now the grid reads as inspiration, not shoppable.
- 4. Add one line of urgency/scarcity ("Limited quantities — reserve yours before they're gone") to justify acting today instead of waiting for general release.
- 5. Vary the cadence — this is the second no-discount editorial arrivals email in two days (8/24, 8/25); alternate with a value mechanic occasionally so the "Last Call: 25% off" pattern from 8/22 (this brand's best-scoring send) isn't diluted by too many full-price sends in a row.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Early access ends soon — lock in sizes before they sell out`
