# Limited time: Earn Triple InCircle Points on Saint Laurent
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-21**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, confident loyalty-points send. A black hero says "TRIPLE POINTS on Saint Laurent" in a high-contrast serif, with "$500+ regular-price purchase" under it and a white "SHOP & EARN" button. The hierarchy works: the eye goes from the brand to the offer to the button without effort. Below the hero is a 3×3 grid of Saint Laurent product shots on grey tiles. It reads as a brand-led, high-end send.
- The weakness is the offer itself. Triple points is an abstract reward with a $500 floor, and the email never says what the points are worth. It also has the fewest hooks of any recent Neiman Marcus send. The Oct 21 "25% off one item" email had a hard price cut. This one asks the reader to spend $500 on Saint Laurent for points they can't value. The legal copy ("Exclusions Apply," then a dense paragraph) undercuts the clean look. Every grid label is just "Saint Laurent," so the grid shows no product names or prices.
- Overall it is a well-executed layout wrapped around a thin offer. It is a good send for a Saint Laurent or InCircle loyalist and a weak one for everyone else. It is also the third Neiman Marcus loyalty or incentive send of the week, after the repeated gift-card-with-purchase emails (Oct 13, 15, 18, 20).

## What's working

- The hero hierarchy is strong: "TRIPLE POINTS" is the largest element, and the white button on black stands out.
- The brand-led framing is credible. Saint Laurent is a desirable, recognizable name, and the products fit it.
- The product grid is tidy and consistent. It mixes bags, shoes, sunglasses and apparel, so it appeals to several tastes.
- The six category buttons below the grid give a clear next step for people who don't want Saint Laurent.
- The deadline is stated in the fine print (10/25/26, 10:00 PM CT), and "Limited Time" in the hero signals urgency.

## What's weak

- The grid labels all read "Saint Laurent." There are no product names, no prices, and no sign of what clears the $500 threshold.
- The points have no stated value. Triple points is meaningless unless the reader knows what a point is worth, and the email never says.
- The deadline is not in the hero. It appears only in the dense fine print, so the urgency is weaker than it should be.
- "Exclusions Apply" sits right under the CTA, and the long legal paragraph is hard to read. Together they make the offer feel like it has strings attached.
- The category buttons point away from Saint Laurent, including "Shop Beauty" and "Shop Men's." That dilutes the single-brand offer.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a date in the hero, for example "Through 10/25," and say what the points are worth, for example "That's up to X% back in rewards." This is the biggest lift, because it turns an abstract perk into a concrete, time-bound one.
- 2. Label the grid with real product names and prices, such as "Loulou Shoulder Bag, $2,990," so shoppers can see what clears $500. Mark the $500+ items.
- 3. Replace the six category buttons with one Saint Laurent-specific CTA, or at least cut them to Handbags, Shoes and Clothing.
- 4. Move the key exclusions into a short line near the hero, and shorten the legal block.
- 5. Lead with the single best-selling Saint Laurent item in the hero, so there is a clear product to click.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Triple InCircle points on Saint Laurent, through 10/25`
- **Subject Alt B:** `3x points on Saint Laurent bags, shoes & more`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Spend $500+ on regular-price Saint Laurent and earn triple points. Ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New bags, heels and sunglasses, plus triple points through 10/25.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible markup is a standard SFMC-built, table-based email. Its main problems are leftover internal template comments in the shipped HTML, a zoom-blocking viewport tag, and no `lang` attribute. Most of the QA warnings (missing headers, skipped tracking links) come from the AgentMail relay and can't be confirmed as defects. The HTML is truncated, so footer, link and `<img>` markup could not be inspected.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **31 tracking links were not probed.** QA skipped them as click-redirect domains, so destination status, redirect chains and final landing URLs are unverified.
- **Plain-text part is mostly URLs.** 5862 of 8151 characters (~72%) are URLs, which is over the 70% threshold. Long tracking URLs in the text alternative can hurt spam scoring and make that part hard to read.
- The visible source gives no evidence of broken, empty or `#` hrefs. The links sit past the truncation point.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom is blocked.** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` disables pinch-zoom on mobile, which fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
- **No document language.** The tag is `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` with no `lang` or `xml:lang`, so screen readers can't pick a pronunciation profile.
- **Hamburger nav depends on the checkbox hack.** The CSS uses `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu` and `.mobileCont:checked~table`. Gmail and Outlook generally don't support `:checked`, so the nav may be inoperable there. Whether a fallback exists is not visible in the truncated source.
- **Exact-match class selectors are fragile.** `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=mobile]` and similar only match when `class` is exactly that value. Any element with a second class silently loses its mobile rule.
- **Minor markup issues:**
  - The XHTML doctype uses an `https://` DTD URL (`https://www.w3.org/TR/xhtml1/DTD/xhtml1-transitional.dtd`), while the canonical identifier is `http://`.
  - `<meta>` tags are not self-closed, which is inconsistent with XHTML.
  - The `<title>` has a trailing space and is generic.
- **Dead code:** several commented-out viewport meta tags are left in `<head>`.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Template notes are exposed.** The first HTML comment is a developer note shipped in the delivered source. It lists SFMC variables (`@email`, `@base_refid`, `@refid`, `@emailid`, `@uEm`, `@cmdid`), internal Data Extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`) and Lookup field names. It also contains the unresolved instruction "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW --> `<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->`". This is information disclosure and a sign the template wasn't cleaned before send.
- No unresolved `%%…%%` or `{{…}}` tokens appear in the visible source, and QA flagged none.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` header not found.** QA notes it may not be captured by the relay.
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not found.** One-click unsubscribe can't be confirmed. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require both headers for high-volume senders.
- **`Authentication-Results` not found.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment for `e.neimanmarcus.com` are unknown.
- **Unconfirmed.** The visible source doesn't show the footer, so the unsubscribe link and physical postal address can't be confirmed. The three header warnings could be relay artifacts, not sender defects.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **UTM and tracking parameters couldn't be verified.** The tracking links weren't probed and no hrefs are visible in the truncated source.
- **Source hints at `refid`-based attribution.** The template comment describes `@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")` and CRM identifiers (`uEm`, `cmdid`), so tracking appears to run through refid rather than standard UTMs. Confirm that the landing pages receive and preserve these parameters.
- **Landing-page alignment is unverified** for the same reason.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip the developer comment block and the commented-out viewport and scenario code from the production template.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag.
3. Add `lang="en"` (and `xml:lang`) to `<html>`.
4. Verify directly in the raw message, not through the relay, that `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` are present and that SPF, DKIM and DMARC pass.
5. Shorten the plain-text part. Use shorter or branded tracking URLs, and drop duplicate links so URLs fall well under 70% of the text.
6. Test the hamburger nav in Gmail and Outlook, and make sure the non-`:checked` fallback leaves the nav links reachable.
7. Re-run the audit with the full HTML and a link probe that follows the tracking redirects. That would let you validate the footer unsubscribe and address, destination status codes and parameter carry-through.
