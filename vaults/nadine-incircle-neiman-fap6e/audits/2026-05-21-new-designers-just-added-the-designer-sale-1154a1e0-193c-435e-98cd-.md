# New designers just added: The Designer Sale
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- This is the fourth Designer Sale send from Neiman Marcus in five days, but it earns its send with a genuinely fresh hook: "new designers just added." After two days of editorial content (the Prada SS26 spotlight on 5/20 and Happy Heels on 5/20), the gap makes this feel less fatiguing than the 5/17–5/19 daily barrage. The hero is blunt and effective — deep red, "UP TO 50% OFF," single white button — and the nine-designer product grid gives it immediate shoppability.
- The main tension is that the subject promises "new designers just added" but the email body never delivers on that specific claim. There's no callout, no badge, no "NEW" label on any product tile to explain which names are fresh. If Jacquemus or ALAIA weren't in the 5/19 send, a subscriber has no way to know. The subject hook lands as a teaser; the body fails to pay it off.
- The secondary "CLAIM YOUR 15% OFF" text-list signup block is smart in concept — it layers a retention mechanism under a sale email — but sandwiched between the product grid and the footer, it creates mild offer confusion. A subscriber may wonder whether they should shop at 50% off now or wait for the 15% text code. These two incentives should be sequenced, not stacked.

## What's working

- **Hero is ruthlessly clear.** Red field, white type, "UP TO 50% OFF" above the fold, one SHOP SALE button — zero ambiguity about what this email is selling.
- **Nine specific brand names surface as product tiles.** Jacquemus, ALAIA, Burberry, Givenchy, Courreges, Cult Gaia — these names do the persuasion work faster than any headline copy could.
- **Category CTAs are well-chosen.** Six deep-link buttons (SHOP WOMEN'S CLOTHING, SHOP WOMEN'S SHOES, SHOP HANDBAGS, SHOP DRESSES, SHOP MEN'S, SHOP MEN'S SHOES) reduce click friction by letting the subscriber route directly to their category without landing on a broad sale page.
- **Two-day reset made the cadence feel fresh.** The non-sale editorial sends on 5/20 acted as a palate cleanser; this email doesn't feel like the fifth consecutive fire-sale.

## What's weak

- **Subject promise undelivered in body.** "New designers just added" is called out in the subject but there's no "NEW" badge, no callout header, and no explicit list of what's new vs. what was already in the 5/19 send (which featured Givenchy, visible again here).
- **"LIMITED TIME" without a date is hollow.** A specific end date (even "Ends Sunday") converts vague scarcity into real urgency. "LIMITED TIME" reads as boilerplate.
- **Offer confusion between 50% off and 15% off.** The text-list signup section offers 15% off mid-email, right after a 50% off sale — the two mechanisms compete rather than complement.
- **No InCircle member pricing or recognition.** Given the 5/19 InCircle recruitment email, showing existing-member pricing or a member-exclusive offer would have been more relevant for engaged subscribers.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add "NEW" product tile badges.** Pick two or three genuinely new-to-sale designers and badge their tiles. The subject hook earns its open only if the body delivers the reveal.
- 2. **Replace "LIMITED TIME" with a real deadline.** "Ends Sunday, May 25" takes three words and creates a concrete reason to act today. "LIMITED TIME" is ignored by anyone who has opened a promotional email before.
- 3. **Move the 15% text signup to the post-click thank-you page or a separate dedicated send.** It dilutes the primary sale conversion and introduces offer confusion at the moment of decision.
- 4. **Add an InCircle member callout.** Even a one-line "InCircle members: early access to new additions" acknowledgment signals that NM knows who their best customers are.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Jacquemus & ALAIA just joined the Designer Sale — up to 50% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The Designer Sale just expanded — ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New names, same savings. See which designers just dropped into the sale.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Up to 50% off Burberry, Givenchy, and freshly added designers.`

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus: "New designers just added: The Designer Sale"

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a standard Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC) deployment with table-based layout and a hamburger-nav pattern for mobile. No hard failures detected in the HTML; four QA warnings relate to relay-layer header visibility and plain-text content quality.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 tracking links skipped by HTTP probe** (`[WARN]`).
All click-through URLs appear to route through a click-redirect domain (likely `click.e.neimanmarcus.com` or similar). The QA probe correctly skipped these — no broken-link signal — but destination URLs and UTM parameters cannot be validated without a live SFMC click-decode pass.

**Plain-text body is 95% URLs** (`[WARN]`): 5,315 of 6,089 characters are URLs.
This is a common symptom of an auto-generated plain-text part that strips HTML without rewriting link anchor text. Spam filters (notably SpamAssassin) penalize high URL-to-text ratios in the plain-text part.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Multiple commented-out `<meta name="viewport">` tags** (lines 7–9 of `<head>`):
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
Dead code — no rendering risk, but indicates template-drift over multiple production cycles. The active viewport tag (`initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1`) suppresses user zoom, which is an accessibility concern (WCAG 1.4.4).

**iOS hamburger nav uses CSS `:checked` trick** — known to break in Gmail App (Android) and some Outlook webmail clients, which strip `<style>` blocks or ignore pseudo-class selectors. The fallback (desktop table layout) renders, but mobile nav becomes non-functional in those clients.

**`@-webkit-keyframes bugfix` animation on `body`** — this is a legacy iOS 9 hack (2015-era). No modern iOS version requires it. Minor: adds unnecessary parse overhead.

**`<title>Neiman Marcus </title>`** — trailing whitespace; cosmetic only.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Unresolved variable comment block visible in source:**
```html
<!--REQUIRED variables: @email @base_refid @refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"") @user Lookup(...) @emailid ... @uEm ... @cmdid ...-->
```
This comment is rendered in the delivered HTML. It exposes internal SFMC data extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`) and field mappings (`uEm`, `cmdid`, `EMAIL_ID`) to any recipient who views source. This is an **information-disclosure issue** — not a merge-failure, but a data architecture leak.

**SHA-256 hashed email scenario commented out:**
```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */-->
```
If the hashed-email path was intended for a clean-room or CDP match use case, it is currently disabled. Verify this is intentional for this send.

No visible unrendered `%%variable%%` or `{{merge_tag}}` tokens in the truncated source — merge execution appears successful.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not captured** (`[WARN]`):
The AgentMail relay did not surface this header. SFMC typically injects `List-Unsubscribe` at send time; the absence in QA capture likely reflects relay stripping rather than a true omission. **Must be verified against raw SMTP headers** received by a seed address — Gmail and Apple Mail use this header to power the native unsubscribe button, and its absence degrades deliverability reputation.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058 one-click) not found** (`[WARN]`):
Google and Yahoo's 2024 bulk-sender requirements mandate one-click unsubscribe (`List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`) for senders above 5,000/day. Neiman Marcus almost certainly exceeds that threshold. If this header is genuinely absent (not just relay-stripped), this is a **compliance gap** against current Gmail/Yahoo sender policies.

**Authentication-Results header not captured** (`[WARN]`):
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status cannot be confirmed from this capture. `e.neimanmarcus.com` is the sending domain — DKIM alignment requires the `d=` tag to match or be covered by DMARC policy on `neimanmarcus.com`. Validate via MXToolbox or a seed-inbox header inspection.

**CAN-SPAM physical address**: Not visible in truncated source — assumed present in footer (standard for NM). Cannot confirm without full HTML.
