# Homepage snapshot · www.neimanmarcus.com · 2026-05-13
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-13**
## Executive summary

- I opened Neiman Marcus on my phone and got a wall of text telling me my access is "temporarily restricted." This is not a homepage — it is a bot-detection block screen. There is no merchandise, no editorial, no offers, nothing that says "welcome back, Nadine." For a brand that talks about relationship retailing, this is a profoundly bad first impression.

## What's working

- The Neiman Marcus wordmark logo renders correctly at the top, so at least I know I'm in the right place — briefly.
- The "Submit feedback" link is present, which is the only actionable element on the page.

## What's weak

- The entire page is an access-denial wall — there is no merchandise, no imagery, no editorial, nothing a shopper can act on.
- The error copy lists "automated (bot) activity on your network" with my IP address printed in plain text (47.41.15.19), which feels accusatory and borderline alarming to a non-technical user.
- "JavaScript disabled or not working" is listed as a possible cause — I am on my phone, this is not a reason I would understand or accept.
- Zero InCircle or loyalty acknowledgment — if the system knows I'm a member, it gave me no preferential treatment whatsoever.
- The duplicate Neiman Marcus logo at the very bottom of the screen peeks through the block page, which looks broken and disjointed.
- There is no estimated resolution, no "try again" button, no path forward other than "submit feedback."

## Recommendations

- **Add a "Try Again" button** above the fold on this block screen — a single clear tap target so someone like me doesn't just close the tab entirely.
- **Remove the raw IP address** from the consumer-facing error message — printing "47.41.15.19" means nothing to me and reads as aggressive/surveillance-y.
- **If the visitor is a recognized InCircle member (cookie/token match), bypass the block or fast-track to a CAPTCHA** rather than a dead stop — loyalty members should never see this cold wall.
- **Soften the copy register** — "We detected unusual activity" sounds like a fraud alert, not a luxury brand. Something like "We need a moment to verify your connection — tap below to continue" would be far less alarming.
