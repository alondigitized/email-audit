# Hurry for up to 40% off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-04**
## Executive summary

- This is Neiman Marcus's seventh sale touchpoint in four days and the fatigue is starting to show. The email is clean and technically well-executed — typographic hero, recognizable designer grid, clear SHOP SALE CTA — but the big problem is strategic malpractice: it's the Fourth of July, one of the most heavily-mailed sale days in US retail, and there is zero holiday reference anywhere in this email. Competitors are running red-white-blue urgency; NM is mailing a recycled "Sale" typographic treatment that has been in rotation since July 1.
- The offer is also confusing in context. Two days ago NM sent "Up to 60% off. Need we say more?" — that email scored 9/10. Today it's "up to an EXTRA 40% off," which technically could stack higher, but the headline discount number has gone from 60 to 40 and a subscriber who got both reads this as a weaker deal, not a stronger one.
- The execution is fine; the strategy is a July 4th miss. This email needed to either fly a flag and own the holiday or stay quiet and let the inbox rest.

## What's working

- Designer product grid (Zimmermann, Dries Van Noten, Manolo Blahnik, Giorgio Armani, Ferragamo, Carolina Herrera) gives browseable variety and anchors aspirational positioning.
- Hero layout is uncluttered — offer quantity, CTA button, and brand logo are all above the fold with no competing distractions.
- The multi-category CTAs in the footer (Women's, Shoes, Handbags, Men's) efficiently serve different buyer intents without cluttering the hero.
- "Exclusions Apply" is disclosed visibly in the hero, which at least doesn't hide the fine print.

## What's weak

- Zero Fourth of July reference on the most competitive sale day of the summer — a glaring contextual miss.
- "Hurry" urgency without any specific end date or countdown is a hollow trigger; the prior "Surprise: Up to an extra 40% off starts now" on July 1 landed more credibly.
- Headline offer has numerically stepped down from the "Up to 60% off" email sent two days ago — creates subscriber confusion about whether the event is winding down or escalating.
- The typographic "S-ale" hero has now appeared across multiple sends in this sequence; creative recycling at this cadence signals a template being re-spun rather than a live campaign.
- No 4th of July styling, color palette nod, or urgency framing that would differentiate this from any other summer-sale send.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put the holiday to work.** Lead with a "4th of July" headline — "The only fireworks that matter: extra 40% off, today only." Even a red-accent button or a single "Happy 4th" copy line would make this email feel intentional rather than scheduled.
- 2. **Anchor to a hard deadline.** "Hurry" without a clock is wasted urgency. "Extra 40% off — ends midnight tonight" would push undecided buyers. If the sale truly has no deadline, say "while sale styles last."
- 3. **Clarify the stacking story.** The "extra 40% off" stacks on existing markdowns — that math is actually exciting (60% base + extra 40% = up to 76% off). Put that math in the hero. "Extra 40% off sale styles = up to 76% off" is far more compelling than the current treatment.
- 4. **Rest the typographic hero.** This is the fourth-plus use of the "S-ale" letterform this week. Swap in a product hero shot — one Zimmermann look at 60%+ off — and lead with the specific rather than the category.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Extra 40% off — July 4 ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 76% off when sale stacks today`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Extra 40% off already-reduced designer styles — Zimmermann, Armani, and more.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stack extra 40% on sale prices. Shop the Fourth while it lasts.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Hurry for up to 40% off"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a Salesforce Marketing Cloud AMPscript-driven send with several unresolved template authoring issues: a scenario-selection block that appears never to have been resolved, a no-op `CONCAT` producing a malformed `@refid`, and compliance headers absent from the relay capture. 43% QA pass rate driven entirely by warnings, not hard failures.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 tracking links not probed.** All click-redirect domains were skipped by the HTTP checker. No broken-link confirmation is possible; this is a monitoring gap, not a confirmed defect.

**Plain-text body is 87% URLs by character count** (5,268 / 6,070 chars). This is a deliverability risk: spam filters (SpamAssassin, Google Postmaster) penalize plain-text parts that are predominantly raw URLs with no prose. The plain-text version appears to be auto-generated from the HTML without prose fallback.

**`@refid` is a no-op CONCAT:**
```
@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")
```
Concatenating with an empty string produces exactly `@base_refid` — no suffix is appended. If this was intended to append a campaign segment code or variant suffix, the value is missing. Any link that uses `@refid` for click attribution will carry an incomplete tracking token.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Three commented-out viewport `<meta>` tags left in `<head>`:**
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
This is template hygiene debt — dead markup shipped to recipients. No functional impact, but it signals the template was copied from an older version without cleanup.

**CSS-only hamburger navigation** uses the `<input type="checkbox">` / `:checked` trick. Support is limited to Apple Mail and a subset of webmail clients. Outlook Desktop (all versions), older Samsung Mail, and Yahoo Mail will not render the interactive state — subscribers on those clients receive a broken nav with no fallback visible state.

**`@-webkit-keyframes bugfix` targets iOS 9** (comment: "Hamburguer fix IOS9"). iOS 9 reached end-of-life in 2017. This is dead CSS shipped on every send.

**`contentInteractive` CSS interaction pattern** (checkbox-driven tab switching) has near-zero client support outside Apple Mail. No JavaScript fallback exists. Recipients in Gmail, Outlook, and Yahoo see the non-interactive state only — confirm the default (`nocontentInteractive`) renders a complete experience without interaction.

**No `alt` text is visible on any `<img>` tag in the truncated source.** If images are blocked (common in corporate Outlook environments), subscribers see blank cells with no context. This is also a WCAG 2.1 Level A failure.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Scenario selector left unresolved.** The template header contains:
```
CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */-->
```
The `/*...*/` block is wrapped in an HTML comment (`<!-- -->`), not AMPscript delimiters (`%%[ ]%%`). AMPscript inside an HTML comment **does not execute** — it is treated as static HTML comment text. If `@user` is only defined in this block, it resolves to an empty string at send time. Any link parameter or personalization token that depends on `@user` (e.g., hashed email for ad matching, CDP stitching) silently fails.

**`@emailid` has a fragile fallback chain:**
```
Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)
otherwise Lookup to Subscriber_New: Lookup(...)
```
The "otherwise" is expressed in a comment, not in AMPscript `IIF`/`ISNULL` logic. If the first lookup misses (subscriber not in `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`), `@emailid` is empty — no guard is present in the visible source.

**`@uEm` and `@cmdid`** are pulled from `NM_CRM_Feed`. If a subscriber has no CRM record, both values are empty. Whether downstream links/tokens handle nulls gracefully is not verifiable from the truncated source.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent** from relay capture. CAN-SPAM requires a functional unsubscribe mechanism; while a footer link satisfies CAN-SPAM minimally, Gmail and Apple Mail use the `List-Unsubscribe` header to surface a native unsubscribe UI. Its absence degrades deliverability scoring with both providers.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) absent.** Gmail has required one-click unsubscribe (`List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`) for bulk senders since February 2024. Absence can trigger Gmail inbox suppression for high-volume sends.

**`Authentication-Results` not captured by AgentMail relay.** SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status is unknown. Neiman Marcus sends from `e.neimanmarcus.com` (a dedicated ESP subdomain), which should have DKIM configured — but this cannot be confirmed from the relay-captured headers. If DKIM is misaligned, Gmail will not show the "via" sender attribution, reducing trust signals.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**`@refid` is incomplete** (see §2). Any UTM or click-attribution parameter built from `@refid` will be truncated at `@base_refid` with no variant/segment suffix. Campaign reporting in analytics will aggregate all variants under a single token.

**26 tracking links unverified.** Landing page UTM parameter consistency (source, medium, campaign, content) cannot be confirmed. Given the `@refid` issue, at least the `utm_content` or equivalent parameter is likely malformed across all 26 links.
