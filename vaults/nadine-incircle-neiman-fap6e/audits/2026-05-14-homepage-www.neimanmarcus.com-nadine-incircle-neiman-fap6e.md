# Homepage snapshot · www.neimanmarcus.com · 2026-05-14
**Score:** 1/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-14**
## Executive summary

- I opened Neiman Marcus on my phone and immediately hit a wall — not a homepage, not a hero shot, not a sale banner. A robot-detection block page. The logo is there, the brand mark is unmistakable, but the only thing the site is communicating right now is that it thinks I'm a bot. For a store where I've spent more money than I care to admit, this is not the welcome I was expecting.

## What's working

- The Neiman Marcus wordmark renders cleanly at the top and bottom — brand identity is intact even in failure state.
- The error message is written in plain English, which is more legible than a cryptic HTTP status code.

## What's weak

- The entire homepage is replaced by a security-block screen — no InCircle member should ever land here; this is a catastrophic first impression for a loyalty customer.
- The copy "Automated (bot) activity on your network (IP 47.41.15.19)" is clinical and accusatory — it reads like I'm being interrogated, not served.
- "Submit feedback" is the only action available; there is no "Try again," no customer-service number, no way back into the site.
- The faint outdoor/fashion imagery bleeding in at the bottom teases the experience I should be having but cannot reach.

## Recommendations

- **Add a graceful fallback:** if the bot-detection layer fires on a returning customer's device, surface a CAPTCHA or a one-tap "I'm not a robot" path — not a dead end with a case ID.
- **Humanize the error copy:** replace the IP-address accusation with something like "We want to make sure it's you — tap below to continue shopping," and sign it with the NM voice.
- **Surface a phone number:** an InCircle member who hits this page on a Tuesday afternoon will close the tab and call her stylist — put that number here so she stays in the brand ecosystem.
- **Investigate the trigger:** a loyal customer visiting on a mobile browser should never be flagged; tighten the bot-detection rules so InCircle members aren't caught in the net.
