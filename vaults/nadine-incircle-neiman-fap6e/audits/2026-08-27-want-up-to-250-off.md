# Want up to $250 off?
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-27**
## Executive summary

- This is Neiman Marcus's clearest, best-executed send in this run of recent audits. Unlike the gift-card mechanic run into the ground earlier this week (8/18, 8/21, 8/25) or the price-less designer-arrival plays (Prada 8/26, Brunello Cucinelli 8/24), this one leads with a real tiered dollar-off offer — spend $400/save $100, spend $1,000/save $250 — stated in plain numbers with a visible promo code and a clear "Ends 9/3/26" deadline in the fine print.
- The hero is clean: mustard-gold block, "LIMITED TIME / UP TO $250 OFF," one unambiguous black SHOP NOW button, then a nine-item product grid spanning dresses, denim, handbags, boots, and sandals across a real range of price points and categories. Brand names only (Mac Duggal, FRAME, Jimmy Choo, Amiri, rag & bone) is consistent with this brand's low-information merchandising style, but at least the offer itself carries the email instead of relying on brand-name recognition alone.
- Where it falls short: this is a mass, ungendered, no-persona blast — nothing here signals "men's" content despite a MEN nav tab, and the product grid is overwhelmingly womenswear (7 of 9 items). No loyalty-tier callout, no urgency beyond "limited time," and the tiered structure ($400 minimum spend) works against anyone browsing casually. Solid, competent execution; unremarkable targeting.

## What's working

- Tiered dollar-off offer ($100 off $400 / $250 off $1,000) is concrete, easy to parse, and paired with a visible promo code (SHOPFALL).
- Single unambiguous black CTA button ("SHOP NOW") sits directly under the offer with no competing calls to action in the hero.
- Nine-item product grid gives real browsing substance immediately below the fold, spanning dresses, denim, shoes, and bags.
- Clean hierarchy: offer block → styles-you-love grid → category shortcut buttons → app/stylist cross-sell → footer legal. No render bugs, no broken images, no overlapping text.
- Deadline and exclusions are stated plainly in the footer ("Ends 9/3/26 at 10:00 PM CT") rather than buried or omitted.

## What's weak

- Grid skews heavily womenswear (7 of 9 items) despite a MEN nav link — no menswear hero product, weak signal for a male-persona subscriber.
- $400 minimum spend to unlock even the first discount tier is a real barrier — no low-commitment entry offer for a casual browser.
- No loyalty/InCircle member benefit mentioned, despite Neiman Marcus running loyalty-point promos constantly this month (Brunello Cucinelli 8/24, gift-card sends 8/18-8/25).
- Nine products with brand name as the only label (no price, no product name) forces a click to learn anything — zero product detail visible in-email.
- Generic filler line ("Treat yourself and save on styles you love") adds no information between the offer and the grid.

## Recommendations

- 1. Fix the gender skew — swap 2-3 womenswear grid slots for menswear pieces so the MEN nav tab isn't contradicted by the content, or split into two gendered grids.
- 2. Add a price or "from $X" under at least the first row of products — right now nothing in the grid gives a reason to click one item over another.
- 3. Layer in an InCircle loyalty angle (e.g., "InCircle members save more") since this brand's loyalty mechanic is a recurring beat — right now this send ignores it entirely.
- 4. Lower the barrier to entry — a smaller no-minimum discount or free-shipping line would capture browsers who won't hit $400.
- 5. Cut the "Treat yourself..." filler line or replace it with something that does work, e.g. naming the season/collection.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$100 off $400, $250 off $1,000 — ends 9/3`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your fall closet, up to $250 off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dresses, denim, and designer bags — all eligible with code SHOPFALL`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Spend more, save more — new arrivals included`
