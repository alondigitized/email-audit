# Earn a $50–$500 gift card with your purchase today
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-12**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean register break from Neiman Marcus. After four consecutive Last Call sale emails (July 8–12) hammering the same red hero block and "extra 25% off" mechanic, NM pivots to a spend-to-earn gift card incentive — a smart cadence move that resets the urgency clock and avoids the confirmation that the "Last Call" offer had no real end date. The tiered gift card structure ($500 spend → $50 card, up to $11,000 spend → $500 card) is front-loaded clearly in the hero with a promo code, and the product grid below it — Jimmy Choo, Zimmermann, FRAME, Veronica Beard — is the right summer luxury mix.
- That said, this email loses its footing below the hero. Six equal-weight shop buttons (Women's, Women's Clothing, Shoes, Men's Clothing, Handbags, Home) create a decision-paralysis floor. "LIMITED TIME" with no visible deadline contradicts "today" in the subject. And the dense fine print block at the bottom — longer than most of the selling copy — signals that the offer is harder to use than it looks.
- Solid top half, unfocused bottom half. A good email that could have been a great one with one primary CTA and a hard deadline stamped in the hero.

## What's working

- **Cadence reset.** After four near-identical Last Call sends, this different offer type (earn vs. discount) prevents subscriber fatigue and avoids confirming Last Call was a fake deadline.
- **Tiered dollar structure is immediately legible.** The SPEND/EARN grid in the hero — $500/$50 through $11,000/$500 — communicates the mechanic in two rows without body copy.
- **Promo code is surfaced in the hero.** It doesn't require scrolling to find, which reduces friction at the consideration stage.
- **Product selection is current luxury.** Jimmy Choo, Zimmermann, Veronica Beard, FRAME, Theory — exactly the right mix for a summer NM send.
- **Subject line lands.** Specific dollar range, a verb ("earn"), and "today" for urgency — all in 50 characters.

## What's weak

- **Six equal-weight CTA buttons, no primary.** Shop Women's, Shop Women's Clothing, Shoes, Men's, Handbags, and Home all share identical visual weight — no clear next step.
- **"LIMITED TIME" with no visible expiration date.** "Today" in the subject and a "LIMITED TIME" banner in the email but no end date/time anywhere in the scroll. Undermines credibility of urgency.
- **Dense fine print block.** The legal copy block is longer than the entire selling section — the disproportion signals conditions that kill confidence in the offer.
- **Top spend tier ($11,000 → $500) is alienating to most of the list.** Showing it prominently in the hero makes the offer feel like it's for someone else.
- **No loyalty/InCircle framing.** This reads like a rewards mechanic but never connects to InCircle — a missed opportunity to reinforce membership value.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse six CTAs to one hero CTA** — "Shop Gift Card Styles" or "Shop Now to Earn" linking to a curated, gift-card-qualifying collection page. Every additional button below it dilutes the click impulse.
- 2. **Stamp a hard deadline** — replace "LIMITED TIME" with "Offer ends July 14 at 11:59 PM" both in the banner and immediately below the spend/earn table.
- 3. **Hide or de-emphasize the $11,000 tier** — truncate the table at $3,000/$250 and put the highest tier in the fine print. Leading with a five-figure spend threshold is demotivating for 95% of the list.
- 4. **Surface InCircle branding if applicable** — even a one-line callout ("InCircle members earn toward their next tier") adds loyalty resonance.
- 5. **Compress fine print to 3 lines + "See full terms ›"** — the wall of legal copy is longer than the selling copy and makes the offer feel inaccessible.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Earn up to $500 back — shop $500+ today`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Gift card on us when you shop $500+ today`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Tiered rewards on Jimmy Choo, Zimmermann, Veronica Beard & more — ends July 14.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The more you shop, the more you earn — up to a $500 gift card back.`

### 1. Technical Summary

SFMC AMPscript template rendered as a table-based HTML email with responsive CSS breakpoints. QA passed at 57% with 3 warnings — all compliance/deliverability headers — and 26 tracking links unverifiable; no hard errors.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 tracking links skipped by QA probe.** Click-redirect domains (presumably `click.e.neimanmarcus.com` or similar) were not HTTP-probed, so destination URL integrity, UTM parameter presence, and redirect chain health are unconfirmed.

**`@uEm` and `@cmdid` in URL parameters (risk, not confirmed bug).** The AMPscript comment block declares `@uEm = Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm", ...)` and `@cmdid = Lookup(...)`. If either resolves to empty string on a subscriber with no CRM Feed match, links containing these tokens will append blank/malformed query params — no visible breakage but dirty analytics and potential 404s depending on landing page validation.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` in live viewport tag — accessibility violation.**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">
```
Prevents browser zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Users with low vision cannot zoom.

**Three commented-out viewport tags — template debt.**
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
Non-functional but indicates unresolved template experimentation. The `user-scalable=no` variant in the first comment is an identical accessibility violation — if uncommented it would replace the live tag with the same problem.

**Duplicate media query breakpoints.** Two separate `<style>` blocks define responsive rules: one at `max-width: 600px` and a second at `max-device-width: 480px` / `max-width: 480px` / `max-device-width: 520px` / `max-width: 520px`. `max-device-width` is deprecated. No functional breakage in current clients, but introduces maintenance risk.

**No `alt` text visible in truncated source for product images.** Could not confirm — HTML is truncated before image blocks. Should be verified against full source.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`@user` scenario selector left as comment — template hygiene issue.**
```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */-->
```
The comment block header says "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW," indicating `@user` is conditionally set to either a CRM lookup or a SHA-256 hash. The chosen path is not visible in the truncated source, but leaving the unchosen scenario as an inline code comment exposes implementation detail (hashing strategy, data extension name `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`) to anyone who views source. No data leakage at send time, but poor template hygiene.

**`@emailid` fallback lookup.** Comment documents: `Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)` as fallback if `@emailid` not in sendable DE. If a subscriber is not in `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `@emailid` resolves to empty string. Downstream impacts depend on whether `@emailid` appears in link params or suppression logic.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not confirmed** (QA WARN). AgentMail relay did not surface this header. If absent at delivery, Gmail and Apple Mail will not show the native one-click unsubscribe UI, and Yahoo/Google bulk sender requirements (enforced since Feb 2024) may not be met for high-volume sends.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not confirmed** (QA WARN). Required for Gmail's one-click unsubscribe to function. SFMC supports this header natively — if missing, it suggests the relay is stripping headers or the sending configuration omits it.

**`Authentication-Results` header not found** (QA WARN). SPF and DKIM pass/fail status unverifiable through the AgentMail relay. Cannot confirm DMARC alignment from this capture. Sending domain is `e.neimanmarcus.com` — should be verified directly against MX/DNS or a full header capture from a live inbox.

No physical postal address or unsubscribe link was visible in the truncated source — these are CAN-SPAM requirements. Cannot confirm presence; must be verified in full HTML.
