# Summer in full swing: 1,000+ new arrivals
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- This is Neiman Marcus's classic "new arrivals" send — clean black hero, bold all-caps headline, two tidy product grids (women's then men's), and a pair of full-width CTA buttons. The brand roster does the heavy lifting: Christian Louboutin, Givenchy, Celine, Santoni, Ferragamo, Canali. If you're a NM luxury shopper, this is exactly the email that earns a slow scroll.
- The problem is what's missing: no price, no discount, no deadline. Paired with the cadence reality — this is the second NM email on 5/21, and at least the sixth in five days — this reads like a courtesy drop rather than an event. The subject's "1,000+" is the only concrete number anywhere in the send, and it doesn't make it below the fold.
- Verdict: technically solid, strategically uninspiring. A well-executed arrival notice that will move a subscriber who is already in a discovery mood and do almost nothing for anyone else.

## What's working

- Black hero with all-white bold type ("BRAND NEW STYLES JUST LANDED") is visually arresting and instantly legible.
- Brand name callouts under each product (Christian Louboutin, Givenchy, Celine, Ferragamo) position this firmly in luxury territory — no ambiguity about price point.
- Dual-gender structure is handled cleanly with distinct section CTAs ("Shop All Women's New Arrivals" / "Shop All Men's New Arrivals"), not a lazy one-size button.
- Zero render bugs — layout, images, and type are all clean and proportional.

## What's weak

- No price anchoring anywhere — not a "starting at," not a single product price. For a 1,000-arrival drop, even one teaser price would create urgency.
- Hero CTAs ("Shop Women's" / "Shop Men's") are plain-text links buried in small type on a black field — easy to overlook in favor of the bolder grid below.
- No deadline, no "this week only," no inventory signal — nothing to motivate a click now vs. tomorrow vs. never.
- This is the second NM email on 5/21; the earlier Designer Sale send (same day, 7/10) will already be in inbox. Two sends in one day from the same brand is cadence debt.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one price anchor to the hero or hero subhead.** "Brand new styles just landed — starting at $195" or "1,000+ pieces, dozens of designers" gives a click-motivating data point. Right now "1,000+" only lives in the subject line.
- 2. **Upgrade hero CTAs to buttons.** The "Shop Women's" / "Shop Men's" text links are invisible against the dark hero; a small white or outlined button would double tap target size and visual weight.
- 3. **Sequence, don't stack.** Two sends on 5/21 is aggressive after a four-day Designer Sale blitz. This email would perform better on 5/22 as a standalone, post-sale fresh-slate send.
- 4. **Add a "new this week" badge or deadline.** "Just landed — updated every Tuesday" or "New arrivals added daily through Sunday" creates a reason to return even if you don't click today.
- 5. **Surface one editorial hook.** "The Prada SS26 editorial" from 5/20 scored a 9 because it had a story. This email has no narrative thread — it's a grid. One pull-quote from a buyer or a "why we love this" line would differentiate it from the website.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Summer in Full Swing: 1,000+ New Arrivals"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is built on Salesforce Marketing Cloud AMPscript and a table-based HTML template. Four QA warnings require attention: missing RFC-compliant one-click unsubscribe headers (a deliverability risk under current Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policies), an unresolvable authentication-results header, and a malformed plain-text part that is overwhelmingly URLs.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 tracking links use click-redirect wrappers** (domain: `e.neimanmarcus.com`) — QA correctly skipped HTTP probes on these. No direct issue, but UTM parameter coverage cannot be confirmed from this audit (see §6).

**AMPscript `@user` variable conflict — potential data exposure in URLs.**
The comment block shows two competing assignment strategies that are not reconciled:

```html
<!-- @user = Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email) -->
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */-->
```

The Lookup assigns `@user` the raw numeric `EMAIL_ID`. The `HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256` assignment is commented out. If `@user` is appended to outbound URLs (common for personalization/retargeting), a numeric internal subscriber ID is being passed in the clear rather than a hashed email. Verify whether `@user` appears in any `href` values and whether the intent was to use `@uEm` (the pre-hashed value from `NM_CRM_Feed`) instead.

**`@emailid` is defined twice** in the variable block with identical Lookup logic — redundant, no functional breakage, but worth cleaning to avoid future confusion.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**CSS hamburger nav will silently fail in most clients.**
The mobile nav relies on the `<input type="checkbox"> + :checked` CSS hack:

```css
*[class].navbar-checkbox:checked~*[class].menu { visibility: visible !important; ... }
```

Gmail (webmail and Android app), Outlook (all desktop versions), and Samsung Mail strip `<input>` elements and do not support `:checked`. The fallback state (hamburger visible, menu permanently hidden) is the only rendering these clients will show. If the nav contains required links, they will be inaccessible to a significant share of the list.

**CSS-only interactive content blocks have the same support gap.**
`.mobileCont:checked~table *[class=contentInteractive]` — same stripping problem as above; any content behind this gate will never render in Gmail or Outlook.

**`maximum-scale=1` in the viewport meta tag blocks user zoom:**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">
```

This prevents visually impaired users from zooming in iOS Mail. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) recommends removing the `maximum-scale` constraint.

**Three commented-out `<meta name="viewport">` tags remain in `<head>`.** These are dead code and indicate unresolved historical viewport experiments:

```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```

**`<title>` tag has a trailing space:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` — minor; affects preview in some clients that display the title.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not found** (QA WARN).
Gmail and Yahoo have required this header for bulk senders (>5k/day) since February 2024. Its absence risks inbox placement penalties and suppresses the native unsubscribe UI those clients surface to users.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not found — RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe not supported** (QA WARN).
Gmail enforces one-click unsubscribe (the `POST` mechanism) for bulk senders. Without it, Gmail may demote deliverability or add friction warnings. Both headers must be present together to satisfy the requirement:

```
List-Unsubscribe: <mailto:unsub@e.neimanmarcus.com?subject=unsub>, <https://...>
List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click
```

Note: these headers may be injected by the ESP at send time and stripped by the AgentMail relay before QA capture. Confirm with the SFMC configuration that both headers are present in live deliveries by checking a seed inbox's raw message source.

**`Authentication-Results` header not found** (QA WARN).
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status cannot be confirmed from this capture. The sending domain is `e.neimanmarcus.com` (SFMC-managed subdomain). Verify DKIM signing key and DMARC policy alignment (`neimanmarcus.com` DMARC should cover or explicitly include `e.neimanmarcus.com`).

**CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe mechanism** — not visible in the truncated HTML. These are expected in the footer; confirm they are present in the full render.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM coverage cannot be confirmed** — all 26 outbound links are SFMC click-redirect URLs (`e.neimanmarcus.com/r/...`). The destination URLs and their UTM parameters are encoded inside the redirect and not inspectable from the HTML source. A separate link-expansion pass (following redirects to final destination) is needed to verify:

- `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` are present on all CTAs
- `utm_content` differentiates individual product/module clicks
- No links resolve to a 404 or the homepage when a specific PDP is expected

**Plain-text part is a URL dump** (QA WARN — 5270/5827 chars are URLs, 90%+).
The plain-text alternative is not a readable version of the email — it is effectively a raw list of redirect URLs. This will trigger spam heuristics on content filters that score the text/plain part independently. It also provides no fallback experience for plain-text clients.

---

### 7. Recommendations

**Priority 1 — Deliverability risk**
- Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` headers are injected by SFMC at send time. If they are being stripped by the monitoring relay, adjust the relay capture to preserve original headers.
- Verify SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass on `e.neimanmarcus.com` by checking raw headers in a seed Gmail inbox.
- Rebuild the plain-text part as a human-readable summary with a single unsubscribe URL, not a URL dump.

**Priority 2 — Data / PII**
- Audit all `href` values for `@user` token usage. If `@user` appears in URLs and the intent is identity resolution (e.g., for retargeting pixels), replace with `@uEm` (the pre-hashed email already available from `NM_CRM_Feed`). Remove the commented-out `HASHED_SHA256` block or activate it to resolve the ambiguity.

**Priority 3 — Rendering reliability**
- Replace the CSS hamburger nav with a static mobile nav that degrades gracefully in Gmail/Outlook, or confirm the nav links are duplicated in the email body and the hamburger is purely progressive enhancement.
- Remove the three commented-out viewport meta tags.
- Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the active viewport tag.
- Remove the duplicate `@emailid` Lookup.

**Priority 4 — Monitoring**
- Run a redirect-following link check against all 26 tracking URLs in a staging send to verify UTM completeness and that no CTAs land on broken pages.
