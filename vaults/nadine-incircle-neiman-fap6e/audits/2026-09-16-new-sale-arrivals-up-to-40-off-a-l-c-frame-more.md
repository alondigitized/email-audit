# New sale arrivals: up to 40% off A.L.C., Frame & more
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-16**
## Executive summary

- This is a straightforward markdown-event catalog send — big "Sale / Up to 40% off" hero, a nine-item product grid spanning women's, men's, and accessories, then a wall of category buttons. It's the fifth distinct creative approach from Neiman Marcus in two days (editorial, urgency-offer, new-arrivals grid, wedding-guest editorial, gift-card table), and this one lands closest to the 9/14 "1,000+ new fall arrivals" send — same undifferentiated grid format, different occasion.
- The designer-name dropping (A.L.C., Frame, Brunello Cucinelli, STAUD) works as a trust signal for a Neiman Marcus shopper, and "up to 40% off" is a real, visible number in the hero. But the grid mixes womenswear, menswear, and shoes with zero segmentation — a men's-focused subscriber sees six irrelevant women's items before reaching anything for them, and there's no gender/category personalization visible at all.
- Solid, on-brand, forgettable. It's a mid-tier list-clearing sale email, not a targeted push.

## What's working

- "Up to 40% off" is bold, specific, and visible immediately in the hero without scrolling.
- Clear single primary CTA ("SHOP SALE") in a high-contrast black button against the hero.
- Nine distinct named-designer products give real browsing substance, not just a banner.
- "Exclusions Apply" is disclosed right in the hero, not buried in fine print — matches the "offer feels honest" signal.
- Category button grid at the bottom (Women's Clothing, Men's, Dresses, Shoes, Handbags) gives multiple exit ramps for different shoppers.

## What's weak

- No pricing shown on any of the nine products — just designer name, no $ or % off per item, so the "up to 40%" hero claim isn't substantiated anywhere in the grid.
- Grid has no gender segmentation — men's blazer and loafers are sandwiched between five women's dresses/pants with no section headers.
- Every product tile is a flat gray background product-cutout — visually monotonous, no lifestyle imagery or hierarchy to indicate a "hero" item within the sale.
- Generic "Discover your new favorites—now on sale" subhead adds no information beyond what the hero already said.
- Same repeated beat as 9/14's arrivals grid — different products, identical layout, so a subscriber who opened that one gets deja vu.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add prices (strikethrough original + sale price) under each of the nine product tiles — right now the 40%-off promise is invisible at the product level, which is the single biggest miss.
- 2. Split the grid into "For Her" and "For Him" sections (or lead with the mentioned brands as two rows) so menswear isn't buried mid-scroll in a womenswear-dominant layout.
- 3. Vary the tile treatment — feature one hero product larger (e.g., the Theia gown or Brunello Cucinelli blazor) instead of nine equal-weight boxes.
- 4. Rotate the subhead to reference urgency or newness instead of the generic "Discover your new favorites."
