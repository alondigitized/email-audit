# Need a summer refresh? Enjoy up to 40% off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-02**
## Executive summary

- This is Neiman Marcus's second swing at the same stacked-discount event within 24 hours — yesterday's "Surprise: Up to an extra 40% off starts now" launched the event, and today's email is a follow-up reminder that doesn't say it's a reminder. The body is largely recycled: same typographic "Sale" hero, same offer level, same brand grid format. The biggest own-goal here is the subject line — it says "up to 40% off" while the hero in the body clearly reads "UP TO AN EXTRA 40% OFF." The word "extra" is the whole value proposition (stacked on top of existing sale prices), and it was dropped from the subject for no visible reason.
- The product grid (Zimmermann, Dries Van Noten, Off-White, Manolo Blahnik, Giorgio Armani, Ferragamo, Carolina Herrera, Akris) is well-chosen for the Neiman Marcus subscriber, but it's a brand roll-call without prices, product names, or any sale context per item. It creates visual interest without creating purchase intent. The loyalty acquisition block at the bottom ("Claim Your 15% Off — Sign Up") is contextually off for an existing subscriber who is already receiving this email.
- Net judgment: technically clean execution of a watered-down reminder. The creative was stronger yesterday. The offer is real; the email just undersells it.

## What's working

- **Brand-appropriate product grid** — nine luxury brands (Zimmermann, Armani, Ferragamo) instantly signal the tier and feel right for the subscriber.
- **Typographic hero is visually bold** — the large "Sale" letterform with the 40% off callout is high-contrast and immediately legible.
- **Six specific category CTAs** — "Shop Women's Dresses," "Shop Handbags," etc. are better than a single generic CTA and give the subscriber a place to land.
- **Subject line length** — 42 characters, clean, no spam triggers, mobile-safe.
- **No render bugs** — layout is clean, images load, text doesn't overlap.

## What's weak

- **Subject dropped "extra"** — the body says "UP TO AN EXTRA 40% OFF" (a stacked discount on sale prices), but the subject only says "up to 40% off," stripping the only genuinely differentiating word in the offer.
- **No urgency, no deadline** — yesterday's "starts now" framing had at least implied freshness; today has none. Neither the subject nor the body shows when this ends.
- **Nine brand thumbnails, zero prices** — the grid reads as editorial browse, not sale urgency. Showing even one "was $X, now $Y" comparison would lift click intent.
- **Loyalty acquisition block feels misplaced** — the "Claim Your 15% Off — Sign Up" block is targeting non-members, but this email is going to subscribers who already receive NM email. It wastes real estate and feels untargeted.
- **Cadence strain** — subscribers have now seen very similar sale creative on June 26, June 29, July 1, and now July 2. The fourth near-identical send dilutes urgency.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Put "extra" back in the subject.** The body already says it. "Extra 40% off" is the hook — "40% off" sounds like the everyday sale tab. This is the single highest-impact fix.
- 2. **Add a deadline.** "Ends July 4" or "sale ends Sunday" would transform this from browse to buy mode. No other change does as much to lift click-through.
- 3. **Show one price-comparison product.** Swap one of the nine thumbnails for a hero product with a crossed-out original price and a sale price. Let the subscriber feel the discount before they click.
- 4. **Reframe the subject line as a day-2 reminder.** Lean into the momentum: "Extra 40% off — still on today" is honest, fresh, and leverages yesterday's launch awareness.
- 5. **Replace the signup block with a member-exclusive callout.** For existing subscribers: "Your extra 40% is already applied. Shop now" is more relevant and removes friction.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Still 40% extra off: Zimmermann, Armani + more`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dresses, shoes, handbags — sale ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop Zimmermann, Ferragamo, Carolina Herrera at up to 40% extra off.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Summer Refresh / Up to 40% Off"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is built on Salesforce Marketing Cloud AMPscript and uses a table-based layout with a CSS hamburger nav. The primary technical concerns are: developer/implementation comments left in the production HTML source, missing compliance headers (List-Unsubscribe, RFC 8058), and unverifiable link destinations due to click-redirect wrapping.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 tracking links skipped — zero destinations validated.**
All outbound links pass through a click-redirect domain. The QA probe correctly skipped them, but this means no broken-link check was performed. Any destination URL change on the redirect side goes undetected.

**Plain-text body is 87% URLs (5,441 of 6,247 chars).**
A plain-text alternative dominated by raw redirect URLs is a known spam-filter trigger. Plain text should be a readable summary of the email, not a dump of tracking links. This inflates the URL-to-content ratio well past the ~30% threshold flagged by aggressive filters.

**`@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")` — suspicious no-op.**
The AMPscript in the comment block shows `@refid` is built by concatenating `@base_refid` with an empty string. This is either an incomplete expression (a suffix was intended and left blank) or dead code. If `@refid` is appended to any tracking URL, this produces no differentiation from `@base_refid`.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Multiple commented-out `<meta viewport>` tags in `<head>`.**
Three alternate viewport declarations are commented out directly above the active one:
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
These are implementation artifacts and bloat the `<head>`. They pose no functional risk but indicate the template was never cleaned post-experiment.

**Hamburger nav relies on CSS checkbox hack.**
The interactive mobile nav uses `:checked~` sibling selectors and `visibility`/`opacity` transitions. Gmail (web and app), Outlook (all versions), and Samsung Mail do not support this pattern. Users on those clients see a broken or invisible nav state.

**Interactive CSS has no fallback.**
`.contentInteractive { display: none !important; }` hides content by default; it only reveals on `.mobileCont:checked`. In non-supporting clients, this content is permanently hidden with no fallback path.

**`<title>` has a trailing space:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` — minor, but indicates the template wasn't sanitized.

**Alt text not assessable** from the truncated source. Should be verified in the full render.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Developer implementation notes are present in the production HTML source.**
The email opens with a multi-line HTML comment block containing AMPscript variable declarations, lookup instructions, and the directive "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW":

```html
<!--REQUIRED variables: @email ... @base_refid ... @refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"") 
    @user Lookup(...) @emailid ... @uEm ... @cmdid ...
    CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW -->
```

This block is visible to any recipient who views source. It exposes: field names from `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated` and `NM_CRM_Feed` data extensions, lookup logic, and the presence of a `cmdid` field (likely campaign/segment ID). This is a data-extension schema leak.

**SHA256 hash option is commented out without a clear active replacement.**
```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->
```
The `@user` variable is declared via a subscriber lookup in the same comment block. It's unclear which resolution path is live in the deployed template. If `@user` is used in any tracking pixel or URL parameter, its value (and whether it's hashed or raw) cannot be confirmed from this source.

**`@uEm` and `@cmdid` are declared but their usage downstream is not visible** in the truncated source. If either is injected into a URL without encoding, URL injection is a risk.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not found (RFC 2369).**
QA warns this may be a relay-capture issue, but it must be confirmed. Gmail and Yahoo Mail have required this header for bulk senders (≥5,000/day) since February 2024. Absence results in deliverability penalties and suppressed placement.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not found (RFC 8058 — one-click unsubscribe).**
Same enforcement deadline as above. Without this header, Gmail's native "Unsubscribe" button cannot trigger a server-side removal; it degrades to a mailto fallback or is suppressed entirely, which increases spam complaints.

**`Authentication-Results` header not found — SPF/DKIM status unknown.**
The sending domain is `e.neimanmarcus.com`. If DKIM or SPF alignment cannot be confirmed through the relay, inbox placement at major providers is at risk. This needs to be verified against actual delivery headers, not just the AgentMail relay capture.

**CAN-SPAM physical address** — not assessable from truncated source; should be confirmed present in footer.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**All 26 links use click-redirect wrapping — UTM parameters unverifiable.**
No destination URL could be probed. There is no way to confirm that UTM source/medium/campaign parameters are appended to landing pages, or that they survive the redirect chain. This should be spot-checked manually: click one redirect, inspect the final URL in browser devtools for `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, and `utm_content`.

**`@refid` no-op (see §2) may corrupt campaign attribution.** If `@refid` is the primary tracking differentiator and it resolves to the same value as `@base_refid`, all variants in this campaign will appear as a single segment in analytics.

---
