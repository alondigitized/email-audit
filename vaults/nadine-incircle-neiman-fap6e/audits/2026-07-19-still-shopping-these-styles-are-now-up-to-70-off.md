# Still shopping? These styles are now up to 70% off
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-19**
## Executive summary

- This is NM's sixth consecutive Last Call sale email in roughly as many days, and it is a visual clone of the July 18 "Up to 70% off new sale arrivals" send (scored 8/10 here). Red hero, same "EXTRA 25% OFF / UP TO 70% OFF" stacking, same "SHOP SALE" CTA, same 9-brand product grid — nothing in the creative has changed except the subject line.
- That subject line swap is the most interesting thing about this send. "Still shopping?" reads like behavioral retargeting — browse abandonment, cart recovery — and it's the right instinct. But the email body delivers a generic batch sale email underneath it. The setup/payoff mismatch is obvious the moment you open: there's no product you browsed, no cart reminder, no personalized hook. It's a mass blast wearing a behavioral costume.
- Functionally clean and offer-forward, but the fatigue of this identical red hero appearing day after day is real. NM's subscribers who've received every send since July 15 have now seen this creative at least four times. The diminishing-returns curve on this template is steep.

## What's working

- "Still shopping?" subject line is the most distinctive opener NM has used in this recent run — it creates a conversational hook that breaks from the standard promo blast format.
- Red hero with large-type offer hierarchy is instantly scannable; the "EXTRA 25% OFF" headline registers in under a second.
- Luxury brand name-drops in the product grid (Isabel Marant, Balmain, Manolo Blahnik, Stuart Weitzman, Theia) do authority work without needing editorial copy.
- "LAST CALL®" sub-brand creates credible urgency — a known NM retail event, not manufactured scarcity.
- Six category CTAs at the bottom (Women's Clothing, Handbags, Shoes, Kids, Women's Dresses, New) give multiple entry points across shopper intent.

## What's weak

- "Still shopping?" sets a behavioral expectation the email body completely ignores — no product, no category, no cart reference beneath it.
- Same red hero creative used July 18, July 16, and earlier — creative fatigue is the single biggest risk to continued deliverability engagement metrics.
- No price points shown anywhere in the product grid — "up to 70% off" is a claim, but seeing "Was $595, Now $178" would anchor it.
- No InCircle or loyalty mention — a glaring omission for a brand whose VIP tier is a key retention lever.
- The in-body subheader "Save on these designer styles before they're gone" is soft and generic; it adds nothing beyond what the hero already said.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make "Still shopping?" real or drop it entirely.** If NM is actually segmenting browse/cart abandoners, surface the category they visited in the hero ("Still looking at shoes? They're now 70% off."). If it's fake personalization on a batch blast, swap to a subject that owns the offer directly — it's more honest and more credible.
- 2. **Show one price.** Even a single "Now $175" callout on the Manolo Blahnik or Stuart Weitzman hero item makes the 70% claim tangible and increases click confidence dramatically.
- 3. **Rotate the creative.** The red hero has now appeared in at least four consecutive sends (July 15/16/18/19). A white background, a single model editorial shot, or a dark luxury treatment would reset visual attention.
- 4. **Add an InCircle callout.** One line — "InCircle members: stack your points on sale" — keeps the VIP segment engaged and signals that NM sees them.
- 5. **Sharpen the preheader.** The current in-body copy ("Save on these designer styles before they're gone") is generic and repeats the hero's message. Swap it for brand specificity.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last call: designer styles now up to 70% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Isabel Marant, Balmain & more — up to 70% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Extra 25% off Isabel Marant, Balmain & more — ends soon`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop designer sale before it's gone — up to 70% off`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Still shopping? These styles are now up to 70% off"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a Salesforce Marketing Cloud (AMPscript) template with standard table-based layout and responsive media queries. No hard rendering blockers were found, but four QA warnings indicate gaps in authentication visibility, one-click unsubscribe compliance, and plain-text payload quality that warrant attention before future sends.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 tracking links skipped by HTTP probe** — all outbound links route through a click-redirect domain (likely `click.e.neimanmarcus.com`). This is expected ESP behavior, but it means link-rot and destination integrity cannot be confirmed programmatically. Manual spot-checks of destination URLs are required post-send.

No broken `href` values or malformed tracking parameters were detectable in the truncated source.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Multiple commented-out viewport `<meta>` tags** remain in `<head>`:
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
These are inert but are template hygiene issues — stale viewport experiments left in production code increase payload size and create ambiguity if a future editor uncomments one accidentally.

**CSS hamburger nav uses `visibility`/`opacity` toggling** — the mobile nav relies on a `<input type="checkbox">` hack with CSS transitions. This is broadly supported but is zero-accessibility: no ARIA roles, no keyboard focus management, and the toggle is invisible to screen readers. Not a blocking rendering issue, but `role="navigation"` and `aria-expanded` should be added.

**No `role="presentation"` on layout tables** — the table-based layout lacks `role="presentation"` attributes, which means screen readers may announce table structure. Low-severity for a promotional email but a best-practice gap.

No issues found with charset declaration (`UTF-8`), or `x-apple-disable-message-reformatting` meta tag.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

The AMPscript variable block at the top exposes the full variable dependency graph as an HTML comment in the delivered source:

```
<!--REQUIRED variables: @email ... @user ... @emailid ... @uEm ... @cmdid ... -->
```

**This comment is visible to recipients who view source.** It leaks internal data extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`) and field mappings (`uEm`, `cmdid`, `EMAIL_ID`). These should be stripped at render time or moved to server-side-only documentation.

**Commented-out scenario switch**: `<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_SHA256 */-->` — a scenario selector left commented in production suggests a deployment process gap. If this controls which persona/segment path fires, its state should be deterministic and validated pre-send, not inferred from comment state.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not confirmed** (QA WARN) — the AgentMail relay did not surface this header. Major mailbox providers (Gmail, Apple Mail, Yahoo) use this header to display the native unsubscribe button. If missing from the actual SMTP envelope, this is a CAN-SPAM and Gmail Bulk Sender Policy violation. Neiman Marcus's ESP almost certainly injects it, but the relay interception gap means it cannot be verified here — confirm directly against raw headers from a seed inbox.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not confirmed** (QA WARN) — one-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo for bulk senders since February 2024) was not detected. Same caveat as above re: relay gap, but this must be confirmed. Absence would trigger inbox suppression at scale.

No issues found with physical mailing address presence (assumed in footer, not truncated away) or visible unsubscribe link in body.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Plain-text version is 70% URLs** (QA WARN) — 5,268 of 6,101 characters in the plain-text alternative are URL strings. This typically means the plain-text was auto-generated from the HTML and consists almost entirely of raw tracking URLs with no surrounding copy. Spam filters (SpamAssassin, Proofpoint) score this pattern negatively. A human-readable plain-text summary with one or two representative links should replace the auto-generated dump.

UTM parameter coverage on the 26 tracked links cannot be confirmed without resolving through the redirect chain. Given the click-redirect architecture, UTM injection likely occurs at the redirect layer — this should be validated against GA4 acquisition reports post-send to confirm `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` are landing correctly.
