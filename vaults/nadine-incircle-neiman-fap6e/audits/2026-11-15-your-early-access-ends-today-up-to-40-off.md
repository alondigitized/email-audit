# Your early access ends today: up to 40% off
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-15**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, single-offer last-call send, and the offer is easy to read. A black hero carries "THE DESIGNER SALE / EXCLUSIVE ACCESS / 40% OFF" in red, with "LIMITED TIME | CODE NMSALE" and a white "SHOP EARLY" button. The eye lands on the red 40% first, as it should. A 3x3 product grid follows, then six category buttons.
- The weak points are the subject and the framing. The subject says "ends today," but the visible hero only says "limited time." The real deadline, 11/15/26 at 10:00 PM CT, is in the footer fine print. "Early access ends today" also sits oddly next to a "SHOP EARLY" button. This is the fourth Designer Sale send in a week. It follows the 11/12 "exclusive access: 40% off" email, which also used a typographic hero with a code. The 11/13 and 11/14 "up to $650 off" sends have the same last-call cadence. It's tidy and on brand, but the creative is a near-repeat and the offer tier has changed again (code NMSALE here, NMACCESS and NOVSAVE before).
- Judgement: a competent, mid-pack promo. It's clear and well laid out, but it hides its deadline, doesn't link products to prices, and adds nothing new for someone who has seen the earlier sends.

## What's working

- The hero hierarchy is clear. The red "40% OFF" on black is the first thing you see, and the code is stated right below it.
- The single white "SHOP EARLY" button contrasts strongly with the black hero and is easy to find.
- The product grid is clean and consistent, with designer names under each item (A.L.C., Chloe, Dolce&Gabbana). It signals a luxury assortment and is easy to scan.
- The six category buttons give shoppers a way in beyond women's items, including men's, handbags, shoes and kids'.
- The email has no render bugs. Images load, text is legible and nothing overlaps. That is an improvement on the 11/12 Tom Ford send, which had an overlapping panel.

## What's weak

- The deadline is buried in the footer fine print. The hero says only "limited time," which undercuts the "ends today" urgency in the subject.
- The grid shows no prices, no sale prices and no product-level detail. Nothing tells you what 40% off actually saves.
- The "SHOP EARLY" CTA conflicts with "ends today." The copy feels like a launch, not a last call.
- The grid is nine unlinked-looking items with no hero product. It reads as a generic mix of dresses, shoes, a bag and men's jeans, so no single persona is addressed.
- The footer is heavy, with a large fine-print block and app/stylist promos that dilute the close. The "View in a Web Browser" link sits at the very bottom.
- The grey-on-black hero small print ("Online only. Exclusions apply.") is low contrast.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the deadline in the hero: "Ends tonight 10 PM CT" replaces "Limited time." This is the highest-impact change because it makes the subject's urgency visible.
- 2. Change the button to "Shop the sale now" or "Shop 40% off." "Early" contradicts the last-call framing.
- 3. Add sale prices or "was/now" pricing under at least the top row, so the 40% becomes concrete.
- 4. Lead with one hero product or category tied to a recognizable pick, such as the Chloe bag, and make it larger than the rest of the grid.
- 5. Shorten the footer. Move the promo-code instructions up near the CTA so they aren't hidden in the fine print.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Ends tonight: 40% off designer, code NMSALE`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last chance: 40% off Chloe, Balmain & more`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Online only through 10 PM CT. Enter NMSALE at checkout.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Dresses, bags and shoes from A.L.C., Chloe, Dolce&Gabbana and more.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible HTML is a table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional email. It leaks an internal ESP/AMPscript comment block into the delivered source and has a few accessibility gaps. The QA run reports 0 hard failures and 4 warnings. Most of those warnings concern headers and links that could not be verified. The HTML is truncated, so findings are limited to what is visible.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **29 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the HTTP probe.** Their destinations, redirect chains and status codes are unverified. This is a coverage gap, not a confirmed defect.
- **Plain-text part is >70% URLs** (5,443 of 6,641 chars). This suggests an auto-generated text alternative made up mostly of long tracking URLs. It is a spam-filter and readability risk.
- No broken or malformed `href` values are visible in the truncated source.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom is disabled.** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom on mobile, which conflicts with WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
- **No `lang` attribute.** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang`, so screen readers cannot pick the correct language.
- **Weak `<title>`.** It is `Neiman Marcus ` with a trailing space and does not describe the message.
- **A comment sits before the DOCTYPE.** The AMPscript comment block comes first in the source. Comments before the DOCTYPE can trigger quirks mode in some legacy renderers.
- **Hamburger menu uses a CSS checkbox hack.** The `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu` selectors in the media query are unsupported in Gmail and several Outlook builds. I could not see the markup, so I can't confirm a fallback exists.
- **Attribute selectors match one class exactly.** Rules like `*[class=mobile]` and `*[class=hide]` fail on any element that carries more than one class.
- **Global `p { margin:0 auto; text-align:center }` override.** It affects any `<p>` that doesn't set its own alignment.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Internal AMPscript is exposed in the delivered HTML.** The opening comment lists internal data extension and field names: `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`, `EMAIL_ID`, `uEm`, `cmdid`, `@base_refid`, `@refid`. This is an information-disclosure and hygiene issue. It also shows the comment was not stripped by the ESP at send time.
- **No unresolved tokens in the visible body.** The `@variable` strings appear only inside the comment, so they won't render to the user. The truncated remainder is not covered.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) are not found.** QA notes that the AgentMail relay may strip them. This is unconfirmed. It matters because Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require one-click unsubscribe.
- **`Authentication-Results` is not found**, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment are unknown. QA flags this as expected via the relay. Check the original sending domain, `e.neimanmarcus.com`, with a direct seed test.
- **Visible footer unsubscribe link and physical postal address** are not in the truncated source, so they cannot be verified.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **UTM and `refid` parameters are unverified.** The code references `@refid` and `@base_refid`, which suggests campaign IDs are appended to links. The 29 links go through click-tracking redirects and were not probed. No landing page, offer or parameter data was captured.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip the AMPscript documentation comment from the template before send. Move it to the template's internal notes.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag.
3. Add `lang="en"` to `<html>` and give the `<title>` a descriptive value.
4. Move the DOCTYPE to the first line of the file.
5. Verify `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` on the raw message from the ESP, not the relay copy. Run a seed test to confirm SPF, DKIM and DMARC.
6. Replace the auto-generated plain-text part with a curated one. Use short URLs and descriptive text.
7. Resolve the 29 tracking links in a separate crawl to confirm landing pages and UTM or `refid` parameters.
8. Confirm the hamburger nav has a working fallback in Gmail and Outlook, and replace exact `[class=…]` selectors with `~=` or class selectors.
