# *3* ways to save: $1,500 off (includes beauty) + extra 25% off + The Designer Sale
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-28**
## Executive summary

- This is the fourth or fifth stacked-offer Black Friday send from Neiman Marcus in five days. It is almost the same creative as the 11/26 "Treat yourself to Black Friday savings" email, which led with $1,500 off, extra 25% and The Designer Sale. The black hero with red frames and three offers is now wallpaper. The subject asks the reader to count three savings ideas, and the body hands over three competing boxes with only one SHOP ALL button underneath.
- The product grid is the best part. Nine designer items (Theia, A.L.C., Creed, Valentino Garavani, Jimmy Choo) are cleanly cropped and brand-labeled. None of them is linked to a price or a discount, though. The email never says what $1,500 off means in practice. The fine print says you must spend at least $250, but that sits at the bottom, below the footer links.
- Judgement: it's competent and on-brand, but it's fatigue-inducing and unfocused. It's also the last-day "Ends 11/28" send, and nothing in the render says so. The deadline is buried in the legal text.

## What's working

- The black hero with red frames is high contrast, and the offers read instantly at a glance.
- The code THANKFUL is visible in the hero, so there's no hunting for it.
- The nine-item designer grid with brand names under each item gives a clear sense of the assortment.
- The category buttons (Women's Clothing, Beauty, Men's, Handbags) let people jump to their own interest.
- The nav bar (Women, Men, Shoes, Sale) is clean and easy to use.

## What's weak

- Three offers compete in the hero. "Up to $1,500 off," "Extra 25% off" and "Up to 60% off" all share equal weight, so no single idea wins.
- There is a single SHOP ALL button for three different offers. Each offer box has no link of its own.
- "Ends 11/28" appears only in the fine print. A last-day deadline is the best urgency lever here, and it's hidden.
- The spend thresholds for $1,500 off are not shown in the hero. The 11/26 send had a spend-and-save table. This one drops it and the offer feels vaguer.
- The product grid shows no prices and no sale markers, and the products aren't tied to any of the three offers.
- The tagline "Time to check off your wish list—and gift list" is faint grey and easy to miss.
- The designer sale box is a flat coral block that looks different from the two framed boxes above it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the deadline and one offer. Put "Ends tonight" in the hero, and make the $1,500 off tiers the headline. Push the other two offers into a single line underneath.
- 2. Add the spend-and-save table back under the hero, as in the 11/26 send. Show the tiers (for example, spend $X, save $Y), so "up to $1,500" is believable.
- 3. Link each offer box to its own landing page, not one SHOP ALL.
- 4. Add sale prices or "% off" tags to the nine grid items so the grid shows the offer working.
- 5. Move the "Ends 11/28, 10 PM CT" line out of the fine print and up beside the hero button.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last call: up to $1,500 off ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Code THANKFUL: your last day for $1,500 off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus extra 25% off and up to 60% off designer. Ends 10 PM CT.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Beauty included. Use code THANKFUL at checkout before midnight.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible source (truncated) has several real code-level issues: an ESP template comment leaked into production HTML, a viewport that blocks zoom, and no `lang` attribute. The automated QA run (50% pass, 0 failures, 4 warnings) could not verify unsubscribe headers, authentication, or any of the 29 click-tracked links.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **29 tracking links not probed.** The QA tool skipped them because they are click-redirect domains. Destination validity, redirect chains and final landing status are unverified.
- **Link-heavy plain-text part.** 5,488 of 7,424 characters (about 74%) are URLs, over the 70% threshold. The long wrapped tracking URLs are the likely cause. This can hurt spam scoring and makes the text part hard to read.
- The HTML was truncated, so individual `href` values, `alt` text and link counts could not be checked.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom blocked.** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` prevents pinch-zoom on mobile. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **No document language.** The tag is `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` with no `lang` or `xml:lang`, which screen readers use for pronunciation.
- **Comment before the DOCTYPE.** The large `<!--REQUIRED variables…-->` block precedes `<!DOCTYPE`. Some clients, such as older IE-based webmail, render in quirks mode when anything comes before the DOCTYPE.
- **Fragile mobile selectors.** The media-query rules use exact-match attribute selectors, such as `*[class=contenttable]` and `*[class=hide]`. They stop matching any element that carries more than one class.
- **Checkbox-hack hamburger menu.** It uses `.navbar-checkbox:checked~…` and `.mobileCont:checked~table`. Gmail and Outlook do not support this pattern, and the fallback could not be verified in the truncated source.
- **Minor CSS issues.**
  - `.show` declares `width` twice (`width:auto !important` and `width:278px`).
  - `<title>` is `Neiman Marcus ` with a trailing space.
  - Several commented-out viewport variants remain in `<head>`.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Leaked template comment.** The comment at the top of the source exposes ESP internals. It names the data extensions `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated` and `NM_CRM_Feed`, the fields `EMAIL_ID`, `uEm` and `cmdid`, and the variables `@email`, `@base_refid` and `@refid`. It is not a rendering failure, but it is information disclosure and should be stripped at send time.
- No unresolved tokens, such as `%%…%%` or `{{…}}`, appear in the visible portion of the source. The body past the truncation point was not checked.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` not found.** The QA tool notes the relay may not capture it, so this is unconfirmed rather than a confirmed defect. It matters for Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender requirements.
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` not found.** The same caveat applies: RFC 8058 one-click support is unverified.
- **`Authentication-Results` not found.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown because of the AgentMail relay. The sender domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` is a dedicated subdomain, which is a good sign, but alignment could not be checked.
- The footer unsubscribe link and physical postal address are not in the truncated source, so they could not be checked.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **Cannot assess.** The visible source has no `href` values, and the 29 tracking links were not followed.
- The comment's `@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")` suggests campaign attribution runs through a `refid` parameter rather than standard `utm_*` tags. This is inferred, not confirmed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Strip the SFMC variable-documentation comment** from the production template. It leaks data extension and field names, and it sits before the DOCTYPE.
2. **Remove `maximum-scale=1`** from the viewport meta tag, and add `lang="en"` to `<html>`.
3. **Re-run QA on a direct-from-ESP copy** or a seed-list test. That would capture `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post` and `Authentication-Results`, which the relay hides.
4. **Probe the tracking links** by following redirects to the final URL. Check for 200 status, redirect hops, and `refid`/UTM carry-through to the landing pages.
5. **Review the plain-text part.** Cut its URL density by shortening or removing redundant links.
6. **Switch to `[class~=…]` or plain class selectors** for the mobile CSS, and confirm the hamburger menu has a non-CSS fallback in Gmail and Outlook.
7. **Re-audit with the full, untruncated HTML.** Footer compliance, `alt` text, token rendering and link inventory could not be checked here.
