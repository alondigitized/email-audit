# First Call: Up to 40% off starts now
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-14**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, loud, and clear markdown send. A red hero inside a heavy black frame says "FIRST CALL® UP TO 40% OFF". The "SHOP NOW" button and an "Exclusions Apply" note sit just below it. The offer is the first thing the eye lands on.
- It is a thin send for a luxury brand. Nine product tiles show brand names only: Alice + Olivia, A.L.C., AG Jeans, Acler, Ksubi, Veronica Beard, Polo Ralph Lauren, Chiara Boni La Petite Robe and Theory. None has a price, a discount or a link cue. The "up to 40%" headline is also softened by the footnote's "25-40%" and "select categories".
- This follows the Oct 13 "sweater weather" editorial (no offer) and the gift-card-with-purchase send, which broke the NMCIRCLE "$50–$200 off" streak. A real subscriber now gets a third different mechanic in three days. It reads as a solid, if generic, sale announcement, and it needs product-level proof to convert.

## What's working

- The hero is bold and unambiguous. "UP TO 40% OFF" is the largest element, and the red-on-black block is hard to miss.
- The subject and hero match. "First Call: Up to 40% off starts now" and the "FIRST CALL" headline carry the same message.
- The 3x3 grid of designer names (Ksubi, Theory, Veronica Beard) is tidy, with consistent image sizing and no visible render bugs.
- The six red category buttons let a shopper route to women's, men's, shoes, handbags, kids' or accessories.
- The text-list "CLAIM YOUR 15% OFF" block is a separate, clearly set-off value add.

## What's weak

- The product tiles show no prices, no "was/now" and no percent-off badge, so nothing proves the 40%.
- The tiles are not labeled as sale items. A reader can't tell a markdown from a full-price arrival.
- The hero has only one CTA, "SHOP NOW", which is small against the huge headline. The "Exclusions Apply" note under it is tiny.
- The footnote ("25-40%... select categories") undercuts "up to 40%". The real range is buried at the bottom.
- The "CLAIM YOUR 15% OFF" text signup is a second offer that competes with the 40% hero, and the "SIGN UP" link is small and underlined.
- The grid skews women's and occasion wear. The men's, kids' and accessories buttons are the only nods to anyone else.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add "was / now" pricing or a "-30%" badge to each tile, and link each tile to its product page. This is the biggest lift.
- 2. State the range in the hero: "25–40% off select fall styles". It's more honest and removes the gap with the footnote.
- 3. Make the "SHOP NOW" button larger and move "Exclusions Apply" into a clearer line.
- 4. Move the 15% text-list offer lower, or drop it from sale sends.
- 5. Add men's, shoes and handbags tiles to the grid, or segment the send.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **Bloated plain-text part:** 5893 of 6869 chars (about 86%) are URLs (`[WARN] Plain-text is >70% URLs`). This points to long, unshortened tracking URLs in the text alternative, which is a spam-filter and readability risk.
- **Not assessable:** the HTML supplied is truncated before any `<a>` markup, so I can't assess individual `href`s or whether `refid` is carried on them.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom on mobile. This fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
- **Missing language:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang`/`xml:lang`, so screen readers can't select the right pronunciation.
- **CSS-only mobile nav:** the `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu` and `.hamburger`/`.close` rules need `:checked` and sibling selectors. Gmail (web and app) and Outlook desktop don't support these, so the nav will likely degrade or be unusable there. Whether a fallback exists is not visible in the truncated source.
- **Exact-match class selectors:** `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=mobile]` and `*[class=hide]` match only when the `class` attribute is exactly that string. Any element with an additional class won't get the mobile rule.
- **Minor:** `.show` declares `width: auto !important` and later `width: 278px` in the same rule, so the second declaration is dead. The `<title>` is `Neiman Marcus ` with a trailing space.
- **Not assessable:** alt text and image dimensions are outside the truncated portion.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **No unresolved tokens:** none are visible in the supplied portion of the rendered body.
- **Leaked template comment:** the top of the document contains the authoring note `<!--REQUIRED variables: @email … @base_refid … @refid … Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", …) … Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm"/"cmdid", …) CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW -->`.
  - It was not stripped before send.
  - It exposes internal Data Extension names and field names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`, `uEm`, `cmdid`), plus the `HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256` option.

  - It sits before the `<!DOCTYPE>`, and content before the doctype can trigger quirks mode in some older clients, mainly legacy IE-based and Outlook rendering.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip the authoring/variable comment, including the DE names and the "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO" note, from the production template at send time. If one comment must stay, put it after the `<!DOCTYPE>`.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta to restore pinch-zoom.
3. Add `lang="en"` and `xml:lang="en"` to `<html>`.
4. Confirm that raw SMTP headers at the source MTA include `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, and that SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass. Don't rely on the relay-captured copy.
5. Provide a non-checkbox fallback for the nav in Gmail and Outlook. Use `[class~=…]` or plain class selectors instead of exact-match `[class=…]`.
6. Shorten or simplify the tracking URLs in the plain-text alternative, or reduce the number of links in it.
7. Re-run QA with the full, untruncated HTML and a probe that follows redirects on the 31 tracking links. That would let you verify final destinations, UTM/`refid` parameters, alt text and footer compliance.
