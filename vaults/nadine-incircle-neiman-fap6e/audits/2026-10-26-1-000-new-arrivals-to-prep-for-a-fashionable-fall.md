# 1,000+ new arrivals to prep for a fashionable fall
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-26**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, on-brand Neiman Marcus new-arrivals send, but it has no offer, no price and no deadline. A black hero reads "BRAND NEW STYLES JUST LANDED." Below it are two tidy 3x2 grids of designer product, one for women and one for men: Saint Laurent, Gucci, Givenchy, McQueen and others. It looks expensive and is easy to scan. It also gives a reader no reason to click today rather than next week.
- The subject promises "1,000+ new arrivals," but the email shows 12 products. The hero's two CTAs, "Shop Women's" and "Shop Men's," are small underlined text links on black, not buttons. The real buttons sit lower, as outlined "Shop All Women's/Men's New Arrivals" boxes. Compared with the Oct 26 "First Call up to 40% off" send, which had a bold offer and a black SHOP NOW button, this is a softer, browse-only email.
- Verdict: a competent catalog email that fits the brand but won't drive urgency. It is closer to the editorial sends of Oct 23 and Oct 25 than to the offer-led Oct 24 and Oct 26 sends. It reads as the fifth Neiman email in four days, so it needs a sharper hook to stand out.

## What's working

- The hero is bold and legible, and it is clearly seasonal "just landed" newness.
- The 3x2 product grids are uniform, with consistent gray backdrops and brand-name captions. They are easy to scan.
- Designer names (Saint Laurent, Gucci, Givenchy) act as the draw for luxury shoppers.
- The women's and men's sections are cleanly split, each with its own outlined CTA button.
- The render is clean. There are no broken images or overlapping text, and the footer utilities (app, style advisor, find a store) are tidy.

## What's weak

- There is no offer, price, loyalty perk or deadline, so nothing creates urgency.
- The hero CTAs are tiny underlined text links on black. They are easy to miss and far weaker than the lower buttons.
- Products show only a brand name. There are no product names, prices or badges like "new" or "low stock," so a shopper learns little before clicking.
- The subject's "1,000+" claim is not supported by the 12 items shown, and "fashionable fall" is generic.
- The lower CTA buttons are thin outlined boxes with light gray text, so they have low contrast and little visual weight.
- The women's and men's items are the same size. The email has no hero product to anchor the eye.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a reason to act now, such as a hero product, a "just in" or "selling fast" badge, or a complimentary-shipping or Last Call tie-in. The email is currently a pure catalog.
- 2. Turn the hero text links into solid white buttons ("Shop Women's," "Shop Men's") and make the lower buttons filled black.
- 3. Add a price or a short product name under each brand ("Beaded bomber jacket, $1,490").
- 4. Lead with one standout item or a "Top 5 new this week" row, so the grid has a focal point.
- 5. Match the subject to the content. Either show a bigger sample or promise something the grid delivers, such as "12 new-season pieces we're wearing now."
- 6. Ease the send cadence, or differentiate this email clearly from the Oct 25 wedding-guest editorial and the Oct 26 First Call send.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible HTML has a few source-level issues: leftover ESP template comments, a zoom-blocking viewport tag, and no `lang` attribute. The four QA warnings are relay or capture limitations, not confirmed defects. The HTML is truncated, so the footer, body links, and image markup could not be checked.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Links not verified.** The QA run skipped HTTP probes on 30 tracking links ("Tracking/click-redirect domains"). Redirect chains, destinations, and expiry are unconfirmed.
- **URL-heavy plain text.** QA reports 5726 of 6565 plain-text characters (~87%) are URLs. This is consistent with long wrapped tracking URLs. Spam filters can penalize this ratio, but it is a warning, not a confirmed failure.
- **Not assessable.** The truncated source shows no `<a href>` elements, so I can't confirm broken or malformed links.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom blocked.** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` prevents pinch-zoom on supporting clients. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 concern.
- **No document language.** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang` or `xml:lang` attribute, so screen readers can't select the right language profile.
- **Weak title.** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` is generic and has a trailing space. It doesn't reflect the subject line.
- **Invalid font-family value.** `.disclaimerText` uses `font-family: Arial, Helvetica, 'sans-serif'`. The quoted `'sans-serif'` is treated as a font name, not the generic keyword, so the generic fallback is lost. Other rules use the unquoted keyword correctly.
- **Hamburger menu depends on CSS-only checkbox state.** The `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu` rules inside the mobile media query only work in clients that support `:checked` (e.g., Apple Mail). Gmail, Outlook, and others typically don't, so the menu state can't be confirmed there. The CSS also hides content with `display: none` by default, so I couldn't confirm that links reachable only through the menu have a visible fallback.
- **Not assessable.** Image `alt` text, `role="presentation"` on layout tables, and image dimensions fall beyond the truncation point.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Template comment left in sent HTML.** The source begins with an ESP authoring comment: `<!--REQUIRED variables: @email ... @base_refid ... @uEm = Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm", ...) ... CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW -->`, followed by `<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->`. It exposes internal Data Extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`) and field names to anyone who views source. It also adds bytes to the payload.
- **Comment before DOCTYPE.** These comments appear before `<!DOCTYPE ...>`, which can trigger quirks mode in some renderers.
- **No unresolved tokens visible.** I found no unrendered `%%...%%` or `{{...}}` tokens in the visible portion. The `@variables` appear only inside comments.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post** (RFC 8058) were not found. QA attributes this to the AgentMail relay possibly not capturing them. Treat it as unverified, not a confirmed gap. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require both headers, so check the raw message at the source.
- **Authentication-Results** was not found. QA notes this is expected via the relay, so SPF, DKIM, and DMARC alignment are unknown. The From domain is `e.neimanmarcus.com`.
- **Footer not assessed.** The visible HTML doesn't reach the physical postal address or visible unsubscribe link.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not assessable. No hrefs appear in the truncated source, and the QA run skipped all 30 tracking links, so I can't verify UTM parameters, `refid` handling, or landing-page alignment. The comment references `@refid`/`@base_refid`, which suggests campaign IDs are passed through the redirect, but I couldn't confirm that.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip the ESP authoring comments (`REQUIRED variables ... CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO`) before send, and keep them out of the DOCTYPE position.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag.
3. Add `lang="en"` and `xml:lang="en"` to `<html>`. Use a descriptive `<title>` that matches the subject line.
4. Fix `'sans-serif'` to unquoted `sans-serif` in `.disclaimerText`.
5. Re-run QA against raw SMTP headers, not the relay copy, to verify List-Unsubscribe, List-Unsubscribe-Post, and SPF/DKIM/DMARC.
6. Run a follow-up check that resolves the 30 tracking links through the redirect chain and records final URLs and UTM params.
7. Re-run the audit on the full, untruncated HTML to cover alt text, table roles, the footer, and link markup.
