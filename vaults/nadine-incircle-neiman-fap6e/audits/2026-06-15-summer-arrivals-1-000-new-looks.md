# Summer arrivals: 1,000+ new looks
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, editorial new-arrivals send — six luxury brands, a strong black hero, no discount, no urgency. Compared to the same-day "Up to $200 off + triple InCircle points" email (9/10) which ran on June 15 as well, this one is the lighter sibling: it exists to surface summer inventory, not to close a transaction. The creative executes competently — no broken images, readable hierarchy, credible brand names — but it has no hook beyond volume ("1,000+ new looks") to pull someone back in after the relentless Extra 20% Off drumbeat of June 9–14.
- The biggest structural problem is sending this on the same calendar day as the InCircle triple-points push. Those two emails are competing for the same inbox slot, and this one loses on impact: no offer, no loyalty angle, no personalization. The June 15 InCircle email gave loyal shoppers a compelling reason to act; this one just says "stuff landed." For a subscriber already fatigued from a six-send sale sequence, a pure browse-and-discover email lands with a thud.
- The brand grid is strong — Brunello Cucinelli, Carolina Herrera, Ralph Lauren Collection, Acne Studios, Burberry. But none of those names are in the subject, none have prices, and the grid is generic enough to be a category page. This is a missed opportunity to make the "new" feel worth clicking.

## What's working

- **Black hero executes cleanly.** "BRAND NEW STYLES JUST LANDED" reads as editorial, not sale-rack, and gives NM's aesthetic a reset after two weeks of red-banner discount creative.
- **Brand names are readable and credible.** Six visible luxury labels (Brunello Cucinelli, Carolina Herrera, Ralph Lauren Collection, Burberry, Acne Studios) instantly signal quality without needing price tags.
- **Dual-CTA structure is logical.** "Shop Women's" / "Shop Men's" in the hero, then sectioned "Shop All [Gender] New Arrivals" CTAs underneath, is intuitive navigation.
- **Subject line specificity is above average.** "1,000+ new looks" is a concrete, countable claim — better than "so many new arrivals!"

## What's weak

- **No InCircle / loyalty mention — a glaring omission on the same day as the triple-points email.** Sending two June 15 emails and stripping the loyalty hook from this one fragments the value proposition unnecessarily.
- **No gender divider between women's and men's.** The transition from women's product grid to men's is marked only by a "Shop All Women's New Arrivals" button — no visual header, no clear section break. Disorienting for a shopper in one category.
- **Hero copy is generic.** "Brand New Styles Just Landed" could be any luxury retailer. Not one brand name, not one silhouette, not one trend word. No reason for the subscriber to feel this is curated for them.
- **Volume claim ("1,000+ looks") is actually a deterrent.** A fatigued shopper coming off a sale binge doesn't want to browse 1,000 SKUs — they want to be told what's worth noticing.
- **Cadence risk.** Two NM emails in one day (this + the InCircle send) is aggressive even with separate sub-sender labels. The brand history shows a subscriber who received 6+ emails in June 9–14; one more day of double-sends will push them toward unsubscribe.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with one or two hero products, not a 3×2 grid.** Pick the single most arresting summer arrival (e.g., a Brunello Cucinelli bag or the Prabal dress) and give it a full-width hero with a brief, editorial descriptor. The grid can follow. This transforms the email from a browse-dump to a curation.
- 2. **Add a soft loyalty nod.** Even one line — "InCircle members earn on every new arrival" — ties this to the same-day triple-points push and rewards members who open both.
- 3. **Add a women's / men's section header.** A simple rule line or bold "WOMEN" / "MEN" label prevents the gender ambiguity in the grid.
- 4. **Reframe the subject from volume to curation.** "1,000+ looks" is overwhelming; lead with a brand name or a trend instead.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Brunello, Burberry, and 1,000+ summer arrivals`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The summer edit just dropped at Neiman's`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New from Carolina Herrera, Ralph Lauren Collection, Acne Studios and more.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Brand new styles, just landed — shop the summer drop before it sells out.`

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Summer arrivals: 1,000+ new looks"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is built on Salesforce Marketing Cloud (AMPscript) and renders via a standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional table layout with a CSS hamburger nav. Four QA warnings — two compliance-critical — plus several AMPscript hygiene issues in the source require attention before the next send.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 click-redirect links unverifiable**
All outbound links route through a tracking/redirect domain; the HTTP probe was skipped for all 26. Destination URLs and UTM integrity cannot be confirmed from the outside.

**Plain-text body is 90%+ URLs by character count**
QA reports 5,150 of 5,700 chars in the plain-text alternative are URLs. This is a strong spam-filter signal (many filters score raw URL density in the text part). The plain-text version needs human-readable prose between links.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Commented-out dead code in `<head>`**
Three alternative `<meta name="viewport">` tags remain in the source as HTML comments. They are harmless but indicate un-cleaned template copy:
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```

**Responsive breakpoint gap (480px–520px)**
Two separate media query blocks target different max-widths: one at `480px` (3-column → single column for `.ImgSize`) and another at `520px` (2×2 grid via `.ImgSize2x2`). Elements falling between these widths may receive inconsistent layout treatment.

**Quoted generic font family**
In `.disclaimerText`:
```css
font-family: Arial, Helvetica, 'sans-serif';
```
`'sans-serif'` is in quotes, making it a named font (looked up literally) rather than the CSS generic keyword. The fallback will silently fail if Arial and Helvetica are absent. Should be unquoted: `sans-serif`.

**Hamburger nav uses CSS checkbox hack — no keyboard accessibility**
The mobile nav relies on `input[type=checkbox]:checked` state with `visibility/opacity` toggling. This pattern is not keyboard-navigable and is invisible to screen readers. Not a blocker for email (most AT ignores complex email nav), but worth noting if this template is shared with an AMP for Email or web variant.

**iOS9 animation workaround still present**
```css
body { -webkit-animation: bugfix infinite 1s; }
@-webkit-keyframes bugfix { from { padding: 0; } to { padding: 0; } }
```
This is a years-old workaround for an iOS 9 rendering bug. iOS 9 is end-of-life. It runs a continuous 1-second animation loop on `body` for no current benefit.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Unresolved scenario selector — `@user` ambiguity**
The AMPscript header comment reads `CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW` and then presents two options for how `@user` is populated. The SHA256 hashing variant is commented out:
```
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */-->
```
If the intent is to pass a hashed email into tracking parameters (common for NM's personalization stack), this line being commented out means `@user` falls back to the raw `EMAIL_ID` lookup — a privacy regression relative to the stated template design.

**`@refid` concatenates with empty string**
```
@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")
```
Concatenating with `""` is a no-op. This pattern typically exists as a placeholder where a content-block suffix (e.g., `"-nm-summer-arrivals"`) should be appended. If left blank, `@refid` equals `@base_refid` exactly, and per-email-module tracking attribution will not be possible downstream.

**`@cmdid` and `@uEm` sourced from `NM_CRM_Feed` lookup**
Both are secondary lookups against a CRM feed DE rather than being passed directly in the sendable DE. If a subscriber's `EMAIL_ADDRESS` is missing from `NM_CRM_Feed` (e.g., new opt-ins), both variables return empty, which can break downstream personalization or suppress segments silently. No null-guard is visible in the truncated source.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header missing** *(QA WARN)*
Gmail and Apple Mail use this header to surface native unsubscribe UI. Its absence means users who can't find the footer link may hit "Report Spam" instead. Per Google's bulk sender requirements (enforced since Feb 2024 for >5k/day senders), `List-Unsubscribe` is mandatory.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) missing** *(QA WARN)*
One-click machine unsubscribe (required by Google for bulk senders) depends on this header pair. Without it, Gmail may suppress the native unsubscribe button and route complaints to spam reporting.

**`Authentication-Results` not captured** *(QA WARN)*
SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown because the AgentMail relay didn't surface the header. The sending domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` is a dedicated ESP subdomain; DKIM alignment should be verified directly against the ESP (likely Salesforce Marketing Cloud's shared signing infrastructure). If DKIM is broken, the missing `List-Unsubscribe` issue compounds deliverability risk significantly.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

The 26 redirect links could not be probed, so UTM parameter presence cannot be confirmed. Given the `@refid` empty-concatenation issue noted above, any tracking that relies on `refid` being a content-module-specific value will aggregate all clicks under the base campaign ID, making module-level attribution impossible in analytics.
