# The Designer Sale starts now: up to 50% off
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-16**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, single-offer launch email. A red-and-black hero reads "THE DESIGNER SALE / UP TO 50% OFF" with a black "SHOP NOW" button. The offer is unmissable and the hierarchy is clear. It's the payoff for the "early access" send on 11/15 ("Your early access ends today: up to 40% off", code NMSALE). Moving from 40% early access to 50% public launch is a credible escalation.
- The weak spot is the product grid. Nine designer items sit in a 3x3 layout with brand names only, with no prices, no sale prices and no per-item discount. A "50% off" email that never shows a marked-down price asks the reader to take the discount on faith. The six category buttons at the bottom are also low-contrast, with muted red text on a red fill.
- Judgement: a solid, trustworthy sale announcement. The hero is strong, but the body doesn't prove the savings, and "Exclusions Apply" sits right under the CTA.

## What's working

- The hero is bold and instantly legible. "UP TO 50% OFF" is the largest element after the title.
- There is one primary CTA, a black "SHOP NOW" button with strong contrast against the red.
- The subject matches the hero copy, so there is no message mismatch after the open.
- The 3x3 grid is varied (apparel, bags, shoes, menswear) and the product photography is clean, on consistent grey backgrounds.
- The seasonal line "Prep your holiday wardrobe with designer savings" fits the moment. It also ties to the recent holiday editorials from Alice + Olivia, Balmain and Versace.

## What's weak

- None of the nine products shows a price or a discount, so the 50% claim is never demonstrated.
- The category buttons ("SHOP WOMEN'S", "SHOP SHOES" and so on) use red-on-red text with poor contrast. They look disabled.
- "Exclusions Apply" is small and low-contrast right under the CTA, which undercuts the offer. The full fine print is buried at the bottom.
- There is no deadline or urgency. Earlier sends in this run carried "LIMITED TIME" and a code (NOVSAVE, NMSALE), and this one has neither.
- The hero is very tall. On mobile, the headline text and button push the product grid well below the fold.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add "Now $X / Was $Y" or a "% off" tag under each product. This is the single biggest lift, because it turns an abstract 50% into concrete savings.
- 2. Fix the category button contrast. Use black fill with white text, or white fill with a black border.
- 3. Add a credible end date, such as "Through Sunday" or "Ends 11/22", to the hero.
- 4. Tighten the hero height so the first product row is visible sooner on mobile.
- 5. Move the exclusions into the hero as readable microcopy, or give a plain statement of which categories are in.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible HTML is a table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional template with a hamburger-nav CSS hack. It has a leaked ESP template comment, a zoom-blocking viewport tag, and no `lang` attribute. The HTML is truncated and the 29 tracking links were not probed, so link, footer and unsubscribe checks rest on the QA data alone.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Not verified:** `[WARN] 29 tracking link(s) skipped`. The click-redirect domains were not probed, so destination status, redirect chains and expiry are unknown.
- **Long plain-text URLs:** `[WARN] Plain-text is >70% URLs: 5488/6504 chars` (about 84%). This points to long tracking URLs in the plain-text alternative, which spam filters can penalize and which make that part hard to read.
- No broken or malformed `href` values are visible in the truncated source.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">`. `maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-zoom on mobile and fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
- **No document language:** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang` attribute, so screen readers can't pick the right pronunciation.
- **Weak `<title>`:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has a trailing space and doesn't reflect the subject.
- **Hamburger menu relies on `:checked`:** the rules `*[class].navbar-checkbox:checked~*[class].menu` and `.mobileCont:checked~table` depend on checkbox-hack support. Gmail and Outlook don't support it, and the fallback isn't visible in the truncated source, so this is unconfirmed.
- **Exact-match class selectors:** `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=hide]` and `*[class=mobile]` only match elements whose `class` is exactly that value. They fail if any element carries multiple classes. I can't confirm that from the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Template comment shipped in the HTML:** the pre-DOCTYPE block `<!--REQUIRED variables: @email ... @user Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", ...) @uEm = Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm", ...) @cmdid ... CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW --> <!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->` was delivered to recipients.
  - It exposes internal Data Extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`) and field names (`EMAIL_ID`, `uEm`, `cmdid`).
  - It shows that a SHA-256 hashed email and CRM IDs are used for identity.
  - It is a QA miss: an unstripped developer note.
  - Content before `<!DOCTYPE>` can also trigger quirks-mode handling in some clients.
- No unresolved merge tokens appear in the visible rendered portion. The `@` variables occur only inside the comments.

## 5. Compliance
- `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header not found` and `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found (RFC 8058)`. The QA note says the relay may strip these. I can't confirm a real gap, but one-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules and should be checked on the raw message.
- `[WARN] Authentication-Results header not found`. SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown from this capture. The sender domain is `e.neimanmarcus.com`.
- The visible HTML doesn't show the footer unsubscribe link or the physical address, so CAN-SPAM body requirements are unverified.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
- No UTM parameters or final landing URLs are visible, because the HTML is truncated and the 29 links were not resolved.
- The comment's `@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")` suggests campaign attribution uses a `refid` rather than UTMs. That is inferred, not confirmed.
- Landing-page alignment can't be assessed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip the ESP and AMPscript developer comments before send, including everything above `<!DOCTYPE>`, so no internal DE or field names leak.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag.
3. Add `lang="en"` to `<html>` and set a descriptive `<title>`.
4. Verify `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` on the raw message, not the relay copy. Confirm SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment from the original headers.
5. Re-run the audit with the full HTML and with the 29 tracking links resolved. This would cover the footer and unsubscribe link, redirect destinations, `refid` or UTM presence, and the nav fallback for clients without `:checked` support.
6. Shorten tracking URLs in the plain-text part, or add readable text around them, to bring the URL ratio down from about 84%.
