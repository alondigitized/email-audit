# Now up to 40% off: coveted styles from Alice + Olivia & more
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-14**
## Executive summary

- This is the third outing of the same big-script "S" Sale hero Neiman Marcus has now run three times this week — 8/11 ("New. Sale. Arrivals," 7.00/10, the original), 8/12 ("Save up to 40% on new sale arrivals," 6.00/10, same dusty-blue recolor), and now this 8/14 send, which reuses that identical dusty-blue hero and "UP TO 40% OFF" / "SHOP SALE" button verbatim. The creative is legible and clean, but the well is visibly running dry — a subscriber who's opened even one of the prior two would recognize this instantly as the same email with a new subject line and a reshuffled product grid.
- The 3x3 grid below the hero is genuinely well-curated and on-brand for a luxury persona — Alice + Olivia sequin top, Tory Burch bag, Jimmy Choo bucket bag, Ungaro gown — and the subject line's callout of "Alice + Olivia" as a named designer is a smart, curiosity-driving upgrade over the generic "Tory Burch, Ramy Brook & more" framing from 8/12. But the hero itself is stale creative, and nothing in the layout signals that this is a materially different sale from the last two.
- Net: solid execution dragged down hard by repetition. Score accordingly — this is a cadence problem, not a design problem.

## What's working

- The 3x3 product grid is diverse and aspirational — shoes, denim, dresses, a bag, a gown — spanning multiple categories without feeling cluttered.
- Subject line names a specific, recognizable designer (Alice + Olivia) rather than a vague "& more," which is a real curiosity/specificity lift over the 8/12 send.
- "SHOP SALE" CTA button is high-contrast (black on dusty blue) and unmissable above the fold.
- Honest, non-buried fine print ("Exclusions Apply" on the hero, full markdown disclosure in the footer).
- Clean secondary navigation (Shop Women's Clothing, Shop Men's, Shop Dresses, etc.) gives multiple entry points below the grid.

## What's weak

- This is the same dusty-blue "S" hero graphic as 8/11 and 8/12 — third use in four days, which will read as visibly repetitive to any regular opener.
- No product prices, member/loyalty pricing, or per-item discount shown in the grid — just designer names, so there's no visible incentive to click any single item over another.
- No urgency or deadline anywhere in the email (no "ends tonight," no countdown) despite this being framed as a live sale.
- Grid images are inconsistently styled — some tightly cropped product-only shots (jeans, dress), others full flat-lay (fringe top, mule) — slightly undercuts the otherwise-polished editorial feel.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Retire the "S" hero for at least a week.** Three uses of the same graphic in four days is the single biggest drag on this send — swap in a lifestyle/model shot or a different typographic treatment so repeat openers don't tune out immediately.
- 2. Add a real deadline to the hero ("Ends Sunday" / "48 hours only") — nothing here currently signals why someone should act now versus next week.
- 3. Show at least a starting price or "$XX" on 2-3 hero grid items to make the 40% off feel concrete rather than abstract.
- 4. Rotate the grid order so the named-in-subject brand (Alice + Olivia) leads the grid instead of sitting in the middle row — right now the subject promises it but the eye doesn't land there first.

- **Preheader Alt B:** `Sequins, denim, and a Jimmy Choo bucket bag, all on sale.`
