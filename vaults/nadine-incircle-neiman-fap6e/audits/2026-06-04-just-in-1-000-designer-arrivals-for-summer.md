# Just in: 1,000+ designer arrivals for summer
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-04**
## Executive summary

- This is Neiman Marcus's second "1,000+ new arrivals" blast in three days — and visually it's nearly identical to the June 1 send that scored a 7. The black-on-white hero ("BRAND NEW STYLES / JUST LANDED"), the gender-split product grids, the same two CTA buttons — a subscriber who opened Monday's email will feel a strong sense of déjà vu. That repeat creative is the dominant story here, not the email itself.
- The product selection is genuinely strong: Missoni, Bottega Veneta, Lafayette 148, Valentino Garavani, Homme Plissé, Cartier — a legitimate luxury mix that earns its "designer arrivals" label. The layout is clean and loads without visible breaks. But there's no price, no offer, no urgency, and no reason to click today versus any other day.
- Verdict: a competent, unremarkable new-arrivals mailer undermined by an identical send sent three days prior. NM is burning subscriber attention on repeat creative during a week when they've already fired gift-card and sale emails daily.

## What's working

- **Designer brand labels are visible and credible** — Bottega Veneta, Valentino Garavani, Missoni, Cartier read instantly as luxury; no aspirational overreach.
- **Gender-split CTAs ("Shop All Women's New Arrivals" / "Shop All Men's New Arrivals")** are specific and unambiguous — each section lands its own call to action.
- **Hero visual hierarchy is crisp** — the black slab with white type reads immediately; the eye goes hero → Women's grid → Men's grid with no confusion.
- **No render bugs** — clean layout, images loaded, no overlapping elements, no lorem-ipsum.
- **Subject line hits the 50-char mobile threshold** at 45 characters.

## What's weak

- **Recycled hero creative** — "BRAND NEW STYLES / JUST LANDED" on the same black background appeared June 1; three days is too short a window to re-use a hero this closely.
- **No offer or price signal anywhere** — pure browse bait with nothing to act on; click incentive is zero for anyone not already in shopping mode.
- **Thumbnail products are too small** — the grid is six across on desktop; individual items are barely identifiable, especially the accessories row.
- **No urgency signal** — no "limited stock," no "just restocked," no deadline; "just in" in the subject does no work in the body.
- **Cadence fatigue** — this lands as NM's third or fourth email of the day (gift card event, Pride editorial, and now this) with the same gift card event resend still fresh from this morning.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Differentiate the creative from June 1** — swap the hero to a single editorial shot (a Missoni dress, a Bottega bag) with the brand name large, rather than the generic "BRAND NEW STYLES" type treatment that is now stale.
- 2. **Add one price anchor** — even a "from $195" or "new under $500" line converts browse intent to click intent; pure new-arrivals emails without price signal are easy to defer.
- 3. **Lead with one hero product instead of six thumbnails** — give the Cartier or Bottega Veneta piece a full-width moment before the grid; it signals curation, not volume.
- 4. **Merge or delay this send** — if the June 1 new-arrivals email is still in window, skip or push this by two days and combine it with a soft editorial angle rather than firing the same message twice in one week.
- 5. **Add a scarcity or restock hook** — "New in: styles added since Monday" or "Restocked: sizes are moving fast" gives an arrival reason specific to today.
- **Subject Alt A:** `New this week: Bottega, Missoni, Cartier and more`
- **Subject Alt B:** `100 new designer pieces you haven't seen yet`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh arrivals from the brands you track — women's and men's.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Including sizes that just came back in stock.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Just in: 1,000+ designer arrivals for summer"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The template is a standard SFMC AMPscript/HTML email with functional CSS structure, but carries several compliance gaps flagged by the relay and unresolved template hygiene issues in the source. The plain-text companion is effectively non-functional due to extreme URL density.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 click-redirect links skipped — destinations unverifiable**
All outbound links pass through a tracking redirect domain. The QA probe correctly skipped them, meaning no destination URL, UTM parameter, or 404 check was performed on any link in this send. This is a coverage gap, not a bug — but it means broken destinations would not be caught pre-send.

**Plain-text version is 90.5% URLs (5,270 / 5,820 chars)**
The plain-text alternative is almost certainly auto-generated from the HTML and consists nearly entirely of click-tracking URLs. Plain-text clients and spam filters that weight the text/plain part will see an incoherent blob of redirector URLs with no readable content. This also inflates spam scores on content-ratio heuristics.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Three dead viewport `<meta>` tags left in source**
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
These are commented-out alternatives from past iterations. Not rendered, but indicative of template drift. The `width=320, initial-scale=0.29` value in particular suggests very old lineage (pre-iPhone 5 era sizing).

**iOS 9 hamburger `@-webkit-keyframes bugfix` hack still active**
```css
body { -webkit-animation: bugfix infinite 1s; }
@-webkit-keyframes bugfix { from { padding: 0; } to { padding: 0; } }
```
This no-op animation loop was a workaround for an iOS 9 rendering bug (2015). iOS 9 has ~0% market share. The animation fires on every render for no purpose.

**No `lang` attribute on `<html>` element**
```html
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">
```
Missing `lang="en"` (or equivalent). Screen readers cannot infer document language; some accessibility-aware clients flag this.

**`<title>` tag contains a trailing space**
```html
<title>Neiman Marcus </title>
```
Cosmetic only, but visible in Gmail's browser-based preview tab.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**AMPscript variable declarations are inside HTML comments in the visible source**
```html
<!--REQUIRED variables: @email ... @user Lookup(...) @emailid ... @uEm ... @cmdid ...-->
```
In SFMC, AMPscript inside `<!-- -->` HTML comments is not executed. The actual executable `%%[ ]%%` blocks that set `@user`, `@emailid`, `@uEm`, and `@cmdid` must exist elsewhere in the full (non-truncated) template. If those blocks are missing or conditionally excluded, all downstream personalization tokens would render as empty strings silently.

**"CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO" comment leaves ambiguity about which `@user` path is live**
```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */-->
```
This comment notation (`<!--/* */-->`) does not execute in SFMC — it is documentation only. It signals the SHA-256 hashed email path was intended, but without seeing the live AMPscript block, it cannot be confirmed from this source fragment that the correct lookup vs. hash scenario is active.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent** *(QA: WARN)*
Not captured by the AgentMail relay. Google and Yahoo require this header for bulk senders (>5k/day). Its absence risks inbox placement degradation and is a CAN-SPAM risk factor.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) header absent** *(QA: WARN)*
One-click machine unsubscribe is required by Gmail and Yahoo for bulk senders as of February 2024. Without it, Gmail will surface a manual unsubscribe prompt instead of the one-click button, increasing spam complaints.

**`Authentication-Results` header not captured** *(QA: WARN)*
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status is unverifiable through the relay. Sending domain is `e.neimanmarcus.com` — alignment between the `From:` header domain and DKIM `d=` tag cannot be confirmed from this source.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**All 26 destination URLs are behind redirect domains — UTM coverage is unverifiable**
Because the QA probe skipped all tracking links, it is unknown whether UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) are appended consistently, present at all, or whether any links resolve to 404s. This is the highest-risk gap in this audit: a broken hero CTA would not have been caught.

No further site-continuity findings can be confirmed from the available source.

---

### 7. Recommendations

| Priority | Issue | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **High** | `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers missing | Confirm these are injected at the ESP/send level (SFMC or upstream MTA), not in HTML. If absent from the actual SMTP envelope, add them before next send. |
| **High** | One-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058) absent | Required for Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender compliance. Add `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` at the MTA layer. |
| **High** | Plain-text version non-functional | Write a minimal human-readable plain-text version. At minimum: subject line, a sentence of copy, and the primary CTA URL in plain form. |
| **Medium** | Authentication-Results unverifiable | Run a test send through a header-capturing tool (e.g. Mail Tester, GlockApps) to confirm SPF/DKIM/DMARC all pass for `e.neimanmarcus.com`. |

| **Low** | AMPscript variable blocks not visible in source | Verify the full template contains active `%%[ ]%%` blocks for all personalization variables. Spot-check a test send with a known seed address to confirm `@user` / `@emailid` render correctly. |
| **Low** | iOS 9 CSS hack + dead viewport tags | Remove `@-webkit-keyframes bugfix` and the three commented-out `<meta viewport>` tags at next template refresh. |
