# Tick. Tock. Shop new markdowns up to 70% off during our Last Call Sale
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-23**
## Executive summary

- This is Neiman Marcus resetting from the three-send personalized InCircle urgency ladder that peaked last night ("Your private offer ends tonight: 25% off one item," 10/10 here on 7/22) back to a mass Last Call blast. The extra 25% off stacking mechanic — applied on top of existing Last Call prices to reach "up to 70% off" — is a proven NM playbook, and the red hero executes it cleanly: bold typography, unmissable percentage, minimal clutter. The "Tick. Tock." subject hook is punchy and clear of spam signals.
- What undercuts it is the abrupt depersonalization. Yesterday, this subscriber had a unique promo code and felt individually selected. Today she's in the same pool as every other NM email address on a generic sale blast. That "Tick. Tock." urgency also goes unsubstantiated in the email body — there is no end date, no countdown, nothing to close the loop on what the ticking clock is counting down to. The designer product grid (Balmain, Manolo Blahnik, Isabel Marant) looks aspirational but carries no prices, which is a meaningful miss for a sale email whose entire hook is the size of the discount.
- Solid execution of a familiar format, but the lack of personalization and hollow urgency keep it from matching the InCircle sends that preceded it.

## What's working

- Red hero with "EXTRA 25% OFF" and "FOR UP TO 70% OFF" is immediately legible — the offer math is visible without any decoding
- Designer brand labels (Balmain, Manolo Blahnik, Stuart Weitzman, Isabel Marant) add instant credibility and signal this isn't an off-price clearance bin
- "Tick. Tock." in the subject is punchy urgency without ALL CAPS, triple exclamation points, or other spam triggers
- Category CTA grid at the bottom (Women's Clothing, Handbags, Shoes, Women's Dresses) offers multiple entry points cleanly without visual noise
- Email renders completely clean — no broken images, no overlapping text, no layout breaks

## What's weak

- "Tick. Tock." urgency in the subject has no backing in the email body: no end date, no countdown timer, no "ends Sunday" — the hook evaporates the moment you open
- Zero InCircle or member-specific framing despite the subscriber being a demonstrated member who just received three personalized sends peaking last night
- "SHOP SALE" is the weakest possible hero CTA — after NM spent three sends making this subscriber feel individually chosen, the generic button is a step backward
- Product grid shows no prices — for a sale email built entirely on discount magnitude, omitting crossed-out and current pricing is a direct conversion miss
- Subject line runs ~70 characters, which truncates on most mobile clients before reaching "Last Call Sale"

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor the urgency with a real deadline** — add "Extra 25% off ends Monday" (or whatever the actual cutoff is) in small text beneath the CTA button. "Tick. Tock." demands a clock face; without one it reads as a cliché.
- 2. **Add prices to the product grid** — show crossed-out original, current sale price, and final price with the extra 25%. Three numbers per product. That's the reason anyone clicks a Last Call email.
- 3. **Bring InCircle back into the frame** — one line beneath the hero: "InCircle members: your extra 25% stacks on member pricing." The InCircle logo costs nothing to include and reconnects the mass blast to the relationship NM just spent three sends building.
- 4. **Make the hero CTA specific** — "Shop Women's Last Call" or "Shop New Markdowns" over the generic "Shop Sale."
- 5. **Tighten the subject for mobile** — drop "during our" to get under 55 characters.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Extra 25% off Last Call — new markdowns to 70% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New markdowns hit Last Call: extra 25% off today`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Designer prices just dropped again — see what's new before it's gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Balmain, Manolo, Stuart Weitzman — and your extra 25% stacks on top.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Last Call Sale" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

SFMC-built email using AMPscript for personalization, sent from a dedicated subdomain (`e.neimanmarcus.com`). QA pass rate is 43% with four warnings; the critical gaps are unverified authentication headers and missing RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe support.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 tracking links skipped** — QA could not probe click-redirect destinations (likely `click.e.neimanmarcus.com` or similar). No broken-link confirmation is possible for those URLs.

**Plain-text body is 86% URLs** (5,268 of 6,101 chars). This indicates the plain-text alternative is auto-generated from the HTML link list rather than authored prose. Spam filters treat a high URL-to-text ratio in the plain-text part as a signal; this also degrades readability for plain-text recipients.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Multiple commented-out viewport meta tags** — three `<meta name="viewport">` variants are left in the `<head>` as HTML comments, indicating unresolved template indecision. These don't render but add noise and suggest the template was not cleaned up before send.

```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```

**`<title>` has a trailing space** — `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>`. Minor, but shows in some preview panes and tab labels.

**Hamburger nav uses CSS checkbox hack** — the mobile nav relies on `:checked` pseudo-selectors and `visibility`/`opacity` transitions. This is non-functional in Gmail (which strips `<style>` blocks), meaning mobile Gmail users see a broken or collapsed nav with no interaction available.

No issues found with legacy table-based layout, XHTML 1.0 Transitional DOCTYPE, or standard email client resets — these are expected for broad-client compatibility.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`@refid` CONCAT is a no-op** — the AMPscript comment block documents:

```
@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")
```

The second argument is an empty string literal, making this a passthrough with no appended suffix. If the intent was to add a campaign variant or version tag (e.g., `CONCAT(@base_refid,"_A")`), it was not filled in. This would cause all tracking `refid` values to equal `@base_refid` with no differentiation.

**"CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW" comment unresolved** — the header comment instructs the sender to select between two `@user` assignment scenarios. One option (`SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256`) is visible but commented out. The selected scenario is not visible in the truncated source. If the wrong scenario was left active (or both were commented out), `@user` would be undefined and any personalization or tracking that depends on it would silently fail or send a null value.

**Variable declarations are inside HTML comments** — the entire AMPscript variable block is wrapped in `<!-- ... -->`. In SFMC, AMPscript inside HTML comments does not execute. If these declarations are only in comments (and not duplicated in a live `%%[ ]%%` block elsewhere), the variables `@uEm`, `@cmdid`, `@emailid`, and `@user` would be undefined at send time.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not detected** — QA notes this may be a relay-capture gap, but the header must be present. Google and Yahoo's 2024 bulk sender requirements mandate `List-Unsubscribe` for senders exceeding 5,000 messages/day.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` not detected (RFC 8058)** — also required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy. Without this, Gmail shows a two-step unsubscribe flow rather than the one-click button, increasing friction and friction-driven spam reports.

**`Authentication-Results` header not captured** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown via the AgentMail relay. Sender subdomain `e.neimanmarcus.com` is a standard ESP subdomain; authentication is expected but unverified for this send.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

Cannot verify landing page alignment or UTM parameters for the 26 skipped tracking links — destination URLs are behind click-redirect domains and were not probed. No issues confirmed, but no clearance either.
