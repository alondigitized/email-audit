# Shop early & get up to $1,500 off your purchase
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-24**
## Executive summary

- This is the clearest version of the Neiman Marcus Black Friday offer so far. The black hero with a red frame puts "UP TO $1,500 OFF" in large type. Directly under the "SHOP NOW" button is a spend-and-save table: $250 saves $50, $500 saves $125, $1,000 saves $250, and $5,000 saves $1,500. A reader can work out their own discount in seconds, and the "up to" headline stops sounding like bait.
- The rest of the email is a standard 3x3 product grid (PURPLE, Jacquemus, Mac Duggal, Maison Francis Kurkdjian, La Mer, Jimmy Choo, Aquazzura) and six category buttons. It is competent but generic. The email also lands one day after the Nov 23 "3 ways to save" send, which led with this same $1,500 offer, and on the same day as the "Designer Sale up to 60% off" send. A subscriber has now seen this offer in at least three emails. The 11/28 expiry is a real deadline, but the copy doesn't use it.
- Verdict: strong on clarity, weak on freshness. The only things that set it apart from the Nov 23 send are the table and the "Shop early" subject.

## What's working

- The hero offer is huge and high-contrast, and it is the first thing the eye lands on.
- The spend/save table makes the offer concrete. The ladder from $250 to $5,000 shows the discount is roughly 20-30% across tiers.
- The promo code THANKFUL sits in the hero, with a single white "SHOP NOW" button that stands out against the black.
- The product grid uses clean, consistent cutouts on a grey background. It mixes beauty, apparel, shoes and handbags, and the "Includes Beauty & Fragrance" line is backed by the La Mer and MFK tiles.
- The six category buttons give shoppers clear routes into the site.

## What's weak

- "Ends 11/28/26 at 10:00 PM CT" appears only in the fine print. The hero says "Limited Time", which is weak urgency for a real five-day deadline.
- The grid items have brand names but no prices, no sale marks and no product links that I can see. That makes the grid decorative, not persuasive.
- The fine print is dense. The $250 minimum on regular-price merchandise and the "Exclusions Apply" line cut against the otherwise clear offer.
- The tone is repetitive. This is the same black-and-red template and the same $1,500 promise as the Nov 23 send, and the Designer Sale email went out the same day.
- The tagline "Time to check off your wish list—and gift list" is soft and does not tie into the Black Friday framing.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the deadline in the hero, for example "Ends Nov 28 at 10 PM CT" next to the code. Real urgency is the cheapest lift available.
- 2. Tie the grid to the offer. Use "Spend $500, save $125" examples with prices, or "Gifts worth the spend" groupings, so the products connect to the table.
- 3. Cut the send cadence or consolidate. Fold the Designer Sale and this offer into one email on days when both run, or alternate creative so the hero doesn't repeat.
- 4. Add a small tier highlight, such as a "Most popular: spend $1,000, save $250" tag, to guide readers toward a higher cart.
- 5. Shorten the fine print visually, or put the minimum spend near the table where readers will see it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Spend $1,000, save $250 — code THANKFUL`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Black Friday early: up to $1,500 off ends Saturday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Spend $250+ and save $50 to $1,500. Beauty included. Ends 11/28.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your savings ladder: $250 → $50 off, $5,000 → $1,500 off. Code THANKFUL.`

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom on mobile. This fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).

- **Checkbox hamburger menu:** the mobile nav uses `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu` and `.mobileCont:checked~table`. These checkbox-hack patterns aren't supported in Gmail and several Outlook clients. The visible source doesn't show a fallback.
- **Other items:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has a trailing space. The commented-out viewport variants (`width=320, initial-scale=0.29`, `width=1024`) are dead code.
