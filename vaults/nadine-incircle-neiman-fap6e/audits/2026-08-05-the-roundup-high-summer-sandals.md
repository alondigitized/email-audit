# The Roundup: High-Summer Sandals
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-05**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, no-discount editorial roundup — a curated grid of designer sandals (Fendi, Jimmy Choo, Givenchy, Miu Miu, Gucci, Toteme) styled on an ornate gilt chair, with zero mention of price, percentage off, or urgency anywhere in the send. It's the same lifestyle-first playbook Neiman Marcus has leaned on all week (8/1 "Field Notes," 8/2 "Day-to-dusk dressing," 8/3 "1,000+ new arrivals"), and like those, it reads more like a Vogue slideshow than a marketing email — which is exactly the brand's polarizing bet.
- The execution itself is tight: no render bugs, a legible hierarchy (hero image → title → two clear CTAs → six-item grid), and the shoe curation spans a real style range (flat, kitten heel, block heel, stiletto) so most sandal shoppers will find something. But there's no reason to act *today* — no member pricing, no countdown, no "only X left." Compared to the 8/4 and 8/5 gift-card sends (both scored 7/10 on a hard incentive), this is a softer, browse-only play that will underperform on clicks even if it looks nicer sitting in the inbox.
- Judgement: solid brand-building creative, weak performance email. Fine as a once-in-a-while palate cleanser between promo sends, but two roundup-style sends in one week (8/3's "1,000+ new arrivals" and this) risks flattening urgency for the whole cadence.

## What's working

- Hero image is genuinely elegant — the two sandals staged on the antique chair signals "curated editorial," not stock catalog.
- Two CTAs ("Shop Sandals" / "Shop Must-Have Shoes") give a clear next step without forcing a single narrow path.
- Six-item grid is diverse across price point, silhouette, and formality (raffia slide to strappy stiletto), so it functions as genuine shoppable inspiration.
- Designer name labels under each product (FENDI, JIMMY CHOO, etc.) let a brand-loyal shopper scan for favorites instantly.
- Layout is completely clean — no overlapping text, no broken images, no lorem ipsum.

## What's weak

- Zero price or offer anywhere in the email — no %, no $, no member pricing, nothing to convert browsing into buying.
- Subject and creative are 100% product-focused with no urgency; nothing time-bound to justify opening today versus next week.
- This is the second "roundup"/catalog-style send in three days (following 8/3's "1,000+ new arrivals") — the cadence is starting to blur together.
- No social proof, reviews, or "as seen on" signals despite the premium designer names, which is a missed trust lever for a $400+ sandal purchase.
- No loyalty/InCircle callout even though these are exactly the aspirational SKUs a loyalty member would want points visibility on.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a soft incentive line under the hero (e.g., "Free shipping on orders $150+" or InCircle points multiplier) — even without a discount, giving shoppers one concrete reason to act now would meaningfully lift clicks on a $500+ sandal purchase.
- 2. Swap one of the two generic CTAs ("Shop Must-Have Shoes") for something more specific tied to the grid, e.g. "Shop the Edit" — "Shop Sandals" and "Shop Must-Have Shoes" overlap in intent and split traffic without adding value.
- 3. Add a one-line price range or "starting at $X" under 1-2 hero SKUs to reduce sticker-shock friction before the click.
- 4. Space out roundup-style sends — this is the second in three days; alternate with the gift-card/percent-off plays to avoid the whole week reading as one long lookbook.
