# It’s here: Adidas x Willy Chavarria
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-10**
## Executive summary

- This is NM's launch email for the Adidas x Willy Chavarria capsule — pure editorial, no offer. The creative is confident: a single model shot in wide-leg black trousers and track jacket with a colorful emblem, anchored by the campaign line "Power Play" in a large serif. It earns the opening. What it fails to do is close. Both CTAs ("Shop New Arrivals" / "Shop Designer Collections") are generic category links — neither surfaces the Willy Chavarria collection by name, which means the email builds a moment and then hands you off to a hallway instead of a room.
- Compare this to NM's June 10 Chanel send, which also ran editorial-only: the Chanel email at least had two paragraphs of brand-heritage copy and a singular CTA. Here, the single sentence of body copy ("Discover fashion that changes the game...") does the work of a caption, not a sell. For a subscriber who already follows designer collabs, the subject line lands — but the body forfeits the momentum.
- Verdict: a well-art-directed email that undersells. The visual treatment is above-average; the conversion architecture is lazy.

## What's working

- **Subject line is sharp.** "It's here: Adidas x Willy Chavarria" names both brands, uses the launch signal "It's here," and stays under 40 characters — inbox-friendly and concrete.
- **Hero photograph is editorial-grade.** The wide-leg silhouette, emblem detail on the bomber, and all-black palette telegraph the collection's aesthetic without needing copy to explain it.
- **"Power Play" headline fits the brand DNA.** Adidas + Willy Chavarria's design ethos (athletic tailoring with social commentary) maps cleanly to that tagline.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no overlapping elements, footer is tidy — the email looks exactly as intended.

## What's weak

- **Both CTAs are disconnected from the launch.** "Shop New Arrivals" and "Shop Designer Collections" would appear on any NM email. Neither links to the Adidas x Willy Chavarria collection specifically.
- **Zero product depth.** No grid, no individual items, no prices — a single outfit on a single model doesn't give a shopper a reason to click *now* versus later.
- **No urgency or offer.** The word "capsule" implies limited availability, but the email never uses that as leverage. No "limited run," no "shop before it sells out," nothing.
- **Second editorial send from NM today.** Chanel Métiers d'art already went out June 10. Two editorial-only, no-offer emails in the same inbox session dilutes both — the subscriber reads them both as "NM is showing me pretty things" rather than "I need to act."

## Recommendations

- 1. **Replace both CTA buttons with one specific, collab-named link.** "Shop Adidas x Willy Chavarria" beats two generic ghost buttons every time. One CTA, high contrast, named destination.
- 2. **Add a product capsule grid below the hero.** Even 3–4 items (jacket, pant, sneaker, accessory) with prices turns a lookbook into a shop. The June 9 Designer Sale did exactly this and scored a 10.
- 3. **Add one scarcity signal in the body copy.** "Limited capsule" or "shop the drop while it lasts" gives the subscriber a reason to act on this email vs. saving it for later.
- 4. **Drop the app-download module.** The "Download the Neiman Marcus app" / "Match with a personal style advisor" service block sits right below the CTAs and competes with the conversion moment. Push it to the footer.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `Power Play: the new Adidas x Willy Chavarria drop is live`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `Adidas x Willy Chavarria is now at Neiman Marcus`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `Wide-leg silhouettes, bold tailoring, limited capsule — shop it now.`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `The Willy Chavarria collab just landed. See the full drop.`
