# Black Friday alert: extra 25% off select styles for up to 70% off
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-25**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, loud Black Friday send. The black hero with a red frame puts "EXTRA 25% OFF" and "UP TO 70% OFF" in huge serif type, with a white SHOP NOW button below. You can't miss the offer. It's the same visual system as the Nov 23 "3 ways to save" email and the Nov 24 "$1,500 off" send, so it's instantly recognizable as Neiman's Black Friday.
- The weak spot is the offer logic. "Extra 25% off select styles for up to 70% off" stacks two percentages with "select styles," "limited time" and "exclusions apply" around them. A reader can't tell what actually gets 25% off. The 3x3 grid is all designer names with no prices, no sale prices and no discount tags, so nothing proves the deal. The six category buttons at the bottom are generic. The deadline (12/2/26, 10 PM CT) appears only in the fine print.
- Judgement: a solid, on-brand, mid-quality promo. It would do better with a visible end date and prices on the products. This is the third Black Friday send in three days, so fatigue is a real risk.

## What's working

- The hero is high contrast. The offer is the first thing the eye lands on, and the black and red frame carries the Black Friday look.
- The SHOP NOW button is unambiguous, white on black, and centered.
- The 3x3 grid is tidy and well shot. It mixes dresses, outerwear, bags, boots, knits and denim, and the brand names are legible.
- The six category buttons split the email by women's and men's, so people can jump to their own category.
- The footer has app download, personal stylist and store-finder links, which add some service value.

## What's weak

- The offer is confusing. Two percentages are stacked with "select styles" between them, and it isn't clear which items get which discount.
- The deadline (Ends 12/2/26 at 10:00 PM CT) is buried in the fine print. The hero says only "Limited Time."
- The product tiles show only brand names. There are no prices, no markdowns and no per-item discount badges.
- Every tile is a different brand, so there is no single hero product. The grid feels scattershot.
- The category buttons ("SHOP WOMEN'S," "SHOP WOMEN'S CLOTHING") overlap, and there is no member or loyalty benefit.
- The hero is very tall, so little product shows on a mobile first screen.

## Recommendations

- 1. Clarify the offer and show the deadline in the hero. For example: "Extra 25% off sale styles, ends Dec 2." Drop one of the two big percentages, or make the relationship explicit: "Already up to 70% off, now an extra 25% off."
- 2. Put was/now prices or "-70%" badges on the product tiles. That proves the deal.
- 3. Replace the 9 mixed brands with 4 to 6 best sellers, or sort the grid by category with one hero item each.
- 4. Consolidate the buttons into clearly different categories (Women's, Men's, Shoes, Handbags) with no overlap.
- 5. Add an InCircle member or free-shipping line.
- 6. Space out the Black Friday cadence, or make each send offer something different.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Extra 25% off sale styles, ends Dec 2`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 70% off, plus an extra 25% off today`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Designer dresses, boots and bags marked down. Exclusions apply.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Ends Dec 2 at 10 PM CT. Shop A.L.C., FRAME, Vince and more.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible source has a few code-level defects: internal template variable documentation left in the delivered HTML, a viewport that blocks zoom, no `lang` attribute, and an invalid font-family keyword. The QA warnings (missing unsubscribe and auth headers, URL-heavy plain text, 29 unprobed tracking links) are mostly unverifiable here because of the relay and the truncated HTML.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **29 tracking links skipped.** The click-redirect domains were not probed, so I couldn't confirm that any destination resolves or lands on the intended page.
- **Plain-text part is URL-heavy.** 5,488 of 6,508 characters (about 84%) are URLs, which is above the 70% warning threshold. That points to long tracking URLs in the text part. It raises spam-filter risk and makes the text version hard to read.
- The truncated source doesn't show individual `href` values, so I can't assess malformed links, missing `target` or `title` attributes, or redirect chains.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom disabled.** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom on mobile, which fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
- **No document language.** `<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml">` has no `lang` or `xml:lang`, so screen readers can't pick the right pronunciation rules.
- **Invalid font-family keyword.** `.disclaimerText` uses `font-family: Arial, Helvetica, 'sans-serif'`. The quotes make `sans-serif` a literal font name instead of the generic fallback, so the stack has no real generic fallback.
- **Checkbox-hack mobile nav.** The CSS relies on `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu`/`.hamburger`/`.close` and `.mobileCont:checked~table`. Gmail and many Outlook builds don't support `:checked`. I can't see the fallback markup in the truncated source, so whether the nav degrades gracefully is unverified.
- **Exact-match class selectors.** Mobile rules use `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=hide]`, `*[class=mobile]` and similar. These only match when `class` is exactly that one value, so any element with a second class won't get the mobile styles. I can't verify this from the truncated source.
- **Generic `<title>`.** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has a trailing space and doesn't reflect the email's content.
- **Content before the DOCTYPE.** An HTML comment precedes the `<!DOCTYPE>`. Some legacy clients and webviews can fall into quirks mode when anything comes before the DOCTYPE. This is low risk but avoidable.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Internal template notes in the delivered HTML.** The pre-DOCTYPE comment contains the build notes: `REQUIRED variables: @email … @base_refid … @refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"") … Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)) … @uEm = Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", "uEm", …) @cmdid … CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW --> <!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->`. This exposes data extension names (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`) and field names to every recipient. It also suggests the template was published with the scenario-selection step unresolved.
- I see no unresolved `%%…%%` or `{{…}}` tokens in the visible portion. The remaining personalization (`@user`, `@emailid`, `@uEm`, `@cmdid`) sits in the truncated part, so I couldn't check whether those values appear in URLs.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe missing.** QA reports the header absent. It notes this may be an AgentMail relay artifact, so it is unconfirmed.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post missing (RFC 8058).** Same caveat. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require one-click unsubscribe, so this should be checked against the raw original message.
- **Authentication-Results not found.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown because the relay strips or rewrites it. This is not a confirmed failure.
- The footer's visible unsubscribe link and physical postal address are not in the truncated HTML, so I can't confirm either.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- **Not assessable.** All 29 tracking links were skipped and no `href` values are visible, so UTM presence and consistency, redirect targets, and landing page alignment with the "extra 25% off, up to 70% off" offer are all unverified.
- The `@refid`/`@base_refid` variables in the comment suggest campaign attribution runs through a `refid` parameter. I couldn't confirm it is on the links.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip the pre-DOCTYPE AMPscript/variable comment from the production template, and move the DOCTYPE to the first line.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta tag.
3. Add `lang="en"` and `xml:lang="en"` to `<html>`.
4. Change `'sans-serif'` to the unquoted generic `sans-serif` in `.disclaimerText`.
5. Check the raw message headers (not the relay copy) for `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, and SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment.
6. Shorten the tracking URLs in the plain-text part, or reduce the number of links there, to bring the URL ratio down.
7. Re-run the audit with the full HTML and with tracking redirects resolved. This covers the footer unsubscribe link and address, UTM and `refid` consistency, and the mobile nav fallback.
