# The June Edit: 1,000+ new designer styles
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-08**
## Executive summary

- This is Neiman Marcus's new-arrivals blast for June — a clean product grid framed by a bold black hero ("Brand New Styles Just Landed") that splits into Women's and Men's sections, each ending with a section CTA. The creative is confident and the brand marks are luxury-appropriate: Proenza Schouler, Miu Miu, Versace for women; Jacquemus, Fendi, Givenchy, Ferragamo for men. No price, no discount, no deadline.
- The email is well-executed at a craft level. The problem is strategic: after a relentless week — the "LAST CHANCE" gift card blast on June 7, the Beauty Event this same day, editorial sends on June 5 and 6 — yet another NM email with no offer and no urgency is asking a lot of inbox goodwill. "1,000+ new styles" is also not new: NM ran an effectively identical frame on June 5's getaway send and earlier in the week.
- Verdict: a solid discovery email that lands at the wrong moment in the cadence. It would have read better standalone; sandwiched between a gift-card urgency play and a GWP beauty event on the same day, it gets lost.

## What's working

- **Hero is visually decisive.** The black panel with white "Brand New Styles / Just Landed" creates clean contrast and stops the scroll.
- **Designer brand labels are prominent.** Showing Proenza Schouler, Miu Miu, Versace, Fendi, Jacquemus by name signals this is aspirational, not sale-rack noise.
- **Dual-gender split is cleanly executed.** Women's and Men's sections both have their own product grid + dedicated CTA, so a recipient can navigate immediately.
- **No render bugs.** All images loaded, no broken gaps, no overlapping text.
- **Subject line character count is mobile-safe** at 42 chars.

## What's weak

- **No offer, no price, no hook beyond "new arrivals."** With no discount or promo code, there's nothing to act on beyond browse curiosity — and NM's inbox has trained subscribers to expect offers.
- **Same-day conflict with Beauty Event.** Sending two NM emails on June 8 (this + the 9/10 GWP email) splits attention and dilutes both.
- **Products shown have no pricing or detail.** Just brand + product shot. Subscribers can't gauge whether this is relevant to their budget.
- **No social proof.** No "just dropped," no "selling fast," no star ratings — every item is anonymous inventory.
- **Cadence fatigue is real here.** This is at minimum the 7th NM email in four days. The June 7 "LAST CHANCE" gift card was a high-urgency send; following it with a no-offer browsing email the next day creates tonal whiplash.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick a lead product and give it a reason.** Feature one hero item (e.g., the Proenza Schouler dress) with a price and a one-line callout ("Just dropped — limited sizes") to create a pull toward the click.
- 2. **Move this email to a standalone day.** Don't co-send with the Beauty Event. Stagger by 48 hours minimum.
- 3. **Add a "just arrived this week" frame.** "1,000+ styles" is overwhelming; "12 new pieces worth bookmarking" or "Just in: this week's standout arrivals" feels curated.
- 4. **Tease one exclusive or one NM-only item.** A single "only at Neiman Marcus" callout justifies the email on its own.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Just landed: Proenza, Miu Miu, Fendi + 1,000 more`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New this week — designer arrivals worth bookmarking`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fresh drops from Proenza Schouler, Versace, Jacquemus and 50+ designers.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `This week's most-wanted new arrivals — shop before they sell out.`

## 1. Overview

This is Neiman Marcus's new-arrivals blast for June — a clean product grid framed by a bold black hero ("Brand New Styles Just Landed") that splits into Women's and Men's sections, each ending with a section CTA. ## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "The June Edit" (NeimanMarcus@e.neimanmarcus.com)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The send is a Salesforce Marketing Cloud deployment using AMPscript with several unresolved variable-scenario ambiguities in the comment block. QA catches two compliance-critical header gaps (List-Unsubscribe, DKIM/SPF) that are now enforced by Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender policy.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 tracking links skipped** — All click-redirect URLs routed through NM's tracking domain were excluded from HTTP probing, so broken-link status is unverified for the majority of CTAs.

**Plain-text part is 90% URLs** — 5,214 of 5,764 plain-text characters are raw URLs (QA finding). This is a strong spam-filter signal; ISPs that score the plain-text part independently may down-rank deliverability.

No issues found with href syntax in the visible HTML fragment.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Dual `<style>` blocks** — Responsive rules are split across two `<style>` elements (600 px breakpoint in the first block, 480 px/520 px in a second block). Email clients that parse only the first `<style>` block (notably older versions of Outlook.com) will miss the second set of rules, breaking the 2-column product grid on narrow viewports.

**Three commented-out `<meta name="viewport">` tags** — Only one viewport meta is active; the others (`width=320, initial-scale=0.29` and `width=1024`) are inert but indicate unresolved legacy experiments. No functional impact, but adds noise and risks accidental uncommenting.

**`'sans-serif'` quoted in font stack** — `.disclaimerText` declares `font-family: Arial, Helvetica, 'sans-serif'`. Quoting a generic font family name is invalid CSS; the fallback will not fire if Arial/Helvetica are absent.

**Interactive hamburger nav (CSS checkbox hack)** — The `:checked~` selector technique has near-zero support outside WebKit/Apple Mail. Clients like Outlook, Gmail app, and Yahoo will silently show no nav. Confirm a static fallback renders for non-supporting clients.

**`<title>` contains a trailing space** — `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>`. Minor, but some clients expose the title in preview or tab text.

No `alt` text quality can be assessed from the truncated source; recommend verifying all `<img>` tags carry meaningful `alt` values.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`@user` variable scenario is ambiguous.** The AMPscript comment block declares two competing definitions:

```
@user = Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", ...)  ← active
/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */                               ← commented out
```

If `@user` resolves to the raw `EMAIL_ID` integer and is appended to any outbound URL (e.g., as a query param), it exposes an internal subscriber identifier to third-party analytics. The hashed-email path is commented out with no explanation; this should be confirmed as intentional.

**`@uEm` and `@cmdid`** are looked up from `NM_CRM_Feed`. If either is null (subscriber not in the CRM feed), downstream URL construction may produce malformed or broken links. No null-guard or fallback is shown in the visible fragment.

**"CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW"** comment suggests a conditional block was expected but the actual conditional logic is not visible. If the wrong scenario was left active at deploy time, personalization segments may have received incorrect data.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent** (QA WARN) — Required under RFC 2369 and now enforced by Gmail and Yahoo for senders exceeding 5,000 messages/day. Missing this header increases the risk of bulk-folder placement and user-initiated spam reports.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent** (QA WARN, RFC 8058) — One-click unsubscribe is mandatory under Gmail/Yahoo 2024 bulk-sender requirements. Its absence means inbox providers cannot surface the native one-click unsubscribe UI, pushing users toward "Report Spam" instead.

**Authentication-Results header not found** (QA WARN) — SPF and DKIM pass/fail status is unknown through the AgentMail relay. Cannot confirm the send domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` is properly aligned with DMARC policy. This is the highest-deliverability risk in this send.

CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe link presence cannot be confirmed from the truncated source; should be verified in the full footer.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Cannot verify UTM coverage** — 26 tracking links were skipped by the HTTP probe. UTM parameter consistency (source, medium, campaign, content) across all 26 CTAs is unconfirmed. Any CTA that omits or mismatches `utm_campaign` will create a gap in attribution for this send.

**`@base_refid` / `@refid` in URLs** — The refid concatenation `CONCAT(@base_refid,"")` appends an empty string, which is a no-op. If additional suffix logic was intended (e.g., segment or test-cell suffix), it was not implemented.

No issues found with the domain used for links (`neimanmarcus.com`) based on the visible fragments.
