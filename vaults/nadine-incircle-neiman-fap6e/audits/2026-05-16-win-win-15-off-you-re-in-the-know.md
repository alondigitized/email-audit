# Win-win: 15% off & you’re in the know! ​
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-16**
## Executive summary

- This is a straightforward email-list welcome/loyalty play from Neiman Marcus: you joined the list, here's 15% off your first online purchase as a reward. The hero message — "You're on the List / ENJOY 15% OFF" — lands clearly above the fold, and the promo code is visible and readable. The structural logic is sound: benefit → code → social proof of what membership gets you → brand grid.
- The problem is the execution feels generic. The product grid below the fold (Veronica Beard, VEJA, Chloe, FRAME, Cing a Sept, Rag & Bone, etc.) reads like a brand dump rather than a curated edit relevant to *me*. There's no personalization signal — no "based on your browsing," no category hint, no seasonal angle. For a luxury retailer whose whole pitch is curation, this feels like a bulk broadcast dressed in nice typography.
- The email is clean and functional, but it doesn't feel premium. At Neiman Marcus's price point, the bar should be higher. This is a competent send that leaves points on the table.

## What's working

- **Hero offer is immediately clear:** "ENJOY 15% OFF" + promo code `NMWELC-419Z-RSG50NM` + "Start Shopping" button — all visible without scrolling.
- **Brand recognition is instant:** Neiman Marcus logo, clean serif typography, and recognizable luxury brand names (Chloe, Marc Jacobs, Rag & Bone) establish credibility quickly.
- **Benefit articulation under the fold:** The "By joining our email list, you'll receive" section explicitly lists personalized content, designer arrivals, trending shoes, and expert tips — good expectation-setting for a welcome email.
- **No render bugs:** Layout is clean, images load, no broken elements, no overlapping text visible.
- **Utility links at the bottom:** Find a store, Contact us, My account — present and readable.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is weak for a luxury brand:** "Win-win: 15% off & you're in the know!" uses bargain-bin energy phrasing ("win-win") that clashes with the Neiman Marcus positioning. The promo code adds a character in the subject that feels like a production artifact.
- **Brand grid is unsegmented:** Eight product images from eight different brands across women's, men's, and accessories — no coherent story, no gender/category personalization.
- **Model imagery is small and indistinct at email width:** The hero lifestyle photo (woman in field) doesn't feature any specific product; it's filler.
- **15% off is the floor expectation for a welcome offer** — luxury welcome bonuses elsewhere go 20%+ or offer a dollar amount. This may feel underwhelming to a prospect comparing options.
- **The promo code itself is buried in fine print length** — the code `NMWELC-419Z-RSG50NM` is long and complex; no "tap to copy" affordance visible.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject line entirely** — drop "win-win," lead with the concrete offer in cleaner language. The current phrasing is more QVC than Neiman Marcus.
- **Subject Alt A:** `You're in — 15% off your first order`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Welcome: your 15% off code is inside`
- 2. **Preheader should complement, not echo:** Currently the preheader appears to repeat the "you're in the know" theme from the subject rather than adding new information.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Use code NMWELC at checkout — shop new arrivals from Chloe, Rag & Bone, and more`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your exclusive welcome offer expires soon — shop designer favorites now`
- 3. **Segment the product grid** — even a basic gender/category split (women's RTW vs. accessories vs. shoes) would make the brand dump feel curated. A women's-only grid of 4 brands is better than 8 mixed brands.
- 4. **Make the promo code copiable / more prominent** — render it larger, in a distinct box, ideally with a "Copy code" tap target. Long alphanumeric strings are a friction point on mobile.
- 5. **Upgrade the hero image** — swap the generic lifestyle shot for a product-forward editorial image (a hero item, a key look for the season). Luxury emails earn the click with desire, not scenery.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "Win-win: 15% off" Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a standard SFMC AMPscript template with competent baseline HTML, but carries three notable technical risks: PII exposure via an uncommitted merge-token choice, missing alt text on all nine product images, and unverifiable compliance headers through the AgentMail relay.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Plain-text part URL density — FAIL**
QA measured 4,745 of 6,137 plain-text characters (77%) as URLs, exceeding the 70% spam-filter threshold. Likely caused by redirect-wrapped tracking URLs being dumped verbatim into the text part instead of being replaced with human-readable anchor text.

**27 tracking links unverifiable**
All click-redirect links were skipped during probing. UTM parameters, destination URL health, and correct redirect behavior cannot be confirmed. If any redirect chain returns a non-200 before the final destination, click attribution silently breaks.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**9 product images missing `alt` text — confirmed**
All product images from the promotional category (`cat86620752`) have no `alt` attribute:
```
https://neimanmarcus.com/promo-email/PROMOTIONAL/cat86620752/product1.png
… through product9.png
```
This fails WCAG 2.1 AA (1.1.1) and causes blank content for image-blocking clients (Outlook default, corporate proxies). For a promotional email where images *are* the product, this is a hard rendering regression for a large segment of B2B recipients.

**Three commented-out `<meta viewport>` variants left in `<head>`**
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
Dead markup; not rendering bugs, but signals poor template hygiene and increases parse weight.

**CSS hamburger nav uses checkbox hack**
The mobile nav relies on `:checked~` CSS selectors with no JavaScript fallback. Functional in modern WebKit, but fragile in Samsung Internet and some Android WebViews. Not a blocking issue but warrants regression testing.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`@user` variable has its hash assignment commented out — potential PII risk**

The HTML comment block reads:
```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */-->
```
The instruction above it says "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW," indicating this is a two-path template where a developer must uncomment the correct block before send. As delivered, `@user` is populated via:
```
Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", "EMAIL_ID", "EMAIL_ADDRESS", @email)
```
…which returns a plaintext `EMAIL_ID`, not a hashed value. If `@user` is appended to any tracking pixel or redirect URL (common for identity resolution), raw subscriber IDs are transmitted in URLs — a PII exposure in server logs, CDN logs, and browser history.

**AMPscript variable names exposed in HTML comment**
The entire variable scaffold (`@email`, `@base_refid`, `@refid`, `@user`, `@emailid`) is visible in rendered HTML source. While not a send-blocking issue, it leaks internal DE naming conventions and template logic to anyone who views source.

---

### 5. Compliance

| Check | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| `List-Unsubscribe` header | **WARN** | Not detected — may be stripped by AgentMail relay; needs raw header verification |
| `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) | **WARN** | Absent — one-click unsubscribe compliance for Gmail/Apple Mail unconfirmed |
| SPF / DKIM / DMARC | **WARN** | `Authentication-Results` header absent via relay; pass/fail unknown |
| Sending domain | OK | `e.neimanmarcus.com` is a dedicated ESP subdomain — structurally correct |
| Physical address in footer | Unverifiable | HTML truncated before footer; standard NM practice assumed |

The missing `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header is the highest-priority compliance gap: Gmail and Apple Mail now require RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe for bulk senders, and its absence can trigger inbox suppression.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

Cannot fully audit. All 27 click links are redirect-wrapped and were skipped by the HTTP prober. Specific gaps to verify manually:

- Whether UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, `utm_content`) are consistently present across all CTA links
- Whether the 15% off promo code is pre-populated or at least surfaced on the landing page for recipients who arrive without a cookie
- Whether the `@refid`/`@base_refid` campaign tracking value is passed through to the site's session or analytics layer

---

### 7. Recommendations

**P0 — Send-blocking risk**
1. **Resolve the `@user` hash scenario** before any future send. Confirm which AMPscript block is active and that `SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256` is uncommented if `@user` appears in any tracking URL parameter.

**P1 — Compliance**
2. **Verify `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers** via raw `.eml` inspection (not relay-captured copy). If SFMC is not injecting them automatically, add via Send Classification or a journey-level header override.
3. **Confirm SPF/DKIM pass** for `e.neimanmarcus.com` by pulling raw headers from a seed-account send.

**P2 — Accessibility / rendering**
4. **Add descriptive `alt` text to all 9 product images.** At minimum, product name + price. For image-blocked clients this is the only content these cells render.
5. **Fix plain-text URL density.** Replace bare redirect URLs in the text part with short descriptive text (e.g., `Shop now: https://...`). Target <50% URL character ratio.

**P3 — Template hygiene**
6. Remove the three commented-out `<meta viewport>` tags from `<head>` — they add noise and increase parse size with no benefit.
7. Strip the AMPscript variable comment block from the rendered HTML, or move it to a server-side comment that does not emit to the inbox.
