# But first, up to 70% off
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-02**
## Executive summary

- This is Neiman Marcus running the exact same "Extra 25% Off Last Call" creative for the third time in a week — same red hero block, same "EXTRA 25% OFF / SELECT SALE STYLES FOR UP TO 70% OFF / LAST CALL / SHOP SALE" stack, same white button, same 9-product grid opening with an Isabel Marant bag, as the 8/1 send ("Don't miss out on an extra 25% off Last Call styles," 7.00/10) and the 7/29 send before it. The subject "But first, up to 70% off" is the only meaningfully new element here — a cuter, more curiosity-driven hook than the last two literal restates — but it opens onto an identical body, so anyone who opened either prior email will recognize this instantly as a rerun.
- The execution itself is clean: clear red/white contrast, unambiguous CTA, a tidy 3x3 grid with visible designer names, and category shortcut buttons below. But cleanliness isn't the issue — fatigue is. Sending the same visual three times in under a week with only the subject line changed reads as low-effort segmentation, and it risks training subscribers to stop opening because they've "already seen this one."
- Judgment: solid mechanics, stale creative. The subject line variety without body variety is a half-measure — it may lift opens marginally but won't rescue clicks from anyone who already engaged with the near-identical 8/1 or 7/29 sends.

## What's working

- Clear, high-contrast hero with an unambiguous offer stack (25% off + up to 70% off) and a single unmistakable "SHOP SALE" button.
- "But first" subject line is a genuine curiosity hook, distinct from the flat "Don't miss out" phrasing used on 8/1.
- 3x3 product grid shows designer names (Isabel Marant, Balmain, Manolo Blahnik, etc.) directly under each image, giving quick browsing value.
- Category shortcut buttons (Women's Clothing, Handbags, Shoes, Kids, Dresses) below the grid give multiple entry points beyond the single hero CTA.
- No render bugs — images, text, and buttons all display cleanly with no overlap or breakage.

## What's weak

- This is a near-identical repeat of the 8/1 and 7/29 sends — same hero copy, same layout, same button placement, same starting product (Isabel Marant bag) — with only the subject line changed.
- "Exclusions Apply" sits directly under the CTA in small type, undercutting the boldness of the "70% OFF" headline the moment someone looks for the catch.
- No urgency or deadline visible anywhere in the rendered body — the fine print at the bottom mentions "Ends 8/31/26" but that's buried past the fold, not surfaced near the offer.
- Subject promises "up to 70% off" but the hero's real actionable number is the extra 25% off already-marked sale items — the framing overstates what's actually new.
- Cadence: this is the third near-identical "Last Call 25%" send in about a week, which risks looking like the brand ran out of new creative.

## Recommendations

- 1. Retire this exact hero/grid combination for at least a full cycle — reusing it a third time in a week is the single biggest drag on this send's performance regardless of subject line quality.
- 2. Surface the 8/31 deadline in the hero itself (e.g., "Ends soon" or a countdown chip) instead of burying it in bottom fine print — it's the one piece of real urgency in the whole email and it's invisible.
- 3. Swap the lead grid product on any repeat send — always opening with the same Isabel Marant bag across three consecutive emails signals a template, not a curated pick.
- 4. Move "Exclusions Apply" smaller/lighter or off to the side so it doesn't read as a direct rebuttal to the 70% headline right above the button.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Extra 25% off — before Last Call ends`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your 70% off styles are almost gone`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Handbags, dresses, and shoes — up to 70% off, ends 8/31`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New markdowns just hit Last Call. Shop before sizes sell out.`
