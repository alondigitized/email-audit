# Summer’s hottest swimwear
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-30**
## Executive summary

- This is a mid-season swimwear editorial from Neiman Marcus — clean, uncluttered, and confidently aspirational. The hero image (a model on a sandy beach in a white bikini) earns its real estate: the photography is genuinely good, and "Shore Up Your Beach Style" is a headline that actually sounds like it came from a writer rather than a template. Structurally, the email is well-organized: hero → women's swim grid → men's trunks CTA, with three distinct "Shop Swimwear" CTAs threaded through.
- The critical miss is what's absent: there is no offer. No price. No discount. No urgency. For a sender that just ran a heavy Designer Sale cycle (60% off, June 23–29) and a multi-touch gift card earn promotion, this lands as a pure editorial pivot with nothing commercial behind it. That's a deliberate strategy — NM cycles between offer-heavy and editorial sends — but it means the click signals are weak. A subscriber looking for a reason to buy will find photography but no hook.
- The email is competent and brand-consistent. It's not a bad send. But it's also not a send that will move units: no price, no offer, no member benefit, no deadline. For swimwear in late June — peak purchase window — that's a missed conversion opportunity.

## What's working

- Hero photography is strong: beach setting, model, warm light — genuinely on-brand for luxury swimwear
- "Shore Up Your Beach Style" headline is clever wordplay, tone-appropriate, and memorable
- Product grid is clean and well-structured — six swimsuit thumbnails for women, three trunk thumbnails for men, no crowding
- Section break between women's and men's is logical and navigable
- No render bugs visible — layout is clean, images load, text is legible throughout

## What's weak

- Zero pricing or offer — no discount, no incentive, no urgency mechanic of any kind
- All three CTAs say "Shop Swimwear" — zero differentiation, no specificity to product or style
- No loyalty/member benefit visible despite NM's InCircle program being a meaningful differentiator
- No brand names called out in the product grid — for a luxury retailer, hiding the designer label is a squandered signal
- Subject "Summer's hottest swimwear" is generic and could have come from any retailer; no NM-specific hook

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one price anchor or offer.** Even a single "from $X" or a member-exclusive benefit would give subscribers a commercial reason to click. This is the single highest-leverage change. Late June is peak swimwear urgency — shipping in without an offer is leaving conversion on the table.
- 2. **Surface designer names.** The product grid is anonymous. Calling out La Mer, Vilebrequin, or whoever is actually in the grid would justify the premium and differentiate from mass retailers.
- 3. **Differentiate the CTAs.** Replace all three "Shop Swimwear" with "Shop Women's Swim," "Shop Men's Trunks," and a specific designer or style — e.g., "Shop One-Pieces."
- 4. **Add an InCircle teaser.** A one-line "Members earn double points on swim through July 4" would add urgency and reward loyalty subscribers without requiring a discount.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Critical — unresolved variable comment block in deployed HTML:**

```html
<!--REQUIRED variables: @email ... @user ... @emailid ... @uEm ... @cmdid ...
CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW -->
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->
```

This comment block — including the `HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256` sentinel and the instruction `CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW` — is visible in the delivered source. AMPscript comments (`%%[/* */]%%`) are stripped server-side; HTML comments (`<!-- -->`) are not. The developer left implementation guidance in HTML comment syntax instead of AMPscript comment syntax. No PII leaks (values are not rendered), but:

- The SHA256 scenario selector is unresolved — it is unclear which `@user` path was actually active for this send.
- `@uEm` and `@cmdid` (CRM identifiers) are referenced but their substitution outcome is unverifiable from the output.
