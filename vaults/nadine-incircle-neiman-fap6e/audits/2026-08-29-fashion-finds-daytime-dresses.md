# Fashion Finds: Daytime Dresses
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-29**
## Executive summary

- This is a straight product-grid dress send — nine dresses, brand-name callouts (Et Ochs, Cinq à Sept, Simkhai, Acler, Staud, Alémais, Self-Portrait), no price anywhere, no discount, no urgency. It's the third variant of the no-discount, designer-catalog template this brand has leaned on repeatedly this week ("Tailored for any occasion" 8/28, "Insider's look" 8/27) — this time swapping the single-hero-model shot for a 3x3 flat-lay grid, which is at least a slightly different visual beat.
- The copy is tight and on-category ("Daytime dresses mean never having to mix, match, or overthink") and the grid itself is clean — no render bugs, good white space, legible product shots. But there's zero reason to click *today* versus any other day: no offer, no seasonal urgency, no price. It reads as a lookbook, not a marketing push.
- Compared to the $250-off sends earlier this week (8/26, 8/27, 8/29 morning), which scored 6-7 on the strength of a visible discount and deadline, this one caps out mid-pack — solid execution, weak incentive.

## What's working

- Clean 3x3 product grid with consistent gray backgrounds — no overlapping text, no broken images.
- Hero copy is specific to the category ("Daytime dresses mean never having to mix, match, or overthink") rather than generic brand fluff.
- Two immediate CTAs directly under the hero ("Shop Dresses," "Shop Contemporary") — no scrolling required to act.
- Designer name-drop line (Et Ochs, Cinq à Sept, Simkhai, Staud, etc.) signals curated/premium assortment for a NM shopper.

## What's weak

- No price, discount, or urgency anywhere — third "browse and admire" send in five days with the same missing ingredient.
- Nine near-identical strappy/flowy sundresses in a grid makes it hard for the eye to land on any one hero product — no visual hierarchy or "pick this one" signal.
- Brand-name row (Et Ochs / Cinq à Sept / Simkhai...) is unlinked plain text under the grid — reads like metadata, not a CTA.
- No sizing, fit, or availability info visible — for dresses specifically, fit is the #1 purchase-blocker and nothing here addresses it.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a value lever — even a soft one ("New markdowns on select dresses") would differentiate this from the last two no-discount sends and give a reason to click today instead of bookmarking for later.
- 2. Cut the grid from 9 to 4-6 and let 1-2 lead as a true hero shot (larger image, "as seen on" or styling context) — right now every dress competes equally for attention and none wins.
- 3. Make the designer name list clickable pills/links instead of static caption text — it's currently wasted real estate.
- 4. Add a one-line fit/sizing reassurance ("True to size, free returns") since sundresses are a common near-cart-abandon category.
