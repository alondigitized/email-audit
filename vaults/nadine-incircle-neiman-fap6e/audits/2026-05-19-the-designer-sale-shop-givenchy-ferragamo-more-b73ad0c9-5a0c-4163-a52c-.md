# The Designer Sale: Shop Givenchy, Ferragamo & more
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- This is the third Neiman Marcus designer sale email in roughly 48 hours — after the May 17 "exclusive access ends tonight" urgency play and the May 18 launch email that earned a 10/10 here. This send's job is to sweep up non-openers with brand-name subject bait (Givenchy, Ferragamo) and push them into the sale before momentum dies. The formula is identical to the launch: red hero, 50% off, 3×3 product grid, category CTA stack. It works, but it's running on fumes from the prior send's energy, not building on it.
- The one genuine craft problem: Ferragamo is named in the subject but doesn't appear in the nine-product grid. That's a bait signal that erodes trust if the subscriber scans the body looking for it and comes up empty. Givenchy does appear (row 2, right), which salvages it partially.
- By day 2 of a sale, a smart follow-up earns its place by adding something new — a price drop, a specific item, a closing-time countdown. This email adds none of that. It's the same creative with a different subject line, and subscribers who opened the launch email will feel it.

## What's working

- **Hero is fast and clear** — "LIMITED TIME / THE DESIGNER SALE / UP TO 50% OFF" lands the entire value proposition in three lines before the fold.
- **Brand-callout subject strategy** — naming Givenchy and Ferragamo in the subject is the right tactic for a second-wave retargeting send; it gives non-openers a more specific reason to engage.
- **Category CTA stack** — six segmented buttons (Women's Clothing, Men's, Women's Shoes, Men's Shoes, Handbags, Dresses) reduce friction by routing shoppers directly to their category.
- **"Before they're gone" scarcity line** — the copy above the grid adds light but credible sell-through urgency without fabricating a countdown.

## What's weak

- **Ferragamo is a ghost** — the subject promises it, the product grid doesn't deliver it. A subscriber hunting for Ferragamo will bounce.
- **No deadline specificity** — "LIMITED TIME" appears in the hero but there's still no date or hour. The May 17 email had a hard close; this one gives up that urgency lever entirely.
- **Creative fatigue** — the design is pixel-for-pixel the same as the May 18 launch. Loyal subscribers have seen this exact red box twice already. No new hook, no new visual.
- **No prices shown** — nine products from recognized luxury houses, zero price tags. A sale email should make the savings feel concrete at a glance.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Show one actual price** — even one "Was $895, Now $447" in the grid would make "up to 50% off" feel real rather than promotional boilerplate.
- 2. **Add Ferragamo to the grid** — swap one of the nine panels for a Ferragamo item so the subject delivers on its promise.
- 3. **Reintroduce a hard deadline** — the May 17 email used "ends tonight" and scored 9/10. This send abandons that playbook for no reason. Even "Sale ends Sunday" would reactivate urgency.
- 4. **Vary the hero visually** — a model shot or a flat-lay editorial hero instead of the identical red lockup would signal to repeat openers that this is new content, not a re-send.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Nine designers. Real prices. Don't leave these in your cart.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "The Designer Sale" Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The template is functional SFMC AMPscript boilerplate but ships with several unresolved scaffolding artifacts, including a critically commented-out merge token assignment. Compliance headers are either absent or not captured by the relay, creating CAN-SPAM/RFC 8058 exposure.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**26 tracking links unverifiable**
All click-links route through an SFMC redirect domain. The QA probe correctly skipped them, but UTM parameter coverage and landing page continuity cannot be confirmed without decoding at least a sample set.

**Plain-text body is 87% URLs**
`5140 / 5910` characters in the plain-text alternative are URLs. This is well above the ~40% threshold where bulk spam filters begin penalizing. Evidence: QA deliverability warn `Plain-text is >70% URLs`.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Three conflicting, commented-out viewport meta tags in `<head>`**
```html
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, user-scalable=no" />-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=320, initial-scale=0.29">-->
<!--<meta name="viewport" content="width=1024">-->
```
Dead code from iterative testing left in production source. No rendering breakage today, but a future editor could uncomment the wrong one.

**Two separate `<style>` blocks with overlapping breakpoints**
Breakpoints at `max-width: 600px` (block 1) and `max-width: 480px` / `max-width: 520px` (block 2) are split across separate `<style>` tags. Gmail and some Outlook clients process only the first `<style>` block; `.ImgSize` / `.ImgSize2x2` responsive rules in block 2 may be silently dropped.

**`<title>` has a trailing space**
```html
<title>Neiman Marcus </title>
```
Minor; some preview-text scrapers surface this verbatim.

**No `alt` text visible in truncated source**
The audit cannot fully verify `alt` attributes on product images given truncation, but should be confirmed — screen readers and image-blocked clients depend on them.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`@user` SHA-256 hash assignment is double-commented out — likely broken**
```html
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */-->
```
This is wrapped in both an HTML comment (`<!-- -->`) and an AMPscript block comment (`/* */`). If `@user` is referenced downstream in tracking link parameters (common for identity stitching), it will resolve as empty string, silently corrupting those links. Evidence: the template header says `@user` is a required variable; the only assignment found in source is disabled.

**Development scaffolding comments shipped to production**
```html
<!--REQUIRED variables: @email ... CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW -->
```
These are internal template instructions visible in rendered source. Not a security risk, but exposes internal variable names and SFMC DE structure (`NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`) to anyone who views source.

**`@refid` concatenation is a no-op**
```
@refid = CONCAT(@base_refid,"")
```
Concatenating with an empty string adds no value; likely a placeholder where a suffix (e.g., variant ID) was intended but never populated.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent**
QA warn: `List-Unsubscribe header not found`. Gmail and Yahoo have required this header for bulk senders (>5k/day) since February 2024. Its absence risks inbox placement degradation or bulk-folder routing.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) absent**
QA warn: one-click unsubscribe not supported. Required by the same Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy alongside `List-Unsubscribe`. Both headers should be present together.

**Authentication-Results header not captured**
QA warn: `Authentication-Results header not found`. SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status is unknown through the AgentMail relay. Should be confirmed at the MTA level to rule out alignment failures on `e.neimanmarcus.com`.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 26 outbound links go through an SFMC click-tracking redirect, so UTM parameters cannot be confirmed from source inspection alone. The following should be spot-checked by decoding 2–3 redirect chains:

- Confirm `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` are present on final destination URLs
- Confirm the `@user` / `@uEm` identity parameters are non-empty (directly tied to the broken assignment in §4)
- Confirm sale landing pages are live and not returning 302→404 (designer sale pages are frequently time-gated)

---
