# The Roundup: Showpiece Bags
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- This is NM's second "The Roundup" bag-focused send in three days — July 12 brought "The Roundup: Saint Laurent's spot-on bags" (7/10), and now "Showpiece Bags" lands on July 15. The format is identical: editorial hero shot, headline, two-button CTA row, six-bag product grid, clean footer. The photography is genuinely beautiful — that opening shot of the cream box bag on ornate lacquered furniture is arresting — but the email is pure aspiration with no price, no urgency, no discount. After a week of relentless Last Call fire-sale emails, a palate-cleanser editorial is smart brand positioning, though the execution has a crucial weakness.
- The fatal flaw is the split CTA: "Shop Crossbody Bags" sitting next to "Shop New Arrivals" forces an unnecessary choice and dilutes conversion intent. Neither button has a dominant visual weight. Compared to the July 12 YSL Roundup, this one also loses the brand specificity that made that subject line punchy — "Showpiece Bags" is a category label, not a destination. The result is a pretty email that will earn opens from bag loyalists but lose clicks to ambiguity.
- Verdict: above-average editorial execution, below-average conversion architecture. A one-CTA revision and a sharper subject would elevate this immediately.

## What's working

- **Hero photography** is genuinely editorial — the cream structured bag on the ornate surface reads luxury-magazine quality, not catalog.
- **Product grid diversity** is strong: red crescent bag, leopard print, quilted blue, black heart-shaped, and two muted camel/gray shapes cover a wide aesthetic range in one scroll.
- **"Shop Crossbody Bags" CTA** is specific and actionable — verb + noun, not the lazy "Discover" or "Explore."
- **Layout is completely clean** — all six product images load, brand labels visible beneath each bag, no broken images or overlapping elements.
- **Format recognition** — "The Roundup" banner above the headline signals a curated editorial series, which NM subscribers who've seen this format before will recognize and trust.

## What's weak

- **Split CTA kills focus** — "Shop Crossbody Bags" and "Shop New Arrivals" compete equally; the second button is generic noise that pulls attention away from the more specific first.
- **No price, no offer, no reason to click today** — pure aspiration with zero commercial hook. After a week of 70% off Last Call promos, subscribers trained on discounts have no anchor here.
- **"Showpiece Bags" is vague** — the July 12 Roundup named Saint Laurent in the subject; this one doesn't call out a single brand despite six featured in the grid, wasting the brand-authority advantage NM carries.
- **No brand labels are legible at mobile size** — the small text beneath each bag image would be unreadable on a phone screen.
- **Cadence overlap** — two Roundup bag emails in three days stretches the format's freshness. Subscribers who noticed July 12's YSL bag focus may see this as re-runs.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Drop "Shop New Arrivals," make one CTA dominant** — "Shop Showpiece Bags" or let the primary CTA match whatever the hero bag links to. One action, one click. This is the highest-impact change.
- 2. **Name at least one brand in the subject** — the July 12 subject ("Saint Laurent's spot-on bags") outperformed this one because the brand name is a search term in the subscriber's brain. Pick the most recognizable bag in the grid and lead with it: e.g., "The Roundup: Loewe, Gucci & more showpieces."
- 3. **Add prices or a light editorial reason to act now** — even "New this week" or a visible price on the hero bag gives the aspirational reader a hook to rationalize the click.
- 4. **Increase brand label legibility** — slightly larger or bolded brand name under each bag; this is the one product detail a handbag buyer cares about most.
- **Subject Alt A:** `The Roundup: Loewe, Gucci & the bags worth staring at`
- **Subject Alt B:** `6 showpiece bags — one of them is yours`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New arrivals that belong in a museum case (or on your arm).`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `From heart-shaped Gucci to quilted Bottega — the week's best bags.`

## Technical Audit — Neiman Marcus "The Roundup: Showpiece Bags"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses Salesforce Marketing Cloud AMPscript with a multi-scenario variable setup that contains an unresolved template branch decision. Compliance posture is degraded: one-click unsubscribe headers are absent, and authentication results are unverifiable through the relay.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**36 click-redirect URLs unprobed** — All outbound links pass through a tracking/redirect domain; HTTP probe was skipped for all 36. No broken-link verification is possible without resolving final destinations.

**Plain-text part is 86% URLs** — QA reports 3,138 of 3,647 plain-text characters are URLs. This typically results from auto-generated plain-text that strips HTML but retains raw tracking links. Spam filters (SpamAssassin `URI_PHISHING` rules, Proofpoint) penalize high URL-to-text ratios in plain-text parts. A human-readable plain-text alternative should be authored separately.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport** — `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` disables user pinch-zoom. This fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and is a violation in some jurisdictions. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Three conflicting viewport tags in comments** — Three additional `<meta name="viewport">` tags exist as HTML comments (commented out), including a width=320/scale=0.29 variant and a width=1024 variant. While inert, this indicates unresolved viewport strategy; a future template edit risks accidentally un-commenting the wrong variant.

**CSS checkbox-based interactive nav** — The hamburger menu and carousel rely on `:checked` pseudo-class (`.mobileCont:checked~table`). Support is limited: Gmail (web) strips `<input type="checkbox">` and associated `:checked` rules. Users in Gmail will see a non-functional interactive element or a fallback layout depending on how the `display: none` defaults are set.

**`<title>` tag has a trailing space** — `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>`. Minor, but it appears verbatim in browser tabs if the email is opened in a web view.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Unresolved template branch for `@user`** — The source contains:

```
<!--/* SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256 */ -->
```

This is an AMPscript line wrapped inside both an HTML comment and a `/* */` block comment, making it inert. The surrounding instruction reads "CHOOSE ONE SCENARIO BELOW," implying the template requires a manual branch selection before deployment. If `@user` was intended to carry a hashed email value for downstream personalization or link decoration, it is currently unset.

**`@uEm` and `@cmdid` from CRM Feed lookup** — Both variables are populated via `Lookup("NM_CRM_Feed", ...)`. If a subscriber is absent from `NM_CRM_Feed`, these resolve to empty strings. Any link or logic that depends on them (e.g., personalized offer IDs) would silently drop the parameter rather than error visibly.

**`@emailid` has a dual-path definition** — The comment documents two resolution paths for `@emailid`: from the sendable DE directly, or via `Lookup("NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated", ...)`. No conditional guard (`%%[IF ...]%%`) is visible in the truncated source to confirm which path is active. A misconfiguration here would send a null email ID to downstream analytics.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent** — QA confirms this header was not captured. Gmail and Yahoo have required `List-Unsubscribe` (and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` for one-click) for bulk senders (>5K/day) since February 2024. Missing headers increase the probability of being routed to spam or having the account flagged for non-compliance.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) absent** — One-click unsubscribe (machine-readable `mailto:` or `https:` POST endpoint) is missing. This is distinct from a footer unsubscribe link; mailbox providers use this header to surface a native "Unsubscribe" button. Its absence degrades sender reputation scoring on Gmail and Yahoo.

**`Authentication-Results` header unverifiable** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown because the AgentMail relay did not surface the header. Sender domain is `e.neimanmarcus.com` (ESP subdomain). Cannot confirm DMARC alignment. If the sending domain is not DMARC-aligned with `neimanmarcus.com`, bulk sender requirements are unmet.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

Cannot verify UTM parameters or landing page alignment because all 36 outbound links passed through tracking redirects and were skipped by the HTTP probe. UTM coverage, parameter values, and landing page match are unaudited.
