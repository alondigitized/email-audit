# Shop new fall collections & earn a $75–$1,500 gift card
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-18**
## Executive summary

- This is the strongest send from Neiman Marcus this week — a real, tiered spend-and-earn promo (spend $500, earn a $75 gift card up to spend $10,000+ for $1,500) laid out in a clean table, stacked on "TRIGGER: Includes Beauty & Fragrance" and Triple InCircle Points for members. Compare it to the repetitive Last Call reruns (8/13, 8/15, 8/16 "ICYMI") and the two zero-discount editorial sends (8/15, 8/16) — this is the first email in the brand history that pairs a genuinely new mechanic (gift-card-back tiers) with a fresh nine-product new-arrivals grid spanning women's ready-to-wear, denim, handbags, shoes, beauty, and menswear.
- The mint-green hero with the black CTA button reads clean and on-brand, and the spend/earn table is genuinely useful information rendered legibly. Where it loses points: the offer is fundamentally a spend-more mechanic, not a discount, so the "reward" requires a large basket before it pays off meaningfully — a $500 spend nets $75 back, which is real but not urgent-feeling. The "CLAIM YOUR 15% OFF" block mid-email (for joining the text list) is also a bit of a bait — it visually reads like a second offer stacked on the gift card promo, which could confuse a scanning reader.
- Solid, well-differentiated execution for this brand's cadence this week — the best candidate for a click of anything reviewed so far.

## What's working

- Clear, scannable spend/earn table gives concrete tiers ($500→$75 up through $10,000+→$1,500) — no ambiguity about the mechanic.
- "Includes Beauty & Fragrance" and "Limited Time | Code NMTREND" sit right under the headline, giving both scope and urgency in one glance.
- Nine-product grid is diverse (apparel, denim, handbags, shoes, beauty, menswear) rather than single-category, broadening appeal.
- Triple InCircle Points banner in black gives loyalty members a clear added reason to act.
- No render bugs — every product image, price tier, and CTA renders cleanly with strong visual hierarchy from hero to grid to category buttons.

## What's weak

- The reward requires spending real money to earn — there's no straight discount, so the offer is less immediately compelling than "70% off."
- "CLAIM YOUR 15% OFF" text-signup block appears directly below the shopping grid and could be mistaken for a second, stacked discount rather than a separate list-join incentive.
- No visible pricing on any of the nine featured products, so the reader can't gauge whether items even clear the $500 threshold.
- Exclusions ("Exclusions Apply") are flagged in the hero but not detailed until the very bottom — a scanning shopper won't know sale/clearance items don't count until they've already committed to the offer mentally.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a starting price or "from $X" under at least the hero products so shoppers can immediately map items to spend tiers — this directly supports the core mechanic and would lift click-through most.
- 2. Separate the 15% text-signup block visually (different color treatment, more spacing) from the gift-card offer so it doesn't read as a stacked second promo.
- 3. Move a one-line exclusion note ("excludes sale, gift cards") closer to the hero table instead of only in the footer legal block.
- 4. Test leading with a lower/easier tier ("Spend $250, get $50") if data shows the $500 floor is suppressing opens among lower-AOV segments.
