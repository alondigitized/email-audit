# Up to 40% off: These First Call styles will sell out...
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-19**
## Executive summary

- This is the fourth First Call markdown send in about a week. It follows Oct 14, Oct 16 ("Up to 40% off: Shop new markdowns on fall styles") and Oct 17 ("First Call for up to 40% off fall styles"). The red-and-black "FIRST CALL® UP TO 40% OFF" hero is the same one the brand has been running, and the offer is easy to read. The subject promises "will sell out," but nothing in the body backs it up.
- The body is a plain 3x3 grid of nine designer products with no prices. A shopper can't see what 40% off means on an Alice + Olivia dress or a Polo Ralph Lauren coat. The six red category buttons at the bottom are clear, but they only lead to department pages. The footer fine print ("25-40%", "select categories") also undercuts the "up to 40%" headline.
- Judgement: a clean, competent markdown blast, but it's repetitive and has no urgency or price proof. At this cadence a subscriber will skim past it.

## What's working

- The red hero with the black frame is high contrast, and "UP TO 40% OFF" is the first thing you read.
- There is one clear primary CTA, a black "SHOP NOW" button directly under the offer.
- The nine-product grid has consistent white-background imagery and brand names, so it's quick to scan.
- The six category buttons give a way in for each shopper (women's, men's, shoes, handbags, kids, accessories).
- There are no render bugs. Images load, the layout is clean, and the nav and logo are tidy.

## What's weak

- Nothing supports the "will sell out" claim. There is no deadline, no low-stock cue and no end date.
- The product grid shows no original or sale prices, so the discount is never shown on an item.
- The hero is nearly identical to the Oct 16 and Oct 17 sends, which makes this the fourth near-duplicate this week.
- "Exclusions Apply" sits in the hero, and the footer says "25-40% off... select categories," which hedges "up to 40%."
- The "Shop coveted fall styles—now up to 40% off" line just repeats the hero, and the product tiles have no individual links or CTAs.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put sale prices on the grid, such as "$398 $259," with a "Save 35%" tag. Showing the markdown on the product is the biggest lift.
- 2. Add a real deadline or scarcity cue, such as "Ends Sunday" or "Sizes selling out," or drop "will sell out" from the subject.
- 3. Differentiate the creative from the last three sends. Use a different hero or a featured designer, and refresh the products.
- 4. Replace the generic "SHOP NOW" with specific copy like "Shop women's markdowns."
- 5. Move the exclusions into one place and make them clearer.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The visible source has a few code-level problems: an internal template comment is left in the delivered HTML, zoom is disabled in the viewport meta, and a font stack is malformed. The truncated HTML and the relay-limited QA data leave the compliance, link and personalization checks unverified. The QA run reported 0 hard failures and 4 warnings.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified:** 30 tracking links were skipped by the HTTP probe because they are click-redirect domains. Redirect resolution, destination status codes and UTM handling can't be confirmed from this data.
- **Plain-text part is mostly URLs:** QA reports 5675 of 6647 characters (about 85%) are URLs, over the 70% threshold. This points to long tracking URLs in the text alternative. It raises spam-filter risk and makes the text part hard to read.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Zoom disabled:** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1">` blocks pinch-zoom on mobile. This fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).
- **Malformed font stack:** `.disclaimerText` uses `font-family: Arial, Helvetica, 'sans-serif'`. The quotes make `sans-serif` a literal font name, not the generic fallback.
- **Brittle mobile selectors:** the responsive rules use exact-match attribute selectors such as `*[class=contenttable]`, `*[class=mobile]` and `*[class=hide]`. They stop matching if an element ever carries more than one class.
- **Checkbox-hack hamburger nav:** the `.navbar-checkbox:checked~.menu` rules need `:checked` support. Gmail and several webmail clients lack it, so the menu will not open there. Whether a non-interactive fallback exists can't be seen in the truncated source.
- **Minor:** `<title>Neiman Marcus </title>` has a trailing space. The XHTML DTD URL is `https://www.w3.org/...`, which is harmless.
- **Not assessable:** image alt text, table `role` attributes, and the language attribute (the `<html>` tag shows only `xmlns`, with no `lang`) can't be fully checked. The body is truncated. The missing `lang` on `<html>` is confirmed from the visible tag.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- **Template comment leaked into the sent email:** the HTML opens with a developer comment containing Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC) variable setup. It names `NM_Subscriber_New_Consolidated`, `NM_CRM_Feed`, and the fields `EMAIL_ID`, `uEm`, `cmdid` and `EMAIL_ADDRESS`. It also has `SET @user = HASHED_EMAIL_SHA256`. The comment is not rendered, but it exposes internal data-extension and field names to anyone who views the source. The CRM-feed lookup keys suggest a hashed-email identifier is passed in links. Strip the comment at send time.
- No unresolved tokens such as `%%...%%` or `{{...}}` are visible in the truncated source.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058):** both are reported missing. The QA tool itself says the AgentMail relay may strip them, so this is **unconfirmed**. Check the original message headers. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require both for senders of this volume.
- **`Authentication-Results`:** not found, and SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. The QA tool attributes this to the relay. It is not a confirmed failure, and the From domain `e.neimanmarcus.com` can't be checked here.
- The footer unsubscribe link and the physical postal address are not visible in the truncated source, so they can't be verified.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not assessable. The tracked links were not resolved, and no UTM parameters or landing-page URLs are visible in the truncated HTML. The `@refid`/`@base_refid` variables in the leaked comment suggest campaign attribution runs through a `refid` parameter, not UTMs. That is inferred from the comment, not confirmed in a link.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Strip the SFMC variable/lookup comment block from the production template, since it leaks data-extension and field names.
2. Remove `maximum-scale=1` from the viewport meta.
3. Fix `'sans-serif'` to unquoted `sans-serif` in `.disclaimerText`.
4. Add `lang="en"` to `<html>`.
5. Check the raw delivered headers for `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, and SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment, bypassing the relay if it strips them.
6. Shorten the plain-text version, or reduce the link count, so URLs fall well under 70% of the text.
7. Re-run link validation against the redirect destinations, and confirm the final landing URLs carry the expected attribution parameters.
8. Confirm a fallback for the hamburger nav in clients without `:checked` support.
