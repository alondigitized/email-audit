# Homepage snapshot · www.quince.com · 2026-07-06
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- This is my homepage. Quince knows exactly who they're talking to — clean editorial aesthetic, "$50 Cashmere" pinned right in the nav, and a calm "THE NEW AMERICANA" hero that reads like a mood board, not a mall sale. The brand is pushing a summer-into-fall wardrobe pivot right now, and honestly, I'm here for it.

## What's working

- **"$50 Cashmere" pinned in the nav** — this is Quince's whole value proposition in three words, and putting it in the top nav means I see it before I see anything else. Instant credibility signal for someone like me who has already done the factory-source research.
- **"Free shipping & easy returns for 365 days"** — the orange strip at the very top is impossible to miss. As someone who returns aggressively on sizing drift, 365-day free returns is a genuine decision factor and it's the first thing I read.
- **"THE NEW AMERICANA" hero** — the styling is aspirational but attainable. Three women in muted linens and summer whites feels editorial without being unapproachable. This is the exact register I want from a brand I trust.
- **Category grid (Dresses, Tops, Sweaters, Skirts)** — round category tiles with lifestyle photography are scannable and warm. I can get to exactly what I want in one tap. Sweaters right there on row one is a quiet-luxury win.
- **App download banner** — "Exclusive drops & more on the app / Luxury essentials, honestly priced" reinforces the brand promise and the word "drops" is the right hook for a shopper who reads every email.

## What's weak

- **No loyalty or account recognition** — I'm a repeat Quince customer with a closet that's 70% their product, and this homepage has zero indication the brand knows I exist. No "Welcome back," no rewards balance, no wishlist tease. The heart icon is in the nav but it's not contextualized.
- **Hero CTA is too small and unreadable** — the dark button under "THE NEW AMERICANA" exists but the copy is illegible at mobile size. I genuinely cannot read what it says from this screenshot. That's a tap I won't take because I don't know where I'm going.
- **No urgency layer** — the hero has no "New Arrivals" badge, no "just dropped," no quantity signal. Quince does limited runs and collaborations but none of that energy is visible here. For someone who monitors emails for new collabs, this page gives me no reason to act now.
- **"Jewelry" and "Furniture" in the same category row as "Shoes"** — the product mix breadth is fine, but surfacing Furniture in the above-fold category grid dilutes the apparel focus. It makes the page feel more department-store than curated closet.

## Recommendations

- **Make the hero CTA legible** — increase font size and button padding on the hero CTA; right now it disappears on mobile. Swap generic copy for something specific like "Shop the Americana Edit" so I know the destination before I tap.
- **Add a personalization strip** — a thin rail below the hero that reads "Your recently viewed" or "Back in stock: items you saved" would make returning customers feel seen without redesigning the page.
- **Inject urgency into "$50 Cashmere"** — add a small "New colorways just dropped" badge or "(Limited quantities)" next to the nav item. Quince already has the factory story; lean into scarcity cues that feel honest, not gimmicky.
- **Swap Furniture out of the above-fold category grid** — replace it with "Cashmere" or "Knitwear" as its own tile so the first eight categories I see are all wearable. Furniture can live one scroll lower.
