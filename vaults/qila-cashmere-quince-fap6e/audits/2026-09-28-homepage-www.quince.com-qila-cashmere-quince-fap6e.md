# Homepage snapshot · www.quince.com · 2026-09-28
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-09-28**
## Executive summary

- I land on Quince and immediately get walled off by an email-capture modal before I even see the site — which, fine, I get it, but it's the first thing my eyes have to deal with. Behind it I can see moody editorial shots (red slip dress, all-black look) and a clean category-circle rail, so this still reads like the quiet-luxury-on-a-budget brand I already shop. Right now it's pushing a "$40 off your order" email signup, not a specific product story.

## What's working

- **The "$50 Cashmere" nav tab** — this is my whole shtick, so seeing it pinned front and center in the nav bar (not buried) tells me the brand knows its audience.
- **Free shipping & easy returns for 365 days banner** — as an aggressive returner, this is the single most reassuring line on the page.
- **The moody editorial hero peeking around the modal** — red silk dress, black tonal look, sunglasses indoors — that's the quiet-luxury mood I'm here for, not loud logos.
- **Category-circle rail** (Sweaters, Bags, Jewelry, Shoes, etc.) — clean, scannable, feels like an edited closet rather than a dumped catalog.

## What's weak

- **The $40-off modal eats the entire above-the-fold real estate.** I can't see the actual homepage hero copy, can't judge the real headline, can't get to nav until I dismiss it.
- **The fine print undercuts the offer** — "$250 or more, discount only eligible in the app" is a bait-and-switch feel for anyone browsing on mobile web (like me, right now).
- **Gender dropdown on the modal is vague** ("GENDER" as placeholder, no options visible) — feels like an extra friction step before I even get the discount.
- **No loyalty/rewards callout visible** — I've shopped here before, and there's nothing acknowledging that; it treats me like a brand-new visitor.

## Recommendations

- Let me X out or see a size-appropriate "skip" without losing the hero — right now the modal fully occludes the editorial imagery that's doing the real selling.
- If the $40 off only works in-app, say that in the headline of the modal, not buried in 8pt fine print below the button.
- Swap the generic gender/email capture for something that reflects returning shoppers — e.g., "Welcome back, here's what's new since your last order."
- Surface the cashmere pricing story ($50 vs. luxury markup) as a banner strip, not just a nav tab — that's the hook that actually converts someone like me.
