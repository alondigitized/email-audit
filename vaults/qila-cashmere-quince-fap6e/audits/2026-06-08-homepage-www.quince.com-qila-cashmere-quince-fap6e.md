# Homepage snapshot · www.quince.com · 2026-06-08
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-06-08**
## Executive summary

- This is exactly the homepage I expect from Quince — clean, European-minimalist aesthetic, no discount noise, everything screams understated quality at a price I can actually justify. Right now they're pushing summer entertaining dressing ("Best dressed guest") and they haven't forgotten that I'm here for the $50 cashmere, which is literally in the nav. It's quiet luxury done right, and it feels like my closet already.

## What's working

- **"$50 Cashmere" as a nav tab** — this is the whole reason I'm here, and they surfaced it before I had to scroll or hunt. Brilliant above-fold placement.
- **"Free shipping & easy returns for 365 days" sticky banner** — as someone who returns aggressively when sizing drifts, that 365-day returns window is a direct conversion signal for me.
- **"Best dressed guest" hero** — the copy is minimal and aspirational without being corporate. It reads my language: I'm going to a wedding in July and this lands.
- **App download banner at the very top** — "Exclusive drops & more on the app / Luxury essentials, honestly priced" is brand voice in two lines. I'm already on mobile; this is a smart moment to prompt the download.
- **Category grid (Dresses, Bedding, Tops, Sweaters, Jewelry, Linen, Shoes, Skirts)** — immediately scannable, no guesswork. I can reach cashmere sweaters in two taps from here.
- **Clean, uncluttered layout** — no countdown clocks, no red sale banners, no "HURRY LIMITED STOCK" panic copy. This is the anti-Amazon experience I pay for.

## What's weak

- **No returning-customer recognition** — I'm in the top 5% of Quince customers by lifetime spend and the homepage has no idea who I am. No "Welcome back," no recently viewed, no loyalty status. That's a miss.
- **Hero subtext is illegible at phone scale** — below "Best dressed guest" there's small copy I can barely read. If that's a promo detail or category description, it's lost.
- **Nav is cut off on the right** — "Wom" is truncated; I can't see the full nav category. On mobile this creates friction.
- **No cashmere product in the category grid** — I see Sweaters as a circle but there's no dedicated "Cashmere" tile in the visual grid, even though "$50 Cashmere" is in the nav. The visual grid and the nav feel misaligned.
- **No social proof above the fold** — no review count, no "4.8 stars from 200K+ reviews," nothing. For a DTC brand competing on trust, that's an unused card.

## Recommendations

- **Make the hero subtext legible** — either increase font size, reduce the subtitle to 6 words max, or remove it entirely. At mobile resolution it's visual noise, not information.
- **Add a personalization layer for returning users** — even a simple "Welcome back, Qila — your size is XS" or a "Recently viewed" strip above the category grid. This is a logged-in experience gap.
- **Add a cashmere tile to the visual category grid** — "$50 Cashmere" in the nav is smart, but a dedicated grid tile with a product shot (a classic crewneck, the Mongolian color palette) would close the loop visually and drive taps from people who missed the nav.
- **Drop a trust badge or review count** — one line beneath the free-shipping banner: "4.9 stars · 400,000+ reviews" would cost zero real estate and convert hesitant first-timers while reinforcing my decision to stay.
