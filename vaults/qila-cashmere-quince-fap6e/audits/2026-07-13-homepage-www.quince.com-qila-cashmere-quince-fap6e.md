# Homepage snapshot · www.quince.com · 2026-07-13
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-07-13**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is my homepage. Quince is doing what it always does — clean neutrals, honest price anchoring, and a Euro-summer aesthetic that I basically live in from June through September. Right now they're leading with "Euro Summer Style" and the $50 cashmere nav tab is front and center, which means they know exactly who opens this app eleven times a week. It's understated, which I respect — no screaming sale banners, just quiet confidence.

## What's working

- **"$50 Cashmere" as the first nav tab** — I know exactly what I'm getting at Quince and leading with the factory-direct price promise is the whole brand in three characters. Love it every time.
- **"Free shipping & easy returns for 365 days" orange strip** — 365-day returns is the reason I return aggressively when sizing drifts. Seeing it above the fold removes all hesitation.
- **"Euro Summer Style" editorial card in the hero** — Linen neutrals, Italian-ish vibes, the season is right. This is my Pinterest board made shoppable.
- **Category tiles (Dresses, Tops, Sweaters, Skirts)** — One tap to get to exactly what I want. Clean circular icons with zero visual noise.
- **App banner is dismissible** — The X button is there. They're not holding me hostage to install the app before I can browse.

## What's weak

- **Hero collage is too fragmented** — Four panels (woman in white, sunglasses product shot, linen pants, editorial card) with no obvious reading order. On mobile, a mosaic layout makes me work harder than I should to find the point.
- **No returning shopper hook** — I'm 70% Quince by closet volume. There's no "welcome back," no recently viewed, no wishlist shortcut in the hero area. I had to find the heart icon in the nav to know my wishlist exists.
- **Sunglasses panel feels off-brand** — The product-flat shot of sunglasses in the hero collage reads more fast-fashion catalog than quiet luxury. It breaks the linen-and-cashmere mood the rest of the collage is building.
- **Hero text is tiny** — "Euro Summer Style" and the subheading copy on the editorial card are nearly unreadable at mobile viewport. I had to squint to confirm what it said.
- **No reviews or social proof visible** — I know Quince has strong ratings; none of that is visible in the first scroll.

## Recommendations

- **Replace the four-panel collage with a single full-bleed lifestyle hero** — One model, one mood, one headline. "Euro Summer Style — Mongolian Cashmere. Italian Linen. $50." That does more work than four competing tiles.
- **Add a persistent returning-shopper strip** — A small "Your wishlist (12 items)" or "Back in stock: your size in the Mongolian Cashmere Crewneck" personalization hook above the categories would convert me instantly.
- **Swap the sunglasses flat lay for a layered outfit shot** — Show cashmere over linen, styled together. That's the Quince purchase pattern for someone like me — the ensemble sell, not individual SKUs.
- **Increase the hero card type size** — The "Euro Summer Style" headline needs to be legible without zooming. Bump it 40% and add a single short subhead: "Linen, cashmere, silk. Honestly priced."
