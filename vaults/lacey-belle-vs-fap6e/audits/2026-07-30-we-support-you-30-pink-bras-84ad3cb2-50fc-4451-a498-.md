# We Support You: $30 PINK Bras
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-30**
## Executive summary

- This is PINK's "Top-Drawer Takeover" bra event, and the hero is genuinely one of the better-built openers this brand has sent recently — a clean 3x3 grid of bra product shots with "LAST DAY / $30 PINK BRAS" dropped into the center tile. It's tidy, on-brand, and reads instantly. The subject line's "We Support You" pun pays off on-theme too, even though the hero headline itself ("TOP-DRAWER TAKEOVER") doesn't literally echo the subject.
- Past that hero, though, this is the same kitchen-sink pattern flagged in every recent PINK audit (July 25, 26, 27): six-plus stacked "LAST DAY" / "LIMITED TIME" offers (bras, sweats, tees, panties, bags, a spend-more discount) all fighting for the same urgency claim in one email. When everything is "last day," nothing is.
- Solid hero, diluted body. This lands as a competent-but-familiar PINK send — worth opening for the bra price point, less compelling to click past the first offer.

## What's working

- The hero grid (9 product photos + centered offer tile) is the cleanest hero composition seen in this brand's recent sends — no clutter, clear price, one obvious "SHOP NOW."
- Subject's "We Support You" bra pun is a nice, on-category hook that's more memorable than a flat "$30 bras" subject would be.
- Preview text ("Plus, the $20 Tees and $35 Fleece to go on top") genuinely adds new info instead of repeating the subject.
- Loyalty touchpoints (Rewards $25 Off box, Cardmember Month callout) are present near the footer for repeat shoppers.
- No visible render bugs — images load cleanly, no overlapping text or broken layout anywhere in the scroll.

## What's weak

- "LAST DAY" is stamped on four separate offers (bras, sweats, tees implied, panties) in a single email — this is the same urgency-stacking problem called out in the July 25/26/27 sends, and it's now escalated to nearly every module.
- The hero headline ("TOP-DRAWER TAKEOVER") doesn't match the subject line's framing, so the pun that got the open isn't reinforced visually.
- Fine print ("Excludes Marshmallow Bras, third party styles and clearance," "Orig. up to $49.50 each") undercuts the clean $30 headline the moment you read past it.
- "SHOP NOW" is used as the CTA on nearly every module — it's not wrong, but it's generic and gives the eye no reason to prioritize one offer over the next five.
- This is at least the fifth near-identical "LAST DAY / kitchen sink" structure from PINK this week — cadence fatigue is real for a subscriber who's opened the last few.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the number of "LAST DAY" claims to one — pick the hero offer (bras) as the actual urgency moment and let the other modules run as "New" or "Also on sale" without competing deadlines. This is the single biggest lever; urgency only works if it's scarce.
- 2. Make the hero headline pay off the subject's pun directly (e.g., swap "TOP-DRAWER TAKEOVER" for something that echoes "We Support You") so open and hero feel like one idea.
- 3. Replace generic "SHOP NOW" with offer-specific CTA copy on at least the hero module — "Shop $30 Bras" costs nothing and sharpens the click intent.
- 4. Surface the exclusions/fine print closer to the offer or drop the "Orig. up to" framing for a flat "was $49.50" — vague pricing language reads as evasive next to a bold $30.
