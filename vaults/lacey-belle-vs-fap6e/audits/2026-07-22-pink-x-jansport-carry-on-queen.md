# PINK x JanSport: Carry On, Queen 👸
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- This is PINK's "Wednesday Drop" format being handed over to a brand collab: PINK x JanSport. The hero executes the premise cleanly — lifestyle models, summery energy, JanSport backpacks as the star product, and the "HOT & UNBOTHERED" headline carrying real brand conviction. The "Wednesday Drop" sender framing is smart positioning — it creates appointment-viewing cadence that last week's daily panty blast couldn't deliver.
- But PINK does what it did on July 16's "What We Do Best": it promises a focused story in the subject and then immediately buries it under a kitchen sink. Scroll past the hero and you hit a $10 beauty module, a 25% off $50+ sitewide discount, the Brow Shop, PINK Colorado Flare, the Summerhouse Bar, and lingerie — all unrelated to the JanSport collab. A subscriber who opened expecting a collab story gets a clearinghouse instead.
- The JanSport angle is genuinely fresh IP and the back-to-school timing is sharp. This could have been a tight, collab-first email that converts well and builds brand equity in a new category. Instead it's another overstuffed Wednesday digest that lets a good partnership idea leak out the bottom.

## What's working

- The PINK x JanSport hero is a genuine creative break from the beauty/panties run — new silhouette, new product category, strong lifestyle energy
- "Wednesday Drop" sender framing builds editorial appointment-viewing habit rather than blast-promo fatigue
- "Carry On, Queen" is playful, brand-consistent Gen Z voice — works well against the JanSport visual
- JanSport partnership is well-timed for back-to-school season and extends PINK into the accessories/bags category
- The $10 full-sized beauty offer is specific and low-friction — concrete value at a glance

## What's weak

- Subject promises a focused collab; body delivers a 6-module kitchen sink — the same structure that undermined July 16's "What We Do Best" email, and it trains subscribers that PINK subject lines over-promise
- The 25% off $50+ minimum is sitewide, not collab-specific — the discount is real but feels disconnected from the JanSport story
- "Wednesday Drop" urgency lives only in the sender name, not in the email body — there's no "today only" or deadline copy to close the loop
- Colorado Flare and Summerhouse Bar modules have zero narrative connection to JanSport, making the email read as a repurposed digest with a collab veneer
- "SHOP NOW" CTA is used across every module — no copy specificity to the collab itself

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to the collab in the body:** The first two modules should be PINK x JanSport only — products, price point, link. Move beauty/brow/lingerie to a footer strip or a separate send entirely.
- 2. **Tie the discount to the collab:** "25% off your collab haul" or "save on your JanSport bundle" gives the discount a reason to exist in this email. If it's sitewide, still anchor the copy to the collab context.
- 3. **Add deadline language inside the body:** "Wednesday only" or "drops this week" needs to appear in-email, not just implied by the sender name.
- 4. **Make CTA copy collab-specific:** "Shop the Collab" or "Get the Bag" beats generic "SHOP NOW" for a partnership launch that deserves its own voice.
- 5. **Lead with social proof:** One UGC image or a "Spotted on campus" framing would give the JanSport collab earned credibility that a sitewide discount can't provide.

### 1. Technical Summary

Email sent via Salesforce Marketing Cloud (ESP domain `e1.victoriassecret.com`) with Merkle Intelligence open tracking and 10 Dotomi/Conversant cookie-sync retargeting pixels. QA recorded a 50% pass rate driven primarily by pervasive HTTP mixed-content on third-party pixel infrastructure.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Duplicate open-tracking pixel**
The SFMC open beacon fires twice — once in a `<div>` immediately after `<body>` and a second time inside the AMPScript content block:
```
https://click.e1.victoriassecret.com/open.aspx?ELGQSY5K7GKU5KDK2OZ4WRE6LM.70250&d=70250&bmt=0
```
Duplicate firing inflates open-rate metrics for this send (`70250`).

**19 click links unverifiable**
All CTAs route through `click.e1.victoriassecret.com` redirect URLs. QA skipped HTTP probing on all 19. UTM parameter population cannot be confirmed from source alone — must be verified via post-click landing page inspection.

**10 Dotomi retargeting pixels**
`http://ads.dotomi.com/cookieredir/2482/pub1.php` through `pub10.php` fire on open. All use HTTP; all lack alt text. These are third-party audience-sync pixels — separate from click tracking.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Mixed content — 13 HTTP image sources**
Modern email clients (Gmail, Apple Mail) proxy or block non-HTTPS images. Affected sources:

| Asset | Domain |
|---|---|
| `o.gif` (Merkle MI beacon) | `http://mi.victoriassecret.com` |
| Dotomi login pixel | `http://login.dotomi.com` |
| `pub1.php`–`pub10.php` (×10) | `http://ads.dotomi.com` |

These will silently fail in clients that enforce HTTPS-only image loading, breaking retargeting audience sync entirely.

**17 images missing `alt` text**
Every tracking/retargeting pixel lacks `alt=""`, plus one content image:
- `5bcec3ac-502d-4d8f-b48c-0a3d20e6e3c2.png` (hosted on `image.e1.victoriassecret.com`) — a product/content image served without alt text, which degrades screen-reader experience and images-off rendering.

Decorative/tracking pixels should at minimum carry `alt=""` (empty string) rather than omitting the attribute; a content image needs descriptive alt text.

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0" />
```
Disables pinch-to-zoom. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and is blocked outright by iOS 10+, which ignores this setting — but it remains an accessibility anti-pattern and may affect Android WebView-based clients.

**Empty `<title>` inside body**
```html
<title style="display: none; max-height: 0px; overflow: hidden;"></title>
```
Placed inside the body as a preheader hook, but the element is empty. Some clients (notably Gmail on Android) may surface this as blank preheader text, displacing the intended preview snippet.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not captured**
QA found no `List-Unsubscribe` header. This may be a relay-capture gap (AgentMail), but if the header is genuinely absent, the email fails RFC 2369 and Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender requirements (mandatory for >5k/day senders since 2024).

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent**
No RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe header detected. Gmail and Yahoo now require this for bulk senders; its absence risks deliverability penalties and inbox filtering.

**SPF/DKIM status unverifiable**
`Authentication-Results` header not present in relay capture. Cannot confirm SPF/DKIM pass for `e2.vspink.com` → `e1.victoriassecret.com` sending chain. This is a relay-capture limitation but blocks full deliverability verification.

**Third-party retargeting pixels — GDPR/CCPA exposure**
10 Dotomi pixels fire on open with a shared cookie token:
```
a4edea106b333e696054ee7efded08d13b357adf450709a7b066a2e459aafc6fe=1
```
This token appears identical across all 10 `pub*.php` requests, suggesting it is a subscriber or session identifier. If EU/CA subscribers are in this send cohort, this constitutes cross-site tracking that must be covered by consent records.
