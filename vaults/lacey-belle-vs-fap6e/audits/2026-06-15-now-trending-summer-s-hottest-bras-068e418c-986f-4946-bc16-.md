# Now Trending: Summer's Hottest Bras
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-15**
## Executive summary

- This is VS's "trending" frame applied to the bra category — an editorial spin meant to give the Semi-Annual Sale blitz a fresh angle by labeling styles as "Now Trending." The hero opens on Balconette Bras with a clean pink lifestyle image and a bold header, which is the right call after days of discount-first messaging. For the first scroll, the email holds together: three bra styles get named callouts (Balconette, T-Shirt, Lace), each with a "Shop Now" link, and the hierarchy is legible.
- Then VS does what VS always does: pivots mid-email. After three bra-style cards, it drops into Panty Packs from $25, then the full Semi-Annual Sale banner with the Extra 20% Off Sale coda — and then spills into the Brand Map/beauty/gift grid that has appeared in nearly every send this cycle. By the bottom third, "Now Trending: Summer's Hottest Bras" has become another catch-all promotional dump.
- The framing is better than the June 13 "Four Words: The World's Best Bras" send (which promised focus and immediately scattered), but the structural problem is identical. This email has a strong top half and a weak, repetitive bottom half. The subject is the most on-brief line VS has written in this entire SAS run — specific, seasonal, trend-forward — and the email half-delivers on it.

## What's working

- **Hero is immediately on-subject.** "Now Trending: Balconette Bras" is visible above the fold with a pink lifestyle model image — rare discipline for this brand.
- **Three named bra styles with individual shop links** (Balconette, T-Shirt, Lace) give the "trending" frame real editorial structure and click targets.
- **Subject line is the strongest VS has written in this SAS run** — specific category, seasonal modifier, trend language all in one short phrase.
- **Sale discount layer (Extra 20% Off Sale) is visible** mid-email as a reinforcing discount signal for anyone who scrolls.
- **No visible render bugs** — images load, layout holds, no broken placeholders.

## What's weak

- **The "trending" editorial frame collapses by mid-email.** Panty Packs from $25 and the full SAS banner have nothing to do with "Summer's Hottest Bras" and dilute the subject line's promise.
- **No price anchor in the bra section.** Bra styles are named but no price or discount is visible in the top half — the offer clarity that made the June 14 "$25 Coupon badge" hero work is absent here.
- **Bottom third is the same grid VS has recycled since June 10** — Brand Map, beauty, gift cards. This send is indistinguishable from five prior sends in the lower scroll.
- **"Shop Now" is the CTA on all three bra cards** — generic copy that doesn't carry the trend language forward ("Shop Balconette," "Shop Lace" would be sharper).
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** This is at least the 6th SAS-adjacent bra-focused send in nine days. The trend framing freshens the subject but the body copy doesn't earn a new open.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Keep the bra editorial frame all the way through.** Drop the Panty Pack module and the full SAS dump. A "Now Trending" email should resolve cleanly with 3–4 style cards and one CTA to the bras sale page. Panties can hit tomorrow.
- 2. **Add a price signal to each bra card.** Even "from $45" or "Up to 60% off" under each style name. The editorial frame and the sale don't need to fight — merge them at the card level.
- 3. **Rewrite CTA copy to match the trend frame.** "Shop Balconette," "Shop T-Shirt Bras," "Shop Lace" instead of three identical "Shop Now" buttons.
- 4. **Subject Alt A:** `Now Trending: The 3 Bras This Summer Actually Needs`
- 5. **Subject Alt B:** `Balconette, T-Shirt, Lace — Summer's Top 3 Bras Are On Sale`
- 6. **Preheader Alt A:** `Up to 60% off the styles everyone's wearing right now.`
- 7. **Preheader Alt B:** `Trending bras, Semi-Annual Sale prices. Shop before they're gone.`

## Technical Audit — Victoria's Secret "Now Trending: Summer's Hottest Bras"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email has a well-established ESP infrastructure (Salesforce Marketing Cloud via `e1.victoriassecret.com`) but carries three categories of real issues: HTTP-only third-party ad pixels that will be blocked by modern clients, missing compliance headers at the relay layer, and a malformed JSON-LD block that will fail schema validation.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Duplicate open pixel**
The open-tracking beacon fires twice in the rendered HTML:
```
https://click.e1.victoriassecret.com/open.aspx?WDIUCRMAUSNUTEH3QDCIELKVXE.70258&d=70258&bmt=0
```
First occurrence is in `<body>` before the container table; second is inside the `#AMPScript` div. This double-fires the open event and inflates open-rate metrics.

**19 click-tracking links skipped**
All outbound links route through `click.e1.victoriassecret.com`. Destination URLs and UTM params could not be verified — see Section 6.

**HTTP dotomi retargeting pixels (11 images)**
All `ads.dotomi.com` cookie-sync pixels use `http://`:
```
http://ads.dotomi.com/cookieredir/2482/pub1.php?...
http://login.dotomi.com/ucm/UCMController?dtm_com=2...
```
Gmail, Outlook, and Apple Mail proxy or block mixed-content images. These pixels are likely silently failing for the majority of recipients on modern clients, meaning retargeting audience sync is broken in practice.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0" />
```
Prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. This is an accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4) and is blocked entirely by iOS Safari 10+, so the restriction has no effect on Apple devices but remains a flag for Android WebView renders.

**`<meta>` and `<style>` placed after body content begins**
The HTML 4.01 Transitional DOCTYPE is declared, but the `<meta viewport>` and `<meta content-type>` tags appear *after* a `<table>` with a `<script>` block has already opened inside `<head>`. This means the head is malformed — parsers that encounter a block element (`<table>`) inside `<head>` will implicitly close `<head>` and open `<body>`, causing the meta tags to be treated as body content. Viewport scaling may not apply correctly in strict-parse environments.

**`<title>` element in body**
```html
<title style="display: none; max-height: 0px; overflow: hidden;"></title>
```
The `<title>` is used as a preview-text suppression hack but placed in `<body>`, which is invalid. It's empty, providing no preview text benefit. Some clients will render it as visible whitespace.

**11 images missing `alt` text**
All dotomi pixels plus one content image (`5bcec3ac-502d-4d8f-b48c-0a3d20e6e3c2.png`) have no `alt` attribute. Screen readers will announce the full URL or filename for the content image.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**No unresolved merge tokens detected** in the truncated source. The `<div id="AMPScript">` wrapper is present (Salesforce MC AMPScript block), indicating dynamic content is served server-side before send — cannot confirm full resolution without the complete rendered output.

**JSON-LD duplicate `@type` key**
```json
{
  "@type": "DiscountOffer",
  "@type": "PromotionCard",
  "image": "",
  "url": ""
}
```
JSON does not allow duplicate keys. The first `@type` is silently overwritten by the second. `image` and `url` are empty strings. Gmail's Promotions tab schema parser will reject this block entirely — no promotional annotation (deal badge, expiry, logo) will surface. This is a missed inbox-placement enhancement.
