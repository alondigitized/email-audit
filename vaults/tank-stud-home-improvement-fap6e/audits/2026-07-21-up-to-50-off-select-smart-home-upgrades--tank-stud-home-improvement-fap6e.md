# Up to 50% OFF select smart home upgrades💡
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- My first reaction scrolling this subject in my inbox: "Smart home upgrades" — I'm thinking Lowe's is going to try to sell me an Alexa speaker when I've got a vanity rough-in half done in my master bath. That's my automatic guard going up. I'm a Pro rewards guy who shops for recessed cans, PVC fittings, and tile spacers — not Ring cameras and Fire TV sticks. But I open it anyway because I've been a MyLowe's member for years and the 50% off line is at least a real number.
- Inside the email, the hero confirms my suspicion: Alexa, Ring, Blink, Fire TV — straight consumer electronics. I'm already scanning past it. Then I hit the middle and things get marginally better: up to 35% off what looks like ceiling fans, 30% off lighting, 40% off bathroom vanities and mirrors. Now *that's* a reno-relevant angle. If I had been handed that as the lead, this is a different email. But it's buried under smart home gear I have zero interest in.
- I'm not clicking anything on the smart home front. I *might* click the ceiling fan or vanity offer if I'm actively spec'ing out a room. I won't forward this — yesterday's patio email (40% off, outdoor power equipment) was more my speed and I barely acted on that one. Mark as read and move on.

## What's working

- **MyLowe's Money Days loyalty banner** — seeing "3X points on qualifying purchases" front and center makes me pay attention as a member. That's actual cash back on my account.
- **The discount numbers are real and big** — 50%, 40%, 35%, 30% are all in the email; Lowe's isn't being coy with a wimpy "save up to 10%."
- **Ceiling fans + vanity lighting buried mid-email** — if that had been the hook instead of Alexa gadgets, this lands right in my project wheelhouse.
- **Email renders cleanly** — no broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds up. Lowe's rarely screws this up and they didn't here.
- **Flash Sale section adds urgency** — "Flash sale is live!" suggests time sensitivity, even if it doesn't give me an end date.

## What's weak

- **Hero is Amazon consumer electronics dressed up as home improvement** — Ring, Alexa, Blink, Fire TV are Best Buy aisle products. I'm here for the lumber, fixtures, and power tools. Wrong hook for me.
- **Too many separate offers at once** — smart home + ceiling fans + lighting + vanities + flash sale + Hisense. Scatter-shot; nothing commits. Yesterday's patio email had a cleaner "here's the category, here's the deal" focus.
- **Flash sale has no deadline visible** — "is live" tells me nothing. Is it dead in 2 hours? 2 days? I'm not going to take a chance on something I can't timeline.
- **Hisense at the bottom feels like a paid placement that doesn't belong** — a refrigerator/TV alongside my reno email breaks trust in the curation. It's pure ad filler.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Re-lead with the reno content.** Lead with ceiling fans, vanity lighting, and bathroom fixtures — all things that show up in real weekend project lists. Smart home can be section two.
- 2. **Put a clock on the flash sale.** "Flash sale ends tonight at midnight" would get me to the CTA immediately. "Is live" is not urgency.
- 3. **Ditch or quarantine the Hisense ad.** Or at least give it a "Sponsor" label. Mixing partner ads into editorial deal emails corrodes trust.
- 4. **Tighten the CTA copy.** "Shop ceiling fans" and "Shop vanity lighting" beats three "Shop now" buttons that all look identical.
- **Subject Alt A:** `35% off ceiling fans + 50% off smart home — this weekend`
- **Subject Alt B:** `MyLowe's Money Days: 40% off bath + smart home at half price`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fans, vanity lighting, Ring devices — now with 3X points for members`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Flash sale is live. Ceiling fans, bath vanities, and more through the weekend.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Confirmed from source:**

**a. Duplicate `local()` font hints (redundant, harmless but sloppy)**
Every `@font-face` block declares the same local name twice:
```css
src: local('Fellix Regular'), local('Fellix Regular'), url('...')
```
The duplicate `local()` hint is a no-op — the second copy is never reached. Affects all 7 declared faces.

**b. DIN Next fonts reference two different scaffold versions**
```
Fellix (regular):  lowescdn.com/scaffold/1.4.4/fonts/din/din-next.woff
Fellix Bold/Black: lowescdn.com/scaffold/1.2.0/fonts/din/din-next-bold.woff
                                           ↑ older version
```
Version inconsistency across the same font family. If the CDN eventually purges `1.2.0`, bold/black weights silently fall back to system font.

**c. Only `.woff` declared — no `.woff2`**
All `@font-face` declarations serve `.woff` only. `.woff2` is supported in every modern email client that supports web fonts at all, and offers ~30% smaller payloads. Not a rendering bug, but a missed optimization.

**d. Overly broad `ExternalClass *` selector**
```css
.ExternalClass * { line-height: 100% }
```
The wildcard applies `line-height: 100%` to every descendant of any `.ExternalClass` element. This can collapse line-height on deeply nested tables in Hotmail/Outlook.com and is more aggressive than the safer per-element rule used just above it (`p, span, font, td, div`). The two rules conflict on covered elements.

**e. Mobile media query uses `-webkit-min-device-pixel-ratio:0` as a client filter**
```css
@media screen and (min-width: 375px) and (max-width: 600px) and (-webkit-min-device-pixel-ratio:0)
```
This triple-condition query targets WebKit-only rendering at that exact size range. The `-webkit-` DPR hack is unnecessary in 2026 and excludes Samsung Internet (which dropped the prefix) and newer Chrome-based clients that no longer honor it. The styles inside (`mobile-hidden`, `drop`, etc.) may silently fail to apply on some Android clients.

**f. `.mobilehide` rule sets `color: #fff` on a hidden element**
```css
a.mobileshow, .mobilehide { display: none; color: #fff; }
```
`color` on a `display:none` element is inert but suggests a copy-paste error. Low risk.
