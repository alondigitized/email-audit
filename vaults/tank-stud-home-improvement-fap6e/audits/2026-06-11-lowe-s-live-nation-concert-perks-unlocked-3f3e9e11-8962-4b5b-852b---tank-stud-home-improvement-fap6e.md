# Lowe’s & Live Nation = concert perks unlocked✨
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-11**
## Executive summary

- Opened this and did a full-stop double-take. I'm on MyLowe's because I need to know when Milwaukee tools go on rebate, when lumber pricing shifts, or when they're running a deal on fasteners before I start drywalling a basement — not because I want tickets to see a legacy rock band from lawn chairs. Six emails from Lowe's in about a week, and this is what comes after grills and kitchen refreshes and a free toy box workshop. A Live Nation partnership. Okay.
- Here's the thing: the email is actually well-built. The dark concert hero, the silhouette crowd, the logos — it looks legitimate, and the member perks (two-for-one tickets, complimentary lawn chair rentals) are specific enough that I understand what I'm being offered without clicking. That's good email execution. But it's targeted at a version of me that doesn't exist in this database — the concert-going lifestyle segment, not the reno-phase hardware shopper they've been emailing all week.

## What's working

- Hero image is clean and polished — dark sky, crowd silhouette, dual branding with Live Nation holds up visually
- Member-exclusive framing ("Members get exclusive access") is the right hook — makes my loyalty card feel like it has off-aisle value
- Perk details are readable without clicking: "complimentary lawn chair rentals" and "two-for-one tickets at select events" tell me exactly what's on the table
- Preview text adds specificity the subject doesn't already say — that's how a preheader is supposed to work
- Email renders clean with no broken images or layout weirdness

## What's weak

- Complete persona miss — I'm on this list because I renovate things on weekends, not because I'm booking summer concert dates; whoever wrote the segmentation logic treated "MyLowe's member" as a monolith
- Triple "Learn more" CTAs with no differentiation — I don't know which one matters and none of them tell me what I'm actually clicking into
- No deadline anywhere — "for the first 50 concerts" is a qualifier, not urgency; without a date I mentally file it and never come back
- This is Lowe's send #6 in roughly a week; the concert pivot after grills, kitchen, and toy workshop emails reads like someone in marketing hit the wrong list
- No bridge back to the store — a standalone entertainment perk with zero connection to a project or tool deal feels like a misdirected ad, not a loyalty reward

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment the list** — lifestyle offers like this belong to outdoor/patio/entertaining buyers, not the broad homeowner/contractor base; send it to people who've browsed concert or outdoor living categories
- 2. **Rewrite all three CTAs** — "Claim your lawn chair rental" and "Get 2-for-1 tickets" are specific actions; "Learn more" is a way to lose people who already know enough to want to click
- 3. **Add a deadline** — "Summer concerts through Labor Day" or "Valid on first 50 shows, ends July 31" gives me a reason to act rather than defer indefinitely
- 4. **Add a secondary section that bridges to hardware** — a "While you're at it, save on [outdoor/project category] this weekend" block earns this email's place in a contractor's inbox instead of landing like a wrong-number text

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header — not confirmed (WARN):**
The QA relay did not capture this header. If it is absent at delivery, this is a compliance gap: Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender requirements (enforced since Feb 2024) mandate `List-Unsubscribe` for senders exceeding 5,000/day to Gmail addresses. `lowes@e.lowes.com` almost certainly qualifies.
