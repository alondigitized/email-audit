# Few slots left for MrBeast Workshop 🎉
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-24**
## Executive summary

- I'm five emails deep from Lowe's inside of a week — came off the $2,110 appliance suite push on Monday, the "your to-do list? Done" email on Sunday, Father's Day on Saturday — and now it's Wednesday and I've got "MrBeast Workshop" in my inbox. I had to read that twice. MrBeast. The YouTube guy with the insane stunts and the Feastables candy. At Lowe's. Saturday morning. That's not my weekend. That's my neighbor's eight-year-old's weekend.
- I understand the play. Lowe's has been running Kids Workshop events for years — smart foot-traffic move. Partnering with a creator that big is a real coup for them. But none of that is for me. I'm a MyLowe's member because I'm tracking lumber costs and loading up on project supplies, not registering my (probable teenager) for a $14 craft event. Whatever kid demo they're chasing, my purchase history should've sorted me out of this send.
- The email is clean, the CTA is obvious, no broken images. But I closed it without clicking and briefly considered forwarding it to my buddy with a 9-year-old before deciding that felt like too much effort. Mark as read, move on.

## What's working

- **"Few slots left" + a specific date is real urgency.** Saturday June 27 with scarcity language — if this were relevant to me, that combo would work.
- **$14 is front and center in the hero.** No digging. Offer is stated, commitment level is clear, boom.
- **"Register now" is a competent CTA.** Verb. Noun. Done. No "Explore the journey" garbage.
- **Clean layout, no render problems.** Lowe's brand is tight and this email stays in that lane — readable hierarchy, no broken assets.

## What's weak

- **Demographic whiff.** Cartoon Ferris wheel, kids-club branding, MrBeast — nothing here signals it was sent to a guy with a tool belt and a weekend project list.
- **MrBeast is not a hook for me.** I know who he is. I don't care about him at Lowe's. If the subject had said "Kids Workshop — Build something this Saturday for $14," I might have forwarded it. "MrBeast" as the lead tells me this isn't segmented for people like me.
- **The digital badges module is noise.** Halfway down there's an app-badge mechanic that I scrolled through without reading. That section kills momentum on an already short email.
- **Cadence is getting heavy.** Five emails in five days from one sender — even a brand I trust starts feeling like spam when it's that relentless.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment this out of the DIY/project-buyer list.** They've got my purchase history. They know I'm not buying craft kits. This is a data problem, not a creative problem — fix the audience before touching the copy.
- 2. **If they must blast broadly, add a "know a kid who'd love this?" pass-along line.** Turn my irrelevance into a referral moment. One sentence in the body converts a wrong-inbox impression into a share.
- 3. **Name what the kid actually builds.** "Build a [specific thing] and take it home" is more compelling than "build, spin and take home the fun." I'm a builder — tell me what the output is.
- 4. **Bury or cut the badge module.** The email's one job is the workshop registration. The second CTA splits focus for no meaningful gain.

No broken links were confirmed, but link destination validation is effectively blind due to redirect wrapping.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text** on at least one image:
- `https://image.e.lowes.com/lib/fe9515707364027471/m/1/a22207c1-63c7-47c0-9e00-61227828e1fe.jpg` — no `alt` attribute present.

Both `@font-face` blocks are wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!--> ... <!--<![endif]-->` conditionals, which correctly suppresses them in Outlook MSO but they will attempt to load in Gmail, Apple Mail, and mobile clients. This is expected behavior, but the `.woff`-only format (`format('woff')`) means no `.woff2` fallback, adding minor load overhead.

**Media query gap** — two breakpoints are defined: `min-width: 375px` to `max-width: 600px` (webkit-only) and `max-width: 480px`. The 481–599px range on non-webkit clients falls into a dead zone with no responsive rules applied.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM Params)

No direct evidence of UTM parameters in the raw HTML source (they would typically appear in the pre-redirect href values or be injected by the ESP at click time). This should be verified by manually clicking a tracked link and inspecting the final URL.

### 7. Recommendations

1. **Verify physical address in footer** — pull the complete HTML source and confirm a postal address is present. If missing, this must be added before the next send.
2. **Confirm `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers upstream** — check the raw headers at the ESP (not via AgentMail relay). If absent, enable one-click unsubscribe at the ESP level to comply with Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy.
3. **Fix plain-text alternative** — regenerate the plain-text part as human-readable content with tracked URLs replaced by descriptive anchor text. The 77% URL ratio is a deliverability liability.
4. **Add `alt` text to all images** — at minimum the flagged `.jpg`; run a full `img[alt=""]` or `img:not([alt])` selector pass across the complete template.
5. **Validate UTM continuity** — manually trace one tracked link through the redirect to confirm UTM params survive to the final `lowes.com` landing page.
6. **Resolve the 481–599px responsive gap** — either extend the upper breakpoint to 600px without the webkit restriction, or consolidate the two media queries into one.
7. **Obtain authentication confirmation** — confirm SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status directly from ESP send logs; do not rely on relay-captured headers for this signal.
