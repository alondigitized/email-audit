# Memorial Day savings⭐ Up to 60% OFF
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- This is the fourth Memorial Day email from Lowe's in four days — I've been counting. Yesterday was the "staycation" lifestyle play that I mentally filed under "not for me," and before that were two straight percentage escalations. Today they bumped from 45% to 60%, which is the right move to get me to re-engage, and I'll admit it worked: I scrolled.
- The thing that actually stopped my thumb was the "Curated with you in mind" section. DeWalt drills and power tools right in the body — that's not a coincidence, that's my purchase history doing its job. The ceiling fan at $99.95 with a real price tag is the kind of specific, project-ready signal I respond to. Memorial Day weekend I'm replacing the fan in the garage workshop. That section alone would've gotten a click from me.
- What I can't ignore is the cadence. Five emails in four days from one sender is inbox carpet-bombing. I'm a loyal Lowe's MyLowe's guy and even I started feeling the fatigue. I'd have opened it, scrolled to the tools section, clicked the DeWalt CTA, and moved on. Would not forward — nothing new enough to text a buddy about.

## What's working

- **"Curated with you in mind" power tools section** — DeWalt drills and equipment front and center. That's my world. The personalization signal is real and earned.
- **60% escalation in the subject** — stepping up from 45% the prior send is the right cadence move and gives me a reason to open again.
- **Ceiling fan at $99.95 with a visible price** — specific product, real dollar figure, no "starting at" hedging. That's how I shop.
- **"Buy more, save more" tier** — contractor-adjacent messaging that acknowledges I don't buy one of anything. I buy in project quantities.
- **Layout is clean and renders without bugs** — no broken images, no overlapping text, reads on mobile without zooming.

## What's weak

- **Fifth email in four days** — I'm a fan, not a mailing list. By send five, you're training me to ignore, not engage.
- **Too many competing offer blocks** — 60% off, 45% off, 25% off, 20% off, "Deals of the Day"... where do I actually go? The eye can't settle.
- **Kitchen/bath lifestyle section** — I'm not remodeling the kitchen this weekend. That block exists for a different subscriber and it dilutes the email for me.
- **No explicit deadline visible** — "Memorial Day savings" implies Sunday, but I want to see "ends Monday 5/26" so I know when the clock actually stops.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Hard-date the deadline.** Add "Ends Monday, May 26" under the hero. Urgency without a date is just noise.
- 2. **Cut the competing percentage stack.** Pick one hero number — the 60% — and back it up with two or three specific products at that price. Four different discount figures in one email reads like a clearance event, not a curated sale.
- 3. **Lead with the tools if you know I'm a tools buyer.** The "Curated" section is buried mid-scroll. If your personalization engine already surfaced it, make it the hero.
- 4. **Drop the kitchen/lifestyle block for tool-buyer segments.** If you're segmenting enough to show me DeWalt, you know I don't need cabinet pulls right now.
- 5. **Ease the send cadence.** After five in four days, one well-curated send tomorrow would outperform three redundant ones.

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound XHTML 1.0 Transitional with functional responsive breakpoints, but has two compliance gaps (missing List-Unsubscribe headers, SPF/DKIM status unknown via relay) and eight images lacking alt text — five of which are tracking pixels that expose the recipient address in plaintext URLs.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**20 tracking links skipped** — all click URLs route through a redirect domain (consistent with ESP click-wrapping). This is expected behavior; no broken links detected via probe.

**Tracking pixels expose PII in URLs (HIGH):**
Five `sli.lowes.com/imp` beacon URLs include the recipient email address as a plaintext query parameter:

```
https://sli.lowes.com/imp?s=1090838&e=cole-drywall-lowes-fap6e@etell.app&p=5172026&lctg=410146703&stpe=static
```

The `&e=` parameter is unencoded. Any proxy, CDN, or mail security appliance that logs image requests will capture the email address in access logs. This is a data hygiene issue regardless of whether this recipient is a test persona.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**8 images missing `alt` text:**

| Image | URL (truncated) |
|---|---|
| Hero image | `.../c301e24e-201b-46f9-8172-27b5acdcbf99.jpg` |
| Open-pixel | `mi.lowes.com/p/cp/.../o.gif?mi_u=410146703` |
| 5× impression beacons | `sli.lowes.com/imp?s=...` |
| Promo image | `.../ad569dc5-361d-4d7d-8bc0-1ae6ec389f05.png` |

The hero and promo images need descriptive alt text for screen readers and image-blocked clients. The tracking pixels and open pixel should carry `alt=""` (empty, not absent) to suppress screen-reader announcements.

**Responsive breakpoints:** Two overlapping media queries defined — `min-width: 375px AND max-width: 600px` (webkit-only) and `max-width: 480px`. The upper bound mismatch (600px vs 480px) means devices between 480–600px get only the webkit-scoped styles, not the full mobile ruleset. This may cause layout issues on tablets in portrait mode on non-WebKit engines.

**Font loading:** Four `@font-face` blocks load `.woff` only — no `.woff2` fallback. Modern clients prefer woff2; woff-only increases load time marginally but will render correctly via system fallback.

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header — NOT FOUND (WARN)**
The AgentMail relay did not surface a `List-Unsubscribe` header. This header is required by Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender policies (enforced since February 2024 for senders >5K/day). If it exists at the ESP layer and was stripped by the relay, this is a monitoring gap. If it is genuinely absent, this is a deliverability risk.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post header — NOT FOUND (WARN)**
RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe (`List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`) is required by Gmail/Yahoo for bulk senders. Absence triggers demotion to spam folder for qualifying sends.
