# Ends tomorrow: Big can't-miss appliance deals‼️
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- Lowe's knows my name — or at least my store — and "Ends Tomorrow" actually gets me to open the thing. Coming off yesterday's Closeout Event tease, this email shows up like a logical follow-up... and then throws a Lionel Messi soccer graphic directly in my face. I'm 49. I own a tile saw. I am not joining anything Messi-adjacent for free or otherwise.
- The appliance hero is clean and the "Ends Tomorrow" badge gives me a real reason to act. The MOEN section — $30 off kitchen and bath solutions, 3x MyLowe's rewards — that's actually the most useful block in this whole email for a guy who's mid-bathroom reno. It's buried below the Messi detour, which is going to cost Lowe's real clicks from real customers like me.
- I'll mark it read. I won't forward it. Might click the MOEN block if I have a project need today, but the overall email feels like it was assembled from three different campaign briefs that nobody reconciled before hitting send.

## What's working

- **"Ends Tomorrow" badge in the hero** — that's credible urgency after a week of Closeout Event buildup; yesterday's email primed me for exactly this.
- **Closeout Event block** — consistent with the 2026-07-17 send, feels like a real campaign not a random blast.
- **MOEN $30 + 3x MyLowe's rewards** — this is the only block that actually speaks to my project spend; reward multipliers move me.
- **Nav bar hits my categories** — APPLIANCES / DAILY DEALS / PATIO / OUTDOOR TOOLS right at the top; at least the chrome knows what I care about.
- **Clean layout, no render bugs** — the hierarchy is legible even on a small screen; nothing broken.

## What's weak

- **The Messi Half Time insert is a full creative block aimed at someone twenty years younger than me** — "You'll want to say you were part of this" is not how you talk to a guy who owns a reciprocating saw; this belongs in a completely separate list segment.
- **Appliance deals are adjacent to my world, not central** — I buy tools and materials for renos; a washer/dryer flash sale is something I flag for my wife, not something I click on Friday afternoon before a weekend project.
- **"Shop now" CTA is generic across every block** — same copy everywhere means nothing stands out; the MOEN $30 offer deserved its own specific CTA like "Grab the MOEN deal."
- **Daily email cadence is wearing on me** — five emails in six days (counting PAW Patrol on the 13th); when I see another Lowe's in my inbox I'm now slower to open.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the Messi block entirely for this segment** — or at minimum suppress it for MyLowe's Pro/HomeImprovement purchasers; the soccer partnership is a completely different audience and kills the flow.
- 2. **Lead the MOEN section higher** — move the $30 + 3x rewards block immediately under the hero for project-oriented accounts; that's the one offer I'd actually act on today.
- 3. **Make the CTA specific to each offer** — "Save $30 on MOEN" beats "Shop now" every time for a guy who's scanning fast.
- 4. **Swap "Big can't-miss" language** — that's breathless; a confident offer doesn't need to tell me I can't miss it.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**7 images missing `alt` text (confirmed by QA):**

| Image | Type | URL fragment |
|---|---|---|
| `c301e24e-...jpg` | Content image | `image.e.lowes.com/lib/.../m/59/` |
| `ad569dc5-...png` | Content image | `image.e.lowes.com/lib/.../m/71/` |
| `imp` (×5) | Tracking pixels | `sli.lowes.com/imp?...` |

Content images without `alt` break screen readers and render blank in image-blocked clients (common in corporate environments). Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` explicitly.

**Overlapping media queries create conflicting rules on 375–480px viewports.** The email defines two separate breakpoints:
- `@media screen and (min-width: 375px) and (max-width: 600px)` — sets `.container { width: 100% }`
- `@media only screen and (max-width: 480px)` — also sets `.container { width: 100%; max-width: 480px }`

On a 375–480px device both fire simultaneously. The second query's `max-width: 480px` on `.container` adds a constraint the first query doesn't have; cascade order determines which wins, but the overlap is unintentional and increases risk of layout bugs on that device range.

**Duplicate `local()` font hints** (cosmetic, no functional impact):
```css
src: local('Fellix Regular'), local('Fellix Regular'), url(...)
```
Every `@font-face` block duplicates the `local()` source. Harmless but indicates template sloppiness.

**`.sub_nav` receives `background-color: #15b5e5` at mobile; `.sub_nav_bottom` does not.** If these elements are intended to be visually paired, the missing background on `sub_nav_bottom` is a rendering gap.

Font loading is correctly gated with `<!--[if !mso]><!-->` conditionals — Outlook clients receive safe system-font fallbacks.
