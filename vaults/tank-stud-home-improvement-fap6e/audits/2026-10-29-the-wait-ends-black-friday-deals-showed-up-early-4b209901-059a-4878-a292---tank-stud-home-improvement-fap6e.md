# The wait ends. Black Friday deals showed up early🤩
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-29**
## Executive summary

- My gut reaction: it's Black Friday in October, again. The red hero says "Get these BIG savings ahead of the big day" and gives me no number. The first real offer is the DEWALT/Craftsman/Kobalt strip, "Save up to $120" on select power tools. That's my aisle, so it got a second look. Then the email spends a full screen on a Samsung appliance block with 35% off, and I'm not buying a fridge this weekend.
- It's better than the Sunday 10/25 "Deals are ready" blank sign, because there are real numbers and real tools here. But it's the same appliance-heavy pattern as Monday's "Pre-holiday upgrade" send. The "Curated with you in mind" row is the best part for me. A DEWALT 20V battery pack and a DEWALT XR router are things I'd actually put on my project list. Then the third tile is a jumbo squishy dumpling toy, and that killed the "curated for me" claim.
- Would I click? Probably yes, on the power tools banner or the router tile. Would I forward it? No. I'd read it, save it for the next reno phase, and wait to see whether the real Black Friday prices beat these.

## What's working

- The "Save up to $120" power tools banner is clear, and the DEWALT, Craftsman and Kobalt tools make it obviously my aisle.
- The nav bar with Appliances, Daily Deals, Bathroom and Outdoor Tools gives me quick routes into the site.
- The DEWALT XR router and the 20V battery pack in the product row are the kind of project-ready items I'd buy.
- The Lowe's logo and the blue-and-red branding are consistent, so I trust it right away.
- The "Members get" Samsung block shows the free delivery, installation and haul-away perks clearly, even though I'm not shopping for appliances.

## What's weak

- The hero has no number, no product and no deadline. "BIG savings" is filler, and the button just says "Shop now."
- Three "Shop now" buttons stacked on top of each other don't tell me what each one opens.
- The Samsung block takes up the most space, and it's not my aisle. The Monday email led with the same thing.
- The jumbo squishy dumpling toy sits between two DEWALT tools in a row labeled "Curated with you in mind." That breaks the targeting.
- The battery pack's product name is cut off and reads "DEWALT 20 -Volt 2 - Pack 8 Ah 8 Ah Lithium-ion ) -". The "Buy now" buttons show no price.
- The fine print is a wall of text, and the offer excludes Pro Rewards members. As a DIYer who might also be on a Pro list, I can't tell which side of that I'm on.
- It doesn't say how long the power tools deal lasts. The only dates are buried in the fine print: valid 10/22 to 11/3.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put the power tools deal first. Give it a headline with a real number and name the tools, like "Up to $120 off DEWALT, Craftsman and Kobalt."
- 2. Show the sale price and the savings under each product tile. Add a stock note like "In stock near you."
- 3. Replace the dumpling toy with a project-bundle item, like a drill and driver kit or a shop vac. Don't claim "curated" unless it is.
- 4. Move the appliance block below the tools and shrink it, or send it as a separate appliance email.
- 5. Put the real end date next to the offers, not in the fine print. Use button copy like "Shop DEWALT deals."
- 6. Add a line for MyLowe's and Pro members that makes the offer rules clear.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
Lowe's Black Friday email, sent from `lowes@e.lowes.com`. The visible source has a malformed DOCTYPE, two content images with no alt text, and tracking pixels that put the recipient's email address in plaintext in the URL. The compliance and authentication warnings come from AgentMail relay capture limits, so they aren't confirmed defects.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **PII in pixel URLs (confirmed):** every `sli.lowes.com/imp` impression pixel carries the recipient address in the query string. Example: `https://sli.lowes.com/imp?s=127768300&e=cole-drywall-lowes-fap6e@etell.app&p=528589&lctg=410146703&stpe=pixel`. This appears in at least 16 pixels. The address is exposed to proxies, logs and referrer-style leakage. It is also unhashed, which is a privacy weakness.
- **Pixel volume:** 16 separate `imp` calls to a third-party ad-server domain (`sli.lowes.com`). That's a lot of request overhead, and any slow response delays image loading in clients that fetch them.
- **Unverified links:** 35 tracking/click-redirect links were skipped by the HTTP probe, so their destinations and redirect health are untested. This is a coverage gap, not a confirmed defect.
- No broken links were detected (0 issues).

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Malformed DOCTYPE (confirmed):** `<!DOCTYPE html PUBLIC"-//W3C//DTD XHTML 1.0 Transitional//EN" ...>` has no space after `PUBLIC`. Strict parsers may fall into quirks mode. The DTD URL and `xmlns` also use `https://`, whereas the canonical namespace is `http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml`.
- **Missing alt text on content images (confirmed):**
  - `https://image.e.lowes.com/lib/fe9515707364027471/m/59/c301e24e-201b-46f9-8172-27b5acdcbf99.jpg`

  If these are informational, they're invisible to screen readers and blank when images are blocked.
