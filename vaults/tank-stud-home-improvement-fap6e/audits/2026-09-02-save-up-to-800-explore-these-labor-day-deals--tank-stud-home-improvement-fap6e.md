# Save up to $800! Explore these Labor Day deals
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-02**
## Executive summary

- Opened this one skeptically after the last week of Lowe's spam — appliances, patio, mowers — and this is another "up to $800" number with basically nothing behind it. A storage tote, a can of Bar Keeper's Friend, and mini blinds? That's not a Labor Day project lineup, that's whatever was sitting in a random SKU feed. Compare that to yesterday's mower/pressure-washer/mulch email — this one feels like a step backward, like they scraped together three unrelated products and slapped an Affirm financing pitch and a "Deals of the Day" newsletter signup ad on top.
- The "$800" headline number has zero connection to anything I actually see below it — no product carries that discount, nothing's tagged with a percentage or dollar figure at all. That's the kind of bait-and-switch that makes me trust the subject line less next time.
- Not clicking, not forwarding. This gets marked read and archived — it's noise, not a project.

## What's working

- The Labor Day hero banner is bold and easy to read, clear "Shop now" CTA button.
- Free same-day delivery for members is a real, concrete perk I'd actually use on a weekend project.
- "Curated with you in mind" section at least tries to feel personalized, even if the products miss.

## What's weak

- The three "curated" products (storage tote, cleaner, mini blinds) have nothing to do with each other or with a fall project — feels like a random grab, not a bundle.
- Nowhere does anything actually cost up to $800 less — the headline number is unsupported by any visible price or percentage.
- No pro/contractor vs. homeowner framing, no rebate info, no in-stock/local-store signal — feels generic, not like they know I'm a DIY guy with two loyalty accounts.
- The Affirm 0% APR pitch and the "Deals of the Day" sign-up ad crowd out actual product content — half this email is ads for other things to sign up for.
- Sponsored lifestyle photo (family on a couch) doesn't match my persona at all — I want a garage or a yard, not a living room scene.

## Recommendations

- 1. Kill the "$800" headline unless there's an actual product or category tied to that number — right now it reads as clickbait, and I've now seen three "up to $X" Lowe's emails this week that don't add up.
- 2. Replace the random three-product grid with an actual fall-project bundle — gutter cleaning, weatherproofing, mulch/soil, storage for winterizing gear — something coherent like the 8/31 lawn-deals email that scored well with me.
- 3. Move the Affirm/financing pitch and the newsletter signup further down or cut them — they're diluting the one email that's supposed to be about a specific offer.
- 4. Add a real % or $ off tag on at least the hero products so the headline discount is traceable.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Labor Day project bundle is up to $800 off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Storage, cleaning, and outdoor gear for your next weekend project`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Members: free same-day delivery on today's Labor Day picks`
