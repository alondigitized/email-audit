# Gifts for Dad Starting Under $50
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-05**
## Executive summary

- Look, I'm probably a dad — so this subject line landed in my brain as "potentially for me," but the second I opened it and saw that outdoor sofa setup under "Summer Hosting," I started mentally handing this email off to my wife. That's not where I live. I live in the garage. I live at the lumber desk. The pivot to Father's Day gifts happens below the fold, and by then my scrolling thumb is already on autopilot.
- The grill section — "For Dads who own the cookout" — is the one beat in here that actually speaks to a guy like me. And honestly the portable AC unit caught my eye too, because I've been sweating through shop time in the garage all week and a unit like that is a real project-weekend tool. But I had to earn those moments by scrolling past patio furniture and a fairly chaotic layout.
- Bottom line: I would not click this one. I'd maybe forward the link to my wife and say "here's some ideas" — but I'm not buying my own Father's Day gift off a Lowe's email. Mark as read, move on.

## What's working

- **Father's Day timing is right.** Sent June 5 with an explicit "Don't wait until 6/21" callout — that's two-plus weeks of runway, not a last-minute panic drop like that stale Memorial Day email on May 31.
- **Price anchors do real work.** "Under $50" and "Under $100" tiles tell me instantly what I'm looking at budget-wise. No guesswork.
- **Grill section has the right voice.** "For Dads who own the cookout" — Master Forge, grill-focused — that's the one moment where the email remembered it was talking to a dude.
- **Loyalty points visible.** The 2x/3x points multiplier in the MyLowe's section is relevant since I'm a member. That's a reason to act before 6/21.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no overlapping text. Email displays fine.

## What's weak

- **Hero image is a couch.** A cushioned outdoor sofa under "Summer Hosting" is not how a DIY weekend warrior opens a Lowe's email. That's a different email going to a different list.
- **Too many storylines competing.** I counted at least five separate pitches: summer hosting, Father's Day gifts, cookout gear, cooling items, and a Deals of the Day module. None of them wins. This is a newsletter, not a campaign.
- **"Shop now" everywhere.** Every section ends with "Shop now." That's not a CTA, that's a filler phrase. It doesn't tell me what I'm shopping for or why now.
- **Gift framing doesn't match how I buy at Lowe's.** I go to Lowe's for a project. When the email is framed around gifting, I mentally step out of the decision loop and wait for someone else to buy it.
- **The Deals of the Day signup at the bottom feels like an afterthought.** Why is that module in a Father's Day send?

## Recommendations

- 1. **Swap the hero entirely.** Lead with the grill section or a tool-focused Father's Day image. A guy in the garage or at a workbench holding a new cordless drill. The furniture shot costs you the DIY dad immediately.
- 2. **Write one story, not five.** Pick a lane: Father's Day tools under $50. Everything in the email should ladder to that one idea. The cooling items can live in a separate send.
- 3. **Upgrade "Shop now" to something specific.** "See grilling tools" or "Browse tools under $50" — verb + noun, tells me exactly where I land.
- 4. **Lead the points multiplier higher.** If I'm a MyLowe's member and there are bonus points on Father's Day picks, that's the open-the-wallet nudge. Bury it at section 4 and I miss it.

## Technical Audit — Lowe's "Gifts for Dad Starting Under $50"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

Structurally sound retail email with standard ESP click-wrapping and impression tracking. Primary technical concerns are missing `List-Unsubscribe` / `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers (failing Google/Yahoo 2024 bulk sender requirements), a likely empty merge token in one tracking pixel, and 10 images missing `alt` attributes.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**20 click-redirect links skipped by QA probe** — all wrapped through the ESP's redirect domain (`e.lowes.com`). Destination URLs and UTM parameter integrity could not be verified programmatically.

**Five impression-tracking pixels** fire from `sli.lowes.com/imp` with the recipient address exposed in plaintext as a URL parameter:
```
https://sli.lowes.com/imp?s=1090838&e=cole-drywall-lowes-fap6e@etell.app&p=642026&lctg=410146703
```
This is standard ESP behavior but means the subscriber email address is transmitted in the query string on every open event.

**Possible empty merge token** in the manage-subscriptions pixel:
```
https://mi.lowes.com/p/rp/8323e445c38bde24.png?mi_u=410146703&mi_state=
```
`mi_state=` has no value. If this is a subscriber-state personalization token (e.g., loyalty tier), it was not filled at send time. If intentionally empty, the trailing `=` is harmless but worth confirming with the ESP template.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**10 images missing `alt` text** (QA-confirmed):

| Image | Type | Issue |
|---|---|---|
| `c301e24e-...jpg` (image.e.lowes.com) | Content image | `alt` missing — screen readers skip silently |
| `ad569dc5-...png` (image.e.lowes.com) | Content image | Same |
| `8323e445c38bde24.png` (mi.lowes.com) | Likely footer/unsub image | `alt` missing |
| `o.gif` (mi.lowes.com) | Open-tracking pixel | Should carry `alt=""` (empty, not absent) |
| 6× `imp` pixels (sli.lowes.com) | Impression pixels | Should carry `alt=""` |

Content images (`c301e24e` and `ad569dc5`) require descriptive `alt` text for accessibility and for subscribers with images disabled. Tracking pixels require `alt=""` (empty string) to suppress screen reader announcements.

**Font loading** — four `@font-face` declarations (Fellix variants, DIN Next family) load `.woff` files from `lowescdn.com`. No `woff2` format offered; `woff2` typically compresses 30% smaller. Both `@font-face` blocks are wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!--> ... <![endif]-->` conditional comments, which correctly suppresses them in Outlook/Word-renderer clients. Fallback stacks are not visible in the truncated source — confirm system-safe fallbacks exist inline.

**Legacy hacks present but benign** — `.ReadMsgBody`, `.ExternalClass` rules targeting Hotmail (pre-Outlook.com era) and `body[data-outlook-cycle]` targeting older Outlook mobile are inert in current clients but add noise with no risk.

**Responsive breakpoints** — two media queries defined: `min-width: 375px / max-width: 600px` (mid-range) and `max-width: 480px` (mobile). The 375–600px range uses `-webkit-min-device-pixel-ratio:0` to limit scope to WebKit, which is correct intent but means non-WebKit clients in that width range (e.g., Firefox on tablet) get neither layout. Low practical impact given email client distribution.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent** (QA-confirmed). Required by RFC 2369 and by Google/Yahoo bulk sender policy (enforced February 2024 for senders >5,000/day). Without it, Gmail will not surface the "Unsubscribe" link in the three-dot menu, increasing spam complaint risk.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent** (QA-confirmed). Required by RFC 8058 for one-click unsubscribe compliance under the same Google/Yahoo policy. Its absence means Gmail cannot perform server-side one-click unsubscribe, forcing users to the ESP's landing page flow or to the spam button.

Both headers are configured at the ESP/MTA layer, not in the HTML template — this is a sending-infrastructure gap, not a template bug. The QA note flags it as potentially an AgentMail relay capture issue rather than a confirmed absence; the ESP should be checked directly.

**`Authentication-Results` header not found** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass status is unknown due to relay interception. Sender domain `e.lowes.com` (subdomain of lowes.com) should have DKIM signed mail; verification against raw headers at the receiving MX is needed to confirm.

No CAN-SPAM issues visible in the truncated source (physical address and unsubscribe link are expected in the footer, which was cut off).

All 20 outbound links are click-wrapped through the ESP redirect system (`e.lowes.com`). Destination URLs and UTM parameter presence could not be verified because the QA probe skipped redirect-chain domains.
