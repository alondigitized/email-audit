# FREE Trick-or-Treat tote! Today only 🎃
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-03**
## Executive summary

- Okay, a free tote sounds fine, but it's a kid's Halloween trick-or-treat bag. It's in-store only, it starts at 10 a.m., and there's a "limited quantities" line under the button. I've got a drywall project half done in the garage. A ghost bag doesn't get me to drive to Lowe's, and it only reads as a gift if my kid's still trick-or-treating.
- The rest of the email is a grab bag. The first-responder Silver status block isn't for me. The HomeCare+ dryer vent cleaning at $99 a year is sort of homeowner-relevant, but it's a service sub and not a tool. The best part is farther down: $10 MyLowe's Money on $200 or more, and $20 if I use the Rewards card, October 6–14. That's the real offer for a guy doing a reno, and it's buried in the fourth block under a Halloween tote. The email never says which projects the $200 would go toward.
- Would I click? Maybe the $10/$20 block, if I'm planning a lumber or tool run next week. I wouldn't forward it. I'd save it and mark it read.

## What's working

- The FREE tote is huge and easy to read, and the "Today Only" and "In-store Only" tags tell me the terms right away.
- The $10 MyLowe's Money on $200+ block is clear. The $20 for Rewards Card holders is a real incentive for someone who already carries the card.
- The dates (Oct 6–14) are stated plainly, so I could plan a purchase around them.
- It reads like an email to a member. The sender is "MyLowe's Rewards" and the preheader mentions earning 3x points.
- The blocks have strong blue contrast, big type and clear buttons, so I could read it on my phone without zooming.

## What's weak

- The lead offer is a kid's Halloween freebie, which doesn't fit a 49-year-old drywall and deck guy.
- The first-responder block takes the second slot, and it's irrelevant to me. It also has a wall of fine print.
- The $10/$20 offer I'd actually use is fourth, below the tote, the first-responder block and the HomeCare+ block.
- There are five different pitches and five buttons: tote, first responders, HomeCare+, MyLowe's Money and builder kits. The email doesn't know what it wants me to do.
- Nothing here is a tool, a rebate or a project bundle, and there's no in-stock signal. That's what I'd open a Lowe's email for.
- The fine print at the bottom is long and dense, and the MyLowe's Money terms ("NO CASH BACK", 30-day expiry, exclusions) make the offer feel less honest.
- I've now had Lowe's emails on smart home, patio and appliances this week. Today's is a fourth theme, and none of them has been a pro or reno pitch.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the $10/$20 MyLowe's Money on $200+ (Oct 6–14). It's the only offer that gets a contractor-type shopper into the store, and it should be the hero.
- 2. Cut it down to two or three modules. Move the first-responder block to a segmented send, since it's useless to most of the list.
- 3. Tie the $200 threshold to projects: "Stock up for your weekend build: lumber, drywall, fasteners." That tells me what the $200 is for.
- 4. If the tote stays, make it a secondary strip. Send it to households with kids, based on purchase history.
- 5. Add one in-stock or store-pickup line for the Oct 6–14 window, so I know I can get what I need.
