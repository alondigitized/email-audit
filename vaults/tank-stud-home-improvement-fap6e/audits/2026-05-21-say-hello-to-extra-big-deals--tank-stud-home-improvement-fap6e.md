# Say hello to Extra. Big. Deals. 🤩
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- I'm on my fifth Lowe's email in five days and my thumb is getting tired. This one shows up with "Extra. Big. Deals." fragmented across the subject like a teaser trailer, and when I open it the *hero* is a washer. Maybe a dryer. I'm a guy who spent last Saturday on a ladder replacing fascia board — I am not in the appliance market right now, and leading with that instantly signals "wrong audience."
- That said, I kept scrolling. The riding mower section with "Save up to $150" and that Pit Boss 850 5-burner grill at $499 both stopped my eye. Grill season is here, I've been pricing grills, and seeing a named model with a hard number is how you make me tap. The countdown timer adds real teeth — not fake "sale ends soon" copy but actual digits ticking down. That part works.
- End verdict: I wouldn't click the appliance hero, but I'd probably tap the grill "Buy now." I would not forward this — it's the fifth one this week and my buddy already got the same emails.

## What's working

- **Countdown timer is real urgency.** Four-day countdown with actual digits ticking — not vague "limited time." That's the one thing that made me pause instead of just scrolling to archive.
- **Pit Boss 850 5-Burner at $499 with model name.** Specific brand, specific model, specific price. That's how you sell a grill to a homeowner in May.
- **Riding mower section speaks to me.** "Select riding mowers / Save up to $150" with an outdoor image — seasonally right, category right, clean presentation.
- **Layout scans well.** Sections are visually separated, offers are easy to parse on a phone. No squinting required.

## What's weak

- **Appliance hero is the wrong lead for a DIY warrior.** Washer/dryer deals are the spouse's inbox, not mine. Putting that front-and-center cost them my attention in the first three seconds.
- **Five emails in five days is cadence abuse.** I've been keeping track since the 16th (staycation lifestyle), 17th (60% off), 18th (MrBeast kids thing), 19th (yard tools) — and now this. Even a solid email loses credibility when you're screaming every day.
- **No MyLowe's Pro pricing or member call-out.** I have the Pro account. Not a single badge, member rate, or exclusive SKU shows up. Treated me like a first-time walk-in.
- **Credit deal section feels like dead weight.** I already have the Lowe's card. Pitching me on 12/18-month financing when I just want to know what's on sale wastes scroll real estate.
- **"Shop now" CTAs are generic.** Every button says the same thing regardless of category — doesn't push me toward any specific action.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Swap the hero to outdoor power or project tools.** Memorial Day is when DIY guys greenlight the big spring projects. Lead with a mower bundle, a pressure washer, or a concrete tool sale — not a Samsung washing machine.
- 2. **Surface MyLowe's Pro pricing visibly.** Even one badge ("Pro exclusive price: $X") makes me feel like a known customer, not a random address they blast.
- 3. **Cut cadence to 2x/week during sale events.** By email five, I'm already mentally unsubscribing. Consolidate the offers and let each email breathe.
- 4. **Make the countdown mean something specific.** Put the exact end date/time in copy next to the timer — "Offer ends Sunday at midnight" converts better than digits alone.
- 5. **Retire the credit deal module for existing cardholders.** Segment it out. Showing me a card I already have is clutter.

- **Subject Alt B:** `$499 grill. $150 off mowers. Ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The countdown hits zero this weekend — here's what to grab first`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

| 5× impression pixels | `sli.lowes.com/imp?...` | Tracking pixels |

Tracking pixels (`o.gif`, `imp`) should have `alt=""` (empty string) to be invisible to screen readers. Content images (`*.jpg`, `*.png`) require descriptive `alt` text per WCAG 2.1 AA.

**Custom web fonts load via WOFF only** (`lowescdn.com/sffonts/Fellix-*.woff`). No WOFF2 format is declared, which is the preferred modern format. Clients that don't support the web font will fall back silently, but loading WOFF2 first would reduce payload size.

**Responsive breakpoints:** Two media queries target `max-width: 480px` and `min-width: 375px and max-width: 600px`. The 375–480px range is covered by both queries simultaneously; confirm specificity ordering doesn't produce unintended overrides on that range.

**Legacy Hotmail/ExternalClass hacks present** (`.ReadMsgBody`, `.ExternalClass`). These are harmless but target clients with <0.1% market share and add noise.
