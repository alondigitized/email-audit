# Win a home for the holidays? Yes, really. 🏠
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-01**
## Executive summary

- My gut reaction: it's a sweepstakes, not a tool email. A $500K credit for a dream home is a fun headline. But I'm a weekend warrior who builds a deck and re-tiles a bathroom. I'm not building a house from scratch. It felt like a lottery ticket, not something for my project list.
- It does look sharp. The house photo with the blueprint half is a nice touch. After the 10/29 "Black Friday deals showed up early" email and the 10/27 "Your fall to-do list" one with a DEWALT kit and $150 off power tools, this is a big step backwards. Those spoke to my aisle. This one doesn't.
- Would I click? Maybe once, out of curiosity, if the entry takes ten seconds. Would I forward it? No. Mark as read, and I'm still waiting for a real tool deal.

## What's working

- The "Enter to win" button is clear and sits right on the hero image.
- The house-to-blueprint image is striking and says "build" better than most stock photos.
- The "Grand Prize" panel lists three plain bullets: $500K credit, a Lowe's preferred builder, and my choice of location.
- The MyLowe's Rewards branding at the top tells me why I got this.
- The phone mockup shows the app flow, so I know what entering looks like.

## What's weak

- There's no product, no tool and no project content. Nothing speaks to a DIYer.
- The legal fine print ("NO PURCHASE NECESSARY...") is tiny, low-contrast white on blue. I can't read it without zooming.
- "Winner is matched with a Lowe's preferred builder" makes this a contractor job. That's the opposite of my DIY world.
- It never says how the sweepstakes ties into my rewards status, or how many entries I'd get. The phone mockup hints at "Earn Bonus Entries," but the text is too small to read.
- The preheader text at the top says "Enter in the Lowe's app," so I wonder whether the button works on the web.
- There's no deadline in the main copy. The end date is only in the tiny phone mockup, in the fine print, and in the subject-adjacent copy I can't read.

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a real deadline next to the button. Something like "Enter by Dec. 15" would give me a reason to act today.
- 2. Add one line to the hero that makes the prize relatable for a DIYer, such as "or win a share of Lowe's project credit."
- 3. Make the fine print bigger and use darker contrast.
- 4. Add a row of seasonal tools or projects below the fold, so I get a deal if I skip the sweepstakes.
- 5. Spell out how many entries I get as a MyLowe's member, and show the bonus-entry option in readable type.
