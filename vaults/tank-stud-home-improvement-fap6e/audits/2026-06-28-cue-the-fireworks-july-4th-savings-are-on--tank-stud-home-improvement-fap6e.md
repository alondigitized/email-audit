# Cue the fireworks! July 4th savings are ON🎉
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-28**
## Executive summary

- Lowe's went back to the well one too many times this week. The June 25th "July 4th prep begins in your backyard" email was tight — one theme, clear intent, exactly where my head was at. This one took that same idea and exploded it into five emails duct-taped together: grills, patio furniture, fence solutions, financing, Affirm, Deals of the Day. Nothing rises to the top because everything is competing.
- "Grills Fired up. Maad set!" stopped me cold — not in a good way. I'm 49. I've been running a circular saw since before Gen Z had teeth. When a hardware store talks like a streetwear TikTok, I mentally clock out. I get that they're trying to be energetic for July 4th, but that slogan does not belong in a MyLowe's email to a weekend warrior with a fence project on deck.
- That said, the 30% off callout is right there, July 4th is six days out, and somehow the Catalyst Fence section in the middle actually hooked me because I've been pricing that project. I'd scroll, maybe click the fence category, and move on. I would not forward this. Would not open the next one they send before the 4th if they do.

## What's working

- **Timing is dead-on.** June 28, long weekend six days out — this is exactly the right moment to catch me in project-planning mode.
- **Grills and outdoor content** is where a DIY weekend warrior lives. Not home décor, not fashion — actual project-relevant stuff.
- **30% off is visible without hunting.** The offer is right there, which is all I ask.
- **Catalyst Fence section was an unexpected hook.** I've been pricing that exact project. It broke through the noise in a way they probably didn't design for.
- **12–18 months financing** is relevant if I'm mid-budget on a bigger reno — I appreciate seeing that in a project-stage email.

## What's weak

- **"Maad set!" is not 49-year-old Gen X language.** I know they're trying to be festive. This lands in the uncanny valley — Lowe's pretending to be a hype brand.
- **Catalog dump with no spine.** Grills, patio, fence, financing, Affirm, Deals of the Day — five different stories told poorly instead of one told well.
- **No MyLowe's or Pro member callout.** I'm a known customer with an account. If there's member pricing in here, I can't find it — that's a straight-up missed opportunity.
- **Email cadence fatigue is real.** This is send number 6 or 7 from Lowe's in about a week. The June 25th backyard email already said most of this. This one feels like the remix nobody asked for.
- **Affirm section is tonally jarring.** You're doing fireworks and grills and then suddenly I'm reading about installment financing. Put that in the footer where it belongs.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill "Maad set!" immediately** — replace with language a 45-year-old project-driven buyer actually uses. "Fire it up this weekend" or "Your 4th of July project list, covered" does more work.
- 2. **Pick one hero and commit.** Grills OR patio OR fence — not all three. This email needs a single load-bearing offer, not a catalog.
- 3. **Add a MyLowe's member tier line** in the hero block. Even "Members get an extra 5%" is enough to make me feel seen as a customer, not a stranger.
- 4. **Move Affirm and financing to the footer.** Buy-now-pay-later doesn't belong above the fold in a holiday send.
- 5. **Rest the July 4th theme for two days.** Send one more, targeted, on July 2nd — not another broad catalog.
- **Subject Alt A:** `July 4th weekend: grills, patio, and fence up to 30% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your project list for the long weekend — 30% off starts now`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Outdoor living, fencing, and grilling gear — shop before the 4th.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Everything for the long weekend, in one place. Members: check your pricing.`

The content images (`*.jpg`, `*.png`) are the more significant gap — images-off rendering in Outlook/Gmail will show a broken placeholder with no context.

**Overlapping media query ranges** — Two breakpoint blocks are defined:
- `@media screen and (min-width: 375px) and (max-width: 600px)` (tablet/mid)
- `@media only screen and (max-width: 480px)` (mobile)

The 375–480px range is covered by both. If a property is set differently in each block, cascade order determines the winner, which is fragile. No confirmed rendering failure, but worth auditing for conflicting `.container` or `.drop` rules.

**Custom web fonts will not render in most email clients.** Four `@font-face` declarations (Fellix Regular/Medium/SemiBold/Bold, DIN Next/Bold/Black) are wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!-->`  — correct exclusion of Outlook. However, Gmail (both webmail and Android app) strips `@font-face` blocks. Fallback stack behavior depends on what inline `font-family` fallbacks are specified downstream; not visible in the truncated source but should be verified.

**Gmail mobilehide hack:**
```css
a.mobileshow, .mobilehide { display: none; color: #fff; /*for Gmail */ }
```
Using `color: #fff` as a Gmail-specific suppression technique for hidden elements is a recognized workaround, but only suppresses text color — it does not hide block-level elements from Gmail's rendering. If `.mobilehide` wraps block content, those elements may be visible in Gmail.
