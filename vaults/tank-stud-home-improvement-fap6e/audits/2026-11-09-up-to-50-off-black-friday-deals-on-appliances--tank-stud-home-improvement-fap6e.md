# Up to 50% OFF! Black Friday deals on appliances
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-09**
## Executive summary

- This is the 11/4 appliance email again. The subject then was "Up to 50% OFF select appliances + FREE delivery," and this one is nearly the same. I'm not shopping for a fridge this weekend. The 50% hero is loud and easy to read, but it's a homeowner-with-a-checkbook message, not a project message.
- The one thing that got my attention was the DeWalt 12V battery/charger kit in the "Curated with you in mind" row. It's buried near the bottom, next to a toilet and a storage tote. After the 11/6 "+2 free power tools" send, which was my favorite, this feels like a step backward. The fine print also bugs me: "up to 50%" is tied to spending $896 or more, and the "up to 40%" vacuum deal has no hero detail. I'd skim it, maybe save it, and not click.
- I wouldn't forward it. If my brother-in-law were redoing his kitchen I might, but that's the only case.

## What's working

- The "Up to 50% off" hero in big white-on-red type is readable at a glance, with no zooming.
- The "FREE Delivery / Basic installation / Haul away" trio spells out what I'd actually get with a big purchase.
- The MyLowe's Rewards banner is at the top, so I know my member status counts.
- The DeWalt kit in the product row is the right tool for a weekend project, even though it sits low in the email.
- The 0% APR for 36 months block is a clear financing hook for a big purchase like a kitchen reno.

## What's weak

- Nothing in the hero is for a DIYer. It's all appliances plus vacuums, with no tools, no pro voice, and no project bundle.
- The "$896 or more" threshold and the dense legal footer make "up to 50%" feel like bait. Nobody gets 50% off everything.
- There are too many competing blocks: Frigidaire, vacuums, credit, Samsung, Deals of the Day. The eye has nowhere to land after the hero.
- Two "Sponsored" ad blocks (Frigidaire pizza, Samsung) make it feel like an ad network, not Lowe's talking to me.
- The product row is generic. A toilet and a plastic tote beside a DeWalt kit shows no sign they know I buy tools.
- "Black Friday" shows up in the top banner but there's no real urgency or deadline in the hero.

## Recommendations

- 1. Segment by purchase history. Send tool buyers a DeWalt/Kobalt/Milwaukee hero, and keep the appliance hero for people who browsed appliances.
- 2. Put a concrete deadline in the hero, such as "Ends 11/26", instead of burying dates in the footer.
- 3. Move the personalized product row above the ad blocks, and fill it with tools and project items.
- 4. Show the $896 threshold next to the 50% claim, not only in the footer.
- 5. Add a Pro or DIY rebate/bundle strip, such as "Buy a battery kit, get a tool free."

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML has a malformed DOCTYPE and 17 images with no alt text, 15 of them third-party ad-impression pixels that carry the recipient's email address in the URL. The header-based compliance checks (List-Unsubscribe, SPF/DKIM) could not be verified, because the AgentMail relay does not expose those headers.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Recipient PII in pixel URLs:** All 15 `https://sli.lowes.com/imp?...` pixels include `e=cole-drywall-lowes-fap6e@etell.app` in plaintext. For example: `imp?s=127768300&e=cole-drywall-lowes-fap6e@etell.app&p=529577&lctg=410146703&stpe=pixel`. The address is exposed in image-request logs and to any proxy or caching layer.
- **Empty parameter:** `li_coord=` has no value in `s=2019286` and `s=1090834`. Each of these appears twice, with `stpe=default`. This looks like an unresolved merge or coordinate value.
- **Duplicate impressions:** `s=2019286` and `s=1090834` each appear twice, which could double-count impressions.
- **Unverified links:** 38 tracking links were skipped by the QA probe as click-redirect domains. Their destinations, status codes and redirect chains were not checked, so I can't confirm or rule out broken links.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Malformed DOCTYPE:** `<!DOCTYPE html PUBLIC"-//W3C//DTD XHTML 1.0 Transitional//EN" ...>` has no space after `PUBLIC`. Strict parsers may reject it, and clients that don't will probably fall back to quirks mode.
- **XHTML namespace:** `xmlns="https://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml"` uses `https`. The canonical namespace is `http://`. This is nonstandard but usually harmless.
- **Missing alt on content images (2):**
  - `https://image.e.lowes.com/lib/fe9515707364027471/m/59/c301e24e-201b-46f9-8172-27b5acdcbf99.jpg`
  - `https://image.e.lowes.com/lib/fe9515707364027471/m/71/ad569dc5-361d-4d7d-8bc0-1ae6ec389f05.png`

  With images blocked or a screen reader, these are silent or announced by filename. I can't tell from the truncated source whether they are decorative.
- **Missing alt on tracking pixels (15):** The `sli.lowes.com/imp` images should carry `alt=""` and `role="presentation"` or `width="1" height="1"`. This is a minor issue.
- **Web fonts:** The `@font-face` fonts (Fellix, DIN Next) are wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!-->`, so Outlook is excluded. The source is truncated, so I can't confirm the fallback font stacks.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Fix the DOCTYPE to `<!DOCTYPE html PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD XHTML 1.0 Transitional//EN" "http://www.w3.org/TR/xhtml1/DTD/xhtml1-transitional.dtd">`, and use `http://` in the `xmlns` value.
