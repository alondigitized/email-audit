# FREE beach bucket for members ☀️
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-29**
## Executive summary

- Second beach bucket email in two days. I'm not joking — I opened the June 28th "Pssst! Your next mini bucket's here" send and thought maybe it was a one-off. Now I'm getting the "official" version with a date, a calendar invite, and a photo of a plastic Lowe's-branded pail in sand. I'm a guy who currently has a bathroom gut-job half done and a deck build queued for fall. I spend real money at Lowe's. My MyLowe's account has purchase history that would tell you exactly who I am. And this is what they're sending me.
- Execution-wise, the email is clean. "FREE" hits big, the July 4th tie-in is legible, the "Add to calendar" block is a nice touch for driving foot traffic. But none of that matters when the product is a sand bucket. The Lowe's algorithm looked at my member profile and apparently decided I'm a dad with a six-year-old headed to the beach. I'm forty-nine, my kids are grown, and this weekend I'm pricing out composite decking.
- Mark as read. Zero clicks. Would not forward — I'd groan about it to whoever's in my garage Saturday.

## What's working

- **"FREE" in the hero** — gets your attention before anything else. You don't have to read any copy to understand the offer.
- **In-store Only badge is visible upfront** — sets expectations cleanly; no bait-and-switch on a click.
- **"Saturday, July 4. Starts at 10 a.m." with the Add to Calendar button** — that's a smart tactic for in-store traffic. Specific time, low friction to save it.
- **Visual hierarchy is tight** — FREE → product → details → CTA flows without confusion.

## What's weak

- **Two beach bucket emails in 48 hours.** The June 28th "mini bucket" email already told them I didn't engage. Sending this one a day later compounds the miss and burns trust.
- **"Made for sand, surf and summer family fun"** — this copy is written for a family beach vacation, not a Lowe's Pro or a weekend reno warrior. Zero connection to my reality.
- **"Learn more" CTA is a dead end** — for an in-store-only offer, this should say "Find my store" or "Get directions." Clicking "Learn more" about a free bucket I have to show up in person to claim feels pointless.
- **No tool, material, or project signal anywhere** — the email could have paired the beach bucket with a July 4th project hook ("Grab your FREE bucket while you pick up supplies for the long weekend") and at least tied it back to the store visit.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Ditch the second beach bucket send entirely.** If I didn't engage with June 28's version, suppress me from this one. Cadence fatigue is real and this is already the fourth promotional email this week.
- 2. **Pair the giveaway with a real project CTA** — "Pick it up when you come in for your July 4th project supplies" with a secondary hero on outdoor stain, composite deck boards, or fasteners. That's what I'm actually buying.
- 3. **Rewrite the CTA** — "Find your store" or "Check store availability" instead of "Learn more." In-store-only offers need wayfinding, not discovery.
- 4. **Segment harder on purchase history** — my account shows power tools, lumber, and fasteners. The beach bucket is a fine member perk; surface it at checkout or in a loyalty-points context, not a standalone promotional email send to a Pro account.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Grab your FREE member bucket — then grab your project supplies`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your FREE Lowe's bucket is waiting — July 4 only, in-store`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Pick it up Saturday while you stock up for the long weekend.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `In-store only, limited qty — plus everything you need for your July 4th project.`

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not captured** by the AgentMail relay. If this header is present at the MTA level but stripped by the relay, Gmail and Apple Mail will not display the native "Unsubscribe" button adjacent to the sender name. This degrades UX and increases spam complaints. Confirm the header is set at send time and survives relay passthrough.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent (RFC 8058).** Without this, one-click unsubscribe (as required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policies for >5,000 daily sends) is not active. This is a compliance risk for a national retailer at Lowe's send volume. The pair required:
```
List-Unsubscribe: <https://unsub.e.lowes.com/...>
List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click
```

**`Authentication-Results` header not captured by relay** — SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status is unknown from the intercepted copy. For an ESP-sent email from `e.lowes.com`, authentication should be verifiable. This is a relay instrumentation gap, not necessarily a live authentication failure, but it should be confirmed.

**CAN-SPAM physical address** and unsubscribe link presence cannot be confirmed from the truncated HTML, but these are standard elements for Lowe's sends. Verify they are present in the footer.
