# 💰Are you ready for MyLowe's Money Days?💰
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- Alright, so Lowe's hits me with another one the same day the July 4th parade ends — and honestly the first thing I notice is a loyalty email pivoting hard toward a rewards event rather than tools or project deals. "MyLowe's Money Days" July 9–12, up to $30 back when you spend $100+. It's fine. As a MyLowe's member I'm vaguely interested in cashback, but this isn't the same as yesterday's "50% off" hammer — it requires me to think, plan a trip, and trust the fine print. The "extra" in quotes in the body copy gave me a half-second pause.
- I wouldn't click through right now, but I might dog-ear this mentally if I was already planning a weekend run to Lowe's. The "July 9–12" window is real and specific — that's useful. The problem is there's no anchor product, no tool, no material that says *"this is the project you're already planning."* It's a loyalty coupon dressed up as an event.
- Mark as read, revisit Thursday before the event window opens. Won't forward — this is personal account stuff, not a "hey, check this out" send to my contractor buddy.

## What's working

- **MyLowe's membership hook is relevant to me** — I actually have this card, so "members get up to $30" lands as something I can use, not noise.
- **July 9–12 dates are prominent and specific** — I don't have to hunt for when this starts; it's right in the hero.
- **Clean layout, no rendering junk** — big bold MONEY DAYS! text, no overlapping elements, renders cleanly in the email client.
- **"For the first time ever" framing** — creates a small moment of genuine curiosity; this isn't just another Lowe's promo beat.
- **Brand is trusted** — MyLowe's Rewards sender is one I recognize immediately; no phishing vibe.

## What's weak

- **Zero product or project anchoring** — a $30 loyalty reward without a "use it on X" hook feels like a bank email, not a hardware store talking to me about my garage.
- **CTA is a tagline, not a direction** — "You Shop. You Save." is a slogan, not an action. Tell me to "Shop Power Tools" or "Build Your Cart."
- **The top-of-email banner note feels like a template artifact** — "NOTE: MyLowe's Rewards is right around the corner. There's still time to..." at the very top is confusing; the rewards program is already live, so what exactly is coming?
- **Coming off five emails in six days, cashback mechanics require more cognitive lift than I have right now** — post-July 4th send fatigue is real; I need either a big emotional hook or a specific product, not a rewards explainer.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor the reward to a real project category** — "Stack $30 back on your next deck, HVAC, or flooring run" makes this instantly applicable to a guy like me. Right now it floats free.
- 2. **Rewrite the CTA button** — "Shop July 9–12 to Earn" or "Start Earning Now" over "You Shop. You Save."
- 3. **Kill the template banner artifact at the top** — "right around the corner" when the program is already running is confusing and erodes trust.
- 4. **Add one featured category tile** — even a single "Shop Power Tools" or "Shop Flooring" section would give my eye somewhere to go after the hero.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Earn up to $30 back this weekend at Lowe's`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Spend $100 or more July 9–12 and pocket extra MyLowe's Money — first time ever.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your MyLowe's membership pays off this weekend. Here's how to claim it.`
