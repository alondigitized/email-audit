# Your to-do list? Done.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- After nine Father's Day emails in two weeks from Lowe's, I'll give them credit — this one felt like a gear shift. "Your to-do list? Done." in the subject line actually spoke to me. Weekend warrior, list of Saturday projects, Lowe's sender I trust — I opened it. That's a win for their team.
- Then the pivot hit me like a stripped bolt. HomeCare+. A $99/year subscription so a Lowe's contractor can come to my house and change my air filters. "Chores piling up?" Yes — because I've got a half-demo'd bathroom and a deck that needs staining, not because I can't figure out a furnace filter. "Leave it to us" is words that have never been in my vocabulary. This is built for the guy who thinks a Phillips head is a beer. Not me.
- The one hook that made me stop scrolling? The 5% off service products — filters, batteries — consumables I actually burn through. That's buried on the third column and it's the only reason someone like me might consider this. Otherwise, this is a read-and-ignore. Not forwarding to anyone. The offer is wrong for my segment.

## What's working

- **"MyLowe's Rewards" sender gets me every time** — brand equity does the heavy lifting here, full stop.
- **$99 price is right in the hero, no hunting required** — I respect when they don't hide the number behind a "learn more" wall.
- **Visual layout is clean** — hero, price, three-column features, CTA, footer. Logical flow, no clutter or render issues.
- **5% off consumables is the buried gem** — refrigerator filters, air filters, batteries are things I actually buy. That's the real pitch for someone like me.
- **Clean break from the Father's Day blitz** — after the June 18-21 holiday run, this felt like a campaign reset. I'll take a pivot over a ninth gift guide.

## What's weak

- **"Leave it to us" is the wrong message for a DIYer** — I ran my own romex and swapped my own garbage disposal last spring. A subscription to outsource light bulb changes is not why I'm on the MyLowe's list.
- **"Learn more" is the laziest CTA in email marketing** — If you want me to subscribe to a $99/year plan, tell me "Start my HomeCare+ plan" or "See what's covered." "Learn more" is where clicks go to die.
- **Changing lightbulbs is literally the first use case listed** — if that's your flagship service, you are not speaking to a guy with a breaker box he's memorized.
- **Zero connection to tools, materials, or projects** — not a single product tie-in for the segment of your list that actually builds things. This is pure service-tier content pushed to the wrong people.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment this list.** Members with heavy tool/lumber/fixture purchase history should get a different track. HomeCare+ is built for the homeowner who calls a handyman. I'm not that guy — and Lowe's has the transaction data to know it.
- 2. **Lead with the 5% consumables discount as a standalone email for the DIY segment.** "Air filters. Water filters. Batteries. 5% off year-round with HomeCare+." — that's a value prop I'd actually consider. The subscription is the mechanism, not the hero.
- 3. **Show me the math if you're keeping the service pitch.** "Four air filter changes a year averages $120 in service calls. HomeCare+ pays for itself." ROI math closes more deals than "chores piling up."
- 4. **Replace "Learn more" with "See what's included" or "Start for $99/year."** Any verb + noun is better than the black hole of Learn More.
- **Subject Alt A:** `"$99/yr for 2 visits + 5% off filters all year"`
- **Subject Alt B:** `"New: let Lowe's handle the 7 chores you keep skipping"`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `"Two in-home visits, 5% off consumables you already buy — new from MyLowe's."`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `"HomeCare+ is live. Here's the math on whether it makes sense for your home."`

**`@font-face` loaded conditionally (`<!--[if !mso]><!-->`)**

**Duplicate `local()` hints in `@font-face`:** Each font declaration repeats the `local()` hint twice (e.g., `local('Fellix Regular'), local('Fellix Regular')`). Redundant but harmless; minor code hygiene issue.

**Media queries:** Two overlapping breakpoints exist (`min-width: 375px and max-width: 600px` + `max-width: 480px`). The `480px` query will override the `375–600px` query on most phones. Behavior is probably intentional but should be verified against target device matrix.
