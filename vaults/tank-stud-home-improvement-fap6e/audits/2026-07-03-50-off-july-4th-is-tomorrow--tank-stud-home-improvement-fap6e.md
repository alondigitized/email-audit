# 50% OFF! July 4th is tomorrow🎇
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-03**
## Executive summary

- Three July 4th emails in three days and Lowe's finally puts something in front of me I can actually use. The subject got my thumb to tap — "July 4th is tomorrow" plus a hard "50% OFF" is exactly the kind of deadline pressure that works on a guy planning a Saturday morning project run. And unlike the July 1st Countdown Deals email that had me curious what each day would bring, this one delivers: grills in the $350-400 range, mowers at $299, a countdown timer with real hours on it. That's the language I speak.
- But they pulled the oldest trick in the book — subject says "50% OFF!" clean, no asterisk, and then the body says "up to 50% off." I've been shopping Lowe's long enough to know what "up to" means, and when I look at a $398 grill that was $479, I'm doing math in my head before I trust that headline. The June 30th MyLowe's Money Days email had me fired up because they told me exactly what I was getting back. This one makes me work for the real discount number.
- I'd click through on the $299 mower — that's a real weekend-warrior price for equipment I've been putting off. I'm not forwarding this to a friend because the "50% off" claim wouldn't hold up and I'd look like I didn't read the fine print. Mark as clicked, not forwarded.

## What's working

- Grills and mowers front and center — finally, no beach buckets, just actual hardware for a guy with a project list
- Countdown timer showing real hours remaining (17 hrs, 16 min) creates genuine urgency that actually moved me
- Specific prices on every product tile — $299, $349, $398, $399 — no "see price in cart" games
- TOOLS tab in the navigation bar gives me a direct escape hatch to what I actually came for
- American flag hero for July 4th feels earned, not forced — seasonally sharp

## What's weak

- "50% OFF!" in the subject vs. "up to 50% off" in the body — that bait-and-switch kills trust fast for a repeat buyer who checks his receipts
- Hero is a lifestyle porch scene, not a product — I have to scroll past it to find out what the deal actually is
- MyLowe's loyalty pricing is nowhere visible — after the June 30th Money Days email built that expectation, going silent on it here feels like a step backward
- Original pricing isn't clearly visible next to the hero products in thumbnail view — I have to click through to confirm the actual savings math
- This is email three in three days on the same July 4th angle; the cadence is starting to wear even when the content is better

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the subject/body mismatch** — pick your single best deal and run it clean: "Grills from $349. Mowers from $299. Last day." Every word earns trust or spends it.
- 2. **Lead with deal tiles, not lifestyle hero on mobile** — I don't need a porch scene to know I want a grill deal; put the price grid where my thumb lands first.
- 3. **Surface MyLowe's member pricing** — even one line: "Members save an extra X% — logged in?" That's what separates this from a random circular.
- 4. **Show was/now pricing on every thumbnail** — if I can see `~~$479~~ $398` in the tile, I don't have to click through to confirm I'm getting a real discount, and my confidence in clicking goes up.
- 5. **Ease the send cadence to every other day** — three in a row trains me to skim instead of engage.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$299 mowers. $349 grills. July 4th ends tonight.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 50% off grills & outdoor — last day before July 4th`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `17 hours left — here's what's still in stock at these prices.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `MyLowe's members: your extra savings stack on today's deals.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional layout with custom web fonts and dual responsive breakpoints. The primary technical concerns are missing alt attributes on all tracked impression pixels, PII exposure in pixel query strings, and compliance headers not captured by the relay.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**21 click-redirect links unresolved.** All outbound links route through Lowe's click-tracking domain; the QA probe skipped them. UTM parameter presence and landing page alignment cannot be confirmed without resolving the redirect chain.

**Impression pixel PII exposure.** Six `sli.lowes.com/imp` pixels and the open-tracking pixel encode the recipient email address in plain-text query parameters:

```
https://sli.lowes.com/imp?s=1090838&e=cole-drywall-lowes-fap6e@etell.app&p=732026&lctg=410146703&stpe=static
https://mi.lowes.com/p/cp/ee216b65ce7c7220/o.gif?mi_u=410146703
```

The `e=` parameter is the raw recipient address logged in every server-side request. Depending on jurisdiction, this constitutes PII in server logs accessible to the ESP and any CDN intermediary.

**CDN font version mismatch.** Two different scaffold versions are referenced in the same email:

- `DIN Next Regular` — `scaffold/1.4.4/fonts/din/din-next.woff`
- `DIN Next Bold / Black` — `scaffold/1.2.0/fonts/din/din-next-bold.woff` / `din-next-black.woff`

If 1.4.4 drops the bold/black variants, fallback behavior will differ across clients.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**8 images missing `alt` text** (QA confirmed):

| Image | URL pattern |
|---|---|
| Hero image | `image.e.lowes.com/…/c301e24e-…jpg` |
| Open-track pixel | `mi.lowes.com/p/cp/…/o.gif` |
| 5× impression pixels | `sli.lowes.com/imp?…` |
| Secondary image | `image.e.lowes.com/…/ad569dc5-…png` |

Tracking/pixel `<img>` elements should carry `alt=""` (empty string) to satisfy screen reader pass-through. Substantive content images require descriptive alt text.

**`.woff`-only font stack.** All `@font-face` declarations load only `.woff`. `.woff2` (20–30% smaller, universally supported in modern clients) is absent. Minor bandwidth impact; no rendering failure.

**Breakpoint targeting hack.** The 375–600 px media query appends `-webkit-min-device-pixel-ratio:0` to restrict styles to WebKit only. This is an intentional client-targeting technique but undocumented; it will silently no-op on non-WebKit mobile clients in that width range.

**Duplicate `@font-face` conditional blocks.** Fellix and DIN Next families are each wrapped in separate `<!--[if !mso]><!--> … <!--<![endif]-->` conditional comment pairs. Functionally equivalent to one block; not a bug, but increases HEAD payload.
