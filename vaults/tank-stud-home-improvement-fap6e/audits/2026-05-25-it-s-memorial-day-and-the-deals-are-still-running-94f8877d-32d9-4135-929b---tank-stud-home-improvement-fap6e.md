# It's Memorial Day, and the deals are still running😎
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- Another Memorial Day email from Lowe's — seventh-ish in eight days, and I'm starting to develop an automatic scroll reflex. I open it expecting maybe a Kobalt or Craftsman bundle since, you know, it's a holiday weekend and I've got a half-finished deck. What I get is another washer front-and-center, a "Love FREE? We do too" bundle section that's clearly appliance-and-dryer territory, and a couple smiling in their laundry room for the 10% financing pitch. The ceiling fan at $79.99 is the first thing in this whole email that even adjacent to my world — I could actually install that this weekend.
- There's a countdown timer running in the header, and I'll be honest, that's the one thing that made me pause. I had a similar moment with the May 22 "save up to $1,700" email before it turned out to be dryers all the way down. Burned twice is about all I can take before this sender hits the promotions tab permanently.
- I'm marking it read and moving on. Not clicking, definitely not forwarding. The fan is the only thing I'd click for, and I'm not scrolling past three appliance sections to get there.

## What's working

- The countdown timer in the header is a legit urgency signal — if I know deals end at midnight, that's real
- $79.99 on a ceiling fan is an actual price point I act on (summer, garage shop needs airflow)
- 3x rewards multiplier shows up clearly — if I'm buying anything this week, I'd want that stacked
- No broken images, no lorem ipsum, clean render — Lowe's at least knows how to send email that doesn't embarrass itself
- "Deals of the Day" section at the bottom signals there might be daily refreshed offers worth checking back for

## What's weak

- Hero is Memorial Day generic — no tool, no lumber, no project angle; just "deals are still here" pointed at appliances
- The couple-in-the-laundry-room photo for the 10% financing block is a demographic miss — that's not me, that's not my Saturday
- "Love FREE? We do too" bundles section is washer/dryer land dressed up in friendly language — I already know I don't need an appliance bundle
- Six competing content blocks means the ceiling fan — the only thing in my lane — is buried below the fold
- Cadence is the real problem: this is too many emails too close together, and they're not differentiated enough to justify the volume

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment tool/hardware buyers away from appliance buyers** — this is the actual fix. I've never bought a washer from Lowe's via email. My purchase history screams power tools and lumber. One send, two populations, two emails.
- 2. **Lead the savings with a category I care about** — "Up to 40% off" means nothing in the hero. Move the power tools, decking, or cordless gear to the top block for the DIY segment.
- 3. **Collapse the block count** — hero + one offer category + rewards + CTA. Four blocks, not six. More white space, faster read.
- 4. **Use the timer more aggressively** — put it next to a specific deal ("$79.99 ceiling fans — offer ends midnight tonight"), not floating above a generic hero.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Impression pixels with exposed recipient address** — five `sli.lowes.com` pixels include the persona's inbox address in plaintext:

The `e=` parameter exposes the subscribed address to Lowe's impression-tracking endpoint on every open. ### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text — 9 images:**

| Image | Type | Source |
|---|---|---|
| `c301e24e-…-8172-27b5acdcbf99.jpg` | Content | `image.e.lowes.com` |
| `ad569dc5-…-8bc0-1ae6ec389f05.png` | Content | `image.e.lowes.com` |
| `o.gif` (×2) | Open pixel | `mi.lowes.com` |
| `imp` (×5) | Impression pixel | `sli.lowes.com` |

Content images need descriptive `alt` values. Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` (empty string) — omitting the attribute entirely causes some screen readers to read out the raw `src` URL.

**Overlapping media query breakpoints** — two separate responsive blocks are defined:
- `@media screen and (min-width: 375px) and (max-width: 600px) and (-webkit-min-device-pixel-ratio:0)` (webkit-only)
- `@media only screen and (max-width: 480px)` (all screens)

Devices at 375–480px wide on WebKit satisfy both queries simultaneously. The second block lacks `-webkit-min-device-pixel-ratio:0`, so rules from the first block can be partially overridden. Low severity given the `.drop`, `.container`, and `.photo` rules are largely consistent between both blocks, but the overlap is fragile.

**`@font-face` declarations load `.woff` only** — no `.woff2` variants are specified for Fellix or DIN Next families. `.woff2` is ~30% smaller and supported in all major email clients that support web fonts at all. No `font-display` descriptor is set, which defaults to `auto` — acceptable but `swap` or `optional` would be more predictable.

**`<!--[if !mso]><!-->` wrapping is correct** — custom font CSS is gated behind non-MSO conditionals; Outlook will fall back to system fonts as intended.
