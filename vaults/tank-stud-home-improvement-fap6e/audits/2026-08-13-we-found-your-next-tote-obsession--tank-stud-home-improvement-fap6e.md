# We found your next tote obsession 🛍️
**Score:** 4/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-13**
## Executive summary

- A rolling tote bag. That's it. That's the whole email. I've got a fence project and a fridge on my radar from the last two Lowe's sends, and now they want me to get excited about a $19.98 tote bag with "Member Only Access." Come on.
- This isn't a project email, it's a merch email dressed up like a big deal — "2 Days Only," "Online Only," a giant hero shot, and a "roll with you" tagline for what amounts to a rolling grocery bag. I get that they're trying to build some kind of lifestyle accessory play, but for a guy who saves emails for the next reno phase, this has zero shelf life. Nothing to bookmark, nothing to plan around.
- Mark as read and move on. Not forwarding this to anybody — my buddy would ask why Lowe's is selling me a purse.

## What's working

- The "Member Only Access" lock badge is at least a nod to loyalty status, which I do notice as a MyLowe's Rewards member.
- Price and product name are big, bold, and unambiguous — I know exactly what it costs and what it is in two seconds.
- "Buy now" button is clean and easy to tap.
- No render bugs — everything's crisp, loads fine, no overlap.

## What's weak

- A tote bag is not a project. I don't shop Lowe's for accessories, I shop it for lumber, fencing, appliances, tools — this is a total category miss for my persona.
- "2 Days Only" urgency feels manufactured for a $20 impulse item, not credible urgency like the appliance rebate windows they've run before.
- No project tie-in at all — no "haul your tools," no "yard cleanup companion," nothing that would make this relevant to a guy mid-reno. Just a lifestyle pitch.
- Compare this to the fencing/pressure-washer email from 8/10 — that one had actual demographic signal (a guy driving screws into cedar). This one feels like it got pulled from a totally different customer file.

## Recommendations

- 1. Kill this as a standalone send, or bundle the tote as an add-on upsell inside a real project email (e.g., "grab this tote for hauling your fence hardware" tacked onto the backyard reset campaign). Standalone, it has no reason to exist for me.
- 2. If it must stand alone, reframe the copy around utility, not lifestyle — "haul mulch, tools, and hardware without wrecking your trunk" speaks to me; "roll with you" does not.
- 3. Swap the vague "2 Days Only" for something with teeth — actual expiration date or tie it to a real event (Labor Day, end-of-summer clearance) so it doesn't read as filler urgency.
- 4. Add a project cross-sell strip below the fold — "pairs well with" tool storage, yard bags, etc. — so there's at least one thing here for the DIY crowd even if the hero isn't for us.
