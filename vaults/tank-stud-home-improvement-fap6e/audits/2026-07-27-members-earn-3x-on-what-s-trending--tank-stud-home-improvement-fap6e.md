# ⭐ Members earn 3x on what’s trending
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-27**
## Executive summary

- Okay, I open this one and my first thought is: who is this for? The hero banner's got a "MrBeast Lab" builder kit and some kind of novelty football sitting next to "3x Points" — that's not a Lowe's email, that's a Toys R Us flashback. I scroll for a drill, a bag of quikrete, anything with a UPC I'd actually put in my cart, and instead I get a mystery bucket teaser for July 31. A literal black bucket with a question mark on it.
- This is Lowe's chasing the "collectible drop" hype train — Stanley cup mania, viral unboxing culture — and slapping their loyalty program on it. Fine for whoever their target demo is, but I'm a 49-year-old guy replacing a deck this summer, not lining up for a mystery bucket. Compare this to the "Smart home. Smarter savings" send from last Thursday (2026-07-24) that scored a 9 with me — that one had actual gear I use. This one's a swing and a miss.
- Not clicking, not forwarding. Marking as read and moving on — though I'll admit the "3x points" headline made me pause for half a second before I saw what the points were actually for.

## What's working

- Clean render, no broken images or overlapping text — at least the execution is tight.
- "Activate now" button is a clear, unmissable single CTA — I know exactly where to tap if I cared.
- "Valid through 8/5" gives me a real deadline, not some vague "for a limited time."
- Recognize the sender instantly — MyLowe's Rewards branding is consistent with every other email in this thread.

## What's weak

- The hero product is a kids' collectible builder kit and a football — zero relevance to a guy doing weekend reno projects. Feels like it landed in the wrong inbox.
- "3x points on viral products" isn't a real offer I can picture — no dollar amount, no percent off, just vague multiplier math I'd have to do myself after checking what's even included.
- "Next drop loading... mystery bucket" — I don't want a surprise bucket, I want to know if 2x4s are on sale.
- Requires me to "activate" the offer before it counts, which after the exclusions asterisk under the CTA is basically Lowe's telling me there's a hoop to jump through before any of this pays off.
- Sixth-ish Lowe's send inside two weeks per my inbox history, and this is the least project-relevant one of the bunch — cadence fatigue plus irrelevance is a bad combo.

## Recommendations

- 1. Swap the hero product entirely — lead with actual trending tool/hardware SKUs (power tools, outdoor equipment, whatever's moving this week), not toy kits. That's the single biggest fix.
- 2. Turn "3x points" into a real number — tell me what 3x actually nets on a $200 cart, or just give a flat % off instead. Points multipliers read soft next to a hard discount.
- 3. Cut the "mystery bucket" teaser or reframe it as a tool storage/organization drop if it's going to a DIY segment — right now it reads like a gimmick, not a product.
- 4. Segment this send — if Lowe's has my purchase history (I'm buying lumber and fasteners, not novelty toys), don't send me the viral-toy version of this campaign.
