# Delivered: Mystery savings 🎁
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-10**
## Executive summary

- Mystery savings. So Lowe's wants me to *tap to reveal* what they're actually selling me. After I opened the July 9th Money Days email — which was legitimately useful because it spelled out the loyalty event clearly — this one feels like a step backward. The whole hero is a locked envelope. That's a mobile app dark pattern, not an email. I already opened the thing; I shouldn't have to play a slot machine to find out if there's a deal I care about.
- Once I scroll past the envelope gimmick, what do I find? Décor. "Project Source." "allen + roth." "Style Selections." Ceiling fans and lighting to refresh a room. This is the same detour as that candle email from July 7th — somebody at Lowe's is misrouting me into the home-refresh segment. I'm a tools guy. I'm mid-reno. I'm looking for dimensional lumber pricing or a cordless combo kit bundle, not a pendant light for my powder room.
- The spend-to-save tiers at the bottom are interesting in principle — $1,000 for 10% off, $5,000 for 20%, $15,000 for 30% — but with zero tools in this send, there's nothing for me to stack them against. Mark as read, move on.

## What's working

- **Lowe's brand is immediately clear** — logo, blue palette, familiar nav bar. Zero question who sent this.
- **"Members get up to 30% off"** callout is visible and reinforces that my MyLowe's membership has actual value in this event window.
- **Spend-and-save tier ladder** is a genuinely compelling mechanic for a big project purchase — the $15K+ / 30% tier speaks contractor or full-gut-reno language.
- **Email is clean** — no broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds. Looks like it actually rendered.
- **"Limited Time Deals" badge** gives some urgency signal even if it's soft.

## What's weak

- **Mystery mechanic is backwards for email** — "Tap to reveal" requires an action inside an email client that may not even support it. I don't want to perform a trick to see a price drop.
- **Entire content block is décor** — Project Source, allen + roth, ceiling fans, bathroom fixtures. Not one power tool, not one lumber promo, nothing project-oriented.
- **Zero continuity from yesterday's Money Days email** — July 9 was "Money Days are HERE," today's send doesn't mention Money Days at all. Feels like two different teams sending to the same list.
- **The spend tiers are orphaned** — no anchor product, no category CTA tied to the thresholds. If you want me to spend $1,000+, point me at the SKUs worth buying.
- **"Deals of the Day" sign-up CTA at the bottom** — I'm already on the list, I'm already getting daily emails. This feels like an acquisition ask sent to an existing subscriber.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the mystery mechanic entirely.** Put the actual offer in the hero — a specific product category, a dollar-off threshold, a tool kit with a price. Mystery works on social. In email it just reads as "we don't know what to say to you."
- 2. **Anchor the spend tiers to tools and building materials.** If I'm going to spend $1,000 to unlock 10% off, show me a miter saw, a pallet of Hardiebacker, a pressure-treated deck bundle. The payoff has to match the ask.
- 3. **Pick a lane on Money Days.** Either this is a Money Days send — in which case say so and extend the July 9th thread — or it's a separate campaign. Right now it's neither, and the event momentum from yesterday is just gone.
- 4. **Remove the "sign up for Deals of the Day" block** for known subscribers. This is a basic segmentation fix that makes me feel like a known customer instead of a cold lead.
- **Subject Alt A:** `30% off for members — this weekend only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your $1,000+ project just got cheaper`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stack your savings on tools, lumber, and building supplies — Money Days through July 14.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Spend $1,000+, save 10%. Spend $5,000+, save 20%. Here's what to buy.`

## Technical Audit — Lowe's "Mystery Savings" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional table-based email with custom web fonts and heavy impression tracking via `sli.lowes.com`. The primary technical concerns are missing alt attributes across multiple images (content and tracking pixels alike) and compliance headers not surfacing through the AgentMail relay.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**19 tracking/redirect links skipped** — QA prober correctly declined to follow click-redirect domains, so final destination URLs and UTM parameter presence cannot be verified from this pass.

**Recipient email exposed in tracking pixel query strings** — five impression pixel URLs include the persona address in plaintext:
```
https://sli.lowes.com/imp?s=1090838&e=cole-drywall-lowes-fap6e@etell.app&p=7102026&lctg=410146703&stpe=static
```
This is standard ESP impression-tracking behavior, but confirms Lowe's ESP (Salesforce/ExactTarget `e.lowes.com`) receives the etell.app persona address on each open. No anomaly, but worth logging for persona exposure awareness.

**Subscriber ID visible in two tracking domains:**

- `lctg=410146703` in all `sli.lowes.com` pixels

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text — content images (real issue):**
| Image | URL |
|---|---|
| Hero/content JPG | `https://image.e.lowes.com/.../c301e24e-...jpg` |
| Content PNG | `https://image.e.lowes.com/.../ad569dc5-...png` |

These should carry descriptive alt text for screen readers and image-blocked rendering.

**Missing alt text — tracking pixels (minor, but noisy):**
Seven tracking pixels (`o.gif` ×2, `imp` ×5) have no `alt` attribute at all. They should carry `alt=""` explicitly to suppress screen reader announcements. Absence of the attribute is technically non-conformant even for spacer/pixel images.

**Overlapping CSS media query breakpoints:**
Two breakpoint blocks apply simultaneously in the 375–480px range:
```css
/* Block 1 */
@media screen and (min-width: 375px) and (max-width: 600px) and (-webkit-min-device-pixel-ratio:0)

/* Block 2 */
@media only screen and (max-width: 480px)
```
Both fire on WebKit devices between 375–480px. Properties like `.container`, `.drop`, `.photo`, and `.mobile-only` are redeclared in both blocks with different constraints (`max-width: 480px` vs `width: 100%`). Cascade order resolves this, but it's a maintenance hazard and may produce inconsistent rendering across WebKit versions.

**Web font format:** All `@font-face` declarations load `.woff` only — no `.woff2`. Email client support for `woff2` is limited, so this is low risk, but the CDN paths suggest `.woff2` variants may exist on `lowescdn.com` and could reduce font payload size.
