# Your kitchen's craving a refresh 👀
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-07**
## Executive summary

- I opened this and my first thought was "did someone at Lowe's forward me the wrong list?" Yesterday's email had me thinking about beach season somehow. The Father's Day free tool drops last week — DeWalt, Craftsman, Kobalt — that was Lowe's talking directly to me. This one opens with "appliance deals for the heart of your home" over a stock kitchen photo, and my brain immediately starts reclassifying the sender. That's lifestyle copy. That's the email my wife gets.
- Now, I'm not completely out. If I'm pricing out a new range for the kitchen gut-and-rebuild I've been pushing off since February, then appliance deals and a 0% APR financing offer for 36 months is genuinely useful information. That financing line is real money — five grand financed interest-free for three years is how you swing a kitchen reno without gutting the project budget. So there's a hook. It's just buried under vibes.
- The G.O.A.T. section grabs me a little — something is ending, there's some urgency — but I can't tell from here if it's a tool, an appliance, or a throw pillow. The "Kitchen design? We got you." section with what looks like a woman in a consultation setting? Completely irrelevant to me. I can design my own kitchen. Mark as read, might come back if I'm actively pricing appliances.

## What's working

- The 0% APR for 36 months financing is a legitimate hook for anyone about to drop serious money on appliances — that's a real project-financing tool
- Lowe's is immediately recognizable; zero trust friction with the sender
- "Shop now" CTAs are visible and unambiguous — no hunting for the button
- The G.O.A.T. teaser creates some urgency; something is ending, which gives me a reason to act now rather than bookmark
- Clean render — no broken images, no layout disasters, readable type

## What's weak

- "Your kitchen's craving a refresh" is magazine copy, not hardware-store copy — no weekend warrior talks like that about his reno
- The "Kitchen design? We got you." section is a complete miss for me; I'm not hiring Lowe's to design my kitchen
- No tools, no hardware, no installation supplies anywhere in the email — I'm a MyLowe's Pro-adjacent shopper and they forgot that half of me entirely
- After the Father's Day free-tool blitz and the beach garden bag email back-to-back, a kitchen lifestyle send feels like I've been dropped into a different segment mid-conversation
- "Limited Time" on the credit deal is vague — limited until when, exactly?

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the project hook, not the lifestyle hook.** "Pricing out a kitchen reno? 0% APR on appliances — 36 months." That's one sentence that turns this into a project-planning email, not a "maybe someday" email.
- 2. **Drop or demote the kitchen design consultation.** Replace it with installation-supplies cross-sell — range hoods, under-mount sink hardware, pull-out drawer organizers. Things I actually buy.
- 3. **Add a specific product hero.** Show me a 30" gas range with a real price and a real model number. Right now it's a kitchen vibe, not a purchase decision.
- 4. **Surface MyLowe's member pricing explicitly.** After the "Member Only Deals" email two weeks ago, I'm primed to look for that — Lowe's trained me. Don't let me down now.
- 5. **Put a deadline on the financing offer.** "0% APR through June 15" is infinitely more clickable than "limited time."
- **Subject Alt A:** `Kitchen reno: 0% APR on appliances, 36 months`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New range, new fridge — finance it at 0% through June`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Major appliances, no interest for 3 years — and something's about to expire.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `36-month 0% financing on the brands you already spec.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**20 click-tracking links skipped** by the QA probe — all routed through Lowe's ESP redirect domain (`e.lowes.com` or similar). This is expected behavior for a commercial ESP, but it means destination URLs and UTM integrity could not be verified programmatically.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing `alt` text — 7 images flagged:**

The two content images with missing `alt` will render as broken image icons in image-blocked environments (Outlook default, many corporate clients) with no fallback text. Screen readers will also have no context for these images.

The 5 impression pixel `alt` omissions are lower severity — tracking pixels are typically `alt=""` by convention, and the empty string is preferable to omission, but neither causes functional breakage.

**Font loading:** Custom fonts (`Fellix`, `DIN Next`) loaded via `@font-face` from `lowescdn.com` and `scaffold/` paths inside `<!--[if !mso]>` conditional comments. Correct pattern — Outlook will fall back to system fonts cleanly. No issues with the fallback chain.
