# What do you get the dad who has everything🤔
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-16**
## Executive summary

- Lowe's is back in my inbox — this is six or seven times in about a week at this point. I open it more from habit than excitement. The "Dad's got everything... except this!" hero is clever copy, I'll give them that, but the whole email is positioning me as a gift-giver when I AM the dad. I don't want to curate a wishlist — I want to know what's on sale that helps my weekend project.
- The bigger thing that stops me cold: the countdown timer shows 00:00:00. Dead clock, right in the hero. Kills any urgency the "10 Days of Father's Day Deals" headline was building. I've seen this same free grill spatula since the June 13 email — that one actually landed because it was fresh. This one feels like a follow-up nudge on creative I already processed.
- I scroll through, catch the 30% off section and the spatula pickup date (June 20), and mark it read. I'm not clicking, I'm not forwarding, and I'm one more Lowe's email away from moving this to a weekly digest folder.

## What's working

- "Dad's got everything... except this!" is punchy Father's Day copy — self-aware enough to not be corny
- FREE grill spatula is a strong member hook; "Get yours on Saturday, June 20" gives me a concrete date, not vague handwaving
- 30% off is a real number on a real outdoor category — I notice that
- Affirm/Apple Pay financing callout is a practical touch for anyone eyeing a big-ticket tool or appliance
- Brand layout is clean, Lowe's colors are right, nothing looks broken aside from the timer

## What's weak

- The countdown timer at 00:00:00 looks like the deal just died — or the JavaScript never loaded — either way it undermines urgency
- I already saw the free spatula offer three days ago on June 13; repeating it without escalation is cadence filler, not new value
- Gift-giver framing when I'm the dad shopping for myself creates friction — I want "deals for you" not "here's what to tell people you want"
- The G.O.A.T. wordplay section reads like they're still riding the NBA promo energy from the Brunson email; loses me completely in DIY mode
- Four competing sections (30% off, free spatula, G.O.A.T., Deals of the Day) dilute each other — no single thing owns this email

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the countdown timer or kill it entirely** — a 00:00:00 clock is worse than no clock; show a real deadline date in plain text if the JS is unreliable
- 2. **Lead with a project hook, not a gift hook** — flip the framing: "Dad's weekend deals are live" speaks to the guy holding the inbox, not the person buying for him
- 3. **Pick one hero offer and own it** — either the 30% off category OR the free spatula, not both competing plus two more sections below
- 4. **Retire the G.O.A.T. NBA tie-in** — it landed awkward in the June 14 Brunson email too; a DIY guy on a Father's Day weekend doesn't need sports cross-promotion in his tool email
- 5. **Throttle cadence to 3x/week max** — at 6-7 emails in a week I stop reading and start filtering
