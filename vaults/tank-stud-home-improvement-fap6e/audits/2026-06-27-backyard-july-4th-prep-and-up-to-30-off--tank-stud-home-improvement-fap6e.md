# Backyard, July 4th prep and up to 30% OFF 😎
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-27**
## Executive summary

- Two days ago Lowe's sent me the July 4th backyard email that actually landed — outdoor living, patio, the kind of stuff I'm thinking about heading into the long weekend. That one I'd have clicked on sight. This one? Lowe's came back to the same well and pulled up water that's about 20 degrees cooler. Hero image is an outdoor lounge set, hero copy leads with patio furniture, and I'm a project guy — not a Pottery Barn guy. The $399 riding mower and the $599 gas grill tiles in the middle are exactly what should have been the hero. They buried the lede.
- The email isn't broken — it renders fine, the bones are there, and Lowe's is a brand I trust enough to actually open. But when the best stuff is below the fold and the hero is a sectional sofa in a color my wife would pick, I'm already scrolling past on autopilot. The LiveNation concert upgrade block might be the wildest misfire I've seen from them in a while — I came here for outdoor power equipment, not Ticketmaster.
- Mark as read. I'm not forwarding this one.

## What's working

- The $399 riding mower and $599 gas grill tiles are exactly my speed — specific prices, specific products, clear "Buy now" buttons that actually go somewhere
- Lowe's Rewards member section is there; I'm in that program and any "FREE" with purchase messaging keeps me reading
- July 4th timing is still relevant — two more shopping days left and I haven't pulled the trigger on a grill yet
- Layout renders cleanly — no broken images, no lorem ipsum, no overlap garbage
- Same-day delivery banner is a real differentiator I use; good to see it called out

## What's weak

- Hero offer is patio furniture ("up to 30% OFF select patio furniture and accessories") — that's decor-adjacent, not project-adjacent; wrong lead for a weekend warrior audience
- This is the second July 4th backyard email in 48 hours; the 6/25 send already did this beat better and I'm already fatigued on the theme
- The LiveNation "Your concert season just got upgraded" block has zero business being inside a Lowe's promotional email — it reads like a brand partnership that slipped in from a different campaign
- "Up to 30% OFF" is classic bait language — a savvy shopper knows "up to" means one clearance end table qualifies, not the whole aisle
- Free offer in the Rewards section looks like it might be a bag or tote — I already got that punchline on the 6/25 email with the Americana tote

## Recommendations

- 1. **Swap the hero.** Lead with the $399 riding mower or $599 grill — that's the July 4th weekend project purchase, not patio cushions. Push furniture to secondary tile.
- 2. **Sharpen the offer.** Drop "up to" and commit to a real number on a specific product category — "30% OFF all cordless outdoor power" hits different than "up to 30% on select."
- 3. **Kill the LiveNation block entirely.** Or park it in a digest send — it pollutes the creative focus of what should be a tight outdoor/project email.
- 4. **Time-bound it explicitly.** "Offer ends July 4th" or "In-store pickup by July 3rd" is the urgency clock that actually moves a project-driven buyer.
- 5. **Rest the July 4th beat.** Two emails on identical territory in 48 hours trains subscribers to ignore the second one. Segment — send grills to buyers who looked at grills, not the same offer again to everyone.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Lowe's "Backyard, July 4th prep and up to 30% OFF"

### 1. Technical Summary
Well-structured HTML email with standard compatibility shims in place. The primary technical concerns are relay-obscured compliance headers, unresolved authentication status, and missing alt text on three images including what appears to be a tracking pixel.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**22 tracking links skipped (unverified)**
All click-through links route through a redirect domain (likely `click.e.lowes.com` or similar). QA probes were skipped for these, so broken-link detection has zero coverage for this send. If the redirect domain has a misconfiguration or CDN issue, all CTAs will silently fail — there is no post-send signal until click-rate drops.

**Tracking pixel present without alt text**
`https://mi.lowes.com/p/cp/baf5745ee8df512a/o.gif?mi_u=410146703` — open-tracking pixel has no `alt=""` attribute. Should be `alt=""` (empty, not absent) for screen reader hygiene and to suppress broken-image icons in clients that block remote content.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text on 3 images** (QA confirmed):
- `c301e24e-201b-46f9-8172-27b5acdcbf99.jpg` — likely a hero/product image; no `alt` attribute
- `o.gif` — open-tracking pixel; no `alt` attribute
- `ad569dc5-361d-4d7d-8bc0-1ae6ec389f05.png` — likely a content image; no `alt` attribute

Images without `alt` attributes (not just empty ones) fail screen readers and degrade the email in image-blocking clients. Hero images in particular should carry meaningful alt text describing the offer for WCAG 2.1 AA compliance.

**Font loading — WOFF only, no WOFF2**
All six `@font-face` declarations (Fellix and DIN Next families) reference `.woff` only. WOFF2 offers ~30% better compression. Not a breakage, but a bandwidth inefficiency on every open.

**Dual media query breakpoints with overlap risk**
Two separate media query blocks target mobile: one at `min-width: 375px and max-width: 600px` and another at `max-width: 480px`. The 375–480px range is governed by both simultaneously, which can produce specificity conflicts on mid-size phones. This is a latent rendering bug rather than a confirmed visual break, but warrants testing on a 390px viewport (iPhone 15 default).

**Legacy Hotmail/ExternalClass shims**
`.ReadMsgBody`, `.ExternalClass`, and Outlook 07/10 `border-collapse` hacks are present. These are harmless but have been dead code for ~8 years — they increase HTML weight with no benefit.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens visible in the truncated source (no `{{field}}`, `%%field%%`, or `[FIRST NAME]`-style literals). The open-tracking pixel includes a subscriber token: `?mi_u=410146703` — this appears to be a hardcoded value in the captured sample, which is expected for a rendered/received copy.

No issues found beyond the sample limitation.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header — status unknown** (QA WARN)
Header not captured by the AgentMail relay. This header is required for Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender compliance (enforced since Feb 2024 for >5K/day senders). If the header exists at the MTA layer but is stripped by the relay, this is a monitoring gap. If it is genuinely absent, this is a deliverability and compliance defect.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header missing** (QA WARN, RFC 8058)
One-click unsubscribe (the `POST` method variant required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy) was not detected. Same caveat as above — relay stripping vs. genuine absence needs to be confirmed at the raw header level.

**SPF/DKIM authentication status unknown** (QA WARN)
`Authentication-Results` header not captured. Sending domain is `e.lowes.com` (ExactTarget/Salesforce Marketing Cloud subdomain). SPF and DKIM almost certainly pass given Lowe's scale and ESP, but the audit cannot confirm it from available data. This is a relay visibility gap.

**CAN-SPAM physical address**
Not confirmed visible in the truncated source — standard for Lowe's emails, but should be verified in the full footer. No issue assumed pending full source.
