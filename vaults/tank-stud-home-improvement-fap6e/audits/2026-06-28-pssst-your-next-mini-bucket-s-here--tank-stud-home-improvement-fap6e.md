# Pssst! Your next mini bucket’s here 🤫
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-28**
## Executive summary

- Opened this, saw "mini bucket," and immediately felt like Lowe's sent the email to the wrong inbox. I'm in the middle of planning a deck project and a bathroom gut — I'm a real customer spending real money at Lowe's — and today's send is about a collectible mini bucket inspired by America's birthday. I've got six actual buckets in my garage right now, and none of them are decorative.
- This is a collector's email, plain and simple. The "Level up your collection" hero copy, the whisper "Pssst!" subject, the cute little teal Lowe's bucket in the corner — this is lifestyle merch content aimed at someone who's building a shelf display. That's not me. The real kicker is that Lowe's already sent me a July 4th email earlier today (the "Cue the fireworks" one). So I got two emails on the same Saturday: one with actual savings content, and one pitching a mini bucket.
- Mark as read, move on. Wouldn't click, wouldn't forward it. If anything, I'd forward it to my nephew who collects sneakers — it's that vibe.

## What's working

- **Brand recognition is instant** — MyLowe's Rewards in the header, the Lowe's colors and bucket imagery. I know exactly who sent this within half a second.
- **Layout is clean** — no clutter, no competing offers, easy to read at a glance. They didn't try to cram in a bunch of product tiles.
- **Subject sets accurate expectations** — "mini bucket's here" tells me exactly what's in the email. No bait-and-switch, which I appreciate.
- **Preheader actually adds context** — "Inspired by 250 years of American independence" tells me this is tied to the 250th anniversary thing, not just a random product drop. It complements the subject instead of repeating it.

## What's weak

- **Wrong product for my profile entirely** — I'm a MyLowe's member because I buy lumber, tools, and paint. Sending me collector merch ignores everything I've ever purchased.
- **No price anywhere in the email** — "Buy now" with zero indication of what it costs. If I were interested, I'd still have to click just to find out. That's friction.
- **Hero copy reads like streetwear, not home improvement** — "Level up your collection" is the language of sneakerheads and toy collectors. Nothing in this email sounds like it was written for a guy who just spent $400 on concrete mix.
- **Two emails in one day is too many** — The "Cue the fireworks" July 4th savings email already hit my inbox today. Back-to-back sends on the same day for a completely different product category feels like Lowe's isn't managing their send schedule.
- **Tied to a campaign I'm already fatigued on** — Brand history shows four Lowe's emails in three days, including the tote bag "member exclusive" on June 25th. The 250th anniversary theme is worn out for me at this point.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment this away from project-driven buyers.** MyLowe's has purchase data — this should go to buyers who haven't purchased power tools or building materials in 90+ days, or to a collector/lifestyle segment. Tank Stud should not be on this list.
- 2. **Show the price in the email body.** One number. Even if it's $14.99. "Buy now" with no price tag is lazy and adds a click barrier.
- 3. **Reframe for the DIY customer if they must send broadly** — tie it to something functional: "Stick one on your workbench. Screws, nails, whatever fits." Give me a use case I recognize.
- 4. **Limit sends to one per day.** Two different campaign emails in the same inbox on the same day hurts the brand.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Mini Lowe's bucket — July 4th edition is back`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The collector's bucket. Only while it lasts.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Hand-painted. Limited run. Grab it before it's gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `250th anniversary drop. Mini size, full Lowe's pride.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional table layout with functional responsive CSS. The primary concerns are compliance gaps (missing CAN-SPAM physical address, unconfirmed unsubscribe headers) and an authentication-results relay gap that leaves SPF/DKIM status opaque.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **29 tracking/redirect links skipped** by HTTP probe — all routed through `e.lowes.com` click-redirect domain. Cannot confirm destination URL health or UTM integrity without resolving these redirects.
- **Plain-text alternative is URL-heavy**: 3,017 of 3,959 chars (76%) are URLs. Spam filters (SpamAssassin, Gmail) penalize plain-text parts that are disproportionately link content. This is the single highest deliverability risk flagged.
- No broken or 4xx image URLs detected in the resolved image hosts (`image.e.lowes.com`, `lowescdn.com`).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- **Missing alt text:** `a22207c1-63c7-47c0-9e00-61227828e1fe.jpg` (`https://image.e.lowes.com/lib/fe9515707364027471/m/1/a22207c1-63c7-47c0-9e00-61227828e1fe.jpg`) has no `alt` attribute. Images-off rendering (Outlook default, many corporate clients) will leave a blank gap with no context.
- **Font loading:** Custom fonts (`Fellix`, `DIN Next`) load via `@font-face` with `.woff` only — no `.woff2` fallback. Minor efficiency issue; `.woff` is universally supported in email clients that honor web fonts, so no rendering failure expected.
- **Dual `!mso` conditional blocks:** Both `Fellix` and `DIN Next` `@font-face` declarations are wrapped in separate `<!--[if !mso]><!--> ... <!--<![endif]-->` blocks. Functionally correct but redundant — could be merged into one block to reduce HTML weight.
- **Media queries:** Two overlapping breakpoints defined (`min-width:375px AND max-width:600px` and `max-width:480px`). The 375–600px query targets only WebKit (via `-webkit-min-device-pixel-ratio:0`), so overlap is intentional, but the 480px cap in the second query creates a dead zone between 481–600px on non-WebKit clients. No fallback styles cover this range.
- **Legacy Hotmail/ExternalClass resets** present — harmless but no longer necessary for any current Outlook/Microsoft 365 client.
