# Don't miss your shot at Messi prizes
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- Third Messi rodeo in four days, and I called this card the second I saw the sender name. That "don't miss" phrasing would normally do something to me — I respond to deadline pressure, I'm a guy who loses sleep over flash sales on cordless combo kits — but I already burned that curiosity on July 19th and got a TikTok halftime show in return. The countdown clock is bold, the layout is clean, and the "Enter now" button is right where your thumb lands. None of that saves it when the offer is a sweepstakes for soccer memorabilia and a 10-foot Messi inflatable.
- Mechanically this is a well-built email. Urgency element, recognizable sender, clear CTAs, product block, secondary offer — it has the bones. What it doesn't have is anything I'd put in my garage, on my deck, or in my bathroom renovation. I'm mid-project on a vanity rough-in and I've got pressure-treated deck boards to price out this weekend. I have no use case for "Messi exclusives."
- Mark as read. No click. No forward. If another one of these arrives next week I'm dialing back my email preferences — and Lowe's is going to lose access to a guy who actually buys things from them.

## What's working

- **Countdown timer showing hours, minutes, and seconds** — real urgency mechanic; if this had been a power tool clearance I'd already have my credit card out.
- **"No luck required" secondary callout** — at least there's a buy-now path alongside the sweepstakes; it breaks the one-dimensional "enter to win" feel.
- **Clean render throughout** — images loaded, text didn't bleed, buttons were properly sized, hierarchy was intact.
- **MyLowe's Rewards sender name** — brand recognition is instant; I know who this is, which is the floor, not the ceiling.

## What's weak

- **Repeat Messi campaign three days after I ignored the first one** — the July 19 send already told me this wasn't for me; this one is just Lowe's proving they don't track that signal.
- **Zero home improvement content in the entire email** — a signed Messi jersey and a novelty inflatable are not in my project pipeline, my wishlist, or my garage.
- **No discount, no price, no savings signal** — the whole thing is "enter a contest" or "buy a conversation piece"; there's nothing here that saves me money on a project.
- **"Even more Messi? Absolutely." copy** — that voice is talking to a soccer superfan, not a 49-year-old who has a weekend window and a tile saw rented.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Suppress this send for the DIY/Pro segment that ignored the July 19 Messi email** — if I didn't click then, you're just burning goodwill now. Behavioral suppression is table stakes.
- 2. **Redirect the countdown mechanic to real product** — the urgency template is good. Put "Last 12 hours: 25% off Ryobi ONE+ cordless" behind that clock and I'm a very different reader.
- 3. **Add one project-shopper callout block** — even if you run the Messi hero for the broader list, give the tool and building materials crowd a secondary module: "Weekend project supplies — lumber, fasteners, concrete" keeps me in the email without blowing up the campaign.
- 4. **Cut the redundant urgency copy** — "Time is running out" in the banner and "Take your shot before the clock runs out" in the hero are the same idea twice; tighten to one.
- **Subject Alt A:** `"12 hrs left: your weekend project picks"`
- **Subject Alt B:** `"Clearance ends tonight — tools, deck, outdoor"`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Up to 40% off project supplies. Offer ends at midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stock up before Sunday. Treated lumber, fasteners, more.`
