# Backyard Deals Are Going Fast — Save Up to 25%
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-01**
## Executive summary

- Lowe's, backyard, summer — fine, I get it. But I'm staring at a hero image of a woman and a golden retriever chilling in Adirondack chairs, and I've already mentally filed this one under "not for me." I'm not decorating a scene from a lifestyle magazine, I'm trying to finish a pergola and maybe swap out the rusted grill grates before July 4th. The 20% off grills section is the first thing in this email that actually speaks to what I'd buy — and I have to scroll past patio furniture and a Memorial Day leftover banner to find it.
- Speaking of which — it's June 1st. Memorial Day was a week ago. That "Ending soon" appliance strip at the top feels like the marketing team forgot to flip the calendar. I clocked it immediately and it knocked a notch off my trust in the offer window for the whole email.
- The cadence is still at full blast — this is I think the fifth or sixth Lowe's email I've gotten this week, and yesterday it was stale Memorial Day yard deals. I scanned the grill deal and the patio umbrella section, but I'm not clicking. This one goes in the "maybe later" mental pile and I'm moving on.

## What's working

- **20% off grills and accessories** — that grill image mid-email is the first thing that actually stops my scroll. Clear product photo, clear discount. That's the language I speak.
- **25% off patio furniture** is at least a round, concrete number — no "up to extra 15% when you buy three items and stand on one foot" nonsense.
- **Lowe's brand recognition** — I know this sender, I'm on the list. Didn't go to spam, didn't confuse me about who sent it.
- **Clean render** — nothing's broken, buttons work, layout holds on mobile. That's table stakes but at least they cleared the bar.
- **Outdoor lighting "Set the vibe" section** — if I'm actually building out a deck this summer, Harbor Breeze post lights are exactly the kind of product I'd add to a project list.

## What's weak

- **Hero image is not me** — woman, dog, Adirondack chairs. I need to see a dude standing at a grill or a power-washed deck, not a Pottery Barn lifestyle shoot.
- **"Ending soon" Memorial Day banner on June 1st** — Memorial Day was May 25th. This strip should've been killed three days ago. It reads as disorganized.
- **Pool care section** — I don't have a pool. This felt like dead weight in an already long scroll.
- **Zero tool content** — I've got a MyLowe's account and I spend money in the tool aisle. The May 28 email had DeWalt and EGO; this one has nothing for a guy mid-project.
- **"Going Fast" urgency is hollow** — five emails in a week all pushing deals. Nothing "goes fast" when the sale never ends.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the grill deal, not the lifestyle hero.** Move "20% off grills" or even "25% off patio" to the top block with a specific product image of a grill a dude would actually want. Lifestyle copy is wasted on me.
- 2. **Kill the Memorial Day banner** — it's June 1st. Swap it for something timely: Father's Day is two weeks out, lean into that angle.
- 3. **Add one tool or project-adjacent hook** — even a "power washers for your summer cleanup" section would keep me in the email longer.
- 4. **Personalize the discount framing** — if you know I bought a grill last summer or browsed deck materials, say so. "Because you've been planning that backyard build" lands harder than generic "backyard deals."
- 5. **Dial back cadence or segment harder** — six emails in a week trains me to ignore them. If you must send, differentiate by segment: send me tool/project emails, send the lifestyle stuff to someone else.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Grills, patio furniture, lights — 20-25% off this weekend`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Father's Day is two weeks out. Grill deals going now.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Grill deals, umbrella sets, and deck lighting — this is the one to open.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Father's Day window opens now — grills and outdoor gear at 20% off.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard XHTML 1.0 Transitional email delivered via Lowe's ESP infrastructure (`e.lowes.com` / `sli.lowes.com`). QA flags three compliance header absences (likely AgentMail relay artifacts) and 7 images without alt text; two source-level defects — a malformed DOCTYPE and a CDN version split on DIN Next fonts — are confirmed in the HTML itself.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**20 click-tracking links not probed** — all route through an ESP redirect domain. Destination URLs and UTM parameter integrity are unverifiable from this source. Spot-check required (see §7).

**5 impression pixels embed recipient email in plain-text query parameter:**
```
https://sli.lowes.com/imp?s=1090838&e=cole-drywall-lowes-fap6e@etell.app&p=612026...
```
Standard ESP behavior; confirms open/impression tracking is persona-email-aware and correctly resolved.

**Duplicate send ID `s=1090834`** appears in two pixels with differing `stpe` values (`default`) and `li_coord` variants (`d`, `m`). This is consistent with desktop/mobile split impression tracking — expected, but confirm deduplication logic is intentional and not a copy-paste error in the send template.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**DOCTYPE malformed — missing space (source defect):**
```html
<!DOCTYPE html PUBLIC"-//W3C//DTD XHTML 1.0 Transitional//EN"
```
Should be `PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD`. Most parsers tolerate this, but it is a source-level defect that could trigger quirks mode in stricter renderers.

**DIN Next fonts load from two different CDN scaffold versions:**
- `DIN Next` → `scaffold/1.4.4/fonts/din/din-next.woff`
- `DIN Next Bold` / `DIN Next Black` → `scaffold/1.2.0/fonts/din/din-next-bold.woff`, `din-next-black.woff`

Version mismatch risks inconsistent glyph metrics if the font binaries differ between versions. Should be normalized to a single scaffold version.

**Duplicate `local()` hints in all Fellix @font-face declarations:**
```css
src: local('Fellix Regular'), local('Fellix Regular'), url(...)
```
Redundant but harmless. Minor source hygiene issue.

**Overlapping media queries — 375–480px range hits both breakpoints:**
- Query 1: `(min-width: 375px) and (max-width: 600px) and (-webkit-min-device-pixel-ratio:0)`
- Query 2: `only screen and (max-width: 480px)`

On a 430px WebKit device (iPhone 14/15 default viewport), both fire. Rule ordering determines the winner. Verify no layout conflicts at current iPhone viewport widths.

**7 images missing alt text (QA confirmed):**

| Image | Type | Issue |
|---|---|---|
| `c301e24e-...jpg` | Product/hero image | Needs descriptive alt text |
| `ad569dc5-...png` | Decorative or badge | Needs `alt=""` if purely decorative |
| 5× `sli.lowes.com/imp` pixels | Tracking pixels | Must carry `alt=""` (empty string) — missing `alt` causes screen readers to announce the `src` URL |

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent** (QA WARN): RFC 2369 compliance unverifiable at this layer. QA attributes this to the AgentMail relay not forwarding the header — confirm by inspecting raw MTA-level headers before treating as a sender-side gap.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent** (QA WARN): RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe not confirmed. Gmail and Apple Mail require this for bulk senders exceeding 5,000 messages/day. If the header is genuinely absent at the sender (not a relay artifact), this is an active deliverability risk for a sender at Lowe's volume.

**`Authentication-Results` header absent** (QA WARN): SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status not captured by relay. Sending subdomain is `e.lowes.com` — verify SPF `include:` coverage and DKIM selector alignment with the `lowes.com` DMARC policy.

**CAN-SPAM footer** not verifiable from truncated source. Lowe's is a known compliant sender; confirm physical address and unsubscribe mechanism are present in the full HTML.
