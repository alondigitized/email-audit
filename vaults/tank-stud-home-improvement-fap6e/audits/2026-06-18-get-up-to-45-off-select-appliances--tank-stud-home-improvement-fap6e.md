# Get up to 45% OFF select appliances 🥳
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-18**
## Executive summary

- I'm at Lowe's at least twice a month — this sender gets my attention by default. The subject drops a hard number (45% off appliances) which is the right hook for me. I click in, and... it's a Father's Day umbrella email that tries to cover appliances, kitchen islands, flooring, home care subscriptions, and gift cards all in one scroll. That's five different asks. I came in for the appliance deal and I had to hunt for it past the "Whatever Dad wants" hero and a countdown clock.
- Here's the thing — I'm not shopping for Dad. I *am* Dad. And when Lowe's frames me as a gift recipient instead of the guy who's about to price out a kitchen island reno, it's a miss. The kitchen remodel content in the middle of the email — islands, flooring — actually speaks to me. That's where I live on weekends. But by the time I get there, I've already half-checked out because the appliance offer in the subject didn't show up clean at the top.
- I'll mark this as read, maybe bookmark the flooring deal. No click, no forward. This is the fourth or fifth Lowe's email in the past week — by this point I'm on autopilot and this one didn't do enough to break through the noise.

## What's working

- **45% off is a real number.** The subject line hook is concrete and I opened it — that's the job done at step one.
- **Flooring and kitchen island content mid-email is legit.** The "from simple refreshes to full makeovers" section with 40% off flooring and the island update angle — that's my reno language.
- **Countdown timer creates real urgency.** "10 Days of Father's Day Deals" with the live clock at the top is a solid pressure move that I noticed immediately.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no layout chaos, readable on the screen — Lowe's executes their templates well.

## What's weak

- **Father's Day as the frame positions me as the receiver, not the buyer.** "Whatever Dad wants" — I'm the dad. This framing feels like I'm supposed to forward it to my wife, not shop it myself.
- **Subject says appliances, email leads with Father's Day gifting.** The bait-and-switch isn't dramatic but it's enough friction that I'm already looking for the exit before I find the appliance module.
- **Gift cards section ("Build it, spin it! / $14.98") is dead weight for me.** I don't buy gift cards at Lowe's. That's a full block of real estate I scrolled past.
- **"Deals of the Day — Sign up" module is a newsletter promo inside a promo email.** Two asks in one send. I'm already subscribed. This just adds clutter.
- **Cadence fatigue is real.** June 14, 15, 16, and now two on June 18 — the Halloween email from the same day didn't help. I'm starting to skim Lowe's by reflex.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the email with the appliance deal instead of the Father's Day umbrella hero.** I came in for 45% off — show me that first, with a specific category (ranges, dishwashers, refrigerators) visible in the hero. Save the umbrella messaging for a secondary banner.
- 2. **Flip the Father's Day framing.** Instead of "Whatever Dad wants," try "Treat yourself to the upgrade you've been putting off" — turns me from a recipient into the shopper.
- 3. **Cut the gift card block.** Replace it with a specific project bundle (e.g., kitchen island + flooring combo deal). That's the kind of cross-sell that actually fires my brain.
- 4. **Consolidate to three sections max.** Appliances → Kitchen/Flooring → One CTA. Five modules is too much for a promotional email in a high-cadence week.
- 5. **Remove the "Deals of the Day — Sign up" module** from a send to existing subscribers. It's noise.
- **Subject Alt A:** `45% off appliances ends soon — weekend pickup ready`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Kitchen reno season: 45% off appliances + 40% off floors`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Ranges, dishwashers, fridges — pick yours up before the deal drops`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stack the appliance + flooring deals before Father's Day wraps`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**10 images missing `alt` text** (QA-confirmed):

| Image | URL (abbreviated) |
|---|---|
| Hero/content `.jpg` | `image.e.lowes.com/…/c301e24e.jpg` |
| Content `.png` | `image.e.lowes.com/…/ad569dc5.png` |
| Open pixel `o.gif` (×3) | `mi.lowes.com/p/cp/…` |
| Impression pixel `imp` (×5) | `sli.lowes.com/imp?s=…` |

Tracking pixels and beacons should carry `alt=""` (empty string) to satisfy screen reader and accessibility linting requirements. Content images need descriptive alt text.

**Duplicate `<!--[if !mso]>` conditional blocks.** The `@font-face` declarations are split across two separate MSO conditionals — one for Fellix variants, one for DIN Next. These could be consolidated into a single block without functional impact.

**Responsive breakpoint overlap.** Two media queries target overlapping ranges:
- `@media screen and (min-width: 375px) and (max-width: 600px) and (-webkit-min-device-pixel-ratio:0)` — WebKit/Blink only
- `@media only screen and (max-width: 480px)` — all agents

The first query's `-webkit-min-device-pixel-ratio:0` qualifier excludes Firefox and Samsung Internet from the 375–480px mobile styles, potentially causing layout inconsistencies on those clients.

**`.mobilehide` color hack.** CSS rule `a.mobileshow, .mobilehide { display: none; color: #fff; }` uses `color: #fff` as a Gmail visibility workaround. Functional, but worth confirming against current Gmail rendering behavior.
