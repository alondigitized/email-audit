# Gifts under $25, $50 and $100? Just a tap away👆
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-05**
## Executive summary

- My gut says this is a holiday catalog with a Black Friday banner stapled on top. I opened it hoping the subject's "just a tap away" meant a quick list of gifts I could grab. What I got was five or six competing pitches: Black Friday, Gift Zone, free gifts, MrBeast, and Deals of the Day. I don't know where to look first.
- The one part that works for me is the "Gifts under $100" tile with the red drill, and the DeWalt router and battery/charger at the bottom. Those are my kind of gifts. The Carhartt-looking beanie is fine too. The rest, like the Roku, the squishy dumpling toy and the MrBeast tees, isn't Lowe's to me. I'd skim it, maybe tap the drill, and probably save it for the next time I'm buying a tool. I'm not forwarding it. It's the fourth Lowe's seasonal email in five days (the Nov 2 Christmas trees, the Nov 3 Traeger grills, the Nov 4 appliances), and none of them has been about a project I'm doing this weekend.

## What's working

- The gift tiles for under $25, $50 and $100 match the subject line, so the email delivers what it promised.
- The red drill under "Gifts under $100" is a real tool gift. That's the one tile that spoke to me.
- The DeWalt 20V battery/charger and the DeWalt XR router at the bottom have product names and Buy now buttons. That's the closest this email gets to a pro-grade offer.
- The "My Lowe's Rewards: FREE same-day delivery" strip near the top is a member perk I noticed. It's the only thing in the email that treats me like a known customer.
- The nav bar (Appliances, Daily Deals, Bathroom, Outdoor Tools) is clean and easy to tap.

## What's weak

- There's no single hero. Black Friday, Gift Zone, FREE gifts, MrBeast and Deals of the Day all fight for attention, and the page runs very long.
- The "Beat Santa to the best holiday gifts this year!" hero shows a living-room scene with no clear product. It's decoration, not an offer.
- "Gifts under $25" opens on a beanie and "$50" on a Roku. Neither one is why I shop at Lowe's.
- "FREE gifts with select purchases" gives no detail on what I'd buy or what I'd get. That's vague, and I'm wary of vague.
- The MrBeast block feels off-brand for a 49-year-old DIYer, and the squishy dumpling toy is just noise.
- The Black Friday banner says "The BIG deals are here" but shows no number. The "FREE same-day delivery*" line has fine print at the very bottom (a $25 minimum, order by 2pm). The "Limited Time" label has no end date.
- There's no rebate, bundle or project angle, and nothing about pro pricing or stock.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one hero and cut the rest. For me it would be "Tool gifts for the guy who has everything," with the drill, router and battery kit as the main block and a clear price on each.
- 2. Lead the price tiles with Lowe's strengths. Put tools in the $25 tile (a tape measure or a bit set) and in the $50 tile, not a beanie and a Roku.
- 3. Spell out the free gift offer: what I buy, what I get and by when. Put the same-day delivery fine print next to the banner.
- 4. Add an end date to "Limited Time" and a price to the Black Friday hook.
- 5. Cut the MrBeast and dumpling-toy blocks, or move them to a segment of holiday-toy shoppers.
- 6. Send fewer seasonal emails in a row. Mix in one project email so the feed doesn't feel like a catalog.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML is structurally intact and uses standard responsive patterns, but it has a malformed DOCTYPE, 17 images with no `alt`, and recipient-identifying data in 15 third-party pixel URLs. Compliance headers and 44 tracked links could not be verified (relay and probe limits). The source is truncated, so the footer and unsubscribe markup were not inspected.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Unverified links:** All 44 tracking links were skipped by the HTTP probe because they are click-redirect domains. Destination validity, redirect chains, and 404s are unknown.
- **Recipient email in pixel URLs:** All 15 `sli.lowes.com/imp?...` URLs carry the recipient address in plaintext (`e=cole-drywall-lowes-fap6e@etell.app`). It is exposed to the pixel host and any proxy or log that sees the image request.
- **Duplicate impression pixels:** Two `s=` IDs appear twice each, which can double-count impressions: `s=2019286` and `s=1090834` (both `stpe=default&li_coord=`).
- **Empty `li_coord=` parameter:** It appears on 4 pixel URLs (`s=2019286` ×2, `s=1090834` ×2). It may be a legitimately server-populated value, or an unresolved token.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Malformed DOCTYPE:** `<!DOCTYPE html PUBLIC"-//W3C//DTD XHTML 1.0 Transitional//EN" ...>` has no space between `PUBLIC` and the quoted identifier. The `xmlns` and DTD URLs also use `https://`, whereas the canonical XHTML namespace is `http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml`. Most clients tolerate this, but it is invalid and can trigger quirks mode in some parsers.
- **Missing `alt` on content images (2):**
  - `https://image.e.lowes.com/lib/fe9515707364027471/m/59/c301e24e-201b-46f9-8172-27b5acdcbf99.jpg`
  - `https://image.e.lowes.com/lib/fe9515707364027471/m/71/ad569dc5-361d-4d7d-8bc0-1ae6ec389f05.png`

  With images blocked or read by a screen reader, these are silent or announce the filename.
- **Missing `alt` on tracking pixels (15):** These are decorative, so they should carry `alt=""` and ideally `width="1" height="1"`, so screen readers skip them.
- **Remote fonts:** The `@font-face` rules for Fellix and DIN Next are wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!-->`, which is correct. I couldn't confirm fallback font stacks in the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No unresolved `%%token%%` or `{{ }}` patterns were found in the visible source. The only questionable value is the empty `li_coord=` noted above. The recipient email is correctly populated in the `e=` parameter, so merge worked, but see the privacy note in section 2.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe:** Not found. QA notes the AgentMail relay may strip it, so this is unconfirmed, not a confirmed defect.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058):** Not found, with the same relay caveat. Gmail and Yahoo require one-click unsubscribe for bulk senders.
- **Authentication-Results:** Not captured, so SPF, DKIM, and DMARC alignment are unknown. The From domain is `e.lowes.com`.
- **Body unsubscribe link and physical address:** Not assessable, because the source is truncated before the footer.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not assessable. The 44 destination links were not probed, and no UTM parameters or final landing URLs are visible in the truncated source. Tracking is evidently done through redirect domains rather than visible query parameters.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Add descriptive `alt` text to the two content images, and `alt=""` plus explicit 1×1 dimensions to all 15 `sli.lowes.com/imp` pixels.
2. Fix the DOCTYPE to `<!DOCTYPE html PUBLIC "-//W3C//DTD XHTML 1.0 Transitional//EN" "http://www.w3.org/TR/xhtml1/DTD/xhtml1-transitional.dtd">` and use the `http://` XHTML namespace.
3. Stop putting the plaintext recipient email in pixel query strings. Use an opaque subscriber ID instead.
4. Dedupe the repeated impression pixels (`s=2019286`, `s=1090834`), or confirm that the double fires are intentional.
5. Confirm that `li_coord=` is populated server-side at request time.
6. Re-run the QA against the raw message, not the relay copy, to verify `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, and `Authentication-Results`.
7. Resolve the tracked links with a redirect-following check, and review the full untruncated HTML for the footer, unsubscribe link, and UTM parameters.
