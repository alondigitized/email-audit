# Kickstart summer with more points ☀️
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-15**
## Executive summary

- I already opened a Lowe's email this morning — the "45% OFF Memorial Day deals" one — and now here's another one asking me to "activate" a points multiplier with a picture of a grill? Look, I own a grill. I also own a house that needs work every single weekend. The grill is fine, but this email isn't talking to the guy who's got a weekend project list sitting on the kitchen counter. It's talking to a generic "summer is coming" audience, and I'm not that guy.
- The loyalty angle isn't nothing — I do care that my Silver status is $500 away, and I've thought about grinding toward Gold. But burying that in a sea of grilled chicken imagery doesn't make me want to reach for my phone. The free same-day delivery banner at the bottom is actually the strongest real-world utility in this whole email, and it's the afterthought.
- Would I click? Maybe the "Shop now" on same-day delivery if I needed something today — but I'd be clicking despite this email, not because of it. Not forwarding this to anyone. Mark as read, move on.

## What's working

- **Loyalty status nudge is real:** "Silver status is only ~$500 away" at the top is genuinely useful for a MyLowe's member who's tracking their tier — that's the most targeted thing in the email.
- **Memorial Day peg is on-brand:** The seasonal hook is correct, at least. This is the right weekend to be in my inbox.
- **Free same-day delivery is a practical win:** If I need something for a project today, that banner matters. Concrete, useful, no hoops.
- **Clean render:** No broken images, no overlapping text, layout held together. Easy to scan.

## What's weak

- **Points activation is not a product deal:** "3x Points" on mystery "Fresh outdoor picks" doesn't tell me a single thing I can act on. What picks? What products? What does 3x points even get me if I don't know what I'm buying?
- **Grill imagery doesn't move me:** I grill, sure. But I came to Lowe's because of the 60% off framing nailer email from four days ago. This isn't that. There's nothing in this hero that says "Tank, there's a deal on something you'll use this weekend."
- **"Activate now" CTA is friction-first:** I have to do a thing before I know what I'm activating for. Show me what's in the deal, then ask me to activate it.
- **Second email today:** I already read a Lowe's email this morning. Two sends in one day and the second one has less meat on the bone — that's the wrong way to sequence this.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with a specific product category, not the points mechanic.** "3x Points on outdoor power equipment this weekend" tells me what I'm getting. "More points" tells me nothing.
- 2. **Swap the grill hero for something in the tool/hardware/outdoor power aisle.** A pressure washer, a string trimmer, a generator — any of those on a summer hero pulls a DIY weekend warrior faster than food on a grill.
- 3. **Move same-day delivery up:** If that's genuinely available today, it's worth being in the top third of the email, not the footnote.
- 4. **Sequence this later in the week:** Don't send a weaker points email the same day as the "45% off Memorial Day" email. Stagger by 48 hours minimum so each send has room to breathe.
- **Subject Alt A:** `3x Points on outdoor tools — activate before Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Earn more on power tools this Memorial Day weekend`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Double down on your Silver status while the deals are still live.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stack points on the outdoor gear you're already buying this weekend.`

### 5. Compliance

**[HIGH] No physical mailing address detected.** CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in the email body. Not found in the truncated source or QA scan. This is a statutory requirement, not a best practice.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe` header absent from relay capture.** QA notes this may be a relay artifact — AgentMail may not forward the header. Confirm the raw envelope at the MTA. If genuinely absent, major inbox providers (Gmail, Yahoo) will suppress the one-click unsubscribe UI and may reclassify bulk mail.

**[WARN] `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent (RFC 8058).** Without this, Gmail's and Yahoo's one-click unsubscribe buttons (required for bulk senders ≥5k/day since February 2024) are not activated. Given Lowe's send volume, this is almost certainly a relay capture gap rather than a real omission — but must be verified against the raw SMTP headers.
