# 💰MyLowe's Money Days end TODAY💰
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-12**
## Executive summary

- Final-day urgency, loyalty currency, and a concrete earn mechanic — this is the kind of email I actually want from Lowe's. After yesterday's prizefight contest email that told me basically nothing actionable, this one leads with "$10 in extra MyLowe's Money on a purchase of $50 or more." That's a real offer I can do math on. I've got a weekend list that'll clear $50 without breaking a sweat.
- That said, the email is a scroll-fest. The hero converts into a stack of four or five separate sub-promotions — DeWalt tools, appliances, 20% off select grills, same-day delivery — and by the time I'm halfway down I've lost the thread. The urgency from "ends TODAY" gets diluted every module you scroll past.
- I'd click the DeWalt section if I needed to top up a battery or grab a drill, and the $10 earn mechanic is legitimate enough that I'd at least open the app. I wouldn't forward this — it's a loyalty-member retention play, not shareable content. Mark it "useful but cluttered" and move on.

## What's working

- **Clear earn mechanic up front** — "$10 in extra MyLowe's Money on a $50+ purchase" is specific and actionable; I can immediately calculate whether my weekend cart qualifies.
- **"FINAL DAY" banner** — the blue badge at the top communicates urgency without screaming at me in all-caps subject-line spam style.
- **DeWalt block lands well** — tool imagery with a branded logo speaks directly to me; this is the product category where I actually buy from Lowe's.
- **Same-day delivery callout** — useful signal for a weekend warrior who realizes mid-project he's short a fitting.
- **Lowe's brand trust is solid** — I know exactly what I'm clicking into; no surprises on the sender.

## What's weak

- **Too many competing modules** — grills, appliances, DeWalt, same-day delivery, and Deals of the Day all stacked vertically dilutes the "$10 earn" urgency I was sold on in the subject.
- **No specific product hero** — the DeWalt block shows a vague tool collage, not a specific SKU with a price; I can't evaluate the deal without clicking.
- **20% off "select grills" at the bottom** — it's mid-July and grilling season peaked six weeks ago; this feels like a clearance dump, not a relevant offer.
- **"Deals you'll love" teaser module** — this is filler. Generic lifestyle photo of a kitchen island with zero offer specifics. Wasted real estate.
- **No loyalty tier or point balance shown** — if I'm a MyLowe's member, tell me how close I am to my next reward. That's what makes "earn $10 more" actually motivating.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse to one hero, one CTA** — drop the grill clearance and the lifestyle teaser entirely; redirect that space to reinforce the $10 earn mechanic with a visible countdown timer or cart-minimum reminder.
- 2. **Make the DeWalt block a specific product** — show one SKU (e.g., "DEWALT 20V MAX Brushless Drill/Driver — $129") with a price instead of a brand collage; that's what gets a click from me.
- 3. **Surface my current MyLowe's balance** — "You have $X in MyLowe's Money — earn $10 more today" turns a generic promo into a personalized nudge.
- 4. **Kill the "Deals you'll love" placeholder** — replace with in-store pickup confirmation copy since this is a same-day/last-day situation.
- 5. **Tighten the subject** — the emoji bookends are fine but the copy could be sharper:
- **Subject Alt A:** `Earn $10 extra today — MyLowe's Money Days close at midnight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last chance: $10 bonus on any $50 Lowe's purchase`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Hit $50, pocket $10 in rewards. One day left.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop tools, appliances, and more before tonight's deadline.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is a standard table-based XHTML 1.0 Transitional build with no critical rendering failures. The primary actionable findings are missing alt text on content images, unverified RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe compliance, and recipient PII exposed in impression pixel query strings.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**20 click-redirect links skipped by QA probe** — all route through Lowe's ESP click-tracker (domain pattern consistent with `e.lowes.com` redirect infrastructure). Destination URLs and UTM parameters could not be validated.

**Recipient email address exposed in pixel URLs (plaintext):**
All five `sli.lowes.com` impression pixels embed the subscriber's email address as a query parameter with no encoding:
```
https://sli.lowes.com/imp?s=1090838&e=cole-drywall-lowes-fap6e@etell.app&p=7122026&lctg=410146703&stpe=static
```
`e=` carries the raw address in the GET request. This is logged in server access logs and any intermediate CDN/proxy. Standard practice is to use a hashed or tokenized subscriber ID instead. The `mi.lowes.com` open pixel similarly exposes subscriber ID via `mi_u=410146703`.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text — content images (2):**
These appear to be product/promotional images, not tracking pixels. Missing `alt` fails WCAG 2.1 AA and will render blank in image-blocking clients (Outlook default, many corporate environments):
- `https://image.e.lowes.com/lib/fe9515707364027471/m/59/c301e24e-201b-46f9-8172-27b5acdcbf99.jpg`
- `https://image.e.lowes.com/lib/fe9515707364027471/m/71/ad569dc5-361d-4d7d-8bc0-1ae6ec389f05.png`

**Missing alt text — tracking pixels (acceptable, but flagged by QA):**
`o.gif` (open pixel) and five `sli.lowes.com` impression pixels have no `alt`. These should carry `alt=""` explicitly rather than omitting the attribute entirely — the distinction matters for some screen reader parsers.

**Font loading — `.woff` only, no `.woff2`:**
All six `@font-face` declarations (Fellix and DIN Next families) load only `.woff`. WOFF2 offers ~30% smaller file size and is supported by all clients that support custom fonts in email. Not a blocking issue, but adds unnecessary payload on supported clients.

**Obsolete client hacks present (non-breaking):**
`.ReadMsgBody`, `.ExternalClass` rules target legacy Hotmail/Windows Live (deprecated 2012). These add CSS weight but cause no harm.

**Media query targeting:**
The primary responsive breakpoint uses `-webkit-min-device-pixel-ratio:0` as part of the selector — this intentionally restricts styles to WebKit/Blink only, which appears deliberate for the 375–600px range. Verify this matches intended targeting.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header: NOT FOUND**
QA notes this may be a relay capture issue (AgentMail), but if genuinely absent from the envelope, this violates Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender requirements (enforced February 2024 for senders >5k/day) and CAN-SPAM best practices. Evidence: `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe header not found`.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058): NOT FOUND**
One-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail for bulk senders. Without this header, Gmail will surface its own unsubscribe prompt which bypasses the sender's list management. Evidence: `[WARN] List-Unsubscribe-Post header not found (RFC 8058)`.
