# Top picks for summer? Right this way👉
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-28**
## Executive summary

- Six Lowe's emails in essentially a week, and this one opens with "Here comes what's hot" over a summer lifestyle hero — okay, I'll bite, because right below that is a DeWalt and EGO section with actual tool deals. That pulls me back in after the MrBeast Workshop nonsense from yesterday. A FREE item with a qualifying purchase and EGO power equipment at $299 is the kind of thing I'll actually read.
- Here's the thing though: Lowe's crammed six distinct content blocks into this email — tools, a savings banner, 3x MyLowe's points, Father's Day gift cards, a "Made here at home" patriotic furniture section, and a Deals of the Day signup. It's like they couldn't decide what they were selling and just sent everything. I came in for tools and ended up scrolling past Adirondack chairs and American flags.
- The 3x points activation is genuinely useful — I'm a MyLowe's member, and that's the kind of hook that makes me feel treated like a known customer. But the subject line burned that credibility before I even opened it. "Top picks for summer" is what you write when you've got six different departments arguing over inbox real estate. I'd click the DeWalt section; I'd mark everything else as read.

## What's working

- **DeWalt + EGO featured up top** — those are both brands I trust and actively shop; seeing them in the hero tells me this email isn't just patio cushions
- **FREE with eligible purchase + $299 EGO pricing** — concrete dollar anchors, not just "big savings" fluff
- **3x MyLowe's points with an "Activate offer" button** — finally treating me like a member with something I can actually use
- **Father's Day timing is sharp** — mid-June is two weeks out, tools make obvious gifts, Lowe's is smart to flag it
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, everything loaded right

## What's weak

- **Six competing sections fight each other** — tools, savings, points, gift cards, American-made furniture, and a newsletter signup all want top billing
- **"Made here at home" / Adirondack chair block** — I'm a tools guy, not a porch furniture guy; this block is pure scrolling tax for me
- **Father's Day gift cards, not tools** — the section promotes gift cards and a "home + care" hook instead of leading with an actual tool I'd want to give or receive
- **Cadence fatigue** — this is email six from Lowe's in a week; the 3x points are saving it from an instant archive, but barely
- **"Top picks for summer?" is deliberately vague** — after the grill tool kit send from May 23rd that nailed the subject line, this feels like a step backward into marketing-speak

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the subject with the actual brand and hook** — the DeWalt/EGO + FREE offer is the news, not "summer picks." Put that in the subject line.
- 2. **Collapse to three sections max** — tools/offer, points activation, and Father's Day tools gift guide. Kill the Adirondack chair block or move it to a separate send.
- 3. **Make the Father's Day section tools-specific** — "Dad wants a miter saw, not a gift card" is a real opportunity; lean into it with actual SKUs.
- 4. **Add a deadline to the 3x points** — "Activate by Sunday" would push me to click same day instead of bookmarking it for later.

- **Preheader Alt B:** `EGO at $299, free item with eligible buy, and points to burn.`
