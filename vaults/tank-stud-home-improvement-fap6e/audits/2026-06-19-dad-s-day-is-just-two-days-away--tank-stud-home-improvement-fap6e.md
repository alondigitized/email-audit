# Dad's Day is just two days away 😳
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- Eight Lowe's emails in a week and change. I opened this one the same way I open most of them now — out of routine, not excitement. "Two days away" with that shocked face made me think there was a last-minute deal drop, something I might actually need before the weekend. What I got was a Father's Day gift email, which is not why I have a Lowe's account.
- The $649 grill in the hero is legitimately interesting to me — I've been thinking about pulling the trigger on a new grill for months. But the email wraps that one relevant hook in a gift card section and "Gifts under $50/$100" buckets that are basically meaningless to me. The "G.O.A.T. is almost G.O.N.E." copy at the bottom is the most interesting thing on the page and it's buried three scrolls down. That's a backwards editorial decision.
- I'd mark this read without clicking. The grill might pull a tap but the surrounding noise works against it.

## What's working

- **Hard price on the grill ($649)** — a specific number gives me something to anchor on. I know if that's a good deal or not.
- **"The G.O.A.T. is almost G.O.N.E." copy** — clever play on the acronym, scarcity angle makes me want to know what the product is.
- **Clean render, no layout issues** — text is readable, nothing overlapping or broken, hierarchy flows top to bottom without confusion.
- **Timely** — at least it's actually Father's Day weekend and not a Halloween preview (I'm looking at you, June 18th).
- **Countdown timer structure** — "10 Days of Father's Day Deals / Today's deals expire in" gives the send a campaign spine rather than just a one-off blast.

## What's weak

- **Gift cards are a non-starter for me** — that whole section is dead weight. I'm not shopping for someone else's dad; I'm the guy who buys cordless drills.
- **"Find gifts that celebrate him"** — hero copy positions me as the gift buyer, not the guy treating himself. Wrong frame for my mindset.
- **"Gifts under $50 / under $100"** — price-bucket sections feel like a department store for people who don't know what they're shopping for. I know exactly what I need; just tell me if it's on sale.
- **No project or tools angle** — this is Lowe's. I own a MyLowe's account specifically for project buys. There's nothing here that connects to lumber, fasteners, paint, or hardware.
- **Send fatigue** — this is the eighth Lowe's email in roughly a week. I'm opening on autopilot at this point, not because the email earned it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the grill deal in the subject** — "48 hours left on the $649 [Weber/Product Name]" converts better than a calendar reminder. Give me the product, not the deadline.
- 2. **Move "The G.O.A.T." section up** — that copy is doing the most work in the email. Burying it below the gift card block is malpractice.
- 3. **Add one project-adjacent block** — even a single "finishing your deck before summer?" callout with a deck stain or hardware deal would make me feel like a known customer instead of a generic dad-gift recipient.
- 4. **Pull back on cadence or segment harder** — seven-plus emails in a week trains me to ignore them. If I get one focused, well-timed email targeting my category, I'm far more likely to click than if I get daily blasts of mixed content.
- 5. **Swap gift card hero copy** — if gift cards have to stay, frame them differently. "He'll pick the right tool — get him a Lowe's card" at least speaks my language.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$649 grill. 48 hours. This weekend's deal.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The G.O.A.T. is almost gone — and so is the Father's Day price`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `10 days of deals — today's best: grills, project gear, and the one tool everyone wants.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Limited time on the $649 grill — plus a scarcity deal you'll want to see before Sunday.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard retail ESP send via `e.lowes.com` (Salesforce Marketing Cloud). The email is structurally sound but has accessibility gaps in tracking pixel alt attributes and authentication header visibility is blocked by the AgentMail relay layer.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**20 tracking links skipped** — all click-through URLs route through a redirect domain (likely `click.e.lowes.com` or similar). HTTP probing was skipped, so broken destination URLs cannot be confirmed or ruled out. This is expected for ESP-wrapped links but means dead-link detection requires manual spot-check or ESP reporting.

No issues found with link structure in the HTML itself.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**9 images missing `alt` text** (confirmed by QA scan):

| Image | URL |
|---|---|
| Hero image | `https://image.e.lowes.com/…/c301e24e-….jpg` |
| Open-pixel (×2) | `https://mi.lowes.com/p/cp/…/o.gif` |
| Impression pixel (×5) | `https://sli.lowes.com/imp?s=109083{4,5,7,8}…` |
| Content image | `https://image.e.lowes.com/…/ad569dc5-….png` |

The `o.gif` and `imp` pixels are tracking beacons and conventionally carry `alt=""` (empty string), which is technically correct for decorative/invisible elements — but they are currently missing the attribute entirely, which causes screen readers to announce the filename or URL. The hero and content images are a harder accessibility failure.

**Font loading:** `@font-face` blocks load `.woff` only — no `.woff2` fallback. Not a breakage, but `.woff2` is universally supported in modern email clients that honor web fonts (Apple Mail, iOS Mail) and would reduce payload by ~30%.

**Duplicate `@font-face` blocks:** Two separate `<!--[if !mso]><!-->` blocks each declare the full Fellix family. Redundant declarations; no functional impact but adds unnecessary parse weight.

**Legacy Hotmail hacks present** (`.ReadMsgBody`, `.ExternalClass`): These target Hotmail/Windows Live Mail, which is EOL. Dead code, but harmless.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not detected** — QA warns this may be a relay-layer visibility issue (AgentMail not forwarding the header), not necessarily an absence in the original send. Lowe's is a large sender and almost certainly includes this header; the gap is in the audit pipeline's header capture, not the email itself. Verify by checking raw headers in the AgentMail message store directly.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not detected** — Same caveat as above. Gmail and Apple Mail use this for one-click unsubscribe. If genuinely absent in the original send, Lowe's would be out of compliance with Google/Yahoo bulk sender requirements (effective Feb 2024). Low probability for a sender of this size; more likely a relay capture issue.

**Action:** Fix the AgentMail relay to forward all received headers before treating these as sender-side compliance failures.
