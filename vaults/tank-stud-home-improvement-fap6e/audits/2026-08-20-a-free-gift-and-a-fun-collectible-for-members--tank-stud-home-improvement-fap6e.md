# A FREE gift and a fun collectible for members
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-20**
## Executive summary

- Free mini cooler and a plush backpack that looks like it walked out of a kid's cartoon? Hard pass — this one's aimed at somebody with a minivan full of school supplies, not somebody standing in the lumber aisle. I get that "member perks" emails are supposed to build loyalty, but a novelty water jug and a $19.98 anthropomorphic backpack bucket bear zero relationship to anything I'm buying at Lowe's.
- The in-store-only, Saturday 10 a.m. timing is at least a real, concrete offer — that's the one thing keeping this from being total noise. But between this, the MrBeast Kids Workshop email from the 14th, and the tote-bag chasing from earlier in the week, I'm noticing a pattern: whoever's writing these keeps defaulting to "free swag/kids stuff" instead of "guy who owns tools and a house." Compare that to the patio clearance send on the 19th, which actually landed because it showed me something I'd use.
- Verdict: I'm not clicking, not forwarding, and this is going straight to "meh, delete." If I get two more of these in a row I might actually start ignoring MyLowe's emails altogether.

## What's working

- The "FREE" callout in lime green against the blue background is impossible to miss — good visual hierarchy on the top offer.
- Giving a specific date/time (Saturday, August 22, 10 a.m.) with an "Add to calendar" button is a smart, actionable touch — I know exactly when to show up if I cared.
- "Limited quantities. While supplies left" and "In-store only" set honest expectations, no bait-and-switch vibe.
- Clean, uncluttered layout — nothing overlapping, images render fine, easy to skim on my phone.

## What's weak

- The cooler jug and sunglasses-on-sand imagery reads more like a summer BBQ giveaway than anything tied to a project I'm doing this weekend.
- The Big Blue backpack mascot is straight-up kid/back-to-school marketing — I don't have young kids at home, so this whole second block is a non-starter for me.
- Zero connection to tools, project categories, rebates, or anything a homeowner/DIYer actually shops for — nothing here builds my next reno phase.
- No loyalty points balance or rewards tier shown even though this is framed as a "members only" perk email — feels like a generic freebie blast, not something personalized to my account.

## Recommendations

- 1. Swap the hero entirely — lead with a member perk tied to tools, seasonal projects, or a rebate, not a cooler jug and a plush backpack. That's the single biggest lift available here.
- 2. If Lowe's insists on sending the freebie/kids content, segment it away from customers whose purchase history skews tools/outdoor/appliances (like me) — I'm clearly not the target and they should know that by now.
- 3. Add a rewards balance or "you've earned X points" line to actually justify the "More perks, just for members" headline — right now it's generic, not personal.
- 4. Pair the in-store event with a relevant secondary offer (grill accessories, power tool bundle) instead of the backpack — give me a reason to actually walk past the freebie table.
