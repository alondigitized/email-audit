# MrBeast Kids Club Workshops are HERE 🚀
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- I opened this email, looked at it for about four seconds, and genuinely thought I'd gotten someone else's mail. MrBeast Kids Club? Workshop registration? At Lowe's? I know Lowe's does Saturday kids workshops — I've walked past them a dozen times mid-shopping trip, little kids in safety glasses gluing birdhouses while I'm loading 2x4s onto a flatbed cart. Those aren't for me. This email isn't for me.
- Everything here is aimed at a parent with a YouTube-age kid. The MrBeast branding is enormous — I get why, that name makes 9-year-olds lose their minds — but I'm 49. My "project this weekend" is a bathroom tile demo and gutter cleanout, not helping a kid build a foam launcher for $14.98. The price might hook a parent looking for cheap structured entertainment on a Saturday morning. For me it's a non-number.
- Then there's the cadence insult. Five Lowe's emails in four days, all hammering Memorial Day deals. I've been counting. Yesterday's staycation lifestyle play at least had a patio angle I could squint at. This one? Complete pivot to a different customer. Mark as read, don't click, don't forward.

## What's working

- Clean, bug-free render — no broken images, no overlapping text, nothing janky.
- $14.98 price is right in the hero, no digging for the offer.
- "Register now" is an unambiguous CTA — zero confusion about what clicking does.
- MyLowe's Rewards header keeps it out of the spam-reflex zone; I recognize the sender.
- The three workshop tiles (each with date + button) are genuinely well-organized; someone put real production effort into this layout.

## What's weak

- Not one thing here applies to a DIY weekend warrior. MrBeast Kids Club is a completely different customer.
- After five straight Memorial Day deal emails, this pivot is whiplash — I feel like I'm suddenly on the wrong list.
- The digital badges section ("collect exclusive MrBeast digital badges") is noise designed for a 10-year-old; it adds nothing for anyone without a kid in the building.
- No cross-sell, no "while the kids build, get X off in the tool aisle" — zero attempt to salvage value for non-parent customers who got this anyway.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Segment the list.** This email has zero business landing in an account with a Pro card and a purchase history full of lumber, tile, and pipe fittings. Parents of school-age kids get this; DIY and contractor-leaning accounts do not.
- 2. **If you must blast the full list, add a cross-sell tile.** One footer module: "Drop off the kids at the workshop — get 10% off in the tool aisle while they build." That's the only way this email earns a click from someone like me.
- 3. **Break the Memorial Day sequence with something relevant, not jarring.** Five consecutive deal emails and then a kids activity pivot is a sequencing failure. Gate MrBeast content to a separate campaign track.
- 4. **Fix the preheader junk leakage.** "View our webpage" trailing out of the preview kills inbox-fit. Replace with specific copy about spots and dates.
