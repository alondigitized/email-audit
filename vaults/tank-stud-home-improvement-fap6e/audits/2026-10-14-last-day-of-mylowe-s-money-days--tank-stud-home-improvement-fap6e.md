# LAST DAY of MyLowe’s Money Days 🚨
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-14**
## Executive summary

- This is the fourth send of the same Money Days offer. I saw it on 10/6, 10/7, 10/9 and 10/11, and now it's back as "last day." The creative is the same blue hero with floating cash and a credit card. The only new thing is the "end soon" banner, and the deadline isn't in the subject line. I'm half-done with a drywall job, and the $200 threshold is the only reason I'd think twice.
- Is a $200 spend realistic for me this weekend? Sure. Joint compound, drywall screws, a couple of boxes of mud, tape and a texture sprayer get me there fast. But the email never says that. It doesn't mention drywall, tools or Pro pricing. It's a generic rewards blast, and the same one I got three days ago. I'd glance at it, and if I were already heading to Lowe's today I'd remember it. I wouldn't forward it.
- Verdict: I'd read it as a reminder, click only if I'm already about to buy, and otherwise mark it read. Last-day urgency is credible and I'll give it that. After four repeats, though, I've stopped taking the urgency seriously.

## What's working

- "Money Days end soon!" in the banner and "October 6 – 14" make the deadline clear. It really is the last day.
- The $10 vs. $20 split is easy to read. The card tier is called out right in the hero, so I can see what the credit card gets me.
- The big "$10" and "$20" numbers are the first thing my eye lands on. The hierarchy is clean.
- The white "Shop now" button on the blue background stands out and is big enough to tap with a thumb.
- The preheader sits at the very top of the email, and "Members, you've still got time to earn more" speaks to me as a member.

## What's weak

- It's the same offer for the fourth time in about nine days. I've seen it enough that the urgency feels manufactured.
- There's nothing for my project, and no drywall, paint, tools or Pro angle. The hero is generic cash and a card.
- "Shop now" goes nowhere specific from what I can tell. There's no product, no category and no "what you can buy to hit $200."
- The fine print is tiny, and "expires 30 days after issuance" is buried in it. If I earn $10, I'd like to know that up front.
- The $200 minimum is easy to overlook next to the giant "$10." For $10 back on $200, that's 5%. It's fine, but it isn't a headline-grabbing deal.
- There's no in-stock or local-store signal and no "pick up today" angle. On the last day, that's the one thing I'd actually want to see.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a project-based hit-$200 module, like "Finish your drywall job: mud, tape, screws, sander." Show a few SKUs with prices so I can see the $200 and the $10 back in one glance. That alone would lift the click.
- 2. Put the deadline in the subject line and say what time it ends ("tonight at midnight"). The countdown should be real, not the same offer reworded.
- 3. Cut the cadence. Send the last-day email only to people who haven't opened or redeemed the earlier ones, and suppress it for the rest.
- 4. Add a "pick up today" or store-availability line so the last-day push has a reason to act now.
- 5. Make the CTA specific, like "Shop DIY essentials" instead of "Shop now," and move the expiration terms up out of the fine print.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Ends tonight: $10 back on $200 at Lowe's`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last chance: earn up to $20 MyLowe's Money`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stock up on drywall, paint and tools before midnight and get $10 back.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Use your Lowe's card for $20 back. Pick up today at your local store.`
