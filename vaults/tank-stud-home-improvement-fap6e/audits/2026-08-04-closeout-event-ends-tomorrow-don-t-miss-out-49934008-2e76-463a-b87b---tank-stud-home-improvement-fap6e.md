# Closeout Event ends tomorrow! Don’t miss out⌛
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-04**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is basically the same appliance lineup Lowe's ran on 7/31 — washer/dryer, fridge, range, dishwasher lined up like a showroom — just repackaged as a "Closeout Event" with a countdown clock bolted on. The LG co-brand and the 35% off + free delivery/haul-away/basic install stack is legit useful info for someone like me mid-reno, and the countdown timer (1 day, 5 hrs) actually creates real urgency instead of the fake "ends soon" garbage most retailers use.
- That said, I've now seen this exact fridge/range/dishwasher/washer-dryer photo cluster on three sends this week, and my brain has started to tune it out — it's starting to feel like Lowe's is just recycling the same creative with a new headline slapped on top.
- I'd click through on the washer/dryer pair since I've actually got a laundry room project queued up, but I'm not opening this with any urgency — I'd have gotten to it eventually even without the countdown. Not forwarding it, but I wouldn't mark it read without at least glancing at the $829 washer/dryer bundle.

## What's working

- The countdown timer (01:05:36:27) is a real deadline, not vague "hurry" copy — that's the kind of thing that actually moves me to act before I forget.
- "Members get FREE Delivery + Basic Install + Basic parts + Haul away" laid out as bullets is exactly the kind of concrete rebate/perk clarity I'm always complaining is missing.
- The $829 washer/dryer bundle with a specific "Save $320" tag is a real, honest number I can act on — no vague "up to" nonsense at that spot.
- LG co-branding with the Lowe's Deals badge signals this is a real vendor promo, not just a house sale — adds some trust.

## What's weak

- I already saw this exact appliance suite (washer/dryer, fridge, range, dishwasher) in the 7/31 "Last chance to save BIG on appliances!" email — this feels like a reskin, not a new offer.
- "Up to 35% off" with the fine print buried at the very bottom in six-point gray text is the classic bait — I know from experience that "up to" numbers rarely apply to what I actually want.
- The two lifestyle shots of people using appliances in-store feel like stock filler — doesn't add anything for me, I'm shopping online, not touring a showroom.
- Middle section drags — "Turn your kitchen into a standout space" and "Give your laundry room a fresh spin" are just generic mood copy stacked between product blocks, not real offers.

## Recommendations

- 1. Stop reusing the same product photo lineup send after send — swap the hero SKUs or at least reorder so it doesn't look like a copy-paste of last week's appliance email.
- 2. Move the "Save up to $600 / $100" bundle discounts up near the top instead of burying them below the fold — that's a bigger hook than the generic 35% headline.
- 3. Cut one of the two lifestyle-photo blocks (kitchen or laundry) — they're redundant and push the actual deals further down.
- 4. Add a line that flags real inventory/in-stock status near my zip — "closeout" implies limited stock, but nothing on the page confirms it's actually running low near me.
