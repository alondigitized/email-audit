The pixels are `s=1090838`, `1090834` (twice), `1090835` and `1090837`. Any proxy, cache or log along the image path can read the address. That is a PII-in-URL concern.

**3. Rendering & Accessibility**
- Seven images are flagged for missing alt text:
  - `https://image.e.lowes.com/lib/fe9515707364027471/m/59/c301e24e-201b-46f9-8172-27b5acdcbf99.jpg`
  - `https://image.e.lowes.com/lib/fe9515707364027471/m/71/ad569dc5-361d-4d7d-8bc0-1ae6ec389f05.png`
  - The five `sli.lowes.com/imp` pixels.
- The two `image.e.lowes.com` files are content images (`.jpg` and `.png`). Without alt text, clients that block images show nothing, and screen readers announce the filename or nothing.
- The tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` and ideally `width="1" height="1"`. I can't confirm those attributes from the truncated source.
- The DOCTYPE is malformed: `PUBLIC"-//W3C//DTD...` has no space after `PUBLIC`. Most clients tolerate it, but it is invalid and may trigger quirks mode in strict parsers.
- The XHTML namespace and DTD URLs use `https://` rather than the canonical `http://`. This is harmless in practice but non-standard.
- Web fonts (`Fellix`, `DIN Next`) are loaded via `@font-face` inside `<!--[if !mso]><!-->` blocks. Gmail and Outlook ignore these and fall back, so I'd check that the font stacks have web-safe fallbacks. The truncated source doesn't show them.
