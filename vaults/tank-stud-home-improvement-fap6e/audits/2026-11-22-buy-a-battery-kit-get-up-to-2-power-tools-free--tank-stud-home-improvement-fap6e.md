# Buy a battery kit, get up to 2 power tools FREE
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-22**
## Executive summary

- This is the Lowe's email I wanted on the 19th, but it shows up late and buried. The subject promises a battery-kit bundle with up to 2 free tools, and that's right in my wheelhouse. Then the email opens with a giant red "Black Friday" banner and a Shop now button. It says nothing about the offer, and I had to scroll past it to reach the free-tools block.
- When I get there, the block is good. DEWALT, CRAFTSMAN and Kobalt are all named, and I can see a blower, a circ saw and a jigsaw. The rest of the email drifts into ladders, a $500k house sweepstakes, a credit card pitch, and a reindeer animatronic and a griddle tool set. It's a catch-all, not a project email.
- I'd open it and I'd probably click the battery-kit block. I'm not forwarding it. It's the same offer as the 11/19 send ("Up to 2 FREE power tools? Yes, please 🛠") with a worse layout, and I've now seen about six Lowe's emails this week.

## What's working

- The "2 FREE" block has big type and a green highlight, so it's easy to spot once I'm scrolling.
- It names the brands (DEWALT, CRAFTSMAN, Kobalt) and shows actual tools. That's how I shop.
- The ladder block says "50% off select ladders." That's concrete, and ladders are a legit project buy.
- The 5% credit card block is clear. I have the card, so that part speaks to me.
- The "Save up to $135" and "25% off tool storage" tiles sit right under the hero, so the garage-and-shop stuff is grouped.

## What's weak

- The top hero is a generic "Black Friday" banner with no offer in it. It pushes the real offer below the first screen on my phone.
- The subject leads with the battery-kit deal, but the email opens with something else. That's a mismatch.
- The header banner says "FREE same-day delivery" but the footnote says a $25 minimum and a 2pm order cutoff. That's the buried fine print I hate.
- The $500k home sweepstakes, the reindeer and the griddle set have nothing to do with a weekend project. They dilute the email.
- There's no rebate or pricing clarity on the free tools. I can't tell which tools are free with which kit, or what the kit costs.
- The product and offer tiles have small tap targets and small text. That's tough on a phone.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the "2 FREE" block to the top and replace the generic Black Friday hero. The email should open with what the subject promised.
- 2. Make the offer specific. Show 2-3 actual kits with the price and the free tool paired next to each, plus the deadline.
- 3. Cut the sweepstakes, the reindeer and the griddle set from this send. Replace them with project bundles like "Kobalt kit + storage" or "DEWALT kit + ladder."
- 4. Put the fine print for the delivery claim right under the banner, not in the footer.
- 5. Add an in-stock or pickup-today signal for the tools and the ladders.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Pick a battery kit. Get 2 tools free.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Jigsaw, circ saw, blower — see which kits qualify. Ends 12/15.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus 25% off tool storage and 50% off ladders for the shop.`

- **Recipient email in pixel URLs:** All 16 `sli.lowes.com/imp` impression pixels carry the recipient address in plaintext. Examples:
  - `https://sli.lowes.com/imp?s=127768300&e=cole-drywall-lowes-fap6e@etell.app&p=531386&lctg=410146703&stpe=pixel`
