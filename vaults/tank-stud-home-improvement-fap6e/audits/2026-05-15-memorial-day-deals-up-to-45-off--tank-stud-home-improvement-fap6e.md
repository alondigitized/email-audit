# Memorial Day deals = Up to 45% OFF😲
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-15**
## Executive summary

- Second Memorial Day email in two days. Yesterday was the launch — "It's LIVE, deals are ready" — and today is the percentage push. I get the playbook. The 45% number in the subject is the right move for a follow-up; you want to escalate the value prop, not just restate the event. I opened it.
- But once I'm inside, the email forgets who I am. The hero is appliances — refrigerators, washers, Samsung bundles — and while I'm not above replacing a fridge, that's not what's queued up in my head for Memorial Day weekend. I've got a bathroom gut-job half-planned and some decking to seal. What I want is the tool deal, the material deal, the "buy a four-pack of stain and save 20%" deal. The buy-more-save-more tiered grid is the one moment this email speaks my language, and I have to scroll to find it.
- I'll mark it read. I might click if I'm bored Thursday night and want to check the outdoor/paint section. I'm not forwarding this to anyone — the guys I know already get Lowe's emails, and this one doesn't have a specific deal worth texting about the way that framing nailer from the May 11 send did.

## What's working

- **Subject line delivers.** "Up to 45% OFF" on a Memorial Day weekend send from Lowe's — that's a credible number from a trusted sender and it cleared my thumb-scroll.
- **Navigation bar puts TOOLS first.** Smart positioning; even if the hero isn't tools, the nav tells me where to go.
- **Tiered buy-more-save-more grid.** Buy 2: 10%, Buy 3: 15%, Buy 4: 20% — this is how I actually shop mulch, stain, or caulk. Clear math, no nonsense.
- **Paint/supplies section with dollar-figure savings.** "Save up to $100" with visible product images is more useful to me than a vague percentage on appliances.
- **Military discount block.** Not relevant to me personally, but it's the kind of brand-integrity content that keeps me trusting Lowe's. They didn't bury it.

## What's weak

- **Appliances dominate the hero.** Samsung bundle, refrigerators, washers — this is the second or third appliance push in a row. I'm a weekend warrior, not a kitchen remodeler on a budget who comparison-shops with Best Buy.
- **"Shop now" is the only CTA everywhere.** Not one button says "Shop Power Tools" or "See Outdoor Deals" — I'm being asked to go hunting when I want a guided path to my category.
- **No specific tool deal in the entire email.** The May 11 framing nailer send set a bar. This one doesn't clear it — not a single SKU I can get excited about.
- **Cadence is piling up.** Five emails in eight days; two of them Memorial Day variants in consecutive days. I'm not there yet, but one more sends me to the unsubscribe page.
- **"Big sunny savings for big summer plans" is soft copy.** That hero line sounds like a copy placeholder. I need you to tell me what I'm saving on, not that the savings are "sunny."

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the hero with a project-framed offer, not "big sunny savings."** Pick one high-signal deal — a specific power tool, a cordless combo kit, a pressure washer — and anchor the hero to it. Make me feel like Lowe's read my mind.
- 2. **Add at least one tool/hardware CTA block above the fold.** Appliances can live below; give me one "Tools & Hardware: Up to 40% OFF — Shop Now" block I can see without scrolling.
- 3. **Make "Shop now" specific.** Swap to "Shop Outdoor Deals," "Shop Tools," "Shop Paint & Stain" based on the section. One verb, one category.
- 4. **Drop the Samsung bundle section or move it to the bottom.** It reads like a paid placement and dilutes the project-DIY vibe.
- 5. **Add a hard deadline.** "Ends Monday, May 27" costs one line and doubles the urgency.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Tools, paint & patio: Memorial Day up to 45% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your next project just got cheaper — Memorial Day deals`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Ends Monday. Tools, stain, outdoor furniture — shop before it's gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Up to 45% off the stuff you actually need this weekend.`

The email is structurally sound XHTML with standard ESP scaffolding. The primary issues are missing alt text on content images, unverified compliance headers (captured as relay gaps rather than confirmed sender failures), and conflicting responsive media query breakpoints.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Open/impression tracking pixels confirmed present:**
- `https://mi.lowes.com/p/cp/4aaacfb5a80dfc43/o.gif?mi_u=410146703` — open pixel
- Multiple `https://sli.lowes.com/imp?s=...` pixels — segment-level impression tracking

**Seed address exposure in pixel parameters:**
```

```
The etell.app inbox address is embedded in cleartext in all `sli.lowes.com` pixel URLs. This is expected for a seed/audit deployment but confirms the ESP is parameterizing the recipient address into tracker URLs — a data point for privacy assessment of live sends.

**No broken links detected** among the non-redirect URLs checked.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text — content images (WCAG 2.1 §1.1.1 failure):**
| Image | URL |
|---|---|
| Hero/product image | `image.e.lowes.com/.../c301e24e-201b-46f9-8172-27b5acdcbf99.jpg` |
| Supporting graphic | `image.e.lowes.com/.../ad569dc5-361d-4d7d-8bc0-1ae6ec389f05.png` |

Tracking pixels (`o.gif`, `sli.lowes.com/imp`) missing alt text is lower severity — `alt=""` is the correct value for decorative/invisible elements, but the QA tool flagged them because the attribute is absent rather than empty.

**Conflicting responsive breakpoints:**
```css
/* Query A */
@media screen and (min-width: 375px) and (max-width: 600px) and (-webkit-min-device-pixel-ratio:0)

/* Query B */
@media only screen and (max-width: 480px)
```
Devices 375–480px wide match **both** queries simultaneously. Query A is WebKit-only; Query B is universal. Any property set in both blocks for `.container`, `.drop`, or `.photo` will have unpredictable cascade order depending on stylesheet position.

**Font loading — woff only, no woff2:**
All six `@font-face` declarations provide only `.woff`. Modern clients support `woff2` (30% smaller). Not a rendering failure, but increases load time.

**Redundant `local()` hints:**
```css
src: local('Fellix Regular'), local('Fellix Regular'), url(...)
```
Each `@font-face` duplicates the `local()` hint. Harmless but indicates a template copy-paste artifact.

**DIN Next CDN version mismatch:**
- Regular weight: `scaffold/1.4.4/fonts/din/din-next.woff`
- Bold/Black weights: `scaffold/1.2.0/fonts/din/din-next-bold.woff`

Different scaffold versions for the same typeface family — risk of metric or rendering inconsistency between weights.

**Legacy Hotmail hacks present** (`.ReadMsgBody`, `.ExternalClass`) — these target Hotmail/Windows Live, which no longer exists. They do not cause harm but represent dead code.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header — not captured by AgentMail relay.**
This is a relay visibility gap, not a confirmed absence. Lowe's at scale would fail inbox provider requirements (Gmail, Yahoo) without this header. Cannot confirm either way from the relay capture alone.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) — not captured.**
Same caveat. One-click machine unsubscribe is required by Gmail and Yahoo for bulk senders (>5k/day) since 2024. If absent on the actual send, this is a deliverability compliance failure.

**`Authentication-Results` — not found via relay.**
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status cannot be confirmed. Sending domain `e.lowes.com` is a dedicated ESP subdomain; alignment with `lowes.com` DMARC policy is unverified from this capture.

**Physical mailing address** — HTML is truncated; cannot confirm CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) compliance (footer address required). Flag for manual verification.
