# Save $2,110 on an appliance suite? YES😍
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-23**
## Executive summary

- Alright, so Lowe's pivoted hard the second Father's Day was over. After nine straight emails angling at gifting my kids something to buy me, this one drops a $2,110 appliance suite save right in my face. That's a gear shift I can respect. Appliances aren't exactly my weekend project sweet spot — I'm more demo-and-rebuild than white-goods-shopping — but my wife and I have been talking about replacing the fridge and dishwasher since last fall, so the timing isn't terrible.
- The email is dense. Really dense. There's a hero with the suite discount, then a stack of individual item prices ($1,799 fridge, $1,998 suite, $729 range, $499 dishwasher), then Member Only Deals, then a sweepstakes play, then an LG section, then Deal of the Day. I felt like I was reading a Sunday circular, not an email. By the time I got to the LG Craft Ice pitch I had already mentally checked out.
- I'd probably click on the hero deal — if my wife is already in the market, that $2,110 lead number is a real number worth verifying. But I wouldn't forward this to a buddy. It's a mass blast, not a targeted send for someone like me.

## What's working

- **The $2,110 headline is hard to ignore.** A specific dollar figure at that scale cuts through noise fast — that's not "up to 20% off" vagueness, that's real money on the table.
- **Price anchors on individual items are visible.** $1,799, $1,998, $729, $499 — I can see what I'm looking at without clicking through, which is how I shop.
- **Member Only Deals callout is clearly sectioned.** As a MyLowe's card holder, seeing a dedicated block tells me there's a reason I stayed subscribed.
- **The appliance category nav at the top** (Refrigerators, Dishwashers, etc.) gives me a quick jump if I already know what I want — useful, not decorative.

## What's weak

- **Way too many sections fighting for attention.** Suite deal, individual items, Member deals, a spin-to-win game, LG Craft Ice, Deal of the Day — pick a lane. This reads like a weekly circular stapled to a loyalty mailer.
- **"Build it, spin it!" feels like it belongs in a different email entirely.** I'm here to buy an appliance, not enter a sweepstakes. This dilutes the offer and confuses the intent.
- **No project context.** Lowe's knows I buy tools and materials — where's the "upgrade your kitchen before the next reno phase" angle? This could've spoken to me but reads like a generic appliance blast.
- **The hero image is small and hard to read at a glance.** A $2,110 save deserves a bigger visual payoff — I had to squint at the thumbnail to confirm it was a real kitchen suite and not just a fridge.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with one suite, one number, one CTA.** Kill everything below the fold except the Member Only Deals. Move Deal of the Day and the LG brand section to separate sends.
- 2. **Add a project hook in the hero copy.** Something like "Kitchen reno this summer? Start with the suite." — that speaks to how I actually buy appliances: project-driven, not impulse.
- 3. **Make the hero image bigger.** If the suite is the sell, show me the suite at full width. I should feel the kitchen upgrade, not squint at a thumbnail.
- 4. **Drop the spin-to-win block.** It undercuts the premium appliance positioning. If there's a sweepstakes, put it in a footer link, not above the fold.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Kitchen upgrade: full suite deal inside`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Fridge, range, dishwasher — all at once, all discounted`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Member pricing unlocks even more off the suite`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing alt text — 2 content images** (high priority):
- `c301e24e-201b-46f9-8172-27b5acdcbf99.jpg` (`image.e.lowes.com`) — likely a hero/banner image; no `alt` attribute
- `ad569dc5-361d-4d7d-8bc0-1ae6ec389f05.png` (`image.e.lowes.com`) — likely a product or promo image; no `alt` attribute

Both will render as broken image icons with no fallback text in image-blocking clients (Outlook default, corporate firewalls).

**Missing alt text — tracking pixels** (low priority): 5 `sli.lowes.com/imp` pixels and the `o.gif` pixel lack `alt=""`. These should carry an empty alt, not be omitted. Minor WCAG 1.1.1 violation but functionally negligible for tracking pixels.

**Overlapping media query breakpoints** — the CSS defines two conflicting responsive ranges:
- `@media screen and (min-width: 375px) and (max-width: 600px)` (Fellix/custom fonts block)
- `@media only screen and (max-width: 480px)` (layout block)

The 375–480px range triggers both simultaneously. In practice this likely causes no visual bug (the second ruleset wins via cascade specificity), but it creates unpredictable rendering on mid-range viewport widths.

**Font loading** — `woff` format only (no `woff2`). Not a rendering defect in email clients, but `woff2` would improve load time on supporting clients (Apple Mail, some mobile). Custom fonts will gracefully fall back to system fonts in Outlook and Gmail.
