# Up to 85% off clearance STARTS TODAY ➡️
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- This email does something the prior five sends never managed: it actually pivots. After hammering "40% off" five consecutive times across July 18–19, Kohl's breaks the pattern with a clearance event headlined at 85% off — a number big enough that a burned-out subscriber would clock it as meaningfully different. The creative execution backs that up; the hero is bold, red, and unambiguous. The clearance story is real and the timing (July clearance) is seasonally defensible.
- That said, the email overloads itself. You walk in for the 85%-off clearance and immediately hit an Amazon Device Deals module, a Kohl's Rewards card pitch, and a separate "40% off today with your card" section — four competing discount stories in one scroll. The compound framing of the headline ("85% after extra 10% off") is also the kind of thing that makes you do mental math at the top of the email, which is a friction moment the hero can't afford.
- The bottom line: a credible refresh after the 40%-off fatigue stretch, undermined by too many offers competing for the same real estate.

## What's working

- **The creative pivot lands.** After five 40%-off emails, 85% clearance reads as a genuine escalation — a real subscriber would notice the difference from yesterday's "40% off ENDS TONIGHT 🚨."
- **The hero is visually punchy.** "CLEARANCE" in large type with "85%" immediately below it establishes the value proposition before you process anything else.
- **Kohl's Cash reinforcement is consistent.** The earn-$10-per-$50-spent reminder appears in the footer without disrupting the main offers — good supporting cadence for loyalty members.
- **Multi-category CTAs give subscribers multiple entry points.** Home, activewear, and Amazon electronics each get their own module with a dedicated button, so there's a lane for different shopping intents.
- **Subject line is efficient.** Under 40 characters, specific discount, concrete action signal — works cleanly on mobile.

## What's weak

- **Four competing discount numbers create cognitive noise.** 85% clearance, 40% home, up to 50% activewear, and 40% Rewards card all live in one scroll — the inbox math becomes exhausting rather than exciting.
- **The Amazon Device Deals module breaks the clearance narrative.** It's a separate campaign tacked inside a clearance email; a subscriber primed for apparel/home deals lands on an Echo or Ring device and has to reorient.
- **"STARTS TODAY" has no end date.** Urgency without a deadline is just noise. A subscriber doesn't know whether this clearance ends tomorrow or in three weeks, so there's no reason to act now.
- **The compound discount framing is the first friction.** "85% after extra 10% off" means the base clearance price gets you to ~75%, then a coupon closes the gap — the headline promise requires a second action that isn't surfaced clearly.
- **Seventh promo email in roughly 48 hours.** Even with the creative pivot, the cadence is aggressive enough that "clearance starts today" lands with some subscriber fatigue baked in.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add an end date to the clearance.** "Clearance starts today — ends Sunday" converts "STARTS TODAY" from announcement to urgency. This is the single highest-leverage change.
- 2. **Drop the Amazon Device Deals module or move it to a separate send.** It fractures the clearance story and feels like a different email accidentally appended. Kohl's clearance subscribers and electronics deal-hunters are overlapping but not identical; serve them separately.
- 3. **Reframe the compound discount.** Either lead with "75% off + extra 10% with your Kohl's Rewards" (transparent stacking) or just headline the base clearance number and save the coupon for a secondary banner — don't bury the condition in small text in the hero.
- 4. **Collapse the discount stack to one hero number.** Pick the 85% story for the email, then let the Rewards card benefit live in the footer — don't compete with yourself in the scroll.
- 5. **Subject Alt A:** `Clearance just started — up to 85% off, 3 days only`
- 6. **Subject Alt B:** `New: 85% off clearance starts today`
- 7. **Preheader Alt A:** `Home, activewear, shoes — clearance prices are live now`
- 8. **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus earn Kohl's Cash on every clearance purchase this week`

### 5. Compliance

**Physical address — FAIL.** No physical mailing address detected in the HTML. CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid postal address. If it exists below the truncation point, this should be verified against the full source; if absent, this is a violation.

**List-Unsubscribe header — WARN.** Header not captured by AgentMail relay. Cannot confirm presence. If absent at the MTA level, Gmail and Apple Mail will not surface the one-click unsubscribe UI, increasing spam complaint rates.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) — WARN.** Same capture gap. Without this header, Gmail's native one-click unsubscribe button does not activate, which is now a bulk sender requirement for >5k/day Google recipients.

**Authentication-Results — WARN.** SPF/DKIM pass/fail status unknown due to relay. Sending domain is `s.kohls.com` (ESP subdomain); DMARC alignment should be verified against `kohls.com` parent domain out-of-band.

**`<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow">` and `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` are present** — correct practice to suppress search indexing of web-view versions and suppress referrer leakage.
