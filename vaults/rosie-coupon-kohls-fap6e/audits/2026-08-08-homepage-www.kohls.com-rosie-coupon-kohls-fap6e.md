# Homepage snapshot · www.kohls.com · 2026-08-08
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-08-08**
## Executive summary

- Well now, this looks like my kind of store! Right off the bat I see "2 Coupons," a "TAKE20" code, 30% off with my Kohl's Card, and Kohl's Cash stacking on top — that's the mailer stack come to life on the screen. It's pushing a big "Ultimate Clearance Event" up to 70% off, with Back to School and college stuff mixed in since it's August. This is a store that knows its coupon crowd.

## What's working

- The "2 Coupons" badge sitting right in the top nav — tells me immediately I've got something waiting to use, which is exactly what gets me clicking.
- "Earn $10 Kohl's Cash for every $50 spent" box — this is the Kohl's Cash mechanic I plan whole trips around.
- "You've got the coupon. Now for the fun part." — somebody in marketing gets me. That's speaking my language.
- The category strip ($10 & Under, Home, Women, Men, Girls) right under that line makes it easy to jump straight to where I'd actually spend.

## What's weak

- No sign of my rewards balance or a "Welcome back" greeting tied to my account — just a generic "Hi, friend!" I'd want it to know it's me.
- The main hero image is just bold text and color blocks, no lifestyle photo, no person — doesn't feel warm or personal.
- That sneaker/Nike "Tek Gear" strip at the very bottom isn't my department — I'm here for the Sephora counter and comforters, not running shoes.
- I don't see anything about free shipping threshold or a return policy reminder, and I return a LOT of stuff, so that's something I look for.

## Recommendations

- Swap the plain clearance-text hero for a lifestyle photo of a woman my age holding a shopping bag with a coupon in hand — make it feel like it's for me, not just loud typography.
- Add a small "Rosie, you have $X in Kohl's Cash" or account-specific rewards teaser right under the greeting.
- Move a Home & Sephora-focused rail up higher instead of leading with sneakers below the fold.
- Put the free-shipping threshold or return policy line somewhere visible up top — that reassures me before I even start browsing.
