# New from Marc Jacobs Beauty 💌
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-01**
## Executive summary

- This is a "New from Marc Jacobs Beauty" launch email, and it's structurally different from Sephora's recent 50%-off streak — it's brand-focused rather than sitewide-discount-focused, which is a genuine change of pace after four straight "select beauty" percentage-off sends (8/28, 8/29, 8/31, and the near-miss 8/26 clean-beauty edit). The free-gift-with-purchase hook up top ("Free Marc Jacobs Beauty Gift... snag this puffy makeup bag") is a real, concrete offer, and the hero section cleanly establishes the brand with a bold purple "MARC JACOBS BEAUTY" wordmark and a single "SHOP NOW" CTA.
- Past that hero, though, the email becomes a long, undifferentiated product catalog dump — eight products in a row (concealer, eyeshadow, lipstick, bronzer, blush, eyeliner, mascara) each with its own tiny "SHOP NOW" link, no single hero product driving the click, and no pricing shown except the one concealer at $36. Then it pivots again into an unrelated 50% off Beauty banner and a Chanel fragrance promo at the bottom, muddying the single-brand story the top of the email set up.
- Net: a decent GWP hook buried under a long, low-hierarchy product grid, with brand focus diluted by a bolt-on discount banner at the close. Middling execution of an otherwise good idea.

## What's working

- The free-gift-with-purchase banner at the very top is a specific, credible offer (a named product — "puffy makeup bag" — for spending $100+).
- The "MARC JACOBS BEAUTY" hero with model shot and single "SHOP NOW" button gives one clear visual anchor early in the email.
- The "NEW • ONLY AT SEPHORA" concealer callout includes a real price ($36) and a specific claim (medium-to-full coverage, built-in brush).
- No render bugs — images loaded cleanly, text is legible, color-blocking is consistent brand purple/teal/coral throughout.

## What's weak

- The product grid (eyeshadow through mascara) has no prices anywhere, undercutting purchase intent for a cosmetics buyer comparing options.
- Eight near-equal product blocks with no ranking or "bestseller" signal creates flat visual hierarchy — nothing tells the eye where to click first after the hero.
- The email breaks its own "New from Marc Jacobs Beauty" premise by tacking on an unrelated "50% off Select Beauty" banner and a Chanel fragrance ad at the bottom — same fatigue-inducing discount beat as the past four sends, now stapled onto a brand-launch email.
- Long scroll with no visible mobile "jump to bestseller" or condensed summary — a reader has to scroll past 7+ screens of product tiles before hitting anything else.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the product grid from 8 items to 3-4 "must-haves," each with a price and a one-line reason to buy — this is the single biggest lever, since right now nothing differentiates item 2 from item 7.
- 2. Move the free-gift threshold ($100+) up near the hero CTA, not just in the top banner, so it reinforces the purchase decision at the point of click.
- 3. Drop or clearly separate the 50% off Beauty and Chanel fragrance blocks — either send them as their own email or move them below a hard visual break, since they contradict the "Marc Jacobs spotlight" framing.
- 4. Add social proof (star ratings) to at least the concealer, since it's flagged "NEW" and unproven products benefit most from review counts.
