# Makeup that feels as good as it looks 🌱
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-26**
## Executive summary

- This is Sephora's "Clean at Sephora" collection email, and it's a solid, if familiar, curated-product-dump format. The concept — clean beauty must-haves under a blue-sky, feel-good banner — is clear and on-brand, and the "Clean" seal badge gives it a recognizable trust signal. But like the 8/23 new-arrivals send, this is fundamentally a product grid with a thin editorial wrapper: one hero pairing (M.PH lip gloss + Haus Labs liner) followed by eight more products in a scroll-forever grid.
- The top of the email is doing a lot of unrelated work before you even get to the clean-beauty content: an App Fest banner (25% off select brands, "Today Only") sits above the fold, which is the third or fourth App Fest touchpoint since 8/17 — cadence fatigue is real here. By the time a reader hits the actual "Clean at Sephora" hero, they've already seen a competing offer.
- Verdict: decent execution of a thin idea, undercut by messaging collision at the top and a CTA-per-product structure that dilutes the primary "Shop Now" push. Solidly average — a 5.

## What's working

- Clear "Clean at Sephora" seal/badge gives instant credibility for the ingredient-conscious shopper.
- Hero product pairing (lip gloss + liner) is well-photographed with clean product-on-white shots and visible prices ($26, $24).
- "NEW • ONLY AT SEPHORA" tags are used consistently and create a sense of exclusivity across most products.
- Bottom modules (Korean hair care, Beauty Insider points challenge, Summer Fridays) add relevant variety without feeling bolted on.
- No render bugs — images load cleanly, text is legible, no overlap.

## What's weak

- The App Fest 25% off banner at the very top competes with and dilutes the "Clean" message the subject line promised — readers get a bait-and-switch first impression.
- No unifying CTA for the whole grid — nine products, one generic "Shop Now" button at the top, then just product names/prices below with no per-item buttons, so scroll-through has low click intent.
- Grid fatigue: 2x2x2x2 product tiles is visually monotonous, same layout as the 8/23 "Psst, something new" send — no new structural idea.
- "Spot it. Shop it." section header is vague and doesn't clarify what the Korean hair care / Beauty Insider modules have to do with clean beauty.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut or move the App Fest banner below the Clean beauty content — don't let a competing promo lead the email when the subject promises something else.
- 2. Add scannable "Shop" links/buttons under each product tile, not just under the hero pair — right now only the top two items have a clear path to purchase.
- 3. Trim the grid from 9 products to 5-6 hero items with slightly larger images — density is hurting scannability.
- 4. Give the "Spot it. Shop it." section a real subhead tying it to clean/wellness (e.g., "More clean picks worth a look") instead of a throwaway pun.

- **Subject Alt B:** `New clean must-haves, just landed`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `The ingredients you skip, the glow you don't`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `9 new clean picks, from lip gloss to foundation`

## Full review
## 1. Overview

This is Sephora's "Clean at Sephora" collection email, and it's a solid, if familiar, curated-product-dump format. The concept — clean beauty must-haves under a blue-sky, feel-good banner — is clear and on-brand, and the "Clean" seal badge gives it a recognizable trust signal. But like the 8/23 new-arrivals send, this is fundamentally a product grid with a thin editorial wrapper: one hero pairing (M.PH lip gloss + Haus Labs liner) followed by eight more products in a scroll-forever grid.

The top of the email is doing a lot of unrelated work before you even get to the clean-beauty content: an App Fest banner (25% off select brands, "Today Only") sits above the fold, which is the third or fourth App Fest touchpoint since 8/17 — cadence fatigue is real here. Verdict: decent execution of a thin idea, undercut by messaging collision at the top and a CTA-per-product structure that dilutes the primary "Shop Now" push. Solidly average — a 5.
