# Starts today! You have 10% off 🎉
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-26**
## Executive summary

- This is Sephora's triggered "welcome offer activated" email for Beauty Insider member Rosie — a clean, well-personalized send built around the 10% first-purchase discount (code SE3ARPH) with a June 1 deadline. The offer mechanics are solid: code is prominent, deadline is visible, and a "SHOP NOW" CTA lands above the fold. The in-store barcode at the bottom is a genuine omnichannel plus.
- The bigger problem is cadence logic. Brand history shows this is the **third** email pushing the same SE3ARPH code: the May 14 welcome email launched it, the May 20 email said "10% off ends soon" — and now on May 26, six days after an urgency-deadline send, the subject says "Starts today!" with the *identical* code. A subscriber who got the "ends soon" nudge and didn't act will find this jarring. It either implies Sephora extended the offer quietly (undermining urgency on future sends) or this is a sequencing error. Either way, it erodes the brand's urgency credibility.
- The illustrated "This one's for you!" panel between the CTA and product recommendations adds visual charm but pushes personalized product cards below the fold unnecessarily. On balance: a technically competent triggered email let down by a confusing re-entry in an already-complete countdown sequence.

## What's working

- **10% OFF hero is unmissable** — large, bold, code-explicit, deadline-visible, all above the fold.
- **Full name personalization** ("Rosie") appears in the body and implicitly in preview, making it feel triggered rather than batch.
- **In-store barcode** at the bottom extends the offer to physical Kohl's/Sephora locations — genuinely useful.
- **"Recommended For You" product grid** adds browsing hooks beyond the generic CTA and could drive category discovery.
- **Beauty Insider context block** ("Wondering Why You Got This?") is smart onboarding for a new member who may not know the loyalty program.

## What's weak

- **Cadence contradiction** — sending "Starts today!" six days after "10% off ends soon" with the same code destroys the urgency signal built on May 20.
- **The illustrated interstitial** ("This one's for you!") breaks the conversion flow and pushes product recs below fold with no payoff — decorative noise.
- **"SHOP NOW" is a generic CTA** — no hook to category ("Shop skincare," "Build your routine") that would increase relevance for a new member.
- **Preheader doesn't leverage the offer** — the top-of-email text ("Shop items before they sell out") is generic stock urgency that ignores the more compelling 10%-off angle.
- **Double asterisk on the hero** ("10% OFF\*\*") — the exclusions caveat is disclosed, but the double asterisk on the largest copy element immediately signals "catch," which erodes conversion intent before the reader reaches the fine print.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the sequence logic first.** If Sephora extended Rosie's offer, say so explicitly: "We extended your 10% off" is honest and actually *rewards* the subscriber for not acting. "Starts today!" after "ends soon" is the single biggest trust erode in this send.
- 2. **Swap the illustrated panel for a category picker.** Three tappable tiles ("Skincare / Makeup / Fragrance") above the product recs would drive segmentation data *and* replace passive art with conversion intent.
- 3. **Make the CTA specific.** "Shop my recommendations" or "Redeem 10% off" beats "SHOP NOW" in click intent for a first-purchase offer.
- 4. **Rewrite the preheader** to reinforce the code and deadline — the highest-leverage inbox-level lever on this send.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Rosie, your 10% off code is live — use it by June 1`
- **Subject Alt B:** `We extended your Beauty Insider welcome offer`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Code SE3ARPH unlocks 10% off your first order — expires June 1`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your Beauty Insider welcome offer just activated — here's your code`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] `maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom on mobile**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
WCAG 1.4.4 failure. Prevents users from scaling text. Should be removed or set to `maximum-scale=5`.

**[HIGH] `text-size-adjust: none` applied to body-level selectors**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  -moz-text-size-adjust: none;
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```
Overrides OS-level font scaling. This broad selector list includes `body` and `div`, which suppresses accessibility zoom across all content. Standard practice is to limit this to specific layout containers, not body-level elements.
