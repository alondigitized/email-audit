# Memorial Day Sale ✅ $10 off your $25 purchase ✅ Kohl’s Cash ✅
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- Kohl's leans into Memorial Day with their sharpest dollar-off threshold in recent memory — $10 off a $25 purchase is a low bar to clear and a tangible saving that outperforms the string of 15% and 20% percentage-off sends from the past two weeks. The hero is clean: a couple in casual attire, the "MEMORIAL DAY SALE" headline front and center, and the coupon callout immediately visible. The calendar hook is both timely and earned — Memorial Day is a legitimate shopping moment, unlike some of the looser justifications Kohl's has used recently.
- The problem, as always, is the relentless scroll. After a strong hero the email fragments into a kitchen-sink grid: 30%, 40%, 30%, 50%, 70% clearance — each in its own module, each with a "Shop Now" button, none with a product-level hook specific enough to feel like it was placed for you. The stacked checkmark emojis in the subject line — `✅ $10 off your $25 purchase ✅ Kohl's Cash ✅` — read like a grocery list, not a headline; it overshoots the clever emoji use in the May 19 send.
- This is a competent but not exceptional send. The offer is the best thing about it. Everything else is boilerplate.

## What's working

- **Dollar-off threshold beats percentage-off** — $10 off $25 is concrete, easy to calculate, and sets a low minimum spend that encourages impulse purchases.
- **Memorial Day timing is credible** — unlike the loosely-themed "getaway styles" from May 19, this is a genuine sale event with an implied end date.
- **Hero visual hierarchy is clean** — the couple, the sale badge, and the coupon callout occupy the viewport without competing.
- **Kohl's Cash stacked on top** — the loyalty mechanic is visible both in the subject and in the bottom coupon module, rewarding the existing subscriber.
- **Clearance at 70% visible low in the scroll** — high-discount anchor at the bottom gives bargain hunters a reason to keep scrolling.

## What's weak

- **Three consecutive ✅ emojis in the subject kill the tone** — it reads like a checklist punch card, not a brand with a voice; the May 19 `🧳` single-emoji subject was far more polished.
- **No standout product or category in the body** — every module is a generic grid with a percentage badge; there is no single "here's the thing to buy" moment.
- **"Shop Now" is used on every CTA** — with six or more modules, the CTA copy is completely interchangeable and adds no directional pull.
- **The body cadence exhausts before the fold** — this is the 7th send in roughly 11 days; a subscriber who opened the May 19 send sees an escalating offer pattern that now feels like perpetual sale mode rather than a genuine event.
- **No size, fit, or product-specific detail** — prices are visible but no social proof, no inventory urgency, nothing to close the gap between browsing and clicking.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill two of the three checkmarks** — Pick one punctuation device: either the Memorial Day headline *or* the offer badge *or* Kohl's Cash. All three with matching emojis dilutes each one. The May 19 single-emoji send proved restraint works better.
- 2. **Add a deadline line to the hero** — "Through Monday" or "Ends May 26" turns the vague Memorial Day implied urgency into a real close. No copy change costs less.
- 3. **Feature one hero product from a specific category** — Pick women's dresses or men's shorts — whichever sold best last week — and give it a named moment above the percentage-off grid.
- 4. **Rename the module CTAs** — "Shop Women's Dresses," "Shop Men's Polos," "Shop Clearance" each point somewhere; "Shop Now" six times points nowhere.
- 5. **Throttle the next send** — Another near-identical email in 48 hours will cannibalize this one's click rate.

- **Subject Alt B:** `$10 off $25 this weekend only 🇺🇸 + Kohl's Cash`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stack it with Kohl's Cash and you're walking out way ahead.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Low bar, big savings — just $25 to unlock $10 off.`

## Technical Audit — Kohl's Memorial Day Sale

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a standard HTML4 table layout with VML/MSO conditional blocks and appears functionally sound at the rendering layer. The primary concerns are compliance header gaps (likely artifacts of the AgentMail relay), missing alt text on tracking pixels, and an unresolved merge token in at least one image URL.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**22 click-redirect links skipped by HTTP probe** — all route through `click.chp.kohls.com` and `click.s.kohls.com`. This is expected for ESP-managed click tracking; no broken links were confirmed, but destination resolution could not be verified.

**Three separate pixel/tracking systems active simultaneously:**
- `click.chp.kohls.com` — Coherent Path behavioral targeting pixel
- `mi.kohls.com` — Movable Ink personalization pixel
- `kohls.demdex.net` — Adobe Audience Manager (AAM) sync pixel (comment `<!-- AAM -->` confirms intent)

No technical conflict, but the surface area for tracking failures is wide. If any one domain is blocked by a privacy proxy (e.g., Apple MPP), the corresponding system loses signal silently.

**Malformed HTML near open-tracking pixel:**
```html
<img src="https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?..." width="1" height="1" alt=""></custom>
```
The `</custom>` closing tag is invalid and orphaned. Most parsers will ignore it, but it is a markup error that could cause issues in strict-mode renderers or future HTML5 validators.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**11 images missing `alt` text** — QA flagged all tracking pixels and several content images (`2a5adc5afb647aad.png`, `f441d45d2128ae53.png`, `c2f81e9ac8daee44.png`, `415c8a18fc2950ea.png`, `69494b48a79b4240.png`, `78308d1d9adb0751.png`, `916d6fa08d185233.png`) with no `alt` attribute.

Tracking pixels should have `alt=""` (empty string) plus `aria-hidden="true"` — the open.aspx pixel has neither. The `o.gif` pixel has `alt=""` and `aria-hidden="true"` set correctly; that pattern should be applied consistently.

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta** — `maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0` prevents pinch-to-zoom. This is a WCAG 2.1 failure (SC 1.4.4) for users who need to enlarge content.

**Empty `<title>` tag** — `<title></title>` is present but blank. Screen readers announce the title when the email is opened as a document; a descriptive title aids navigation for assistive technology users.

**Google Fonts load via both `<link>` and `@import`** — Roboto and Montserrat are fetched twice each (once via `<link rel="stylesheet">`, once via `@import` inside a `<style>` block). This doubles the font-load requests in clients that support web fonts. Wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!--> ... <![endif]-->` so it won't affect Outlook, but it's redundant overhead in all other clients.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Unresolved token in image URL:**
```
mi_kc_redm_end_dte=&mi_kc_event_pe
```
The `916d6fa08d185233.png` Movable Ink URL contains `mi_kc_redm_end_dte=` with no value — the token was not substituted before send. Depending on how Movable Ink uses that parameter, this could cause the rendered image to fall back to a default or error state at open time.

No other visible unresolved merge tokens (`{{`, `%%`, `*|`) detected in the truncated source.

The List-Unsubscribe and physical address gaps are flagged as relay-capture artifacts by QA, which is plausible — ESPs typically inject these at the SMTP envelope layer, not in the HTML body.
