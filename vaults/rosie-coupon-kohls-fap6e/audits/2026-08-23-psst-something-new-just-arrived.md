# Psst, something new just arrived 👀
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-23**
## Executive summary

- This is a straightforward new-arrivals product dump dressed up with a single before/after hero image, and it's one of the weaker structural ideas in Sephora's recent run — no editorial concept, no curation logic, just eight brand blocks stacked vertically (Tower 28, Drunk Elephant, Chris McMillan, One/Size, Aestura, Dr. Groot, Dolce & Gabbana) each tagged "NEW • ONLY AT SEPHORA." The App Fest banner at the very top ("25% off Select Brands," today only) is actually the most time-sensitive, actionable offer in the whole email, but it's crammed into a thin strip above the fold and gets buried the instant the eye drops to the big "NEW AND ONLY HERE" hero.
- The before/after skin photo is a smart hook for the Tower 28 foundation block right below it, and it's the one genuinely well-executed visual moment in the send. Everything past that is a long, undifferentiated scroll of product-and-price blocks that reads more like a category-page export than a curated moment — compare this to the Aug 20 OLIVE YOUNG send, which had an actual organizing idea (co-branded edit, Hall of Fame grid, GWP). This one has none of that.
- Net: functional but forgettable. It'll get opened on subject-line curiosity but won't hold attention past the first two screens, and the real offer (App Fest 25% off) is under-sold relative to its urgency.

## What's working

- The before/after skin close-up is a strong, scroll-stopping visual and ties directly to the Tower 28 foundation claim below it.
- Each product block cleanly states brand, "NEW • ONLY AT SEPHORA," a short benefit line, and price — consistent formatting throughout.
- The App Fest 25% off banner and Rewards-points-multiplier block give the email two concrete, quantifiable offers rather than vague "shop now" language.
- No rendering bugs — images load, text doesn't overlap, layout holds together end to end.

## What's weak

- The genuinely urgent offer (25% off, "TODAY ONLY") is squeezed into a thin top banner and gets visually dominated by the "NEW AND ONLY HERE" hero seconds later — hierarchy is backwards.
- Eight near-identical brand blocks in a row with no grouping, no "shop the edit" framing, and no differentiation between them — by block five or six it's just scroll fatigue.
- Subject line "Psst, something new just arrived" and the "New Rare Beauty Fragrance" teaser near the bottom aren't reflected anywhere prominent — the actual new-arrival hook of the subject gets diluted across seven equal-weight brands instead of leading with one hero product.
- This is the fourth "new-drops-and-launches" flavored send in about a week (Aug 20 OLIVE YOUNG, Aug 19 Chanel launch, Aug 21 KJH.brand) — cadence fatigue is real for anyone who's opened those too.

## Recommendations

- 1. Lead with the App Fest 25% off banner as the actual hero — bigger, above the before/after image, with a countdown or explicit end-time since it's "today only." That's the highest-urgency, highest-value offer in the email and it's currently an afterthought.
- 2. Pick one hero "new" product (Tower 28 foundation, since it already has the before/after proof) and lead the scroll with it; move the other six brands into a condensed 2-up or 3-up grid below rather than seven full-width repeating blocks.
- 3. Add a line of specificity to the subject/preview about the App Fest deadline, since "today only" urgency is currently invisible until deep in the email.
- 4. Cut redundant brand blocks or group them under a single "New This Week" grid tile so the email doesn't feel like a category export.
