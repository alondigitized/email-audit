# Save 20% | Find outfits for every summer occasion ✨
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-19**
## Executive summary

- This is Kohl's second consecutive day arriving with the same structural offer. June 18 sent "Save 20% | Summer style on a budget? No sweat 😎" and June 19 now sends "Find outfits for every summer occasion." The subject variation is cosmetic — the underlying deal, coupon bar, and Kohl's Cash call-out are identical sends dressed in slightly different language. Any subscriber who opened yesterday has already seen this.
- The hero copy, "Put an end to 'I have nothing to wear' days," is genuinely the strongest line Kohl's has written in this run — emotionally resonant, conversational, and earns the summer fashion frame. The creative rapidly loses focus after that: women's fashion hands off to a handbag feature, then a family styles block, then a Toy Story licensed-character section, then — the most damaging element — a Father's Day gift module four days after Father's Day passed. That last block is a content hygiene failure. It signals automated assembly from a shelf of reusable modules with no human QA pass.
- Net judgment: the hero concept is the best this brand has deployed in weeks, but it's buried inside a five-section kitchen-sink layout with a stale holiday module still running. The bones are good; the editorial discipline is not.

## What's working

- **Hero headline is the best in this send window.** "Put an end to 'I have nothing to wear' days." is specific, emotionally real, and directly motivates the summer fashion frame.
- **20% off is immediately visible.** The top banner + the side callout next to the hero makes the offer scannable without scrolling.
- **Kohl's Cash reinforcement.** The loyalty earn line in the header is a consistent trigger for longtime Kohl's subscribers — it's working harder than most brands do with their loyalty hooks.
- **Family styles block broadens reach cleanly.** The Women / Men / Girls / Boys segmentation underneath the family imagery is one of the better-executed multi-audience sections this brand has run recently.
- **Clean render throughout.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no layout failures visible.

## What's weak

- **Same deal, second day.** A 20% off email the day after a 20% off email trains the subscriber to wait for the permanent sale rather than act. The cadence has collapsed into noise.
- **Stale Father's Day module.** A "Gifts he'll use on repeat" section running four days after Father's Day (June 15) tells the subscriber this was assembled automatically. No one looked at it before it went out.
- **Toy Story section breaks the editorial voice.** "Old friends. New outfits. Endless play." works as a licensed-character play for kids, but it sits awkwardly between adult summer fashion and family lifestyle content with no visual bridge.
- **No urgency or deadline anywhere.** The 20% coupon has no visible expiry. After consecutive same-day sends, there is no credible reason to click today vs. tomorrow.
- **Five competing sections with equal visual weight.** The email has no point of view — it scrolls like a category browse, not a curated editorial moment.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Remove the Father's Day module immediately.** This is the single most damaging element. Stale holiday content on June 19 signals automation failure. Replace it with a summer clearance or "outfit builder" block.
- 2. **Add a hard coupon expiry.** "Ends Sunday" or "3 days only" in the offer bar and near the CTA converts this from a perpetual sale into a credible deadline. Without it, the repeat-send cadence actively suppresses urgency.
- 3. **Cut to 3 sections maximum.** Hero / fashion editorial → family styles → one lifestyle anchor. Drop the Toy Story block from this email or give it its own dedicated send where it can breathe.
- 4. **Differentiate the subject from June 18's send.** The two subjects are variants of the same line. Rotate the product story, not just the wording — a different occasion or a different product angle creates a reason to open.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Save 20% | The 'I have nothing to wear' edit is here`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your summer outfit problem: solved. (Plus 20% off.)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `From the backyard to the beach — all summer styles, one discount. Ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Tops, sandals, family fits — 20% off, limited time.`
- 5. **Make the hero CTA destination earn the copy.** "Shop Now" under "Put an end to 'I have nothing to wear' days" should land on a curated summer occasion page, not the Kohl's homepage. The copy promises a point of view; the destination should deliver one.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Kohl's "Save 20% | Find outfits for every summer occasion"

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound for legacy ESPs but carries several compliance gaps (missing List-Unsubscribe headers, no visible physical address) and accessibility defects (blocked zoom, missing alt text on content images). One stray invalid HTML tag is present in the open-tracking block.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Stray invalid tag in open-tracking block**
```html
<img src="https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?2I65NGU536REBALXAS35L3LDBU.60267..." width="1" height="1" alt=""></custom>
```
`</custom>` is an orphaned, non-standard closing tag immediately after the open pixel. Some parsers will surface this as a DOM error; in strict renderers it may break surrounding layout.

**Triple open-tracking pixels**
Three separate 1×1 open trackers fire on render:
- `click.chp.kohls.com/o/...` (CoherentPath)
- `mi.kohls.com/.../o.gif` (MessageGears)
- `click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?...` (Salesforce Marketing Cloud)

Three vendors tracking the same open event will produce overcounting across platforms. This is a data hygiene issue, not a deliverability one, but worth flagging.

**22 click-redirect links unverifiable**
All CTAs route through `click.chp.kohls.com` and `click.s.kohls.com`. QA skipped HTTP probing on all 22. UTM parameter presence on resolved destinations cannot be confirmed from source alone (see §6).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0" />
```
Blocks pinch-to-zoom on mobile. This is a WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 failure and is rejected by iOS Safari 10+ (Apple ignores this flag, but Android WebView honors it).

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Several clients (Gmail web, some Apple Mail versions) display the `<title>` in tab or preview context. Empty title is a minor accessibility gap.

**Missing alt text on content images**
QA flagged 11 images missing `alt=""` or descriptive alt text. Six are tracking pixels where `alt=""` is correct and present (`aria-hidden="true"` on the o.gif is correct). The remaining five are content images served from `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/`:
- `2a5adc5afb647aad.png`
- `f441d45d2128ae53.png`
- `c2f81e9ac8daee44.png`
- `415c8a18fc2950ea.png`
- `69494b48a79b4240.png`

These appear to be rendered personalization images (filename pattern matches MessageGears dynamic image rendering). Missing alt text means image-off or screen-reader recipients see nothing for these blocks.

**Redundant Google Fonts loading**
Roboto and Montserrat are loaded three ways simultaneously: two `<link>` tags and two `@import` calls inside `<style>`. This fires up to four separate network requests for the same assets in clients that support web fonts, and does nothing in clients that don't.

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens visible in the provided HTML (no `{{token}}`, `[FIELD]`, or `%%token%%` patterns found in source).

`mi_u=604230016` in tracking pixel URLs and `mi_ecmp=1015592_2026619` in image parameters appear to be correctly resolved subscriber/campaign IDs — not placeholders.

No issues found beyond the dynamic image alt-text gap noted in §3.

---

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe header absent** (QA confirmed)
RFC 2369 requires `List-Unsubscribe` in the email header. Its absence means Gmail and Apple Mail cannot surface the native unsubscribe UI. This also increases spam-report rate as the primary unsubscribe path.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post absent** (QA confirmed)
RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is required by Gmail and Yahoo for senders exceeding 5,000/day as of February 2024. Its absence puts high-volume sends at inbox placement risk with both providers.

**Physical mailing address not detected** (QA confirmed)
CAN-SPAM §7(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in every commercial email. The address may exist in the truncated body content, but QA did not detect it — this needs manual verification against the full rendered output.

**`meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer"`**
```html
<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />
```
This suppresses the HTTP `Referer` header on all outbound link clicks from email clients that respect it. Intended to block email tracking by recipient mail servers, but it also strips referrer data from any web analytics that fall back to `document.referrer` rather than UTM params.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM params unverifiable** — All 22 CTAs route through click-redirect domains. The resolved destination URLs and their UTM parameters cannot be confirmed from source. The image tracking URLs include `email_name=260619_DG_Email_Friday` (internal send label), which confirms campaign metadata is being passed to MessageGears but says nothing about GA4/Adobe Analytics attribution on the landing page.

**`no-referrer` meta breaks fallback attribution** — As noted in §5, the `no-referrer` policy means any visitor who arrives without UTM params on the final URL will show as `(direct)` in referrer-based attribution rather than `email`.

No issues found with domain consistency — `s.kohls.com` (sending domain) and `kohls.com` (expected destination) are aligned.
