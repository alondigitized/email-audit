# Epic Deals right this way 👉
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-26**
## Executive summary

- This is another straight rerun of Kohl's category-blitz template — purple "EPIC DEALS" hero, hot-pink price tiles, coupon/barcode footer wall — the same skeleton that's carried at least six sends since 09-20 (09-25's "Epic Deals in your cart," 09-24's rewards push, 09-23's two sends, 09-22, 09-21). What's actually new here is thin: the deal grid is reshuffled (sweaters, bath towels, casual shoes, sneakers, tees, fleece, pajamas, Sephora, bedding, decor), and the "Baby & Toddler Stock-Up Sale" banner is inserted mid-scroll as the one semi-fresh beat.
- The render itself is clean — no broken images, no overlapping text — and the visual hierarchy is legible: purple hero → Home/Women/Kids nav → tile wall → stock-up banner → rewards math block → footer coupons. But there's no single hero product or offer; it's ten-plus disconnected deals stacked vertically, which forces the reader to keep scrolling to find something relevant instead of landing on one clear CTA.
- Given the brand history, this is fatigue territory. A subscriber who's seen five nearly identical "Epic Deals" sends this week has no reason to open a sixth with a nearly interchangeable subject line ("right this way" vs. "who's fired up" vs. "still going strong"). Judgment: functional but forgettable — a 5/10 send, consistent with the rest of this cadence.

## What's working

- Clean render — no layout breaks, no image failures, no lorem ipsum.
- Clear top-of-email hierarchy: status bar → hero → nav tabs → first deal tile, all above the fold.
- Concrete, specific pricing throughout ($24.99, 6.99, 25% off, 5/$35) rather than vague "save big" language.
- Rewards math is spelled out visually ("Take $10 off $50 + Take $10 off $50 = 3x rewards"), which is a genuinely helpful loyalty mechanic.
- Home/Women/Kids nav bar gives some structure to an otherwise sprawling tile wall.

## What's weak

- No single hero offer — the eye has nowhere obvious to land; it's a wall of ten+ competing tiles with equal visual weight.
- Subject line is functionally identical to five prior sends this week ("Epic Deals right this way" vs. "still going strong," "who's fired up," etc.) — pure fatigue.
- The Baby & Toddler Stock-Up Sale banner is buried mid-scroll after seven other deal tiles, when it's arguably the most differentiated offer in the email.
- Demographic mix in imagery is generic family/lifestyle stock — nothing signals a specific persona focus for this send.
- Excessive coupon/barcode footer wall repeats the same visual block this brand has run six times running, adding scroll fatigue with no new information.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the generic "Epic Deals" umbrella subject entirely for a cycle.** Six near-identical sends in a week has trained subscribers to skip this pattern. Lead with the one genuinely time-limited, differentiated offer in the email — the Baby & Toddler Stock-Up Sale ("on sale only twice a year," through Oct 4) — instead of another generic deals blast.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Baby & Toddler Stock-Up Sale: twice-a-year event ends Oct 4`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Take $10 off $50 in baby & toddler — 6 days left`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus 3x rewards on top of your Kohl's Cash`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stock up before this one closes back down for months`
- 2. **Move the Stock-Up Sale banner to the top**, right under the hero, since it's the only offer in this email with real urgency (twice-a-year, dated deadline) — everything else is evergreen category discounting.
- 3. **Cut the tile count.** Ten-plus competing deals with no hierarchy dilutes all of them; group into 3-4 category cards max and let people click through to see the rest.
- 4. **Vary the subject formula going forward.** Rotate between category-specific and urgency-specific framings rather than reshuffling the same "Epic Deals + emoji" pattern each send — track opens by variant to see if fatigue is measurable.
