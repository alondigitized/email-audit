# Just dropped: point of view.
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-03**
## Executive summary

- This is a "point of view." brand launch email — Sephora's own private-label makeup line getting a founder-story treatment, built around a 3-step "prep it / snatch it / wear it" routine with product shots, quotes, and pull-tested copy like "my skin looks visibly smoother, firmer, and way more snatched." It's a real departure from the daily 50%-off sitewide dumps this brand has been sending (8/28–9/2), and that alone is worth crediting — but the execution buries the actual news under a wall of near-identical black-background product tiles that scroll forever before any real differentiation shows up.
- The top strip still leads with "50% off Select Beauty" (PHILR, Urban Decay, The INKEY List) — so the email opens with yesterday's promo, not today's headline. That's a genuine hierarchy miss: the subject and hero say "introducing point of view," but the very first thing the eye hits is a discount banner for other brands. By the time you get through the routine sections, it's an 11-product catalog of a launch, not a story.
- Net: interesting change of pace after a fatigue-inducing discount streak, but it's overstuffed and undersells the news with a leftover promo banner up top. Middling send — better than the last several, still not tight.

## What's working

- Clean "introducing point of view." hero with a clear black-on-white intro and a single "SHOP IT" CTA.
- The prep/snatch/wear routine structure gives the product dump actual narrative logic instead of a random grid.
- Real customer-style quotes ("Every time I use it, my skin looks visibly smoother, firmer...") add texture and social proof.
- Individual product callouts show name, price, and a one-line benefit — genuinely useful detail for a skincare-focused shopper.
- Departure from the 50%-off cadence that's dominated the last 5+ sends is a welcome reset.

## What's weak

- The very top of the email is a leftover "50% off Select Beauty" banner for PHILR/Urban Decay/The INKEY List — it competes with and precedes the actual launch news.
- Eleven+ product tiles in a row (prep, snatch, wear) makes this feel like a catalog scroll, not a curated "point of view," undercutting the editorial premise.
- No visible pricing/urgency framing on the hero itself — "own your POV" section has no discount or deadline, just "shop it."
- After the routine, the email pivots into three unrelated modules (Skincare Value Sets, Kilian, Olive Young) — dilutes focus right when it should close on the new line.
- Visual monotony: nearly every product shot uses the same black-background flatlay style, so nothing pops as "the hero."

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the 50%-off banner from the top entirely (or move it below the fold) — a launch email should open with the launch, not a competing discount for other brands.
- 2. Trim the routine to 1 hero product per step (3 total) instead of 2 each — pick the bestseller from prep/snatch/wear and let the rest live on a "shop the full line" page.
- 3. Add a real incentive to the hero — "New. Only at Sephora." plus something concrete (Beauty Insider points multiplier, GWP, or intro price) would give the eye something to act on beyond curiosity.
- 4. Drop or relocate the Skincare Value Sets / Kilian / Olive Young blocks to a separate send — they have nothing to do with "point of view" and dilute the launch focus.
- 5. Tighten the preview text so it complements rather than duplicates promo boilerplate.

- **Subject Alt B:** `New from Sephora: point of view has landed`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Prep, snatch, wear — the routine, in 3 steps`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Only at Sephora: the debut of point of view`
