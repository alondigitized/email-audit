# Save 20% | Summer style on a budget? No sweat 😎
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-18**
## Executive summary

- This is Kohl's first clean pivot out of its brutal Father's Day run — and the timing is right. Sent June 18 (Father's Day was June 15), the brand drops the gift-buying frame and lands on "Move more. Spend less." as a summer athletic anchor. That headline is the single best thing in this email: tight, tonal, and actually earned by the Nike/Tek Gear brand bar above it. The 20% off coupon is prominent at the top, and the Kohl's Cash earn offer at the bottom with a June 21 deadline provides real urgency.
- The problem is structural — four or five competing content modules below the hero fight for attention without a clear hierarchy. The Haggar menswear block ("Polished and effortless styles designed for the moments that matter") feels like it was pasted in from a different brief than the athletic opener. The "Finds that keep their cool, even when summer won't" grid and the "Dress like the deals depend on it" block underneath it further dilute the athletic narrative. This email is trying to be a full-store summer event and a focused activewear pitch at the same time, and it lands as neither.
- Compared to the June 15 "time crunch" send (the strongest of the Father's Day run), this one steps back in focus. The creative is clean and renders fine, but the strategy is unfocused.

## What's working

- "Move more. Spend less." is punchy, seasonal, and directly supported by the Nike/PLX/Tek Gear brand logos above it — one of the better hero headlines this brand has used in the audited series.
- The 20% coupon is visible without scrolling — positioned above the hero where it can't be missed.
- The Kohl's Cash deadline (June 21) is surfaced at the bottom, giving a real end-date that the middle-of-the-series Father's Day sends mostly lacked.
- Brand logos (Nike et al.) serve as credibility anchors that make the athletic section feel curated rather than generic.
- No visible render bugs — clean layout throughout.

## What's weak

- The Haggar block is a tonal non-sequitur dropped mid-email; "polished and effortless for the moments that matter" directly contradicts the "move more / no sweat" athletic energy established above it.
- Multiple competing section headlines ("Outfit the whole family," "Finds that keep their cool," "Dress like the deals depend on it") fragment the eye — there's no single dominant story after the hero.
- No urgency in the subject line; the June 21 Kohl's Cash deadline is buried at the bottom rather than being surfaced where it can drive the open.
- The "Outfit the whole family (Even the one who never acts still)" copy is trying too hard to be clever and reads as filler.
- The 25% badge visible in the hero section is never clearly attributed — it reads as a floating element without context.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the Haggar block entirely or move it to its own send.** The menswear "polished moments" narrative needs its own brief, not a middle slot in an activewear email. Replacing it with a category grid (athletic footwear, shorts, sports bras) would tighten the story.
- 2. **Surface the June 21 Kohl's Cash deadline in the subject line.** This is the single biggest lift available — a real deadline with a concrete dollar amount is exactly what the prior series was missing. "Earn $10 Kohl's Cash — ends June 21" beats "No sweat."
- 3. **Collapse to one hero CTA.** The Women / Men / Kids triple-button pattern appears at least twice before the fold — pick one primary entry point (Shop Activewear) and let the secondary links live in the body.
- 4. **Clarify the 25% badge.** If it's a specific brand or category discount, label it. Floating percentage badges without attribution feel like a design asset that wasn't filled in.

## Technical Audit — Kohl's "Save 20% / Summer Style" Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email uses a standard table-based layout with three independent tracking stacks (CoherentPath, MessageGears, Adobe AAM) and passes QA at 33% due to pervasive missing alt text and absent compliance headers. One invalid HTML tag in the body is the most acute rendering risk.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Invalid closing tag in body**
```html
<img src="https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?XXUMK42PYS4U3LXHHGSTH275YI.60264..." width="1" height="1" alt=""></custom>
```
`</custom>` is a stray/invalid tag — almost certainly a template rendering artifact from the ESP. Depending on the mail client's HTML parser, this can break DOM structure for everything that follows it.

**22 click-tracking links unverified**
All destination URLs pass through `click.chp.kohls.com` (CoherentPath) and `click.s.kohls.com` (MessageGears). Final redirect targets and UTM parameters could not be probed. This is expected behavior for tracked emails but means destination URL correctness is unverifiable from source alone.

**Three overlapping tracking stacks**
- `click.chp.kohls.com` — CoherentPath CDP
- `mi.kohls.com` + `click.s.kohls.com` — MessageGears ESP
- `kohls.demdex.net` — Adobe Audience Manager

Redundant open/click tracking is common at this scale but increases pixel count and load time.

**Empty favicon `<link>` tag**
```html
<link href="" rel="shortcut icon">
```
An empty `href` on a `<link>` causes some clients to fire a request to the current URL. Harmless in most email renderers but is noise; this should be removed.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Zoom disabled on mobile**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0" />
```
`user-scalable=0` and `maximum-scale=1` together prevent pinch-to-zoom. This is an accessibility failure under WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and is blocked by some accessibility-focused email clients.

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Screen readers announce the title when entering a message. An empty title leaves a blank announcement. Should be set to the subject line or a descriptive string.

**12 images missing alt text** (per QA)
Includes both tracking pixels and content images:
- Tracking pixels (`1f22b1a7…`, `o.gif`, `event`, `demdex`) — these should use `alt=""` (decorative); several are missing it entirely rather than having an empty string
- Content images (`2a5adc5afb647aad.png`, `f441d45d…`, `415c8a18…`, product images `54703482_root_2x`, `54703482_1_2x`) — these require descriptive alt text; without it, image-off rendering (Gmail app, Outlook) produces no fallback text

**Google Fonts loaded via `<link>` and `@import` — duplicated**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Roboto...">
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Montserrat...">
@import url("https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Roboto...");
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Montserrat...);
```
Both Roboto and Montserrat are loaded twice (once via `<link>`, once via `@import`). The `@import` inside `<style>` is inside an `<!--[if !mso]>` conditional, but the `<link>` tags are unconditional. This causes duplicate font requests in non-MSO clients. More critically, Google Fonts are blocked by the majority of email clients (Apple Mail Privacy Protection, Gmail, Outlook) — the fallback stack (`Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif`) should always be tested as the primary render.

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM: No physical mailing address detected** (QA WARN)
The visible HTML body contains no physical address. CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid postal address in every commercial email. Either it appears below the truncation point or it is absent — verify against the full source.

**List-Unsubscribe header absent** (QA WARN)
Neither `List-Unsubscribe` nor `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) headers were detected. These may have been stripped by the AgentMail relay rather than being absent from the original send, but the net effect is that Gmail's native one-click unsubscribe UI will not appear for this message. At Kohl's list volume, Gmail deprioritizes bulk senders without RFC 8058 one-click support.

**SPF/DKIM authentication unknown** (QA WARN)
`Authentication-Results` header not captured. Sender domain `s.kohls.com` (MessageGears subdomain) should have DKIM alignment with `kohls.com`. Cannot confirm pass/fail from available data.

**`<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow">`** — correct; prevents web-cached versions from being indexed.

**`<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">`** — see Section 6.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**`no-referrer` meta strips referrer on outbound clicks**
```html
<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />
```
This directive instructs the browser/webview to send no `Referer` header when the subscriber clicks through. If the CoherentPath/MessageGears redirect does not inject UTM parameters into the final destination URL before handing off to the browser, analytics on kohls.com will classify these sessions as Direct rather than Email. Since all 22 links go through tracked redirects, verify that the redirect resolves to URLs with explicit `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` parameters — do not rely on referrer passthrough.

**Campaign ID visible in pixel parameters**
`mi_ecmp=1015491_2026618` and `email_name=260618_DG_Email_Thursda` (truncated) are present in MessageGears render-tracking pixels, confirming campaign attribution metadata is set. Verify the same `mi_ecmp` value maps to the correct campaign in the MessageGears dashboard.

**Destination URL correctness unverifiable**
All 22 click links resolve through `click.chp.kohls.com` redirects — final landing page URLs and their UTM strings cannot be confirmed without probing the redirect chain.
