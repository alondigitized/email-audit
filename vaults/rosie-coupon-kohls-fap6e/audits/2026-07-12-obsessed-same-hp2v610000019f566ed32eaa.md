# Obsessed? Same.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-12**
## Executive summary

- This is the third editorial send in five days following Sephora's punishing Stacking Weekend blitz — and by design, it's deliberately not a sale. The "Going Viral" editorial concept is well-timed: social media beauty culture is the dominant conversation in this category, and featuring RHODE (Hailey Bieber's brand, perennially trending on TikTok) as the hero pick is exactly the right call. The "HOT ON SOCIAL" badge and marquee ticker treatment signal that Sephora knows its audience.
- The problem is the email tries to be six different things at once. "Going Viral" hero → "May You Also Like" product grid → "Spot It. Shop It." social gallery → Hair Lineup → Sephora Lip → Harlem Perfume Co. — that's five distinct editorials in a single scroll. The AESTURA loyalty redemption banner competes at the very top without relation to any of the content below it. The visual hierarchy scatters attention rather than funneling it.
- "Obsessed? Same." is a sharp, on-brand subject line with genuine voice. But after July 11's "These picks are everywhere" and July 8's "Summer's hottest beauty," Sephora is running its third consecutive editorial email with no promotional hook. The subject doesn't hint at what you're obsessed *with*, and the email doesn't answer that question cleanly once you open. Good vibes, diffuse execution.

## What's working

- **"Going Viral" marquee ticker** is the strongest single creative choice — immediately signals this is curated, trending-first content rather than another sale.
- **RHODE as hero** is an excellent pick: culturally current, high visual brand equity, naturally aligned with the "obsessed" subject line framing.
- **"HOT ON SOCIAL" badge** on the hero product provides earned social proof without requiring a copy block.
- **AESTURA trial redemption** at the top is a genuine loyalty perk that adds value — smart placement even if it feels disjointed from the editorial below.
- **"Harlem Perfume Co." brand spotlight** shows editorial range and supports founder/indie-brand discovery, which indexes well with Sephora's modern shopper.

## What's weak

- **No editorial through-line**: five distinct content blocks with different themes kill momentum — the reader has no throughline from "obsessed" to any single destination.
- **"Obsessed? Same." resolves nothing** — the email never clearly explains what everyone is obsessed with, which makes the subject feel like a tease without a payoff.
- **AESTURA banner is disjointed** from the "Going Viral" concept below it; the tonal jump from a loyalty perk redemption to a scrolling editorial is jarring.
- **Third consecutive editorial in five days** with no offer — cadence fatigue is real and this send doesn't differentiate itself from July 8 or July 11 beyond the concept name.
- **"May You Also Like" recs** feel algorithmically generic here; they undercut the curated "these picks are going viral" premise with what reads as standard personalization logic.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Commit to one hero and kill three of the five sections.** If this is "Going Viral," every section should live under that editorial umbrella. Drop the Hair Lineup and Sephora Lip columns or make them products explicitly called out as viral, not just catalog items.
- 2. **Make the subject payoff visible in the hero header.** "Obsessed? Same." needs a first line inside the email that names the obsession — e.g., "RHODE's Peptide Lip Treatment has 1.2M TikTok saves. You're not alone." That converts intrigue into click.
- 3. **Move AESTURA below the fold or into a dedicated loyalty moment.** Leading with a points-redemption banner immediately before "Going Viral" is a mood mismatch that cheapens both.
- 4. **Add one urgency signal.** Not necessarily a sale, but "available while supplies last" or "trending now — 500 sold in 24 hours" would pull the click without needing a promo.

- **Subject Alt B:** `The lip treatment everyone is obsessed with`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `1M TikTok saves. 0 reasons not to try it.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Swipe for what's trending on your For You page right now`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` on viewport meta — confirmed issue**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents user-initiated zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). iOS Safari since v10 ignores this, but Android WebView still respects it.

**Missing `lang` attribute on `<html>` — confirmed issue**
```html
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" xmlns:v="urn:schemas-microsoft-com:vml" ...>
```
No `lang="en"` present. Screen readers cannot infer document language; pronunciation and braille output degrade. WCAG 3.1.1 failure.

**Overly broad `text-size-adjust: none` — confirmed issue**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  -moz-text-size-adjust: none;
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```
Applied to `body` and all block/inline elements. Suppresses OS-level font scaling for users with accessibility settings. Standard practice is to scope this to `100%` on `body` only, not `none` on every element.

**Mobile breakpoint at `max-width: 480px` only**
Modern phones (iPhone 15 Pro: 393px, Pixel 8: 412px) fall under this, but the single breakpoint leaves no room for tablet-intermediate layouts. Low severity for email; noted for completeness.
