# 15% off + summer style upgrades ... we're in 😎
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-11**
## Executive summary

- This is a classic Kohl's kitchen-sink send: women's dresses, men's tops, sports licensing gear, and a 15% coupon all packed into one scroll. The subject line leads with the discount, which is the right hook — but that 15% off coupon is buried at the very bottom of the email, after three entirely different product stories. Anyone who opened because of the offer has to survive a lot of scroll before they find it.
- The creative is competent and clean. The summer fashion hero ("A full calendar calls for a full closet") is a decent seasonal frame, the pricing callouts ($34.99, $9.25) add tangibility, and the Kohl's Cash layer adds loyalty value. But the email is fighting itself: it's simultaneously a women's style email, a men's basics email, and a sports gear email, which means it's really targeting no one in particular.
- The send works fine as a promotional blast but won't punch above its weight. It reads like a weekly circular that was adapted for email rather than something designed for an inbox.

## What's working

- **Concrete prices are visible** — the $34.99 and $9.25 callouts give the reader a real anchor, not just vague "great deals."
- **Seasonal framing lands** — "A full calendar calls for a full closet" is summer-timely and fits a May send.
- **Kohl's Cash double-dip** — stacking the 15% coupon with a $10 Kohl's Cash earn is a genuine value layer that loyal Kohl's shoppers will recognize.
- **Render is clean** — no broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds together across sections.
- **Sports gear section is clearly labeled** — the Soccer/MLB/PGA/NHL logos make the category legible at a glance.

## What's weak

- **The 15% off coupon — the subject line's main promise — is at the very bottom.** Readers who opened for the discount have to scroll past three product stories to redeem it.
- **Too many competing stories with no priority.** Women's dresses → men's tops → sports gear → coupon is four distinct audiences, which dilutes focus and click intent.
- **No urgency surfaced above the fold.** The "through May 10" deadline exists but is only revealed at the bottom, removing the pressure that would drive faster action.
- **CTA fragmentation.** Multiple "Shop Now" buttons with no dominant action make it unclear what Kohl's actually wants the reader to do.
- **The three category tiles (Kids / Shop / Home)** right after the hero feel orphaned — no offer, no copy, just icons that interrupt the scroll before the first product story.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the 15% off coupon to the hero.** Lead with the discount — make it the first thing visible, not the last. The subject line created the expectation; the body should pay it off immediately.
- 2. **Pick one primary audience per send.** Split the women's fashion story and the sports gear story into separate sends to different segments. A women's shopper does not want to scroll past soccer jerseys to find dresses.
- 3. **Surface the deadline above the fold.** "Ends May 10" placed next to the hero CTA would convert fence-sitters who are otherwise scrolling without urgency.
- 4. **Consolidate to one primary CTA.** Replace the four "Shop Now" buttons with a single high-contrast button that links to the 15%-off landing page.
- 5. **Cut the orphaned category tile row** (Kids / Shop / Home) — it breaks visual flow and adds no offer value.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Extra 15% off ends May 10 — summer looks inside`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your 15% off coupon + new summer arrivals`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dresses from $34, men's tops from $9 — shop before the code expires.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stack with Kohl's Cash. 15% off code inside, expires May 10.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Kohl's "15% off + summer style upgrades" (2026-05-11)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound for a legacy HTML4 table-based layout, but carries three classes of real defects: a malformed closing tag in the tracking pixel block, missing alt text on content images (not just pixels), and absent RFC-compliant unsubscribe headers that expose the send to inbox filtering.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**22 click-redirect links unverified.** All CTAs route through `click.chp.kohls.com` and `click.s.kohls.com`. Destination URLs and UTM parameter chains could not be validated by the probe.

**Three distinct open-tracking pixels:**
- `https://click.chp.kohls.com/o/645be212-…` (CoherentPath)
- `https://mi.kohls.com/p/up/88954bbbbcab3c0e/o.gif` (MessageGears)
- `https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?OVYMVIAN4L6E3BWGGIXWBN2CYI.60259` (Salesforce MC / ExactTarget)
- `https://kohls.demdex.net/event?d_sid=13245196` (Adobe Audience Manager sync)

Running three simultaneous open-tracking stacks is redundant and inflates pixel load time on slow connections.

**Malformed closing tag in pixel block:**
```html
<div style="font-size:0; line-height:0;">
  <img src="https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?...">
</custom>   <!-- ← stray </custom>, no opening tag -->
</div>
```
This is invalid HTML and will trigger parser error recovery behavior across email clients.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta** (`maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0`) — blocks pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).

**Empty `<title>` tag** — screen readers announce the email window as untitled.

**11 images missing `alt` text** confirmed by QA. The critical ones are content images (not just pixels):
- `2a5adc5afb647aad.png`, `f441d45d2128ae53.png`, `c2f81e9ac8daee44.png`, `415c8a18fc2950ea.png` — all routed through `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/` with `email_name=260511_DG_Email_Monday`. These appear to be personalized/rendered content images that carry visual meaning.
- The tracking pixels at `click.chp.kohls.com` and `kohls.demdex.net` lack `alt=""` + `aria-hidden="true"` (the `mi.kohls.com/p/up/` open pixel is correctly marked `aria-hidden="true"`; others are not).

**Google Fonts double-loaded:** Both `<link>` tags and `@import` rules fetch the same Roboto and Montserrat families, causing two HTTP requests per font family in non-MSO clients.

**Empty favicon href:**
```html
<link href="" rel="shortcut icon">
```
Triggers a 404 or self-request in some clients; should be removed entirely.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Hardcoded `mi_u` value across all MessageGears image URLs:**
```
mi_u=604230016
```
This value is identical in every `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/` and `mi.kohls.com/p/up/` URL in the provided source. If this is a static placeholder rather than a per-recipient merge token, all opens/clicks from this recipient segment would be attributed to a single subscriber ID, corrupting engagement analytics.

**`mi_mid` in the CoherentPath pixel** (`019e148c-4e80-7000-89d5-0ae527cb9943`) appears to be a per-message GUID — correct.

No unresolved `{{token}}` or `%%token%%` syntax visible in the truncated source.

---

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM / RFC)

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent** — QA confirmed not present (or not surfaced through the AgentMail relay). Required by Gmail and Yahoo's 2024 bulk sender policy for lists over 5K/day. Without it, Gmail may suppress the unsubscribe UI entirely or route to spam.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent** — RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe not supported. Gmail bulk sender requirements mandate this for high-volume senders.

**`Authentication-Results` not captured** — SPF/DKIM pass/fail status unknown. Sending domain is `s.kohls.com` (Salesforce MC subdomain); DMARC alignment cannot be confirmed from available data.

**`noindex, nofollow` meta present** — correct; prevents web-cached copies from being indexed.

**`referrer: no-referrer` meta present** — correct; prevents referrer leakage to landing pages.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 22 CTAs pass through click-redirect domains (`click.chp.kohls.com`, `click.s.kohls.com`), so UTM parameters on final destination URLs cannot be confirmed without live link resolution. The `email_name=260511_DG_Email_Monday` parameter in the `mi.kohls.com` image URLs confirms campaign tagging is in place at the ESP layer, but downstream UTM propagation to `kohls.com` is unverified.
