# Summer is looking stylish (and super affordable) 😎
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- This is Kohl's first send after the Father's Day 40%-off blowout on June 21, and the hangover is palpable. The subject drops any offer specificity entirely — "super affordable" is marketing fluff, not a number — which is a significant step back after a day of "40% OFF ENDS TONIGHT." The hero creative is clean and seasonally appropriate: a woman in a blue summer dress with a breezy headline that works. But the email quickly falls apart below the fold with four separate mini-campaigns crammed in — summer fashion, Ninja air fryer appliances, a Deal Days teaser, and a Pride Month block — making it feel like a catch-all content dump rather than a focused send.
- The 20% off offer is technically present but buried in fine print ("select styles") and repeats the same discount that ran on June 18 and June 19. Subscribers who saw 40% off yesterday are being handed a step-down offer inside an email that doesn't even state a percentage in the subject line. The Ninja appliance module is the biggest creative misfire: it has zero connection to the summer fashion narrative and competes directly with the fashion CTAs for scroll attention.
- This is a filler send — safe, unambitious, and unlikely to drive meaningful action beyond habitual clickers. The Deal Days preview buried near the bottom is actually the most newsworthy hook in the email and is wasted as an afterthought.

## What's working

- **Hero image and copy land well** — the blue dress photo is attractive and "almost more fun than your plans" is the strongest writing in the email.
- **$19.99 starting price gives a concrete anchor** — a real number makes the fashion section feel shoppable.
- **Four-category grid (Women / Men / Kids / Shoes)** covers the family purchase occasion cleanly in one visual block.
- **No render bugs** — the email displays cleanly with no broken images, overlapping text, or empty fields.
- **Deal Days "STARTS TOMORROW" block** — the urgency hook is genuine and timely; it just needs to be higher in the email.

## What's weak

- **Ninja appliance module completely breaks the fashion narrative** — a rotisserie air fryer has nothing to do with summer outfits and is jarring mid-scroll.
- **No offer specificity in the subject line** — "super affordable" after yesterday's "40% off ENDS TONIGHT" feels like a downgrade with no number to back it up.
- **Offer fatigue: 20% off for the fourth time in five days** — June 18, 19, and now 22 all carry the same discount tier; subscribers have been trained to ignore it.
- **Deal Days — the freshest hook in the email — is buried after the Ninja block** near the bottom rather than leading or at minimum placed second.
- **Pride Month block feels performative and disconnected** — no offer, no product, just a "Learn More" button at the very bottom of an already unfocused send.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with Deal Days, not fashion filler** — "Starts tomorrow" is genuine news. Move the Deal Days teaser to the hero or second slot. Yesterday was 40% off; today should tease tomorrow's event, not rerun June 18's email.
- 2. **Cut the Ninja module or spin it into its own send** — appliance cross-sells kill fashion scroll momentum. If Ninja sponsorship requires inclusion, relegate it below Deal Days, not above it.
- 3. **Put a number in the subject** — "super affordable" is forgettable. The email contains a $19.99 anchor; use it, or reveal that Deal Days are coming with a concrete tease.
- 4. **Drop the Pride Month module or integrate it into a product offer** — a stand-alone "we support Pride" block with no product link drives zero revenue and reads as checkbox marketing at the bottom of a promotional email.
- 5. **Preheader should tease the Deal Days hook** — whatever the inbox preview currently shows (likely leaking nav/boilerplate given the email structure), swap it to "Deal Days start tomorrow — here's your preview."
- **Subject Alt A:** `Deal Days start tomorrow — preview what's dropping`

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Summer looks starting at $19.99. Plus: Deal Days begin June 23.`

## Technical Audit — Kohl's "Summer is looking stylish" (2026-06-22)

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound for a bulk commercial send but carries several confirmed accessibility violations, a malformed HTML tag, and three compliance gaps that the QA relay could not verify due to header capture limitations. No unrendered merge tokens detected.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Malformed closing tag on open-pixel div**
```html
<img src="https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?..." width="1" height="1" alt=""></custom>
```
The `</custom>` tag is invalid HTML. It will be silently ignored by most parsers but can cause downstream AST parse failures in any email rendering or analytics pipeline that re-parses the source.

**Three separate open-tracking pixels fired on load:**
- `click.chp.kohls.com/o/6b165fdb-...` (CoherentPath)
- `mi.kohls.com/p/up/88954bbb...o.gif` (Messagegears)
- `click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?...` (Kohl's ESP)
- `kohls.demdex.net/event?d_sid=13245196` (Adobe Audience Manager / demdex)

Four pixels for one open event is redundant and increases load time; demdex may be blocked by aggressive privacy filters (Brave, iOS Mail Privacy Protection) differently than the other three.

**22 click links unverifiable:** All destination URLs pass through redirect domains (`click.chp.kohls.com`, `click.s.kohls.com`) — expected, but means broken-link detection cannot confirm landing page availability.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta** — disables pinch-to-zoom. Violates WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Many enterprise email clients ignore this, but it is still a compliance signal in accessibility-regulated industries.
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0" />
```

**Empty `<title>` tag** — `<title></title>` renders as no subject in some browser-based webmail preview tabs and is a minor deliverability signal for spam filters that parse document metadata.

**Empty favicon `href`** — `<link href="" rel="shortcut icon">` generates a 404 request to the current host in some webmail clients that honor this tag.

**Duplicate Google Fonts loading** — Fonts are loaded via both `<link>` elements and `@import` inside a `<style>` block for both Roboto and Montserrat. This fires two separate HTTP requests per font family, adding ~200–400ms render latency before fallback fonts display.

This may be a relay artifact rather than a true absence, but cannot be confirmed. If absent from the actual SMTP delivery, this breaks one-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058) required by Gmail and Yahoo's 2024 bulk sender rules for senders exceeding 5,000/day.
