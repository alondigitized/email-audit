# Save 20%  ✅ Score Kohl's Cash ✅ Repeat 🔁
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- This is email number twelve (or more) in Kohl's promotional blitz since July 1 — and it arrives the morning after two consecutive 30%-off closing-urgency emails. The step-down to 20% isn't just a discount — it's a trust signal, and it's negative. Any subscriber who clicked "HOURS LEFT for 30% off" yesterday is going to feel like they just paid a premium for the privilege of being first.
- The creative itself is competent but overstuffed. The email opens with a legible home-entertaining hero — "Save on must-haves that make guests linger. (Sorry in advance.)" is genuinely good copy — then abruptly pivots into a Back-to-School multi-category grid before finishing with a coupon barcode. These are two distinct campaigns crammed into one send, and neither gets enough room to breathe.
- The subject line's "Repeat 🔁" is the most interesting creative choice in the batch and also the strangest: framed as a three-step recipe (Save → Score → Repeat), it has a self-aware charm that 99% of Kohl's subscribers will gloss right past. The execution doesn't earn the concept — there's nothing in the body that follows through on the "repeat" hook. Net result: a mid-range promotional email that would score higher if sent in isolation, but suffers badly from cadence and offer regression.

## What's working

- **Home hero copy is the best writing in this streak.** "Make guests linger. (Sorry in advance.)" has wit and specificity that most Kohl's subject lines never attempt.
- **Tiered discount architecture is visible and credible.** 20% headline + 50%/40% coupon-based categories give shoppers a reason to scroll.
- **Back-to-School section is seasonally appropriate** — Girls, Juniors, Shoes, Boys, Young Men, Backpacks is a complete grid with clean category navigation.
- **Kohl's Cash reinforcement is prominent** and positioned early (before the hero), which matters for loyalty members.
- **Barcode at the bottom** is a practical, frictionless in-store conversion tool — rare to see, and it works.

## What's weak

- **20% after 30% is a punishing sequence.** Any subscriber paying attention to the 2026-07-08 "HOURS LEFT for 30% off" send now sees this as a demotion, not an offer.
- **Two campaigns in one email dilute both.** Home entertaining and Back to School have different buyers, different mindsets, and different urgency windows — neither gets its best shot here.
- **"Repeat 🔁" in the subject lands as filler.** The Save→Score→Repeat conceit is never developed inside the email, so it reads as subject-line padding.
- **No deadline visible in the above-fold area.** The 50% and 40% coupon offers presumably expire, but the urgency hook is buried.
- **The OUTDOOR/INDOOR vertical text labels** (rotated 90°) add visual noise without adding navigation value on mobile.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Don't step down the offer without a narrative.** If 20% is the new floor, name a new earn ("Earn double Kohl's Cash this weekend") or add a category exclusion frame ("Now: home + BTS at 20% off — your wardrobe categories are next") to give the discount regression a reason to exist.
- 2. **Split home and Back-to-School into separate sends.** The home "guests linger" creative is strong enough to own a full email. BTS with its grid of kids/teens/shoes is a completely different scroll.
- 3. **Follow through on "Repeat 🔁" in the body.** If you're going to use the Save→Score→Repeat mechanic in the subject, anchor it visually in the email — a timeline graphic, a punch-card visual, or a "here's what you earned last time" stat.
- 4. **Add a visible deadline above the fold.** The coupon-based 50%/40% offers need an expiry date in the banner, not buried in footer copy.
- 5. **Retire the vertical OUTDOOR/INDOOR text labels** — they eat visual real estate and add no click value.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Back to school starts now — save 20% + earn Kohl's Cash`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Home refresh + BTS picks: 20% off today`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus 50% off patio and 40% off kitchen — with your coupon, through July 13.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Girls, boys, shoes, backpacks — plus home deals at 20% off.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**22 tracking links skipped by HTTP probe.** All click links route through `click.chp.kohls.com` and `click.s.kohls.com` redirect chains — not probeable without live session cookies. No broken destination URLs confirmed, but cannot rule out dead links in this send.

Three distinct pixel/tracking systems fire on open:

**No issue with multiples per se**, but the CoherentPath pixel `img` is missing `alt=""` and `aria-hidden="true"` (both present on the Marigold pixel — inconsistent treatment).

**Malformed tag confirmed in source:**

immediately after the `click.s.kohls.com` open pixel `img` — this is an unclosed/invalid custom element that will produce a parse warning in strict renderers.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Alt text absent on all 9 flagged images** — includes both tracking pixels and what appear to be content images (`2a5adc5afb647aad.png`, `415c8a18fc2950ea.png`, etc. served from `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/`). The Marigold open pixel correctly uses `alt="" aria-hidden="true"`; that pattern is not applied consistently.

**`<title>` is empty** (`<title></title>`). Screen readers and some preview panes surface this; it should be set to match the subject line or a sanitized equivalent.

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta** — `maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0` blocks pinch-zoom on mobile, which fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text).

**Google Fonts loaded three times** — once via `<link>` and twice via `@import` inside a `<style>` block for both Roboto and Montserrat. Redundant; adds parse overhead in clients that support web fonts.

**`meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer"`** — this suppresses referrer on outbound clicks, which will cause UTM params to be invisible to any server-side referrer parsing on Kohl's landing pages (though query-string UTMs are unaffected if present in the click URLs themselves).
