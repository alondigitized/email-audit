# Yes, please! Grab up to 50% off.
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-18**
## Executive summary

- This is Sephora's fourth "bait-and-switch" structure this week, and it's the most blatant yet. The subject "Yes, please! Grab up to 50% off" and the top banner both promise "SURPRISE! 50% OFF Select Beauty" — but that's where the honesty stops. The instant you scroll past the banner, the email pivots hard into "IN THE MIX," a new-product showcase led by a $38 shampoo, followed by nine more new-launch tiles (Kosas, Biodance, Crown Affair, DECDOLL, Make Up For Ever, InnBeauty, Urban Decay, Ole Henriksen), a Rhode sampler pitch, YSL fragrance, a 4x points push, and a Marc Jacobs concealer — none of which are the 50%-off offer.
- This brand history now shows a clear pattern: 09-15's "Select brands are 50% off today" pulled the same trick, 09-16's "Beauty's most wanted" did too, and now this one repeats it almost verbatim just three days later. A subscriber opening this for the discount gets a single banner's worth of it, then a kitchen-sink product catalog. The banner itself is well-executed — bright, urgent, clear CTA — but the email around it is unfocused and doesn't serve the subject line at all.

## What's working

- The hero banner delivers exactly what the subject promises: "SURPRISE! 50% OFF" with a clear "SHOP NOW" button, pink/purple gradient makes it pop against the white space above it.
- "In the mix" section has strong product photography — the frosted glass shampoo bottle shot is genuinely eye-catching.
- Green "CLEAN AT SEPHORA" and "ONLY AT SEPHORA" badges give quick trust/differentiation signals without cluttering copy.
- Grid layout for the ten new-launch products is clean and scannable, each with price/value comparison (e.g., "$36 ($68 value)").
- No render bugs — images load, text doesn't overlap, layout holds together end to end.

## What's weak

- Massive bait-and-switch: the 50%-off offer that got the subject line and the open gets one banner, then is abandoned for 90% of the email's length.
- No product-level clarity on what's actually included in "Select Beauty" — no visible list, category, or brand names tied to the discount.
- The email tries to be everything at once: a flash sale, a new-arrivals catalog, a Rhode promo, a fragrance points push, and a concealer launch — none gets the room to land.
- This is the third time this week (09-15, 09-16, now 09-18) the brand has run this exact subject-vs-hero mismatch; repetition will train subscribers to stop trusting the subject line.
- "Exclusions/terms apply" fine print sits directly under the banner in tiny text — the one meaningful catch on the headline offer is the least visible line on the screen.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the email match the subject.** If 50% off is the hook, the whole first screen (and ideally a follow-up section) should show what's included — specific brands/categories — not pivot to unrelated new launches after one banner.
- 2. **Cut the kitchen-sink stacking.** Ten new-product tiles plus Rhode plus YSL plus 4x points plus Marc Jacobs is too much for one send — split into a dedicated new-launches email and keep this one focused on the sale.
- 3. **Surface the exclusions honestly and early**, not as an afterthought under the CTA — pair "Select Beauty" with 2-3 examples of what qualifies right in the banner.
- 4. **Vary the subject pattern.** Three near-identical "X% off" bait subjects this week will erode open rates; alternate between sale sends and launch sends so each subject is trustworthy.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Today only: 50% off these Sephora favorites`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your 50% off pick list is ready`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shampoo, skincare, makeup — see what qualifies before it's gone`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `An extra day of 50% off, but only through today`
