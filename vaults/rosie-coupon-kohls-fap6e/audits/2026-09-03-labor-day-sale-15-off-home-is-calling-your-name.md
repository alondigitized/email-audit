# Labor Day Sale + 15% off home is calling your name 📞
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-03**
## Executive summary

- This is a "everything sale" grab-bag dressed up with a home hook in the subject line, and the disconnect between subject and hero is the first problem — "15% off home is calling your name" promises a home-focused send, but the hero banner leads with the generic Labor Day $10-off-$25 + Kohl's Cash mechanic (same one from today's other Kohl's send, "Labor Day Sale + $10 off your $25 purchase STARTS TODAY 👋", 2026-09-03), and home doesn't even appear until three screens down. By the time the reader hits the actual "Take an extra 15% off your home purchase" banner, they've already scrolled past women's tees, jeans, dresses, and bath towels.
- Once you get past the top, the execution is actually decent: category nav bars, real price points ($9.99, $7.64, up to 60% off furniture), a clean CLEARANCE 85% banner, and a "Picked with you in mind" product grid with visible price anchors. But structurally this reads like the standard Kohl's kitchen-sink template with a home-flavored subject line bolted on for segmentation testing, not a genuine home-first campaign like 2026-09-02's Martha Stewart send ("Fall home picks at great prices... yes & yes 😍", 6.00/10).
- Verdict: functional but mistargeted — the copy oversells a home focus the layout doesn't deliver, and it's the second near-identical Labor Day $10/$25 mechanic email today, which is exactly the cadence fatigue this brand history has already flagged repeatedly.

## What's working

- Clear top-of-funnel offer stack: $10 off $25+ coupon plus $5 Kohl's Cash per $25 spent, both legible in the hero without scrolling.
- Home does get a dedicated banner and its own 15% coupon further down, plus barcodes/promo codes shown clearly in the footer for in-store use.
- Product grid ("Picked with you in mind") shows real prices and real products (mattress topper, bath towel, jeans) — no lorem ipsum, no broken images.
- Category nav pills (Women/Kids/Home, then Home/Women/Men/Kids again near Clearance) make it easy to jump to a section.

## What's weak

- Subject promises "15% off home" but the hero and first four sections (Women's tees, jeans, dresses) have nothing to do with home — home content doesn't appear until roughly the fifth scroll section.
- The email repeats the category nav bar twice (once near top, once before Clearance) with no differentiation, which reads as template filler rather than intentional structure.
- Visual hierarchy is muddled — there are at least seven distinct percent-off callouts (10, 15, 40, 30, 20, 60, 20, 40, 85) competing for attention with no single dominant offer.
- Same $10-off-$25 + Kohl's Cash mechanic as today's other Kohl's Labor Day send — a subscriber getting both in one day sees duplicate creative with a different subject line slapped on.

## Recommendations

- 1. Rewrite the hero to actually match the subject — if this send is meant to be a home-segment email, lead with the Martha Stewart / home banner and the 15% home coupon first, then push apparel below the fold. Right now the copy writes a check the layout doesn't cash.
- 2. Cut one of the two duplicate category nav bars — use the first one for navigation and turn the second (pre-Clearance) into a "shop by home category" bar instead, reinforcing the subject's promise.
- 3. Pick one hero offer and visually dominate with it (either the $10/$25 or the 15% home coupon, not both diluted); demote the smaller percent-off badges into the section they belong to instead of stacking them all as equal-weight banners.
- 4. Add real preview/preheader text — none is visible in this send, which wastes prime inbox real estate right next to the subject line.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your home refresh: 15% off + Labor Day savings`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Home's on sale — up to 60% off + extra 15%`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Furniture, bath, decor — plus $10 off $25 storewide`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stack an extra 15% off home with your Labor Day coupon`
