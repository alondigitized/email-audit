# Take 10% off, Rosie
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-14**
## Executive summary

- This is a triggered welcome email for a new Beauty Insider member named Rosie. The mechanics are solid: a prominent 10% off hero, an explicit promo code (SE3ARPH), a hard expiry date, a product recommendation grid, and a loyalty tier visualization — all in one scroll. Sephora knows how to build this kind of email and it shows.
- The standout move is the "Wondering Why You Got This?" section. Proactively explaining the send to a new member is unusually transparent and earns more trust than a generic urgency countdown. The personalized subject line is also well-executed — 20 characters, first name, concrete offer.
- The weakest point: the "SHOP NOW >" CTA sends Rosie to the Sephora front door rather than anywhere curated for a new member with an active first-purchase code. That's the single biggest conversion gap in an otherwise clean execution.

## What's working

- **Name in subject + concrete offer** — "Take 10% off, Rosie" is short, personal, and unambiguous at inbox glance.
- **Full offer disclosure above the fold** — code (SE3ARPH), discount amount (10%), and expiry (5/25/2026) are all visible without scrolling.
- **"Wondering Why You Got This?" copy** — rare and effective transparency that converts skepticism into trust for a new member.
- **Beauty Insider tier visualization** — reinforces loyalty program value at exactly the right onboarding moment.
- **"Recommended For You" product grid** — gives Rosie something specific to click into immediately after seeing the discount.

## What's weak

- **"SHOP NOW >" is a dead-end CTA** — generic, no destination signal, no product category alignment for a new member.
- **"This one's for you!" illustrated section** adds scroll depth without adding any information — it's decorative filler between the hero and the product grid.
- **Double asterisks on "10% OFF\*\*"** telegraph exclusions; the fine print is small and the full offer scope isn't clear above the fold.
- **No social proof anywhere** — no star ratings, no bestseller labels, nothing in the recommendation grid to build purchase confidence.
- **Dual barcode block at the footer** is visually heavy and in-store-focused, which may be irrelevant friction for a new digital-first shopper.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Replace "SHOP NOW >" with a destination-specific CTA** — "Shop Your Picks" linking to a curated new-member page, or a category CTA like "Explore Skincare" — biggest single lift.
- 2. **Move the expiry date into the subject or preheader** — urgency is currently buried in body fine print; surfacing "expires 5/25" doubles the open pull.
- 3. **Add bestseller or star-rating badges to the recommendation grid** — one line of social proof per product card meaningfully increases click confidence.
- 4. **Cut the illustrated "This one's for you!" section** — it delays the product grid on mobile and adds nothing actionable.
- 5. **Surface the exclusion scope as a plain-English one-liner** directly below the code instead of relying on asterisks alone.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Rosie, your 10% off code expires 5/25`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your welcome gift: 10% off your first Sephora order`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Code SE3ARPH — valid on thousands of products. Starts today.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `First-purchase discount + picks we think you'll love.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — Invalid `robots` meta directive**
```html
<meta name="robots" content="no index" />
```
`no index` with a space is not a recognized directive; the valid token is `noindex` (no space). This affects the web/mirror version if indexed.

**Issue 2 — `maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom (WCAG 1.4.4 violation)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Suppressing zoom fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA). Users with low vision who rely on pinch-zoom cannot enlarge content. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**Issue 3 — Overly broad `text-size-adjust: none`**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```
Applying this to `body` overrides OS-level font size preferences. Limit to specific table cells where layout-breaking auto-adjustment is the actual risk.

No issues found with VML namespace declarations or Outlook conditional comment structure (not present in visible source, but namespace inclusion is standard).
