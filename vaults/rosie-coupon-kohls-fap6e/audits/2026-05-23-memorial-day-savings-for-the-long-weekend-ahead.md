# Memorial Day savings for the long weekend ahead ➡️
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- This is Kohl's third Memorial Day email in three days, and it shows. The creative has more depth than the prior two sends — the clearance-to-70% callout is new, and the multi-tier discount ladder (30%/20%/50%/70%) gives the email more visual range — but the subject line is the weakest of the trilogy. Yesterday's send named the dollar amount; today's says "savings for the long weekend ahead" and tells the reader nothing about what to expect inside. That's a step backward on the most important line in the email.
- The body itself is a classic Kohl's kitchen-sink scroll: Memorial Day hero, category nav strip, six or seven individual deal modules, a bold clearance block, and then the persistent $10-off-$25 offer anchoring the bottom. The clearance "up to 70% off" is the strongest offer on the page and deserves to be in the subject — it isn't. The rest of the email is competent but familiar to any Kohl's subscriber who has opened the last three sends.
- On balance, this email exists to capture late Memorial Day weekend shoppers who haven't converted yet. It does that job adequately. But after three near-identical sends, offer fatigue is real, and today's subject gives recipients no new reason to open.

## What's working

- **Clearance callout earns its space.** The "CLEARANCE up to 70% off" block is the sharpest offer in the email and gives the send something genuinely new vs. May 21 and May 22.
- **Memorial Day hero is prominent.** The sale badge lands first; the eye goes there before anything else. Hierarchy is intact.
- **Discount ladder is well-structured.** 30% → 20% → 50% → 50% → 70% creates a sense of escalation and gives something for every budget.
- **Kohl's Cash and $10-off-$25 are visible.** The loyalty mechanics appear both in the hero badges and reinforced at the bottom — subscribers who care about stacking will see both.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no visible layout bugs at the thumbnail scale.

## What's weak

- **Subject buries the lead.** "Memorial Day savings for the long weekend ahead" names zero discounts — the 70% clearance, the $10 off $25, and the 50% sections all go unmentioned where they'd have driven opens.
- **Same $10-off-$25 offer for the third straight day.** At this point it reads like a standing discount, not a Memorial Day urgency play. It loses its conversion pull.
- **Kitchen-sink structure diffuses focus.** Seven-plus deal modules compete for attention; there's no single hero product or category that the email commits to.
- **"Shop Now" CTAs are generic throughout.** Not one button names what you're shopping — "Shop Clearance," "Shop Bedding," or "Shop Women's" would add specificity without much effort.
- **The clearance module is buried mid-to-bottom.** The strongest offer in the email appears after several weaker modules rather than being elevated closer to the fold.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the subject with the 70% clearance.** This is the sharpest, freshest angle in the email and it's buried in the body. Surfacing it in the subject breaks the pattern of the last two sends.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 70% off clearance + Memorial Day savings`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Clearance: up to 70% off — plus $10 off your $25`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Grab deals before the weekend ends — $10 off $25 when you spend today.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Kohl's Cash, 50% off select styles, and clearance up to 70% — long weekend deals.`
- 2. **Move the clearance block above the fold.** Swap the first 30%-off module with the 70%-off clearance section — or at minimum put a clearance callout in the hero. The strongest offer shouldn't require scrolling.
- 3. **Retire the $10-off-$25 from the hero.** After three days it no longer reads as a Memorial Day event — it reads as the always-on threshold. Either time-gate it with an explicit expiry ("ends Monday") or replace the hero badge with the clearance number.
- 4. **Sharpen CTA copy on at least the top two modules.** "Shop Clearance," "Shop Bedding Sale," "Shop Women's 50% Off" — any verb+noun beats the generic "Shop Now" and makes the email scannable for a subscriber who already knows the Kohl's layout.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Kohl's Memorial Day Email (2026-05-23)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound for a legacy table-based layout but has several accessibility violations, a malformed HTML tag, duplicate font-loading requests, and compliance gaps that are unverifiable due to AgentMail relay header loss. Two Movable Ink parameters appear to have fired empty, suggesting possible personalization logic gaps.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Multiple open-pixel redundancy**
Three separate open-tracking pixels fire on load:
- `click.chp.kohls.com/o/cd85224e...` (CoherentPath)
- `mi.kohls.com/p/up/88954bbbbcab3c0e/o.gif` (Movable Ink)
- `click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?753VQ2PO33RELNS2GSFWF7WKWQ.60257` (Salesforce Marketing Cloud)

This is not an error per se, but triple-counting opens across three platforms will produce inconsistent open-rate figures across dashboards.

**22 click-tracking links unresolvable**
All CTA links route through `click.s.kohls.com` or `click.chp.kohls.com` redirects. The QA probe skipped all 22 — destination URLs and UTM parameters cannot be verified without resolving the redirect chain.

**Malformed `</custom>` closing tag**
```html
<img src="https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?..." width="1" height="1" alt=""></custom>
```
The `</custom>` tag is invalid HTML. Most email clients will ignore it, but it can cause parse errors in strict renderers and may corrupt DOM structure in Outlook's Word renderer.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some email clients (notably Apple Mail) display the `<title>` value in thread previews and tab labels. An empty title degrades the experience in those contexts.

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0" />
```
This blocks pinch-to-zoom on mobile. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) requires content be resizable up to 200% without assistive technology. This is a Level AA violation.

**11 images missing alt text (confirmed by QA)**
Includes both tracking pixels (which should use `alt=""`) and what appear to be content images (`2a5adc5afb647aad.png`, `f441d45d2128ae53.png`, `c2f81e9ac8daee44.png`, `415c8a18fc2950ea.png`, and others). Tracking pixels correctly using `alt=""` is fine; content images without alt text fail screen readers entirely when images are disabled.

**Duplicate Google Fonts loading**
Both `<link>` elements and `@import` rules load the same Roboto and Montserrat fonts:
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Roboto&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
<!-- and inside <style>: -->
@import url("https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Roboto&display=swap");
```
This triggers two HTTP requests per font per client that supports web fonts, adding ~200–400ms latency on first render.

**Empty favicon link**
```html
<link href="" rel="shortcut icon">
```
No functional impact in email, but generates a 404 request against the sender domain in some clients.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**Two Movable Ink parameters appear to have fired empty**
In image URL params:
```
&mi_kc_redm_end_dte=&mi_kc_event_pe
```
Both `mi_kc_redm_end_dte` and `mi_kc_event_pe` have no values. These look like conditional content keys (redemption end date, event period) that were not populated. If Movable Ink uses these to serve dynamic content, empty values could cause fallback content to render rather than the intended personalized block.

**Subscriber ID tokens appear correctly fired**
`mi_u=604230016` and `mi_ecmp=1013105_2026523` are present and non-empty across all Movable Ink image URLs — subscriber-level tracking is active.

---

### 5. Compliance

**List-Unsubscribe / List-Unsubscribe-Post headers unverifiable**
QA `[WARN]`: Both headers are absent from what the AgentMail relay captured. These are required for Gmail and Apple Mail one-click unsubscribe compliance (Google/Yahoo 2024 sender requirements). This may be a relay capture gap rather than a true absence — needs direct header inspection on a raw `.eml`.

**Physical mailing address not detected**
QA `[WARN]`: CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in the email body. The HTML was truncated before the footer — this must be confirmed against the full source. If absent from the rendered footer, this is a CAN-SPAM violation.

**Authentication-Results header not captured**
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status cannot be confirmed via the AgentMail relay. Sender domain is `s.kohls.com` (subdomain); alignment between envelope-from and DKIM `d=` domain should be verified on raw headers.

**`no-referrer` referrer policy is set**
```html
<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />
```
This suppresses the `Referer` header on all link clicks, meaning any server-side analytics relying on HTTP referrer to attribute email traffic will receive no signal. This is intentional in most ESP setups (UTM params carry attribution instead), but worth flagging if server logs are part of the attribution stack.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 22 CTAs route through tracked redirect URLs — destination URLs and UTM parameters cannot be verified. The presence of `mi_ecmp=1013105_2026523` on Movable Ink assets (format: `YYMMDD_campaign`) confirms campaign-level tracking is wired at the ESP layer. UTM parameter correctness on final landing pages requires resolving the redirect chain manually or via a link audit tool that follows redirects.
