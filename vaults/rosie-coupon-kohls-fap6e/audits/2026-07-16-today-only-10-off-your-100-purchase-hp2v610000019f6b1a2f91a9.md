# Today only ‼️ 10% off your $100+ purchase.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- This email is caught between two briefs: the subject line sells a straight discount ("10% off your $100+ purchase — today only") but the visual hero delivers a "Free Hair Sample Set" booking pitch. That's a structural mismatch that plays out the moment you open the email — you arrived expecting a price break, and you're looking at a service CTA instead. For a Sephora subscriber who knows the brand well, neither offer is offensive, but the creative incoherence costs both.
- Coming off yesterday's "Just Your Type" fragrance send — which was Sephora's most editorially coherent email this month — this one feels like two separate briefs were merged into one deployment. The discount is real, the free sample is real, and the render is clean. But the hierarchy fails the subscriber at the moment of decision.
- The 10% off a $100 basket is also a weaker absolute offer than the Stacking Weekend's 30% off + 2X points (July 7) — it needs a tighter, more confident execution to carry its weight, and it doesn't get one here.

## What's working

- Sephora's sender trust is high enough to generate an open regardless of subject complexity
- "Today only" is a concrete, credible urgency trigger — not manufactured scarcity
- The email renders cleanly with no broken images, overlapping text, or lorem ipsum
- "New 100-Point Rewards Are Here!" gives loyalty members a third engagement path that's genuinely additive
- Hair and beauty product photography is on-brand and visually appealing

## What's weak

- The subject promises a discount; the hero delivers a hair sample booking flow — a classic bait-and-switch structure, even if unintentional
- "BOOK TO REDEEM" implies a salon-style appointment process — higher friction than "Shop Now" for a subscriber who opened for a 10% discount
- Multiple competing CTAs (BOOK TO REDEEM, product grids, BOOK NOW for rewards) with no single dominant conversion action
- The 10% off offer — the entire reason a subscriber opened this email — is visually subordinate to a free sample pitch
- No preview text crafted to extend the subject; the email body's first text defaults to whatever banner copy sits at the top

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the hero match the subject.** If 10% off $100 is the lead, the email should open on a full-width discount banner with a direct "Shop Now, Save 10%" CTA — not a booking module for a free sample set.
- 2. **Split the two offers into two sends.** The free hair sample redemption is a strong standalone concept with a service angle; give it its own email where it can breathe without competing against a price promo.
- 3. **Rewrite the CTA for the sample.** "BOOK TO REDEEM" reads as an appointment — too much commitment for an impulse click. "Claim Your Free Set" or "Get Your Free Sample" removes the friction implication.
- 4. **Promote the 100-Point Rewards section earlier.** As a loyalty-first email sub-segment play, this has the clearest value for Insiders and should appear in the first scroll zone, not at the bottom.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom (WCAG 1.4.4 violation)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Disables user-initiated zoom on mobile. Fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Remove `maximum-scale=1` or raise to `maximum-scale=5`.

**No `lang` attribute on `<html>` element**
```html
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" xmlns:v="..." xmlns:o="...">
```
Missing `lang="en"` prevents screen readers from selecting the correct language profile. Add `lang="en"` (or appropriate locale).

**`text-size-adjust: none` applied to broad selector set**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td { -webkit-text-size-adjust: none; ... }
```
Disabling text-size-adjust on `body` and block-level elements is overly aggressive. If a mail client overrides this, the cascade is unpredictable. Scope to `td` only, which is the standard email pattern.

**Generic `<title>` tag**
```html
<title>Sephora</title>
```
Not a rendering blocker, but a non-descriptive title reduces accessibility for screen reader users who open the email in a browser tab (e.g., via "view in browser" links).

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**Cannot assess unsubscribe link or physical address** — both are required by CAN-SPAM (15 U.S.C. § 7704) and must appear in the email body. The truncated source does not include the footer. Verify:
- One-click or clearly labeled unsubscribe link present
- Sephora's physical mailing address present in footer

**`robots` meta directive has an illegal space**
```html
<meta name="robots" content="no index" />
```
`"no index"` (with space) is not a valid robots directive token. The correct value is `"noindex"`. While email clients ignore this, if the email is scraped or cached by a web renderer, the directive will not be honored. Fix: `content="noindex,nofollow"`.

**Authentication headers** — DKIM, SPF, and DMARC cannot be verified from HTML source alone. These must be confirmed via raw message headers (`shop@beauty.sephora.com` sending domain should pass SPF/DKIM aligned to `sephora.com`).
