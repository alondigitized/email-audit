# Move more & spend less during the Active Sale 👏
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-05**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, well-produced Kohl's send — no clipped starbursts, no garbled art, no overlapping elements, consistent with the run of solid technical renders logged since 7/30. The hero is legible and hierarchy is obvious: red-gray diagonal split, model kneeling in athleisure, "UP TO 40% OFF Activewear and shoes" in oversized type, single "Shop Active Sale" button.
- Where it loses points is redundancy and specificity. This is functionally the third "Active Sale" themed email in a week (following 8/4's "Score Active Sale deals on Nike, adidas, Tek Gear," 7.00/10, and 8/1's "Take 20% off... Active Sale," 9.00/10), and the subject line itself carries no number, no urgency, no personalization — all the concrete offer info lives in the preheader and hero image, not the subject a recipient actually sees first in the inbox list.
- The product tiles do real work: Nike on a Black woman, adidas on a tween girl, Under Armour shoes, Tek Gear on a boy — decent age/gender spread that broadens who feels targeted. Net: technically excellent, message clear, but this is the same "Active Sale" beat for the third time this week with a vaguer subject than its higher-performing predecessor.

## What's working

- Hero hierarchy is immediate: "UP TO 40% OFF" dominates the frame, with category ("Activewear and shoes") directly beneath it.
- Brand logo wall (Nike, adidas, Champion, Under Armour, FLX, Tek Gear) signals breadth of selection at a glance.
- Rewards status is called out at the top ("KOHL'S REWARDS MEMBER · Earn 5% REWARDS") — a real loyalty touchpoint, not generic.
- Product tiles show diverse models (adult woman, tween girl, boy) across four different brands, reinforcing "something for everyone."
- No render bugs anywhere — clean image loads, aligned nav buttons, legible starburst-free banners throughout.

## What's weak

- Subject line carries zero specifics — no percentage, no deadline, no product category named plainly (just "Active Sale" as a label).
- Exclusion fine print sits right in the hero ("Coupons do not apply to select brands"), undercutting the 40%-off headline before the reader even scrolls.
- The secondary "Ultimate Clearance Event — Save up to 70%" banner is a near-verbatim repeat of creative used in the 8/1–8/3 clearance sends, adding fatigue rather than a fresh reason to click.
- Preheader is diluted by trailing "View this email in a browser" boilerplate, wasting inbox-preview real estate that could carry more of the 70%-off hook.
- No urgency or deadline anywhere in subject, preview, or hero — nothing pushes "click today."

## Recommendations

- 1. Put a number in the subject line. "Up to 40% off" is sitting unused in the hero — move it to the subject where it'll actually move open rate.
- 2. Trim the preheader to drop "View this email in a browser" and let the 70%-off clearance teaser stand alone — that junk tail is actively hurting inbox-fit.
- 3. Add a light deadline cue ("ends Sunday," "today only") somewhere in the hero or preheader — this send has no time pressure anywhere.
- 4. Vary the clearance banner creative from the 8/1–8/3 version — reusing it back-to-back reads as recycled inventory, not a fresh push.
- 5. Since this is the third Active Sale-flavored send in a week, differentiate this one's angle (e.g., lead with kids'/back-to-school sport gear, which the tiles already support) rather than repeating the same generic activewear framing.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 40% off Active Sale: Nike, adidas & more`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Gear up: 40% off activewear ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus, clearance is stacking up to 70% off — shop before it's gone.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New activewear drops for the whole family, up to 40% off today.`

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **10 images with missing/empty `alt` text**, including the primary product image `56060938_5_2x` (`images.am.kohls.com/is/image/kohlsprod/56060938_5_2x?scl=1`) — a real content image, not just a tracking pixel, so this is a genuine accessibility gap for screen-reader users. The remaining flagged images are tracking pixels (`o.gif`, `event`, and several `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/*.png` beacons) where missing alt is low-impact but still fails strict WCAG checks.
