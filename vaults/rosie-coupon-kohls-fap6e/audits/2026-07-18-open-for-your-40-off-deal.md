# Open for your 40% off deal 😍
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-18**
## Executive summary

- This is Kohl's second email of July 18 — the morning send "Enjoy 40% off + earn Kohl's Cash 🤩" already covered the same territory. The subject here ("Open for your 40% off deal 😍") frames itself as a personalized coupon delivery, but the email body leads with Summer Home Essentials content and buries the actual coupon below a lifestyle product shot, a 50%-off block, and a full clearance section. A deal-aware Kohl's subscriber who opened the first send this morning will recognize this immediately as a duplicate and move on.
- The creative itself is clean. No broken renders, readable button hierarchy, and the clearance block with "Up to 70% off" is a legitimately stronger secondary hook than anything in the morning email. But the structure inverts the payoff: the one thing the subject promises (the coupon) doesn't appear until after significant scroll depth, which punishes mobile openers most.
- The real problem is strategic: this email should not have gone out. Two near-identical 40%-off sends in a single day trains subscribers to tune out both. The savings frequency since early July has been relentless, and today's double-send is the most visible example of cadence fatigue in this audit series.

## What's working

- Clearance block with "Up to 70% off" and "New Markdowns Taken" is a specific, credible escalation that morning email didn't lead with
- Coupon section with visible barcode is a functional, friction-low path to the discount
- Kohl's Cash pairing with the coupon is well-placed — loyalty double-dip is a strong retention hook
- "Shop Home," "Shop Now" button copy is clean and unambiguous throughout

## What's weak

- Subject promises a coupon but the email hero leads with Summer Home Essentials — coupon is below the fold on mobile, creating a bait-and-scroll experience
- This is the second 40%-off send today; cadence exhaustion makes this email disposable before it's opened
- The $3.99 price point visible in the hero grid anchors the email as a small-goods/clearance event, underselling the overall 40% coupon narrative
- No visible expiration on the coupon — urgency from the subject ("your deal") dissipates immediately once inside the email

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the coupon to the top.** If the subject promises a personal deal, the barcode and discount percentage need to be the first thing visible — before any product sections. Every pixel of delay costs clicks.
- 2. **Add a deadline to the coupon.** "Valid through Sunday, 7/20" would convert an idle browser into an action. No urgency is visible anywhere in the body right now.
- 3. **Don't send this email today.** The morning send already covered 40% off. If this is a retarget for non-openers, segment the list and say so — otherwise merge into one cohesive evening send that escalates the offer (40% + clearance stacked together).
- 4. **Replace the $3.99 hero grid item** with a mid-tier aspirational home item at 40% off — something that shows the coupon doing real work on a $40–$80 purchase.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your 40% coupon expires soon — grab it now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `40% off everything + up to 70% clearance, stacked`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Valid through Sunday — tap to save your coupon before it's gone`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Home essentials, clearance deals, and Kohl's Cash — all in one place`

## Full review
## 1. Overview

This is Kohl's second email of July 18 — the morning send "Enjoy 40% off + earn Kohl's Cash 🤩" already covered the same territory. The subject here ("Open for your 40% off deal 😍") frames itself as a personalized coupon delivery, but the email body leads with Summer Home Essentials content and buries the actual coupon below a lifestyle product shot, a 50%-off block, and a full clearance section. A deal-aware Kohl's subscriber who opened the first send this morning will recognize this immediately as a duplicate and move on.

The creative itself is clean. No broken renders, readable button hierarchy, and the clearance block with "Up to 70% off" is a legitimately stronger secondary hook than anything in the morning email. But the structure inverts the payoff: the one thing the subject promises (the coupon) doesn't appear until after significant scroll depth, which punishes mobile openers most.

The real problem is strategic: this email should not have gone out. Two near-identical 40%-off sends in a single day trains subscribers to tune out both. The savings frequency since early July has been relentless, and today's double-send is the most visible example of cadence fatigue in this audit series.

## 2. What worked

- Clearance block with "Up to 70% off" and "New Markdowns Taken" is a specific, credible escalation that morning email didn't lead with
- Coupon section with visible barcode is a functional, friction-low path to the discount
- Kohl's Cash pairing with the coupon is well-placed — loyalty double-dip is a strong retention hook
- "Shop Home," "Shop Now" button copy is clean and unambiguous throughout

## 3. What didn't

- Subject promises a coupon but the email hero leads with Summer Home Essentials — coupon is below the fold on mobile, creating a bait-and-scroll experience
- This is the second 40%-off send today; cadence exhaustion makes this email disposable before it's opened
- The $3.99 price point visible in the hero grid anchors the email as a small-goods/clearance event, underselling the overall 40% coupon narrative
- No visible expiration on the coupon — urgency from the subject ("your deal") dissipates immediately once inside the email

## 4. What I'd change

1. **Move the coupon to the top.** If the subject promises a personal deal, the barcode and discount percentage need to be the first thing visible — before any product sections. Every pixel of delay costs clicks.
2. **Add a deadline to the coupon.** "Valid through Sunday, 7/20" would convert an idle browser into an action. No urgency is visible anywhere in the body right now.
3. **Don't send this email today.** The morning send already covered 40% off. If this is a retarget for non-openers, segment the list and say so — otherwise merge into one cohesive evening send that escalates the offer (40% + clearance stacked together).
4. **Replace the $3.99 hero grid item** with a mid-tier aspirational home item at 40% off — something that shows the coupon doing real work on a $40–$80 purchase.

### 5. Compliance

- **`List-Unsubscribe` header not detected** — may not have been captured by the AgentMail relay. CAN-SPAM requires a functional unsubscribe mechanism; Gmail and Apple Mail also use this header for one-click unsubscribe UI. If the header is absent at the MTA level, inbox providers may suppress or penalize delivery.
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not detected** — one-click unsubscribe (required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy since 2024) cannot be confirmed. If absent, Google may show a degraded unsubscribe experience or route to spam.
- **`Authentication-Results` header not found** — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status is unknown. The sending domain is `s.kohls.com` (subdomain); alignment with `kohls.com` DMARC policy should be verified directly with mail headers from the original MTA. This is a relay capture limitation, not a confirmed failure.
- `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` suppresses referrer data on outbound clicks, which will cause direct/none attribution in site analytics regardless of UTM params — this is intentional for privacy but worth flagging for analytics teams.
