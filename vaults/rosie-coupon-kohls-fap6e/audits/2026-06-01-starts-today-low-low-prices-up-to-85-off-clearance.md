# STARTS TODAY: Low, low prices & up to 85% off clearance 🙌
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-01**
## Executive summary

- Kohl's opens June with a smart seasonal pivot — "Lowest Prices of the Season" is a credible new hook after last week's Memorial Day slog, and the 85% clearance badge in the hero gives the subject line teeth. The email earns its claims: the discount depth is real, the hierarchy is clean, and the Pride Month module at the bottom shows deliberate calendar awareness for June 1. This is probably the strongest Kohl's send since the May 29 reset.
- The problem is length and structure. After the strong hero, the email devolves into a catalog scroll — apparel, home, outdoor, intimates, beauty, Pride, Kohl's Cash — each section shouting its own discount. A subscriber scanning on mobile will mentally clock out after the third "50% off" block. The clearance narrative gets buried under category breadth.
- The Kohl's Cash callout finally resurfaces at the very bottom, which is a shame — it's one of the two most differentiating Kohl's mechanics and it's an afterthought here. Overall: good send, structurally bloated, scores well but leaves conversion on the table.

## What's working

- **Hero is unambiguous and powerful.** "LOWEST PRICES OF THE SEASON" + "CLEARANCE 85%" badge reads instantly — best top-fold this brand has shown in recent sends.
- **Tabbed quick-nav (Men / Women / Kids & Baby / Bed & Bath)** near the hero gives experienced Kohl's shoppers a fast path to their department without scrolling.
- **Pride Month section is well-placed.** It lands in the second half of the email and signals calendar intentionality on June 1 without overriding the sales message.
- **Discount depth is concrete throughout.** 40%, 50%, and 85% clearance are specific enough to justify clicking — not the vague "up to X% off everything" language that plagued some of the May sends.
- **Clean render.** No broken images, no overlapping text, no layout failures visible.

## What's weak

- **Catalog dump after the hero.** Seven-plus distinct product modules in a single scroll means no single category gets sufficient emphasis — apparel, home, outdoor, beauty, and intimates all compete for the same attention.
- **Kohl's Cash is buried at the very bottom.** One of the strongest retention mechanics in Kohl's arsenal gets treated like footer fine print. It drove the May 29 email's strongest moments; here it's an afterthought.
- **"Shop Now" CTAs are generic throughout.** With this many discount tiers and categories, each section CTA needed a verb+noun specific to the block — "Shop Clearance Tops," "Shop Outdoor Deals" — not a generic Shop Now.
- **Sender display name is campaign copy, not brand name.** "Kohl's Lowest Prices of the Season" as the from-name is a common Kohl's tactic but it reads as spam-adjacent in crowded inboxes — it dilutes the recognizability signal that "Kohl's" alone would carry.
- **No social proof or ratings.** Not a single star rating, review count, or bestseller callout visible. A $19.99 intimates section without any trust signal is a hard sell.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Promote Kohl's Cash to the hero or second module.** "Earn $10 Kohl's Cash on every purchase today" alongside the 85% clearance hero creates a double-pull — discount + reward — that the May 29 send proved works. Moving it from the bottom to the top would be the single highest-lift structural change.
- 2. **Collapse to three category blocks max.** Pick the three highest-conversion departments for June (Women's clearance, Home clearance, Activewear) and cut the rest. Depth beats breadth at this scroll length.
- 3. **Rewrite CTAs to be category-specific.** "Shop Women's Clearance," "Shop Outdoor Deals," "Shop Intimates under $20" — each button earns the click for its section rather than relying on generic momentum.
- 4. **Standardize the sender display name to just "Kohl's."** The campaign tagline in the from-name is easy to confuse with promotional noise.
- 5. **Add star-rating callouts to at least the top two featured products** — especially the $19.99 intimates block, which needs a trust signal to close.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Clearance up to 85% off — starts today`
- **Subject Alt B:** `85% off clearance + earn Kohl's Cash today`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Shop 40–85% off apparel, home & more — earn $10 Kohl's Cash on every order`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Prices this low don't last — shop clearance + earn Kohl's Cash now`

## Technical Audit — Kohl's "Lowest Prices of the Season" (2026-06-01)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a standard ESP-sent promotional HTML using HTML 4.01 Transitional with legacy MSO/VML compatibility layers. The primary infrastructure concerns are missing compliance signals (List-Unsubscribe headers, physical address) and pervasive missing alt text on tracking pixels and content images.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **22 tracking/redirect links skipped** by HTTP probe — all route through `click.chp.kohls.com` and `click.s.kohls.com`. Destination URL health is unverified.
- **Three separate tracking pixel systems** are active simultaneously:
  - `click.chp.kohls.com/o/...` — CoherentPath click tracker
  - `mi.kohls.com/p/up/...` — MessageGears open pixel
  - `click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?...` — a second open pixel (Salesforce Marketing Cloud signature)
  - `kohls.demdex.net/event?...` — Adobe Audience Manager sync pixel
- **Malformed HTML tag** at the open pixel block: `</custom>` is a non-standard closing tag with no matching open tag — likely a template artifact left in production HTML.

```html
<img src="https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?PLW4PD5SBD3UPOXAHS7R7CNRKU..." ...></custom>
```

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- **11 images missing `alt` text** confirmed by QA, spanning both tracking pixels and content images (product images via `images.am.kohls.com/is/image/...`). Tracking pixels should use `alt=""` explicitly; content images require descriptive alt text for screen readers and image-off rendering.
- **`<title>` element is empty** — screen readers announce the email window as untitled; some clients surface this in the tab or preview.
- **`user-scalable=0`** in the viewport meta tag prevents zoom on mobile, which is a WCAG 2.1 AA failure (SC 1.4.4).
- **Google Fonts loaded via both `<link>` and `@import`** in the same `<head>` — this is redundant and doubles the font request count on clients that support external CSS.

```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Roboto&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
<!-- AND separately: -->
<style>@import url("https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Roboto&display=swap");</style>
```

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens (`{{`, `%%`, `[%`) visible in the truncated source. The `mi_u=604230016` subscriber ID parameter appears consistently across all MessageGears pixel URLs, indicating merge token substitution completed correctly.

---

### 5. Compliance

- **`List-Unsubscribe` header not detected** via AgentMail relay — header may be stripped or absent. Gmail and Apple Mail require this for bulk sender compliance; Google's February 2024 mandate requires one-click unsubscribe for senders >5k/day.
- **`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent** — RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe not confirmed. This is a hard requirement under Google/Yahoo 2024 bulk sender rules.
- **No physical mailing address detected** in the HTML body (truncated source may contain it further down, but QA scanner did not find one). CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address.
- **`<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow" />`** — correct for email, prevents indexing if HTML is ever web-viewable.
- **`<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />`** — suppresses referrer on outbound clicks. This will strip UTM parameters from referrer headers at the destination, which has downstream analytics implications (see §6).

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

- The `no-referrer` meta policy means destination servers cannot read the HTTP `Referer` header — UTM attribution must be carried entirely in the click URL query string. Whether UTM params are appended to destination URLs is unverifiable since all 22 click links were skipped by the HTTP probe.
- The `cp_tp=v3.eJx9j7FL...` parameter on `click.chp.kohls.com` links is a base64/JWT-encoded payload — this is CoherentPath's personalization token, not a standard UTM param. If GA4/Adobe Analytics is the attribution system, verify that the landing page correctly maps CoherentPath click tokens to sessions.
- Email name tag `email_name=260601_DG_Email_Monday` appears in MessageGears pixel URLs — confirms send date/segment tagging is active for internal reporting.
