# They’re always SO good 👑
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-16**
## Executive summary

- "The Drop-Everything Edit" is Sephora's cult-favorites roundup, and it's basically the Aug 11 "Not a contest, but this beauty is winning" trending grid wearing a different script font — same skeleton, same "eight-ish products in a scroll" structure, just retitled. There's no organizing logic beyond "these are popular": Miss Dior and Sauvage perfume up top, then a random eight-pack spanning eye cream, hair serum, shampoo, hand sanitizer, and cleansing balm. That's not an edit, it's inventory.
- The trial-size offer banner and the top nav bar do more real work than the hero — "Choose a Trial Size" with a $30-purchase-with-code mechanic is at least a concrete, actionable deal, buried above a vague cursive headline that promises nothing specific. By the time you hit "Spot it. Shop it." and the $5-off Beauty Insider banner, the email has stacked three separate offers with three different rules, none foregrounded.
- Judgement: a low-effort send propped up by good product photography. Score in line with the brand's typical grid sends, not a standout.

## What's working

- Trial-size banner at the top has a real, specific offer (free Medik8/Aesop/Ultra Violette samples with a $30 purchase, promo code visible).
- Product photography is clean and consistent — no broken images, no layout breaks.
- "ONLY AT SEPHORA" and "Clean at Sephora" badges give a couple of products a differentiator.
- Beauty Insider tier bar (Insider/VIB/Rouge) at the footer reinforces loyalty segmentation, which matches a member persona.

## What's weak

- Hero headline "The Drop-Everything Edit" is pure vibes copy with no offer, no product name, no reason to scroll.
- No unifying theme to the product grid — perfume, eye cream, hair serum, shampoo, hand sanitizer, and anti-aging serum all mixed with no category grouping.
- Three competing offers (trial size, $5 Beauty Insider Cash, store events) fragment attention instead of one clear CTA.
- This is a near-identical structure to the Aug 11 "winning" trend grid and Aug 8 "new arrivals" grid — third time in a week readers see this same shape.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one organizing idea and cut the grid to match it — e.g., "This Week's Cult Restocks" with 4 products max, not 8 unrelated categories. Specificity beats volume.
- 2. Lead the hero with the trial-size offer instead of burying it above a content-free script headline — it's the only concrete deal in the email.
- 3. Consolidate to one CTA per send; move the $5 Beauty Insider Cash and store-events banners to a separate loyalty-focused email so they don't compete.
- 4. Replace the cursive "Drop-Everything Edit" headline with a copy line that names an actual benefit or product category.
