# These picks are everywhere
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-11**
## Executive summary

- After the punishing seven-send Stacking Weekend sprint (July 1–7), Sephora is correctly in editorial cruise control. "These picks are everywhere" is a social proof play — trending product content structured around a "Hot on Social" hero, a Patrick Starrr / One Size new-launch feature, a rhode new-arrivals section, and a 4X points loyalty sweetener. The email is clean, well-rendered, and appropriately light on hard-sell energy. Sephora earns the right to send this kind of editorial content after training subscribers on big discount events.
- The problem is the subject line. "These picks are everywhere" is curiosity bait with zero specificity — no product named, no offer hinted, no category called out. The July 3 Stacking Weekend email scored 9/10 partly because the subject front-loaded every offer detail. This one does the opposite. A subscriber skimming 40 emails at 8am has no reason to prioritize this over anything else in the pile.
- Verdict: a well-executed editorial send let down by the inbox entry point. The body delivers; the door doesn't open itself.

## What's working

- **"Hot on Social" editorial framing is timely** — Hailey Bieber's rhode brand and Patrick Starrr / One Size are exactly the kind of social-media-saturated picks the subject promises, and both are genuinely trending.
- **Patrick Starrr / One Size new-launch feature is prominent** — a named creator with a specific product gives the mid-section a concrete anchor most editorial emails lack.
- **4X Points loyalty sweetener** — visible mid-email, gives Beauty Insiders a mechanical reason to click even without a hard discount.
- **Clean, render-bug-free layout** — the visual stack from hero → product grid → bundles → rhode is crisp with no broken images or overlapping text.
- **Cadence is correct** — four days after the July 8 editorial reset, Sephora is not burning subscribers with a fifth identical promo.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is the weakest in the recent run** — "These picks are everywhere" is vague; compared to the July 3 "Ends 7/7: 30% off ALL Sephora Collection + 2X points + FREE Same-Day Delivery 📣" (9/10), there's no hook, no product, no offer, no urgency.
- **"Get a Mini" top-bar competes with the hero** — the promotional ribbon above HOT ON SOCIAL creates two competing entry points; the eye has to pick a starting point.
- **No personalization visible** — the July 5 email called out "Rosie" by name; this one has nothing, which is a missed opportunity on an editorial send where relevance drives clicks.
- **CTAs are generic** — "SHOP NOW" repeated across every section; even one section-specific label like "Shop the TikTok Picks" or "Shop rhode Arrivals" would have lifted click intent.
- **No offer feels concrete above the fold** — the 4X points promotion is buried mid-scroll; a subscriber opening expecting a deal will feel let down before they reach it.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject to name a product or creator** — "Patrick Starrr's new drop is everywhere" or "rhode's newest arrivals are already selling out" are more clickable than the current abstract social-proof hook.
- 2. **Move the 4X Points callout into the preview text or subject parenthetical** — it's the strongest mechanical reason to click and it's buried below the fold.
- 3. **Disambiguate the top-bar** — the "Get a Mini" ribbon and the HOT ON SOCIAL hero both compete for attention at open. Either consolidate the mini offer into the hero or demote it to footer.
- 4. **Make section CTAs specific** — "Shop Hot On Social," "Shop One Size," "Shop rhode New Arrivals" over repeating "SHOP NOW" everywhere.
- 5. **Add first-name personalization to the subject or preview** — given that the July 5 email demonstrated Sephora has the data to use "Rosie," skipping it here is a waste.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**WCAG 1.4.4 violation — zoom disabled:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` prevents pinch-to-zoom on mobile. This blocks users with low vision from enlarging text and fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text). Recommend removing `maximum-scale=1` or using `maximum-scale=5`.

**OS text-size-adjust suppressed globally:**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  -moz-text-size-adjust: none;
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```
Applied to 9 element types including `body`. Disabling text-size-adjust at `body` level overrides user accessibility preferences on iOS/Android. Standard practice is `-webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%` (preserve layout without blocking user zoom), not `none`.

**Duplicate CSS rule block:**
The `.appear` responsive rule is defined twice with identical declarations — lines immediately following `.break`:
```css
*[class=appear], .appear { display: block!important; display: table!important; }
/* ... then again ... */
*[class=appear], .appear { display: block!important; display: table!important; }
```
No functional impact but adds template byte weight; indicates a copy-paste error in the responsive CSS block.

**Large mobile padding values (verify against images):**
`padc6`: `padding: 19px 0px 148px 0px`, `padc8`: `padding: 15px 0px 159px 0px`, `padc10`: `padding: 188px 0px 0px 0px` — these are unusually large and often indicate image-height workarounds. If images fail to load, these paddings will produce large blank gaps. Confirm each is paired with an image that fills the space.
