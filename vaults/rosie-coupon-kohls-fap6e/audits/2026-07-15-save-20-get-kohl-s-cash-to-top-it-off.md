# Save 20%  + get Kohl's Cash to top it off  🤑
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- This is Kohl's twentieth-ish promotional email in fifteen days, and it's a structural clone of every send since July 1: 20% off banner at the top, clearance callout, lifestyle hero with a budget-friendly tagline, product grid, home-goods section, and a barcode coupon in the footer. The one genuine addition is the "up to 70% off" clearance headline — that's a harder, more credible number than the standard 20%, and it lands above the fold. The "beach day for your budget" hero copy is breezy and season-appropriate, a notch fresher than last week's "find must-have looks for a stylish summer."
- But the email can't escape its own cadence. The "today only" urgency on the 20% coupon has been undermined by fourteen consecutive days of that exact same offer. A subscriber who's been watching this inbox knows the discount will still be there tomorrow. The structure works on paper; the problem is that the audience has been trained to tune it out.
- Net verdict: competent template execution, marginally differentiated creative, fundamentally suppressed by fatigue. The clearance hook was the right instinct — it just got buried under the same old frame.

## What's working

- **Clearance callout is the strongest creative choice in weeks** — "NEW MARKDOWNS TAKEN · Save up to 70%" with its own "Shop Now" button is a harder offer than the ubiquitous 20%, and it appears high in the scroll.
- **"Like a beach day for your budget!" hero copy** is punchy and seasonal — better than generic lifestyle headers from recent sends.
- **Price anchors are visible in the product tiles** — $7.00 and $29.99 with "was $X" crossouts give the wallet math upfront, no click required.
- **Kohl's Cash is front-and-center in both the subject line and body** — the dual mention reinforces loyalty value without feeling repetitive.
- **No render bugs** — layout holds cleanly across sections, barcode coupon renders legibly.

## What's weak

- **"Today only!" urgency is a credibility hole** — this is the nineteenth-plus consecutive send with a same-day 20% coupon; the subscriber knows it resets daily.
- **Clearance is undersold** — 70% off deserved the hero slot, not the women's lifestyle image; instead it's a thin band below the nav.
- **CTA copy is all-generic "Shop Now"** — no single link connects to a specific category or product, so intent-driven clicks require the subscriber to self-navigate.
- **Home section is a late-scroll afterthought** — the home-goods grid (Bedding, Bath, Kitchen, Patio, Decor, Outdoor Toys) appears well below the fold, which kills its reach on mobile.
- **No personalization signal anywhere** — no first name, no "because you shop X" framing, nothing that distinguishes this from a broadcast blast.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Swap the hero** — put the 70% clearance image and number in the hero position. The savings magnitude is the most differentiated signal in this send; burying it under a lifestyle photo wastes it. The 20% coupon banner can stay at the very top as a persistent nudge.
- 2. **Kill "today only" or make it real** — after two weeks of daily resets, this phrasing is actively eroding credibility. Replace with "valid through [specific date]" or drop the urgency frame entirely until it's actually true.
- 3. **Specifify the CTAs** — "Shop Clearance," "Shop Sandals," "Shop Bedding" each cost zero characters more than "Shop Now" and give the scan reader a reason to click.
- 4. **Surface the home section earlier** — given the July home-refresh message from the July 14 send, a "refresh your space before summer ends" placement mid-scroll (not bottom) would hold subscribers who aren't apparel buyers.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 70% off in clearance (+ 20% on top)`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Clearance just dropped to 70% off — grab it today`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New markdowns added. Your coupon is already in your wallet.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Beach-worthy deals start at $7 — clearance shelves just got deeper.`

## 1. Overview

### 1. Technical Summary

Structurally standard retail ESP email (HTML4 Transitional, MSO conditionals, table-based layout) with no broken links detected. Primary concerns are relay-layer header gaps masking compliance signals, and pervasive missing alt text on tracking and content images.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

- **22 click-redirect links skipped** — all route through `click.s.kohls.com` and `click.chp.kohls.com`. HTTP probe was not attempted; broken destination URLs cannot be confirmed or ruled out. Destination integrity must be verified directly against Kohl's ESP (Coherent Path / Salesforce Marketing Cloud) click reports.
- **Three distinct pixel/tracking domains in use:** `click.chp.kohls.com` (Coherent Path open pixel), `mi.kohls.com` (MI3 platform), `kohls.demdex.net` (Adobe Audience Manager). No technical issue, but the fingerprint surface is high — aggressive privacy filters (iOS Mail Privacy Protection, uBlock) will suppress all three.
- **Malformed closing tag:** `</custom>` appears in the open-pixel `<div>` block (`<img ...><custom>`). This is an unclosed/invalid element that could cause parse tree issues in strict renderers.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

- **11 images missing `alt` text** — confirmed by QA. Includes:
  - Open pixel: `click.chp.kohls.com/o/904e2e5d...` — alt="" acceptable for tracking pixels but absent entirely
  - `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/2a5adc5afb647aad.png` through `916d6fa08d185233.png` — these appear to be content/product images based on the `email_name=260715_DG_Email_Wednesd` parameter; missing alt text on content images fails WCAG 2.1 AA (SC 1.1.1) and renders as blank in image-blocked clients
  - `kohls.demdex.net/event?d_sid=13245196` — tracking pixel, alt="" or `aria-hidden="true"` should be explicit (the `mi.kohls.com/p/up/88954bbbbcab3c0e/o.gif` pixel does correctly use `aria-hidden="true"` — others do not)
- **`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta** — disables pinch-zoom on mobile; this is a WCAG 2.1 AA failure (SC 1.4.4). Common in retail email but worth flagging.
- **`<title>` is empty** — `<title></title>` means screen readers announce no document title. Should be set to the subject line or a descriptive string.
- **Font loading:** Google Fonts loaded via both `<link>` and `@import` for the same two families (Roboto, Montserrat). Duplicate requests add latency; the `<link>` tags are inside `<!--[if !mso]><!--> ... <![endif]-->` guards, so the `@import` in the unguarded `<style>` block is redundant.
