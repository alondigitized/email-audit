# Gear up for a great season with great prices 🏈🏆
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-01**
## Executive summary

- This is a genuine departure from the Kohl's Friends & Family fatigue we've been tracking — instead of another 20%-off coupon reskin, it leads with an "85% off clearance" banner then pivots into a fully realized sports-licensing hero: NFL, NCAA, and MLB team gear (Bears, Eagles, Buckeyes/Wolverines, Yankees, Dodgers) staged on turf-green backgrounds under "Every great season starts with great gear." It's a smart seasonal hook for early September, but the email tries to do three unrelated things at once — clearance, sports gear, Sephora beauty deals — and none of them get to breathe.
- The sports-gear block is the actual hero and it's well executed: real jerseys, real team logos, clear category CTAs (Shop NFL/NCAA/MLB). But it's buried below a clearance banner and four nav buttons, and then immediately followed by a jarring Sephora 50%-off insert and a grab-bag "picked for you" grid (a drink maker, bath towels, jeans, a mattress topper) that has nothing to do with sports or clearance. The subject line undersells what's actually the best part of the email.
- Judgment: a solid creative concept dragged down by scope-creep — this should have been a standalone sports-gear send, not three campaigns stapled together.

## What's working

- The sports-gear hero is genuinely seasonal and timely (September = NFL/NCAA kickoff) with real jerseys and licensed team logos, not stock photography.
- Three clear category CTAs (Shop NFL, Shop NCAA, Shop MLB) give an unambiguous next step for a sports shopper.
- The clearance banner leads with a real number (85%, "after an extra 50% off") — no vague "up to X%" bait.
- No render bugs — images, buttons, and text all display cleanly with good contrast.

## What's weak

- The subject ("Gear up for a great season with great prices 🏈🏆") undersells the actual hero content and reads generically — could describe almost any Kohl's send.
- The email crams three unrelated offers (clearance, sports gear, Sephora beauty) into one send with no visual or narrative transition between them.
- The "picked with you in mind" grid (drink maker, towels, jeans, mattress topper) is a random assortment that dilutes the sports theme entirely and feels like filler.
- No sport-specific discount is called out — the hero shows jerseys/hats but no price or % off, so the "great prices" subject promise isn't delivered in the hero itself.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split this into a standalone sports-gear send — lead with team jerseys and a real price/discount on them, drop the Sephora block and the random grid entirely so the whole email reinforces one idea.
- 2. Add a visible price or discount to the NFL/NCAA/MLB hero images (e.g., "Jerseys from $29.99") — right now the sports section shows product but no reason to buy now.
- 3. Move personalization to the top — if this subscriber has browsed a specific team/sport before, lead with that team's gear instead of a generic three-league grid.
- 4. Cut the Sephora insert from this send — it belongs in its own beauty-focused email; here it just interrupts the sports narrative.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your team's gear is back — shop NFL, NCAA & MLB`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Jerseys, hats & more for game day 🏈`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New team gear just landed for football season`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop NFL, NCAA & MLB gear before kickoff`
