# New beauty to obsess over 😍
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-25**
## Executive summary

- This is a straight product-catalog send disguised as "new beauty to obsess over" — and it delivers exactly what the subject promises: a long, tile-by-tile scroll of new launches (Libre Vanille, Dior primer, Innisfree, M∙A∙C, Oribe, Lys Beauty, Sol de Janeiro, Unite Hair, Eadem). Subject and top banner actually match for once, which is a rarity for this brand's recent cadence — but the top banner isn't even the "new beauty" hook, it's a generic "Get 4X Points on All Korean Beauty" + "Up to 50% off Hair Faves" stack competing for attention before the stated theme even starts.
- The layout is a wall of near-identical colored tiles with almost no visual hierarchy — nothing tells the eye where to land first, and by the fifth or sixth "NEW · ONLY AT SEPHORA" tile it reads as fatigue rather than discovery. There's no single hero offer, no urgency, and no differentiation between "spend money because it's new" and "spend money because it's discounted" — those two messages get smashed together at the top and then abandoned.
- Verdict: on-brand execution of a low-effort format. It'll perform fine with beauty loyalists who scroll everything, but it will not convert casual openers — there's too much to parse and no clear single action.

## What's working

- Subject "New beauty to obsess over 😍" is honestly represented by the body — mostly new-launch tiles, not bait-and-switch like several of this brand's recent sends (see 2026-09-18, 2026-09-19).
- "CLEAN AT SEPHORA" badges are visible and legible on relevant products (Obart, Dior, Innisfree, Lys Beauty), giving ingredient-conscious shoppers a real signal.
- Each tile has a distinct, clean product photo — no broken images, no rendering glitches, no lorem ipsum.
- "NEW · ONLY AT SEPHORA" / "NEW · LIMITED EDITION" labels are consistent and give scannable exclusivity cues throughout.

## What's weak

- No single hero offer or CTA — it's 10+ stacked product tiles with no visual priority, so nothing "pops" on open or on a quick scroll.
- The top banner ("Get 4X Points on Korean Beauty" / "Up to 50% off Hair Faves") contradicts the "new beauty" subject line before the actual new-launch content even starts — two competing offers in the first screen.
- Pricing is inconsistent tile-to-tile (some show price, some show "$44+", some show none at all like Innisfree and M∙A∙C), making it hard to judge value at a glance.
- No sense of urgency or deadline anywhere in the body — "Today Only" appears only in the banner, disconnected from the new-launch narrative.
- Extremely long single-column scroll with repetitive colored-background tiles creates fatigue; nothing breaks the pattern until the "New and Iconic EADEM" and "Beauty Insider" modules near the bottom.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick ONE hero launch (Libre Vanille & Santal Couture is the obvious choice — it's already first) and give it a proper full-width hero treatment with a single clear CTA, then demote the rest to a "More new arrivals" grid below the fold. Right now everything has equal visual weight, so nothing wins.
- 2. Cut the top banner's competing offer or align it — either make this email about the Korean Beauty/points promo, or about new beauty, not both fighting in the first screen.
- 3. Standardize pricing display across every tile — a shopper comparing $44 Innisfree to $155 Dior needs that up front, not inconsistently shown.
- 4. Add a light urgency element tied to the new-launch angle itself (e.g., "First 48 hours" or "While supplies last") rather than none at all.
- 5. Break the visual monotony — alternate tile widths or introduce a 2-up grid partway through so it doesn't read as one endless scroll of identical blocks.
