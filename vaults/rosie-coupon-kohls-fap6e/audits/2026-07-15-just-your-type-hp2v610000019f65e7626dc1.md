# 💘JUST YOUR TYPE 💘
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-15**
## Executive summary

- "Just Your Type" is Sephora's strongest editorial concept since the July 11 "These picks are everywhere" send — a personality-matching frame for fragrance discovery, anchored by the new Jo Malone London Sea Salt & Bergamot Cologne. The pun works. The heart emojis are charming if slightly Valentine's-coded for mid-July. And the mini deal (Buy 2 Minis, Get Both 50% Off) loaded above the fold gives the editorial send actual commercial muscle. On concept alone, this is a step above the generic July 13 "New products have arrived" — which had no coherent theme to speak of.
- The problem is that the editorial discipline collapses mid-scroll. An email that promises to tell you "your type" of scent gets interrupted by a Leave-In Conditioners module and a ONE/SIZE by Patrick Starr makeup block. The fragrance thesis, which was working, dissolves into a category dump. Every CTA is the same generic "SHOP NOW" — there's no thread connecting the concept to the individual offers the way good editorial does. The July 11 send stayed lane-disciplined on social-proof beauty picks and earned it. This one doesn't.
- Verdict: Sephora has a better idea here than most of their recent sends, but they don't trust it enough to execute it cleanly. Fix the module stack, sharpen the CTAs, and this scores 9+.

## What's working

- **Strong hero concept** — "This is for YOU" with the "Just Your Type" framing sets up a personalization premise that draws the eye immediately.
- **Promotional hook above the fold** — the "Buy 2 Minis, Get Both 50% Off" banner is loaded at the very top, so there's a real offer even if you don't scroll.
- **Jo Malone London anchors the fragrance theme credibly** — new product badge + price + full name gives the featured product real weight.
- **"By Maria Jane / Joy of Perfume" adds editorial voice** — influencer sourcing lends authority to the fragrance curation, consistent with the personality-matching concept.
- **Beauty Insider 2X Points visible** — loyalty members get a clear benefit in-email; this rewards the segment without requiring a separate send.

## What's weak

- **Leave-In Conditioners module breaks the fragrance thesis entirely** — nothing in "Just Your Type" set up hair care; this looks like a slot sold to a separate category team.
- **Heart emojis read Valentine's Day, not July** — 💘 twice is charming but seasonally dissonant; summer fragrance discovery deserves warmer visual cues.
- **Every CTA is the same generic "SHOP NOW"** — the concept promised personality matching; every button should be doing something to reinforce that (e.g., "Find Your Scent," "Shop Sea Salt & Bergamot").
- **No urgency mechanism on the mini deal** — the 50% off offer floats with no deadline, which reduces the purchase impulse the banner creates.
- **ONE/SIZE by Patrick Starr at the bottom** — a makeup editorial module after fragrance + hair care fully breaks any single-theme journey. This is three emails in one.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the Leave-In Conditioners and ONE/SIZE modules entirely.** Stay fragrance all the way down. If a separate hair or makeup send is needed, send it separately. The July 11 "These picks are everywhere" scored 8 because it didn't try to be everything. Same lesson applies here.
- 2. **Replace all "SHOP NOW" buttons with concept-reinforcing copy.** On the Jo Malone hero: "Shop Sea Salt & Bergamot." On the quiz-ish fragrance grid: "Find Your Type." On the mini deal: "Build Your Duo." These make the concept felt rather than stated.
- 3. **Add a soft deadline to the mini deal.** "Offer ends Sunday" in the banner costs nothing and makes the 50% off pop harder. The Stacking Weekend final-day push (July 7) had clear deadline language and it worked.
- 4. **Swap the double heart emojis for something more summer-coded.** 🌸 or ✨ or even plain text. 💘💘 in July generates a mild cognitive dissonance that doesn't help the open rate.
- 5. **Make the "Just Your Type" concept interactive.** A "take the quiz" CTA that resolves to a landing page with a scent-type filter would actually fulfill the promise of the subject line. Right now the concept is stated but not delivered.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`robots` meta value has a space:**
```html
<meta name="robots" content="no index" />
```
Correct value is `"noindex"` (no space). Robots don't crawl email, so this has no practical effect, but it's a malformed token.

**`<title>` is generic:**
```html
<title>Sephora</title>
```
Some clients (notably desktop Outlook, older Thunderbird) display the `<title>` as the window/tab label. Using the subject line or a descriptive variant is preferred.

**No `lang` attribute on `<html>`:**
```html
<html xmlns="..." xmlns:v="..." xmlns:o="...">
```
Missing `lang="en"` (or appropriate locale). Screen readers default to OS language when absent, which can produce incorrect pronunciation for non-English subscribers.

**`maximum-scale=1` in viewport:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents user zoom — a known accessibility barrier. Low client-side enforcement risk in email, but flagged per WCAG 1.4.4.
