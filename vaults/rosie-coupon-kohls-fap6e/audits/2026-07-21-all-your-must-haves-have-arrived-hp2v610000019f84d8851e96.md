# All your must-haves have arrived
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- This is Sephora's new-arrivals editorial roundup — a "what just dropped" format stacking seven brand spotlights (Hung Vanngo Beauty, Jo Malone London, Crown Affair, Touchland, Note by Mary Phillips, Innisfree, and others) under the banner "Just Dropped & Only Here." The concept is sound: Sephora exclusives are a legitimate open hook, and most of the brands here have real cultural cachet among Insider subscribers. But seven equal-weight spotlights means no single product gets its moment; the email reads like a catalog index more than a curated editorial.
- Compare this to the July 18 Grande Cosmetics launch (8/10), where one celebrity anchor gave the email a clear center of gravity. Here that ambition has diffused into a content dump. The "Just Dropped & Only Here" hero is doing real work, but the subject line "All your must-haves have arrived" doesn't carry that energy — it's generic retail language that could come from any sender on any given Tuesday.
- No discount, no deadline, no social proof. The entire value proposition is discovery, which can work, but only if the editorial voice is sharp and the curation ruthless. This email is neither.

## What's working

- **Exclusivity signal in hero**: "Just Dropped & Only Here" is a genuine differentiator — Sephora-only drops are real news for a loyal Insider.
- **Brand selection has credibility**: Hung Vanngo (pro MUA crossover debut), Jo Malone (prestige fragrance), Crown Affair (cult hair care), Innisfree (K-beauty) — these names land for an engaged subscriber.
- **4X loyalty callout**: The 4X points element ties the new arrivals to the ongoing loyalty promotion from the July 19 send, giving members an action reason beyond passive browsing.
- **Clean, brand-specific photography**: Each module has its own product imagery and visual identity; no broken images or layout collapse across the full scroll.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is generic retail filler**: "All your must-haves have arrived" names no brand, no category, no offer — it could come from any retailer about anything.
- **No single hero product**: Seven equal-weight spotlights mean the eye has nowhere to land after the opener. No product wins, so no click intent forms.
- **Zero price or offer signal**: No discount, no GWP, no free-shipping threshold — the email asks for a click with nothing in return but "new things exist."
- **Visual hierarchy collapses after the hero banner**: Post "Just Dropped," the layout becomes a stack of identically structured brand modules with no escalating CTA and no editorial throughline to hold the email together.
- **No urgency**: "Just Dropped" implies recency but sets no deadline. If the 4X points window is still open, this email should be weaponizing that clock — it isn't.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one brand as anchor, surface the rest as "and more"**: Hung Vanngo Beauty is the most newsworthy drop here — a pro MUA brand's Sephora debut is an event. Lead with that, then two others. Seven equals is seven zeros.
- 2. **Rewrite the subject to name a brand or create real exclusivity FOMO**:
- **Subject Alt A:** `Hung Vanngo Beauty just dropped — Sephora exclusive`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New exclusives: Hung Vanngo, Crown Affair + 5 more`
- 3. **Surface a 4X deadline in the body copy**: If the points promo is live, the email should read "earn 4X on these new drops through [date]." That transforms a passive browse into a timed action.
- 4. **Rewrite the preheader to add what the subject omits**:
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New Sephora exclusives — earn 4X points on today's drops`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `7 new arrivals. Only at Sephora. Insiders have them first.`
- 5. **Tag Sephora-exclusive products with a badge in-module**: "Only Here" in the hero should be reinforced at the product level so the exclusivity cue follows the reader down the scroll.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[CONFIRMED] Pinch-to-zoom disabled — WCAG 1.4.4 violation**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

`maximum-scale=1` prevents users from zooming in on mobile. This is a Level AA failure (Resize Text). Fix: remove `maximum-scale=1` or replace with `maximum-scale=5`.

**[CONFIRMED] Broad `text-size-adjust: none` — WCAG 1.4.4 concern**

```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  -moz-text-size-adjust: none;
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```

Applied to virtually every element. This suppresses OS-level text scaling for users with low vision. The Outlook-specific `-ms-text-size-adjust: none` on `td` is a known workaround, but the wholesale application to `body`, `div`, `a`, and `span` is overly aggressive. Scope it to `td` and `table` only.

**[MINOR] Duplicate media-query rule blocks**

The `.appear` class rule is declared twice identically within `@media only screen and (max-width: 480px)`, adding unnecessary CSS weight (no functional impact, but signals template hygiene issues).
