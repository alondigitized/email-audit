# JUST IN: Kohl's Deal Days + Free Shipping + Daily Deals 🤗
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-23**
## Executive summary

- This is Kohl's event-launch email for "Deal Days" — a six-day daily-deals format dropping on June 23, the day after the unmemorable "Summer is looking stylish (and super affordable) 😎" send that drew a 7/10 for offer vagueness. The strategic intent is smart: reframe the cadence around a named event so daily promos feel like a series to follow rather than inbox clutter to delete. The execution is messier. The email is a dense gallery of seven-plus product tiles — $4.99 tops, 50% off bedding, a $15.99 Crock-Pot, BALI intimates at $21.99, and a 25% off teaser at the bottom — each competing for the same eyeball. Nothing in the layout says "start here." The Deal Days banner leads, then the grid fractures.
- The subject line is the most damaging single choice: "JUST IN: Kohl's Deal Days + Free Shipping + Daily Deals 🤗" is three separate value props bolted together with plus signs. "JUST IN" is the right instinct — it positions this as news — but the rest undercuts it by trying to sell the event, the perk, and the mechanic simultaneously. Pick one. "Free shipping" buried in the middle of a 57-character subject is invisible on mobile.
- Net judgment: a solid concept (event frame, time-bound daily deals, specific dollar prices) hampered by a scattered subject and a gallery layout that turns the email into a department-store flyer. Better than the June 22 soft-launch rebound, not as sharp as the 40%-off finishers from June 21.

## What's working

- **"Deal Days" event framing is a genuine reset.** After the Father's Day marathon, naming this as a structured six-day event gives subscribers a reason to keep opening — there's a new deal each day.
- **Specific price points throughout.** $4.99, $2.99, $15.99, $3.99, $21.99 — these are honest, concrete numbers that build trust and click intent more than percentage-only banners.
- **"6 Days of Deals" section header creates narrative continuity.** It signals this isn't a one-and-done blast, which seeds future-open habit.
- **Free shipping is in the subject.** It's buried, but it's there — and it's a real transactional lever for borderline buyers.
- **"Shop Now" CTAs are present throughout.** Even in a gallery layout, every product tile appears to have an action path.

## What's weak

- **The hero fractures immediately.** The Deal Days banner makes a strong opening, then the layout immediately atomizes into seven competing product tiles with no visual through-line or anchor hierarchy.
- **Subject tries to carry three messages.** "Kohl's Deal Days + Free Shipping + Daily Deals" is redundant (Deal Days IS daily deals) and bloated at 57 characters — it likely truncates to "JUST IN: Kohl's Deal Days + Free Sh…" on most mobile clients.
- **No urgency signal on the hero.** "Today's Featured Daily Deals" implies rotation but never tells the subscriber when today's deal expires. The best daily-deal emails put a countdown or "today only" in the hero — this doesn't.
- **BALI intimates and kitchen appliances in the same scroll.** The category mix (women's apparel, cookware, bedding, intimates) signals poor segmentation; nothing here says this subscriber's deal was curated for them.
- **"25% off" close at the bottom is orphaned.** It appears as a standalone offer panel without clear context — 25% off what, under what conditions — which makes it feel like a catch-all fallback rather than a closing hook.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with ONE deal in the hero, not seven.** Pick the sharpest price ($2.99 towels or $4.99 top) and make it the single visual anchor above the fold. Then let the gallery follow. This is what the strongest daily-deal emails do — one knockout lead, then the depth.
- 2. **Add an expiry clock or "today only" label to every Day 1 deal.** "Today's Featured Daily Deals" is soft. "Ends at midnight" is a click trigger.
- 3. **Cut the subject to one hook and let the preheader carry the second.** "JUST IN: Day 1 of Deal Days — deals this good don't last" uses the event narrative and implies scarcity without packing three clauses in.
- 4. **Segment the gallery by category.** A subscriber who bought women's apparel last shouldn't be scrolling past Crock-Pots. Even a light personalization pass (lead with your highest affinity category) would lift CTR materially.
- 5. **Anchor the 25% off bottom panel.** Give it a condition ("on almost everything" or "Kohl's Cash members") and a tighter CTA than "Shop Now."
- **Subject Alt A:** `Day 1 of Deal Days: $2.99 towels, $4.99 tops + free shipping`
- **Subject Alt B:** `JUST IN: Deal Days starts now — today's picks won't last`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Six days of daily deals kick off today. Here's what's dropping first.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Free shipping + steals from $2.99. Day 1 is live — tomorrow's deals are different.`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a standard ESP-rendered promotional send using CoherentPath (chp.kohls.com) and Movable Ink (mi.kohls.com) for dynamic content and tracking. No broken links or hard rendering failures detected, but compliance headers are absent from the captured relay copy and accessibility coverage is poor across tracking pixels and content images.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**22 click-redirect links skipped** — all routed through `click.s.kohls.com` and `click.chp.kohls.com`. HTTP probe was not performed; destination URLs and redirect chains are unverified.

**Three distinct tracking pixel domains** observed in the `<body>` before visible content begins:
- `click.chp.kohls.com/o/...` — CoherentPath open pixel
- `mi.kohls.com/p/up/...` — Movable Ink open pixel
- `click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?...` — ESP open pixel (likely Salesforce Marketing Cloud)
- `kohls.demdex.net/event?d_sid=13245196` — Adobe Audience Manager sync pixel

Three pixels for a single open event is redundant and adds load latency, though this is not a blocking issue.

**Malformed tag detected:**
```html
<img src="https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?..." width="1" height="1" alt="">
<custom>
```
A stray `<custom>` closing tag appears immediately after the ESP open pixel `<img>`. This is invalid HTML and may cause parsing errors in strict clients.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**11 images missing `alt` text** — confirmed by QA. Affected images include what appear to be content/product images (not just tracking pixels), based on filenames like `2a5adc5afb647aad.png`, `f441d45d2128ae53.png`, etc., served from `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/`. These are likely Movable Ink-rendered dynamic content blocks.

Tracking pixels (`o.gif`, `event`, CoherentPath open pixel) lack `alt=""` as well. The Movable Ink pixel does have `aria-hidden="true"` correctly set — the others do not.

**`user-scalable=0`** is set in the viewport meta tag:
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0" />
```
This prevents pinch-to-zoom, which fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and is increasingly blocked by mobile browsers.

**`<title>` tag is empty** — `<title></title>` — reduces accessibility for screen readers and may affect tab/preview rendering in some clients.

**Google Fonts loaded three times** (two `<link>` tags + two `@import` statements for the same families). Redundant, adds HTTP overhead.
