# NEW SUNSCREEN 💦 ☀️ 🏖️
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-24**
## Executive summary

- This is a new-arrivals dump wearing a sunscreen costume. The subject line promises "NEW SUNSCREEN" and earns the open, but the email immediately pivots to a generic "SOMETHING NEW IS HERE" hero that could describe any given Tuesday at Sephora. The dedicated sunscreen section does exist — Clean Whip SPF 30, $34, is cleanly presented — but the email then meanders through Summer Fridays products, a "Visible" brand feature, an auto-replenish hot deal, and a "For Dry Hair" section that has no business being in a sunscreen send.
- The seasonal palette (yellow, summery photography) is on-brand and the product photography is clean. But Sephora's editorial sprawl problem is fully visible here: instead of a tight, product-led sunscreen launch moment, subscribers get a catch-all that dilutes the sunscreen hook the subject created.
- The cadence is also starting to show wear. Comparing to recent history — July 22 was "4X points on hair care," July 23 was "Summer Juice Bar" fragrance editorial — and today's send drops a dry hair section into a sunscreen email. The themes aren't stacking into a coherent season; they're just rotating widgets.

## What's working

- Dedicated "NEW SUNSCREEN" module appears early in the scroll with the product name, SPF spec, and price — a subscriber who opens for that specific reason finds it fast.
- Yellow/summery visual palette reinforces the seasonal context without feeling forced.
- Multiple "SHOP NOW" CTAs are consistently placed and clearly styled — no ambiguity about where to click.
- The SPF 30 callout is a concrete product detail that gives a buyer something to evaluate.
- Sephora Beauty Insider branding at the footer provides trusted social anchoring.

## What's weak

- Hero copy ("SOMETHING NEW IS HERE") is generic and breaks continuity with the specific sunscreen subject line — the eye lands on a mismatch.
- "For Dry Hair" section is completely off-theme for a sunscreen send and reads like filler padding the template.
- No promotional hook anywhere above the fold — full prices only, nothing to create a sense of occasion around the launch.
- "SHOP NOW" CTAs throughout are vague; not one links to a specific product category by name.
- ALL CAPS "NEW SUNSCREEN" in the subject reads slightly aggressive, bordering on the spam-signal range.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lock the hero to the subject.** Swap "SOMETHING NEW IS HERE" for copy that closes the loop: "Your clean SPF upgrade just landed." The current disconnect is the single biggest lift opportunity.
- 2. **Cut the "For Dry Hair" section entirely.** Or save it for a dedicated hair send. It signals to subscribers that these emails are just template slots being filled.
- 3. **Add a soft urgency hook to the sunscreen launch.** "New in — limited first run" or a "Be first to review it" CTA costs nothing and creates a reason to click today vs. next week.
- 4. **Make one CTA product-specific.** Even "SHOP CLEAN WHIP SPF 30" over the sunscreen tile would outperform the generic "SHOP NOW."
