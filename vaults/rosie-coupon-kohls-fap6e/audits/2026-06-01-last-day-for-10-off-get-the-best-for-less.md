# LAST DAY for 10% off 🚨 Get the best—for less.
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-01**
## Executive summary

- This is the final expiry-reminder for Rosie's 10% off welcome code SE3ARPH, timed to close out today (6/1/2026). It's the logical endpoint of the May 26 "Starts today! You have 10% off 🎉" activation send — the countdown timer is running a real clock, the hero is unambiguous, and the barcode at the bottom adds a smart omnichannel conversion layer. The email does exactly what a last-day triggered send should: make the closing window unavoidable.
- Execution is clean. The 10% OFF figure is the visual anchor, personalization surfaces in the right place, and the "Wondering Why You Got This?" section adds a transparency touch that reduces unsubscribe pressure. No render issues.
- The one strategic problem is one this render can't fix: this is the second full expiry cycle Rosie has seen for what appears to be the same code. The May 20 "10% off ends soon, Rosie" (scored 9/10) ran the same play on a different deadline. Running two LAST DAY sequences twelve days apart trains Rosie that "LAST DAY" is negotiable — and every future urgency signal from Sephora pays the credibility tax.

## What's working

- Countdown timer at the top converts a generic urgency claim into live, real-time pressure — no fake deadline vibes.
- "Rosie" in the hero copy lands as intimate rather than mechanical; it earns its place without overplaying the personalization.
- 10% OFF is the undeniable visual anchor — nothing else competes with it above the fold.
- Barcode + multi-touchpoint redemption instructions (sephora.com, kohls.com, in-store, Sephora inside Kohl's) meaningfully widen the conversion surface.
- "Wondering Why You Got This?" is a small but effective trust signal for a triggered send to a relatively new subscriber.

## What's weak

- "SHOP NOW" is a generic CTA with no apparent landing destination — a hesitant first-time buyer needs a curated shelf, not the homepage.
- The `**` on "10% OFF" signals exclusions at the hero moment; the fine print only resolves deep in the scroll, which creates a brief moment of doubt at exactly the wrong time.
- "Recommended For You" products render small and generic — they read like boilerplate filler rather than a persuasive push for a subscriber who still hasn't made a first purchase.
- The subject's second clause ("Get the best—for less.") adds zero new information after "LAST DAY for 10% off 🚨" and dilutes the urgency with travel-ad copy.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make the CTA specific.** Swap "SHOP NOW" for "Shop Bestsellers" or "Get My 10% Off" and link to a curated first-purchase shelf — not the homepage. This is the same gap the May 20 expiry send had; closing a code-deadline email without a category hook leaves conversion on the table.
- 2. **Cut the subject line's second half.** "LAST DAY for 10% off 🚨 Get the best—for less." — everything after "🚨" is filler.
- **Subject Alt A:** `LAST DAY: Your 10% off, Rosie 🚨`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Rosie, your code expires tonight 🕛`
- 3. **Rewrite the preheader.** "Here's a discount just for shopping" is soft and tautological for a deadline close.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Code SE3ARPH expires tonight — use it in-store or online.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Don't let SE3ARPH expire. Midnight deadline.`
- 4. **Replace the product grid with a tight "3 bestsellers under $X" block.** A subscriber who hasn't purchased yet is stuck on what to buy. A curated, low-AOV entry-point recommendation converts better than a faceless algorithm grid.
- 5. **Address the code-extension optics.** Two full expiry cycles for code SE3ARPH (May 20 and June 1) quietly teaches Rosie that Sephora deadlines aren't real. If the code was deliberately extended, say so — "We gave you a little more time" — rather than re-running the identical urgency playbook. The honesty pays forward to every future campaign.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue 1 — User zoom disabled (WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 violation)**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
`maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom for low-vision users on mobile. Should be removed or replaced with `user-scalable=yes`.

**Issue 2 — Aggressive text-size-adjust suppression**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  -moz-text-size-adjust: none;
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```
Applied globally across nearly every element. This disables OS-level text scaling for users who rely on it. Should be scoped only to elements with intentionally fixed font sizes (e.g., a specific pixel-locked header `td`), not `body` and `div` broadly.

**Issue 3 — Malformed robots meta value**
```html
<meta name="robots" content="no index" />
```
The value is `no index` (with a space) rather than the correct `noindex`. Most parsers will ignore this entirely. Low severity in email context, but indicates template hygiene issue.
