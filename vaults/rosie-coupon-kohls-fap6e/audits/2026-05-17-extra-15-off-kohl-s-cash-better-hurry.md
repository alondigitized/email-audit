# Extra 15% off + Kohl's Cash? Better hurry 🏃‍♀️‍➡️
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- This is Kohl's sixth email in nine days — the cadence is getting aggressive — and it follows the exact playbook as the May 15 send: a stacked offer (15% off + Kohl's Cash) with a "TODAY ONLY" urgency badge, a kitchen-sink product grid (women's tees, men's shorts, kids' clothing, sandals, a laundry-product module), and a barcode coupon strip at the bottom. The subject tries to punch up urgency with "Better hurry" and a running emoji, which at least gives it a slightly fresher feel than yesterday's summer home-entertaining send.
- The fundamental problem is the same one that's plagued this Kohl's run: there's no editorial throughline. A single scroll passes through graphic tees, Birkenstock-style sandals, a Samsara (or similarly named) fashion module, and a laundry detergent pitch. None of it adds up to a story. For the Kai Stride Athletic persona, the only athletic-adjacent content is a pair of men's shorts and the sandals — and neither is presented with any performance framing.
- The offer is real and the coupon is unambiguous, but the email earns its click only from deal-hunters who already know what they want. It does not surface an irresistible, specific reason to act today.

## What's working

- **Urgency is anchored** — "TODAY ONLY" badge at the top of the email makes the deadline visible without scrolling, and it's reinforced by the subject's "Better hurry."
- **Kohl's Cash mechanic is prominent** — The "$10 Kohl's Cash for every $50 spent" call-out near the footer and the hero banner line ("Plus, earn Kohl's Cash!") give loyalty shoppers a concrete stacking incentive.
- **Multiple discount layers are legible** — The 50% off, extra 20%, and 15% offers each get their own visual stripe; a deal-seeker can scan and understand the stack quickly.
- **Sandals module lands visually** — The starting-at-$24.99 Birkenstock-adjacent sandal section has clean product photography and a clear price anchor.

## What's weak

- **No activewear anywhere** — Yesterday's send explicitly called out "affordable activewear"; today's drops that entirely. For the Kai Stride Athletic persona, there's nothing here that speaks to the core interest.
- **Six category jumps in one scroll** — Women's tees → men's shorts → kids' gear → sandals → fashion brand module → laundry detergent. No narrative through-line; just an inventory dump.
- **"Shop Now" is doing all the heavy lifting** — Every CTA says "Shop Now." None names a category or product. Generic verbing on every button is a lost opportunity.
- **Coupon strip is visually dense** — Three stacked barcodes (50% off, extra 20%, 15%) with overlapping fine print creates friction for anyone trying to understand which applies to what.
- **Cadence fatigue risk is real** — This is the sixth Kohl's send in nine days. The emotional register ("Better hurry," "win-win," "we're in") is beginning to blur.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with activewear if you have it; drop the laundry module entirely.** The detergent section ("Dirt happens. Overpaying doesn't have to.") kills momentum and signals that Kohl's ran out of relevant product. One on-persona module beats four off-persona ones.
- 2. **Name the categories in CTA copy.** "Shop Men's Shorts" or "Shop Sandals Under $25" converts better than five identical "Shop Now" buttons.
- 3. **Consolidate the coupon strip.** Pick the single best offer for this audience segment and make it the only coupon shown. Three barcodes in a row reads as desperation and creates confusion about what actually applies.
- 4. **Add a single urgency timestamp.** "TODAY ONLY — offer ends at midnight CT" is more actionable than "Better hurry." Give shoppers the real clock.
- 5. **Vary the cadence hook.** After five sends in a row using "15% off" as the lead, even subscribers who want the deal are developing blindness to it.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Today only: 15% off + stack your Kohl's Cash 🕐`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Sandals under $25 + extra 15% off — today only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your Kohl's Cash earns faster when you shop today's sale`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stack 15% off + earn $10 Kohl's Cash — offer ends tonight`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Kohl's "Extra 15% off + Kohl's Cash" (2026-05-17)

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally functional but has three compliance gaps (missing one-click unsubscribe headers, unverifiable authentication), a malformed HTML tag, redundant font loading, and a referrer policy that silently suppresses outbound analytics. Alt text is absent on 11 images, including content images.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Tracking pixel stack — 4 separate beacons fired on open:**
| Beacon | Domain | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `click.chp.kohls.com/o/f1765314…` | Cheetah Digital / CHP | Open tracking |
| `mi.kohls.com/p/up/88954bbb…/o.gif` | Marigold (formerly Cheetah) | Open tracking (duplicate) |
| `click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?TDK2V4…` | Kohl's ESP secondary | Open tracking (triplicate) |
| `kohls.demdex.net/event?d_sid=13245196` | Adobe Audience Manager | Audience sync |

Three distinct open-tracking pixels is redundant and increases load for privacy-sensitive clients that pre-fetch images (Apple MPP). Consolidate or document why each is required.

**22 click links not probed** — all wrapped in `click.chp.kohls.com` redirector. Cannot confirm destination URLs, redirect chain depth, or UTM parameter presence without live resolution.

**Stray `</custom>` tag** immediately after the `click.s.kohls.com` open-pixel `<img>`:
```html
<img src="https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?TDK2V4…" width="1" height="1" alt=""></custom>
```
`<custom>` is not a valid HTML element. Some parsers will create an unexpected DOM node; others will silently ignore it. Remove it.

**Empty favicon `href`:**
```html
<link href="" rel="shortcut icon">
```
Triggers a second HTTP request to the document's own URL. Remove the tag.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag** — screen readers announce the window/tab as blank. Should reflect the email subject or sender at minimum.

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta** prevents pinch-to-zoom. This is an accessibility failure (WCAG 1.4.4, Level AA):
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0" />
```

**11 images missing `alt` text** (QA confirmed). Three are tracking pixels and can accept `alt=""` (already correct on the o.gif pixel). The remaining content images — including `2a5adc5afb647aad.png`, `415c8a18fc2950ea.png`, and others served from `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/` — carry no alt attribute at all, meaning screen readers skip them entirely and images-off clients show broken icons.

**Redundant Google Fonts loading** — both `<link>` tags and `@import` rules load the same two families (Roboto, Montserrat), doubling DNS lookups and blocking render:
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Roboto…">  <!-- + -->
<style>@import url("https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Roboto…");</style>
```
Use one method only; `<link>` is preferred for email (avoids blocking CSS parse).

**HTML 4.01 Transitional DOCTYPE** — this triggers quirks mode in some older clients. Modern commercial email uses XHTML 1.0 Transitional or HTML5 `<!DOCTYPE html>`.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**`mi_u=604230016`** appears as a static integer in two Marigold pixel URLs. If this is a hardcoded fallback/default subscriber ID rather than a rendered merge tag, open events will be attributed to a single phantom subscriber. Confirm the value is dynamically substituted per recipient at send time.

**`email_name=260517_DG_Email_Mystery`** — the string `Mystery` in the campaign label (present on five image URLs) is either intentional (A/B test label) or an unfilled template token. Flag with the ESP team.

No other unrendered merge tokens (`{{`, `%%`, `*|`, `[%`) detected in the truncated source.

### 5. Compliance

| Issue | Severity | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| `List-Unsubscribe` header absent | High | QA: "Header may not be captured by AgentMail relay" |
| `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent | High | QA: RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe not detected |
| SPF/DKIM status unknown | Medium | QA: `Authentication-Results` header not found |

**One-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058)** has been enforced by Gmail and Yahoo for bulk senders since February 2024. Its absence risks inbox placement demotion or filtering. The QA note attributes this to the AgentMail relay layer — confirm whether headers are stripped at ingest or genuinely absent in the original message.

**`<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />`** is present in the `<head>`. While appropriate to suppress the email client URL from leaking into landing-page referrer logs, it also strips the HTTP `Referer` header on all outbound clicks. If landing pages use server-side referrer validation for campaign attribution, this will break it silently (see §6).

CAN-SPAM physical address and unsubscribe link are presumably present in the email footer (not visible in the truncated source) — cannot confirm from available data.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**Referrer suppression** (`no-referrer` meta, confirmed above) means GA4/Adobe Analytics landing-page sessions sourced from this email will appear as `(direct)` if UTM parameters are absent from click URLs. Since all 22 links are wrapped in the `click.chp.kohls.com` redirector, the actual destination URLs and their UTM strings cannot be verified without resolving the redirects.

**`mi_ecmp=1012717_2026517`** is present on Marigold image requests — this is the Marigold campaign ID, not a UTM parameter. Confirm that destination URLs appended by the click redirector include `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign` so web analytics can attribute sessions independently of the ESP.
