# Save up to 85% on must-have clearance ➡️
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-31**
## Executive summary

- This is a genuinely different beat for Kohl's after nearly two weeks of Friends & Family 20%-off reskins — "Save up to 85%" clearance leads, and the hero delivers a real number (85% after an extra 50% off) instead of another coupon-and-barcode close. But it's also a Frankenstein send: clearance hero, then a full Denim Deal Days section, then a Levi's 30%-off callout, then a Denim Guide banner, then a Halloween/pumpkin category grid, then four unrelated "picked for you" products (a $349 slushi maker, bath towels, jeans, a mattress topper). Four distinct campaigns are stapled together with no throughline.
- The clearance hero itself is strong — bold typography, one clear "Shop Now" CTA, honest fine print ("online price includes applied discount"). But by the time a reader scrolls past three more sub-campaigns to the personalized product block, the 85%-off promise is a distant memory. This reads like a merchandising committee got everyone's section into one email rather than a curated send. Grade: solid hero, undermined by scope creep.

## What's working

- The 85%-off clearance hero has real hierarchy: bold "CLEARANCE" headline, specific discount math, single unambiguous "Shop Now" button.
- "Online price includes applied discount" in the hero is an honest, friction-reducing disclosure — no bait-and-switch feel.
- The Levi's 30%-off denim block has a clean, recognizable brand lockup and a specific offer.
- The "picked with you in mind" section shows real prices and product photography (not lorem-ipsum, no broken images).
- No visible render bugs — every image loads, no overlapping text, layout is clean top to bottom.

## What's weak

- Five separate mini-campaigns (clearance, Denim Deal Days, Levi's, Denim Guide, Halloween/category grid, personalized picks) are jammed into one email — no single message survives to the bottom.
- The subject's "85% off clearance" promise gets buried after just one screen; the rest of the email pivots to full-price denim and unrelated home goods.
- "Picked with you in mind" recommendations feel random — a $349 slushi machine, bath towels, jeans, and a mattress topper share no logic a subscriber can see.
- The Halloween pumpkin category tiles feel like a seasonal tack-on with no connection to clearance or denim.
- This is now Kohl's second multi-week campaign-stacking pattern in a row (see 2026-08-29's three-sends-in-one), suggesting a template habit rather than a one-off.

## Recommendations

- 1. Split this into two sends: a clearance-only email (hero + category grid + fine print) and a separate denim-launch email (Denim Deal Days + Levi's + Denim Guide). Combining them dilutes the 85%-off hook that earned the open.
- 2. Cut the Halloween pumpkin tiles entirely or move them to their own seasonal send — they compete with, rather than support, the clearance message.
- 3. Replace the generic "picked with you in mind" grid with actual clearance-priced items so the email closes on the same offer it opened with.
- 4. Move the Levi's 30%-off block higher if denim must stay in this email — right now it's the strongest single offer after the hero but it's buried mid-scroll.

- **Subject Alt B:** `85% off clearance is here (today only)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Extra 50% off already-reduced prices — while sizes last`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `New clearance markdowns just dropped across every category`
