# Today only: 20% off home 🤩 New summer picks await ☀️
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- This is Kohl's ninth consecutive promotional email since July 1, and the most notable thing about it is the subject line finally breaking the "20% off + Kohl's Cash" template by calling out a specific category — home. That's the right instinct. The execution, however, undercuts it: the email body is dominated by a women's New Arrivals fashion section, a Nike activewear promotion, and general clearance, with home relegated to a four-tab navigation strip. A shopper who opened for a home deal will scroll right past dresses and joggers before finding anything relevant.
- The three-value-proposition structure (20% off sitewide, Nike up to 50%, clearance up to 70%) gives the email real visual density and a reason to explore. The summer "New Arrivals" imagery is genuinely fresh — better than the generic department shots used in the July 3–5 runs. But after eight prior sends with the same core "today only, 20% off" mechanism, "today only" is no longer an urgency signal — it's just how Kohl's works.
- The send is competent and probably converts at a baseline rate, but it is structurally deceptive: it promises home, delivers apparel. Fix the subject-to-body alignment and this email has real potential.

## What's working

- **Category-specific subject hook** — "20% off home" is a first in this eight-email sprint and gives deal-seeking home shoppers a genuine reason to open.
- **Three-tier offer structure** — 20% off, Nike 50%, clearance 70% gives the email multiple entry points for different shoppers.
- **Summer New Arrivals imagery** — the fashion photography reads genuinely seasonal and is more editorial than the prior clearance-heavy sends.
- **Kohl's Cash is visible above the fold** — loyalty members see their incentive immediately without scrolling.
- **"Today only" urgency** — technically credible as a single-day home sale, distinct from the five-day run of July 1–5.

## What's weak

- **Subject promises home, body delivers apparel** — the hero section is women's fashion and Nike activewear; home appears only as a navigation row (Decor, Bedding, Bath, Kitchen & Dining).
- **Generic CTAs throughout** — every button says "Shop Now"; no button tells you *what* you're shopping, missing an easy specificity win.
- **No single hero product** — three disparate sections (arrivals, Nike, clearance) compete for attention; nothing anchors the click.
- **Nike exclusion caveat** — "Coupons do not apply" on a 50%-off section creates confusion about what the 20% stacks with, adding friction mid-scroll.
- **Cadence fatigue is real** — this is email #9 in six days; even a legitimate category shift is harder to land when the inbox has been hammered this consistently.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Align the body to the subject** — if the subject says "20% off home," open with a home hero: a styled room shot with specific SKUs (rug, throw, bedding set) at actual prices before and after discount. Move apparel to a secondary module.
- 2. **Differentiate the CTAs** — replace all "Shop Now" with "Shop Home Deals," "Shop Nike," "Shop Clearance" — one extra word per button and a meaningfully better click experience.
- 3. **Show one specific hero item** — a $79 area rug marked down to $63, or a specific Nike legging at $24, creates a concrete anchor that generic "up to X% off" never does.
- 4. **Drop the subject length** — the current subject is ~53 chars with emojis; trim the second clause to let "Today only: 20% off home" own the preview real estate instead of competing with "New summer picks await."
- 5. **Clarify Nike stacking rules** — if the 20% doesn't apply to Nike, say so clearly at the top of that section rather than burying it in fine print after the offer hook.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Today only: 20% off home — decor, bedding, bath`
- **Subject Alt B:** `20% off your home refresh, today only ☀️`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Rugs, bedding, bath and more — grab it before midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Decor, bedding, kitchen: 20% off ends tonight. Plus Nike up to 50% off.`

## Technical Audit — Kohl's "Today only: 20% off home" (2026-07-06)

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is a standard table-based HTML mailer with three separate pixel trackers and 22 click-redirect links. Compliance headers were not captured by the relay, and accessibility gaps are widespread across tracking and content images.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Three distinct open-pixel systems are firing simultaneously:**

- `https://click.chp.kohls.com/o/6ca9786a-...` — CoherentPath behavioral tracker
- `https://mi.kohls.com/p/up/88954bbbbcab3c0e/o.gif?mi_u=604230016` — Marigold Engage (Sailthru) open pixel
- `https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?ZI6FPHUZG3TELE7LL45K2...` — secondary ESP open tracker (likely Braze or Cheetah)

Triple-counting opens means engagement metrics in any single platform are unreliable by default.

**22 click links skipped** by the HTTP probe — all route through `click.s.kohls.com` or `click.chp.kohls.com` redirect chains. No broken links were confirmed, but redirect depth and final destination health are unverified.

**Malformed HTML tag** in the pixel block:
```html
<img src="https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?..." width="1" height="1" alt=""></custom>
```
The `</custom>` closing tag is invalid and orphaned — no matching opening tag. Most clients will ignore it, but it is a parse error that could trip strict validators or certain rendering engines.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**11 images missing `alt` text** — confirmed by QA scan. All are either tracking pixels or content images served from `mi.kohls.com`. Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` (they do, per QA flags, so the issue is the *absence* of the attribute entirely vs. an empty string — these render as broken image text in some clients when images are blocked).

Specific flagged srcs:
- `o.gif` (open pixel) — no `alt`
- `event` (Adobe Audience Manager pixel at `kohls.demdex.net`) — no `alt`
- 6 content/rp images — no `alt`, meaning image-off renders are completely blank for those product areas

**`<title>` tag is empty** (`<title></title>`). Screen readers announce the tab/window title; an empty title is a WCAG 2.4.2 failure.

**`user-scalable=0`** in the viewport meta tag disables pinch-to-zoom — a known WCAG 1.4.4 violation and accessibility barrier on mobile.

**`max-height: auto`** in the responsive CSS is not a valid value (`auto` is not accepted for `max-height`). Browsers silently ignore it; no functional breakage, but dead code.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unrendered merge tokens (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `%%first_name%%`) visible in the truncated source. The `mi_u=604230016` parameter in Marigold pixel URLs is a resolved subscriber ID, confirming personalization tokens processed correctly at send time.

No issues found.

### 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, Unsubscribe, Authentication)

**Physical address:** Not present in the truncated HTML source. QA confirms no address detected. CAN-SPAM §7(a)(4) requires a valid postal address. If it appears below the truncation point, this is a non-issue — but it must be verified in the full render.

**List-Unsubscribe / List-Unsubscribe-Post headers:** Not captured by the AgentMail relay. These are required by Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender policies (enforced since Feb 2024) for senders at Kohl's volume. The relay gap means status is unknown — not a confirmed absence, but must be verified at the MTA level.

**RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe (`List-Unsubscribe-Post`):** Same relay gap. Non-compliance at this send volume would affect Gmail placement.

**Authentication-Results header:** Not present via relay — SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status unverifiable from this capture. Sending domain `s.kohls.com` should be checked directly.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM Params, Landing Page Alignment)

All 22 click links route through `click.s.kohls.com` or `click.chp.kohls.com` redirect chains. UTM parameters on final landing page URLs cannot be confirmed without resolving the redirect chain — the HTTP probe skipped all 22.

The `mi_ecmp=1016725_202676` and `email_name=260706_DG_Email_Monday` parameters on the Marigold image URLs are campaign-tagged, indicating the campaign ID and date are wired correctly into the tracking layer. Whether the same campaign parameters survive through to `kohls.com` product pages is unverified.
