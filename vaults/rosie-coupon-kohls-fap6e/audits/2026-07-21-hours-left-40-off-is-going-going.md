# HOURS LEFT ⏳ 40% off is going, going ...
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- This is Kohl's ninth 40%-off email in roughly four days, and the creative team is clearly running out of runway. The subject — "HOURS LEFT ⏳ 40% off is going, going ..." — borrows from an auctioneer's playbook, but after eight prior sends with the same core discount, the urgency has completely lost its teeth. No subscriber believes "hours left" at this point; they've seen this countdown reset daily.
- Inside the email, the creative is actually decent. The summer BBQ headline is warm and seasonal, the Nike "Up to 50% off LIMITED TIME ONLY" module is a legitimate strong hook, and the clearance 85% off banner — carried over from the July 20 send that broke the pattern — gives a genuine secondary reason to click. But that strength is buried under an overwhelming stack of competing offers: 40% off, 85% clearance, 50% Nike, and an Amazon Device Deals block that has no business being in a summer fashion email. The eye has nowhere to land.
- The Amazon module is the single most damaging element. It reads like a revenue-sharing placeholder that makes the entire send feel unfocused and slightly desperate. A subscriber mid-scroll sees Kohl's fashion, then suddenly Echo devices — and the trust in the editorial voice evaporates.

## What's working

- **Summer BBQ headline** is the warmest, most seasonally grounded copy in this entire Kohl's run — a genuine improvement over prior generic "savings" language.
- **85% clearance banner** carried over from the July 20 pivot send; still the strongest value signal in the email, gives subscribers with depleted trust a new reason to look.
- **Nike "Up to 50% off LIMITED TIME ONLY"** is a specific brand offer with a credible limited-time badge — the most click-worthy module in the email.
- **Concrete price anchors** ($7.79, $8.99 summer items) give shoppers a specific entry point without requiring a click to evaluate.
- **"TODAY ONLY" badge** at the top is clearly rendered and visually dominant even at small screen sizes.

## What's weak

- **Amazon Device Deals module** is an editorial non sequitur — it breaks the summer fashion thread and signals that Kohl's is monetizing list attention rather than serving it.
- **Four competing discount tiers simultaneously** (40%, 85%, 50%, 60%) create cognitive overload; the reader can't determine what's actually the best deal without doing math.
- **"HOURS LEFT" urgency is fully exhausted** — this is the same countdown framing used in the July 19 "ENDS TONIGHT 🚨" and July 19 "here today, gone tomorrow" sends. No subscriber reading this believes 40% off expires today.
- **Multiple "Shop Now" CTAs** with no clear hierarchy — the email has at least five separate CTA buttons across unrelated categories, none elevated as the primary action.
- **No personalization or segmentation signal** anywhere in the email — every module feels like a broadcast blast, not a curated send.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Drop the Amazon Device Deals module entirely.** It undermines editorial credibility in a fashion/lifestyle email. If the partnership must stay, isolate it to a dedicated send.
- 2. **Pick one dominant offer and commit.** Either lead with 85% clearance (the stronger hook) or 40% off — not both simultaneously stacked with Nike and Amazon. The July 20 clearance send earned an 9/10 because it made one clear bet.
- 3. **Rebuild the CTA hierarchy.** One primary button above the fold ("Shop Summer Clearance" or "Shop 50% Off Nike"), secondary CTAs below. Right now every module competes equally for the click.
- 4. **Kill the 40%-off urgency cycle for at least 48 hours.** The brand history shows this promotion has been "ending" since July 18. Running a non-discount content email (new arrivals, style guide, back-to-school preview) would restore trust and make the next discount land harder.
- 5. **Swap subject to lead with what's actually new.** "HOURS LEFT" on a discount that has been "ending" for four days is the weakest possible framing.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Nike & clearance: up to 85% off today only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `$7 summer styles before the BBQ weekend ends`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Clearance hits 85% + 50% off Nike — both end tonight`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Summer staples from $7.79 · Kohl's Cash still running`

## Full review
## 1. Overview

This is Kohl's ninth 40%-off email in roughly four days, and the creative team is clearly running out of runway. The subject — "HOURS LEFT ⏳ 40% off is going, going ..." — borrows from an auctioneer's playbook, but after eight prior sends with the same core discount, the urgency has completely lost its teeth. No subscriber believes "hours left" at this point; they've seen this countdown reset daily.

The eye has nowhere to land.

The Amazon module is the single most damaging element. It reads like a revenue-sharing placeholder that makes the entire send feel unfocused and slightly desperate. A subscriber mid-scroll sees Kohl's fashion, then suddenly Echo devices — and the trust in the editorial voice evaporates.

## Technical Audit — Kohl's "HOURS LEFT ⏳ 40% off" (2026-07-21)

### 1. Technical Summary

Email uses a standard ESP-rendered HTML 4.01 Transitional template with three separate pixel-tracking systems firing on open. Automated QA flagged compliance gaps (missing physical address, unsubscribe headers not captured) and pervasive missing alt text across 13 images.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Three overlapping open-tracking systems detected:**
- `https://click.chp.kohls.com/o/59f9e3e7-...` — CoherentPath pixel
- `https://mi.kohls.com/p/up/88954bbbbcab3c0e/o.gif?mi_u=604230016` — MessageGears open pixel
- `https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?PFUYD2JBHH5...` — Salesforce Marketing Cloud open pixel

Firing all three on a single open inflates open-rate metrics across platforms and creates attribution conflicts. The CoherentPath pixel also carries `mi_mid` and `cp_tp` query params that duplicate MessageGears identity data.

**22 click-redirect links skipped by HTTP probe** — destination URLs and HTTP status codes unverified by QA.

**Adobe Audience Manager sync pixel present:**
- `https://kohls.demdex.net/event?d_sid=13245196` — third-party data sync firing on open, no alt text, no `aria-hidden`.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**13 images missing `alt` text** (confirmed by QA). Affected sources include content images, not just tracking pixels:

| Image | Source |
|---|---|
| Product image | `kohlsprod/55869996_root2_2x` |
| Product image | `kohlsprod/55405645_root_2x` |
| Promo banners (×4) | `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/2a5adc5afb647aad.png`, `f441d45d...`, `c2f81e9a...`, `415c8a18...` |
| Tracking pixels (×3) | CoherentPath, MessageGears, Demdex — should carry `alt=""` + `aria-hidden="true"` |

Tracking pixels (`o.gif`, `event`, `59f9e3e7...`) are missing both `alt=""` and `aria-hidden="true"`, which causes screen readers to announce raw filenames.

**`<title>` element is empty** — `<title></title>`. Some screen readers and email clients announce the document title; an empty title degrades accessibility and may reduce deliverability scoring.

**Malformed closing tag in open-tracking block:**
```html
<img src="https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?..."> <custom>
```
`</custom>` is not a valid HTML element. Strict parsers may reject or misrender the surrounding block.

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta** — blocks pinch-to-zoom on mobile, an accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4).

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

### 5. Compliance

**CAN-SPAM:**
- No physical mailing address detected in the truncated source. Required by 15 U.S.C. § 7704(a)(5). May be present below the truncation point — verify in full source.

**List-Unsubscribe headers:**
- `List-Unsubscribe` header not captured by AgentMail relay.
- `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058 one-click) header not captured.
- Gmail and Apple Mail require `List-Unsubscribe-Post` for native one-click unsubscribe UI; absence degrades inbox placement signals.

**Authentication:**
- `Authentication-Results` header not captured. SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass/fail status unknown through the relay. Sending domain `s.kohls.com` should be verified independently.

**`<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />`** — suppresses referrer on all outbound clicks. This is intentional (common practice) but means GA4/site analytics will not receive email referrer attribution on any click-throughs that bypass the click-redirect tracker.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

`mi_ecmp=1018061_2026721` is present on MessageGears pixel URLs but **UTM parameters are not visible** on any destination URLs in the truncated source. All 22 click links route through `click.chp.kohls.com` or `click.s.kohls.com` redirectors — whether those redirectors append UTM params to the final kohls.com URL is unverifiable without resolving the redirect chain.

`<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />` (noted above) will strip HTTP referrer on any direct link that bypasses click tracking, further breaking site-side attribution.
