# Everyone’s obsessed with these rn
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-28**
## Executive summary

- This is the same subject/body disconnect Sephora has run every single day this week — 07-25 through 07-27 all promised one thing in the subject and delivered a Lip Day markdown in the hero. "Everyone's obsessed with these rn" is the vaguest version yet: it doesn't even gesture at a category, let alone the actual offer. What's inside is a clean, well-merchandised 20% off Select LANEIGE hero for National Lip Day, followed by a genuinely strong "Trending Picks for You" grid spanning Prada, Kérastase, Farmacy, Shiseido, and more.
- The body execution is honestly the best part of this send — clear hierarchy, visible pricing on every tile, loyalty status bar up top, no render bugs. But the subject line is doing the email a disservice. Vague, clickbait-y "these" copy with zero specificity or urgency is a weak hook, especially as the fourth-plus Lip Day-adjacent send this week — recipients who've already opened two or three of these will recognize the pattern and scroll past.
- Net: strong body, weak subject, tired cadence. This would perform meaningfully better as a "Trending Picks" or personalized-recs subject rather than a generic curiosity hook riding on an offer it doesn't name.

## What's working

- The hero banner leads with a concrete, specific offer (20% off Select LANEIGE) and a clear "Today only!" deadline, visible without scrolling.
- Loyalty/status context is front and center — "INSIDER," "You have 2 points," and the INSIDER/VIB/ROUGE tier bar in the footer.
- The "Trending Picks for You" grid is genuinely well-built: 8 distinct products, consistent pricing display, "Only at Sephora" and "Clean" badges used to add credibility without clutter.
- No visual bugs — clean product photography, no overlapping text, no broken images, consistent brand styling throughout.

## What's weak

- Subject line names no product, category, or offer — "these" is undefined until you're several screens deep, which is a real bar to opening from a crowded inbox.
- This is the same National Lip Day sale/offer beat Sephora has now run in some form across four-plus consecutive sends (07-25 "hair" fake-out, 07-26 lip must-haves, 07-27 lip faves, and now this) — subscribers who opened those will feel the repetition.
- Primary CTA copy is the generic "SHOP NOW" repeated five times through the email with no variation tied to what's actually being shopped.
- No social proof (star ratings, review counts) anywhere in the trending-picks grid, despite it being the section most likely to drive discovery clicks.

## Recommendations

- 1. Rewrite the subject to actually name the offer or category — vague curiosity hooks only work when the payoff is fresh; this one isn't, so specificity will outperform mystery here.
- 2. Vary CTA copy per section ("Shop Lip Deals," "Shop Trending Picks," "Shop Under $45") instead of five identical "SHOP NOW" buttons — gives scanners more to latch onto.
- 3. Add a review-count or star-rating overlay to at least the "Trending Picks" tiles — this section is closest to a discovery/browse experience and is the one most likely to benefit from social proof.
- 4. Pause or retire the National Lip Day framing after this send — four sends on the same seasonal moment risks list fatigue; rotate to a genuinely new hook for the next one.
