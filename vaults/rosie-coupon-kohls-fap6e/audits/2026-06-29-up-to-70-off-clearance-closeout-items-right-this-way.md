# Up to 70% off clearance & closeout items right this way ➡️
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-29**
## Executive summary

- Kohl's makes a clean editorial pivot here: after a Deal Days campaign that burned through twelve sends over six days, June 29 opens with a clearance/closeout frame — no "ends tonight" clock-ticking, no Deal Days banner for the thirteenth time. The creative freshness is real. The hero lands on "CLEARANCE – Save up to 70%" with a patriotic stars-and-stripes model pair that reads squarely as pre–4th of July, which is exactly where the calendar is. This is Kohl's at its most competent: seasonal hook, legitimate discount tier, and a clean product scroll from denim to patio to Levi's brand block.
- The problem is the inbox context. Subscribers just received twelve Deal Days emails, including two "Ends today" sends on June 28 alone. No matter how fresh this creative is, it arrives against a backdrop of severe cadence exhaustion. "Up to 70% off" after a week of "Deal Days Final Hours" feels less like a discovery and more like the next alarm. The brand trust tax from that run is real, and this send pays a portion of it whether or not it deserves to.
- The clearance email itself is good. The brand cadence situation surrounding it is not. Judge accordingly.

## What's working

- **Clean creative break.** No "DEAL DAYS" banner in sight — the clearance/closeout hero is visually distinct from the previous twelve sends.
- **4th of July timing is nailed.** Stars-and-stripes models on June 29 hit the right seasonal beat without being gratuitous; "Stars, stripes and seriously cute picks" is light copy that earns its place.
- **Multiple offer tiers visible.** "Save up to 70%" at the hero, "Save up to 40% off select styles" mid-email, and specific starting-at price points ($24.99, $3.99) give different value-threshold shoppers something to click.
- **Levi's brand block adds credibility.** A named brand partnership breaks the house-brand monotony and adds a reason to scroll.
- **Fast & Free Store Pickup strip + Kohl's Cash are both present.** Two evergreen deal levers surfaced together without competing for attention.

## What's weak

- **"Up to 70%" is a soft anchor.** In clearance emails, most items sit at 30–40% off; the 70% number sets an expectation the body rarely delivers on, especially as the email's mid-section only promises 40% off select styles.
- **Generic "Shop Now" CTAs throughout.** Every section button reads "Shop Now" — no specificity like "Shop Clearance Denim" or "Shop Patio Closeouts." The button copy does no persuasion work.
- **No deadline or urgency mechanism.** After a week of "Deal Days ends tonight," this email has no closing signal at all. Clearance items sell out, but nothing in the creative acknowledges that.
- **Email is long with no anchor section.** There are at least five distinct product sections visible in the scroll — denim, footwear, patio, Levi's, multi-category grid — with no hierarchy between them beyond order of appearance.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a soft urgency signal to the hero.** "Limited quantities — clearance sells out fast" costs nothing to add and is genuinely true for closeout items. The brand just conditioned this list to expect a deadline; removing it entirely is a lost lever.
- 2. **Make the CTAs category-specific.** Replace "Shop Now" with "Shop Clearance Women's," "Shop Patio Closeouts," etc. Each button becomes a filter that reduces scroll friction and improves click attribution.
- 3. **Lean harder into the 4th of July tie.** The stars-and-stripes section is buried below the clearance hero. A subheadline like "Gear up for the 4th — and save up to 70% while you're here" bridges the two themes instead of running them as separate blocks.
- 4. **Cull one or two mid-scroll sections.** The patio section and the multi-category "Something for everyone" grid compete rather than complement. Pick the stronger performer from last cycle and cut the weaker one.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Clearance sale: up to 70% off — while it lasts`
- **Subject Alt B:** `4th of July style starts at $3.99 — clearance is on`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stars, stripes, and seriously steep discounts on closeout styles`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Denim, patio, Levi's — clearance goes fast, shop now`

## Technical Audit — Kohl's Clearance Email (2026-06-29)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound for a commercial blast but has a malformed HTML tag, 13 images missing alt text, and compliance headers (List-Unsubscribe, physical address) that are either absent or unverifiable through the relay. Authentication status cannot be confirmed.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Malformed HTML in open-pixel container**
The ExactTarget/SFMC open-tracking pixel block contains a stray closing tag with no matching opener:
```html
<img src="https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?HSEBHYL2KURUVOLTKBJZGEGNNU.60266..." width="1" height="1" alt=""></custom>
```
`</custom>` is invalid and will generate a parse error in strict renderers. Low breakage risk in practice, but should be removed.

**Three separate open-tracking pixels**
The email fires three distinct open/impression pixels on load:
- `click.chp.kohls.com/o/98735bda-...` (Coherent Path)
- `mi.kohls.com/p/up/88954bbbbcab3c0e/o.gif` (MessageGears/mi)
- `click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?HSEBHYL2...` (Salesforce Marketing Cloud)
- `kohls.demdex.net/event?d_sid=13245196` (Adobe Audience Manager — 0×0, no alt)

Triple-firing is unusual and could inflate open-rate counts across platforms if not deduplicated upstream.

**22 click-tracking links unverifiable**
All CTA links route through `click.chp.kohls.com` or `click.s.kohls.com`. HTTP probing was skipped. UTM parameters and final destination URLs cannot be confirmed from source alone (see §6).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0" />
```
Blocks pinch-to-zoom on mobile. This is a WCAG 1.4.4 failure (Resize Text, Level AA) and will trigger accessibility flags in audits. Standard practice is to omit `user-scalable` or set it to `yes`.

**13 images missing `alt` text** (per QA)
Tracking pixels (1×1, 0×0) account for 4 of these — acceptable to leave blank or set `alt=""` explicitly and `aria-hidden="true"`, which two of the four already do. The remaining flagged images are content images via `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/...` and `images.am.kohls.com/is/image/kohlsprod/...`:
- `54952180_4_2x` (product image — no alt)
- `54991917_3s_2x` (product image — no alt)
- Multiple `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/*.png` rendition images — no alt

Product images with no alt text fail screen readers and will render as broken descriptions in image-off environments.

**Empty `<title>` tag**
```html
<title></title>
```
Some clients (Outlook, Apple Mail) surface the title in previews or accessibility trees. Should be set to the email subject or a short descriptor.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header absent** (QA: WARN)
Not detected by the relay. Gmail and Apple Mail use this header to surface one-click unsubscribe UI. If absent at the MTA level (not stripped by AgentMail), this will suppress native unsubscribe prompts and may trigger spam classification on high-volume sends.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header absent** (QA: WARN)
RFC 8058 one-click POST unsubscribe is required by Google and Yahoo bulk sender policies (>5k/day). Absence is a deliverability risk.

**Physical address not detected** (QA: WARN)
HTML source is truncated — address may exist below the fold. If genuinely absent, this is a CAN-SPAM §5 violation (15 USC 7704). Needs manual confirmation against the full rendered footer.

**`<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />`**
Referrer suppression is intentional for privacy (standard practice), but means inbound analytics on kohls.com will show no email referrer for image loads. Not a bug, but worth noting for analytics teams.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 22 CTAs pass through `click.chp.kohls.com` or `click.s.kohls.com` redirect chains — final destination URLs and UTM parameters are not visible in source and could not be probed. The following cannot be verified:
- Whether `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` are appended at redirect time
- Whether the `email_name=260629_DG_Email_Monday` campaign code is passed through to site analytics
- Landing page existence and relevance to offer copy

The `mi_ecmp=1016160_2026629` parameter in rendition image URLs suggests MessageGears campaign tracking, but this only covers opens/image loads, not click-through attribution.

**Recommendation:** manually spot-check 2–3 CTA redirect URLs to confirm UTM chain is intact before reporting email-attributed revenue.
