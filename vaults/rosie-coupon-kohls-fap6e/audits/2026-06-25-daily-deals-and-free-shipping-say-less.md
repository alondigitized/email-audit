# Daily Deals AND free shipping? Say less 🤗
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-25**
## Executive summary

- This is Kohl's seventh Deal Days email in roughly 48 hours, and structurally it is the same email as the previous six: the Deal Days hero banner, the free-shipping callout, the category nav strip, the stacked 50%-off and 40%-off product tiles, the Kohl's Cash reminder, the footer. The only genuine creative innovation in this send is the subject line — "Say less 🤗" reads as casual Gen-Z affirmation ("say no more, I'm already in"), which is the most distinct voice in this entire Deal Days run. It's a shame the body doesn't follow that register at all.
- The catalog structure is functional. Five categories (men's, women's, kids' apparel, LEGO, beauty) give any subscriber a scent trail regardless of what they want, and the price anchors ($14.99, 50% off, 5/$35) are visible without scrolling. But that breadth is also the problem: nothing in this email is targeted to anyone specific. It's a catalog blast dressed as a daily deal.
- The cadence is causing real damage. Seven near-identical emails in three days for a six-day event means the brand is outpacing its own event calendar — two sends per day when one send per day is literally what "6 Days of Deals" implies. Each additional send chips away at list health past the lifetime of this promotion. This individual email is solid; the send strategy around it is not.

## What's working

- **"Say less 🤗" is the most differentiated subject in this entire Deal Days run** — it injects a personality the previous six sends completely lacked.
- **Multiple concrete price anchors** ($14.99, 50% off, 40% off, 5/$35) are immediately visible — a subscriber who spends two seconds skimming understands the value proposition.
- **Category nav strip** (Men / Women / Boys / Girls / $15 & Under / $35 & Under) is efficient wayfinding that doesn't require a homepage click.
- **Kohl's Cash** is called out in both the hero and the footer — a genuine loyalty benefit that stacks real value on top of the sale pricing.
- **Clean render** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no layout breakage visible in the screenshot.

## What's weak

- **"Say less" doesn't carry through to the body** — the subject signals casual and modern; the email body is a standard catalog grid. The voice shift is a bait-and-switch of tone.
- **All six-plus CTAs say "Shop Now"** — with equal visual weight and identical copy, there's no signal directing the subscriber toward any particular item or category.
- **Zero demographic or behavioral targeting** — men's, women's, kids', LEGO, and beauty in one email signals this is an unfiltered blast, not a curated recommendation.
- **No social proof** — no star ratings, no bestseller callouts, no "X people bought this." At these price points, a review count would meaningfully lift click confidence.
- **No explicit deadline** — the "6 Days of Deals" framing implies an end date but the email never says when. A countdown or "ends Sunday" line would convert passive interest into urgency.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Enforce one email per day for the six-day event** — the event's name literally promises six days, not twelve emails. Two sends per day violates the implied contract with the subscriber and compounds list fatigue with every additional blast.
- 2. **Make CTAs product-specific** — "Shop Jeans," "Shop Dresses," "Shop LEGO" take three words and immediately differentiate the six adjacent buttons.
- 3. **Carry "Say less" energy into the hero copy** — if the subject is casual, the first headline should match. "Free shipping. 50% off. You already know." would hold the register.
- 4. **Add an explicit deadline** — "Deal Days ends Sunday" or a countdown widget turns the always-on catalog feel into a time-bounded event.
- 5. **Surface one star rating or bestseller badge** on the hero product — even "★★★★★ 4.8 · 2,400 reviews" at $14.99 would lift click confidence on that tile.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Today only: $14.99 jeans + free shipping`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Deal Days Day 3 — 50% off ends tonight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free shipping, no minimum. Kohl's Cash stacks. Ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your daily deal dropped. $14.99 jeans, 50% off dresses, free ship.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

No broken image URLs detected. The `<link href="" rel="shortcut icon">` has an empty `href` — harmless but noisy.

**Google Fonts double-loaded** — both `<link>` tags and `@import` rules fetch Roboto and Montserrat. In clients that honor `<link>`, this fires two separate HTTP requests per font family.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Malformed HTML — stray `</custom>` tag:**
```html
<img src="https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?…" width="1" height="1" alt=""></custom>
```
The `</custom>` closing tag has no matching open tag. Strict-mode parsers (Outlook, some webmail) may bubble parse errors from this point forward.

**Zoom disabled via viewport meta:**
```html
maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0
```
Prevents users from pinching to zoom — a WCAG 1.4.4 violation and a friction point for low-vision recipients.

**Missing alt text on content images** — 6 personalized content images from `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/` have no `alt` attribute. The three 1×1 tracking pixels correctly carry `alt=""`, but the rendered product/promo images do not. Screen readers will announce raw filenames (e.g. `2a5adc5afb647aad.png`).

Affected srcs (sample):
- `https://mi.kohls.com/p/rp/2a5adc5afb647aad.png`
- `https://mi.kohls.com/p/rp/f441d45d2128ae53.png`
- `https://mi.kohls.com/p/rp/415c8a18fc2950ea.png`

**Empty `<title>` tag** — `<title></title>` renders as a blank tab label in webmail clients that display it (Gmail web, Yahoo). Not critical but low effort to fix.

**DOCTYPE is HTML 4.01 Transitional** — expected for broad email client compatibility; not flagged as an issue.

### 5. Compliance

**Physical address absent from visible HTML source.** CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid postal address in the message body. It may exist below the truncation point, but the QA checker did not detect it — confirm it is present in the full render.

**List-Unsubscribe header not captured** — the AgentMail relay did not surface this header. If Kohl's ESP sends it (standard for reputable senders), the relay is stripping or not forwarding it. Without it, Gmail and Apple Mail cannot render their native one-click unsubscribe UI — increasing spam complaint rate.

**List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058) not captured** — same root cause. Google and Yahoo's 2024 bulk sender requirements mandate one-click unsubscribe for senders above 5,000/day. Absence here is a deliverability risk flag, not necessarily a Kohl's defect — depends on whether the relay is suppressing the header.

**SPF/DKIM/DMARC unverifiable** — `Authentication-Results` header absent from relay capture. Cannot confirm pass/fail for `s.kohls.com` sending domain. This is a relay instrumentation gap, not a confirmed auth failure.

**`no-referrer` meta tag present:**
```html
<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer" />
```
Suppresses the HTTP Referer header on all outbound clicks. This is intentional for privacy but means GA4/Adobe Analytics on landing pages will receive direct/none traffic instead of email attribution — unless UTM params in the click URLs compensate.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All CTAs pass through click-redirect domains (`click.chp.kohls.com`, `click.s.kohls.com`), so final landing page URLs and their UTM parameters cannot be inspected without live redirect resolution. The `no-referrer` policy (noted above) makes UTM params on destination URLs the only attribution mechanism — if any CTA link omits `utm_source`, that session will be unattributed.

The `mi_ecmp=1015935_2026625` parameter on pixel URLs encodes campaign identity in MessageGears format, but this does not substitute for standard `utm_*` params on CTA hrefs.

No mismatched or placeholder offer copy detected in the truncated source; full source review would be needed to confirm landing page offer alignment.
