# Save 20% … now’s the time to shop!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-09**
## Executive summary

- This email lands the morning after Kohl's ran a two-email closing blitz on 30% off — and now it's asking shoppers to celebrate 20% off instead. That step-down is the single biggest damage this email does to itself before anyone even opens it. Subscribers who received "HOURS LEFT for 30% off every brand" yesterday are being asked today to get excited about a worse deal.
- Inside the email, the execution is technically competent — a clean summer dress hero, seasonally appropriate copy, and a clear product spotlight at $19.99 (reg. $34.99). But the subject line promises "Save 20%" while the body never explicitly shows a 20% off callout. What the body shows instead is a sale price on one dress, a Kohl's Cash earn banner dated July 18–25 (ten days away), and a multi-category grid that reads as a homepage, not a promotional email. The offer and the creative feel decoupled.
- The verdict: a competent content email wearing a promotional subject line. The 20% claim in the subject creates an expectation the body can't meet on its own terms, and after the 30% streak, this send will feel like a downgrade to anyone paying attention.

## What's working

- **Summer dress hero is tight.** One dress, one price, one category line — the $19.99 vs. $34.99 callout is the clearest discount signal in the email.
- **Visual hierarchy holds.** The eye goes dress → price → "Shop Now" without fighting the layout.
- **Zero render bugs.** Clean image loading, no overlapping elements, no blank content blocks.
- **Kohl's Cash loyalty framing.** Showing the Kohl's Rewards member header and the Kohl's Cash earn section in one email keeps the loyalty loop visible.

## What's weak

- **30% → 20% step-down kills the deal's credibility.** Yesterday's "HOURS LEFT for 30% off" makes this offer feel like a consolation prize, not a fresh opportunity.
- **Subject/body disconnect.** "Save 20%" is the headline promise, but the body never shows a 20% off discount explicitly — only a product sale price and future Kohl's Cash earn dates.
- **"Now's the time" is empty urgency.** No end date, no countdown, no specific window — the phrase works against itself.
- **Kohl's Cash dates (July 18–25) clash with "savings already started."** It's July 9. The "go ahead and shop" + "savings already started" copy sits next to a Kohl's Cash offer that doesn't start for nine more days. Confusing timeline.
- **Multi-category scatter dilutes focus.** Women → Men → Kids → Home → Beauty → Shoes in a single promotional email means nothing is actually promoted.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Anchor the 20% discount explicitly in the body.** Put a clear "20% off your entire purchase" hero callout — not just a sale-priced product. The subject promises a discount; the body needs to deliver it visually.
- 2. **Add a real deadline.** "Now's the time" is doing no work. Even "Valid through Sunday" lifts click urgency meaningfully.
- 3. **Pick one or two categories and cut the rest.** This email tries to be a homepage. Concentrate it on the summer dress moment — or explicitly segment Women vs. Men lists.
- 4. **Separate the Kohl's Cash teaser from the active sale.** If Kohl's Cash earn starts July 18, that's a different email. Mixing future-dated loyalty earn with a current-sale message creates cognitive load.
- 5. **Don't step down from 30% without a reason.** If the 30% event is over, say so and give shoppers a bridge reason to still care: "30% is over, but your wallet still wins: 20% off + Kohl's Cash starting the 18th."
- **Subject Alt A:** `Summer dresses from $19.99 — shop 20% off today`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your 20% off starts now (and Kohl's Cash comes next)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stripe and floral styles from $19.99 — this weekend only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Save now, earn Kohl's Cash July 18–25. Don't miss both.`

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Kohl's "Save 20%" Email

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email renders with one confirmed broken experience (unfilled merge token) and has multiple compliance gaps — missing physical address, absent one-click unsubscribe headers, and unverifiable authentication status via the AgentMail relay. Eleven images lack alt text, a malformed HTML tag is present in the pixel block, and Google Fonts are loaded redundantly.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Three open-tracking pixels detected — one via a malformed tag:**

| Pixel | Domain | Issue |
|---|---|---|
| `c0357954-...` | `click.chp.kohls.com` | Cheetah Digital open tracker; no `alt` attribute |
| `o.gif` | `mi.kohls.com` | Marigold open tracker; no `alt` |
| `open.aspx?7GTRL...` | `click.s.kohls.com` | Third open tracker; wrapped in a `<div>` that closes with `</custom>` — malformed tag |
| `event?d_sid=13245196` | `kohls.demdex.net` | Adobe Audience Manager sync pixel; no `alt` |

The `</custom>` closing tag at the third pixel is invalid HTML and may cause parse errors in strict renderers:

```html
<img src="https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?7GTRL...">
</custom>   ← invalid — should be </div>
```

**22 click-tracking links** routed through `click.chp.kohls.com` and `click.s.kohls.com` were not probed — destination URLs and UTM continuity cannot be confirmed from HTML source alone.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta** — prevents pinch-to-zoom in mobile email clients that honor this attribute. WCAG 1.4.4 failure for low-vision users:
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="..., maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0" />
```

**Empty `<title>` tag** — screen readers and some email clients use the title for tab/window labeling:
```html
<title></title>
```

**11 images missing `alt` text** (per QA) — includes both tracking pixels and content images (`2a5adc5afb647aad.png`, `f441d45d2128ae53.png`, `c2f81e9ac8daee44.png`, `415c8a18fc2950ea.png`, and others). Content images require descriptive alt text; tracking pixels should use `alt=""` explicitly.

**Google Fonts loaded twice** — both `<link>` tags and `@import` rules load Roboto and Montserrat, adding redundant network requests on clients that render `<head>` CSS:
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Roboto...">  ← link
@import url("https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Roboto..."); ← also @import
```

**Empty favicon `href`** — triggers a wasted network request:
```html
<link href="" rel="shortcut icon">
```

**Schema.org `<div>` placed inside `<head>`** — the `Organization` itemscope block is in `<head>`, which is invalid; browsers will relocate it unpredictably:
```html
<head>
  ...
  <div itemscope itemtype="http://schema.org/Organization">...</div>
</head>
```

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

**[FAIL] One unfilled merge token confirmed in rendered source:**

```
%%offer_evnt_nm%%
```

This token was not substituted before send, meaning recipients received the raw variable string. Impact depends on where it appears in the template (likely a promotional event name), but it constitutes a broken customer experience for all recipients.

No other visible merge tokens detected in the truncated HTML.

|---|---|---|
| `List-Unsubscribe` header | WARN | Not captured by AgentMail relay; may be present upstream but unverifiable |
| `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058 one-click) | WARN | Not found — Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender requirements expect this |
| Physical mailing address | FAIL | Not detected in HTML source or QA scan; CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid postal address |

| `referrer` meta | Info | `no-referrer` suppresses HTTP Referer on clicks — intentional, but eliminates referrer-based attribution fallback |

The physical address absence is the most critical compliance item. CAN-SPAM violations carry per-email fines; if Kohl's footer was truncated from the HTML sample, this should be verified against the full source before escalating.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM parameters not confirmed.** All 22 click links are double-redirect URLs through `click.chp.kohls.com` and `click.s.kohls.com`. The QA checker skipped HTTP probes on these domains, so whether Kohl's appends UTM params (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) at the redirect layer is unverified.

**Campaign identifiers visible in pixel URLs:**
```
mi_ecmp=1017141_202679
email_name=260709_DG_Email_Thursday
```

These are Marigold/Cheetah Digital internal campaign IDs — they will appear in ESP reporting but will not populate standard UTM dimensions in Google Analytics or other web analytics tools unless the redirect layer also appends `utm_*` params.

**`no-referrer` meta** (noted in §5) means even referrer-based fallback attribution is suppressed on the email side. If UTMs are missing from destination URLs, this campaign will appear as direct traffic in site analytics.
