# Take 20% off | Find fall home picks at prices you'll love 🏠
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-12**
## Executive summary

- This is Kohl's fifth send in the same three-day tier-stacking cycle, but it's the strongest execution yet because it finally has a real thematic hook — "Home essentials for essentially every budget" — layered on top of the segmented offer instead of standing alone. The Martha Stewart collection placement is a genuine differentiator that hasn't shown up in any of the last five audited sends, and it gives this email a reason to exist beyond another coupon stack. The "Status: KOHL'S REWARDS MEMBER" bar at top (same personalization trick from the 09-10 Customer Appreciation send) is a nice touch that keeps this feeling tailored rather than blasted.
- That said, this is still fundamentally the same 30%/20%/15% tiered offer running for the fifth consecutive day, and a recipient who's opened even two of the prior sends will recognize the layout instantly — the maroon tier boxes, the pink category buttons, the "There's always room for another great deal" filler copy are all repeats. The home/kitchen framing is a good seasonal pivot, but it's buried under six separate offer blocks (home, Martha Stewart, bedding/bath/storage/decor, family apparel, "picked for you" products, wallet coupon) that dilute rather than reinforce the subject line's home focus.
- Judgment: solid execution of a stale format — the Martha Stewart tie-in and clean render lift it above the mid-week sends, but cadence fatigue caps how much credit it earns.

## What's working

- Clean, bug-free render — no overlapping text, broken images, or placeholder content anywhere in a long scroll.
- Martha Stewart collection is a genuine fresh creative element not seen in the prior five audited sends, with a clear headshot, badge, and "Up to 25% off" callout.
- Membership status bar ("KOHL'S REWARDS MEMBER · Earn 5% REWARDS") personalizes the top of the email immediately.
- Specific, varied product callouts near the bottom (bath towel $2.99–$9.99, Ninja Slushi $349.99, jeans $29.99 marked down from $52, mattress topper with strike-through pricing) give concrete reasons to click.
- Home/kitchen imagery (cutting board, dinnerware, stand mixer) matches the subject line's "fall home picks" promise in the first screen.

## What's weak

- Same 30%/20%/15% tiered structure as the last four sends (09-10 through 09-11) — a subscriber who's opened this week has seen this exact box design three times already.
- Subject promises "fall home picks" but the email pivots into women's/men's/kids' apparel, accessories, and Sephora beauty halfway down — the home focus doesn't hold.
- Six distinct offer sections (home, Martha Stewart, bedding/bath/storage/decor, apparel, curated products, wallet coupon) create a scattershot hierarchy with no single hero the eye lands on first.
- "There's always room for another great deal" is generic filler copy carried over from the same playbook as prior sends, not fresh seasonal language.
- Fine print block at the very bottom (dense legal/coupon exclusions) is the kind of buried-terms pattern that erodes trust even if not deceptive.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email to one clear hero: lead with Martha Stewart + home essentials only, and move apparel/beauty/bedding into a follow-up send — this send is trying to be three emails at once.
- 2. Retire the tiered 30/20/15 box design for at least one send this week; the repetition is the single biggest drag on engagement given four prior nearly-identical uses.
- 3. Move the wallet coupon code and Kohl's Cash earn-rate higher, right under the hero, so the concrete offer isn't competing with six other CTAs for attention.
- 4. Trim "There's always room for another great deal" and similar filler headlines in favor of specific savings language tied to the season.
- 5. Add a "shop the collection" carousel format for Martha Stewart items instead of a single static product image — it's the freshest asset in the email and deserves more visual weight.
- **Subject Alt A:** `The Martha Stewart Collection is here + 20% off fall home`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New: Martha Stewart cookware, plus your 20% off code`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Kitchen, bedding & decor picks for your fall refresh — code inside`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus: earn Kohl's Cash on every $50 you spend`

# Technical Audit: Kohl's "Take 20% off" Email

## 1. Technical Summary
This is a Coherent Path-served (chp.kohls.com/mi.kohls.com) promotional email with standard multi-client HTML/MSO scaffolding; automated QA passed only 43% of checks, primarily due to missing authentication/unsubscribe headers and widespread missing `alt` attributes on tracking pixels.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- Multiple first-party tracking pixels fire on load: `click.chp.kohls.com/o/c50cf3f7-...` (1x1, no alt), `mi.kohls.com/p/up/88954bbbbcab3c0e/o.gif` (open tracking), `click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?7LWVA24OEGIU5PUHS466EI6W2U.60267...` (secondary open tracker), and `kohls.demdex.net/event?d_sid=13245196` (Adobe Audience Manager beacon).
- Body/preheader links route through an encoded Coherent Path redirect (`click.s.kohls.com/?qs=ABB7InYiOjEsImQiOjQ5OTd9AAYAAAAABQM0...`) rather than direct destination URLs — standard for this ESP but means link destinations can't be verified from source alone.
- QA flagged 71 tracking/click-redirect links skipped from HTTP probing (by design, since these are one-time-token or redirect-chain URLs) — no destination validation was possible for any CTA in this email.
- `<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow">` and `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` are present — appropriate for a transactional/marketing email rendered as a web page, not an issue.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 8 images flagged by QA as missing `alt` text: the two open-tracking pixels, the Demdex event pixel, and five `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/*.png` re-pixels. These are all 0-size tracking beacons, not content images, so the missing alt has no visible accessibility impact but should still carry `alt=""` for strict HTML validity (the `o.gif` pixel already does this correctly; the others don't).
- Responsive breakpoints are defined (`@media max-width:500px`) with width overrides for `.container`, `.w_50pc`, `.w_60pc`, `.w_35pc` — standard fluid-hybrid pattern, no issues found in the visible CSS.
- MSO/Outlook conditional comments (`<!--[if gte mso 9]>`) and `.ExternalClass` resets are present for Outlook desktop compatibility — no issues found.
- Malformed markup: `<img ... aria-hidden="true" /></custom>` — a stray closing `</custom>` tag appears after the `click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx` pixel with no matching opening tag. This is invalid HTML and should be removed.
- `<title></title>` is empty — not a rendering blocker but means webmail "view as web page" tabs will show a blank title.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
- No unresolved merge tags (e.g. `{{first_name}}`, `%%FIELD%%`) are visible in the truncated source.
- Tracking URLs carry recipient-specific identifiers (`mi_cid=67791fba63db790b`, `mi_mid=01a094d8-5280-7000-b31e-847e1330ad83`), confirming per-recipient personalization is active at the tracking layer.
- Cannot confirm on-page personalization (name, loyalty tier, etc.) from the truncated body content.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe header**: not found by QA. Cannot confirm one-click unsubscribe support at the mail-header level.
- **List-Unsubscribe-Post (RFC 8058)**: not found. Combined with the above, this email may not support one-click unsubscribe in Gmail/Yahoo/Outlook inbox UI, which risks spam-folder placement under 2024+ bulk sender requirements.
- **Physical mailing address**: not detected by QA anywhere in scanned content — a CAN-SPAM Act requirement. Note the source provided is truncated, so this may exist in the unscanned footer; recommend re-verifying against the full HTML before treating as confirmed.
- **Authentication-Results header (SPF/DKIM)**: not found — QA notes this may be an artifact of the AgentMail relay rather than the sender's actual DNS/auth posture; cannot confirm sender authentication status either way from this data alone.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
- Tracking links use Coherent Path campaign parameters (`mi_ecmp=1021652_2026912`, `email_name=260912_DG_Email_Sat_PM`) rather than standard `utm_*` query params — this is expected for this ESP's redirect-based click tracking, but means standard UTM-based attribution tools won't parse these links directly.
- Because all 71 outbound links resolve through `click.s.kohls.com` token redirects, final landing-page URLs and their alignment with the email's promoted offer ("20% off fall home") cannot be verified from source or QA data.

## 7. Recommendations
1. Confirm List-Unsubscribe / List-Unsubscribe-Post headers are actually present on the raw sent message (outside the relay) — required for inbox provider bulk-sender compliance.
2. Confirm a physical mailing address is present in the full (non-truncated) footer; if genuinely absent, this is a CAN-SPAM violation.
3. Add `alt=""` to the remaining tracking pixels (`event`, and the five `p/rp/*.png` beacons) for HTML validity consistency with the already-correct `o.gif` pixel.
4. Remove the stray `</custom>` closing tag after the `click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx` pixel.
5. Verify SPF/DKIM/DMARC authentication results directly against Kohl's sending infrastructure rather than relying on relay-captured headers, since QA could not determine pass/fail status.
