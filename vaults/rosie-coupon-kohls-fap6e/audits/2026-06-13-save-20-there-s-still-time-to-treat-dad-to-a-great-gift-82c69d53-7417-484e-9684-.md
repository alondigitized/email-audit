# Save 20% | There's still time to treat Dad to a great gift 🎁
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-13**
## Executive summary

- This is Kohl's third Father's Day email in six days and the second consecutive send built on the same 20% off coupon — the identical deal that "STARTED TODAY" per the June 11 send. The directional shift to "there's still time" is correct for T-8 days out from Father's Day, and the body earns it: products are specific and price-anchored, Kohl's Cash earn runs through June 21, and the hero copy ("For the dad that saves it all but buys him nothing") is the wittiest line Kohl's has put in an email this month. The clearance 70% off block adds a budget-gifting angle that works for a department store at this moment in the calendar.
- The execution problem is CTA fragmentation. Between "Explore the Gift Shop," "Shop Now" on clearance, "Gifts $10 & Under," "Gifts $25 & Under," and a second 20% off block anchoring the footer, there are at least six competing calls to action and no single button owns the scroll. For a weekend-close email whose job is to convert gift-deferred shoppers in the next 72 hours, that's a lot of exits built into the path.
- Verdict: the ingredients are right — live deal, specific products, stacking loyalty incentive, credible deadline — but the layout buries the urgency close under a clearance campaign that belongs in its own email.

## What's working

- **20% off coupon is the first thing you see** — the discount leads above the navigation bar, before any product imagery, which is the correct hierarchy for a coupon-gated send.
- **Dual deadline anchors** — "Time's running out" in the header banner and "Father's Day is June 21" in the gift-shop section give two timeboxing signals without feeling repetitive.
- **Price-anchored product pods** — "Starting at $9.99 (was $39.99)" and "Starting at $12.49" show discount depth in real numbers, not just a percentage.
- **JBL speaker in Electronics** — a recognizable brand name in a gift-appropriate category outperforms a generic "gifts for him" placeholder.
- **Kohl's Cash earn appears in both the upper band and footer** — the stacking incentive is surfaced twice without feeling spammy.

## What's weak

- **Same offer as June 11 with no creative escalation.** Two days ago this coupon "STARTED TODAY"; now it's just "still available." No expiry date printed, no volume signal, no urgency upgrade from one send to the next.
- **Six competing CTAs with no primary.** "Explore the Gift Shop," "Shop Now" (clearance), two price-tier gift guides, and a second coupon block at the bottom — no visual hierarchy tells the eye where to go.
- **Subject line at ~61 chars truncates on most phone lock screens.** The emoji pushes it further; the "There's still time to treat Dad to a great gift" tail is what gets cut.
- **No coupon expiry visible in the body.** The 20% requires a coupon but I cannot see a stated expiration date — on a weekend-close send, that uncertainty is a conversion leak.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Print the coupon deadline.** If the 20% expires Sunday, say "Coupon valid through Sunday 6/15" in the hero band. That single addition converts the casual browser into an urgent buyer. Highest-lift change available.
- 2. **Drop the clearance section or relegate it to a footer strip.** Clearance competes with the Father's Day occasion framing; a gift-buyer and a clearance hunter are different mindsets. Pick one for this send.
- 3. **Cut the subject to under 50 chars and include a date.** The current subject signals urgency but withholds the deadline — putting a specific date or day in the subject line would sharpen both open rate and buyer intent.
- 4. **Consolidate CTAs to two at most.** Primary: "Shop Father's Day Gifts" (gift guide). Secondary: "Browse Clearance" (budget option). Everything else is noise.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Save 20% for Dad — coupon ends Sunday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last weekend: 20% off Father's Day gifts + Kohl's Cash`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Coupon valid through 6/15. Father's Day is June 21 — shop gifts starting at $9.99.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Starting at $9.99 for Dad. Earn Kohl's Cash through June 21.`

## Technical Audit — Kohl's "Save 20% / Treat Dad" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

Standard HTML 4.01 Transitional email rendered via CoherentPath/Responsys stack with three concurrent tracking pixel systems. One malformed HTML tag and ten images missing `alt` attributes are the primary code-level defects; compliance header gaps are relay artifacts but still require verification.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Redundant tracking pixel stacks (3 systems firing simultaneously):**
- CoherentPath open tracker: `click.chp.kohls.com/o/ef5e913d-...`
- Kohl's MI open tracker: `click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?L2IGND2DFLWEZOYY...`
- Adobe Audience Manager pixel: `kohls.demdex.net/event?d_sid=13245196`
- Kohl's MI render pixels: `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/...` (multiple)

This is standard for this stack but inflates payload and adds latency on slow connections. No broken links confirmed in the truncated source.

**22 click-redirect links unverifiable:** All CTAs route through `click.chp.kohls.com` and `click.s.kohls.com`. Destination URLs and UTM parameter integrity cannot be confirmed without resolving redirects (skipped per QA config). This is a coverage gap in the audit, not a confirmed defect.

**Malformed HTML tag on open-tracker div:**
```html
<img src="https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?..." width="1" height="1" alt=""></custom>
```
`</custom>` is an invalid closing tag with no opening counterpart. Most parsers will silently discard it, but it is invalid HTML and could cause rendering anomalies in strict parsers.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Empty `<title>` tag:**
```html
<title></title>
```
Screen readers and some email clients surface the `<title>` as the document label. An empty value degrades assistive technology experience.

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta:**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=0" />
```
Prevents pinch-zoom on mobile. This fails WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) and is a real accessibility defect for low-vision users.

**10 images missing `alt` text** (confirmed by QA):
- `click.chp.kohls.com/o/ef5e913d-...` (CoherentPath pixel — should be `alt=""`)
- `kohls.demdex.net/event?d_sid=13245196` (AAM pixel — should be `alt=""`)
- `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/2a5adc5afb647aad.png` through `916d6fa08d185233.png` (content or render-tracking images)

Tracking/spacer pixels should have `alt=""` (empty, not absent) to signal intentional decoration to screen readers. Content images require descriptive alt text.

Note: `mi.kohls.com/p/up/.../o.gif` correctly includes `alt=""` and `aria-hidden="true"` — that pattern should be applied consistently to the other tracking images.

**Duplicate Google Fonts loading:**
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Roboto...">      <!-- link tag -->
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Montserrat...">
@import url("https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Roboto...");    <!-- also @import -->
@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Montserrat...);
```
Both `<link>` and `@import` load the same two font families. This doubles the external font requests for no gain.
