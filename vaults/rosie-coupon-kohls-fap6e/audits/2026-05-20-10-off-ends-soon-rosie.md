# 10% off ends soon, Rosie
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- This is a triggered expiry-reminder email — the logical conclusion of the welcome flow that started on May 14 when Rosie got her Beauty Insider welcome with code SE3ARPH and a 10% off promise. Six days later, with hours left on the clock, Sephora fires the urgency closer. The email does its job: countdown timer above the fold, name in the hero, promo code repeated twice (in the body and as a scannable footer), and a Beauty Insider context section for anyone who forgot why they're getting it.
- The core mechanics are sound and the hierarchy is clean. The countdown makes the "ends soon" framing credible rather than manufactured. The one structural weakness is the Kohl's exclusion copy — "Valid for kohl's/sephora below" in a Sephora-branded email creates a split-brain moment that a new member absolutely shouldn't have to navigate. A first-purchase discount shouldn't require channel disambiguation.
- The "SHOP NOW" CTA is doing too little work for an email that earns the click. This is a well-executed trigger with one sloppy handoff at the conversion point.

## What's working

- **Personalization is tight**: "Rosie" appears in both the email hero ("Rosie, here's a discount to start shopping") and the subject line — earned, not decorative.
- **Countdown timer is real**: With 12 hours showing, the "ENDS TODAY" banner and the live countdown align — urgency feels credible, not manufactured.
- **Code consistency**: SE3ARPH is present in three places — hero, fine print, and the barcode footer — making it impossible to lose.
- **Trigger logic is clean**: Six days from welcome to expiry nudge is the right interval. Not nagging, not late.
- **"Wondering Why You Got This?" section**: A smart addition for triggered emails; new members often forget they signed up. Reduces confusion before it causes unsubscribes.

## What's weak

- **Kohl's exclusion language is disorienting**: "Valid for kohl's/sephora below" buried in the hero fine print tells a new Sephora member this might not work at Sephora — that's a conversion killer.
- **"SHOP NOW" CTA is generic**: For an email where the offer is precisely "10% off your first purchase," the button could say "Claim My 10% Off." The current copy throws away a specificity gain the rest of the email earns.
- **No product-level urgency in the grid**: The "Recommended For You" section exists but the products don't connect to the discount. There's no "Use code at checkout for these picks" framing to bridge the offer and the grid.
- **Double asterisk on "10% OFF\*\*"** signals exclusions before the reader even processes the number — small, but it chips at trust in the hero moment.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the CTA from "SHOP NOW" to "Claim My 10% Off"** — immediate specificity lift, reinforces the offer at the conversion point.
- 2. **Clarify or cut the Kohl's channel copy** — if the offer works at Sephora.com, say only that. If it's Kohl's-only, the entire email needs a different frame. The current dual-channel note in fine print will confuse and lose first-time members.
- 3. **Tighten the subject urgency** — "ends soon" is vague when the real answer is "ends at midnight tonight." Use the specific fact.
- 4. **Bridge the product grid to the offer** — add "These picks are in your cart? Your code applies." as a section header above the recommendations.
- 5. **Remove the double asterisk from the hero number** — keep the disclosure but shift it below the CTA so "10% OFF" reads clean.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Rosie — your 10% off expires tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last call: 10% off ends at midnight, Rosie`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Code SE3ARPH · Expires today · Your first Sephora order`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `12 hours left to use your Beauty Insider welcome discount`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Missing `lang` attribute:**
```html
<html xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" ...>
```
No `lang="en"` (or locale equivalent). Screen readers cannot infer language; WCAG 2.1 §3.1.1 failure.

**`text-size-adjust: none` applied too broadly:**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  ...
}
```
Blanket suppression prevents OS-level font scaling for low-vision users. Should be scoped to the root container only, not every element.

**Single narrow breakpoint:**
```css
@media only screen and (max-width: 480px) { ... }
```
480px misses many modern viewport widths (375–430px logical width on current iPhones). No `min-width` tablet breakpoint exists. Layout may break on devices between 480px–600px.

**No dark mode media query:**
`prefers-color-scheme: dark` is absent. Emails with hard-coded `#ffffff` backgrounds invert poorly in Apple Mail dark mode and Outlook for Mac.

**`<title>` is generic:**
```html
<title>Sephora</title>
```
Some email clients render the title as a tab label or preview header. The subject-line offer is not reflected here, which is a minor missed opportunity but not a rendering defect.

**Sender domain:**
`shop@beauty.sephora.com` uses a subdomain. Deliverability depends on whether `beauty.sephora.com` has its own SPF, DKIM, and DMARC records aligned with the envelope sender. If SPF/DKIM are inherited from the root `sephora.com` policy without explicit subdomain delegation, alignment may fail. Verify with:
```

dig TXT _domainkey.beauty.sephora.com  # DKIM selector lookup
```

**`<meta name="robots" content="no index">` — minor:**
Robots meta is irrelevant inside email clients, but the value `no index` (with a space) is non-standard; the correct token is `noindex`. Not a deliverability issue, but indicates a copy-paste artifact.
