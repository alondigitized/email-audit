# Save 20%  on summer home must-haves (& more)🏡
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-11**
## Executive summary

- This is Kohl's 14th-plus promotional email in eleven days, and it's pulling a recognizable move: shift the category label (home this time) while keeping the same 20%-off framework. The home focus is actually the most defensible thing here — "summer home must-haves" is a genuine category angle, not just an excuse to rerun the apparel blast — and the loyalty-status sub-header is working harder than usual. But the email is structured around the weakest hook first: the hero leads with 20% off when the clearance section below it contains the email's actual best offer ("up to 70% off"), buried halfway down.
- The bedding/soft-home imagery is pleasant but small and indistinct. No single product earns a real hero moment. Every CTA is category-level at best, and the prior-send pattern — two emails yesterday, one on July 9, two more on July 8 — means the subscriber's inbox is already trained to skip these unless the subject line triggers something new.
- The clearance block is the strongest element in this send. Leading with it would have made a materially different email.

## What's working

- Loyalty status bar ("KOHL'S REWARDS MEMBER | Earn Kohl's Cash!") is visible above the fold and personalizes the header without being intrusive
- Clearance module ("Save up to 70%") is the highest-value offer in the send and would stop a deal-scanner mid-scroll
- Home category focus is more cohesive than the typical split Men/Women/Kids scatter-shot
- "Ends July 14" deadline is visible in the hero — gives the 20% offer at least some temporal teeth
- Render is clean — no broken images, no layout collisions

## What's weak

- Clearance (the best offer) is buried below the fold; the hero leads with the weakest discount in the email
- No single featured product with a visible price and a direct link — every CTA points at a category page
- "Save 20%" as the hero is deeply fatigued; this is effectively the same send as July 9 and July 10 with a home label swapped in
- The 50% savings block has visible qualifying copy ("select styles," coupon required) that immediately undercuts the headline number
- Cadence remains the biggest structural problem — two emails hit yesterday before this one arrived today

## Recommendations

- 1. **Flip the structure: lead with clearance, not 20% off.** "Up to 70% off" is differentiated and hasn't been the hero once in this streak. Swap the module order.
- 2. **Feature one hero product in the home section.** A specific sheet set or comforter with a before/after price ("Reg. $59.99, now $19.99 with coupon") would give subscribers a reason to click something specific.
- 3. **Rename the primary CTA.** "Shop Home Clearance" or "Shop Bedding" outperforms "Shop Now" for click intent — even minimal specificity helps.
- 4. **Pull the July 14 deadline into the subject line.** The urgency is real but invisible from the inbox.
- 5. **Hold one day.** With two sends yesterday, this email is already fighting subscriber fatigue before it opens.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Bedding, bath & storage — all on clearance now.`

## Full review
## 1. Overview

This is Kohl's 14th-plus promotional email in eleven days, and it's pulling a recognizable move: shift the category label (home this time) while keeping the same 20%-off framework. The home focus is actually the most defensible thing here — "summer home must-haves" is a genuine category angle, not just an excuse to rerun the apparel blast — and the loyalty-status sub-header is working harder than usual. But the email is structured around the weakest hook first: the hero leads with 20% off when the clearance section below it contains the email's actual best offer ("up to 70% off"), buried halfway down.

The bedding/soft-home imagery is pleasant but small and indistinct. No single product earns a real hero moment. Every CTA is category-level at best, and the prior-send pattern — two emails yesterday, one on July 9, two more on July 8 — means the subscriber's inbox is already trained to skip these unless the subject line triggers something new.

The clearance block is the strongest element in this send. Leading with it would have made a materially different email.

- Clearance module ("Save up to 70%") is the highest-value offer in the send and would stop a deal-scanner mid-scroll
- Home category focus is more cohesive than the typical split Men/Women/Kids scatter-shot
- "Ends July 14" deadline is visible in the hero — gives the 20% offer at least some temporal teeth
- Render is clean — no broken images, no layout collisions

## 3. What didn't

- Clearance (the best offer) is buried below the fold; the hero leads with the weakest discount in the email
- No single featured product with a visible price and a direct link — every CTA points at a category page
- "Save 20%" as the hero is deeply fatigued; this is effectively the same send as July 9 and July 10 with a home label swapped in
- The 50% savings block has visible qualifying copy ("select styles," coupon required) that immediately undercuts the headline number
- Cadence remains the biggest structural problem — two emails hit yesterday before this one arrived today

## 4. What I'd change

1. **Flip the structure: lead with clearance, not 20% off.** "Up to 70% off" is differentiated and hasn't been the hero once in this streak. Swap the module order.
2. **Feature one hero product in the home section.** A specific sheet set or comforter with a before/after price ("Reg. $59.99, now $19.99 with coupon") would give subscribers a reason to click something specific.
3. **Rename the primary CTA.** "Shop Home Clearance" or "Shop Bedding" outperforms "Shop Now" for click intent — even minimal specificity helps.
4. **Pull the July 14 deadline into the subject line.** The urgency is real but invisible from the inbox.
5. **Hold one day.** With two sends yesterday, this email is already fighting subscriber fatigue before it opens.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Kohl's "Save 20% on summer home must-haves" (2026-07-11)

---

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is structurally sound for a legacy HTML4 table-based layout, but has three compliance gaps (unsubscribe headers, SPF/DKIM visibility) and pervasive missing alt text on tracking pixels and content images. No broken links or merge token leaks detected.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

22 click-tracking links were skipped during HTTP probing — all route through `click.s.kohls.com` and `click.chp.kohls.com` redirect domains. This is expected behavior, but means dead-link detection had zero coverage on destination URLs this send.

Three separate tracking pixel systems are firing in parallel:
- `click.chp.kohls.com/o/...` — CoherentPath open pixel
- `mi.kohls.com/p/up/...` — MessageGears open pixel
- `click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?...` — Salesforce Marketing Cloud open pixel
- `kohls.demdex.net/event?...` — Adobe Audience Manager sync pixel

**Issue:** The `<custom>` tag after the third pixel (`</custom>`) is invalid HTML and not a recognized email client construct. It will be ignored by renderers but signals a template assembly error — a closing tag with no matching opener.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Alt text missing on 10 images** per QA — all are tracking pixels or rendered content images hosted on `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/`. Tracking pixels (`width="1" height="1"`) should carry `alt=""` and `aria-hidden="true"` (the MessageGears pixel does this correctly; the CoherentPath and SFMC pixels do not).

Content images at `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/*.png` are missing alt text entirely — this breaks screen reader accessibility and degrades the fallback experience when images are blocked (common in Outlook/corporate environments).

`user-scalable=0` in the viewport meta tag is a known accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4) — prevents users who rely on pinch-zoom on mobile.

`<title></title>` is empty. Some clients (Gmail web, iOS Mail) surface this in the tab or preview. It should be set to match the subject line or a descriptive fallback.

Google Fonts are loaded via both `<link>` and `@import` simultaneously — duplicate requests, minor performance waste, no functional impact.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unrendered merge tokens (e.g., `{{first_name}}`, `[FIRST_NAME]`, `%%first_name%%`) detected in the visible HTML fragment. The `mi_u=604230016` parameter in tracking URLs is consistent across all MessageGears pixels, indicating subscriber ID was resolved correctly at send time.

The `mi_ecmp=1017306_2026711` parameter (campaign/send ID) is also consistent. No issues found.

---

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not present** in captured headers (QA WARN). This header is required by Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender policies (enforced since Feb 2024 for senders >5k/day). Its absence here is likely an AgentMail relay artifact — the header may exist in the actual SMTP envelope but was not forwarded. Confirm against raw SMTP headers from a direct seed account.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) not present.** One-click unsubscribe via POST is required by Gmail/Yahoo policy for bulk senders. Same caveat — may be a relay gap, but needs verification.

**`Authentication-Results` header not captured** — SPF, DKIM, and DMARC pass/fail status is unknown from this relay path. The sending domain `s.kohls.com` should be verifiable independently; this is a monitoring gap, not necessarily a delivery problem.

`<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow">` and `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` are present and correct — these suppress web crawler indexing of the email archive URL and suppress referrer leakage to linked sites.

CAN-SPAM physical address was not present in the truncated HTML fragment — unable to confirm. Must appear in footer.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 22 tracked links route through `click.s.kohls.com` (SFMC) — UTM parameters, if any, would be appended at redirect resolution and are not inspectable from the source alone. No raw destination URLs are visible in the HTML to confirm UTM coverage.

The `email_name=260711_DG_Email_Saturda` parameter on several `mi.kohls.com` image URLs is truncated (255-char URL limit likely hit). This affects analytics attribution in MessageGears but not delivery.

---

| Priority | Issue | Action |
