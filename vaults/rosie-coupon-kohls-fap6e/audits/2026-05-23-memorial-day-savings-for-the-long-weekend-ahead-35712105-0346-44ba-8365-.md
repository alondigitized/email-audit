# Memorial Day savings for the long weekend ahead ➡️
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- This is Kohl's third consecutive Memorial Day send — May 21, May 22, and now May 23 — and today's email is the weakest of the three by a significant margin. The core problem is the subject line: yesterday's send led with "$10 off your $25 purchase + Memorial Day Sale?" which named the exact offer; today's drops to "Memorial Day savings for the long weekend ahead ➡️" — vague, no dollar anchor, no hook. The offer is actually identical to yesterday's, making this feel like a copy-paste with the interesting bit stripped out.
- Inside the email, the kitchen-sink structure is standard Kohl's fare: a strong hero ($10 off the $25 threshold, $5 Kohl's Cash) backed by a cascade of department discounts ranging from 20% to 70% clearance. The 70% clearance block buried near the bottom is the best single offer in the email and belongs at the top. The Memorial Day framing is surface-level — a banner slapped over a standard multi-department blast — rather than a story about the holiday weekend.
- By day three of an identical offer, the brand is burning goodwill on a message that isn't escalating. The May 19 send at least upgraded the discount narrative (15% → 20%); this one stalls. Open rates are almost certainly declining into this send.

## What's working

- **Hero offer is immediately legible**: "$10 off" and "$5 Kohl's Cash" land above the fold without hunting.
- **70% clearance block** is the sharpest single offer in this send — a genuine value signal for deal-seekers.
- **Clean render**: no broken images, no overlapping text, layout holds across the visible viewport.
- **Category nav row** (Women / Men / Beauty / Baby / Kohl's Cash) gives quick-scan shoppers a direct path.
- **Multiple deep discounts stacked** (20%, 30%, 50%, 70%) create a credible value story even if it's noisy.

## What's weak

- **Subject line is a step backwards**: removes the $10-off anchor that defined the last two sends — a concrete offer replaced with "savings."
- **Three Memorial Day emails in three days** with the same core offer destroys cadence; this one will feel like déjà vu for most subscribers.
- **70% clearance is buried** below multiple lower-discount sections — biggest offer, worst placement.
- **CTA copy is purely generic**: every button reads "Shop Now" with no category specificity even though the email has at least six distinct department sections.
- **No escalation from yesterday**: May 22's email already scored 8/10 with the same $10-off mechanic — today adds nothing new.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Surface the $10-off dollar amount in the subject** — that's the one tangible number that drove opens on May 21 and 22, and today's subject abandoned it for abstract "savings."
- 2. **Move the 70% clearance block to the hero or second position** — it's the strongest offer and it's invisible until a subscriber scrolls past four other sections.
- 3. **Give at least one CTA button a category verb**: "Shop Bedding," "Shop Clearance," "Shop Kids" — each one is a micro-segmentation signal and reduces the generic-blast feel.
- 4. **Retire this creative pattern after today**: four consecutive Memorial Day sends (May 21–24) with identical $10-off mechanics will train subscribers to ignore future sends; hold the last fire for a "last day" escalation only if the offer upgrades.
- 5. **Introduce one new reason to open**: flash a limited-quantity product, upgrade the threshold ($10 off $20?), or call out a new category that wasn't in yesterday's email.
- **Subject Alt A:** `$10 off + 70% clearance: Memorial Day weekend deals`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last weekend: $10 off, 70% clearance, + $5 Kohl's Cash`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Clearance up to 70% off — plus stack your $10 off $25 through Monday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The biggest clearance of the season ends Monday — shop 70% off now.`

## Technical Audit — Kohl's "Memorial Day savings for the long weekend ahead"

### 1. Technical Summary

Functionally standard promotional email built on HTML 4.01 transitional with VML/MSO conditionals and Google Fonts. Primary technical concerns are missing alt text on tracking pixels, unverifiable compliance headers due to relay capture gaps, and a malformed HTML tag in the open-tracking block.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**22 click-redirect links skipped** by HTTP probe — all routed through `click.s.kohls.com` and `click.chp.kohls.com`. Destination URLs and redirect integrity cannot be confirmed without live resolution.

**Three separate open-tracking pixels** are present in this email:
- `click.chp.kohls.com/o/cd85224e-...` (CoherentPath)
- `mi.kohls.com/p/up/88954bbbbcab3c0e/o.gif` (MessageGears)
- `click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?753VQ2...` (Salesforce Marketing Cloud)

Triple-pixel open tracking inflates open rate metrics by increasing the probability that at least one pixel fires, and creates attribution fragmentation across three separate platforms. This is not inherently broken but is a data integrity concern.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Malformed HTML tag** in the open-tracking block:

```html
<img src="https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?..." width="1" height="1" alt=""></custom>
```

The `</custom>` closing tag is invalid and unclosed. Most parsers will silently ignore it, but it is technically malformed and may cause unexpected behavior in strict rendering environments.

**11 images missing alt text** (all confirmed by QA):
- The three tracking pixels (`cd85224e`, `o.gif`, `kohls.demdex.net/event`) — acceptable to leave blank (`alt=""`) as they are presentational/tracking only.
- Eight content/retargeting images from `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/` lack alt text. These appear to be product or promotional images and should carry descriptive alt text for screen reader compatibility.

**`user-scalable=0`** in the viewport meta tag prevents users from pinching to zoom on mobile — a WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text) failure.

**`<title>` tag is empty.** Some email clients display the title as a tooltip or in the tab bar.

**`max-scale=1` + `user-scalable=0`** is a double restriction on zoom, compounding the accessibility concern above.

---

### 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No unresolved merge tokens or placeholder strings (`{{`, `*|`, `%%`) visible in the truncated source. Subscriber ID (`mi_u=604230016`) and campaign ID (`mi_ecmp=1013105_2026523`) are present and appear to be correctly resolved.

---

### 5. Compliance

**Physical mailing address:** Not detected in the truncated HTML. CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address. If it appears below the truncation point, no issue; if absent, this is a legal defect.

**List-Unsubscribe / List-Unsubscribe-Post headers:** Not captured by the AgentMail relay. These are transport-layer headers, not HTML — their absence in QA output does not confirm they are missing from the live send, but cannot be verified. RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe is expected by Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender requirements (>5k/day threshold).

**`<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow">`** — correctly present.

**`<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">`** — correctly present, prevents leaking subscriber tokens via referrer header on link clicks.

**Adobe Audience Manager pixel** (`kohls.demdex.net/event?d_sid=13245196`) fires on open. This is a third-party data sync; subscribers should be informed of this via privacy policy. Not a CAN-SPAM issue but relevant under CCPA/GDPR depending on list geography.

---

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

**UTM parameters:** Not visible in the truncated source. All 22 links are wrapped in `click.s.kohls.com` or `click.chp.kohls.com` redirects — UTM params may be appended at the redirect layer rather than in raw HTML. Cannot confirm without resolving redirects.

**Campaign code in pixel URLs** (`mi_ecmp=1013105_2026523`, `email_name=260523_DG_Email_Saturda[truncated]`) suggests MessageGears campaign attribution is configured. The `email_name` param appears truncated in the pixel URL — confirm the full value is not being cut off server-side.
