# Open for our bag edit
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-20**
## Executive summary

- This is Sephora's "bag edit" editorial format — a curator play built around the question "what's in our bag?" with Charlotte Tilbury as the feature anchor. The concept has legs: CT is aspirational, the GWP mascara banner gives the send an actual offer hook, and the "Spot It. Shop It." social section is a format Sephora has used well before. But the subject line "Open for our bag edit" does zero work — it's a pure open-bait prompt that gives subscribers no product, no brand, no offer reason to click through. Against a high-cadence sending week (the July 19 points event, the July 18 Grande Cosmetics launch, two sends in 48 hours), asking a subscriber to just trust the editorial concept is a hard sell.
- Below the Charlotte Tilbury hero, the email sprawls across at least five distinct sections — a product grid, "Spot It. Shop It.," Oil-Balancing Hydration, and Best of Lip — with no connective tissue between them. The bag edit premise implies editorial curation, but the layout reads more like a category digest. The free mascara offer that could drive urgency sits in a small banner above the hero rather than commanding the send.
- Compared to the July 18 Grande Cosmetics launch (8/10), this email lacks a focused hook. That send built everything around a single cultural moment. This one distributes attention across too many bets.

## What's working

- **Charlotte Tilbury as editorial anchor** — a recognizable luxury name in the "JUST IN FROM" frame is credible, aspirational, and earns the open for anyone already in the CT ecosystem
- **Free mascara GWP banner** — the most actionable element in the email; surfaces above the fold before the hero so the value proposition arrives early
- **"WHAT'S IN OUR BAG?" headline** — clean, large type, strong editorial voice; the curiosity framing works at the top of the funnel
- **"Spot It. Shop It." section** — the product-in-context device is an effective social proof format that Sephora executes well
- **Clean render, no bugs** — layout is stable with no broken images or overlapping elements visible

## What's weak

- **Subject line earns nothing** — "Open for our bag edit" names the format without giving a reason to care; no brand, no offer, no specificity — especially weak following three strong Sephora sends since July 18
- **Too many sections, no throughline** — the Charlotte Tilbury story ends at the product grid and then the email pivots to skincare hydration and lip color, leaving no editorial logic connecting the sections
- **Free mascara offer has no urgency signal** — no expiry date, no "limited quantity" language; the GWP could drive action today but gives no reason not to wait
- **No personalization hook** — a fragrance shopper and a skincare-first buyer see the same undifferentiated email despite being very different conversion targets

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the free mascara offer into the subject line** — it's the strongest concrete hook in the email and it's being wasted on a small banner above the hero. Any mention of Charlotte Tilbury OR a free product in the subject would lift opens materially.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Free Charlotte Tilbury mascara — open to claim`
- **Subject Alt B:** `What's in our bag: Charlotte Tilbury + picks we love`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Our editors' current bag, ft. new CT Pillow Talk Mascara — redeem yours now`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Bag edit ft. Charlotte Tilbury: the mascara everyone's asking about`
- 2. **Cut to one editorial throughline** — Charlotte Tilbury IS a strong bag-edit anchor on its own. Remove or defer the Oil-Balancing Hydration and Best of Lip blocks to a dedicated skincare/lip send. Every section added after the CT product grid dilutes the story and competes for the click.
- 3. **Add a deadline to the GWP offer** — "Free Charlotte Tilbury mascara through Sunday / while supplies last" converts the banner from awareness to urgency. Even vague scarcity copy ("limited availability") is better than none.
- 4. **Make "SHOP TO REDEEM" the primary CTA above the hero, not a secondary banner** — it should be the button the eye lands on first, not the scroll-past header. Right now it reads like a header advertisement rather than the email's primary call-to-action.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Zoom restriction — confirmed issue.**

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```

`maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom on iOS. WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text) prohibits suppressing user-initiated zoom. Should be removed or set to `maximum-scale=5`.

**`text-size-adjust: none` applied too broadly — confirmed issue.**

```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  -moz-text-size-adjust: none;
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```

This blanket rule overrides OS-level text scaling for visually impaired users across all block and inline elements. Standard practice targets only specific containers (e.g., `body`, `.wrapper`), not `a`, `span`, `strong`.

**`robots` meta value malformed — minor.**

```html
<meta name="robots" content="no index" />
```

`no index` (space-separated) is not a valid directive; the recognized value is `noindex`. Email clients ignore this tag, but if the email is hosted for browser viewing (view-in-browser link), web crawlers would index it. Correct to `content="noindex,nofollow"`.
