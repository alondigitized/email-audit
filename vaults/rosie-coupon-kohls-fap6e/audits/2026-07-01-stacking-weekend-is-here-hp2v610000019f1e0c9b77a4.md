# ⭐️ STACKING WEEKEND IS HERE ⭐️
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- This is Sephora's "Golden Stacking Weekend" launch email, sent on the first day of the July 4th holiday weekend. The central offer is a 30% off + 2X points combination for Sephora credit cardholders, layered over the standard Beauty Insider tier structure (Insider/VIB/Rouge tier links present). This is a well-known Sephora event format — existing loyalists know exactly what "stacking" means, and the email delivers that signal cleanly at the hero level.
- The problem is that the email keeps going — past the loyalty pitch, past the points explainer, into an app push, then into a "Just Dropped: Delectable Gummies" product feature, then a Happy 4th message, then a barcode-laden footer. It's doing four jobs where two would be enough. The hero communicates a real, valuable offer; everything below the fold dilutes it.
- After the discovery-pivot of the June 29 "something new is here" send, relaunching with a hard promotional frame makes sense cadence-wise. But the complexity of stacking math (credit card discount + tier + bonus bags + $15 off threshold) asks a lot of a reader mid-scroll. Strong for the engaged cardholder; weaker for everyone else on the list.

## What's working

- The 30% + 2X badge hierarchy at the hero is immediately scannable — you clock the offer in under two seconds.
- Tier-segmented "Shop Now" links (Insider / VIB / Rouge) feel personalized and reduce friction for members who already know their tier.
- The "Golden Stacking Weekend" campaign name is distinct enough to anchor memory across multiple sends this weekend.
- The "Just Dropped: Delectable Gummies" product feature adds editorial freshness — breaks the pure-promo monotony.
- Seasonal tie-in (4th of July Happy message, Sunday 7/6 deadline) grounds the urgency in a real-world calendar event.

## What's weak

- "STACKING" is insider jargon; casual subscribers or non-cardholders will hit cognitive friction in the first line of body copy.
- The email is extremely long — at least seven distinct content blocks before the footer — which buries the core offer with noise.
- The credit card offer occupies hero real estate, but non-cardholders are effectively seeing a promotion they can't fully access, with no on-ramp (no "apply now" CTA visible).
- The app section and product drop feel copy-pasted in from a different brief; they don't reinforce the stacking theme.
- Double star bookends in the subject (⭐️ STACKING WEEKEND IS HERE ⭐️) plus ALL CAPS reads as visually loud without adding specificity.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Tighten the offer to one line up top.** "30% off + 2X points, ends Sunday" should be the only thing in the hero. Kill the free bag and $15 tier callout — move them to a secondary row below the fold.
- 2. **Add a non-cardholder on-ramp.** Even a small "Don't have the card? Apply and use today" CTA would rescue the email for the portion of the list that doesn't qualify for the marquee offer.
- 3. **Cut the email in half.** The app push and gummies drop belong in a separate send or a "what else is new" digest — not embedded in a loyalty-stacking send.
- 4. **Make the Sunday 7/6 deadline bigger.** It's visible but not prominent. The deadline is the reason to act today; it should be as large as the percentage.
- **Subject Alt A:** `30% off + 2X points — Stacking Weekend starts now`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Stacking Weekend: 30% off ends Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cardholders get 30% off select purchases + a free Growth Bag through 7/6.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Layer your card discount with 2X points — ends Sunday 7/6.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Issue — `maximum-scale=1` blocks user zoom (WCAG 1.4.4 violation):**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
Prevents low-vision users from pinching to zoom on mobile. Remove `maximum-scale=1` or replace with `maximum-scale=5`.

**Issue — `text-size-adjust: none` applied globally:**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  -moz-text-size-adjust: none;
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```
Disables the OS-level text inflation that mobile browsers apply to improve readability. Combined with `maximum-scale=1` above, this creates a compounded accessibility barrier. Scope this only to containers where size inflation would genuinely break layout, not every element globally.

**Minor — `robots` meta value uses non-canonical spacing:**
```html
<meta name="robots" content="no index" />
```
The canonical value is `noindex` (no space). Most crawlers tolerate the space, but `no index` is not the spec-compliant token. Change to `content="noindex, nofollow"`.

**Note — VML namespaces present:**
```html
xmlns:v="urn:schemas-microsoft-com:vml"
xmlns:o="urn:schemas-microsoft-com:office:office"
```
Correct for Outlook background image support. No issue.
