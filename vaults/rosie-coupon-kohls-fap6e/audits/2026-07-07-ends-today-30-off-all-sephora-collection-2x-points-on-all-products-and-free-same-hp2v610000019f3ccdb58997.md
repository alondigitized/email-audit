# 🚨 ENDS TODAY: 30% off ALL Sephora Collection, 2X points on all products, and FREE Same-Day Delivery
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-07**
## Executive summary

- This is Sephora's fifth send in the Stacking Weekend campaign — the final-day push. By now any subscriber who was going to convert has likely already done so, and the offer (30% off Sephora Collection + 2X points + free same-day delivery) is identical to the July 3 send that scored 9/10 in brand history. The render itself is solid: the hero block is clean, the 30% badge and 2X circle dominate the fold, and the long product grid below gives browsers enough to click. What kills this email isn't the design — it's that the audience has seen this exact pitch four times already this week.
- The subject line tries to compensate for fatigue with maximum urgency (🚨 + "ENDS TODAY" + every single offer stacked into one line), but the result is a 101-character string that reads like a panic attack. On mobile, the subject gets cut to "🚨 ENDS TODAY: 30% off ALL Sephora Col…" — everything after the first offer is wasted characters. The preheader also appears to echo the offer rather than add a new hook.
- Bottom line: strong creative execution on a deeply fatigued campaign. This would have been a 9/10 had it run on July 2. On July 7 as the fifth near-identical send, the marginal uplift it generates is earned entirely by deadline urgency, not by a compelling new reason to act.

## What's working

- **Hierarchy is immediately legible** — the 30% badge and 2X circle lock the eye on the offer before any copy is read.
- **"LAST CHANCE to Earn 2X Points on These Faves!" section** breaks the scroll with a specific product grid, giving category browsers something concrete to click.
- **No render bugs visible** — images load, layout is intact, text isn't overlapping anywhere in the render.
- **Loyalty framing (2X points) is persistent** — it appears in the hero block and again in the mid-email section, reinforcing member value twice.

## What's weak

- **Subject is 101 characters** — three full offers crammed into one line; everything past "Sephora Collection" is invisible on mobile.
- **🚨 emoji + "ENDS TODAY" all-caps** is a textbook spam-signal stack; combined they increase filter risk with no meaningful urgency lift over a clean subject.
- **Fifth send of an unchanged offer** — no creative evolution, no new hook, no new product angle. Subscribers have been told this ends "today" since July 6.
- **No personalization visible** — in a loyalty-driven campaign this late in the funnel, showing a name or tailored category would have meaningfully improved click relevance.
- **Email is very long** — the fragrance and "Spot a Shop" sections below the fold dilute focus. This final-day email needed fewer categories and harder CTAs, not a full browse experience.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the subject to one offer max** — the 30% + 2X + free delivery triple-stack is unreadable in a preview pane. Lead with the single most motivating lever (the free same-day delivery is actually the freshest and most time-sensitive on July 7) and let the preheader carry the second.
- 2. **Drop the 🚨 and the all-caps** — urgency is already baked into "ENDS TODAY." The punctuation escalation reads as spam, not excitement.
- 3. **Tighten the email to two sections** — hero offer + one curated product row. Five product grids on a final-day email dilutes the singular action you want: buy now.
- 4. **Add first-name personalization** — even "Rosie, last call…" (as Sephora did on the July 5 send) would meaningfully separate this from the generic blasts.
- 5. **Preheader should add something the subject didn't say** — "Your cart's still waiting. Ships free today only." works harder than a repeat of the offer.
- **Subject Alt A:** `ENDS TODAY: 30% off Sephora Collection + free same-day delivery`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last call — 30% off + 2X points ends at midnight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Free same-day delivery disappears at midnight — shop your list now.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your points multiplier is live until midnight. Don't leave them on the table.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**[HIGH] `maximum-scale=1` blocks pinch-to-zoom on mobile**
```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1" />
```
WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize Text, Level AA) is violated by preventing user-initiated zoom. This also triggers Apple Mail accessibility warnings. Remove `maximum-scale=1`.

**[MEDIUM] `text-size-adjust: none` applied globally**
```css
div, a, tr, table, body, span, img, strong, td {
  -webkit-text-size-adjust: none;
  -ms-text-size-adjust: none;
  -moz-text-size-adjust: none;
  text-size-adjust: none;
}
```
Disabling text-size-adjust globally prevents the OS from making text legible on high-DPI small screens. Acceptable on email-specific elements but blanket application to `body` and `div` is overly aggressive.

**[LOW] `robots` meta value has a space**
```html
<meta name="robots" content="no index" />
```
The correct value is `noindex` (no space). Unlikely to matter in email clients but technically invalid. Some email-to-web-archive proxies do crawl the hosted version.

**[LOW] `<title>` is non-descriptive**
```html
<title>Sephora</title>
```
Screen readers announced to visually-impaired users in some webmail clients. Should match the subject line or campaign theme at minimum.
