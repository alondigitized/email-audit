# Memorial Day Sale ✅ $10 off your $25 purchase ✅ Kohl’s Cash ✅
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- This is Kohl's seventh email in eleven days, and the team has made a structural pivot: they've abandoned the 15-20% percentage discount (used six consecutive sends) in favor of a dollar-off mechanic — $10 off a $25 purchase. At minimum spend that's 40% off, which is a materially stronger hook than the 20% from the May 19 "Getaway styles" send. The Memorial Day peg is precisely timed (today is May 21), the hero is clean with a patriotic red palette, and there are no render bugs. This is the most actionable offer in the recent burst.
- The persistent weakness is the kitchen-sink structure: women's tops, men's wear, home goods, beauty, intimates, and 70% clearance all compete in a single long scroll. The 70% clearance banner — the single most compelling number in the email — is buried near the bottom, after multiple 30-40% modules that dilute its impact. The triple ✅✅✅ subject pattern reads like a spam template and is a step backward from the more creative emoji choices on May 19 (🧳) and May 15 (🏆).
- Bottom line: the offer mechanics are the best Kohl's has deployed in this run, the seasonal timing is right, and the email renders cleanly. But cadence fatigue is now a real liability — a subscriber who has seen six prior sends in ten days has a trained reflex to skip this one regardless of how good the deal is.

## What's working

- **Dollar-off anchors a basket**: $10 off $25 is more tangible and conversion-friendly than 15-20% — it sets a concrete spend floor and feels like a guaranteed win rather than a math problem.
- **Memorial Day hero is on-brief**: Red palette, couple in summer clothes, clean layout — no bugs, no overlapping elements, renders well.
- **Wide price-point coverage**: $7.90 tops, $9.99 items, $17.99 clothing, plus 30/40/50% off modules — visible entry points for multiple budget levels.
- **70% clearance closer is a high-intent hook**: For scrollers, the CLEARANCE 70% banner is the most action-triggering moment in the email.
- **Stacked offer is explicit in the footer**: $10 off + extra 15% + Kohl's Cash are shown together, giving members a clear compound value signal.

## What's weak

- **Triple ✅ reads like a spam template**: After the creative emoji work on May 19 (🧳) and May 15 (🏆), three sequential checkmarks in the subject is a regression — it signals bulk-send, not brand.
- **Subject is ~62 chars and truncates on mobile**: The third offer, "Kohl's Cash," gets cut on most phones. Subscribers only see the first two checkmarks.
- **70% clearance is buried**: The biggest discount in the email is positioned after multiple lower-discount modules. Anyone who doesn't scroll all the way down never sees it.
- **No explicit deadline anywhere in the body**: "Memorial Day Sale" implies urgency but no banner, badge, or copy states when the sale ends. The May 17 send had a "TODAY ONLY" badge — this one has none.
- **Cadence is at the fatigue ceiling**: Seven emails in eleven days. Even holiday-weekend sending patterns have limits; this level of frequency is measurably compressing per-send engagement.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Move the 70% clearance module up** — immediately after the hero or into the second module slot. The highest-discount number is the scroll hook; reward readers early, not at the end.
- 2. **Add a concrete deadline to the hero**: "Ends Monday night" or a countdown badge. Without it, Memorial Day urgency is ambient, not actionable.
- 3. **Fix the subject**: One ✅ maximum. The current subject looks like a mail-merge test. Trim to fit mobile.
- 4. **Synthesize the stacked offer into one headline**: Instead of listing $10 off + extra 15% + Kohl's Cash as three separate footer items, write one line: "Stack it: $10 off + 15% + Kohl's Cash — your best weekend cart."
- 5. **Hold to one send this weekend**: A single, well-timed Monday morning "last chance" send would outperform two sends diluted by subscriber fatigue.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Best deal yet — $10 off your Memorial Day cart`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Ends Monday — stack with Kohl's Cash and earn while you save`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `$7.90 tops, 70% off clearance, and a holiday weekend deal worth opening`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**22 tracking links skipped** — all click URLs route through `click.s.kohls.com` and `click.chp.kohls.com` redirect domains; the QA probe correctly deferred these and could not confirm final destination health or UTM passthrough.

**Three distinct tracking pixel vendors** are loaded at document open:
- `click.chp.kohls.com/o/...` — CoherentPath behavioral tracker
- `mi.kohls.com/p/up/...` — MessageGears open pixel
- `kohls.demdex.net/event?d_sid=13245196` — Adobe Audience Manager (AAM) segment fire
- `click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?...` — secondary open tracker (ESP-level)

This is 4 parallel open-tracking calls on render — no functional bug, but worth noting as a pixel audit surface.

**Malformed tag detected:**
```html
<img src="https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?..." width="1" height="1" alt=""></custom>
```
The stray `</custom>` closing tag has no matching open tag. Most parsers silently ignore it, but it is invalid HTML and could cause rendering edge cases in strict parsers.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**10 images missing `alt` text** — all are tracking pixels or dynamic content images served from `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/`. While tracking pixels conventionally carry `alt=""`, the dynamic content images (e.g., `2a5adc5afb647aad.png`, `f441d45d2128ae53.png`) are likely rendered content and should have descriptive alt text for screen-reader compatibility.

Confirmed instances with empty/missing alt on non-pixel images:
- `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/2a5adc5afb647aad.png?...&email_name=260521_DG_Email_Thursda`
- `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/f441d45d2128ae53.png?...&email_name=260521_DG_Email_Thursda`
- `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/c2f81e9ac8daee44.png?...&email_name=260521_DG_Email_Thursda`
- `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/415c8a18fc2950ea.png?...&email_name=260521_DG_Email_Thursda`

**`user-scalable=0` in viewport meta** — disables pinch-to-zoom, which is an accessibility violation (WCAG 1.4.4).

**`<title>` is empty** — `<title></title>` renders as a blank tab/window title, which affects screen reader announcement when the email opens in a browser view.

**Google Fonts loaded via both `<link>` and `@import`** for the same two families (Roboto, Montserrat). This is a duplicate load — double HTTP requests on render for clients that execute `<link>` tags.

### 5. Compliance

**`List-Unsubscribe` header not found** (QA WARN) — The relay did not capture this header. If it is absent at the SMTP layer, this is a deliverability risk: Gmail and Yahoo require `List-Unsubscribe` for bulk senders (>5K/day) under their 2024 sender requirements. Cannot confirm presence or absence without raw SMTP headers.

**`List-Unsubscribe-Post` header not found** (QA WARN, RFC 8058) — One-click unsubscribe (`POST` method) is required by Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender policy. Same caveat as above — relay may not have captured it.

**No physical mailing address detected in truncated source** (QA WARN) — CAN-SPAM §5(a)(5) requires a valid physical postal address in the email body. The source is truncated; the address may appear in the footer. This should be verified against the full source.

**`robots` meta tag present:** `<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow">` — correct practice for email web-view pages.

**`referrer` meta present:** `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` — suppresses referrer on outbound clicks, which will cause landing page analytics to show direct traffic rather than email-attributed traffic unless UTM params are used (see §6).

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity

All 22 outbound links pass through `click.s.kohls.com` (MessageGears click tracking). The probe could not inspect final redirect destinations or confirm UTM parameter presence on landing URLs.

**Known risk:** The `no-referrer` meta (§5 above) means HTTP `Referer` headers will be stripped on click. Any landing page attribution that falls back to referrer parsing will misattribute these sessions. UTM params on destination URLs are the only reliable attribution signal — their presence on the actual `click.s.kohls.com` destination targets is unverifiable from the truncated source.

**`mi_kc_redm_end_dte` param is empty** in at least one pixel URL:
```

```
This suggests a Kohl's Cash expiration date merge field rendered blank — likely intentional for this campaign type, but worth confirming it is not a failed personalization substitution.
