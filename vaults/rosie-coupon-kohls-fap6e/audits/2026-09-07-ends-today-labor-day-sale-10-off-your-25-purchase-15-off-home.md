# ENDS TODAY: Labor Day Sale + $10 off your $25 purchase + 15% off home ⏳
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-07**
## Executive summary

- This is the seventh near-identical "$10 off $25 + $5 Kohl's Cash" send in a week, and "ENDS TODAY" finally gives it a real reason to open — urgency that's been mostly absent from the prior six. The hero is clean: "ENDS TODAY / LABOR DAY / $10 off $25 + $5 Kohl's Cash" stacked with a big countdown-style badge, immediately followed by Women/Kids/Home category nav. That's a legitimate improvement in hierarchy over 2026-09-06's cluttered dual-coupon stack.
- The problem is everything after the fold: this is one of the longest, most sprawling grab-bag sends in the whole week's cadence — Carter's, floor care, bedding, home decor, women's clothing, Sephora beauty, luggage, rugs, athletic shoes, team apparel, clearance, and a "Picked with you in mind" product grid, all before a second coupon barcode block at the bottom. The subject's "15% off home" promise is buried roughly a third of the way down, not upfront. For a persona tuned to home/lifestyle content this is fine directionally, but the sheer number of unrelated categories dilutes any single offer's impact.
- Verdict: strongest hook of the week (genuine deadline), worst focus of the week (12+ competing offers). Trim it hard and this becomes the best send in the sequence; as-is it's a middling 6.

## What's working

- "ENDS TODAY" + Labor Day badge at the very top creates real, credible urgency — first time this week the deadline is this explicit in the hero.
- $10-off-$25 and $5-Kohl's-Cash offers are both quantified and visible without scrolling.
- Women / Kids / Home nav pills right under the hero give a fast path past the noise for a decision-ready shopper.
- Clean product photography throughout, no broken images or overlapping text anywhere in the render.
- Two coupon barcodes at the bottom (SHOP10, HOME15) make the offers actionable at checkout without hunting.

## What's weak

- Way too many category blocks (11+ distinct offers) — no single hero product or category dominates, so the eye has nowhere confident to land after the top fold.
- The "15% off home" promised in the subject doesn't appear until well past several unrelated blocks (Carter's kids clothing, floor care), breaking the subject/body contract seen in prior sends too.
- Repeats the same $10/$25 + $5 Kohl's Cash + extra-15%-home-coupon stack from 2026-09-05 and 2026-09-06 almost verbatim — a subscriber who opened either of those gets zero new information here.
- Clearance block (up to 85% off) competes directly with the Labor Day hero for attention instead of supporting it.
- "Picked with you in mind" personalization block is generic home-goods (coffee maker, towels, mattress topper, jeans) with no visible signal it's actually tailored — reads as filler, not personalization.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email to 3-4 blocks max: hero deadline banner, the promised 15%-off-home block (moved up second, right after hero), one flagship category (e.g., bedding since it's 50% off), then the coupon/barcode footer. Everything else (Carter's, Sephora, luggage, rugs, shoes, team apparel, clearance) should be a single "more deals" links row, not full image blocks.
- 2. Move the "extra 15% off home purchase" block immediately under the hero — the subject sold "home," the body should deliver it first, not fourth.
- 3. Drop the second half entirely for repeat openers of this week's prior sends (2026-09-03 through 09-06) — segment fatigue is real at this point; if this must go to everyone, at minimum vary the creative from the last three sends.
- 4. Make "Picked with you in mind" feel personalized — swap generic mattress toppers/jeans for something that ties to browsing or past purchase category, or cut the block.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last call: 15% off home + $10 off $25 ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Today only — home savings before Labor Day sale ends`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Extra 15% off bedding, decor & more with code HOME15`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Plus $5 Kohl's Cash for every $25 you spend today`

Kohl's promotional email built on the CoherentPath/Kevel (chp.kohls.com) ESP platform with standard Outlook/MSO conditional markup; automated QA shows a 43% pass rate driven by missing alt text, absent unsubscribe/authentication headers, and no detectable physical address.

- 68 tracking/redirect links (click.chp.kohls.com, click.s.kohls.com, mi.kohls.com) were skipped by the automated HTTP prober because they are click-redirect domains — destination validity is unconfirmed and should be manually verified before send.
- Multiple beacon/pixel trackers present: `click.chp.kohls.com/o/e8b61235-...` (primary open pixel), `mi.kohls.com/p/up/88954bbbbcab3c0e/o.gif`, `click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?U6GUSDPWMTQUHKPYXHAEEGAVI4.60264`, and Adobe Audience Manager beacon `kohls.demdex.net/event?d_sid=13245196`. No functional issue, but confirms multi-vendor tracking stack (CoherentPath + Adobe AAM).
- `<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow">` and `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` are present — expected for transactional/marketing email, no issue.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- 8 images flagged with missing `alt` text, including tracking pixels (`o.gif`, `event`, several `mi.kohls.com/p/rp/*.png` render-tracking pixels) and the primary click-tracking image `e8b61235-...`. Tracking pixels should carry `alt=""` (empty, not absent) with `aria-hidden="true"` for spec compliance — one pixel (`mi.kohls.com/p/up/...`) already does this correctly, but others (the `chp.kohls.com/o/...` beacon, `demdex.net/event`, and the `/p/rp/*.png` render pixels) do not.
- Markup uses legacy HTML 4.01 Transitional doctype with MSO conditional comments (`<!--[if gte mso 9]>`) and table-based layout — standard for Outlook compatibility, no issue.
- One malformed tag: `<img src="https://click.s.kohls.com/open.aspx?..." ... /></custom>` — a stray unmatched closing `</custom>` tag appears after a self-closing `<img>`. Not valid HTML; unlikely to break rendering in mail clients but should be cleaned up.
- Web font `@import`/`<link>` calls to `fonts.googleapis.com` (Roboto, Montserrat) are wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!-->` — correct pattern to prevent Outlook fallback issues.
