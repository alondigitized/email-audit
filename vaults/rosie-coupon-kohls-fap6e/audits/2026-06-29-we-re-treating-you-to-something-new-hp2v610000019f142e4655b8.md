# ▶ We’re treating you to something new
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-29**
## Executive summary

- After the bruising June 22–23 "LAST CHANCE / ENDS TODAY" sprint on Major Summer Deals, Sephora wisely pivots here to a discovery frame — "SOMETHING NEW IS HERE" — anchored by a trial-size offer for what appears to be Jane Iredale (the "3 Jane Trial Size"). That's a sensible reset: it gives over-promod subscribers a reason to open that isn't another countdown clock. The email also teases a "Stacking Weekend" with a 30% callout buried midway down, which is the stronger conversion hook and should have been the hero.
- The fundamental problem is structural overload. This email is trying to do four things simultaneously: introduce a new product trial, tease an upcoming stacking event, show off "Just Arrived" newness, and serve as a general brand catalog scroll. None of those jobs gets done cleanly because each section competes with the others. The 30% Stacking Weekend is genuinely newsworthy — it appears in a "START EARLY" module — but it's lost two-thirds of the way down a very long email rather than headlining.
- The subject line is the softest link in the chain. "We're treating you to something new" is charming but earns zero opens on its own — it neither names the product nor signals the category. Given that Sephora sent two "last chance" emails on June 23 alone, a vague softness here might actually help lift open rates out of promo fatigue, but it leaves too much to chance. This is a 6/10 effort with a buried 8/10 idea inside it.

## What's working

- **Tone reset lands correctly.** After seven straight promotional subject lines, a "new arrival" frame feels fresh and doesn't telegraph "another discount."
- **Hero headline is visually dominant.** "SOMETHING NEW IS HERE" in large type gives the eye a clear first landing point.
- **Stacking Weekend 30% callout is genuinely compelling.** An upcoming savings event framed as "start early" is a smart loyalty play when visible.
- **Sephora INSIDER branding throughout** reinforces membership value, subtly reminding the reader they belong to a tiered program.
- **Clean render — no visible broken images or layout breaks.**

## What's weak

- **The single best offer (30% Stacking Weekend) is buried below the fold.** A subscriber who doesn't scroll never sees it.
- **Subject gives nothing away.** "We're treating you to something new" could be from any brand in any category — zero purchase intent signal.
- **Hero offer is small-label and niche.** A "Jane trial size" will resonate strongly with clean-beauty loyalists but leave the broad Sephora base cold if they don't recognize the brand.
- **Too many competing modules.** Trial size + Stacking Weekend + Just Arrived + product grid = no clear single action the email is asking the reader to take.
- **No time boundary on any offer.** The Stacking Weekend presumably has a date but it isn't visible from the screenshot; "start early" with no end date is low urgency.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Flip the hierarchy — lead with Stacking Weekend, not the trial.** A 30% savings event is a bigger hook than a trial-size SKU. The trial could live as a secondary offer below the fold or in a separate send entirely.
- 2. **Put the Stacking Weekend dates in the subject and hero.** Even "July 4–6" or "This Weekend Only" transforms vague urgency into a calendar peg.
- 3. **Cut at least one email module.** Trial size, Stacking Weekend, and Just Arrived can't all be equal. Pick two; cut one to a footer mention or a separate send.
- 4. **Sharpen the CTA copy.** "SHOP NOW >" for a "new arrival" send should say "Try the Trial Set" or "Shop New Arrivals" — verb + noun, not a generic doormat.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your 30% Stacking Weekend starts now — here's your early access`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New arrivals + 30% off this weekend — shop early`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Plus: try the Jane Iredale 3-piece trial set, new to Sephora`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Stacking Weekend runs [dates] — get in before it opens to all`
