# Homepage snapshot · www.zappos.com · 2026-05-23
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- Zappos is putting on its athletic performance face right now — very runner-coded, very "chase your PR" energy — and I'm here for the 30% off but not especially for the lifestyle narrative. As someone who has had a devoted relationship with Zappos since their free-return promise basically changed my life, this homepage feels a little less like *my* Zappos and a little more like they're auditioning for a Nike audience. The bones are solid, the offer is real, but the welcome mat is laid out for someone younger and sweatier than me.

## What's working

- **The green 30% off promo banner** — large, high contrast, the HOKA Clifton 10 name-drop makes it feel curated rather than generic sitewide clearance
- **"Shop Women's / Shop Men's / Shop Kids'"** — exactly where I need it, no hunting, one tap and I'm in my lane
- **Clean, uncluttered header** — logo, search, bag, menu; nothing is fighting for attention up top and on mobile that matters
- **"Running Weather" timing** — late May is literally the moment people are dragging themselves outside; the campaign is seasonally appropriate

## What's weak

- **No free-returns or free-shipping signal above the fold** — this is ZAPPOS. That's their whole identity. I should not have to scroll or hunt to see it. For me personally, that promise is the reason I chose this URL over six competitors.
- **The hero model is performing for someone younger and more athletic** — I'm 39, I buy shoes like a hobby, I'm not chasing a PR. The aspirational framing doesn't land for me the way a "new season, new wardrobe" or "40 pairs? Still not enough" angle would.
- **No loyalty hook anywhere in this viewport** — Zappos VIP is a thing. Where is my account status, my reward points, anything that says "hey, we know you"?
- **"Get The App" takeover bar at the very top** — I'm already on your site; the first thing you show me is an ad for a different surface. Minor, but it's friction before value.
- **"UP TO 30% OFF" is a floor, not a ceiling** — the hedge language is fine, but without seeing a clear price example it still feels like a bait number.

## Recommendations

- **Put the free-shipping + free-returns promise in the top banner strip** — swap or split the "Get The App" bar with "Free overnight shipping. Free returns. Always." That's worth more than an app download prompt for a first viewport.
- **Add a returning-member ribbon or personalization nudge** — even a subtle "Hi, Solene — your VIP perks are active" or a rewards-balance chip near the cart icon would make this feel like home.
- **Swap the solo-athlete hero for a lifestyle flat-lay or multi-shoe editorial** — show four different shoes, a closet rack, something that speaks to the collector instinct; leave the performance athlete shot for the running category page.
- **Add a specific product tile with price + reviews below the hero** — the HOKA Clifton 10 is named in the banner; show me the shoe, its stars, and its sale price right there. One fewer tap to conversion.
