# Homepage snapshot · www.zappos.com · 2026-05-07
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-07**
## Executive summary

- Okay, Zappos is doing "spring sandals" right now and honestly the vibe is not terrible — breezy, no-drama, very "I'll order three pairs from my couch." The copy is casual millennial and the product photography is clean. But above the fold there's zero mention of free returns or VIP shipping, which is literally why I have a Zappos bookmark at the top of my Safari.

## What's working

- **"Slip On / Head Out" headline** — short, casual, reads in half a second on a phone. The subline "never overthink when it's 75 & sunny" is exactly the permission slip I need to justify a $140 impulse buy.
- **Multi-sandal hero image** — showing five distinct sandal styles (strappy sport, Birkenstock-look, platform slide, red strap, novelty print) signals range without making me dig through a menu. I can already tell if my vibe is there.
- **"SHOP SANDALS" CTA** — big, green, above the fold, no ambiguity. One tap to get where I'm going.
- **Top-of-page promo banner** — "Sandals for Spring. Groundbreaking —we know." The self-aware copy is charming. It earns a click just for the mild sarcasm.
- **"Summer Shoe Checklist" teaser** — just starting to peek at the bottom of the viewport, and the yellow background makes me want to scroll down to see it.

## What's weak

- **No free-returns mention anywhere above the fold** — this is Zappos's single biggest competitive weapon and it's invisible here. I buy 3 sizes and return 2; if I didn't already know the policy I'd bounce to a site that shows it upfront.
- **No VIP/loyalty hook** — Zappos VIP is real and I use it. Showing my shipping tier or rewards balance on the home screen would keep me here instead of comparison-shopping.
- **No concrete offer** — the banner is vibes-only. "Sandals for Spring" doesn't move me to tap the same way "25% off select sandals" would.
- **Product-only hero, no model** — five floating sandals on a bare background is clean but anonymous. A person actually wearing these would help me visualize fit, especially for slides and strappy styles.
- **No price anchor visible** — I have no idea if we're talking $40 Targets or $200 Birkenstocks until I tap through. Even a "starting at $X" would help.

## Recommendations

- **Add a free-returns/VIP badge to the hero** — drop "365-day free returns · Free overnight for VIP" as a two-line strip between the subheadline and the CTA. I need to see that before I commit to browsing.
- **Swap the banner copy for a real offer** — "Up to 30% off spring sandals" or even "Free 2-day shipping on every order" is more compelling than a self-congratulatory headline about sandals.
- **Add one lifestyle shot alongside the product spread** — a single candid of a woman in her 30s actually walking out the door in a pair of these would anchor the "Head Out" story.
- **Surface a loyalty/member module** — a small "You're VIP. Your next order ships overnight free" callout would make me feel seen and accelerate the add-to-cart.
