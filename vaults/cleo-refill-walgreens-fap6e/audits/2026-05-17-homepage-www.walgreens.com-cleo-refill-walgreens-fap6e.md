# Homepage snapshot · www.walgreens.com · 2026-05-17
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- Well, I'll say this — Walgreens knows their customer. The minute I open this, I see "Manage prescriptions" and "Schedule vaccinations" right there without having to dig through anything. It feels like a pharmacy website, not some fancy boutique, which suits me just fine. Right now they're pushing a sitewide sale with coupon codes, which is the kind of thing I'd clip out of the Sunday paper if I could.

## What's working

- **"Manage prescriptions" quick-tile:** This is the whole reason I come to Walgreens. Having it as a prominent tap on the homepage means I don't have to hunt through menus. Love it.
- **"Schedule vaccinations" tile:** At my age, flu shots and shingles boosters matter. Walgreens putting this front and center tells me they know who walks through their door.
- **Sitewide sale with plain-English codes:** MAY15 and MAY20 are easy to remember, and the $35/$50 thresholds are reachable on a normal pharmacy run where I'm already picking up essentials.
- **Location bar ("2331 S Atlantic Blvd…"):** My store is already showing. That's a nice touch — it knows I'm a regular.
- **Top banner coupon (WELL15):** A second offer right at the top on wellness or beauty — that's a double-dip I'd actually use when I'm buying vitamins or lotion.

## What's weak

- **Huggies and potato chips in the hero promo:** That sitewide sale imagery — diapers and snack chips — doesn't speak to me at all. I'm not buying Huggies. A vitamin bottle, a blood-pressure monitor, or even a cosmetics deal would feel more relevant.
- **No MyWalgreens points balance in view:** I earn points on everything I buy, but I can't see my balance or a "you have X points" nudge anywhere in this viewport. That's a missed loyalty hook for a daily customer like me.
- **GLP-1s for Weight Loss tile:** I understand it's a hot topic, but the clinical-sounding phrasing feels a bit abrupt without any context. Doesn't land naturally for me, though I can see why it's there.
- **Coupon codes feel like extra homework:** MAY15 and MAY20 are fine, but I'd prefer a one-tap "apply to cart" rather than memorizing a code. At 67, I don't want to retype codes at checkout.

## Recommendations

- **Swap the Huggies/chips promo visual for health & wellness staples** — vitamins, allergy meds, or a senior shopper with a basket. The sale is the same; the imagery just needs to match who's actually shopping.
- **Surface the MyWalgreens points balance in the top nav or below the account icon** — even "You have 1,240 points" in small text would make me feel seen and remind me to keep earning.
- **Add a one-tap "Clip coupon" button on the MAY15/MAY20 promo** so the discount auto-applies rather than requiring me to remember a code at checkout.
- **Move a senior health or prescription savings callout** — even a small strip like "Save on generics with MyWalgreens" — just below the utility tiles to reinforce why I chose Walgreens over CVS.
