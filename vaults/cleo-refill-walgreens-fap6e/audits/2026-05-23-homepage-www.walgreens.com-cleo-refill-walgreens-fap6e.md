# Homepage snapshot · www.walgreens.com · 2026-05-23
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- Well, this is my Walgreens — I recognize it right away with that big W. Right at the top they've got "Manage prescriptions," which is exactly why I opened this thing in the first place, so I appreciated seeing that front and center. The big pink sale banner is pushing a Memorial Day sitewide deal with those promo codes, which is fine, but the products they chose to show — Huggies diapers and a bag of potato chips — have nothing to do with someone like me.

## What's working

- **"Manage prescriptions" tile** — This is the first thing I'm looking for and it's right there, second row left, with a clear pill-bottle icon. Whoever put that above the fold understood why someone like me comes to this site.
- **"Schedule vaccinations" tile** — At 67, keeping up with shots matters to me. Nice to see that option without digging through a menu.
- **The sitewide promo banner** — "15–20% OFF" with explicit codes (MAY15, MAY20) is exactly the kind of deal I'd clip from the Sunday paper. It's clear, it stacks on my usual essentials run, and it doesn't bury the terms.
- **Location bar showing my store address** — "2331 S Atlantic Blvd" appearing at the top tells me the app knows my store. That's reassuring. I walk there; knowing it's pinned to my location is helpful.
- **Top banner repeating the code** — "20% OFF $50+ sitewide\* with code MAY20" sits right at the very top, so I see the deal before I even get to the nav.

## What's weak

- **No MyWalgreens points balance** — I earn points on everything. The homepage should greet me with "You have 3,240 points" or at least a loyalty module. I have to tap into "Account" and hunt for it. That's a missed opportunity for someone who checks points the way I check my bank balance.
- **Hero imagery shows diapers and chips** — Huggies and Kettle Backyard Barbeque potato chips are the featured products in that big pink banner. I'm not shopping for diapers. This banner feels like it was built for a 35-year-old with toddlers, not a retired woman picking up metoprolol.
- **"GLP-1s for Weight Loss" tile** — I know this is a big topic right now, but leading with it as a top-four tile feels a bit clinical and pushy. It's not what I came here for, and for my generation it can feel presumptuous.
- **No promo end date visible** — The MAY codes imply they expire end of May, but I don't see "ends May 31" anywhere. I'd feel more urgency — and more trust — if they said so plainly.
- **No free-shipping mention** — I shop online sometimes when my knees act up. The banner touts the sitewide discount but says nothing about delivery or free shipping. That's information I need to decide whether to order ahead vs. walk in.

## Recommendations

- **Add a MyWalgreens points balance chip to the top bar** — right next to "Account." Even a small "1,240 pts" would make returning customers feel recognized and would push them to spend up to the next reward tier.
- **Swap the Huggies/chips hero image for health and beauty essentials** — vitamins, a blood pressure cuff, cold-and-flu staples — products that speak to the 50+ customer who drives Walgreens' pharmacy traffic. Keep the same discount offer, just change what's pictured.
- **Add a free-shipping threshold line to the promo banner** — something like "Free shipping on orders $35+" is one sentence that could close the deal for people who can't always make the walk.
- **Put a visible "ends May 31" deadline on the MAY codes** — plain text, no countdown timer needed, just the date. It creates honest urgency without feeling manipulative.
