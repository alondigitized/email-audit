# Homepage snapshot · www.walgreens.com · 2026-05-14
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-14**
## Executive summary

- Well, I know this store like the back of my hand — I walk there three mornings a week for my prescriptions. Opening the website, I can see right away they put "Manage prescriptions" front and center, which I appreciate. But then they slap a big hero on me full of Pampers diapers and GLP-1 supplements like I'm a new mother or a weight-loss influencer. The codes and percentage-off math are a little fussy for my taste, but the bones here are familiar enough that I wouldn't slam the door.

## What's working

- **"Manage prescriptions" quick-action tile** — it's the very first thing I see after the header, big enough to tap with reading glasses on. That's my whole reason for being here.
- **"Schedule vaccinations" tile** — at my age I keep up with every shot the doctor recommends; having that one tap away feels genuinely useful.
- **Store address already shown** — "2331 S Atlantic Blvd" is right under the header, which tells me the app already knows my store. That saves me fussing around.
- **Top banner promo** — "15% OFF $35+ on select wellness or beauty with code WELL15" is readable and specific, not just a vague "savings await you" teaser.
- **15–20% OFF wellness promo block** — two tiers of savings, both codes spelled out in big text. I can copy that code before I head out the door.

## What's weak

- **Pampers Swaddlers in the hero** — I love babies, but that's not what I came for. A giant package of Swaddlers in the main promo image tells me this page isn't talking to me.
- **No MyWalgreens points balance** — I earn points on everything I buy. Not seeing my balance anywhere is like opening my mailbox and finding no Sunday circular. It's the whole reason I'm loyal.
- **GLP-1s tile framing** — "GLP-1s for Weight Loss" right there in the shortcuts grid feels more like a diet-clinic ad than a pharmacy feature. A 67-year-old taking GLP-1 medications is usually doing so under a doctor's order for diabetes, not a weight-loss trend. The copy feels off.
- **No end date on the promo** — "15–20% OFF" is nice, but when does it expire? Without a deadline I'll put it in my pocket and forget about it until it's gone.
- **Huggies teaser at the bottom** — again, baby products starting to dominate. The bottom of the screen already shows a Huggies deal before I've even scrolled once.

## Recommendations

- **Swap Pampers out of the primary hero** — replace with a wellness or over-the-counter health product (vitamins, pain relief, blood pressure monitors) that speaks to the 55+ customer who fills prescriptions weekly.
- **Surface the MyWalgreens points balance in the header** — right next to "Account" or below the store address: "You have 1,240 points." One line. It's the hook that keeps regulars coming back.
- **Add a promo expiration date** — even "Offer ends Sunday, 5/18" gives me a reason to act now instead of maybe later.
- **Rename or reframe the GLP-1 tile** — "GLP-1 Medications" or "Specialty Rx" reads as pharmacy, not diet culture, and covers more of the actual patient population tapping that button.
