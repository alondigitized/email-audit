# Better sleep for busy summers.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-27**
## Executive summary

- Casper keeps the "Back to School sale" (25% off) running from July 22, but wraps it in an entirely new creative concept this time: a "day in the life of summer" structure — after the concert, after the road trip, after the beach, after a day of play — each pointing to a different product category. It's a smart way to make one flat discount feel like four different relevant offers instead of a wall of percentage badges, and it's a nice evolution from the "Hot sleeper?" persona-hook that worked well on July 18.
- The execution has a real disconnect, though: the hero image shows a grey-haired couple laughing on a couch, which reads as 55-65, but two sections down "After a full day of play" pushes a crib/bassinet (Snug™) at parents of infants. Those are not the same household, and pairing empty-nester hero art with a baby-product pitch undercuts the persona targeting the copy is otherwise doing well.
- This is a solid, well-organized email let down by a demographic mismatch and a very long single-scroll structure (six stacked sections, no anchor nav) that buries the beach and kids' sections below several screens of scrolling. Call it a 7 — good creative reset, needs tighter targeting.

## What's working

- The "after [activity]" framing (concert, road trip, beach, day of play) gives the flat 25% offer four distinct, relevant hooks instead of one generic banner.
- Every product tile has a clear "SAVE X%" badge, product name, one-line benefit, and a "Shop now" link — consistent and scannable.
- Hero CTA ("Shop sleep essentials now") and top banner CTA ("Shop the sale") are both large, high-contrast orange buttons that stand out against the navy and white backgrounds.
- No visible render bugs — images load cleanly, text doesn't overlap, layout bands are crisp.

## What's weak

- Hero imagery (older couple) doesn't match the "Snug" bassinet section aimed at new parents — the email is targeting two different life stages without acknowledging it.
- Six full sections stacked vertically means the beach and kids' offers require heavy scrolling on mobile — no jump links or section teaser at top.
- "Save up to 25%" is repeated as the sole banner offer, but individual tiles show 20%, 25%, or 100 off — the top banner overpromises relative to what most tiles actually deliver (mostly 20%).
- Fine print ("*Based on Comp. Value") is present but genuinely tiny and easy to miss under the top offer — borderline on the "honest offer" signal.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Split the audience.** Pull the Snug/baby section into a separate send to new-parent segments and keep this email's hero/couple imagery consistent with an all-adult occasion set (concert, road trip, beach) — this is the single biggest lift since it fixes the persona mismatch driving down both trust and click relevance.
- 2. **Add a top-of-email jump strip** ("Mattresses | Pillows | Bedding | Kids") so recipients can skip to their category instead of scrolling six sections — this is already present at the *bottom* of the email, just move a version of it up top.
- 3. **Reconcile the headline percentage with tile reality** — either lead with "Save 20%" to match most tiles, or bump the lower tiles to 25% so "up to 25%" doesn't feel like bait on 80% of the products shown.
- 4. **Tighten the fine print visibility** — bump the asterisk disclosure text size slightly so the comp-value caveat isn't functionally invisible next to a bold 25%.

- **Subject Alt B:** `One sale, every summer moment.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Concerts, road trips, beach days — sleep better after all of them.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `20-25% off mattresses, pillows, bedding & more.`
