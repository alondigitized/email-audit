# Save up to 35% on Casper favorites.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-04**
## Executive summary

- This is Casper's thirteenth-or-so email in three weeks, and the subject line finally abandons "Memorial Day" in favor of the softer "Casper favorites" — a quiet admission that the holiday anchor is expired. The problem is that the email body never got the memo: the hero still reads "Memorial Day Sale" in large type, nine days after the holiday itself. That disconnect will land badly with anyone who has been opening these. The June 1 "summer reset" send tried the same pivot and landed at 7/10; this send partially reverts it.
- The product grid is actually the best creative choice Casper has made in this campaign. Category-specific savings (20% foam, 30% hybrid, $100 off Snug™, 35% bundles) give a subscriber real information to act on rather than the generic "up to 35% off" hero that's been running for weeks. The subhead copy — "everything you need to sleep through the heat" — is a genuinely good seasonal frame that's being wasted underneath a stale holiday headline.
- The email is technically clean, the layout works, and the offer is real. The execution is just careless: expired holiday branding, no deadline, no social proof (the May 28 send had a strong reviews section that disappeared entirely), and a subject/hero mismatch that signals a brand unsure of its own campaign.

## What's working

- Category-specific savings in the grid (20%, 30%, $100 off, 35%) give the subscriber something concrete to compare rather than a blanket "up to X% off"
- "Everything you need to sleep through the heat" is a timely, seasonal subhead that does actual work — just buried under the wrong headline
- Clean render with no broken images, no text overlap, no visible layout failures
- The Snug™ Mattress tile calls out a specific product with a specific dollar-off figure — the most actionable tile in the grid
- "Shop now" CTAs under each category tile let a subscriber skip directly to their category without hunting

## What's weak

- Hero says "Memorial Day Sale" on June 4 — the holiday was May 26; this reads as lazy, not just late
- Subject line says "Casper favorites" but body says "Memorial Day Sale" — the two frames contradict each other in a way a repeat subscriber will notice immediately
- No deadline anywhere — the offer floats without urgency, which hurts click motivation on a catalog-style email like this
- No social proof — the May 28 send's "Don't just take our word for it" review block was this campaign's strongest element; dropping it here is a visible regression
- Nine product tiles is a lot; the catalog feel dilutes urgency and makes the email feel like a clearance dump rather than a curated send

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill "Memorial Day Sale" from the hero.** Replace with "Summer Sleep Sale" or "Our biggest sleep savings" — the subhead copy ("sleep through the heat") already points in this direction; the hero just needs to follow it. This single fix removes the credibility hit.
- 2. **Add a deadline.** "Ends Sunday, June 8" in the hero subhead or the CTA button costs nothing and would materially lift clicks on a no-urgency catalog send.
- 3. **Bring back the review block.** The May 28 "Don't just take our word for it" section with product-specific star ratings was the single best creative element of this campaign. Its absence is felt.
- 4. **Resolve the subject/body mismatch.** If the subject is "Casper favorites," the hero should say something like "Our most-loved products, on sale." The two surfaces should tell the same story.
- 5. **Trim the grid to 6 tiles.** Lead with the three highest-discount categories (Bundles at 35%, Hybrid at 30%, Max at 30%), then let the remaining tiles follow smaller. Fewer choices, clearer hierarchy.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Up to 35% off — our summer sleep favorites`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Sleep through the heat: save up to 35%`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Foam, hybrid, bundles, pillows — every category, one sale. Ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Save $100 on the Snug™ and up to 35% on everything else.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

The tracking pixel URL encodes a protobuf payload. The base64 segment `ZnJlcXVlbmN5X3NlZ21lbnQSEkNvcmUgU2xlZXBlcnMgVGVzdA==` decodes to include `frequency_segment` → `Core Sleepers Test`. ```


**External font stylesheets will not load in most clients**
Two `<link>` stylesheet references point to `brxcdn.com` (Beefree CDN). Gmail, Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo strip `<link>` tags in the `<head>`. These fonts will silently fall back to system fonts with no error.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Link affordance suppressed in Gmail**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none; }
```
This selector targets Gmail's wrapper and removes all visual link cues (underline, color). Sighted users relying on link affordance will not see clickable elements; screen reader users are unaffected. Intentional or not, it removes a standard accessibility signal.

**Tracking pixel has empty `alt`** — acceptable; `alt=""` on a 1×1 tracking pixel is correct and suppresses VoiceOver announcement.

**MSO/VML conditionals present** — correct; `PixelsPerInch`, `AllowPNG`, and VML namespace declarations are all in place for Outlook rendering.

**`lang="en"` on `<html>`** — present and correct.

**Preheader padding technique** — dual-hidden approach (invisible `<span>` + zero-width-space `<div>`) is technically valid but adds ~4 KB of character padding. Some aggressive spam filters penalize high ratio of invisible to visible content.
