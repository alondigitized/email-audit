# Memorial Day Exclusive: Save an EXTRA 5% on customer-favorites.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-18**
## Executive summary

- This is Casper's third consecutive daily email — welcome on 5/16, stacked Memorial Day savings on 5/17, and now a near-identical "extra 5%" framing on 5/18. The creative is clean and the social proof section ("Rave reviews for Casper snooze") is the strongest element, surfacing four distinct products with real quote snippets. But the offer is functionally the same as yesterday's send, and the hero still carries an "EXCLUSIVE WELCOME OFFER" badge that has now run three days in a row — which is the opposite of exclusive.
- The multi-product layout (Dream Mac, Original Pillow, HyperElite Sheets, Glow Light) shows catalog depth but diffuses focus. There's no single hero product to anchor the click. The educational Sleep Tip #3 block is a nice brand-building beat, but it's buried too deep to matter on a promotional send.
- The email is competent but fatigued. A subscriber who opened yesterday's "Save an extra 5% on mattresses" is being handed essentially the same pitch in a different wrapper. Without a new angle or a harder deadline, the incremental lift is low.

## What's working

- **Social proof is prominent and specific** — the "Rave reviews for Casper snooze" section leads with named reviewers and quote snippets, not vague star counts.
- **Product-level CTAs are clear** — "Shop mattresses," "Shop pillows" are better than a generic homepage link.
- **Visual hierarchy holds** — the offer banner sits at the very top, eye tracks naturally through the hero to the reviews grid.
- **Catalog breadth on display** — four distinct product categories (mattress, pillow, sheets, lamp) signal that this isn't a one-SKU brand.
- **No render bugs** — layout is clean, images load, no broken elements visible.

## What's weak

- **Same offer as yesterday** — 5/17 said "Save an extra 5% on mattresses + up to 35% savings"; 5/18 says the same thing with "customer-favorites" swapped in. No new reason to act.
- **"EXCLUSIVE WELCOME OFFER" badge is stale** — day three of a welcome offer is not a welcome offer. It signals automation over relevance.
- **Hero CTA is generic** — "Shop now" is the weakest possible verb-noun combo when product-specific CTAs are already present lower in the email.
- **No hard deadline visible** — Memorial Day is implied but no "offer ends Monday" or countdown creates actual urgency.
- **Three emails in three days is aggressive** — cadence is burning goodwill fast for a brand whose welcome email promised a curated relationship.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a visible deadline.** "Offer ends Monday, May 26" in the hero sub-copy is a one-line change that turns implied urgency into real urgency. This is the single highest-leverage edit.
- 2. **Retire the "EXCLUSIVE WELCOME OFFER" badge or rotate it to "Memorial Day Exclusive."** The welcome framing expired on day one.
- 3. **Pick one hero product and go deep.** Feature the Dream Mac with a specific price point or "starting at $X with your extra 5%" instead of routing everyone to a category page.
- 4. **Rewrite the hero CTA** from "Shop now" to "Shop mattresses — extra 5% off" or match the sub-section label exactly.
- 5. **Pull back cadence to every other day** through the Memorial Day window. Three consecutive sends with the same offer trains unsubscribe behavior.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Memorial Day ends Monday — your extra 5% is waiting`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your exclusive 5% is still on. But not for long.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Stacked savings on mattresses, pillows, sheets — through Memorial Day weekend only.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Dream Mac, Original Pillow, HyperElite Sheets — all at your member price.`

### 1. Technical Summary

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Open pixel uses `display:none` — rendering risk**
```html
<img width="1" height="1" alt="" src="https://cdn.us1.exponea.com/casper-prod/e/.../open" style="display: none;">
```
`display:none` is blocked or ignored by several clients (notably iOS Mail with MPP, some Outlook builds). The 1×1 dimensions alone are the standard approach; adding `display:none` on top is redundant and can suppress the load in clients that respect it. Remove `display:none` and rely solely on `width="1" height="1"`.

**CTA link tracking: cannot assess** — HTML is truncated before any `<a href>` CTAs appear. Verify all destination URLs carry `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, and `utm_content` parameters.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Link color/decoration stripped globally**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none; }
```
This selector applies in older Android Gmail and some Samsung clients. Any in-body links will inherit surrounding text color with no underline, making them visually indistinguishable from body copy. Scope this rule more narrowly (e.g., to the header logo or image-linked areas) rather than applying globally.

**External font stylesheets — single point of failure**
```html
<link href="https://brxcdn.com/us1-app-storage/5a801692.../bcace13e..." rel="stylesheet">
<link href="https://brxcdn.com/us1-app-storage/5a801692.../491c165a..." rel="stylesheet">
```
Both custom fonts are loaded from `brxcdn.com` (Bloomreach CDN). If this CDN is degraded, there is no declared `font-family` fallback stack visible in the `<head>` — rendering falls back to the client's default serif/sans. Confirm the Bloomreach-hosted CSS files include a web-safe fallback stack.
