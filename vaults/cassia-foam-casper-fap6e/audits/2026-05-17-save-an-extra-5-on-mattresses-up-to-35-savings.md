# Save an extra 5% on mattresses + up to 35% savings.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- Casper pivots fast — yesterday was the warm welcome email, today is a full-court promotional press. The offer structure is actually smart: stacking an "extra 5%" on top of Memorial Day savings creates a genuine layered value proposition that rewards the member who just signed up. The hero lands that message clearly with a lifestyle image and a visible blue CTA.
- The problem is everything below the fold. Seven mattress models — Wave Foam, Cloud One, One, Dream, Dream Max, Snow, Snow Max — are dumped into a scrolling product grid, each with its own "Shop Now" button. That's not a product recommendation email; it's a catalog. When everything competes, nothing wins. The recipient most likely to convert is the one who doesn't know which mattress to buy yet, and this email doesn't help them choose — it just restates the lineup.
- One day after a welcome email is an aggressive cadence, and the "find your match" framing doesn't do enough to soften that. This is a decent promotional send, not a great one.

## What's working

- **Stacked offer is clear in the hero** — "extra 5% on everything on top of Memorial Day savings" is immediately legible and feels like a real member perk.
- **Blue Shop Now button in the hero has strong contrast** — no ambiguity about where to click above the fold.
- **Memorial Day timing is on-brand** — May 17 send is well within the holiday's promotional window, not stale.
- **Lifestyle hero image (couple in bed)** reinforces the aspirational framing without being cheesy.
- **Clean layout, no visible render bugs** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum visible.

## What's weak

- **Seven competing hero products with no editorial hierarchy** — the grid treats Cloud One and Snow Max as equals; a real subscriber has no guidance on where to start.
- **"Shop Now" on every product is undifferentiated** — generic verb, no noun, no product-specific pull ("Try the Dream" beats "Shop Now" every time).
- **The Sleep Tip #1 section is an awkward tonal shift** mid-promotional email — educational content this deep in a conversion-focused send kills momentum.
- **No explicit deadline for the Memorial Day offer** — "Memorial Day savings" implies urgency but doesn't close it with a hard date, which lets readers defer.
- **Second email in 24 hours without name personalization** — coming off a welcome email, this feels like a list blast, not a member communication.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse the product grid to 2–3 curated picks** with an explicit recommendation frame ("If you're new to Casper, start here"). Remove or link-out the full catalog.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline** — "Offer ends May 26" turns the Memorial Day framing from ambient to actionable.
- 3. **Rewrite every "Shop Now" button to be product-specific** — "Try the Dream," "Build Your Snow," "Start with the One."
- 4. **Cut the Sleep Tip section entirely** — save it for a nurture email. In a promo send, it's a conversion leak.
- 5. **Lead the preview text with the stacked-savings hook**, not a repeat of the subject.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Members: stack your extra 5% on Memorial Day deals`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 35% off + your member bonus — ends May 26`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `New to Casper? Here's where most people start.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your welcome discount + Memorial Day savings — both active now.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**DEFECT — Open tracking pixel suppressed by `display:none`**

```html
<img width="1" height="1" alt="" src="https://cdn.us1.exponea.com/casper-prod/e/CgxqC..." style="display: none;">
```

The pixel has `display: none` applied inline. Gmail, Apple Mail, and Outlook will not load images hidden this way — open rate data for this send will be artificially suppressed. Standard practice is a 1×1 pixel with no display override (just `width="1" height="1"`).

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**DEFECT — External web fonts loaded via `<link>` (stripped by major clients)**

```html
<!--[if !mso]><!-->
```

The `<!--[if !mso]><!-->` guard correctly exempts Outlook, but Gmail (web and Android), Yahoo Mail, and Samsung Mail strip `<link>` tags from `<head>` entirely. If the inline text elements lack a declared `font-family` fallback stack, those clients will render system-default fonts. Fallback stack presence cannot be confirmed from the truncated source — requires full HTML.
