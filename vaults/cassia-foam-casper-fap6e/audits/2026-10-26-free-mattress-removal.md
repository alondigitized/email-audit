# Free mattress removal*
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-26**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, single-idea Casper send. The hook is free mattress removal, a "$150 value", and the hero line "You relax, we remove." is a good one. The offer is easy to grasp, and the "Use code: REMOVAL" badge and the "Upgrade your mattress" button sit high in the hero.
- The body weakens it. Four near-identical product cards (The One, Cloud One, Snow, Dream Max) each carry a "Shop now" button and a King-size price. That is a catalog, not a pitch. The removal offer shows up only in the hero and is never repeated near a product. The code requirement, the UPS Ground + LoadUp shipping condition and the asterisks add friction, and the real terms are in the grey fine print at the bottom.
- It is a solid, competent send, but it is a utility offer where Casper's recent sends, such as "Pay for a Queen, sleep on a King.", had a sharper hook. Cadence is also heavy. This is the 6th or 7th Casper send in two weeks, and it follows the Friends & Family sales that ran from 10/15 through 10/21.

## What's working

- "You relax, we remove." is a clear, friendly hero line, and "$150 value" quantifies the benefit.
- The code badge ("REMOVAL") and the primary button sit together in the hero.
- Each product card has a short descriptor (for example "Responsive, breathable, medium-firm") and a was/now King price, so the savings are concrete.
- The light-blue layout is consistent and easy to scan, and every image renders cleanly.
- The footer has useful secondary paths: the mattress quiz, the store finder, and the SMS signup.

## What's weak

- The removal offer is never repeated near the products, so the cards read as a plain price list.
- Four equal cards with four identical "Shop now" buttons give no hero product and no guidance on which to pick.
- The prices are King only, with no starting price, and the "$150 value" is not tied to a price or percent discount.
- The terms are buried. The asterisks, the "limited time†" language, the shipping-method condition and the "select" qualifier appear only in the tiny grey footer text.
- The strikethrough and savings ("King was $1,649, now it's $1,149") are in small text, and there is no reviews or social proof anywhere.
- The email is long, and the user has to scroll a long way before reaching the footer links.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the four cards to two. Lead with the bestseller (The One) and add a "Which mattress is right for you?" quiz button for everyone else. Repeat "+ free removal" on each card.
- 2. Give the hero button a specific label, such as "Shop mattresses + free removal", and state the end date next to the code instead of "limited time".
- 3. Add social proof, such as a star rating or review count on the lead product.
- 4. Move the key conditions (code required, UPS Ground + LoadUp at checkout) into a one-line visible note under the hero.
- 5. Consider a quick "How removal works" strip: order, schedule, we haul it away.
- **Subject Alt A:** `We'll haul your old mattress away, free`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Free $150 mattress removal ends soon`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Use code REMOVAL at checkout. We pick up the old one, you sleep on the new one.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Casper mattresses from $1,149, with pickup of your old one on us.`

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Probe coverage is partial.** 12 material links were probed and 18 were skipped, so the 18 skipped links are unverified. The unsubscribe link is always probed, but its result isn't in the data provided.
- **Plain-text part is mostly URLs.** The QA check reports `16978/19532 chars are URLs` (about 87%). This points to long tracking-wrapped URLs in the text alternative, and the plain-text part is likely auto-generated. Long redirect URLs in the text part raise the risk of spam-filter scoring and are unreadable for text-only readers.
- **Open-tracking pixel:** `https://cdn.us1.exponea.com/casper-prod/e/.../open` is a 1×1 image with `style="display: none;"`. Some clients skip `display:none` images, which would undercount opens. Apple Mail Privacy Protection will inflate them.
- **Stylesheet links:** two `<link rel="stylesheet">` tags point to `brxcdn.com/.../media/original/...`. They are wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!-->`, so Outlook skips them. Gmail and some other clients strip `<link>` tags, so font fallbacks should be specified in the inline styles. This can't be confirmed from the truncated source.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **QA "Image missing alt text" is a false positive.** The flagged image is the Exponea pixel, and its source shows `alt=""` with `width="1" height="1"`. That is correct for a decorative or tracking image.

- **Preheader:** there is a `<span>` with `mso-hide:all` and `display:none`, plus a padding block (`&#8199;&#847;` and `&shy;` repeats). This is a standard pattern and works as intended.
- **Possible link-styling override:** `#MessageViewBody a{color:inherit;text-decoration:none}` removes link color and underline in the clients that wrap the body in that ID. Inline text links could then lose their visual distinction there. Whether any are affected depends on markup beyond the truncation, so this is a low-confidence note.
