# Homepage snapshot · casper.com · 2026-06-09
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-06-09**
## Executive summary

- I landed on casper.com and the first thing that hijacked my whole screen was a cookie consent modal — so I can barely tell you what they're selling right now. What I *can* see: a sitewide discount banner up top ("Save up to 25% sitewide"), a clean nav with Mattresses, Pillows, Furniture, and a "Take the Quiz" shortcut, and what looks like the edge of a pink hero image underneath the modal wall. Casper presents as a modern, direct-to-consumer sleep brand, but this consent gate is a conversion killer before I've seen a single product.

## What's working

- **"Save up to 25% sitewide" banner** — that top strip is exactly the kind of signal that keeps me on the page. I came here for the markdown and it's confirmed in the first pixel.
- **Category nav chips (Mattresses, Pillows, Furniture, Take the Quiz)** — four clean tap targets, immediately scannable. I know exactly where to go once this modal is dismissed.
- **"Take the Quiz" shortcut** — smart inclusion. As someone who bought the Original but might be upgrading or buying for a guest room, a quiz funnel is a soft re-entry point.

## What's weak

- **Full-screen cookie consent modal** — this isn't a subtle banner, it's a three-button wall that buries the entire hero. Before I've seen one product image or sentence of brand copy, I'm being asked to make a data-privacy decision. That friction is real and it's the dominant first impression.
- **Hero completely obscured** — I can see a sliver of a pinkish background with a "+" symbol. That's it. Whatever the campaign is, it's invisible until I deal with the modal.
- **No returning-shopper recognition** — I own a Casper mattress. There's no member login prompt, no "Welcome back" moment, no loyalty hook. I'm being treated like a cold stranger.
- **"Save up to 25% sitewide" is vague** — "up to" is classic bait language. No product callout, no floor price, no threshold. For a holiday-markdown buyer like me, I want specifics.

## Recommendations

- **Shrink the consent modal to a bottom banner** — put "Accept" and "Manage Preferences" in a slim strip, not a modal that eats 80% of the viewport. The hero needs to be visible immediately.
- **Make the offer specific in the top banner** — change "Save up to 25% sitewide" to something like "Mattresses from $XXX — 25% off through June 15" so markdown buyers like me have a reason to stay.
- **Add a returning-customer login hook in the nav** — a small "Sign in / rewards" link next to the cart icon would immediately signal that I'm known, and it costs nothing visually.
- **Surface the 100-night trial in the visible nav or sub-banner** — that trial is the brand's most powerful trust signal for people who already own one; it reassures me on an upgrade purchase.
