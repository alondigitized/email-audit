# Save up to 30% on your mattress match.*
**Score:** 10/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-16**
## Executive summary

- This is a competent, well-structured send from Casper — but subscribers who paid attention to the last two weeks will clock the discount regression immediately: Memorial Day and the June 10-14 July 4th early-access window both led with 35%, and this email quietly walks that back to 30%. The "ends tonight" VIP urgency hook that opened this campaign on June 10 has now appeared in multiple sends; it's starting to feel like the alarm clock no one turns off. The "mattress match" subject-line hook is the smartest creative choice here — it ties the shopping grid to the quiz CTA at the bottom and gives the whole email a coherent narrative spine.
- The product grid is genuinely useful. Sleep-type callouts ("perfect for back & stomach sleepers," "perfect for hot sleepers") do real targeting work that most bedding emails skip. And the tiered structure — Everyday Comfort (20% off) → Support/Breathability (30%) → Max Comfort (30%) → Snug kids (flat $100) — is a legitimate upsell architecture. The problem is the email rewards browsers, not deciders. There's no social proof anywhere, and the quiz CTA appears only after six product sections, well below the fold.
- Net: a solid B-tier promotional send that's hamstrung by a weaker offer than the prior run and an urgency mechanism that has cried wolf too many times this month.

## What's working

- "Your VIP access ends tonight" in the hero adds genuine deadline pressure and personal framing above the fold.
- Sleep-type callouts under each product ("perfect for hot sleepers," "combination sleepers and couples") do real self-selection work.
- Tiered discount architecture (Everyday 20%, Hybrid 30%, Max 30%) creates a natural upsell path.
- "Mattress match" copy in the subject ties back to the quiz CTA — gives the email a throughline beyond a flat promotional grid.
- Visual hierarchy is clean: hero offer lands first, products second, soft quiz CTA third.

## What's weak

- 30% ceiling is a visible step-down from the 35% Casper ran through June 7-14; deal-savvy subscribers will notice and may wait for a better offer.
- "Ends tonight" urgency has appeared across multiple sends in this campaign window — credibility is eroding with each use.
- No social proof (star ratings, review counts) anywhere in the scroll; competitor sends at this price point almost always include it.
- The "mattress match" framing in the subject sets up quiz expectations, but the email leads with a six-product shopping grid — the quiz only appears at the very bottom, below the fold.
- Five Casper emails in eight days is aggressive; subscriber fatigue is a real cost that won't show in open rate until the next send.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Restore the offer or reframe it.** 30% < 35% is the first thing a deal-tracking subscriber notices. Either match Memorial Day pricing or explicitly position this as a "different" sale (e.g., "our biggest bundle deal, not just a percentage off") to justify the perceived step-down.
- 2. **Put the quiz above the fold.** If "mattress match" is in the subject, make the first CTA the quiz — then let the quiz result anchor the product section below. Right now the subject promise is buried five sections deep.
- 3. **Add review counts under hero products.** Even a single line "★ 4.8 · 12,400 reviews" under The One and the Dream would lift click confidence materially.
- 4. **Fix "Take An Mattress Quiz" → "Find Your Mattress Match →"** — corrects the grammar, echoes the subject-line copy, and removes the word "quiz" which implies friction.
- 5. **Cap cadence.** June 10 → 12 → 14 → 16 is unsustainable. Batch the next two sends into one with a genuine mid-campaign refresh.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Which Casper is yours? Up to 30% off tonight.*`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `VIP deal expires midnight — find your match and save.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The One, the Dream, the Snow — all on sale until midnight.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Open-tracking pixel — `display:none` suppression risk**
```html
<img width="1" height="1" alt="" src="https://cdn.us1.exponea.com/casper-prod/e/CgxqCN..." style="display: none;">
```
`display:none` is applied to the open-tracking pixel. While most email clients still fire the HTTP request for hidden images, Apple Mail Privacy Protection and certain proxy-caching clients pre-fetch all images regardless of display state, making this moot in practice. However, a small subset of clients (older Lotus Notes, some corporate proxies) suppress hidden-image loads entirely, potentially undercounting opens. Standard practice is `width="1" height="1"` without `display:none`.

**Encoded subscriber data in pixel URL**
The tracking URL embeds what appears to be a base64-encoded payload (`CgxqCN4759egjazxb5gSIA5sdTrhoo7gK...`) containing subscriber segment data (`CoreSleepersTest`, frequency segment). ### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Link underline suppression**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none; }
```
This global rule strips underlines from all anchor tags in Gmail's web client. WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.1 requires links to be distinguishable from surrounding text by more than color alone. If CTA buttons are image-based or have strong visual treatment this is lower risk, but any inline text links in the body will be invisible to color-blind users.

**External font CDN dependency**
Two stylesheets are loaded from `brxcdn.com`:
```
https://brxcdn.com/us1-app-storage/5a801692-033d-11ef-8779-ca524c9ef7ea/media/original/9c4a4f1a-...
https://brxcdn.com/us1-app-storage/5a801692-033d-11ef-8779-ca524c9ef7ea/media/original/9c30fa4c-...
```
No `font-family` fallback stack is declared in the inline `<style>` block. If `brxcdn.com` is unreachable or requests are blocked by a corporate firewall, the email will fall back to the client's default serif/sans — which may break layout metrics if the font has significantly different metrics than the fallback.

**Preheader padding payload**
The invisible-character block (130+ `&#8199;&#847;` pairs + 200+ `&shy;` entities) is a legitimate anti-bleed technique but adds ~2.5 KB of markup for no user-facing value. Some aggressive spam filters penalize unusually high invisible-character ratios. Consider trimming to 50–80 pairs, which is sufficient to push Gmail's preheader cutoff.
