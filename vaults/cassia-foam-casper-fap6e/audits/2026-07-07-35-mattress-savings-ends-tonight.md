# 35% mattress savings ends tonight.
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-07**
## Executive summary

- This is Casper's eighth consecutive July promotional email — and its second on July 7, following the Max-specific "35% savings on Maxes ends tonight" that scored 8/10 earlier today. Where that email was correctly focused on a single product line, this one pulls back out to the full mattress catalog for a closing-night sweep. That is a rational strategy: catch the Max-undecided with a broader net before the sale clock runs out. The execution is clean, the tiered product layout (Max → standard → Foundation) works, and individual CTAs like "Shop Dream Max" beat generic "Shop Now" every time.
- The structural problem is cadence, not creative. Eight Casper emails in seven days — two in a single day — is subscriber-list territory that email fatigue curves punish hard. Open rates on this send will be materially suppressed not because the email is bad, but because the inbox is already Casper-saturated. The "end in just a few hours" urgency is credible and appropriate for a last-call email, but it reads as the ninth cry of "last chance" in a week.
- The "up to 35%" hero framing is also mildly honest-feeling at first glance and mildly bait-and-switch on scroll — the Dream and Snow (non-Max) mattresses show only 20% off, which is a visible discrepancy. That said, "up to" is disclosed, and the savings badges per product are visible. Clear enough. This is a competent execution of a structurally fatigued sprint.

## What's working

- "End in just a few hours" in the hero copy is specific urgency without manufactured scarcity — credible for a final-night send.
- Per-product CTAs ("Shop Dream Max," "Shop Snow Max," etc.) reduce friction and direct intent cleanly vs. a single "Shop Now."
- Savings badges (Save 35%, Save 20%) on individual product cards make the discount scannable without reading body copy.
- "Looking for something simpler?" section break smartly segments premium vs. entry-level consideration inline, addressing price-objection mid-scroll.
- Tiered layout — Max → core → Foundation — covers the full price spectrum and gives every subscriber an entry point.

## What's weak

- No social proof visible anywhere: no star ratings, no review counts, no "X,000 sleepers agree" — significant miss for a high-consideration, high-ticket purchase.
- "Up to 35%" in the hero vs. "Save 20%" on Dream/Snow mattresses further down creates a visible discount gap that reads as bait-and-switch on scroll.
- Two Casper emails in one day (Max-specific earlier, full catalog now) is aggressive even for a last-call window — cadence management is broken at the sprint level.
- Hero lifestyle image is indistinguishable from prior sends in this sprint — no visual freshness signaling this is the final send.
- Some lower-tier product sections appear to lack savings badge labels (The One Mattress), creating inconsistency across the product grid.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Resolve the hero-to-product discount gap** — change "Up to 35%" to "Save 20–35% on every mattress" so the tiered savings are set up front. Removes scroll dissonance entirely.
- 2. **Add one trust signal in the hero** — a single aggregate rating ("4.6 stars · 92,000+ reviews") above or below the CTA anchors the urgency in social proof rather than just price.
- 3. **Suppress this send to subscribers who received the Max email today** — if segmentation was the goal, validate it; if it wasn't, this is the fix. Two sends/day on day 7 of a sprint destroys the cadence score.
- 4. **Add one specific product-level detail per card** — firmness tier, cooling tech name, or trial length. Makes the "Shop" CTA feel like an informed choice, not just another click.
- 5. **Give the hero image a last-call visual signal** — a countdown-style overlay or a "sale ends midnight" graphic distinguishes this from the prior six sends visually.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Mattress sale ends tonight — save 20–35% on every style`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Last hours: 35% off Casper mattresses`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dream Max, Snow Max, and the full lineup — all on sale until midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Every mattress. Every price point. Sale closes tonight.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Open-tracking pixel suppressed by `display:none`**
```html
<img width="1" height="1" alt="" src="https://cdn.us1.exponea.com/casper-prod/e/CgxqCN47..."
  style="display: none;">
```
The pixel is declared 1×1 but then hidden via inline `display: none`. Some email clients (Outlook desktop, older Thunderbird) skip image fetches on hidden elements, which would undercount opens. Standard practice is to omit `display:none` entirely and rely on the 1×1 dimensions to make it visually invisible.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**External CSS via `<link>` tags will be stripped**
```html
<link href="https://brxcdn.com/us1-app-storage/.../9c4a4f1a-..." rel="stylesheet">
<link href="https://brxcdn.com/us1-app-storage/.../9c30fa4c-..." rel="stylesheet">
```
Gmail (web), Outlook (all versions), and Yahoo strip `<head><link>` tags. Any web fonts or layout styles loaded from these sheets will silently fall back to system fonts. If the design depends on a specific typeface from these files, that typeface will not render for the majority of recipients. Fallback font stacks should be explicit in inline `font-family` declarations.

**Duplicate preheader technique — redundant, not broken**
The email uses both a `<span>` with `visibility:hidden/opacity:0` stacking and a `<div>` with ~130 zero-width non-joiners (`&#8199;&#847;`) plus ~200 soft hyphens (`&shy;`). Both serve the same purpose (pad preview text to prevent body bleed). The `<div>` filler approach alone is sufficient; the extra `<span>` is dead weight adding ~400 bytes.

**`lang="en"` present on `<html>` tag** — correct.

**MSO VML namespace declared** — correct for Outlook button/shape compatibility.
