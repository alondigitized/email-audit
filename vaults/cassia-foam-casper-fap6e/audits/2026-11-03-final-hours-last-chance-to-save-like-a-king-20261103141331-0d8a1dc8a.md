# FINAL HOURS: Last chance to save like a King.*
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-03**
## Executive summary

- This is the last-day close-out of Casper's "King for a Queen" size-up promo, which ran in the 2026-10-22 send "Pay for a Queen, sleep on a King." The hero is clean and the offer is clear. "Last chance to get more bed for your buck.*" sits beside a charming line illustration of a couple and a dog, with "Sale ends tonight." in bold. The "Trickle-Down sleeponomics" box (King for Queen pricing, Queen for Full, Full for Twin XL) is the best idea in the email. It explains the mechanic in three lines.
- After that the email loses focus. Below the size-up hero it adds a $99 QuickCool cover, 25% off bundles and adjustables, and 10% off 2+ pillows. Every section ends in the same generic "Shop now" or "Shop mattresses" button. A final-hours send should be about one deadline and one action. This one turns into a catalog tour. It also repeats the "Shop mattresses" button twice in the first screen and a half.
- The urgency is credible, since the footer says the offer expires 11/3 at 11:59pm PT. The creative is polished and consistent with the brand. Still, the subject's "King" pun and the "FINAL HOURS" caps are more generic than the strongest hook in the body. Overall it is a competent, on-brand closer, but not a sharp one.

## What's working

- The "Trickle-Down sleeponomics" box turns a confusing price ladder into three scannable lines.
- "Sale ends tonight." is stated plainly in the hero and repeated in the second headline, so the deadline can't be missed.
- The illustrated hero and the soft blue palette make the email feel like Casper and keep it easy to scan.
- The offers are concrete: a $99 QuickCool cover, a SAVE 25% badge on bundles, and 10% off 2+ pillows.
- The subject carries over the "King for a Queen" hook from the 10/22 send, so it reads as a real continuation of that campaign.

## What's weak

- The email has four offers (size-up, cooling cover, bundles/adjustables, pillows) under one deadline, which dilutes the final-hours push.
- The CTA copy is generic and repeated: "Shop mattresses" twice and "Shop now" three times, with no specific product or offer named.
- The hero asterisk points to "Savings based on Comp. Value" in small grey text, and the rest of the fine print is tiny and low-contrast. That undercuts the "honest offer" feel.
- No product, price or review appears in the size-up hero, so there is no number to anchor "more bed for your buck." The email never says what a King costs.
- There is no social proof anywhere, such as reviews or ratings.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the email down to the size-up offer plus one secondary offer. Drop the pillows block, or fold the cooling cover and adjustables into a one-line "also ends tonight" strip.
- 2. Put a real price in the hero, such as "King from $X, Queen pricing", and make the button specific: "Upgrade my size" or "Shop King mattresses."
- 3. Add a countdown or clock-time deadline ("Ends 11:59pm PT") in the hero instead of the generic "tonight."
- 4. Add a star rating or review count near the first CTA.
- 5. Make the fine print larger and higher contrast, and put the "Comp. Value" disclosure near the asterisk.

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML is a standard template-builder email (Bee/Exponea-style table layout) with MSO conditionals, a mobile media query and a dual preheader. The truncated source and the relay-stripped headers leave compliance, link and UTM checks mostly unverifiable. The one real finding is a URL-heavy plain-text part.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Plain-text part is URL-dominated.** QA reports 16,330 of 18,092 characters (about 90%) are URLs. This usually means long tracked redirect URLs are printed in full in the text alternative, which can raise spam-filter scores and makes the text part hard to read.
- **Link probing was partial.** 12 material links were probed and 18 were skipped (footer, social and utility links were deprioritized). No broken or redirecting links were reported among the probed set, but the skipped links are unverified.
- **Open-tracking pixel:** `<img width="1" height="1" alt="" src="https://cdn.us1.exponea.com/casper-prod/e/…/open" style="display: none;">`.
  - This is a normal Exponea (Bloomreach) open pixel.
  - The pixel is hidden with `display:none`. Some clients skip hidden images, so open counts may be undercounted.
  - Apple Mail Privacy Protection will also inflate opens.
  - Neither is a defect, but opens are unreliable for this email.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **QA false positive on alt text.** The "Image missing alt text" warning points at the tracking pixel, but the source shows `alt=""`. An empty alt on a decorative 1×1 pixel is correct, so no fix is needed. The QA check should be updated to ignore empty-alt pixels.
- **Present and correct:**
  - `lang="en"`, `<title>`, and the viewport meta.
  - VML/Office namespaces and an MSO `PixelsPerInch` block.
  - A `max-width:620px` breakpoint with stacking columns.
  - A hidden preheader span ("Act fast to buy a King for the price of a Queen.") followed by a padding block of `&#847;`/`&shy;` characters.
- **External font stylesheets.** The two `<link rel="stylesheet">` tags to `brxcdn.com` are wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!-->`. Gmail and several other clients strip `<link>` tags, so they will use fallback fonts. I can't confirm the fallback stacks because the source is truncated.
- Beyond that, I can't assess alt text on content images, heading structure or contrast, because the body was truncated.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. The visible source has no unresolved merge tokens (`{{ }}`, `%%`, `[[ ]]`). The tracking pixel path suggests personalized Exponea rendering, with the segment name `Core Sleepers Test` visible in the encoded data. The body past the truncation point was not checked.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **`List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) were not found.** QA attributes this to the AgentMail relay possibly stripping them. This is unconfirmed: it can't be established as a sender-side failure from the data provided. If the headers really are absent, that would conflict with Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender requirements, which expect one-click unsubscribe.
- **`Authentication-Results` is missing.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. The sending domain `e.casper.com` is a dedicated subdomain, which is good practice, but alignment can't be verified.
- **Footer not inspected.** The in-body unsubscribe link and physical postal address are in the truncated portion. QA's note says an unsubscribe link was included in probing, but its result was not reported.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not assessable. No `href` values appear in the truncated source, and QA did not list probed destination URLs or their UTM parameters. No issues are confirmed.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Plain text:** shorten or remove the tracked URLs in the text alternative, for example by using shorter link-tracking domains or fewer links. Target well under 70% URL content.
2. **Unsubscribe headers:** check the raw message at the sending ESP (Bloomreach) for `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`. If they are present, fix the relay or capture path so QA can see them.
3. **Authentication:** read the headers from the receiving mailbox (not just the relay) to confirm SPF, DKIM and DMARC pass and align on `e.casper.com`.
4. **QA tooling:** suppress the alt-text warning for `alt=""` tracking pixels (it is a false positive here), and capture the full HTML so footer, UTM and alt-text checks can run.
5. **Link coverage:** probe the 18 skipped links at least once per campaign. The unsubscribe and preference links matter most.
6. **Fonts:** confirm the web-font stacks end in system fallbacks, since the `brxcdn.com` `<link>` stylesheets won't load in Gmail.
