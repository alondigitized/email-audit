# Meet the cooling system in every Snow Hybrid Mattress.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-11**
## Executive summary

- This is email #10 in Casper's July sprint, and it's the most strategically interesting one since the July 1 opener. Instead of yet another "35% off" drumbeat, Casper pivots to product education — explaining the cooling architecture inside the Snow Hybrid: QuickCool Cover, Phase Change Material, HeatDelete Bands. The "Did you know memory foam sleeps hot?" hook gives the email a genuine reason to exist, and the three-panel tech breakdown is specific enough to be credible. For an audience that has now received ten consecutive promotional emails in eleven days, this is a breath of fresh air.
- The problem is Casper couldn't fully commit to the pivot. The 35% sitewide sale banner sits right at the top, and "Sale ends tonight" appears again — the same line they've sent multiple times. The educational and promotional missions fight each other visually, and subscribers who've seen "ends tonight" repeatedly won't believe it. The email is strong on content but weak on positioning discipline.
- The subject line is the best of the July sprint — specific, curiosity-driving, no discount theatre. The body earns the click. But at send #10, cadence fatigue is real and even a well-crafted email is swimming against the current.

## What's working

- **Subject line is the strongest of the sprint** — "Meet the cooling system" is specific, product-forward, and refreshingly different from ten days of percentage-off headlines.
- **The cooling tech breakdown is genuinely informative** — QuickCool Cover, Phase Change Material, and HeatDelete Bands with short descriptors gives subscribers something to learn, not just a sale to click.
- **Snow vs. Snow Max comparison is clean** — two product cards with distinct "20% off" vs. "35% off" badges lets the reader self-select without friction.
- **Accessory cross-sell section is well-executed** — Hybrid Pillow, HyperAir Sheets, Breathable Protector, Lightweight Duvet fill out basket opportunity naturally under "The secret to sleeping cooler."
- **Render is clean and unbroken** — no image gaps, no layout blowouts, no broken copy.

## What's weak

- **Dual-mission conflict kills the educational positioning** — the "35% sitewide" sale banner at the top signals "this is a promo email," and the educational body signals "this is a content email." Neither wins.
- **"Sale ends tonight" has been sent so many times it's invisible** — Casper used this phrase (or its equivalent) in the July 6, July 7 (twice), July 8, and now July 11 emails. No one believes it.
- **No personalization or segmentation** — a product education email is a perfect vehicle for "you viewed the Snow" or "based on your purchase" messaging; this is a full-list blast.
- **No social proof anywhere in the body** — the educational angle cries out for a real review of the Snow Hybrid calling out the cooling benefit; there's none visible.
- **Cadence is unsustainable** — ten emails in eleven days from a single brand will have pushed meaningful portions of the list to mute or unsubscribe by now.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one mission: education OR sale, not both.** Remove the sitewide banner or relegate it to a small text link in the footer. Let the cooling tech content carry the open. If you want the sale, add a single product-specific CTA at the bottom — not a sitewide banner competing with your hero.
- 2. **Replace "Sale ends tonight" with a hard date** — "Sale ends Sunday, July 13 at midnight" is credible. "Ends tonight" on the eleventh consecutive promotional email is not.
- 3. **Insert one real review anchored to the cooling claim** — a 5-star quote ("I used to wake up sweating; I sleep through summer nights now") placed directly after the tech breakdown would validate the educational pitch at the moment of peak persuasion.
- 4. **Narrow the send to Snow/Snow Max purchaser lookalikes or cart abandoners** — this content is wasted as a full-list blast; it would convert dramatically better if targeted at people who have shown cooling interest.
- 5. **Add a hard cadence break after this send** — a 3–4 day pause after this email would reset subscriber attention before the next promotional push.

- **Preheader Alt B:** `The cooling breakdown — plus 35% off the Snow Hybrid this weekend only.`

## Technical Audit — Casper "Snow Hybrid Mattress Cooling System" Email

### 1. Technical Summary

Built on Beefree/BEE editor (brxcdn.com CDN), deployed via Exponea (Bloomreach CDP) with standard VML Outlook conditionals. Two confirmed issues warrant immediate attention: the open-tracking pixel is hidden via `display:none` (non-standard, risks suppressed open counts), and the Exponea pixel URL encodes a hardcoded audience segment labeled **"Core Sleepers Test"** — a likely uncleaned test configuration on a production send.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Open pixel suppressed by `display:none`**
```html
<img width="1" height="1" alt="" src="https://cdn.us1.exponea.com/casper-prod/e/..." style="display: none;">
```
Standard practice is a 1×1 visible-but-imperceptible pixel. `display:none` causes strict clients to skip image loading entirely, silently under-counting opens.

**Hardcoded test-segment token in pixel URL**
The Exponea open-pixel path contains base64 that decodes to:
- `frequency_segment` → `"Core Sleepers Test"`

This segment identifier appears to be baked into the tracking URL at campaign-build time rather than dynamically injected per-recipient. If this is a live send to a production list, the campaign was configured against a test audience definition that was never updated.

**Full CTA link audit blocked** — HTML is truncated; cannot verify redirect chains, broken links, or UTM parameters on CTAs.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**External fonts stripped by Gmail and Outlook**
Two stylesheets loaded from `brxcdn.com`:
```html
<link href="https://brxcdn.com/us1-app-storage/.../9c4a4f1a-..." rel="stylesheet">
<link href="https://brxcdn.com/us1-app-storage/.../9c30fa4c-..." rel="stylesheet">
```
Gmail proxies and strips external CSS. Outlook ignores `<link>` tags entirely. No fallback `font-family` stacks are visible in the inline CSS, so both clients will fall back to the system default — likely breaking intended typographic hierarchy.

**Global link underline removal fails WCAG 1.4.1**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none }
```
Removes underlines from all links in body text. With color as the only distinguisher, this fails WCAG 1.4.1 (Use of Color). Links in running text become indistinguishable from surrounding copy for color-blind readers.

**Preheader dual implementation** — The email uses both a hidden `<span style="display:none !important">` and a zero-width-character padding `<div>`. The `display:none !important` on the span means some clients may skip it entirely and use the padding block as the preheader anchor. This is common practice and functionally harmless, but means the two techniques are partially redundant.

**VML/MSO conditionals present and correct** — `PixelsPerInch`, `AllowPNG`, and `DontUseAdvancedTypographyReadingMail` are all set. Outlook rendering baseline is handled.
