# Our biggest July 4th savings start now
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-01**
## Executive summary

- Casper's July 4th sale opener is the strongest structural email in this recent sprint. Unlike the June 20 "Save 35% when you go hybrid" send — which buried a subject/hero discount mismatch and earned a 7/10 — this one gets the hierarchy right: the hero names the exact offer (35% off the Max line), the Compare Maxes section provides genuine product differentiation, and the product grid below cascades the rest of the sale at honestly tiered, lower rates. The three-block layout — hero offer → comparison tool → full catalog — mirrors what Casper executed well on the June 24 adjustable bases send (9/10) and matches that level of execution.
- The primary drag is the subject line. "Our biggest July 4th savings start now" is urgency language that withholds the one piece of information that actually moves the needle at the inbox level: the 35% number. A subscriber scanning 40 emails doesn't know this is a 35% deal until they open. That's a self-inflicted open-rate handicap on an otherwise well-built email.
- The product grid — six category tiles below the compare section — tips toward catalog mode. It's informative but diffuse after the tight Max-focused hero. A tighter edit would amplify the campaign message rather than dilute it.

## What's working

- Hero states the offer precisely: "Save 35%\* on Dream Max and Snow Max mattresses" — product-specific, not vague "up to" language
- Compare Maxes section earns its keep: Most Supportive vs Most Cooling is a genuine decision-support tool, not just two product tiles
- Tiered discounts in the grid (30% on foundations/bases/bundles, 25% on pillows) are transparently differentiated from the Max-specific 35% — unlike the June 20 subject/hero mismatch
- Individual model CTAs ("Shop Dream Max," "Shop Snow Max") reduce click friction by linking to specific products, not the homepage
- July 1 send date for a July 4th sale gives the right campaign runway — three days of urgency without feeling last-minute

## What's weak

- Subject line buries the 35% discount — the strongest conversion signal is withheld from the inbox view
- Asterisk on the hero headline ("Save 35%\*") introduces fine-print doubt before the email has built any trust
- Six product-category tiles after the hero push this toward catalog mode; the July 4th campaign energy dissipates in the scroll
- Preview text ("Our most advanced sleep at our best deal of the summer") is a vague superlative — no discount, no product, no deadline

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead the subject with the number.** "35%" in the subject line is the single highest-lift change available. "Our biggest savings" signals a sale; "35% off" triggers intent.
- 2. **Add a visible deadline to the hero.** "Ends July 4th" — even in small text below the hero — converts implied urgency into actionable urgency. None is visible in the current render.
- 3. **Trim the product grid to three tiles.** Max mattresses, adjustable bases, and one bedding/bundle tile. That keeps the July 4th energy tight; a "See all deals →" CTA handles the rest.
- 4. **Rewrite the preheader with specificity.** Match the hero's offer in the preview text so the inbox view reinforces rather than retreads.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Save 35% now — Casper's biggest July 4th sale`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Dream Max & Snow Max: 35% off for July 4th`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `35% off our most advanced mattresses — ends July 4th`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop Dream Max or Snow Max before the July 4th deals expire`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

This selector (Gmail web client) strips underline and color inheritance from all `<a>` tags, making links visually indistinguishable from body text unless the template applies explicit inline styles to every anchor. Confirm all CTAs use explicit `color` and some visual affordance inline.

**External font CDN — single point of failure**
