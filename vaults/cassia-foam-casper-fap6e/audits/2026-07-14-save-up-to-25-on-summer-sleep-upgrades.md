# Save up to 25% on summer sleep upgrades.*
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-14**
## Executive summary

- Casper has rebranded the July sprint mid-run, swapping "July 4th Sale" for "Stay Cool Sale" — which is the smartest creative decision they've made in two weeks. The summer cooling angle (Snow Hybrid, cooling pillows, Hyperite sheets) gives each product section a thematic hook rather than the shotgun sitewide dump that ran July 6–12. The email renders cleanly, the visual hierarchy works, and four distinct product CTAs give engaged subscribers an actual reason to browse.
- The glaring problem is the offer itself. This campaign spent eleven emails hammering "up to 35% off" with fake deadline after fake deadline — "ends tonight," "ends TOMORROW" — and today the number has quietly regressed to 25%. Any subscriber who opened even one of those prior sends will notice the step-down and read it as confirmation that every urgency claim was theater. The credibility damage is real and the email can't undo it with a palette refresh.
- The mixed discount rates (Snow mattresses at 20%, bundles and bedding at 25%, pillows at 20%) also create unnecessary cognitive load. Pick one clean number or acknowledge the difference explicitly.

## What's working

- "Stay Cool Sale" framing is the right seasonal pivot — more relevant on July 14 than "July 4th Sale."
- Each product section (mattress, bundle, pillow, bedding) has its own headline, badge, and CTA — this is a real improvement over single-hero sends.
- Visual design is clean: no broken images, consistent blue-purple palette, clear badge placement at top-right of each section.
- "Save up to 25%" appears large in the hero — offer is legible at a glance on mobile.
- Subject line at 41 chars lands cleanly inside mobile preview without truncation.

## What's weak

- The offer regressed from 35% to 25% after two weeks of "ends tonight" urgency — subscribers who opened the July 7 "35% savings ends tonight" email will feel baited.
- No urgency signal anywhere in the email — no deadline, no countdown, no "limited stock" — after burning that lever with fake deadlines, Casper now has nothing credible to put there.
- Mixed discount rates (20% on Snow, 25% on bundles/bedding) require the reader to do math instead of absorbing one clean message.
- Cadence is unsustainable: twelfth email in ~fourteen days; open and click rates are likely collapsing by now regardless of creative quality.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Acknowledge the offer change honestly.** If the sale is winding down at a lower rate, say so — "Final week: up to 25% off" is more credible than pretending 25% is the news after two weeks of 35%.
- 2. **Add a hard end date.** "Sale ends July 20" is more believable than any "ends tonight" ever was at this point. Give subscribers a real reason to act now.
- 3. **Unify the discount.** Either lead at 25% sitewide or call out the Snow-specific 20% separately. Don't make the hero say "25%" while the Snow badge says "20%" three lines down.
- 4. **Cut cadence immediately.** One more email before the sale ends. One recap email when it ends. That's it.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 25% off — last week of the Stay Cool Sale`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Snow mattresses, cooling pillows, Hyperite sheets — all on sale.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Sale ends July 20. Shop by category below.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**External font stylesheets will not load in most email clients:**

External CSS is blocked by Gmail, Outlook, and Yahoo. These are guarded by `<!--[if !mso]><!--> ... <!--<![endif]-->` which correctly excludes Outlook, but Gmail will also discard them. Verify that inline `font-family` fallbacks cover the full type stack.

**Preheader implementation is non-standard:**
The preheader text "Cooling comfort from mattress to pillow." is placed in a `<span>` with `display:none !important`:
```html

```
Gmail reads `display:none` text for inbox snippet, so this works in Gmail. Apple Mail and Outlook may not surface this text as the preview. Standard preheader technique puts text first without `display:none`, then uses a filler `<div>` — the order here is reversed from best practice. Low severity, but preview rendering may vary across clients.
