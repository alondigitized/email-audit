# Memorial Day Doorbusters: Save up to 35%
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-25**
## Executive summary

- This is Casper's ninth email in ten days, and "Memorial Day Doorbusters" is word-for-word recycled from the May 21 send. A subscriber who opened that email will pattern-match this subject before the preview text even loads and treat it as a skip. That's a self-inflicted problem — because the creative inside is actually the best-organized Casper has sent all campaign window.
- The Snow vs. Snow Max "warm sleeper / hot sleeper" differentiation is the first time in this run that the brand has made a product decision feel personal. The "Ends Tonight" section with the visible promo code (M-BND5) is the most credible urgency Casper has run in nine tries. The product grid is clean and complete. All of this is buried under a subject line the subscriber saw on May 21.
- The structural frustration: three overlapping discount tiers (35% on Snow/Snow Max, 30% on everything else, plus an extra 5% on bundles with a code) force the subscriber to do math when the email should be doing the work. The asterisk on "35%" pointing to "Pricing based on today's deals" is too vague to reassure anyone already skeptical from email fatigue.

## What's working

- Snow / Snow Max segmentation by sleep temperature is the best product-specific copy in the entire May campaign run.
- "Ends Tonight" + promo code M-BND5 in a clearly delimited callout block is the sharpest urgency signal Casper has used since the campaign started.
- Individual CTAs per product ("Shop Snow," "Shop Snow Max," "Shop Pillows") beat the generic "Shop Now" that dominated earlier sends.
- Full-setup grid — pillows, adjustable bases, bedding, Snug — covers the whole purchase occasion without feeling cluttered.
- Hero visual is summer-fresh and render-clean; no broken images or layout breaks visible.

## What's weak

- "Doorbusters" is already spent — it ran as the May 21 subject hook and opens identically. Repeating it nine days later reads as a template mishap.
- Three discount tiers (35% / 30% / extra 5% with code) create decision paralysis; the asterisk hedge makes the headline feel smaller than it is.
- The bundle promo block ("Ends Tonight / extra 5% with code M-BND5") is below the fold and visually disconnected from the hero, despite being the single strongest close in the email.
- No social proof anywhere — no star ratings under Snow or Snow Max, no testimonials — in a campaign stretch where a hesitant buyer needs third-party trust to convert.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Retire "Doorbusters" and lead with the warm/hot sleeper angle** — that's the freshest hook in the email and the only thing a subscriber hasn't seen from Casper yet this month. The product differentiation is the story; the sale is the reason to act today.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last day: Snow 35% off — made for hot sleepers`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Hot or warm sleeper? Both are 35% off tonight only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Snow Max for hot sleepers. Snow for warm ones. Sale ends tonight — extra 5% on bundles with M-BND5.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Pick your sleep temp and save 35%. Bundle code M-BND5 expires tonight.`
- 2. **Move "Ends Tonight + M-BND5" into or directly under the hero.** The promo code is the most actionable element in the email; burying it halfway down is leaving conversions on the table for subscribers who bounce after the hero.
- 3. **Collapse the discount tiers into one clear statement.** Kill the asterisk confusion. "Save up to 35% sitewide — extra 5% on bundles with M-BND5, tonight only" is cleaner than managing three separate rates across separate sections.
- 4. **Add at least one trust signal under Snow / Snow Max.** A star rating badge or a single pull-quote review under each mattress card costs nothing and would meaningfully help close buyers who've been sitting on the fence since email #3.

## 4. What I'd change

1. **Retire "Doorbusters" and lead with the warm/hot sleeper angle** — that's the freshest hook in the email and the only thing a subscriber hasn't seen from Casper yet this month. The product differentiation is the story; the sale is the reason to act today.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Preheader implementation — flagged.** The preheader text span uses:
```html
<span style="color: transparent; display: none !important; font-size: 0px; line-height: 0px; height: 0; max-height: 0; max-width: 0; opacity: 0; overflow: hidden; mso-hide: all; visibility: hidden; width: 0;">
  + Exclusive bundle savings end tonight!
</span>
```
`display: none !important` is problematic in Apple Mail (macOS/iOS), which does not honor this declaration and may render the preheader text visibly in the email body. The safe cross-client approach omits `display: none` and relies solely on `max-height: 0; overflow: hidden; font-size: 0; line-height: 0`.
