# Save 20% on our cooling mattresses & pillows.*
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-16**
## Executive summary

- This is Casper's latest entry in a July sprint that has now featured double-digit sends, and it has a structural problem no amount of good creative can fix: the discount stepped backward. July 7 offered 35%, July 14 offered 25%, and today lands at 20%. For a subscriber who has been watching this sale play out, that reversal reads as either a mistake or a quiet contraction of the promo — neither is a good story. The "STAY COOL SALE" banner correctly continues the branding pivot from July 14, and the four-feature cooling-tech breakdown (HeatDelete® Bonds, Phase Change Material, QuickCool™ Cover, SpencerAir™ Breathability) is the strongest product-education section Casper has published this month. But the pillow section introduces a conflicting 30% OFF badge while the headline reads 20%, creating offer confusion that undercuts both numbers.
- The render is clean and hierarchy is solid — hero, education, product, accessories, bundle, footer — but two copy errors are visible: the pillow section header reads "Don't hot head." (missing at least one word), and the bundle section reads "Everything you ned for" (missing an 'e' in "need"). For a brand at Casper's price point, those are embarrassing on a send this deep into a sale sprint.
- Overall judgement: structurally sound, visually competent, but the discounting regression and copy sloppiness make this one of the weaker July sends despite strong product storytelling.

## What's working

- **Cooling tech feature grid lands well.** Four named technologies with short benefit lines is the right level of depth — educational without being a spec sheet.
- **"STAY COOL SALE" brand consistency.** Continuing the banner language from July 14 builds recognizable sale identity instead of resetting to generic.
- **Pillow upsell is well-placed.** The 30% OFF pill badges and "Best for:" call-outs give subscribers a genuine reason to scroll past the mattresses.
- **Bundle CTA at the bottom.** Offering a bundled path with an explicit discount badge is smart — it increases AOV and gives fence-sitters a different decision frame.
- **Clean render, no broken images.** Visual layout is intact end-to-end; the multi-section scroll flows logically.

## What's weak

- **Discount regressed from 25% to 20%.** A subscriber who opened the July 14 "Save up to 25%" email is now seeing a worse deal — the exact opposite of sale urgency psychology.
- **"Don't hot head."** The pillow section header is visibly missing words. This is a live copywriting error in a finished send.
- **"Everything you ned for"** is a typo in the bundle section call-out.
- **No urgency signal anywhere.** No deadline, no countdown, no "ends soon" — a mid-sale email with no time pressure is just a catalog.
- **"Shop now" is the primary CTA across four sections.** Generic verb-only copy wastes every product section's specificity momentum; each earned a better button.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Stop regressing the discount.** If the sale is winding down, lean into "last chance" framing at the current rate — don't quietly lower the offer. Every subscriber who opened July 14 noticed.
- 2. **Fix the visible copy errors before next send.** "Don't hot head." and "ned for" need a QA gate. A brand selling $1,500 mattresses cannot ship typos.
- 3. **Add a deadline.** Even "Offer ends Sunday" turns a catalog into a decision. Without it, the discount has zero urgency function.
- 4. **Rewrite primary CTAs to match product.** "Save 20% on Snow" > "Shop now." "Complete the cooling setup" > "Shop now." Each section earned a specific button.
- 5. **Reconcile the offer math.** 20% on mattresses + 30% on pillows needs a cleaner headline — "Save up to 30% on the full cooling setup" unifies both without confusion.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last chance: 20% off Snow + 30% off cooling pillows`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your Stay Cool Sale offer expires soon.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Snow, Snow Max, and pillow deals — all ending this weekend.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Complete the cooling setup while savings last.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Web fonts will fail in most clients.** Two `<link>` stylesheets load from `brxcdn.com`:
```html
<link href="https://brxcdn.com/us1-app-storage/5a801692.../9c4a4f1a..." rel="stylesheet">

```
External CSS via `<link>` is stripped or ignored by Outlook (all versions), Gmail (web + Android), and Samsung Mail. Rendering falls back to system fonts with no warning. Verify the fallback font stack produces acceptable visual output.

**Global link style override is accessibility-concerning:**

This removes all visual link differentiation in clients that apply `#MessageViewBody` (primarily Gmail). Linked text becomes visually indistinguishable from body copy unless the template overrides color/decoration inline on each `<a>`.
