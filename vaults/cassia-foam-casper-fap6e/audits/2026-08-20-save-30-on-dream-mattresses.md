# Save 30% on Dream Mattresses.*
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-20**
## Executive summary

- This is the strongest hero Casper has run in this stretch — "Save 30% on Dream & Dream Max*" plus "up to 35% on everything else" sits right at the top with two clear, differently-colored CTA buttons ("Shop mattresses" and "Shop sale"), no scroll required. It edges past the August 18 "Save up to 35%" send by naming the specific product line (Dream & Dream Max) instead of a generic "everything" claim, which gives it more concrete pull for anyone actually shopping mattresses rather than pillows or sheets.
- The Dream vs. Dream Max comparison table is the standout module — actual prices ($1,395 vs $2,355 for a queen), firmness levels, layer counts, and "best for" use cases are all visible in the render. That's rare product-decision detail for a marketing email and directly helps a shopper choose. The rest of the email is a long, repetitive scroll of category tiles (Premium Foam, Hybrid & Max, Bundles, Pillows) each with its own discount badge, which dilutes the urgency established at the top.
- Overall this is a well-built, high-effort send let down by length and a slightly buried Labor Day framing (a small pill badge, not a headline). Judgement: strong hero and genuinely useful product comparison, but the email overstays its welcome and cadence risk is real given how many near-identical Casper sales have hit this week.

## What's working

- Hero states a specific percentage tied to a named product ("Save 30% on Dream & Dream Max*") — more concrete than the August 18 "everything" framing.
- Two distinct, high-contrast orange CTA buttons ("Shop mattresses" / "Shop sale") are visible without scrolling.
- The Dream/Dream Max comparison table gives real decision-making data: price, firmness, layers, and use-case guidance.
- Visual hierarchy is clean at the top — eye lands on "Save 30%" then the model's face, no clutter.
- No render bugs — images, badges, and buttons all display correctly.

## What's weak

- The email is very long — four nearly identical category modules (Premium Foam, Hybrid & Max, Bundles, Pillows) each repeat the same layout/badge/CTA pattern, which fatigues rather than persuades.
- "Labor Day Sale" is a small pill badge above the headline, not a prominent banner — the seasonal hook is underused compared to the August 18 send.
- Asterisked "Savings based on comp. value" sits in fine print at the bottom of the hero — a mild honesty flag on the 30%/35% claims.
- This is at least the sixth Casper promotional send in under two weeks with overlapping "save X%" messaging — cadence fatigue risk is real for a subscriber who's seen the August 18, 17, 15, 13, 11, and 9 sends.
- The model image is decorative rather than product-demonstrating (no one shown sleeping on the mattress, no room context).

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut the bottom four category tiles down to two, or consolidate into a single grid — the long repetitive scroll dilutes the strong hero and comparison table.
- 2. Promote "Labor Day Sale" to a full-width banner treatment above the headline instead of a small pill, matching the urgency of the August 18 send.
- 3. Move the comparison table's "Now $1,395" and "Now $2,355" price highlights closer to the hero, or tease specific pricing in the hero itself, since real numbers work harder than percentages.
- 4. Add a deadline (e.g., "Ends Labor Day") near the hero CTA — none is visible in this render, unlike the August 17 "ENDS TONIGHT!" send which scored higher on urgency.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Missing alt text**: the Exponea tracking pixel (`src=".../open"`) has `alt=""` combined with `style="display:none;"` — QA correctly flags this as a missing-alt warning; low severity since the image is 1×1 and intentionally hidden, but best practice is `alt=""` is actually already empty/correct for a decorative pixel — flag as informational only, not a real defect.
- **Preheader padding**: the hidden preheader div repeats `&#8199;&#847;` (~150×) and `&shy;` (~230×) to push Gmail/Apple Mail snippet text out of view. This is a common technique, but some screen readers do not fully suppress `display:none` combined with zero-width/soft-hyphen characters consistently across clients, risking a long string of whitespace being announced. Worth spot-checking with VoiceOver/NVDA against Gmail specifically.
- **External stylesheet links**: two `<link rel="stylesheet">` tags from `brxcdn.com` are loaded inside an `<!--[if !mso]><!-->...<!--<![endif]-->` block (i.e., served to non-Outlook clients). Most major webmail clients (Gmail, Yahoo, Outlook.com) strip external `<link>` stylesheets from the `<head>` entirely — any styling not also inlined or duplicated in the embedded `<style>` block will not render for those recipients. This is a real rendering risk, not just a defensive fallback.
- Responsive `@media (max-width:620px)` rules are present and target specific row/column classes appropriately; no issues found in the embedded CSS itself.
