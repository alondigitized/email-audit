# Prime Time Deals end tonight: Save an extra 5% sitewide*
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-29**
## Executive summary

- This is a last-day urgency send closing out Casper's Prime Time sale — essentially an end-of-campaign reminder bolted onto the same "extra 5% sitewide" mechanic Casper already ran on June 22. The hero is clean: "Extra 5% savings on everything" with the EXTRA5 code and a midnight deadline lands immediately. That part works.
- The problem is what comes after. The email devolves into a very long product-category scroll — at least six separate discount tiles (20%+5%, 30%+5%, 35%+5% for mattresses, pillows, adjustable bases, and accessories) each with its own "Shop X" CTA. By the bottom third, there's a clearance pitch stacked on top of the Prime Time countdown. The decision architecture collapses under the weight of its own thoroughness. The urgency is real and credible; the execution below the fold undermines it with catalog fatigue.
- The brand history context also matters here: this is nearly the same email as June 22's "Exclusive Prime Time Deals: Save an extra 5% sitewide.*" (9/10). Subscribers who received both see a sequel, not a fresh campaign. The June 22 send earned that 9 because it had Prime Day novelty; this one is a countdown reminder, which is a weaker creative position even when the deal is identical.

## What's working

- **Hero is immediately legible.** "Extra 5% savings on everything" + code EXTRA5 + midnight deadline + "Shop now" — zero ambiguity before any scrolling.
- **Stacked discount math is compelling.** "35% + Extra 5%" on adjustable bases (already the best-in-brand category per the June 24 9/10 send) is the strongest offer visible.
- **"End Tonight" framing is credible.** Midnight deadline feels real and specific, not vague "limited time."
- **Clean Casper aesthetic maintained.** No render bugs, brand colors consistent, no broken image blocks.
- **Clearance call-out adds incremental value.** "Extra 20% on items already marked up to 50% off" is a genuine secondary hook for deal-seekers below the fold.

## What's weak

- **Cadence fatigue.** This is the same mechanic as June 22 ("extra 5% sitewide") with near-identical creative. Subscribers who got both are seeing a repeat, not a final-hours escalation.
- **Too many CTAs.** Six or more "Shop X" buttons across product tiles diffuse focus and create decision paralysis — the urgency the hero builds up is slowly bled out by the scroll.
- **No single hero product.** The hero links to the whole sale, not a specific product. There's nothing to anchor a purchase decision for someone who's been on the fence.
- **Clearance placement is awkward.** Burying a clearance module between the Prime Time countdown and the footer sends mixed signals — is this a premium sale or a clearance event?
- **Subject asterisk ("sitewide\*") signals exclusions.** The fine print footnote is visible at the bottom, but the asterisk right in the subject sets off caution in any trained shopper.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse the product tiles to two.** Pick the two highest-AOV categories (adjustable bases at 35%+5% and hybrid mattresses) and kill the rest from the scroll. A tight, curated offer drives more clicks than a catalog.
- 2. **Add a countdown timer graphic.** "Ends tonight at midnight" is good; a visible clock visual in the hero would convert the time-bound urgency from text to feeling.
- 3. **Give the clearance section its own email.** The "Extra 20% on clearance" module belongs in a segmented send to deal-seekers, not buried in a sitewide urgency email — it undercuts the premium positioning.
- 4. **Differentiate subject from the June 22 send.** The current subject is nearly identical to a week-old email. A re-opener or final-hours framing would signal "this is the last chance," not "same deal again."
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last call: Extra 5% ends at midnight →`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Final hours — stack an extra 5% on your Casper cart`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Your stacked discount disappears tonight. Code EXTRA5 at checkout.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `35% off mattresses + an extra 5% — expires midnight.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Open-pixel suppression risk**
```html

```
`display: none` on the tracking pixel is non-standard. Apple Mail Privacy Protection and some corporate proxies use CSS visibility as a signal to skip image fetches. Standard practice is `width:1px; height:1px; border:0;` without any display override. This will undercount opens.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Link de-styling in OWA**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none; }
```
This global rule strips underlines from all anchor tags in Outlook Web App. If CTA buttons rely on this for text appearance, any plain-text links (e.g., unsubscribe, footer) will be visually indistinguishable from body copy.

**Web font URLs lack file extensions**
```
https://brxcdn.com/us1-app-storage/5a801692.../9c4a4f1a-f357-11ef-a8ef-5a86f91ecd85
```
Extensionless paths require the CDN to serve the correct `Content-Type: text/css` header. If the CDN returns `application/octet-stream` or a generic type, strict clients will refuse to parse the stylesheet. Font fallback will engage silently with no rendering error, but should be verified.

**MSO conditionals are correct** — web fonts are guarded with `<!--[if !mso]><!--> ... <!--<![endif]-->`, preventing Outlook breakage. No issue.
