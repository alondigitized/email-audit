# Here’s the scoop: Save up to 25%
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-19**
## Executive summary

- National Ice Cream Day gives Casper the thematic hook this sprint has been missing since the July 11 Snow Hybrid education send. "Sweet dreams with sprinkles on top" is light, seasonal, and earns its wordplay — this is the first July send where the creative conceit actually carries through the full email: hero → "Choose Your Scoop" → "How about some toppings?" → "Go ahead. Add the extras." → "Sweet Dreams, Served Cold." The ice cream menu architecture is genuinely clever and the best full-body creative execution of the month.
- The problem: 25% is the same number the July 14 send led with. Subscribers who've been paying attention have seen "up to 25%" twice in five days (with a 20% sandwiched in between on July 16). The creative disguise is good enough to avoid looking like a copy-paste, but the underlying offer is stale. "Before these sweet savings melt away" gives the deadline zero teeth — no date, just vibes.
- Overall: smartest creative of the July sprint by a comfortable margin. Offer credibility and the absence of any urgency anchor are the only things keeping this from being a genuinely strong send.

## What's working

- Ice cream menu framing ("Choose Your Scoop," "How about some toppings?," "Add the extras") is cohesive and fun — Casper has never sustained a theme this cleanly across a single send this month.
- 25% badges are visually prominent on every product tile; no hunting required.
- Visual hierarchy flows naturally: hero → product grid → accessories → footer CTA.
- "Sweet Dreams, Served Cold." closes the thematic loop at the footer — satisfying copy work.
- "Here's the scoop" in the subject earns its pun without being cringe-y — genuinely rare.

## What's weak

- "Up to 25%" is the exact same offer as July 14, two days after a lower 20% send on July 16 — discount credibility is quietly eroding.
- No urgency anchor: "before these sweet savings melt away" is playful but physically means nothing without a date or countdown.
- Zero social proof in the email — no reviews, no star ratings, no "X customers purchased" signals anywhere in the scroll.
- Product descriptions are thin functional copy ("help you sleep comfortably all night long") — no pricing, no trial period, no spec callouts to close a hesitant browser.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lock in a hard deadline.** "Ends Sunday at midnight" or "through July 21" would turn the "melt away" metaphor into actual urgency — right now it's atmosphere with no conversion pressure behind it.
- 2. **Price-anchor at least one product.** Show a before/after ("Was $1,895 → Now $1,421") on the Snow Mattress tile; making 25% feel concrete rather than aspirational lifts add-to-cart confidence.
- 3. **Add one review callout.** A four-star badge and a single quote on the hero mattress tile would do more click work than any copy tweak.
- 4. **Differentiate the offer.** Same percentage as July 14 is a missed opportunity — "25% + free pillow" or "25% + free mattress protector" would add novelty without touching the core promo structure.

### 1. Technical Summary

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Open pixel suppressed by `display:none`**
The Exponea open-tracking pixel carries `style="display: none;"` in its inline style:
```html
<img width="1" height="1" alt="" src="https://cdn.us1.exponea.com/casper-prod/e/CgxqCN47..." style="display: none;">
```
`display:none` can prevent the pixel from firing in Gmail and certain mobile clients, undercounting opens. The correct approach is `display:block` (or no display override) with the dimensions clamping visibility. `visibility:hidden` or `max-height:0;overflow:hidden` on a wrapper div is the safer suppression technique.

**External font CDN (`brxcdn.com`)**
Two `<link>` stylesheets are loaded from `brxcdn.com`. These are guarded with `<!--[if !mso]>` so Outlook/Word rendering engine skips them, but Gmail strips all `<head>` content regardless. If the font declarations aren't also embedded in a `<style>` block inside `<body>`, Gmail recipients will fall through to the system default. Fallback stack legibility cannot be confirmed from the truncated source.

**CTA links / UTM parameters** — not auditable; HTML is truncated before body content.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Preheader text may be suppressed in inbox preview**
The preheader span uses `display: none !important` among its hiding properties:
```html
<span style="color: transparent; display: none !important; font-size: 0px; ... visibility: hidden; width: 0;">
  Nat'l Ice Cream Day = Sweet Casper Savings
</span>
```
`display:none` instructs the rendering engine to remove the node from layout *and* accessibility trees. Several clients (notably iOS Mail, Samsung Mail) only surface preheader text that is in-flow but visually clipped (`max-height:0; overflow:hidden`). The result is the preheader string may not appear in the inbox preview line despite being present in source. Remove `display:none !important` and rely solely on `max-height:0; overflow:hidden; font-size:0; color:transparent`.

**Tracking pixel `alt=""` on a hidden image** — acceptable; decorative/functional pixels with empty alt are correct.

**VML/MSO namespace and `DontUseAdvancedTypographyReadingMail`** — correctly declared; no issues.

**Responsive breakpoint** — `@media (max-width:620px)` stacks columns and resets padding; structure looks standard. Cannot verify image `srcset` or retina handling from truncated source.
