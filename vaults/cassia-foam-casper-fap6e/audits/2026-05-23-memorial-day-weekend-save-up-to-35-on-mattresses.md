# Memorial Day Weekend: Save up to 35% on mattresses
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- This is Casper's second email on May 23 alone — and at least the seventh in eight days. The send itself is clean: a tight hero with a clear 35% off headline, a recognizable "Shop mattresses" CTA, and a single-column product grid that scrolls through eight SKUs. The visual execution is competent. The strategic problem is that it is nearly indistinguishable from the May 21 "Doorbuster" send and the May 23 morning send — same offer, same format, same product order, same pun territory in the sub-hero ("Don't Sleep on Your Summer Rest").
- The catalog-dump structure hurts click intent. Eight product cards with near-identical "Shop Now" buttons flatten decision-making rather than guiding it. The Dream Max shows "Save 42%" — higher than the headline 35% — which is a buried win that should be the lede, not buried at card three. The Foundation's "Save 5%" at the bottom actively undercuts the email's discount energy.
- The honest read: this is a technically solid promotional email sent at the wrong cadence, with the wrong architecture. The May 21 email made the same offers. A subscriber who hasn't converted in seven sends is not going to be moved by a eighth near-identical one.

## What's working

- Hero hierarchy is clean — the 35% offer and "Shop mattresses" button are immediately legible without scrolling.
- Single-column layout is mobile-appropriate; product cards are well-proportioned.
- Savings badges per product (35%, 42%) give a sense of item-level specificity even inside a catalog format.
- "Memorial Day Weekend" framing is timely and creates implicit urgency without resorting to countdown timers or exclamation stacking.
- No render bugs visible — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum.

## What's weak

- Two emails in one day from the same brand, both on the same 35% off offer, is aggressive to the point of self-sabotage.
- Catalog dump of eight products with identical button copy ("Shop Now") creates choice paralysis rather than a single motivated click.
- The Foundation card's "Save 5%" is a momentum killer — it closes the email on a weak offer after the strong mattress discounts.
- The Dream Max "Save 42%" is the best offer in the email and it's buried at card three; it should be the hero.
- No social proof anywhere in the render — the May 18 email had a "Rave reviews" section; this one drops it entirely.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Make Dream Max the hero.** 42% off is a stronger number than 35%. Lead with it, name it, give it the hero image slot. The "up to 35%" headline is technically accurate but undersells what's actually available.
- 2. **Cut to three products max.** Pick the three highest-margin or highest-converting SKUs. A focused send outperforms a catalog on click rate almost universally.
- 3. **Drop The Foundation card.** 5% off at the bottom erodes perceived value. Pull it or hold it for a separate "complete your setup" flow.
- 4. **Add one review quote or star count** back in — the May 18 send showed it works; this one omits it for no visible reason.
- 5. **Don't send twice on the same day with the same offer.** Either replace the morning send or hold this one for Sunday morning when Memorial Day urgency peaks.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Our biggest mattress deal ends Monday`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dream Max hits 42% off — the most we've ever cut.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `35–42% off every mattress. Ends Monday night.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Segment data exposed in open-pixel URL**
The tracking pixel encodes what decodes to `frequency_segment` and `"Core Sleepers Test"` directly in the URL path:
```
https://cdn.us1.exponea.com/casper-prod/e/CgxqCN47...ZnJlcXVlbmN5X3NlZ21lbnQ
SEkNvcmUgU2xlZXBlcnMgVGVzdIACAw...
```
This leaks internal segment/cohort names in a client-visible HTTP request. If proxied through privacy relays (Apple MPP, iOS 15+), the segment label is logged against Apple's IP, not the user — but the label itself is readable by anyone inspecting the URL.

**Font CDN dependency**
Two stylesheets load from `brxcdn.com` (Bloomreach CDN):
```
https://brxcdn.com/us1-app-storage/5a801692-.../media/original/9c4a4f1a-...
https://brxcdn.com/us1-app-storage/5a801692-.../media/original/9c30fa4c-...
```
These are third-party-hosted, unversioned URLs. Gmail, Outlook, and most corporate mail clients block external CSS entirely — custom fonts will silently fall back to system fonts. This is expected behavior for email, but worth noting as a dependency risk if the fallback stack isn't defined in the inline styles.

**CTA links unverifiable** — source was truncated before any `<a href>` CTAs appeared. UTM coverage and redirect chain cannot be audited from the supplied source.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Link underlines suppressed globally**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none; }
```
This removes all link underlines in Gmail's `#MessageViewBody` wrapper. For users with low vision relying on underline as the sole link indicator, this fails WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.1 (Use of Color). The fix is to preserve underlines on text links or ensure sufficient color contrast with surrounding body text.

**Preheader/subject mismatch**
The hidden preheader span reads: `"Stay cool for the summer."`
Subject line: `"Memorial Day Weekend: Save up to 35% on mattresses"`

These are semantically inconsistent. The preheader is the first copy rendered in inbox previews (iOS Mail, Gmail) — a summer-cooling message misrepresents a mattress sale send. This is borderline content/technical; flagged here because the preheader is a code-level element.

**Positive findings:**
- VML namespaces and MSO conditional comments correctly declared for Outlook rendering.
- `<o:PixelsPerInch>96</o:PixelsPerInch>` and `<o:AllowPNG/>` present.
