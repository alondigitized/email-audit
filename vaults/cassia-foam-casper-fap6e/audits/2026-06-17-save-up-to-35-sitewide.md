# Save up to 35% sitewide.*
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-17**
## Executive summary

- Casper returns to the well with a July 4th sale anchor, and the visual execution is solid — clean blue-on-white hero, a clear "Stars, stripes, and up to 35% sitewide" headline, and a tidy category grid below. The timing is actually smarter than it looks at first glance: sent June 17, it catches Father's Day shoppers four days out (Father's Day 2026 = June 21) while simultaneously pre-selling the July 4th event. The dual-occasion play gives this email two legitimate reasons to exist.
- The problem is the offer itself. Subscribers paying attention will recall that Casper floated "early access" to 35% off back on June 10, ran 35% bundles on June 14, briefly dipped to 30% on June 16, and is now back to 35% sitewide. This is the same ceiling, same structure, same asterisk — just with a fresh flag emoji and a different CTA button. There is no new signal here for anyone who opened the last week of mail.
- The deeper structural flaw: the hero shouts "35% sitewide" but accessories are buried at 10%. That gap is technically disclosed (badges are visible per-section), but it creates a bait-and-switch texture that will erode trust over time.

## What's working

- **Dual-occasion timing is genuinely smart** — Father's Day gift card section at bottom is well-placed for a Thursday send four days before the holiday.
- **Visual hierarchy holds** — hero offer lands first, category grid below is legible, badge-per-section clearly communicates the tiered discounts.
- **"Stars, stripes" headline earns its creative** — it's the first July 4th copy that actually tries to be seasonal instead of just slapping the holiday name on a promo.
- **CTA specificity in the hero** — "Shop July 4th Sale" is a verb-plus-noun that beats the department-store-generic "Discover" or "Shop Now" used in every subordinate section.
- **No render bugs** — clean layout, images load, no overlapping text or lorem ipsum visible.

## What's weak

- **Offer fatigue is real** — "up to 35% sitewide" has been Casper's ceiling since at least June 10; subscribers who opened the June 16 email 24 hours ago saw a near-identical pitch. There is no escalation signal here.
- **Ten percent on accessories vs. 35% sitewide headline** — "sitewide" sets a 35% expectation; the accessories section delivering 10% will sting anyone who scrolled looking for bedding deals.
- **Five-plus CTAs below the fold fragment the click** — Shop Bundles / Shop Pillows / Shop Adjustables / Shop Accessories / Shop Gift Cards in sequence with no visual hierarchy creates decision paralysis. There is no second anchor pulling toward a single action.
- **No deadline visible** — "July 4th" implies an end date but nothing says "ends July 4" or "through Sunday." After two weeks of "limited time" language that never resolved, subscribers have learned to disbelieve urgency signals from this brand.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add an explicit end date to the hero** — "Sale ends July 4" or a countdown strip. This is the single highest-leverage change. Casper has used vague urgency for three weeks; a real date would cut through the noise.
- 2. **Retire one of the CTAs** — Collapse the five subordinate section buttons into two: "Shop Mattresses & Bundles" (where the real 30-35% lives) and "Find a Father's Day Gift." Fewer exits, more concentrated click pressure.
- 3. **Call out the accessories discount honestly in the hero** — Change "35% sitewide" to "Up to 35% off — mattresses, pillows, and more." The asterisk currently absorbs all the nuance and subscribers notice.
- 4. **Differentiate the subject from June 14** — "Save 35% on bundles*" was eight days ago. A subject that doesn't distinguish this offer from that one is wasted inbox real estate.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Stars, stripes, and 35% off — ends July 4`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Dad's Day + July 4th: two reasons to shop this week`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Father's Day is Sunday — grab a gift card while the sale is on`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `35% off mattresses, 30% off pillows, sale ends July 4th`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Mixed CDP/ESP tracking systems**
Open tracking pixel resolves to `cdn.us1.exponea.com` (Bloomreach), while the template and font assets are served from `brxcdn.com` (Braze). These are two separate platforms recording the same open event. Opens will be attributed inconsistently depending on which system is queried.

**Subscriber segment name exposed in tracking URL**
The Exponea pixel URL embeds a Protobuf-encoded payload in the path:
```
/e/CgxqCN4...ZnJlcXVlbmN5X3NlZ21lbnQSEkNvcmUgU2xlZXBlcnMgVGVzdIACAw.../open
```
Decoding the base64 segment yields plaintext: `frequency_segment` → `"Core Sleepers Test"`. The internal audience segment name is visible in the raw HTML to any recipient who inspects source. Not a security vulnerability, but a data hygiene issue — internal test/segment labels should not surface in customer-facing payloads.
