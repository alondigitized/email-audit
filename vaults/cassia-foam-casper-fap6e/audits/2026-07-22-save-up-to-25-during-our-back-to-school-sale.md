# Save up to 25% during our Back to School sale.*
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-22**
## Executive summary

- Casper has done the smartest thing they could do on July 22: killed the "Last Chance" drumbeat from yesterday and relaunched with a fresh seasonal hook. "Back to School sale" is a credible reason to reset the clock on the same 25% discount, and the creative commits to it — the bundle section calls out "dorm or apartment setup" explicitly, the Rise Light section ties to building bedtime routines for the school year, and the kids' bed "$100 off" offer adds a parent angle alongside the student one. The pivot is real, not cosmetic.
- The problem is that the 25% ceiling hasn't moved. Yesterday's email said "last chance to save up to 25%" — today it's a brand-new sale at the same rate. Any subscriber paying attention will notice the continuity, and that erodes the urgency of both sends. The creative is solid but the offer architecture undercuts it.
- Multi-section layout works here because each product pod has a distinct discount badge and a specific CTA. There's no dead scroll — every section earns its place. This is a well-executed email trapped inside a sprint that has cried wolf too many times.

## What's working

- **Seasonal pivot is legitimate.** "Back to School" is a real retail moment on July 22, not a forced theme — the bundle and Rise Light sections genuinely support it.
- **Dorm/apartment specificity.** "A smoother dorm or apartment setup" in the bundle section is the sharpest audience-acknowledgment copy in the email.
- **Mixed discount signals.** "Save $100" on kids' beds alongside percentage-off badges adds variety and makes the dollar figure feel concrete next to the abstract "up to 25%."
- **Clean render, no layout breaks.** Every section loads, every CTA is readable, discount badges are clearly visible throughout.
- **Rise Light placement is thematic.** "Stay on schedule / Build better bedtime habits for the year ahead" earns the slot by tying directly to the school-year angle.

## What's weak

- **"Last chance" was yesterday — same discount today.** The July 21 email explicitly closed the sprint; this reopens it at identical terms. That sequence trains subscribers to ignore deadlines.
- **No deadline in the email body.** Multiple sections have discount badges but nothing says when Back to School ends — urgency is implied by the theme, not delivered.
- **"Up to 25%" is a ceiling, not a promise.** After a full month of this qualifier, the asterisk reads as a warning. The 20% badges in individual sections actually look worse than the headline.
- **Hero copy is generic for the moment.** "Built to keep up with your every early morning and late night" could have been written for any of the prior twelve sends — a missed opportunity to lean into a specific student or parent identity hook.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard end date to the hero and every section badge.** "Back to School sale ends August 4" gives the reset legitimacy and removes the "we said last chance yesterday" whiplash. If the sale genuinely ends, say when.
- 2. **Lead with the dorm angle, not the generic mattress angle.** Swap the hero sub-copy to something like "Everything you need to sleep well in a new space" — it earns the Back to School label and differentiates from the July cooling sprint instantly.
- 3. **Consolidate the percentage into one clean number.** Either every pod says 25% or the hero drops "up to" — the mismatch between 20% badges and a 25% headline headline reads as fine print, not an offer.
- 4. **Give the bundle section a featured product visual.** It's the most concept-forward section (dorm bundle) but shows the least product detail. A flat-lay of a mattress + pillow + topper would make it shoppable.
- 5. **Drop one of the five sections.** Five product categories with five CTAs is one too many for a mobile scroll — the Rise Light is the most niche and least likely to convert; consider moving it to a dedicated send.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your dorm sleep setup: 25% off everything.*`
- **Subject Alt B:** `New year, new room — save up to 25% on all of it.*`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sale ends August 4 — bundle a mattress, pillow, and topper for your best sleep yet.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Dorm or apartment, we've got a bundle for it. Ends soon.`

## Full review
## 1. Overview

Casper has done the smartest thing they could do on July 22: killed the "Last Chance" drumbeat from yesterday and relaunched with a fresh seasonal hook. Multi-section layout works here because each product pod has a distinct discount badge and a specific CTA. There's no dead scroll — every section earns its place. This is a well-executed email trapped inside a sprint that has cried wolf too many times.

## 3. What didn't

4. **Give the bundle section a featured product visual.** It's the most concept-forward section (dorm bundle) but shows the least product detail. A flat-lay of a mattress + pillow + topper would make it shoppable.
5. **Drop one of the five sections.** Five product categories with five CTAs is one too many for a mobile scroll — the Rise Light is the most niche and least likely to convert; consider moving it to a dedicated send.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Web fonts load from external CDN — will fail in most clients:**
```html
<link href="https://brxcdn.com/us1-app-storage/5a801692-.../9c4a4f1a-f357-11ef..." rel="stylesheet">
<link href="https://brxcdn.com/us1-app-storage/5a801692-.../9c30fa4c-f357-11ef..." rel="stylesheet">
```
Gmail (web + Android), Outlook (all), and Yahoo strip `<link>` tags entirely. These fonts will silently fall back to system fonts. No fallback stack is declared in the visible CSS — if the font stacks in these stylesheets don't include web-safe fallbacks, text may reflow unexpectedly.

**MSO/VML blocks present** — Outlook compatibility handled correctly via conditional comments.

**Preheader implementation:** Uses dual hidden-span + whitespace-stuffing pattern (`&#8199;&#847;` repeating ~130×). Effective at suppressing body preview bleed, but the stuffing div uses `display:none` which some older Android clients ignore — minor risk of showing raw whitespace in preview pane.

**`role="presentation"` on layout tables** — confirmed, correct.

**Image alt text:** Tracking pixel has `alt=""` (correct). Alt text on content images not verifiable from truncated source.

**`lang="en"`** on `<html>` — present, correct for screen reader routing.
