# Up to 35% savings ends tonight.*
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-07**
## Executive summary

- Casper has finally written a subject line smart enough to drop "Memorial Day" — but the body of the email still leads with a giant "Memorial Day Sale" hero banner, almost two weeks after the holiday. That mismatch is the dominant story here: the send team clearly knows the holiday anchor is dead (the subject says "ends tonight," not "Memorial Day"), but the creative team didn't get the memo. A subscriber who has been receiving Casper emails since late May will feel like they're being recycled the same promotion with a cosmetic countdown bolted on.
- The multi-product layout — foam vs. hybrid, bundles, pillows, adjustable bases, accessories — is thorough and well-organized visually. The hierarchy is clear, each section has a specific save percentage and a named CTA, and the overall render is clean. Casper knows how to build a functional promotional email.
- What this email cannot do is feel current. "Memorial Day Sale" in June isn't urgency — it's evidence of a stale campaign running on autopilot. The "ends tonight" deadline lands hollow when the hero is still waving a holiday flag from two weeks ago.

## What's working

- **Subject-line urgency without the stale holiday anchor** — "Up to 35% savings ends tonight.*" is concrete, short, and drops the embarrassing "Memorial Day" language that plagued earlier sends.
- **Clear offer hierarchy per section** — each product block (foam, hybrid, bundles, pillows, adjustable bases) has its own named savings percentage and a specific CTA button, reducing friction.
- **Visual cleanliness** — no broken images, no overlapping text, no lorem ipsum; the layout reads correctly on a simulated mobile view.
- **Product specificity** — naming "Premium Foam Mattresses," "Hybrid & Max Mattresses," and "Adjustable Bases" separately helps a buyer at different consideration stages self-select.

## What's weak

- **"Memorial Day Sale" hero is embarrassingly stale on June 7** — the holiday was May 25; running this banner 13 days later signals creative negligence, not urgency.
- **Subject/body mismatch undermines trust** — "ends tonight" in the subject sets a credible deadline, but nothing in the visible body copy reinforces it; the deadline evaporates once you open.
- **No social proof anywhere in the render** — earlier sends (notably the May 28 high-scorer) had a "Don't just take our word for it" review block; it's completely absent here.
- **Decision overload** — six distinct product categories with separate CTAs; without a single hero product to anchor attention, the "up to 35%" promise diffuses across too many destinations.
- **Cadence fatigue is compounding** — per brand history, this is approximately the fifteenth email in three weeks; a subscriber who has seen "35% off" in every single one has already priced the deal into their mental model.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill the "Memorial Day Sale" banner immediately.** Replace it with "Summer Sleep Sale" or simply "35% off ends tonight." The holiday anchor is not just stale — it's actively eroding trust on every send that keeps using it.
- 2. **Reinforce the deadline inside the body.** A visible countdown or a bold "Offer expires midnight tonight" line transforms the subject-line urgency into an in-body call to act. Right now, the deadline disappears after the open.
- 3. **Add back the review block** from the May 28 send. That email scored 9/10 partly because of specific social proof. Its absence here is a regression.
- 4. **Collapse to one hero product.** Lead with the Snow or Wave mattress at the highest discount, then let the multi-product grid follow. One anchor converts better than six equal options.
- 5. **Revisit send cadence.** A 24-hour countdown email lands differently if subscribers haven't received five near-identical promos that week.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Tonight only: 35% off every mattress`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your last shot at 35% — offer ends at midnight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Foam, hybrid, pillows, bundles — all on sale until tonight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Shop the full sleep lineup before this deal closes.`

Any custom web fonts these deliver will silently fall back to system fonts in those clients with no error. If these are web fonts only, that's an acceptable graceful degradation — but it should be verified that fallback stacks are defined in inline styles.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Stale preheader text — highest severity finding.**
The hidden preheader element reads:
```
"Memorial Day savings are almost gone!"
```
The subject line is `"Up to 35% savings ends tonight.*"` with no Memorial Day reference. This is a campaign recycling error: the preheader was not updated before send. It will render verbatim in Gmail, Apple Mail, and iOS Mail preview panes alongside the subject, creating a contradictory message to the subscriber.

Evidence:
```html
<span style="color: transparent; display: none !important; ... opacity: 0; ...">
  Memorial Day savings are almost gone!
</span>
```

**Link color/decoration override may cause accessibility issues in Outlook.com context.**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none }
```
If any `<a>` inherits a text color that matches its background in that wrapper, the link becomes invisible. Acceptable risk if all CTAs use button blocks with explicit background colors, but warrants verification.

**MSO/VML namespace and conditional comments are present and correctly structured.** `lang="en"` set on `<html>`. `<title>` matches subject line. No issues.

**Mobile breakpoint at 620px** — standard and correct for a 600px container.
