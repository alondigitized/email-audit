# Save up to 35% on your summer reset
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-01**
## Executive summary

- This is Casper's twelfth-ish email in roughly fifteen days, and the brand is caught between two identities: the subject line has finally moved on from "Memorial Day" to the fresher "summer reset" framing, but the email hero still shouts **Memorial Day Sale** in your face. That creative disconnect is the defining problem here — the copy team updated the subject, the email team didn't sync. A subscriber who has been receiving these since May 19 will notice the stale label immediately.
- The multi-section editorial layout ("Get guest-ready," "Hot nights, cool Zzzs," "Vacay-level sleep," "Lighten up") is Casper's strongest structural move in this run — it segments the catalog into use cases rather than just dumping SKUs. Snow Technology gets a dedicated call-out, which is a real differentiator. That's the good news.
- The bad news: "Memorial Day Sale" ended six days ago, "Ends soon" is vague and unbelievable at day fifteen, and the bottom section undercuts the "up to 35%" hero promise with a 10% off line item. This is a solid email that would have been excellent on May 23 — on June 1, it's misfiled.

## What's working

- **Thematic product clustering** ("Get guest-ready," "Hot nights, cool Zzzs") gives each scroll zone a reason to exist — this is the most editorial Casper has looked in this entire run.
- **Snow Technology callout** ("Easy, breezy sleep, all night long thanks to Snow Technology") is the one place where product differentiation, not just discount, does the selling.
- **Hyperlite Sheet Set at 35%** is a high-value, seasonally relevant hero offer — breathable bedding in summer is a natural fit.
- **Clean visual hierarchy** — the eye lands on the Memorial Day hero, then descends naturally through the four product clusters.
- **No render bugs** — layout is intact, images loaded, text is legible.

## What's weak

- **"Memorial Day Sale" hero label is 6 days stale** — Memorial Day was May 26; it's June 1. The subject line already moved to "summer reset" but the email body didn't catch up.
- **"Ends soon" is a burned signal** — Casper has used some form of this urgency marker on at least five of the last twelve sends. Nobody believes it anymore.
- **10% off on the Lightweight Down Alt Duvet** at the bottom directly contradicts the "up to 35%" headline — it feels like the fine-print clause rather than a featured offer.
- **No social proof** — zero reviews, star ratings, or "X people bought this" signals across the entire email, in a category (mattresses, bedding bundles) where third-party validation meaningfully drives conversion.
- **"Shop now" on every product card** — identical CTA copy across eight products collapses the editorial effort the clusters created.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Kill "Memorial Day Sale" from the hero entirely.** The subject says "summer reset" — make the hero say the same. "Your summer sleep upgrade starts here" or "Summer Reset: Save up to 35%" keeps the offer while retiring the expired event.
- 2. **Promote Hyperlite Sheet Set to the hero position.** 35% off breathable sheets on June 1 is a more credible summer-season hook than a mattress sale that started two weeks ago.
- 3. **Replace "Ends soon" with an actual date.** "Sale ends June 4" is infinitely more credible and adds genuine urgency to a subscriber who has seen "Ends soon" a dozen times.
- 4. **Remove or upgrade the 10% duvet line.** Either pull it from this email or reframe it as a bundle add-on so it doesn't deflate the headline discount.
- 5. **Differentiate the product CTAs.** "Shop Snow Mattresses" / "Shop Bundles" / "Shop Sheets" on the section-level CTAs — the editorial clusters earned that specificity.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Summer Reset: 35% off ends June 4`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Hot nights sorted — save up to 35%`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Breathable sheets, cool mattresses, bundle deals — all on sale.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Snow Technology, Hyperlite sheets, and more at up to 35% off.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is built on a standard Braze MSO-conditional table layout with responsive media queries. Two critical infrastructure issues identified: external stylesheets will be stripped by Gmail, and audience segment metadata is exposed unencrypted in the open-tracking pixel URL.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**External CSS stripped by Gmail**
Two `<link>` stylesheet tags in `<head>` load custom fonts/styles from Braze CDN:
```
https://brxcdn.com/us1-app-storage/5a801692-033d-11ef-8779-ca524c9ef7ea/media/original/9c4a4f1a-f357-11ef-a8ef-5a86f91ecd85
https://brxcdn.com/us1-app-storage/5a801692-033d-11ef-8779-ca524c9ef7ea/media/original/9c30fa4c-f357-11ef-a8ef-5a86f91ecd85
```
Gmail (web and app) strips all `<link>` tags from `<head>`. Any font loading or styles defined only in these files will silently fail for Gmail recipients. Inline styles or `<style>` block fallbacks are required.

**Segment name exposed in open-tracking pixel URL**
The 1×1 pixel at `https://cdn.us1.exponea.com/casper-prod/e/Cgx...` contains base64-encoded audience metadata. Decoding the payload reveals:
- `frequency_segment` key
- Segment value: **`Core Sleepers Test`**

This is cleartext-readable by any party with access to email server logs, proxy logs, or the recipient's network. Segment names should not be embedded in tracking URLs unencrypted — use opaque IDs that resolve server-side.

**Remaining links not auditable** — HTML is truncated; CTA hrefs and UTM parameters could not be inspected.
