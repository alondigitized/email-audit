# Ends soon | July 4th Sale | Save up to 35%*.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-08**
## Executive summary

- Nine emails into Casper's July sprint, and this one has a credibility problem baked into the subject line. Yesterday Casper sent two separate emails — one for all mattresses, one specifically for Maxes — both saying the sale **"ends tonight."** Today, July 8, the sale is still going, now reframed as "ends soon." That is a textbook deadline extension, and anyone paying attention will notice. It erodes the urgency that the last two sends built up carefully.
- The email itself is visually clean: a strong hero establishing 35% off sitewide, five distinct product sections, clear CTAs per category. The structure is solid template work. But the July 4th holiday ended four days ago and the creative still leads with "Stars and stripes" and "July 4th Sale." The cultural hook is stale.
- The deeper issue is the 10% off accessories line at the bottom. The hero promises "up to 35% sitewide" — then the last featured section delivers 10%. That gap undermines the lead offer for anyone who scrolls.

## What's working

- **Hero is immediate**: "35% savings sitewide" is above the fold, large, and unambiguous — no teaser, no mystery.
- **Category-level CTAs**: Each section (bundles, pillows, adjustables, accessories) has its own button and deal callout, making it scannable for someone already in-market for a specific item.
- **No render bugs**: Clean layout throughout — images load, text doesn't overlap, spacing is consistent.
- **Casper branding is clear**: Logo, recognizable color palette, and lifestyle photography are all on-brand.

## What's weak

- **Deadline extension destroys trust**: "Ends soon" today, after two "ends tonight" emails yesterday, is a broken promise — visible to any subscriber who opened yesterday's send.
- **July 4th is four days stale**: "Stars, stripes" and "July 4th Sale" branding has no credibility on July 8.
- **Vague urgency**: "Ends soon" gives no date, no time, no countdown — the one tool that could justify sending a ninth email is used in its weakest form.
- **10% accessories undercuts the hero**: A subscriber who scrolls to the bottom sees a 10% offer after being promised "up to 35%." Technically accurate; practically deflating.
- **No social proof anywhere**: Nine emails in and Casper has never used a review, rating, or customer quote. A strong "4.6★ from 38,000 sleepers" in the hero would add conviction without adding length.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick a real deadline and name it.** "Ends midnight Thursday" > "ends soon." If the deadline keeps moving, stop pretending there is one — or collapse this into one final send with a hard timestamp.
- 2. **Retire the July 4th frame.** Replace "July 4th Sale" with "Summer Sale" or just the offer itself. The holiday is gone; the discount can stand on its own.
- 3. **Anchor with a specific product hero.** Rather than five equal sections, lead with the single highest-converting SKU (historically the Original or Snow) with a real price-drop number, then let categories follow.
- 4. **Drop or reframe the accessories section.** 10% off at the bottom of a 35% email is a momentum killer. Either raise the accessories discount or omit that section entirely.
- 5. **Add one review.** Even a short pull-quote in the hero ("Finally slept through the night — Sarah T.") would differentiate this send from the previous eight.

### 1. Technical Summary

Email uses a standard MSO-compatible table layout sent via Bloomreach Engagement, with assets hosted on Brevo CDN. The HTML is structurally sound for the visible portion, but several infrastructure issues are confirmed below.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Open pixel has `display:none` applied**
```html
<img ... src="https://cdn.us1.exponea.com/casper-prod/e/CgxqCN47..." style="display: none;">
```
The 1×1 tracking pixel is explicitly hidden via inline `display: none`. In clients that suppress hidden images (Outlook, some Gmail), this suppresses the load event entirely — open tracking will undercount. The pixel should carry no style or at most `width:1;height:1;border:0` with no `display` override.

**Truncated HTML prevents full link audit** — CTA URLs, redirect chains, and click-tracking domains cannot be verified from the provided source. If the full source is available, check that all `href` values pass through the Exponea click-tracking domain and include UTM parameters.

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**External web fonts will not render in major clients**
```html
<link href="https://brxcdn.com/us1-app-storage/5a801692.../9c4a4f1a-..." rel="stylesheet">
<link href="https://brxcdn.com/us1-app-storage/5a801692.../9c30fa4c-..." rel="stylesheet">
```
Gmail (all variants), Outlook (all versions), and Samsung Mail strip `<link>` tags. These font loads will silently fail for the majority of recipients. Verify that a web-safe fallback stack is declared in the same CSS rules — if fonts are defined only in these external sheets with no inline fallback, the rendering chain is broken.

**Preheader text is short**
```html
<span ...>Save on deals for the summer </span>
```
At ~28 characters this will be padded by the filler block (U+2027 / U+FEFF / `&shy;` sequence), which is correct practice. No rendering issue, but worth noting the preheader copy is generic relative to the subject line.

**`lang="en"` present on `<html>`** — correct.
**MSO conditional blocks (`PixelsPerInch`, `AllowPNG`, VML namespace)** — correct for Outlook compatibility.
**Apple data-detector suppression and `#MessageViewBody a` override** — both present and correctly scoped.

**Image `alt` on tracking pixel is `alt=""`** — correct for a decorative/tracking image.

Full alt-text audit on content images requires the untruncated source.
