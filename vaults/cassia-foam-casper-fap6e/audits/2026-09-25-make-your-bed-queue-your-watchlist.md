# Make your bed. Queue your watchlist.
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-25**
## Executive summary

- This is Casper doing shoppable content marketing again — a "cozy fall setup" hub organized around three curated "moods" (Polished, Relaxed, Functional), each pairing a numbered shoppable bed photo with four product tiles. It's the most editorial, least discount-driven send since the 09/18 spine piece, and it's genuinely well-built: the numbered hotspot photography is a smart pattern, repeated three times with clear visual consistency. But the "September Savings — up to 25%" banner at the very top is disconnected from everything below it — the email pivots into pure content (mood boards, a "Queue the Cozy" watchlist of shows/movies/books) and never returns to reinforce the offer or tie it to a specific product.
- The "Queue the Cozy" section is a fun, distinctive touch — pairing sleep products with a fall watchlist is on-brand and memorable — but it's also completely non-transactional filler taking up a fifth of the email's real estate. Compared to 09/20's "The proof is in the sleeping" (this brand's best recent send, per prior history), this trades hard product proof for lifestyle styling, which is a fine occasional beat but won't move units the way a clear price-led hero does.
- Judgement: a nicely produced content send that will underperform on clicks relative to Casper's discount-led emails, because the CTA path from mood board to purchase requires real interpretation, and the top-of-email 25% offer isn't revisited anywhere near the shoppable content.

## What's working

- Numbered hotspot photography (bed shot with circled 1-2-3-4 tags matching product tiles below) is a clean, scannable pattern executed consistently across all three mood sections.
- Clear section labels ("MOOD") and distinct fall-appropriate headlines ("Polished comfort," "Relaxed, welcoming," "Functional, calming") give the email real structure.
- "Queue the Cozy" adds a distinctive, non-generic brand voice — pairing product with lifestyle content is memorable and different from the last six discount-hammer sends.
- Dog Bed inclusion in the "Relaxed" mood is a nice touch that broadens appeal without feeling forced.
- No render bugs — layout is clean, images load, product tiles are legible.

## What's weak

- The 25% savings banner at the top is never reinforced near any product — no prices, no "% off" badges on any of the 12 product tiles.
- No single, obvious primary CTA after "Make your bed" — the rest of the email has no button until the very bottom nav bar, just product name + one-line description tiles.
- "Queue the Cozy" (shows/movies/books) consumes significant vertical space with zero product linkage or purchase path.
- Three "mood" sections with 4 products each is 12 products total — no hierarchy tells the reader which one to actually buy.
- Preview/subject promises "watchlist" but the hero banner leads with a generic savings claim, so the two big ideas (discount vs. content) compete instead of reinforcing each other.

## Recommendations

- 1. Pick one lead: either make this a 25%-off shopping email with prices/badges on the mood tiles, or drop the savings banner entirely and let it be a pure content/discovery send — right now it's neither.
- 2. Add a price or "% off" badge to at least the hero product in each mood section so the discount promise has a visible payoff.
- 3. Cut "Queue the Cozy" to a single row or move it to a follow-up send — it's charming but it's diluting a shoppable email's real estate.
- 4. Give each mood section one clear CTA button ("Shop this mood") instead of relying on tile taps.

- **Subject Alt B:** `3 bedroom moods for cozy season`

## 1. Overview
