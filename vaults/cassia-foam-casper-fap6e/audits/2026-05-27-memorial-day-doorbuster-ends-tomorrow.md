# Memorial Day doorbuster ends tomorrow!
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-27**
## Executive summary

- This is Casper's tenth email in roughly ten days, and the subject — "Memorial Day doorbuster ends tomorrow!" — is essentially a word-for-word recycling of the May 21 and May 25 sends. Worse, Memorial Day itself was May 25; sending "ends tomorrow" on May 27 (a Tuesday) means the sale has already survived two days past the holiday, which undermines the urgency framing rather than reinforcing it. A subscriber who has been hit nine times by this campaign has long since decided whether to buy.
- The body is a long-scroll product catalog — six-plus mattress models, then a "complete your sleep setup" accessories grid — rather than a tight conversion email. For a "doorbuster ends tomorrow" message, that structure is exactly backwards. The brand should be collapsing to one hero product, one deadline, one button. Instead it asks the reader to browse.
- The "Shoppers think we're cool, too." headline following the offer badge is the email's most puzzling choice. It reads like social proof copy, but there are no visible star ratings or quote excerpts to back it up in the hero. The line floats without a payload. The email isn't broken, but it is fatigued, unfocused, and two days past the credibility window for its own urgency claim.

## What's working

- **Deadline copy in subject line is concrete** — "ends tomorrow" is a specific time signal, not vague urgency.
- **35% savings figure is visible in the hero** — the offer is legible without scrolling.
- **"Shop Now" CTA button is present and unambiguous** at the top of the email.
- **Memorial Day branding is visually consistent** — the banner header ties to the campaign running across all prior sends.
- **No visible render bugs** — layout is clean, images loaded, no broken gaps or overlapping elements.

## What's weak

- **Calendar math breaks the urgency** — Memorial Day was May 25; "ends tomorrow" on May 27 means the sale is already two days past the holiday. The deadline feels stretched rather than credible.
- **"Shoppers think we're cool, too." is an orphaned headline** — it promises social proof but delivers no visible rating, quote, or number to back it up.
- **Long catalog structure dilutes the deadline message** — six mattress models plus accessories is a browse email; a "last chance" email should be a single-product, single-CTA sprint.
- **Tenth send in ten days with near-identical creative** — the subject "Memorial Day doorbuster" is a direct repeat of the May 25 send; subscribers who didn't open then almost certainly will not open now.
- **No price anchors or specific dollar savings visible** — "up to 35%" is the only number; no "was $X / now $Y" framing to create tangible value.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Collapse to one product, one price, one button.** Pick the best-selling mattress, show "was $X / now $Y," put one CTA. Kill the catalog scroll entirely for a last-chance send.
- 2. **Correct the calendar framing.** If the sale genuinely runs through May 28, say "Sale ends Wednesday, May 28" — a specific date beats "tomorrow," which will be literally untrue to anyone who sees it after midnight.
- 3. **Replace "Shoppers think we're cool, too." with an actual quote or star rating.** One sentence from a verified buyer with a star count earns the social-proof claim. The current line is an empty setup.
- 4. **Cut the send or shift the offer.** A tenth email in ten days with recycled creative is an unsubscribe driver. If the sale window must extend, at minimum change the angle — free shipping, bundle bonus, or a new category lead.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your mattress deal expires Wednesday — 35% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sale closes May 28. The Nova, Wave, and Original are all in.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `This is the final send — 35% off and free delivery through tomorrow.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**External web fonts will be stripped by Gmail, Outlook, and Yahoo.**
Two `<link>` stylesheet tags pull from `brxcdn.com` (Bloomreach CDN):
```
https://brxcdn.com/us1-app-storage/5a801692-.../9c4a4f1a-f357-11ef-...
https://brxcdn.com/us1-app-storage/5a801692-.../9c30fa4c-f357-11ef-...
```
`<link>` tags are stripped by Gmail (both web and Android), Outlook, and Yahoo. No system-font fallback stack is visible in the inlined `<style>` block. If these are custom display fonts, affected clients will fall back to the browser/OS default serif/sans, which may degrade layout. Recommendation: inline critical font-family declarations with a full fallback stack.

**`#MessageViewBody a` removes all link affordances in Gmail.**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none; }
```
This selector targets Gmail's message wrapper and makes every anchor inherit body text color with no underline. Links become visually indistinguishable from body text for Gmail users — this is a WCAG 1.4.1 (Use of Color) failure and will suppress click-through. This override is almost certainly unintentional; the pattern exists to defeat Apple Mail's blue auto-detection, but the Gmail selector is too broad.
