# Save up to 35% on deeper sleep*
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-05**
## Executive summary

- This is Casper's fourth July 4th sale email in five days — following the July 1 opener (9/10), the July 3 reminder (7/10), and the July 4 Doorbuster (7/10). The holiday was yesterday. The creative is clean and the product catalog is comprehensive, but the email arrives without any visible hook explaining why a subscriber should still care: no "sale extended," no "ends tonight," no new mechanic. It's the same "up to 35% off mattresses" offer wearing a softer subject line ("deeper sleep" instead of "mattresses"), which actually reduces specificity rather than adding intrigue.
- The catalog format — seven mattress models stacked vertically, each with a "Save 30%" badge and a generic "Shop Now" button — reads like a reference document, not a conversion nudge. There's nothing here to make a decision. The hero lifestyle image (woman reading in bed) is warm and on-brand, but it does zero differentiation work at the point in a sale sequence when subscribers are most likely to delete-on-sight.
- Judgment: a competent execution of a brief that should have been rethought. The creative team delivered the email; the strategy team didn't deliver a reason for it.

## What's working

- Hero is unambiguous — "Save up to 35% on mattresses.*" lands immediately above the fold with a lifestyle image that sets the mood.
- Product catalog is well-structured: each model gets its own image, brief descriptor, and consistent red "Save 30%" badge — easy to scan.
- Layout is clean with no visible render bugs, broken images, or overlapping text.
- Footer reinforces the sitewide offer and adds an app download nudge, giving subscribers an alternate action without cluttering the body.

## What's weak

- This is the fourth consecutive send of essentially the same offer — cadence fatigue is severe, and there's nothing visible that signals a new reason to engage.
- July 4th was yesterday; with no "extended sale," "last chance," or deadline visible in the hero, the email feels misdated.
- Subject swaps the concrete noun "mattresses" (used in the July 4 Doorbuster) for the softer "deeper sleep" — less specific, not more compelling.
- Seven mattress models with identical "Save 30%" badges and identical "Shop Now" CTAs create decision paralysis rather than a single clear conversion path.
- No visible urgency, deadline, or social proof anywhere in the scroll.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a sale-status hook to the hero** — one line: "Extended through Sunday" or "Today only" gives the subscriber a reason this email exists on July 5 instead of July 4. Without it, the whole catalog reads like a ghost.
- 2. **Pick one hero product** — after four sends, pick the highest-converting SKU (likely Dream or Snow) and anchor the hero around it. The catalog grid can stay, but the hero should make a single decision easy.
- 3. **Replace "Shop Now" with something specific** — "Shop Dream Max," "Build my Sleep Setup," "See the Snow" — anything that signals what the click does.
- 4. **Add a countdown or expiration line** — "Offer ends July 6 at midnight" would do more work than any copy change here.
- 5. **Reframe the subject for cadence exhaustion** — signal something new, even if the offer is the same.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Extended: 35% off mattresses ends tonight`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Still on: our biggest sleep sale of the year`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Sale was extended — every mattress, every size, up to 35% off through midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Dream Max, Snow, Cloud One — all 30% off. One more day.`
