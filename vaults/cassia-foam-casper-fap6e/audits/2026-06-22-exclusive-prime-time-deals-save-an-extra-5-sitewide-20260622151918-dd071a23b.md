# Exclusive Prime Time Deals: Save an extra 5% sitewide.*
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-22**
## Executive summary

- This is Casper's Amazon Prime Day piggyback email — an "extra 5%" subscriber exclusive stacked on top of the July 4th sitewide sale that has been running since at least June 14. The mechanic is plainly stated: existing 20–35% discounts still apply; enter code EXT5PD at checkout for an incremental 5% on top. The email doesn't hide the math, and that transparency is to its credit.
- The execution is clean — a crisp hero, a well-organized category grid, consistent Casper aesthetic. But the "exclusive" positioning is thin. An extra 5% on a sale subscribers have already seen five or six times in ten days reads less like a reward and more like a rounding error dressed in Prime Time language. Compare this to the June 12 hot-sleepers send (9/10): that email had genuine product-education energy. This one is pure discount arithmetic.
- The email's highest-energy content — "Prime Time for Clearance Savings" with an effective 40% off — is buried below six category rows. That sequencing is backwards.

## What's working

- Hero is immediately scannable: offer, code, and rationale all above the fold without scrolling.
- Stacked discount math ("Save 20% + Extra 5%") is per-category and easy to parse — no ambiguity about what the code does.
- "As a subscriber" framing gives the extra 5% a legitimate-feeling exclusivity hook rather than blasting it to everyone.
- The clearance block at the bottom creates a genuine second conversion opportunity for price-sensitive shoppers.
- No render bugs — clean, pixel-consistent Casper aesthetic throughout.

## What's weak

- "Prime Time" borrows urgency from an Amazon event Casper isn't part of; a subscriber who notices this reads it as a framing trick, not a real hook.
- No visible deadline anywhere — "Prime Time" implies urgency but the offer could end tomorrow or in three weeks; subscribers have no way to know.
- An extra 5% is a weak hook after five consecutive 30–35% sale emails in ten days; the incremental feels almost trivial against that backdrop.
- Six identical-format category rows create visual monotony — the clearance section (highest effective discount at 40%) is penalized by its position at the bottom.
- No social proof, no product-level detail, no reviews — pure discount arithmetic without any reason to prefer a specific product.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add an explicit end date in the hero.** "Ends July 15" or "through Tuesday" would make the Prime Time urgency real and actionable, not implied.
- 2. **Surface clearance earlier.** The 35%+5% clearance block is the highest-discount offer in the email and should compete for hero real estate, not close the send.
- 3. **Trim and sharpen the subject.** At 55 characters, it front-loads "Exclusive" — the weakest word in the line — before getting to the actual offer.
- 4. **Feature one specific product in the hero** with the stacked price shown before and after, not just a code over a generic bed image. A single product anchor converts better than six category links.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Prime Time exclusive: extra 5% on everything — ends [date]`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Stack an extra 5% on our July 4th sale — today only`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `On top of our July 4th sale — use EXT5PD at checkout for your subscriber bonus`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Up to 40% off Clearance + your extra 5% on top — ends [date]`

### 1. Technical Summary

The email is built on Braze (brxcdn.com) with Exponea/Bloomreach for open-tracking. Infrastructure is dual-platform. One confirmed open-tracking defect and one accessibility violation; remainder are risk flags from the truncated source.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Open pixel suppressed by `display:none`**

```html
<img width="1" height="1" alt="" src="https://cdn.us1.exponea.com/casper-prod/e/CgxqCN..." style="display: none;">
```

`display:none` will cause Gmail, Apple Mail, and most image-blocking clients to skip the request entirely, zeroing out open-count accuracy. The standard pattern is to omit the style attribute entirely and rely only on `width="1" height="1"`. This is a confirmed tracking defect.

**Dual-platform tracking stack**

Open pixel resolves to `cdn.us1.exponea.com` (Bloomreach); all template assets and fonts serve from `brxcdn.com` (Braze). If these platforms are not reconciled, click and open data will land in separate systems. Confirm downstream attribution is unified.

**CTAs and UTM parameters not auditable** — the HTML source is truncated before body links appear. Verify all CTA hrefs carry consistent UTM params (`utm_source=email&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=prime-time-2026`).

---

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Web fonts will fail in major clients**

```html
<link href="https://brxcdn.com/...9c4a4f1a..." rel="stylesheet" type="text/css">
<link href="https://brxcdn.com/...9c30fa4c..." rel="stylesheet" type="text/css">
```

`<link>`-loaded web fonts are not supported in Gmail (web or Android), Outlook (Windows/Mac), or Yahoo Mail — collectively the majority of inboxes. Verify that all text elements declare inline `font-family` fallback stacks so rendering does not collapse to browser default serif.

**Link styling strips visual differentiation**

```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none; }
```

This removes all visual distinction between links and body text. Violates WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.1 (Use of Color). CTA buttons are likely differentiated via background, but inline text links (if any) will be invisible as links.

**Preheader technique is correct** — dual-element pattern (`<span>` with hidden preheader text + `<div>` with `&#8199;&#847;` hair-space padding and `&shy;` soft-hyphens) is a well-supported method to control preview text length. No issue.

**MSO/VML conditionals present** — `xmlns:v`, `xmlns:o`, and `<!--[if mso]>` blocks are present. Outlook rendering path is covered.

**`lang="en"` on `<html>`** — present. Correct.
