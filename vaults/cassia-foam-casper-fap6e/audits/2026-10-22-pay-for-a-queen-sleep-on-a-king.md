# Pay for a Queen, sleep on a King.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-22**
## Executive summary

- This is a Casper "size-up" promo with a real hook. The subject "Pay for a Queen, sleep on a King." is the strongest thing in the send, and the hero delivers on it: "Size up for less!" with a "King for a Queen" script badge, a clear explanation of the size-up ladder, and a navy "Shop the sale" button. It's concrete, easy to grasp, and a change from the Friends & Family percent-off sends on 10/15, 10/16 and 10/21.
- The body undercuts it. After the hero there's a second "California King for the price of a Queen" headline that repeats the offer, then four stacked blocks with four different CTAs and three different offers: a $99 QuickCool cover, 25% off bundles, 25% off adjustable bases, and 10% off pillows. The size-up deal gets one image-less hero and one repeat headline, then the email wanders into a catalog. The eye has nowhere to settle after the first screen.
- Judgement: a good idea with a diluted execution. It's better than the recent repeat sends, but it still reads as a promo roundup. The hero's size-up offer needed to be the whole email, or at least have its own product image.

## What's working

- The "Pay for a Queen, sleep on a King" framing is a clear, easy-to-grasp offer, and the hero spells out the full size ladder (King/Cal King for a Queen, Queen for a Full, Full for a Twin XL).
- The hero is on-brand and has a distinct look: a light-blue field, a script badge and a line-art illustration of a family and pet. It stands apart from the navy-photo Friends & Family sends.
- The "Shop the sale" button is high-contrast navy, sits above the fold, and reads as the primary action.
- Each module has a clear headline, one line of copy and a single button, so the layout is easy to scan.
- The footer has a text-to-join SMS block and quiz/store links, which give non-buyers another way to engage.

## What's weak

- The size-up offer gets no product image. Its headline is repeated below the hero ("California King for the price of a Queen"), then the bed photo underneath is captioned "Make your new bed a cooler bed" and sells a $99 cover instead.
- Four modules push four different offers (the $99 cover, 25% off bundles, 25% off adjustables, 10% off pillows), which dilutes the hook.
- The "Shop the sale" CTA is vague. It doesn't say what sale or which sizes.
- The hero's asterisk points to "Savings based on Comp. Value", which is tiny, grey, and sits under the button. That makes the "pay for a Queen" claim look softer than the headline suggests, and the exclusions are buried in the footer.
- The pillow module has no price or product detail, and the nav, footer and legal text are very small.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the size-up offer the whole email. Show a King bed photo with a simple price table (Queen price = King price) and drop the adjustables and pillow blocks, or cut them down to one line each.
- 2. Say which sale and which sizes in the main button, for example "Upgrade my size."
- 3. Put the real terms next to the offer instead of in the footer: "Valid through 11/3, mattresses only."
- 4. Add proof or reassurance near the hero, such as a star rating or "100-night trial."
- 5. Pick one secondary offer, the 25% bundle, and cut the rest.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Queen price, King bed. Today only.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Upgrade your bed size, not your budget`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Get a King or Cal King for the price of a Queen, plus 25% off bundles.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Every size up is on us. Shop mattresses before the offer ends.`

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Confirm the headers at the source.** Check the raw message from the original ESP send (Bloomreach) for `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, and `Authentication-Results`. Do this before treating the compliance warnings as real defects.
2. **Suppress the pixel alt warning.** Treat `alt=""` on 1×1 tracking pixels as a pass in the QA rule.
3. **Review the plain-text part.** Check whether the URL-heavy text (about 90%) comes from tracking-wrapped links, and shorten or drop link wrapping in the text part if possible.
4. **Re-run the audit on the full HTML.** The truncation hides the body, footer, and merge tokens. A full-source pass should check the UTM parameters, the unsubscribe link and postal address, and `alt` text on content images.
