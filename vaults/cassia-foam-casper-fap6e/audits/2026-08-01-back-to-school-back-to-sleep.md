# Back to school. Back to sleep.
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-08-01**
## Executive summary

- This is Casper's fourth "Back to School" variant since July 22, and it's the weakest of the bunch structurally — the actual discount (up to 25%) is buried at the bottom of the email, four full product modules deep, while the hero only promises vague "smoother bedtimes and brighter mornings" with no number attached. Compare that to the July 30 "Moving in made easy" send, which put "Save 25%" front and center as the strongest visual hook in the email; here the green-circled "25%" only shows up after a scroll through Snug Mattress, The One Mattress, Freshman Bundle, and the Glow Light.
- The creative concept — a mom eating cereal in bed while getting her household back into a school routine — is a nice change of pace demographically (family/parent angle rather than solo sleeper), and the four-product ladder (kids' mattress, adult mattress, dorm bundle, sleep gadget) sensibly covers a multi-persona "back to school" household in one send. But leading with lifestyle copy and no offer, then stacking four separate CTAs before the actual sale banner, asks a lot of patience from a scanning inbox reader.
- Net: solid segmentation instinct, weak sequencing. This would score meaningfully higher if the 25% badge in the hero image did the job the green circle does at the bottom.

## What's working

- The mom-eating-cereal-in-bed lifestyle photo is warm and on-theme for a "new routines" back-to-school message, and reads clearly on a small screen.
- Four distinct product tiles (Snug Mattress -$100, The One Mattress -20%, Freshman Bundle $299, Glow Light -20%) let different sub-audiences (parents of young kids, adults, dorm-bound students) each find their own relevant offer.
- Each tile has a visible individual discount badge, so scrolling readers get incremental reasons to keep going even before the closing sale banner.
- The Freshman Bundle module clearly diagrams what's included (sheet set, topper, pillow) with labeled callouts, which reduces "what am I actually buying" friction for a bundle.
- No broken images, overlapping text, or layout glitches — the render is clean top to bottom.

## What's weak

- The hero has no offer at all — "New routines. Same great sleep." is pure lifestyle copy while the real number (up to 25%) doesn't appear until the very bottom of the email.
- Four separate "Shop now" CTAs before the final "Shop the sale" button dilute the primary action — there's no single unambiguous thing to click.
- The closing "Save up to 25%" line uses a hand-drawn green circle around "25%" that looks slightly off-brand/sloppy next to the otherwise clean, corporate Casper layout used everywhere else.
- "Back to school. Back to sleep." as a subject is catchy but generic — it doesn't hint at the 25% offer or that this is a household/family-multi-product email, which is the actual news here.
- Cadence is a concern: this is at least the 4th back-to-school-themed Casper send in under two weeks (July 22, 24, 27, 30, now Aug 1), and the wordplay ("New routines," "Get A's in better ZZZs") is starting to feel like it's reaching for a new angle on the same promo rather than genuinely new news.

## Recommendations

- 1. Move the "Save up to 25%" badge into the hero image itself, next to or below "New routines. Same great sleep." — right now the strongest offer in the email is invisible until the very last scroll, which is the single biggest lift available here.
- 2. Cut the number of mid-email CTAs from four down to two or three, or convert the weaker ones (Glow Light) to smaller text links so "Shop now" doesn't lose meaning through repetition.
- 3. Replace the green hand-drawn circle around "25%" with a badge/pill treatment consistent with the "SAVE $100" / "SAVE 20%" badges used earlier in the same email — it's an inconsistent visual style in an otherwise cohesive design.
- 4. Reorder modules so the Freshman Bundle (dorm-specific, most clearly "back to school") sits higher, right after the hero, since it's the most literal match to the subject line's back-to-school premise.
- 5. Vary the "back to school" hook itself next send — five sends in two weeks on the same seasonal wrapper risks fatigue; consider pivoting to a "new sale ends [date]" urgency angle instead.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Mattresses, bundles, and sleep gear for the whole household.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Dorm bundles from $299. Kids' mattresses save $100.`

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- `lang="en"` and viewport meta are correctly set.
- MSO conditional comments (`<!--[if mso]>`/`<!--[if !mso]><!-->`) are used to gate Outlook-specific handling — correct pattern.
- CSS is loaded via `<link rel="stylesheet">` pointing to two `brxcdn.com` (Braze) URLs, gated inside `<!--[if !mso]><!-->` for non-Outlook clients. External `<link>` stylesheets are stripped by Gmail and several other major webmail clients, which do not fetch external CSS — any styling that depends solely on these linked sheets (rather than inlined `style` attributes) will not render for a large share of recipients.
- **[WARN]** (QA) Tracking pixel has `alt=""` — flagged as "missing alt text," but an empty `alt` on a hidden 1x1 tracking pixel is standard/correct practice, not a real accessibility defect. Low severity, effectively a false positive.
- Preheader hack uses a very long run of repeated `&#8199;&#847;` and `&shy;` entities (100+ repetitions) to pad hidden preview text. This is a working technique but adds unnecessary bytes to the payload; a shorter run (~15-20 repetitions) achieves the same suppression effect with less bloat.
