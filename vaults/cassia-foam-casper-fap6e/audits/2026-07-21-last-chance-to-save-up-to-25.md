# Last chance to save up to 25%.*
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-21**
## Executive summary

- This is Casper's closing email on a July sprint that has now run at least 13 sends since July 1. The creative is clean and structurally solid — four product categories, each with a persistent discount badge and a specific CTA — but the strategic positioning is exhausted. "Last chance to save up to 25%" is the third appearance of 25% in eight days (July 14, July 19, and now July 21), and the urgency language ("ends tonight") echoes the July 12 "ends TOMORROW" send that clearly didn't end anything. Subscribers who've been paying attention will smell the bluff.
- The multi-section layout (cooling mattresses → bundles → pillows → sheets) is the right play for a final send: it covers the full product range, gives multiple entry points, and surfaces the 25% bundle deal as the highest visible discount. The visual execution is Casper's best work this sprint — consistent badge placement, legible CTAs, no render issues.
- The strategic problem is that this "last chance" arrives at a lower discount ceiling than the sprint opened with (35% in early July). If this is genuinely the last send, it's closing the sale on a worse deal than the one subscribers passed on two weeks ago. That's a hard urgency case to make honestly.

## What's working

- Four-section product breakdown (mattresses, bundles, pillows, sheets) gives subscribers multiple relevant entry points without requiring scroll fatigue.
- Persistent "Save 20%" / "Save 25%" badges on each section make the discount unmissable — no hunting for fine print.
- "Shop the sale" hero CTA is clean, visible, and direct.
- The summer/cooling theme ("Summer heat, meet cooler sleep") is seasonally earned and consistent with the July 18 identity-hook that scored 9/10.
- "Last chance" urgency is shopworn after the July 12 "ends TOMORROW" send that clearly wasn't the end — credibility is the casualty.
- 25% is the third consecutive send at this ceiling; the sprint opened with 35% and has never returned to it, making this "last chance" a worse deal than what subscribers already declined.
- No social proof anywhere — no star ratings, no review snippets, no "X people bought this" signals at a moment when subscribers need a nudge to act.
- The asterisk on 25% appears in both the subject and hero but the qualifying fine print is not legibly rendered — subscribers will notice the hedging without seeing the explanation.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Acknowledge the timeline honestly in the hero.** Instead of a generic "last chance," say something like "Final 24 hours — then summer pricing ends." Specificity makes the deadline feel real rather than recycled.
- 2. **Lead with the best deal, not the umbrella.** 25% off bundles is the headline offer — put that front and center, not "up to 25%." Vagueness in the hero hurts click confidence.
- 3. **Add one social proof anchor.** A single star rating or a pull quote ("I sleep so much cooler now") under the Snow Hybrid section would do more for conversion than a fifth CTA button.
- 4. **Reframe the discount history.** If 25% is genuinely where the sprint lands, own it: "Our best cooling deal of the summer" is more compelling than a number the brand already offered twice.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Your sleep upgrade: 25% off ends at midnight`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cooling mattresses, pillows, bundles, and sheets — all on sale through midnight`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Snow Hybrid, cooling pillows, summer sheets — 20–25% off, ending tonight`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Issue 1 — "Test" segment in apparent production send.** The segment name `Core Sleepers Test` contains the word "Test." If this is a live deployment, this suggests either (a) the wrong Exponea segment was selected at send time, or (b) internal test infrastructure was promoted to production without renaming. Confirm intended segment in the Bloomreach campaign config before treating open-rate data as reliable.

**Issue 2 — Internal segmentation nomenclature is passively disclosed.** The segment name is recoverable via a simple base64 decode of the pixel URL — no key required. This is a minor information-hygiene issue, not a security vulnerability, but worth noting for senders with strict data-classification policies.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Redundant preheader implementation.** Two preheader techniques are stacked:
1. A `color:transparent` `<span>` with the actual preheader text.
2. A `display:none` `<div>` with 130+ `&#8199;&#847;` filler pairs followed by 200+ `&shy;` soft-hyphens.

Both are technically valid, but the double-stack is unnecessary. The filler div pads out the preview to suppress body text bleed — functional — but combining it with an already-hidden span creates redundant hidden content that may trigger spam heuristics in aggressive filters.

**`#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none; }`** — This global rule strips underlines and color from all `<a>` tags in the body (targets Outlook.com). If CTAs elsewhere in the email rely on link color or underline for visual distinction and are not styled via inline `color` + explicit `text-decoration`, they may render as invisible text on white in Outlook.com. Cannot confirm impact without the full CTA markup.

**Tracking pixel `display: none` style** — `<img ... style="display: none;">` is present. This is intentional and expected for Exponea open-tracking; however, a small number of clients (notably older Yahoo Mail variants) suppress hidden images differently than `height:0/width:0` patterns. Low risk but worth noting if open-rate tracking appears suppressed on Yahoo.

No issues found with MSO/VML namespace declarations or `<meta viewport>`.
