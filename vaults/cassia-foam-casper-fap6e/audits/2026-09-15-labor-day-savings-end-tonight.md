# Labor Day Savings End Tonight
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-15**
## Executive summary

- This is Casper's Labor Day finale, and it's the cleanest execution of the "35%" number they've been repeating since 08/23 — but that's exactly the problem. The 09/08 send ("Final hours to shop our Labor Day Doorbuster") already claimed the closing-bell moment with real urgency copy; this one recycles the same beat two weeks past the actual holiday with a slightly different headline. Visually it's tight: clear hierarchy, legible hero, one unambiguous CTA, no render bugs.
- The creative itself is solid craft — hand-drawn circle around "35%," warm lifestyle photo, bold "ENDS TONIGHT" label — but subscribers who've opened five-plus of these since August are staring at reheated leftovers. The fine print does the honest thing by listing exclusions, which helps trust, but doesn't offset fatigue.
- Judgement: well-built, poorly timed relative to its own campaign history. Ship it as the last email in this arc, then go dark on "35%" messaging for a while.

## What's working

- Single, high-contrast CTA ("Shop Labor Day Sale") in orange against the blue/white palette — impossible to miss.
- Hero offer ("Save up to 35% on everything") is visible with zero scrolling.
- Fine print discloses expiration date/time and exclusions right in the footer — no bait-and-switch feel.
- Warm, real-person lifestyle photo (not stock-generic) reinforces the "rest easy" message.
- Category nav (Mattresses/Pillows/Bedding/Accessories) gives a fallback path if the hero offer doesn't land.

## What's weak

- This is the same "35% + hand-circled highlight" visual Casper has run repeatedly since Labor Day started (08/23 onward) — zero creative refresh for the closing send.
- Subject says "Ends Tonight" but the actual expiration in the fine print is 9/15/26 at 11:59pm PT — today — which is fine, but the brand already used near-identical "Ends Tonight"/"Final Hours" urgency language on 09/07, 09/07, and 09/08.
- No product specificity — "everything" with no hero SKU, price point, or size/model detail for someone deciding whether to click through.
- No social proof or review signal despite "Reviews" being a top-nav link — a missed chance to reinforce the discount with trust cues.

## Recommendations

- 1. Retire the circled-percentage visual for at least a full quarter — five-plus repeats in three weeks is the single biggest thing dragging this down; a genuinely new hero image/layout would do more than any copy tweak.
- 2. Add one named product with a price (e.g., "Casper One Mattress — now $X") next to the general 35% claim so there's something concrete to click into, not just "everything."
- 3. Pull in a star rating or review snippet near the CTA — "Reviews" is already in the nav, so the asset likely exists.
- 4. Tighten the subject/preheader pairing since preview text is currently empty/junk in-render — see below.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Last Call: 35% Off Everything`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Tonight's Your Last Shot at 35% Off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Mattresses, bedding, and more — all discounted until midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `No code needed. Sale ends 11:59pm PT tonight.`
