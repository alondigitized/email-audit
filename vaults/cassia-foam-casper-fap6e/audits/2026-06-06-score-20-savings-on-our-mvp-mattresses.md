# Score 20% savings on our MVP mattresses.*
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-06**
## Executive summary

- Casper pivots to a sports creative — "Armchair coach, mattress pro." / "Win the night, no matter the score" — presumably riding NBA Finals timing in June, and it's a genuinely smart reframe after nearly three weeks of Memorial Day saturation. The two-mattress comparison layout (The One vs. Cloud One, each with a "Save 20%" badge and scannable feature bullets) is clean and gives browsers a real decision to make. The copywriting has energy and the visual hierarchy works.
- The critical problem is the footer: it still carries "Memorial Day Sale" branding alongside 30% and 35% discount badges that directly contradict the 20% offer being sold throughout the body. Any subscriber who opened the May 25–28 sends promising 35% off will immediately register that the deal got worse — and the stale footer looks like a production error, not a deliberate tiered offer. That single inconsistency erodes the trust the sports hook earns.
- This is approximately Casper's fourteenth send in three weeks. The creative is getting better as subscriber patience runs out — same pattern as the excellent May 28 send — but the cadence tax is now severe.

## What's working

- Sports angle is genuinely timely for June (NBA Finals) and rescues the creative from the exhausted "Memorial Day" frame.
- "Save 20%" badges are cleanly placed on both product cards — offer is unambiguous in context.
- The One vs. Cloud One two-up layout with feature bullets is a textbook comparison structure; adding the hybrid option below rounds out the choice architecture.
- "Armchair coach, mattress pro." is punchy wordplay that earns a second read.
- "Shop Now" buttons are visible, high-contrast, and consistently placed after each product block.

## What's weak

- The footer shows "Memorial Day Sale" copy with 30% and 35% discount badges — directly contradicting the 20% in the body; looks like a template not fully updated from prior sends.
- The offer is a visible regression: the May 25 and May 28 sends ran at 35%; a subscriber who saved that email now sees a step backward to 20%, which breeds resentment rather than urgency.
- No visible deadline anywhere in the body — "save 20%" without a cutoff is not a sale, it's a price.
- The asterisk in the subject line ("MVP mattresses.*") plus fine-print in the footer creates trust friction before the customer even clicks.
- Cadence is at critical mass; this is email ~14 in roughly 22 days from Casper.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the footer before anything else** — the Memorial Day 30–35% badges must be removed or replaced with the current 20% offer. This is the highest-leverage fix; it's visually incoherent and actively undercuts trust.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline** — "Offer ends Sunday" or "48 hours only" transforms "save 20%" from ambient noise into a reason to act now.
- 3. **Sharpen the CTAs** — replace "Shop Now" with "Shop The One" and "Shop Cloud One" respectively; the sports theme gives you permission to write "Take the shot" or "Go to overtime" if you want to lean all the way in.
- 4. **Drop send cadence immediately** — batch the next two sends into one; the list needs a rest cycle or unsubscribes will spike.
- 5. **Acknowledge the offer change if intentional** — if 20% is genuinely lower than last week's 35%, frame it explicitly ("New summer pricing, now through the Finals") so it doesn't read as a bait-and-switch.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Open pixel has `display:none` applied:**
```html
<img width="1" height="1" alt="" src="https://cdn.us1.exponea.com/casper-prod/e/...open" style="display: none;">
```
Standard practice omits `display:none` on tracking pixels — it should be `display:block` or unstyled. While major clients (Gmail web, Apple MPP pre-fetch) load images regardless, certain clients respect `display:none` and suppress the network request, silently suppressing open events. This understates open rates.

**CTA links and UTM parameters:** Not analyzable from the truncated source. Confirmed ESP click-wrapping is present (Exponea `casper-prod/e/` path visible in the pixel domain), which implies click tracking is wired — but UTM passthrough on destination URLs cannot be confirmed without full source.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Preheader text is double-hidden:**
```html
<span style="color: transparent; display: none !important; font-size: 0px; ...visibility: hidden; width: 0;">
  Red cards for bad sleep.
</span>
```
The `display: none !important` is the problem. Modern email clients (Gmail, Apple Mail, Samsung Mail) read preheader text from the first parseable text node — `display:none` blocks that parse in many clients. The correct technique uses `max-height:0; overflow:hidden; opacity:0` without `display:none`. As coded, "Red cards for bad sleep." likely does **not** render as preheader text in Gmail and Apple Mail, wasting the slot.

**External font stylesheets (2 requests to brxcdn.com):**
```html
<link href="https://brxcdn.com/us1-app-storage/5a801692.../9c4a4f1a..." rel="stylesheet">
<link href="https://brxcdn.com/us1-app-storage/5a801692.../9c30fa4c..." rel="stylesheet">
```
Outlook (all versions), Gmail Android, and several Samsung clients block external CSS entirely — fonts will silently fall back to the system stack. This is expected behavior for Beefree-generated emails, but the design must be verified with system fallback fonts active.

**Alt text and image accessibility:** Cannot confirm from truncated HTML. The tracking pixel correctly uses `alt=""`.
