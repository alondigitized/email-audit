# Homepage snapshot · casper.com · 2026-05-19
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-19**
## Executive summary

- I opened Casper expecting that Memorial Day sale energy — I've literally been waiting for this markdown to pull the trigger on a new pillow — and instead I get a full-screen cookie consent wall before I can see a single product. What little I can see underneath tells me there IS a "Save up to 30% on mattresses" banner and what looks like a Memorial Day hero starting to form, but the modal nukes the whole first impression. This is a clean, recognizable brand trapped behind a legal gate that it clearly didn't design for mobile conversion.

## What's working

- **"Save up to 30% on mattresses" promo banner** — it's the one thing I can actually read, and it's the exact hook that gets me to click through from a podcast ad. Right message, right moment (Memorial Day).
- **Category nav bar** (Mattresses, Pillows, Furniture, Take the Quiz) — even with the modal up, I can see it, and one tap gets me to Pillows, which is my usual add-on item to hit free shipping.
- **Casper logo legibility** — clean, familiar, instantly tells me I'm in the right place.
- **Top banner carousel** (arrows visible) — suggests more offers are rotating, which signals depth of promotion.

## What's weak

- **Full-viewport privacy modal** — this is the homepage experience on mobile. Before I see a single product photo, I'm reading cookie policy language. For a brand whose entire identity is "sleep trust," opening with a legal wall is a vibe killer.
- **Hero content completely buried** — I can see "Memorial" in stylized script starting to peek out, but the modal sits on top of it. I genuinely don't know what the main value prop or CTA is.
- **Three-button consent design** — "Reject Optional Cookies," "Accept Optional Cookies," "Your Privacy Choices" — that's cognitive load before I even start shopping. Most sites consolidate this to one prominent Accept + a quiet link.
- **No visible trust signals or social proof** — no star ratings, no "X million sleepers" badge, nothing to reinforce the brand before asking me to make a privacy decision.

## Recommendations

- **Shrink the cookie banner to a bottom strip**, not a full takeover — let the Memorial Day hero breathe. Competitors like Brooklinen do this; the GDPR compliance doesn't require a modal.
- **Pin the promo offer inside the modal or banner strip** so that even consent-gated visitors see "30% off Memorial Day" before they decide to accept or reject — that's the reason to stay.
- **Surface the 100-night trial language** somewhere above the fold — it's Casper's single strongest trust signal for a first-timer or a returner like me reconsidering a new model.
- **Add a "returning customer" shortcut** in the nav or banner: "Back for more? Your trial clock resets" — that's exactly the message that would pull me from a podcast ad click to a cart.
