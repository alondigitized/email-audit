# 35% savings on Maxes ends tonight.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-07**
## Executive summary

- This is Casper's seventh consecutive July promotional email, and for the first time in this sprint it narrows its entire focus to a single product line — the Max mattresses — rather than pushing sitewide savings. That is the right call, and it partially rescues this send from the creative exhaustion that dragged the July 6 email ("Ends tomorrow: Save up to 35%") down to 5/10. The "ends tonight" subject is the hardest, most credible deadline language Casper has used since the July 1 opener (9/10), and yesterday's "Ends tomorrow" subject actually set this one up logically. The sequencing is sound.
- The body earns the subject's promise: the hero speaks one message, Dream Max and Snow Max each get their own named section with distinct benefit copy and a dedicated CTA, and the secondary grid ("Don't forget the rest of your rest") smartly fills the scroll without competing with the hero. Structurally, this is the cleanest product-line send in the July sprint.
- The drag is fatigue. This is the same blue-star banner, the same 35% badge, the same sans-serif product grid subscribers have opened every day for a week. "Ends tonight" on day 7 of a sale that started July 1 also tests credibility — if tomorrow brings a new deadline, the trust cost will outweigh whatever lift tonight's urgency generates. Technically solid; contextually tired.

## What's working

- "Ends tonight" is a hard, clock-stopping deadline — sharper than every other subject in this sprint except the July 1 opener
- Narrowing to Max specifically gives this email a product identity the sitewide sends lacked
- Dream Max and Snow Max each get named hero sections with distinct benefit language ("deeply restorative" vs. "advanced cooling for hot sleepers") — actual differentiation, not copy-paste
- The secondary product grid is well-structured — Foundation, Bundles, Hyperice, and Adjustable Bases fill out the scroll without fighting the Max hero
- Clean render — no broken images, no layout breaks visible in the webview

## What's weak

- "Maxes" in the subject reads as casual shorthand; "Max Mattresses" or just "the Max" would feel more intentional and less rushed
- The secondary section shows **30%** savings on Foundation and Bundles — a different tier from the 35% hero — that discrepancy isn't flagged clearly and could read as a discount bait-and-switch on scroll
- The hero asterisk ("35% savings\*") signals fine print exists but gives no indication of scope; after a week of Casper emails a subscriber may wonder what's actually excluded
- No social proof anywhere in a scroll that's asking someone to spend $2,000+ on a mattress — not a single star rating or review count visible
- The blue-star banner creative is visually identical to every prior send in this sprint; one different visual element would reset the fatigue signal

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add one social proof element under Dream Max** — "4.8 stars · 12,000+ reviews" printed directly below the headline would do more for conversion than any layout tweak, especially for a high-consideration purchase
- 2. **Make the deadline concrete in the hero** — "Offer expires tonight at midnight" beats "ends tonight" for a $2,000 decision; vague time-of-day urgency is weaker than a specific cutoff
- 3. **Surface the asterisk scope** — even a single short line ("Excludes bundles and adjustable bases") removes doubt and prevents cart-abandon
- 4. **Retire the blue-star banner** — swap in a different accent color or a lifestyle image to break the visual monotony for the week's final send
- 5. **Clarify the discount tiers upfront** — "Max Mattresses: 35% off. Everything else: 30% off." as a one-line subhead under the hero removes the scroll-surprise
- **Subject Alt A:** `Dream Max + Snow Max: 35% off ends at midnight.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Tonight only — Max Mattresses 35% off.`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dream Max or Snow Max — pick your sleep upgrade before the offer disappears.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Two Max mattresses. One 35% discount. Gone tonight.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**External CSS will be stripped by most clients**
Two `<link>` stylesheets are loaded from `brxcdn.com`:
```
https://brxcdn.com/us1-app-storage/5a801692-033d-11ef-8779-ca524c9ef7ea/media/original/9c4a4f1a-f357-11ef-a8ef-5a86f91ecd85
https://brxcdn.com/us1-app-storage/5a801692-033d-11ef-8779-ca524c9ef7ea/media/original/9c30fa4c-f357-11ef-a8ef-5a86f91ecd85
```
Gmail, Outlook, and Yahoo all strip external `<link>` stylesheets. If these carry font declarations or layout rules not duplicated in the inline `<style>` block, rendering will degrade silently in the majority of inboxes. Wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!-->`/`<!--<![endif]-->` guards so Outlook never sees them — but that means Outlook gets zero custom font stack.

**Segment data exposed in open-tracking pixel URL**
The Exponea open pixel URL contains a base64 payload that decodes to protobuf fields including `frequency_segment` and the string `"Core Sleepers Test"`:
```
...ZnJlcXVlbmN5X3NlZ21lbnQSEkNvcmUgU2xlZXBlcnMgVGVzdA...
/open
```
Internal A/B test variant names and segmentation logic are readable by anyone who inspects the email source. Not a security breach, but leaks campaign test structure.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Duplicate preheader implementation**
The preheader text "Max out your savings before midnight." is injected twice:
- `<span style="display:none !important; ...">` — hides via CSS; unreliable in clients that strip `<style>`
- `<div style="display:none; max-height:0; overflow:hidden;">` followed by ~200 `&#8199;&#847;` and `&shy;` padding characters — the correct, robust approach

The `<span>` is redundant. In clients that ignore CSS (some Outlook versions) it may render as visible blank space before the body content.

**VML and MSO guards are present and correct** — no issues.

**Responsive breakpoint at 620px** with `@media` — standard and expected.

**Tracking pixel alt attribute** is `alt=""` — correct for a non-content image.
