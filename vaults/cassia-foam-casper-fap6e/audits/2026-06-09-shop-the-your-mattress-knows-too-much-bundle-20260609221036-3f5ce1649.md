# Shop the “Your Mattress Knows Too Much” Bundle.
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-09**
## Executive summary

- After three-plus weeks of relentless Memorial Day hammering followed by a brief sports pivot, Casper has finally done something genuinely different: a co-branded campaign with Hulu's *Not Suitable for Work*, a Mindy Kaling comedy about post-grad chaos. The creative is confident and uncommonly coherent — the "Your Mattress Knows Too Much" bundle name, the "first apartment" life-stage copy, and the Hulu partnership all pull in the same direction. This is the first send in the recent history where the subject line and the email body are actually telling the same story.
- The execution has real warmth. The "First Apartment, First Reset" section earns its real estate because it speaks to an actual moment — moving in with strangers, building a life from scratch — rather than just labeling a discount. The Casper X Suitable co-branding banner is distinctive without feeling cluttered.
- The main structural problem: there is no offer. No discount, no member pricing, no bundle savings callout. This is pure brand-content marketing dressed as a shopping email, and a subscriber who opens expecting a deal — especially after a month of 35% off messaging — is going to feel the absence. The "Watch Now" section at the bottom further muddies the commercial intent; the email isn't sure whether it wants you to buy a mattress or subscribe to Hulu.

## What's working

- **Creative break from sale fatigue** — after a month of near-identical discount emails, this partnership email reads as a genuine refresh; the Casper X Suitable co-branding banner is visually distinctive.
- **Life-stage copy lands** — "First Apartment, First Reset. New city. New roommates." is the most human-sounding copy Casper has sent in weeks and targets a real purchase trigger.
- **Bundle is specifically named** — "The One or Cloud One + Original Pillows + Percale Sheet Set" gives the bundle real product identity rather than a vague "sleep bundle."
- **Clean, bug-free render** — no broken images, no overlapping elements, the layout reads well top to bottom.
- **"Shop Foam Mattresses" secondary CTA** — smart to add a category-level escape hatch for subscribers who want a mattress but not the full bundle.

## What's weak

- **No offer, no price, no savings signal** — after a month of 35% off conditioning, a bundle with zero stated value looks like a missed conversion opportunity; there's nothing to make "Shop the Bundle" feel urgent or advantageous.
- **"While supplies last" is vestigial urgency** — no quantity signal, no deadline, reads as boilerplate; subscribers have learned to ignore this phrasing.
- **The "Watch Now" section dilutes purchase intent** — a full-width Hulu CTA at the bottom turns the email into a dual-purpose ad; subscribers who came to shop will bounce before reaching it, and those who click Watch Now are gone.
- **Above-the-fold is all brand collab, no offer** — the large Casper X Suitable banner + Hulu logo block eats the top without surfacing any shopping hook; mobile subscribers have to scroll past a streaming ad before they see the bundle.
- **No social proof** — the prior Memorial Day sends used reviews and star ratings effectively (especially the May 28 "Don't just take our word for it" section); this email drops them entirely, which weakens the ask for a full bundle purchase.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a bundle savings callout** — even "Save $X vs. buying separately" or "Valued at $Y" gives the bundle economic gravity. Without it, "Shop the Bundle" is just a product page link. This is the single highest-impact change.
- 2. **Move the Hulu CTA to a slim footer strip** — the full-width "Watch Now" block competes with the purchase CTA; shrink it to a single-line brand mention ("Inspired by *Not Suitable for Work* on Hulu") and let it live near the footer.
- 3. **Pull the bundle into the above-the-fold zone on mobile** — the co-branding banner is large; consider stacking hero copy + bundle CTA before the extended show description so mobile openers see the offer immediately.
- 4. **Bring back one review** — pull a single star-rated quote from a first-apartment customer ("Perfect for my first real bedroom" or similar); social proof would make the first-apartment narrative feel earned rather than aspirational.
- 5. **Surface a credible deadline** — "Bundle available through June 15" beats "while supplies last" every time.

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Tracking pixel suppressed by `display:none`**
```html
<img width="1" height="1" alt="" src="https://cdn.us1.exponea.com/casper-prod/e/CgxqCN4759eg...open"
```

Open-tracking pixels must be visible (or at minimum `display:block` with 1×1 dimensions) to fire in clients that skip hidden elements (notably some versions of Apple Mail with Mail Privacy Protection and certain corporate proxies). The `display: none` declaration is non-standard for a tracking pixel and risks undercounting opens. Correct to `width:1px; height:1px; border:0; display:block;` or remove the inline style.
