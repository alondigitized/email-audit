# Save up to 35% on our coziest sleep essentials.
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-20**
## Executive summary

- This is Casper's fourth email since May 16 — a Memorial Day sale send mixing a genuine 30%/35% offer with a long, editorial "Your best rest, step-by-step" content block that reads more like an onboarding sequence than a time-limited promo. The tension between the two halves hurts both: the urgency of Memorial Day gets diluted by the educational detour, and the educational content feels rushed because the promo frame is still peeking through.
- Critically, this email walks back the offer. May 17 introduced a compelling stacked structure — extra 5% on top of Memorial Day savings — and May 18 reinforced it. Today that stack is gone; we're back to just "up to 35%," which a subscriber who got either of the last two emails has already been offered. That's a perceived downgrade, not a new reason to act.
- The email is technically clean and the brand voice is consistent, but the creative decision to go educational-mixed-with-promotional on day four of daily sends, while simultaneously softening the offer, is a misstep. This sends weaker than the two days before it.

## What's working

- **Memorial Day offer is front and center** — 30% on mattresses and 35% on everything else is visible without scrolling, and the split framing clearly communicates the two tiers.
- **No render bugs** — layout is clean, images load, no broken sections visible in the render.
- **Step-by-step content section is well-structured** — four labeled steps with imagery gives the long-scroll section a logical flow and adds legitimacy to the brand's expertise positioning.
- **"Shop mattresses with stress-free ZZZ's" CTA block** — the section near the bottom consolidates the purchase path with a clear value-add framing.
- **Footer category nav** (Mattresses, Pillows, Bedding, Accessories) — gives non-mattress shoppers easy lateral movement.

## What's weak

- **The extra-5% stack is gone** — anyone who opened May 17 or May 18 was offered a better deal; this email quietly regresses the offer without explanation.
- **"For the love of sleep" headline buries the offer** — it's a brand-warmth line dropped into what should be an urgency-driven promotional email; it slows the eye before the CTA.
- **Educational content mid-email kills promotional momentum** — four steps on temperature, spine alignment, durability, and "sleepover experiments" belong in a welcome or browse-abandon flow, not in a Memorial Day sale send.
- **No visible deadline** — Memorial Day is implied but no date or countdown is visible in the render; the urgency stays abstract.
- **Fourth consecutive daily email** — cadence fatigue is real; without a meaningfully new hook this send will underperform opens on 5/17 and 5/18.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Restore the stacked offer** — reinstate the "extra 5% on top of sitewide savings" mechanic from 5/17–5/18. The regression is a conversion kill; subscribers feel the offer getting worse, not better.
- 2. **Add a hard deadline** — put "Sale ends Monday" or "Ends midnight May 26" in the hero block. "Memorial Day" is not a deadline; it's a vibe.
- 3. **Cut the step-by-step block or move it below the fold** — let the entire first scroll be sale content, then let the educational section serve as a slow-scroll add if someone is still reading.
- 4. **Replace "For the love of sleep." with the offer headline** — something like "Your Memorial Day discount is still on." or a specific product call-out keeps the reader on mission.
- 5. **Suppress to recent openers / high-intent segments only** — daily sends to the full list are compressing open rates; a smaller, more targeted send on day 4 would protect deliverability and list health.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Ends Monday. Mattresses, pillows, bedding — all on sale.`

The `display:none` is non-standard and may reduce open event fidelity in some clients.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**`#MessageViewBody a` rule**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none; }
```
This Gmail-targeted hack suppresses the browser's default link underline and overrides link color to match surrounding text. Any CTA links inside Gmail webmail will lose visual affordance unless they carry explicit inline `color` and `text-decoration` attributes. If CTA buttons are image-based this is low risk; if any links are text-only, they'll be invisible as links in Gmail.

**Preheader padding technique**
The hidden `<div>` uses the `&#8199;&#847;` (HAIR SPACE + WORD JOINER) sequence followed by `&shy;` soft-hyphens to push inbox preview text. This is a widely-used, accepted technique. No issue.

**Dual preheader blocks**
There are two preheader elements:
1. `<span>` with `color:transparent; display:none!important` — hidden, carries the actual preview text "The latest and greatest in sleep innovation."
2. `<div>` with `display:none; max-height:0` — carries only the padding characters.

The `<span>` using `display:none!important` may be suppressed before the inbox preview is extracted by some clients (particularly Outlook.com), meaning the padding div's invisible content may surface instead. The safer pattern is a single block: visible preheader text followed immediately by the padding characters, all in one hidden container.

**`lang="en"` on `<html>`** — Present. Good for screen reader compatibility.

**VML/Office XML** — `<o:PixelsPerInch>96</o:PixelsPerInch>` and `<w:DontUseAdvancedTypographyReadingMail/>` are present. Standard and correct for Outlook desktop.

**Image `alt` text**: The tracking pixel carries `alt=""` (correct). Product image `alt` attributes cannot be verified from truncated source.
