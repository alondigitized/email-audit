# Ends tomorrow: Save up to 35%*
**Score:** 5/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-06**
## Executive summary

- This is Casper's sixth July sale email in six days — and it shows. The creative is a near-carbon copy of the July 5 send, with the same product grid layout, the same hero copy structure, and the same "35%" headline discount. The "ends tomorrow" hook in the subject is the only meaningful delta from yesterday, and even that is undermined by the fact that Casper has been running this same sale since July 1 (and the holiday it was anchored to ended two days ago).
- The "before the sparks fade" headline is trying to hold onto the Fourth of July moment, but by July 6 that urgency is stale — the sparks already faded. The product grid is genuinely useful: categories are organized by sleep problem (Aches & Pains, Hot Sleep, Tossing & Turning) and the "Upgrade Bedtime" section broadens the appeal. The discount badges are visible and readable.
- The honest read: this email is competent execution of an exhausted campaign. A subscriber who bought into the July 1 opener (the 9/10) and didn't convert by now has seen essentially the same pitch five more times. The send has diminishing-returns written all over it.

## What's working

- Category-based product organization (Aches & Pains, Hot Sleep, Tossing & Turning) is a genuinely useful way to browse — it gives a reason to keep scrolling even if you're not a mattress shopper.
- Discount badges are placed directly on product images, making the offer visible at a glance without reading the body copy.
- "Ends tomorrow" in the subject is the clearest deadline signal in any of the five prior sends — specific and credible if you trust it.
- No render bugs visible: images load, text is legible, layout is intact, buttons are present.
- "Upgrade Bedtime" accessory section (Glow Light, Blanket Robe) broadens the basket without muddying the hero.

## What's weak

- "Before the sparks fade" is post-holiday stale copy — July 4th ended two days ago and this metaphor no longer holds.
- The hero section is nearly identical to the July 5 send; no creative refresh signals this email is different or more urgent.
- "Shop Sale" is the weakest possible CTA — no product specificity, no verb-noun specificity, no reason to click it over the product-level "Shop now" buttons.
- No loyalty or member pricing callout despite the prior sends establishing Casper has a rewards program.
- The asterisk-heavy subject ("Save up to 35%*") and fine-print-heavy footer dilute the "offer feels honest" signal — "up to" discount always reads as the floor being much lower.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Retire the Fourth of July creative entirely.** Replace "before the sparks fade" with something forward-looking: "Last day. Sleep better by the weekend." The holiday anchor has expired and it's actively making the urgency feel fake.
- 2. **Freshen the hero with a countdown or specific end-time.** "Ends tonight at midnight" or a visible clock widget beats "ends tomorrow" because it creates a specific mental deadline.
- 3. **Upgrade the primary CTA** from "Shop Sale" to something category-specific — "Shop Mattresses" or "Find Your Fit" — to give the hero click a destination that feels curated.
- 4. **Add social proof** — even one review quote or a "10,000+ 5-star reviews" line — somewhere in the upper half. Every prior send in this sprint omitted it; this is the last shot to convert the holdouts.
- 5. **Cut one email from this cadence.** Six consecutive sends on the same offer is subscriber-list attrition risk. If revenue data doesn't justify it, this send should not have gone out.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Aches, heat, tossing — there's a Casper for every bad night.`
