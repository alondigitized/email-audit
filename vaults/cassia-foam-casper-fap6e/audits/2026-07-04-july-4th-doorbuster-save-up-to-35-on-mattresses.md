# July 4th Doorbuster: Save up to 35% on mattresses.*
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-04**
## Executive summary

- This is Casper's third July 4th send — following the strong July 1 opener (9/10) and the July 3 reminder (7/10) — and it earns its place more than the July 3 did, but just barely. The "Doorbuster" hook in the subject promises something special: a limited, curated deal carved out of the broader sale. The email doesn't quite deliver on that promise — the body reads as another full-catalogue swing rather than a tight, doorbusters-only feature. The pivot to **bed-frame-plus-mattress bundles** as the organizing logic is smart and differentiates this send from its predecessors, giving it a reason to exist on day 4 of the sale. But "Doorbuster" without a specific hero product or an explicit end-time deadline leaves the credibility of the hook half-finished.
- The bottom half catalog — Bundles, Pillows, Hyperlife Bedding, Accessories — undercuts the doorbuster framing further. Doorbusters imply scarcity and urgency; a wide category sweep implies "everything's on sale, browse at your leisure." The two creative modes are in tension and the email can't resolve them cleanly.
- Verdict: better than July 3's fatigued reminder, weaker than July 1's clean opener. The pairing mechanic earns real UX credit; the subject line promise goes partially unfulfilled.

## What's working

- **Pairing mechanic is distinctive.** The bed-frame + mattress combo grid ("Foundation + The One," "Haven Bed Frame + Snow Max," etc.) gives customers a decision shortcut and something to browse that's structurally different from the prior two sends.
- **Hero discount is prominent and above the fold.** "Save up to 35% on mattresses" lands at eye level; no hunt required to find the offer.
- **Four product bundles give depth without clutter.** Each row has a lifestyle image, product names, and a "Shop Now" button — scannable and clean.
- **"Shop more holiday weekend savings" lower section adds breadth** without competing with the hero — it correctly lives below the fold.

## What's weak

- **"Doorbuster" in the subject is unfulfilled in the body.** Nothing in the email reads as a special, time-limited, or quantity-restricted doorbuster deal — it's the same 35% sitewide sale with a pairing wrapper.
- **No visible urgency deadline in the body.** "July 4th" in the subject implies today-only, but nothing in the rendered email states when the sale ends — a critical miss for a third-send that depends on FOMO to close.
- **CTAs are all generic "Shop Now."** Four pairs, four identical buttons. No differentiation by price tier, best-seller status, or deal depth — a missed opportunity to guide intent.
- **Cadence is at maximum stretch.** Three Casper July 4th emails in four days; the July 3 send already showed strain, and this one risks unsubscribe pressure from less-engaged subscribers.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Deliver on the doorbuster promise.** Feature one or two explicitly time-gated, high-discount products front and center (e.g., "Today only — 40% off The One Queen") so "Doorbuster" means something. The current body reads as a sale reminder, not a door-buster.
- 2. **Add a visible countdown or end-time statement.** "Sale ends tonight at midnight" or a countdown graphic — placed directly under the hero CTA — would close the urgency loop the subject opened.
- 3. **Differentiate the CTA copy per product tier.** Replace "Shop Now" with something like "Shop The One (from $X)" or "Best Seller — Shop Now" on the top pair to guide decisions.
- 4. **Drop the accessories/bedding bottom section.** On a "Doorbuster: mattresses" send, kitchen-sinking Accessories dilutes the urgency signal.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Today only: 35% off mattresses + free frame pairings`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Doorbuster closes tonight — up to 35% off Casper mattresses`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Four curated sleep setups. One day to grab them.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Pick your perfect pairing before the sale ends tonight.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Web fonts loaded from external CDN (brxcdn.com):**
```html
<link href="https://brxcdn.com/us1-app-storage/5a801692.../media/original/9c4a4f1a-..." rel="stylesheet">
<link href="https://brxcdn.com/us1-app-storage/5a801692.../media/original/9c30fa4c-..." rel="stylesheet">
```
Gmail (web/Android) and all Outlook versions will block these and fall back to system fonts. No `font-family` fallback stack is visible in the `<style>` block — confirm that fallback stacks are defined inline on typographic elements.

**Global link color/decoration reset:**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none; }
```
This suppresses default link affordances for all anchors within Gmail's `#MessageViewBody` container. If CTAs rely solely on button background color to communicate interactivity, this creates an accessibility issue (WCAG 1.4.1 — use of color alone). Acceptable if every CTA is a button with visible contrast, but needs verification against the full render.

**Open pixel uses `display:none` inline:**
```html
<img width="1" height="1" alt="" src="https://cdn.us1.exponea.com/.../open" style="display: none;">
```
Some clients (older iOS Mail, certain Outlook builds) ignore `display:none` on images and render a 1×1 phantom pixel gap. Standard practice; acceptable risk, but worth noting.

**MSO/VML and viewport meta are both present** — correct for cross-client support. `lang="en"` on `<html>` is present. `alt=""` on tracking pixel is correct.
