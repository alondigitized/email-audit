# Homepage snapshot · casper.com · 2026-05-17
**Score:** 3/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-17**
## Executive summary

- I landed on Casper and immediately got buried under a cookie consent wall before I could even see the hero. The promo strip says "Save up to 30% on mattresses" which, as someone who literally bought her first Casper at a holiday markdown and still watches for the next one, is exactly the right hook — but I can barely see it because a full-screen privacy modal ate the page. Casper clearly has a Memorial Day push going, which is my buying window, but they fumbled the first impression hard.

## What's working

- **Promo strip copy** — "Save up to 30% on mattresses" is plain, honest, and hits my trigger. I read that and I'm interested.
- **Category navigation bar** — Mattresses, Pillows, Furniture, Take the Quiz. Four taps, no mystery. Pillows is right there, which is how I always add one to hit free shipping anyway.
- **Memorial Day timing** — Catching that partial hero text tells me the campaign calendar is actually aligned to my purchase cycle. If the modal weren't blocking it, this could land.

## What's weak

- **Full-screen cookie consent modal** — This is the entire above-the-fold experience on mobile. I see a privacy wall, not a mattress brand. By the time I dismiss it, my attention is already taxed.
- **No 100-night trial mention visible** — That trial story is why I bought the Original. It should be in the promo strip or sticky header, not buried. First-time scrollers and returning buyers both need that reassurance.
- **No returning-customer hook** — I own a Casper. Nothing on this screen acknowledges that. No "welcome back," no member pricing, no loyalty callout. Feels like I'm a stranger.
- **Three-button cookie UX** — "Reject Optional Cookies," "Accept Optional Cookies," "Your Privacy Choices" — that's decision fatigue before I've even seen a mattress.

## Recommendations

- **Suppress the full-screen modal on return visitors** — use a slim cookie banner at the bottom instead of a page-blocking overlay; it's legal and far less punishing.
- **Move the 100-night trial into the promo strip** — rotate it with the 30% off message so the brand's core trust story is always one scroll away from the hero.
- **Add a "welcome back" or account-recognition nudge** — even a "Sign in to see member pricing" chip in the header would make returning customers feel seen.
- **Make the Memorial Day deadline explicit** — swap "Save up to 30% on mattresses" for "Save up to 30% — ends May 26" so the urgency is baked into the strip, not implied.
