# Don't snooze on savings up to 35% this Memorial Day!
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-23**
## Executive summary

- This is Casper's sixth email in eight days, and fatigue is the dominant story. The creative is nearly identical to the May 21 "Doorbuster" send: same hero phrasing, same 35% offer, same product grid. The sleep pun in the subject ("Don't snooze on savings") was already on thin ice two sends ago — by now it reads as lazy copy on autopilot. That said, the email itself is visually clean, the offer is clear, and the discount-at-checkout mechanic removes friction. The execution is fine; the problem is volume.
- The "Sleep Tip #6" block is the single most confusing creative choice in this send. Dropping a humidity factoid ("indoor humidity should range from 30% to 50%") into the middle of a time-sensitive doorbuster sale actively breaks the urgency you're trying to build. It looks like an editorial module was copy-pasted from a newsletter template without anyone asking whether it belonged here.
- The product grid shows four equally weighted items — Dream Max, Original Pillow, Percale Sheet Set, Glow Light — with identical "Shop now" buttons and no pricing. In a sale email, the absence of a before/after price or a "was $X, now $Y" callout is a genuine missed click opportunity. Most subscribers won't click a generic "Shop now" button when they don't know what they're clicking toward.

## What's working

- **Offer is explicit and at the top.** "Save up to 35% on mattresses & up to 30% on everything else" is visible in the hero without scrolling.
- **No promo code friction.** "Discount applied at checkout" removes a common abandonment point — the right call for a sale of this size.
- **Clean render, no bugs.** Layout holds, images load, typography is clear throughout.
- **Memorial Day framing is on-calendar.** The send date (May 23) is appropriate for a last-push before the holiday.
- **Contact block with real channels.** Phone number, email, and store-finder give the email a credibility floor most DTC brands skip.

## What's weak

- **Six emails in eight days with near-identical offers.** The cadence has moved past urgency and into fatigue; this send will hit an inbox where the last five had the same headline.
- **"Sleep Tip #6" is dead weight in a doorbuster email.** A humidity fact destroys the urgency arc and makes the email feel like two emails stapled together.
- **No pricing in the product grid.** Four products, four "Shop now" buttons, zero price anchors — no reason to click one over another.
- **Subject pun has expired.** "Don't snooze on savings" is the third variation of this exact wordplay across the last two sends.
- **Glow Light as a featured product.** Pairing a night light alongside Dream Max in a mattress-first sale email dilutes the hero category signal.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Cut the Sleep Tip block entirely.** Every line of copy in a doorbuster email should push toward conversion. This one pushes toward Wikipedia.
- 2. **Add sale pricing to the product grid.** "Dream Max — Was $3,395, now $2,886" earns the click. "Shop now" doesn't.
- 3. **Collapse to a single featured hero product with a specific CTA.** Lead with the Dream Max, give it a discounted price and a "Shop the Dream Max" button — then let the secondary grid carry the rest.
- 4. **Pull back to one more email max before Memorial Day.** The cadence is working against Casper's brand equity; the May 17 email scored an 8 because it was early and felt purposeful. This one arrives exhausted.
- 5. **Give the urgency a specific deadline.** "Memorial Day" is vague. "Sale ends Monday at midnight" creates real pressure.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Mattresses up to 35% off — sale ends Monday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your last chance: Memorial Day savings end soon`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dream Max, pillows, and sheets — all on sale, no code needed.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Up to 35% off mattresses. Discount auto-applies at checkout.`
