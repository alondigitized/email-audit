# Designed with your spine in mind
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-18**
## Executive summary

- This is Casper trying something genuinely new: "Chiropractic Founders Day" content marketing dressed up as an editorial deep-dive on spinal support, anchored by an actual named employee (Jonathan Eilenberg, "Casper's Professional Ergonomist") and an ACA-endorsed product callout. After a solid month of "35% + extra 5%" discount hammering, this is the first send in the recent history that leads with expertise and mechanism rather than a coupon code, and it's a smart change of pace.
- The problem is execution length and offer clarity. The email runs extremely long — a hero banner, a founder's-day section, a bio module, a product comparison, an anatomy diagram, and a neck/pillow upsell — and by the time a reader scrolls through all of it, the actual discount math (25% top banner vs. 20% Dream Max vs. 10% pillow) is muddled across three different numbers with three different asterisks. The "science" angle is a good differentiator but it's buried under promo clutter that dilutes the credibility play it's going for.
- Net: interesting positioning experiment, poor execution discipline. It reads like two emails (an editorial/authority piece and a standard sale email) mashed into one.

## What's working

- The top banner ("Save up to 25% on sleep solutions") gives an immediate, scannable offer before any scrolling.
- Featuring a real person with a title and headshot (Jonathan Eilenberg, Professional Ergonomist) is a credible trust signal rarely seen in this brand's recent sends.
- The ACA-endorsed badge on Dream Max and Snow Max adds third-party validation that pure discount emails lack.
- The cushion/align/support cross-section diagram is a clean, on-brand way to visualize a real product benefit.
- Clear category footer nav (Mattresses, Pillows, Bedding, Accessories) helps browsers self-select.

## What's weak

- Three different, unreconciled discounts appear (25% hero, 20% Dream Max, 10% pillow) with no single clear "the" offer — visual hierarchy doesn't resolve to one primary CTA.
- The email is very long for a single send; the ergonomist story, the product grid, the anatomy diagram, and the pillow upsell each compete for the "hero" role.
- "Chiropractic Founders Day" is an odd, unexplained occasion — it's never clarified what this manufactured holiday actually is or why now, which undercuts the credibility angle it's trying to build.
- The Snow Max/free adjustable base tile is visually cramped next to Dream Max, with a lot of small, dense text competing for attention.
- No urgency or expiration date anywhere in the email, despite this being framed as a "Founders Day" event — feels open-ended and skippable.

## Recommendations

- 1. Cut this to one offer and one hero product. Pick either the Dream Max (ACA-endorsed, ergonomist-backed) or the sitewide 25%, not three overlapping discounts — right now nothing reads as "the" deal.
- 2. Move Jonathan's quote/credibility block higher, right under the hero, and cut the standalone bio module — let expertise support the CTA rather than sitting in its own detour section.
- 3. Shorten the whole email by ~40%: drop either the anatomy diagram or the neck/pillow section — both are good content but this send doesn't need all of it.
- 4. Add a real deadline to "Chiropractic Founders Day" (e.g., "Ends Sunday") — an invented occasion with no urgency reads as filler, not an event.
- 5. Clarify what "Chiropractic Founders Day" actually is in one line of copy — right now it's a label with no explanation.

- **Subject Alt B:** `Chiropractic Founders Day: 25% off spine-friendly sleep`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Meet the ergonomist behind Casper's most supportive mattress`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Science-backed support, now 20% off Dream Max`
