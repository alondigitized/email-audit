# Black Friday just got better: Save up to 35% on mattresses*
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-11-24**
## Executive summary

- This is the strongest Casper Black Friday send in the recent run. The hero is a lime "Black Friday Doorbuster" pill, a giant "35%" on navy, "Our absolute best discount for Black Friday," and a white "Shop Maxes" button. The offer is explicit, the hierarchy is clear, and the stars-on-navy look matches the 11/21 and 11/23 sends. Unlike "Zero plans = big plans" (11/20) or "Time for an upgrade!" (11/21), the subject carries the offer, so the subject and the hero agree.
- The body is long but well organized. Two hero products (Dream Max and Snow Max) each get a "Save 35%" badge and a button. A "More ways to upgrade for less" section then adds bundles at 35%, pillows at 20% and The One + Cloud One at 30%. The weakness is what's missing: no promo code is visible, which was the anchor in the 11/21 and 11/23 emails (5-BFCM). The tiered discounts also dilute the "one offer" focus. It's also the roughly seventh Casper Black Friday send this month, so fatigue is the real risk.
- Overall it is a solid, clear promo that does its job, with a better subject line than its recent predecessors.

## What's working

- The hero "35%" in oversized lime type on navy is the first thing the eye lands on, and "Our absolute best discount for Black Friday" gives it credibility.
- The subject's "up to 35% on mattresses" matches the hero, so there is no bait-and-switch between inbox and body.
- Each Max product has a "Save 35%" badge, a one-line benefit ("6° cooler for 12+ hours," "pressure-relieving foam") and its own CTA.
- The "Limited Time" script and "Doorbuster" pill add urgency without shouting.
- Consistent button styling (white pills) and clear sections make the long scroll easy to navigate.

## What's weak

- There is no visible promo code or auto-applied message, even though earlier sends featured 5-BFCM prominently. A shopper can't tell whether the discount applies automatically.
- The "up to 35%" headline sits above a stack of 35%, 30% and 20% offers, which blurs the single message.
- The deadline is only in the fine print (the footer mentions an expiry of 12/2/26), so the hero has no credible "ends on X" date.
- Of the three buttons below the Max section, two are a generic "Shop now" and the third is "Shop Maxes" or similar, so the copy is weak.
- The long scroll with six near-identical product blocks makes the lower half skimmable at best. There are no reviews or ratings anywhere, so no social proof.

## Recommendations

- 1. Add a clear deadline and an auto-applied or code line to the hero, such as "Ends 12/2. Savings applied at checkout." This is the single biggest lift.
- 2. Cut the lower section to two or three blocks and lead with the best-selling mattress rather than bundles and pillows.
- 3. Add star ratings or a short review quote under Dream Max and Snow Max.
- 4. Replace the "Shop now" buttons with specific copy like "Shop bundles" and "Shop pillows."
- 5. Show the savings as a price ("Queen from $X") to make the 35% concrete.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Dream Max + Snow Max, 35% off through 12/2. Savings applied at checkout.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The HTML source is structurally sound. It has a `lang` attribute, a `<title>`, a viewport meta tag, MSO conditionals, `role="presentation"` layout tables and a preheader. The QA run reported 0 hard issues and 4 warnings. Two of those warnings are header-capture limits of the relay. Another is a false positive. The source is truncated, so the footer, the unsubscribe markup and most links could not be inspected.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Probe coverage is partial.** 12 material links were probed and 21 were skipped (footer, utility and social links are deprioritized). No broken or redirecting links were reported among the probed set. The 21 skipped links are unverified.
- **Plain-text part is mostly URLs.** QA reports 17,316 of 19,154 characters (about 90%) of the plain-text part are URLs. That is typical of long ESP click-tracking URLs, but it makes the text alternative close to unreadable and raises spam-filter risk. This is a real, if low-severity, finding.
- **Open pixel is hidden with inline CSS.** `<img width="1" height="1" src="https://cdn.us1.exponea.com/casper-prod/e/…/open" style="display: none;">` is the Exponea open-tracking pixel. Clients that skip `display:none` images won't register opens, so open rates may be undercounted. This is a measurement caveat, not a rendering defect.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Alt-text warning is a false positive.** QA flags the `…/open` image as missing alt text. The source shows `alt=""`, which is the correct markup for a decorative or tracking pixel. No action is needed. The check should exclude 1x1 pixels.
- **Web fonts are progressive enhancement.** Two stylesheets are loaded via `<link>` from `brxcdn.com`, inside `<!--[if !mso]><!-->`. Gmail and several other clients strip `<link>` tags, so a fallback font stack must exist. The truncated source doesn't let me confirm that it does.
- **Preheader is duplicated but harmless.** A `display:none` span ("Sleep on the best.") is followed by a `display:none` div of padding characters (`&#8199;&#847;`, `&shy;`). Both are standard preheader hacks. The first span uses `mso-hide:all`, but the padding div has no `mso-hide`. Outlook desktop may therefore render that block's whitespace, a minor risk.
- **Other markup is correct.** I found no problems with `lang="en"`, `<title>`, `role="presentation"`, the Apple data-detector override, the `@media (max-width:620px)` stacking rules or the `.desktop_hide` handling.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unresolved merge tokens (`{{…}}`, `%%…%%`, `[FIRST_NAME]`) appear in the visible source or the subject line. The truncated remainder is unchecked.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post headers were not found.** QA attributes this to the AgentMail relay possibly not capturing them. The status is therefore unconfirmed, not a confirmed defect. If the headers are truly absent, Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules (RFC 8058 one-click) would be violated. Verify against the raw message at the sending MTA.
- **Authentication-Results was not found.** SPF, DKIM and DMARC alignment are unknown. This is a relay limitation. The From domain is `e.casper.com`, so check DNS for SPF, DKIM and DMARC on that subdomain directly.
- **The unsubscribe link is unverified.** QA says one is always probed, and it reported no failure. The physical-address footer was not visible in the truncated source.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Insufficient data. The visible source doesn't include the CTA hrefs, so UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) and landing-page alignment couldn't be checked. The probe found no broken destinations. The subject's trailing asterisk ("up to 35% on mattresses*") implies a terms disclaimer, but whether it exists and links to terms is unverified.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Confirm the headers at the source.** Check the raw message for `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, and for SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass on `e.casper.com`. Fix the AgentMail header capture so this stops producing ambiguous warnings.
2. **Trim the plain-text part.** Use a human-readable text alternative with fewer or shorter URLs, for example one tracked link per CTA.
3. **Suppress the QA false positive.** Skip the alt-text check for 1x1 or `display:none` tracking pixels that already have `alt=""`.
4. **Add `mso-hide:all` to the padding preheader div.** This stops Outlook desktop from rendering the filler.
5. **Check the web-font fallbacks.** Confirm every `font-family` stack ends in a safe system font, since Gmail will drop the `brxcdn.com` stylesheets.
6. **Re-run with the full HTML.** Include the untruncated source and probe the 21 skipped links, so UTM consistency, footer compliance and merge tokens can be verified.
