# Out of office. Into deeper sleep
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-27**
## Executive summary

- Casper wraps a familiar discount stack in a summer OOO pun, and the creative conceit mostly earns its keep. "Out of Office. Into Deeper Sleep." is a clean, brand-appropriate hook that pivots the seasonal moment from "you're traveling" to "you're staying home — make it great." The execution follows through with a coherent staycation narrative, and the "Your staycation plans? Cancelled." section is the most interesting copy in the email — genuinely funny, on-brand, and differentiated from the six previous sends.
- The problem is the offer. The "Save an extra 5%" banner at the top is the same stacked discount mechanic Casper used on June 22 for Prime Day. Subscribers who've been paying attention — and they've been getting hit every two or three days — will immediately recognize this as the same extended promo wearing a new costume. The thematic wrapper adds creative value but can't fully mask the discount fatigue at this cadence.
- Verdict: a well-dressed version of the same email Casper has been sending for two weeks. The OOO hook is legitimately good creative; the underlying offer is exhausted.

## What's working

- **Subject line pun lands.** "Out of Office. Into Deeper Sleep." is compact, witty, and instantly legible — a step up from the blunt discount subjects of recent sends.
- **"Staycation plans? Cancelled." section.** The mock-itinerary format (No flights, No overbooked hotels, No jet lag) is the most differentiated piece of copy in the email and aligns the product benefit to a real emotional moment.
- **Visual hierarchy is clean.** Hero first, discount banner visible at scroll-start, product grid below — the eye travels naturally down the page.
- **Consistent naming throughout.** Dream, Dream Max, Cloud One, Best-Selling Bundle — product names are legible and consistent with prior sends, building recognition.
- **Summer timing is right.** Late June OOO messaging is seasonally appropriate, unlike a repromoted Memorial Day offer in July.

## What's weak

- **The "extra 5%" banner is stale.** It appeared identically in the June 22 Prime Day email and feels like an evergreen discount toggle rather than a real subscriber benefit.
- **CTA copy is generic throughout.** Every button reads "Shop Now" — no specificity about the product or the offer behind the click.
- **No urgency signal.** The email has no deadline, countdown, or end-date — which matters when subscribers have been told the sale is on since at least June 16.
- **Discount regression is visible.** The June 17 send offered "up to 35% sitewide"; this send caps at "up to 30% + extra 5%," which nets the same but looks like a step down on first read.
- **The staycation section buries the lede.** The best creative idea (the cancelled itinerary) appears below the fold rather than in the hero.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Lead with the staycation concept in the hero, not the discount banner.** Flip the structure: open on the "Staycation plans? Cancelled." hook, let it breathe, then reveal the offer. The humor is the differentiator — lead with it.
- 2. **Give the CTAs a job.** Replace every "Shop Now" with something specific: "Shop the Dream Max," "Build My Bundle," "Find My Pillow." This alone will lift click-through.
- 3. **Add a credible deadline.** "Offer ends July 6" or "Sale ends Sunday" converts the open into urgency without fabricating scarcity.
- 4. **Retire or evolve the "extra 5%" banner.** It appeared on June 22 for Prime Day. Using it again 5 days later without new framing trains subscribers to ignore it.
- 5. **Surface one hero product, not six.** The product grid is dense. Pick one mattress, write a single sharp sentence about why it's the perfect staycation upgrade, and link it directly.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your bed called. It wants you home.`
- **Subject Alt B:** `The only trip worth booking right now`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Save an extra 5% on top of up to 30% off — through Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `No luggage fees. No middle seats. Just 30% off sleep upgrades.`

### 1. Technical Summary

Email is built on Braze (brxcdn.com CDN) with Bloomreach/Exponea handling open-tracking analytics. The truncated HTML reveals two confirmed infrastructure issues: externally-linked stylesheets that major clients will strip, and an open-tracking pixel with an unusually long encoded URL that may trip spam filters.

---

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**External CSS via `<link>` tags — high risk**
```html
<link href="https://brxcdn.com/us1-app-storage/5a801692-.../9c4a4f1a-..." rel="stylesheet">
<link href="https://brxcdn.com/us1-app-storage/5a801692-.../9c30fa4c-..." rel="stylesheet">
```
Gmail (web and Android), Outlook.com, and Yahoo strip `<link>` stylesheets. Any layout or font rules in these files are silently dropped for the majority of recipients. All critical styles must be inlined or duplicated in the `<style>` block already present in `<head>`.

**Open pixel — oversized URL**
```
https://cdn.us1.exponea.com/casper-prod/e/CgxqCN4759egjazxb5gSILnQptspVGrBBBP1rDCw-7FzVfHo986e23v...
```
The pixel URL is ~400+ characters of base64-encoded payload. Some spam filters score long encoded query strings negatively. Confirm Exponea cannot use a shorter redirect slug instead.

**Remaining links not assessable** — HTML is truncated before the body CTA links appear; UTM and click-tracking coverage cannot be confirmed from the provided source.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Link underlines suppressed globally**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none; }
```
This removes the default underline from all anchor elements as rendered in Apple Mail / Outlook. WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.1 requires links to be distinguishable from surrounding text by more than color alone. If CTAs rely solely on button background color (not underline), this is only a concern for inline text links — verify none exist.

**Preheader padding technique — acceptable**
The `&#8199;&#847;` (HAIR SPACE + WORD JOINER) sequence followed by `&shy;` characters is a standard method to collapse preview-pane bleed. No issue, just noting it is intentional.

**MSO/VML headers — correct**
```html
xmlns:v="urn:schemas-microsoft-com:vml"
xmlns:o="urn:schemas-microsoft-com:office:office"
<o:PixelsPerInch>96</o:PixelsPerInch>
<o:AllowPNG/>
```
Properly included for Outlook desktop rendering. No issues.

**Tracking pixel alt attribute — correct**
```html
<img width="1" height="1" alt="" src="https://cdn.us1.exponea.com/...">
```
Empty alt is appropriate for a tracking pixel.
