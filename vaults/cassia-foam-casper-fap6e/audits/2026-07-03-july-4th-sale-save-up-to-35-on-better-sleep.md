# July 4th Sale: Save up to 35% on better sleep*
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-03**
## Executive summary

- This is Casper's second July 4th send — the sale-in-progress reminder following the July 1 opener that scored 9/10 here. Where that email was the strongest structural piece in the recent sprint, this one is the inevitable follow-up: same sale, same "up to 35%" mechanic, now organized as a product-category grid. The creative conceit is minimal ("while you're celebrating" / "4th of July recovery") but it's serviceable — the real job of this email is catalog navigation, not brand storytelling.
- The grid execution is competent. Four categories — Max Mattresses, Adjustable Bases, Bundles, Pillows — each with a product photo, a "Save 35%" badge, and a category CTA. That's the right playbook for a mid-sale reminder. The problem is that this send is structurally nearly identical to the June 24 Adjustable Bases send (9/10) and the July 1 July 4th opener — Casper is running the same creative muscle three times in a week, which blunts urgency.
- The bottom "Save up to 35%*" repeat block is redundant but harmless. The SMS sign-up call and "take our mattress quiz" both compete for attention in the footer without adding value for a user who's already in-sale. Net judgment: clean and functional, but derivative — a 7 where 8 was available with one structural change.

## What's working

- **Category grid is scannable** — four product sections with consistent photo / badge / CTA structure let a buyer navigate quickly to their category.
- **"Save 35%" badge is visually prominent** on every product image — the offer reads instantly without copy.
- **Hero copy is punchy** — "Save up to 35% while you're celebrating" ties the sale directly to the holiday moment without overworking it.
- **No render bugs visible** — images load cleanly, text is legible, badges align, layout holds.
- **Footer repeat block reinforces the top offer** — the dark "Save up to 35%" close section functions as a second CTA sweep for scroll-skimmers.

## What's weak

- **No urgency signal** — nowhere in the email does it say when this sale ends. The July 1 opener set a "starts now" frame; this send doesn't advance the clock. A subscriber has no reason to click today vs. tomorrow.
- **"Recovery" hook is underdeveloped** — "Prep for your 4th of July recovery today" is a mildly clever frame for sleep after the holiday, but the body copy doesn't follow through on it at all. Every category section reverts to generic product language.
- **Pillow section discount badge shows "Save 20%"** while the hero and footer both say "up to 35%" — the inconsistency is visible in the render and reads as fine print bait-and-switch for pillow buyers specifically.
- **SMS sign-up + mattress quiz in footer** — two soft-conversion asks compete with the primary sale CTA at scroll-end; neither adds value at this stage of a sale cycle.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a sale deadline to the hero** — "Sale ends July 5" or a countdown badge is the single highest-leverage change. Without it, this email generates browse sessions, not purchases. The June 29 "Prime Time Deals end tonight" send (7/10) proved that even a blunt deadline drives action.
- 2. **Reconcile the discount across categories** — if Pillows are only 20% off, either badge them differently in the grid ("Save up to 20%") or lead the hero with the honest range ("Save 20–35%"). The current state invites click-abandonment from pillow buyers.
- 3. **Commit to the recovery hook or drop it** — either use "recovery sleep" as the editorial frame for every category section (Max = deep recovery, Adjustable = elevation for soreness, Pillows = neck support after fireworks crowds) or replace it with a plain "July 4th Sale — ends Sunday" line.
- 4. **Cut the SMS sign-up from this email** — it dilutes the sale close. Save it for a dedicated list-grow send, not mid-sale.
- **Subject Alt A:** `July 4th Sale ends Sunday — save up to 35%*`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Up to 35% off: mattresses, bases, bundles + pillows`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Four categories, one sale — shop before it's over.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Recovery sleep sorted. Sale ends this weekend.`

### 2. Link & Tracking Issues

**Open-tracking pixel anomaly**
The 1×1 tracking pixel is rendered with `display: none`:
```html
<img width="1" height="1" alt="" src="https://cdn.us1.exponea.com/casper-prod/e/CgxqCN4759..." style="display: none;">
```
`display: none` suppresses the pixel in some clients (notably Gmail image-caching proxies may not fire the request on re-open). Standard practice is to omit `display:none` and use `width`/`height` alone. This may undercount open rates.

**External stylesheets — two separate requests**
```html
<link href="https://brxcdn.com/us1-app-storage/5a801692-033d-11ef-8779-ca524c9ef7ea/media/original/9c4a4f1a-..." rel="stylesheet">
<link href="https://brxcdn.com/us1-app-storage/5a801692-033d-11ef-8779-ca524c9ef7ea/media/original/9c30fa4c-..." rel="stylesheet">
```
Both are correctly wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!-->` so Outlook never attempts to load them. However, two separate stylesheet requests increase render-blocking latency on clients that do load them (iOS Mail, Apple Mail). Could be consolidated.

**CTA links not assessable** — HTML is truncated before any anchor tags appear. UTM coverage and click-tracking wrappers cannot be verified.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Preheader implementation is correct but verbose**
Two-element preheader pattern used:
- `<span>` with `color:transparent; display:none !important; font-size:0px; line-height:0px; height:0; max-height:0; opacity:0; visibility:hidden` — carries the preview text "Get ahead of the celebration."
- `<div>` with ~130 repetitions of `&#8199;&#847;` (word joiner + invisible separator) plus `&shy;` soft hyphens — pads the preview to prevent body bleed.

This is a known technique and functionally correct, but the sheer volume of invisible filler characters adds ~4 KB to the HTML payload for no additional benefit beyond ~60–80 characters of padding.

**Tracking pixel `alt=""`** — correct; empty alt on a tracking pixel is the right call (avoids screen-reader noise).

**`lang="en"` on `<html>`** — present, good.

**Viewport meta** — present (`width=device-width, initial-scale=1`), good.

**Remaining rendering concerns cannot be verified** — the truncated source cuts off before the body table content; image `alt` text on product images, button contrast, and table structure are unassessable.
