# Moving in made easy: save 25% on bundles*
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-30**
## Executive summary

- This is a clean, well-built email — the bright green "Save 25%" pop against the blue background is the strongest visual hook in the whole send, and the layout does a good job segmenting three distinct bundles (Freshman, best-selling, Snug) with clear product specifics under each. But zoom out to the brand history and the story gets messier: this is Casper's third distinct discount framing in eight days — 25% on July 22, a quieter 20% on July 28, and now back up to 25% again on July 30, this time re-skinned as "moving in made easy" instead of "back to school." A subscriber who opened Monday's email and this one within 48 hours will notice the percentage bounced around with no clear logic.
- The execution quality is genuinely good — no render bugs, clear CTAs, specific product callouts (Twin XL topper, Percale sheet set) — but the underlying promo cadence is starting to blur together, which is the actual risk here, not the creative.
- Judgement: strong build, tired campaign. This send earns clicks from anyone who hasn't unsubscribed yet, but it's coasting on repetition rather than a fresh reason to act now.

## What's working

- The neon-green "Save 25%" callout is the clear focal point — eye lands there first, no competition from other elements.
- Bundle sections (Freshman, best-selling sleep bundle) include specific component call-outs — "2" Topper (Twin XL)," "Percale Sheet Set," "Original Pillow" — which gives shoppers real product detail before they click.
- CTA buttons are consistent, high-contrast navy pills with unambiguous copy ("Shop Bundles," "Shop Freshman Bundle," "Shop now").
- Good product-line breadth at the bottom (Mattresses, Toppers, Pillows, Glow Light) gives multiple entry points without cluttering the hero.
- No layout bugs — every image renders, no overlapping text, no broken badges.

## What's weak

- The 25% headline carries a small asterisk ("*Savings based on Comp. Value") directly under the first CTA — a soft caveat, but it's the kind of thing that erodes trust on repeat viewing across sends.
- No urgency deadline anywhere except a vague "Limited Time Offer" badge on one bundle — no date, no countdown, nothing that forces action today.
- Zero social proof — no star ratings, review counts, or testimonials anywhere in a fairly long scroll.
- The email is long (7+ distinct product sections) with no single dominant CTA — hierarchy fragments across "Shop Bundles," "Shop Freshman Bundle," "Shop Snug," and four "Shop now" buttons, diluting the primary action.
- Coming three days after the July 28 20%-off send, the percentage bump back to 25% reads as inconsistent rather than escalating urgency.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Fix the discount-messaging whiplash first.** Pick one number and one campaign name for the full back-to-school window instead of alternating 25%/20%/25% every few days — repeat subscribers will notice the flip-flop faster than they notice the offer.
- 2. **Add a real deadline.** Replace the vague "Limited Time Offer" badge with an actual date ("Ends August 3") to give the 25% a credible reason to act now instead of later.
- 3. **Cut the email by 2-3 sections.** Consolidate "Freshman Bundle," "best-selling sleep bundle," and the 4-tile grid — three distinct bundle pitches plus a product grid is too many asks in one send.
- 4. **Add a proof point.** A single "4.8★ from 30,000+ reviews" line near the hero would do more for conversion than another product tile.
- 5. **Tuck the fine-print asterisk** into the footer disclosures instead of directly under the primary CTA — keep the path to click friction-free.

- **Preheader Alt A:** `Mattress, topper, sheets, pillow — one easy checkout.`
