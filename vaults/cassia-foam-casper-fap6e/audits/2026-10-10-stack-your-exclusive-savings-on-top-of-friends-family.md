# Stack your exclusive savings on top of Friends & Family.
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-10-10**
## Executive summary

- This is a near-repeat of the 10/08 send ("Friends save. Subscribers save +5% more.*"). It has the same Subscriber Exclusive banner and the same FF-EXT5 code. The only real change is the body, which swaps six mattress cards for four bundle tiles. If you've been getting the Friends & Family sends since 09/27, this reads as the fifth or sixth version of one promo. The subject is the weakest part. "Exclusive savings" gives no number, and the real hook, an extra 5% with a code, only appears in the render.
- The layout itself is clean. A pale-blue banner leads with "Extra 5% savings." and a code badge. A lifestyle hero follows with "Save 30% when you buy one of our bundles." Four bundle cards with matching "Save 30%" badges and blue "Shop now" buttons come next. The offer stack is the problem. It has an extra 5%, up to 30%, bundles at 30%, and a "More friends and fam savings" grid showing 20%, 25%, 30% and 20% on 2+. That makes two competing heroes and five different numbers. The asterisks, "comp. value" fine print and the code tucked in a small corner badge also work against the "honest offer" read.
- Judgement: a competent, on-brand catalog send. It is predictable and unhurried, with no deadline anywhere, and it won't lift a subscriber who has already seen the last four.

## What's working

- The "Subscriber Exclusive" banner at the very top gives members a reason to feel recognized.
- The bundle cards are consistent. Each has a title, one line of copy, a "Save 30%" badge and a single blue button, so the page scans fast.
- The "Save 30%" hero with a lifestyle photo gives a clear emotional anchor above the fold.
- The copy has some personality, for example "You can't spell friendship without comfort and versatility."
- The footer is tidy. It has a nav, a text-to-join SMS capture, a mattress quiz link and a store locator.

## What's weak

- The extra 5% and the FF-EXT5 code sit in a small tilted badge in the top corner. The subject's main promise is easy to miss.
- Two stacked heroes, the 5% banner and the 30% bundles hero, compete for attention. The grid at the bottom adds 20%, 25% and 20% on 2+, which muddies the message.
- There is no deadline or end date anywhere in the visible body. That is a missed urgency lever after the "Final hours" angle on 10/07.
- Every bundle button says "Shop now". There are no prices, no star ratings and no product names beyond the generic bundle titles.
- The fine print is tiny and heavily asterisked ("Savings based on comp. value"), which weakens trust in the offer.

## Recommendations

- 1. Make the extra 5% the one hero. Put "Extra 5% off with FF-EXT5" in the headline at large size, with a copy-code button, and drop the second "Save 30%" hero.
- 2. Add a real end date. Something like "Ends Sunday" would give this send a reason to differ from the 10/08 one.
- 3. Cut the bottom "More friends and fam savings" grid down to a single row, or remove it. Four bundle tiles plus a second discount grid is too many numbers.
- 4. Use specific CTAs and proof on the tiles, such as "Shop the Comfy Bundle", a from-price and a star rating.
- 5. Space the cadence out or vary the creative. Four Friends & Family sends in a row (09/27, 09/29, 10/07, 10/08) before this one is fatigue.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Your extra 5% code: FF-EXT5`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Subscribers only: 5% more off 30% bundles`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Add code FF-EXT5 at checkout. Stacks with up to 30% off bundles. Ends Sunday.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Bundle the Original Pillow, sheets and a base, then take an extra 5% off.`

## Technical Audit

## 1. Technical Summary
The email is a Bloomreach/Exponea-sent template with correct basics: `lang="en"`, a viewport meta tag, an MSO conditional block and a 620px responsive breakpoint. The supplied data can't confirm the unsubscribe mechanism, authentication or UTM tagging. The HTML is truncated, and the header checks were blind because of the AgentMail relay.

## 2. Link & Tracking Issues
- **Link probing:** 12 material links were probed with no failures reported (0 issues). 24 footer, utility and social links were skipped, so their health is unverified.
- **Open pixel:** `https://cdn.us1.exponea.com/casper-prod/e/CgxqCN47…/open` is a 1x1 `<img>` hidden with `display:none`. Some clients (e.g. Outlook desktop) ignore `display:none` on images and still fetch it, so the open count is not reliably suppressed or blocked.
- **Leaked campaign name:** The pixel token looks like base64 and decodes to a string containing `Core Sleepers Test`. That suggests an internal campaign or test name is embedded in the pixel URL. I decoded this by eye from the truncated token, so confirm it before acting.
- **Truncation:** Tracking-link formats and redirect behaviour for CTAs can't be assessed because the body is cut off.

## 3. Rendering & Accessibility
- **Alt text (QA flag is a false positive):** The QA check flagged the open pixel as missing alt text. The source has `alt=""`, which is correct for a tracking or decorative image. No action is needed.
- **Web fonts:** Two `<link rel="stylesheet">` tags load from `brxcdn.com`, wrapped in `<!--[if !mso]><!-->`. Gmail and several other clients drop `<link>` tags, so web fonts will silently fall back. Fallback font stacks in the inline styles can't be verified in the truncated source.
- **Preheader:** Two hidden blocks are present. The first is a `<span>` with the "Extra 5% savings, just for subscribers" text. The second is a `<div>` of `&#8199;&#847;` and `&shy;` filler. The structure is valid, but the filler is very long and adds payload with little benefit.
- **Plain-text part:** QA found 19,035 of 20,884 characters (about 91%) are URLs. The text alternative is almost all raw tracking links, which is poor for plain-text readers and can add to spam-filter scoring.
- **Image alt text and tap targets:** Alt text on content images and tap-target sizes can't be verified from the truncated source.

## 4. Personalization & Merge Tokens
No issues found. No unrendered tokens (`{{ }}`, `%%`, `[FIRST_NAME]`) appear in the visible source. The tail of the body is truncated, so this is not fully confirmed.

## 5. Compliance (CAN-SPAM, unsubscribe, authentication headers)
- **List-Unsubscribe / List-Unsubscribe-Post:** Neither header was found. The QA note says AgentMail may not relay them, so this is unconfirmed rather than a confirmed defect. If the headers are truly absent, there is no RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe, which Gmail and Yahoo require for bulk senders.
- **Authentication-Results:** Not found, so SPF, DKIM and DMARC status is unknown. This is also likely a relay artifact. Check the raw message headers at the source.
- **Body unsubscribe link and postal address:** The QA summary says an unsubscribe link was included in the probe set, but the footer is truncated from the HTML. I can't confirm the link target or the physical postal address.

## 6. Email-to-Site Continuity (UTM params, landing page alignment)
Not assessable. The truncated HTML shows no CTA hrefs, so UTM parameters, the final landing URL and whether the offer code or "Friends & Family" pricing carries through can't be checked. The 12 probed links returned no errors.

## 7. Recommendations
1. **Verify headers on the original message.** Check List-Unsubscribe, List-Unsubscribe-Post, and SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment for `e.casper.com`. Don't treat the relay warnings as sender defects until you have.
2. **Re-run the audit on the full HTML.** This would confirm UTM tagging, the footer address and unsubscribe, merge tokens, and fallback fonts.
3. **Trim the plain-text part.** Cut the URL-heavy plain-text alternative, or add readable link labels.
4. **Review the campaign name in the pixel token.** Confirm whether `Core Sleepers Test` is an internal or test identifier that shouldn't appear in a production send.
5. **Suppress the QA alt-text warning.** Treat `alt=""` on tracking pixels as valid so this stops showing up as a false positive.
6. **Check the web fonts in Gmail.** The `<link>`-based fonts fall back there, so confirm the fallback stacks look acceptable.
