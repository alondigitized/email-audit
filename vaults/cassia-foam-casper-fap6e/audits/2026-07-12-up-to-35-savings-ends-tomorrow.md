# Up to 35% savings ends TOMORROW.*
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-07-12**
## Executive summary

- This is the eleventh Casper sale email in twelve days, and the subject-line deadline game has collapsed entirely. "Ends TOMORROW" lands four days after "35% mattress savings ends tonight" (July 7) and three days after "Ends soon" (July 8). A real subscriber who noticed either of those emails will read this subject as evidence the sale never actually ends — which erodes urgency just when the send needs it most.
- Inside, the email is a well-structured tiered discount grid: 20% on standard mattresses, 30% on Hybrids/Waves, $100 off Sleep Kits, 35% on bundles, up to 35% on bases, 30% on adjustable bases, plus accessories. The logic of the layout is sound — it helps comparison shoppers find their tier — but it scatters attention across seven distinct CTAs with no single hero product anchoring the scroll. The July 11 product-education send (cooling systems) was a stronger creative choice; Casper abandoned it after one email to reload the same discount template.
- The email renders cleanly. The dark-navy sparkle hero is visually sharp and the product photography is consistent. But creative execution doesn't compensate for deadline fatigue: this send is fighting its own calendar.

## What's working

- **Tiered discount architecture is legible** — 20/30/$100/35% breakdown by product category makes it easier for a browser to find their price point than a flat "up to 35%" grid
- **Hero section is visually strong** — dark navy + large white type + sparkle graphics create immediate contrast and land the offer instantly above the fold
- **"Comfier sleep all summer" framing** adds a seasonal hook that was absent in the July 7–8 sends
- **Section CTAs are specific and scannable** — "Shop Hybrids & Waves", "Shop Bundles", "Shop Bases" are better than generic "Shop Now" buttons

## What's weak

- **Deadline credibility is broken** — "ends TOMORROW" after two prior "ends tonight/ends soon" subject lines in the same sprint; any engaged subscriber knows the sale rolls over
- **No hero product or anchor SKU** — seven categories, seven buttons, zero reason to prioritize any one over the others; the email teaches rather than converts
- **"TOMORROW" in all-caps reads as pressure, not urgency** — combined with the asterisk on "ends TOMORROW.*" (is the deadline itself asterisked?), this undercuts the one job the subject line has
- **The pivot back from product education hurts momentum** — the July 11 cooling-system send was well-received precisely because it wasn't another discount table; abandoning that angle in 24 hours signals the calendar is driving creative, not strategy

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one hero product and build around it** — a single mattress (e.g. Snow Hybrid) with "30% off — tonight only" creates a sharper conversion path than a seven-category menu; save the grid for a secondary scroll position
- 2. **Retire the rolling deadline** — if the sale truly ends tomorrow, say the date: "Sale ends July 13" is more credible than a vague "TOMORROW" that subscribers have heard since July 7
- 3. **Carry forward the product-education angle** — the July 11 cooling-system hook was distinctive; this send could have led with "The cooling you felt in our Snow Hybrid? 30% off today only" instead of resetting to generic discount copy
- 4. **Collapse the CTA count** — seven buttons creates decision paralysis; anchor on one primary CTA (mattresses) and surface the rest as secondary links below
- **Subject Alt A:** `Snow Hybrid: 30% off ends July 13`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your mattress shortlist — 30–35% off, final day`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Cooling tech, real price. Shop Snow Hybrid before midnight.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `The sale your cart has been waiting on — ends tomorrow night.`

**Dual preheader injection:**
