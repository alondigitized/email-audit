# Memorial Day Doorbuster: Take a test nap & save up to 35%
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-21**
## Executive summary

- This is Casper's fifth email in six days, and it lands on the wrong foot: the subject line promises a "Doorbuster" deal, but the body is almost entirely a drive-to-store campaign. The sale offer gets a banner at the top — real and legible — and then the email immediately pivots to nap appointments, sleep specialists, in-store pickups, and Sleep Tip #5. The deal gets buried the moment you scroll past the fold.
- The in-store pivot isn't inherently wrong; driving pre-purchase mattress trials is a legitimate Memorial Day strategy. But the subject line and body are having two different conversations, and the hybrid format serves neither goal well. A reader primed by "Doorbuster: save up to 35%" who finds a "visit your nearest Sleep Shop" email is going to feel like they were baited.
- The "Sleep Tip #5" inclusion is the most damaging symptom. Tips belong in an onboarding or nurture sequence — not inside a promotional send where every inch of copy should reinforce the sale. It signals that someone copy-pasted from a template instead of writing an email with a single purpose.

## What's working

- The top banner is clean and specific: "Save up to 35% on mattresses / Up to 35% on everything else" is immediately readable with a visible "Shop Now" CTA.
- "Sample the snooze at a Sleep Shop near you" is genuinely clever headline writing — differentiated from the standard "shop now" cadence of the prior emails.
- Trust signals at the bottom (free returns, 100-night trial, 10-year warranty, Affirm financing) are clearly laid out and reinforce purchase confidence.
- No render bugs — clean layout with no broken images or overlapping elements.

## What's weak

- The body content is a drive-to-store email dressed up in a "Doorbuster" subject line — the mismatch erodes trust.
- Sleep Tip #5 appears mid-email, completely disrupting the promotional flow; it belongs in an onboarding sequence, not a sale send.
- Three competing in-store CTAs ("Book a nap," "Find a store," "Find a Sleep Shop") create decision paralysis with no clear primary action.
- The 35% offer has appeared in three of the last five emails — the doorbuster framing adds urgency that isn't actually new.
- No specific end date for the sale; "Memorial Day" implies urgency but doesn't create the deadline tension that "Ends Monday 11:59 PM" would.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Pick one job for this email.** Either make it a hard-close doorbuster (online purchase, deadline, specific SKUs) or a drive-to-store email (appointments, local inventory, the nap experience) — not both. The current hybrid dilutes both conversion paths.
- 2. **Kill Sleep Tip #5.** Remove it entirely. It belongs in a nurture sequence, not a doorbuster send. Replace that space with a specific hero product at the discounted price.
- 3. **Add a hard deadline.** "Sale ends Monday" or "48 hours left" in the banner and at least once in the body. Memorial Day is a week away in context; "doorbuster" implies scarcity that isn't substantiated.
- 4. **Collapse the three in-store CTAs into one.** One "Find your Sleep Shop + book a nap" CTA is cleaner and creates a single decision.
- 5. **Reframe the subject for the actual email.** The current subject overpromises on the sale angle.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Test a mattress this weekend — up to 35% off`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Your nap appointment is waiting (+ 35% off)`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Book a free nap at a Sleep Shop near you before the sale ends.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Find a store, try your mattress, and save up to 35% this Memorial Day.`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Positive signals confirmed in source:**

- MSO conditionals properly wrap VML and `<link>` elements

**Preheader padding technique uses heavy character injection:**

`&#8199;` (figure space) + `&#847;` (combining grapheme joiner) is the standard Litmus/Klaviyo spacer pattern. Functionally fine, but `&shy;` (soft hyphen) at high repetition can cause unexpected line-break artifacts in some older Android mail clients. Not a blocking issue.
