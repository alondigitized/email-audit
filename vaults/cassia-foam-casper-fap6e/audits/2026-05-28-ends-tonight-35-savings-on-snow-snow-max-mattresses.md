# Ends TONIGHT! 35% savings on Snow & Snow Max mattresses
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-05-28**
## Executive summary

- This is Casper's eleventh email in roughly ten days, and the creative quality has actually *improved* at exactly the moment subscriber patience has run out. The "Don't just take our word for it" section — product-specific customer quotes paired with product photos and explicit discount badges (30% vs 35%) — is the strongest execution in the entire Memorial Day run. The pivot to a summer-cooling narrative ("Stay cool for the summer") finally creates distance from the dead Memorial Day frame. The email is well-structured, hierarchy is clean, and the Snow vs Snow Max differentiation is honest.
- The problem is the subject line. "Ends TONIGHT!" is a literal copy of the urgency beat deployed in the May 25 send, the May 23 send, and arguably the May 21 send. Any subscriber who opened even two of the prior ten emails will dismiss this as crying wolf. The all-caps TONIGHT adds a desperation signal that clashes with Casper's typically composed tone. This is a well-designed email wearing a credibility-burned subject line.
- Call: worth sending — the body earns it — but only after a subject line rewrite that buries the urgency language and leads with the product story.

## What's working

- **Social proof section is a genuine lift.** Two customer quotes tied to specific models, each with a discount badge and a dedicated CTA. This is product-level persuasion, not generic brand copy.
- **30% vs 35% differentiation is honest.** Rather than flattening everything to "up to 35%", the email shows Snow Max at 30% and Snow at 35% — respects subscriber intelligence.
- **Summer cooling pivot is seasonally fresh.** "Stay cool for the summer" is the first body-copy frame in this entire campaign that isn't Memorial Day language, and it holds water post-holiday.
- **CTAs are specific and stacked.** "Shop Snow Mattresses" in the hero, "Shop Snow Max" and "Shop Snow" in the body. Every module has a clear exit ramp.
- **Render is clean.** No broken images, no layout breaks, consistent column structure end-to-end.

## What's weak

- **"Ends TONIGHT!" has zero credibility left.** This is the third or fourth urgency deadline Casper has declared in 10 days — the May 25 send and May 27 send made similar claims. Subscribers who opened either will ignore this.
- **ALL-CAPS TONIGHT is a spam-tone signal.** It reads panicked, not confident. Casper's visual identity is calm and premium; this subject line contradicts it.
- **"Stay cool for the summer" cross-sell dilutes the Snow/Snow Max message.** The hero commits to Snow and Snow Max; the bottom module introduces other products. Works structurally but weakens focus.
- **No personalization signal anywhere.** First name, purchase history, browsing signal — nothing. On the eleventh email of a campaign, some acknowledgment of "you haven't bought yet" would outperform generic urgency.
- **Preheader is not visible / not readable in the render**, which means it's likely defaulting to body text — a missed amplification slot given the subject line's weak credibility.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Rewrite the subject to lead with the product story, not the deadline.** "Ends TONIGHT" punishes opens before they happen; the social proof inside the email is compelling enough to pull opens on its own. Bury the urgency in the preheader or drop it entirely.
- **Subject Alt A:** `"Snow & Snow Max: here's what customers say"`
- **Subject Alt B:** `"Sleep cooler this summer — 35% off Snow ends tonight"`
- 2. **Use the preheader to add credibility the subject can't.** The current preheader is likely junk fallback; replace it with the strongest review snippet to set a concrete expectation.
- **Preheader Alt A:** `"The cooling is next to none" — see what they mean, 35% off today.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Final hours on 35% off Snow — here's why it works.`
- 3. **Drop the ALL-CAPS TONIGHT.** If you keep urgency in the subject, write "ends tonight" in sentence case — it reads confident, not desperate.
- 4. **Anchor the cross-sell grid to the cooling theme.** Rename the "Stay cool for the summer" product thumbnails so they visually connect back to Snow — a pillow or protector from the Snow line would be more on-brief than a generic Dream Max card.
- 5. **Add a single personalization hook.** Even a segment-level line ("you browsed cooling mattresses") would re-anchor credibility after an exhausting campaign run.

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**External CSS from brxcdn.com (2 stylesheets)**
```html
<link href="https://brxcdn.com/us1-app-storage/5a801692-033d-11ef-...
```
Gmail (webmail and some Android builds), Samsung Mail, and Yahoo Mail strip external `<link>` stylesheets. Custom fonts declared in these files will silently fall back to system fonts. If font metrics differ significantly, layout reflow is possible on affected clients. Custom fonts should be inlined via `@import` within a `<style>` block as a fallback strategy, or the design must be validated with web-safe fonts.

**`#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none }`**
This rule suppresses underlines globally on all `<a>` tags in the IBM Notes/web view context. Any inline text links in the body will lack both color contrast and underline cues — WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.1 (Use of Color) failure risk if links are not otherwise visually distinguished.

**`a[x-apple-data-detectors]` override** — present and correct; suppresses iOS automatic phone/address link injection.

**VML namespaces and MSO conditionals** — correctly declared. `<o:PixelsPerInch>96</o:PixelsPerInch>` is present for Outlook DPI normalization.
