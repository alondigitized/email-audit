# Save 35% on bundles*
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-14**
## Executive summary

- Casper has taken the 4th of July discount first floated on June 10 and rebuilt it around a genuinely smart bundle framework — "Summer Sleep Starter Kits" with a step-by-step Build Your Own Bed (BYOB) section embedded directly in the email. That structural choice is the standout creative decision here: instead of another "X% off sitewide," the email becomes a decision guide for a considered, multi-SKU purchase (mattress + base + accessories), which is the exact friction point Casper needs to solve.
- The creative threads together coherently with the June 12 "hot sleeper" send — that email established the summer sleep problem, this one sells the solution. The promo code is prominent in the hero, the bundle naming (Adjustable Pro, Snow, Dream, Comfy, Cool Sleep) provides meaningful self-selection for different buyer types, and the layout is clean.
- The main drag: no deadline. After the early-access framing of June 10, subscribers know there's a 4th of July window — but this email drops all urgency signaling. That single omission is the difference between a browsing email and a buying email.

## What's working

- **BYOB stepped builder** — "Pick size → mattress → base → accessories" embedded as four illustrated steps is a standout format for a purchase category where overwhelm kills conversion
- **"Summer Sleep Starter Kits" framing** — extends the hot-sleeper narrative from June 12 coherently; this is brand storytelling across sends, not isolated discount blasts
- **Bundle naming earns self-selection** — Adjustable Pro, Snow, Cool Sleep, Dream are descriptive enough that a buyer can route themselves without reading a spec sheet
- **"Less scrolling, more snoozing"** — the best brand-voice moment in the email; earns a half-smile while justifying the curated-bundle section below
- **Promo code visible in the hero** — no burying in fine print or "see subject line" friction; the code is right there

## What's weak

- **No deadline on a 35% offer** — the June 10 early-access email implied a 4th of July window; this email omits it entirely, removing the single most effective purchase trigger
- **Third email in five days, same 35%** — the discount has now appeared in four emails since June 10; cadence fatigue is real and the marginal open value keeps declining
- **The asterisk on "35%\*" goes unexplained** — no visible fine print or exclusion notice in the hero; an unexplained asterisk breeds distrust on exactly the line that matters most
- **No social proof** — for a bundle that presumably runs $800–$1,500+, there are no star ratings, review counts, or bestseller signals adjacent to any of the specific products shown

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add a hard deadline in the hero** — "Sale ends July 4" or "Ends Sunday" converts a browse email into a buy email. Highest single lift available.
- 2. **Resolve the asterisk in-email** — one visible line ("Excludes previous purchases and select items") is enough to convert skeptics and pre-empt cart abandonment
- 3. **Add social proof to the top two bundles** — "★4.8 · 3,200 reviews" next to the Adjustable Pro Bundle would close fence-sitters on a considered purchase
- 4. **Compress the BYOB to a single CTA on mobile** — four illustrated steps read well on desktop but add scroll weight on mobile; a "Build your bundle →" button linking to a pre-configured landing page might outperform the embedded flow
- 5. **Surface the 4th of July anchor** — the promo code implies it (EA-JL4 style), but the email never says "4th of July" — adding it to the hero adds urgency without new creative work

- **Subject Alt B:** `35% off bundles ends July 4 — build yours`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Pick your size, mattress, base, and accessories. We made it easy.`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Starter kits for hot sleepers. Promo code inside.`

### 5. Compliance

**Subject line asterisk**: Subject reads `Save 35% on bundles*`. CAN-SPAM and FTC guidelines require that any material condition attached to an advertised offer (the asterisk footnote — likely an expiration date, exclusions, or minimum spend) be clearly disclosed **within the email body**. This cannot be confirmed from the truncated source. Verify the footnote is present and legible in the rendered email.

**Physical mailing address and unsubscribe link**: Required by CAN-SPAM. Not visible in the truncated source (typically in the footer). Both must be present.

**Sending domain**: `support@e.casper.com` — `e.casper.com` is a subdomain used for ESP sending. Confirm DKIM signing is scoped to this subdomain and that SPF/DMARC records for `e.casper.com` are correctly configured. Bloomreach Engagement supports per-domain DKIM; this is standard setup but should be verified.

### 6. Email-to-Site Continuity
