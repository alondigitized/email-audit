# Your Extra 5% Is Back—But Not for Long.*
**Score:** 6/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-09-09**
## Executive summary

- This is the same "extra 5%" Subscriber Exclusive hero Casper has now run at least five times since 08/23 — same hand-circled highlighter graphic, same "LDAY-EX" code family (now LDAY-EX2), same "up to 35% + extra 5%" stacking math. The subject's "But Not for Long" framing tries to manufacture urgency around a discount that's demonstrably been recycled all week, and a subscriber who opened the 09/07 "FINAL HOURS" email with the identical hero will notice this immediately — "final hours" plus this coming back two days later reads as false urgency, not real scarcity.
- Where this send does earn credit is structure: it's a clean, well-organized category catalog (mattresses, bundles, pillows, furniture, bedding) each with a specific savings badge and its own CTA, so once someone's in the email, navigation is easy. But the top-of-funnel promise is stale and the whole email is one long scroll of near-identical blue cards — there's no product hero, no single clear "click here" moment, just six sequential category tiles.
- Judgement: functional but fatigued creative. The offer math and code are consistent with the last several sends, which is good for trust but bad for urgency — Casper has burned "final hours" and "back but not for long" language enough times this week that a repeat subscriber has reason to distrust both.

## What's working

- Clear, specific offer stated immediately in the hero: "extra 5% on top of up to 35%" with a legible orange CTA button.
- Code is visible and easy to copy: `LDAY-EX2`.
- Each product category (mattresses, bundles, pillows, furniture, bedding) has its own savings badge, lifestyle photo, and dedicated CTA — easy to scan for a specific need.
- No render bugs — images, badges, and buttons all display cleanly with consistent brand styling.
- Footer includes a text-signup module and store locator, giving lower-intent readers another path to engage.

## What's weak

- "Back for a limited time... but it won't stick around for long" directly contradicts the 09/07 "FINAL HOURS" send from two days ago — repeat subscribers will clock the inconsistency and discount future urgency claims.
- No single hero product or lifestyle image up top — the email opens with graphic/text only, pushing the first real photo below the fold.
- Six near-identical blue category cards in a row create visual monotony; nothing signals which category is the "best" deal at a glance.
- The "Save up to X% + extra 5%" badge stacking (30%, 35%, 20%, 10%, 10%) is confusing — it's not clear if these are the same "up to 35%" from the hero or separate category-specific ceilings.

## Recommendations

- 1. Drop "But Not for Long" / "won't stick around" language for at least one send — after five extra-5% sends in three weeks including two "FINAL HOURS" emails, repeated urgency claims erode trust faster than they drive clicks. Replace with a concrete expiration (e.g., "Ends Wednesday 11:59pm ET") or retire the extra-5% offer for a genuinely new promo.
- 2. Add a hero product photo (best-selling mattress) directly under the offer banner so there's a clear visual focal point before the category list starts.
- 3. Reorder or visually distinguish the highest-value category (35% bundle) so it doesn't read as equal-weight to the 10% furniture/bedding tiles — e.g., a "Best Deal" ribbon on the bundle card.
- 4. Clarify the badge math with one line near the hero: "Category savings shown below already include your extra 5%" so the stacking isn't ambiguous.
- **Subject Alt A:** `Casper: Extra 5% ends Wednesday`
- **Subject Alt B:** `One more day of extra 5% off`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `Pair it with up to 35% off sitewide — code LDAY-EX2`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your mattress upgrade, plus 5% more`
