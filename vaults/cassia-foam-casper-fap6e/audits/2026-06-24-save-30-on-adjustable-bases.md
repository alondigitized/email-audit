# Save 30% on adjustable bases.
**Score:** 9/10 · **Type:** Email audit · **2026-06-24**
## Executive summary

- This is a focused, single-category send from Casper — 30% off adjustable bases across three product tiers. The creative is clean: the hero delivers the discount front and center, the three-tier product grid below gives meaningful purchase structure, and the Prime Day yellow banner stacks a bonus 5% for attentive subscribers.
- The problem is that this email arrives as the sixth Casper send in ten days, and it's a narrower offer than what came before. Sitewide 35% has quietly become category-specific 30%, and adjustable bases are a high-consideration specialty purchase that most mattress buyers don't have on their radar. The email executes well for the audience segment actively shopping for an adjustable base — but that's a small slice of a general sleep list.
- What saves it: the three-tier product presentation is genuinely useful, the 30% badge is consistent and honest across all three products, and the "Complete your summer setup" cross-sell grid at the bottom gives subscribers less interested in adjustable bases a reason to keep scrolling.

## What's working

- **Three-tier product grid is clean and useful.** Base, Pro, and Max each have names, brief descriptions, and individual "Shop now" CTAs — high-intent buyers can comparison-shop without leaving the email.
- **Hero delivers the core offer above the fold.** "30% savings on adjustable bases" is front and center; no hunting required.
- **30% badge is consistent across all three products.** No subject/hero mismatch — a problem that hurt the June 20 send ("35% hybrid" subject vs. "30%" hero).
- **Stacked Prime Day banner is a loyalty signal.** The yellow banner advertising extra 5% sitewide rewards subscribers who've been tracking the sale window.
- **"Complete your summer setup" cross-sell at the bottom** keeps the email relevant to the majority of subscribers who aren't in the market for an adjustable base.

## What's weak

- **No urgency in the body.** The Prime Day banner implies a limited window, but the email body never makes a deadline explicit — the hero reads as an evergreen offer, not a timed event.
- **Sixth email in ten days; discount regression will be noticed.** Subscribers who've tracked the run from the June 14 bundle send (sitewide 35%) to today (category-specific 30%) feel the series converging downward.
- **Adjustable bases is a low-impulse, high-consideration category.** Most list members aren't waking up thinking about adjustable bases — this email's primary offer requires prior intent that a majority of recipients simply don't have.
- **No social proof anywhere in the body.** The "One base, endless possibilities" section uses feature bullets, not customer quotes or star ratings — a missed opportunity for a $500+ purchase where reviews are a key trust signal.

## Recommendations

- 1. **Add an explicit deadline to the body.** "Sale ends Sunday" or "Prime Day pricing through July 17" below the hero CTA is the single biggest available lift — it converts passive interest into timed urgency that the body currently doesn't create.
- 2. **Add a review snippet to at least one product card.** A 4.8-star rating or "Best sleep upgrade I've made" pull quote on the Adjustable Base Pro provides social proof exactly at the decision point.
- 3. **Surface the Prime Day stacking mechanic in the hero copy.** "30% off + an extra 5% this weekend" as a sub-headline makes the yellow banner findable without requiring the subscriber to notice a banner they may scroll past.
- 4. **Segment this to high-intent audiences.** Mattress purchasers within 6–12 months, or anyone who browsed the adjustable base page, would make this feel surgical rather than broadcast.
- **Subject Alt A:** `30% off adjustable bases — Prime Day weekend only`
- **Subject Alt B:** `Stack your savings: 35% off adjustable bases this weekend`
- **Preheader Alt A:** `All three tiers — Base, Pro, and Max — on sale through Sunday`
- **Preheader Alt B:** `Your mattress's best upgrade, now 30% off + Prime Day extra 5%`

### 3. Rendering & Accessibility

**Web font loading via `<link>` tags:**
```html
<link href="https://brxcdn.com/us1-app-storage/5a801692.../9c4a4f1a..." rel="stylesheet">
<link href="https://brxcdn.com/us1-app-storage/5a801692.../9c30fa4c..." rel="stylesheet">
```
Both are guarded with `<!--[if !mso]>` so Outlook falls back to system fonts (expected). However, **Gmail web strips `<link>` tags entirely** — custom webfonts will silently fail there. The CSS fallback font stack must be tested in Gmail to confirm the layout doesn't degrade.

**Link color suppression:**
```css
#MessageViewBody a { color: inherit; text-decoration: none; }
```
This strips underlines and color from all links in Gmail. If CTA buttons rely on background-color alone for differentiation, this may pass, but any inline text links would be visually indistinguishable from body copy — a WCAG 2.1 §1.4.1 (Use of Color) failure.

**Preheader implementation:**
The preheader uses two elements:
1. A `<span style="color: transparent; display: none !important; ...">` containing the actual preheader text.
2. A `<div>` packed with `&#8199;&#847;` (U+2027 + U+034F) and `&shy;` characters to pad preview length and prevent body bleed.

The `display: none !important` on the `<span>` may suppress the preheader in Apple Mail (some versions ignore hidden-node text). The safer pattern is `font-size: 0; max-height: 0; overflow: hidden` without `display: none`. Low severity, but worth testing in Apple Mail 16+.

**`lang="en"` present on `<html>` tag** — correct.

**MSO VML namespaces and `<o:PixelsPerInch>96</o:PixelsPerInch>`** — present and correctly structured.
