# Homepage snapshot · www.target.com · 2026-05-13
**Score:** 8/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-13**
## Executive summary

- Okay, this is giving me full resort-girl fantasy and I'm not mad about it. Target is leading with "The Vacation Edit" — a warm, sun-drenched lifestyle play that feels perfectly timed heading into summer. The model, the terracotta swimsuit, the tropical leaves — it's aspirational without being unattainable, which is peak Target energy. That said, the above-the-fold experience is basically one big mood board with zero pricing or rewards hooks, so it's all vibe and no "add to cart."

## What's working

- **"The Vacation Edit is here" headline** — big, confident, underlined for emphasis. It signals a curated moment, not just a clearance dump, and that's how I like Target to talk to me
- **Hero lifestyle image** — terracotta swimsuit, oversized sunglasses, pearl necklace, turquoise headscarf against lush green foliage. This is a full-on Pinterest pin and I would save it immediately
- **Ghost/outlined CTA button** — "Explore the collection" floats cleanly over the hero copy area without competing with the image below. Smart visual restraint
- **Clean mobile nav** — hamburger, bullseye logo centered, account + cart in the top right. It's muscle memory for me at this point, frictionless
- **Seasonal timing** — "Vacation Edit" in mid-May is hitting exactly when I'm starting to think about a summer trip. Right message, right moment

## What's weak

- **Zero loyalty integration** — I have Circle and I use it every single week. My rewards balance, a "Circle member exclusive" badge, anything — gone from this view. This is a returning shopper's homepage and it feels like I'm a stranger
- **No price anchoring whatsoever** — "resort-ready staples" sounds cute but I came here because Target is affordable. Show me a $29.99 swim cover-up. Give me something to grab onto
- **"Explore the collection" is soft CTA copy** — I don't want to explore, I want to shop. "Shop the Edit" would convert harder
- **No category shortcuts visible** — I can't tap straight to Home, Women's, or Beauty without opening the hamburger. That extra tap costs engagement from someone like me who grocery-runs and impulse-buys in the same session

## Recommendations

- **Swap "Explore the collection" for "Shop the Vacation Edit"** — sharper verb, same vibe, more intent signal
- **Add a Circle rewards strip above the hero** — even one line: "Circle members: extra 5% on this collection. Sign in →" — this would personally stop my scroll cold
- **Anchor at least one price point in the subtext** — "Discover versatile, resort-ready staples. Starting at $19.99." That one number makes the whole thing feel like Target and not a magazine
- **Add a horizontal category chip row below the hero** — Women's, Home, Beauty, Swim — so I can peel off to my usual aisles in one tap without hitting the hamburger
