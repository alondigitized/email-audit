# Homepage snapshot · www.target.com · 2026-05-28
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-28**
## Executive summary

- Okay, Target knows summer is here and they are leaning *in* — the "Summer is calling" hero with pool floaties and a price anchor of $1 is very Bullseye on-brand. But this feels like it skews younger and more kid-activity than my usual Thursday run vibe; I live for throw pillows and Magnolia tables, not inflatable crabs. It's cheerful and seasonal, but it's not exactly talking to *me*.

## What's working

- **"Summer is calling" headline** — punchy, casual, exactly the tone Target does best. Feels like a friend texting me, not a retailer yelling.
- **"From $1" price anchor** — before I've even scrolled, I know there's a deal. That's the kind of thing that keeps me in the app instead of bouncing to Amazon.
- **Sub-image product teasers** (beach ball, ice cream cone float) — colorful, fun, and immediately shoppable-feeling even without a button.
- **Top bar context** — "Ship to 91754" tells me it knows my location, which means Drive Up is ready to go. That's a quiet but real trust signal for me.
- **Clean mobile render** — the layout is tight, the bullseye is centered, and nothing is jacked up. That matters more than people think.

## What's weak

- **No Circle Rewards hook above the fold** — I check my points balance before I shop anything. If Target Circle isn't surfaced immediately, I feel like a guest instead of a loyal Cartwheel OG.
- **No visible CTA button** — the hero has great copy but I can't see a "Shop outdoor picks" button. Copy without a tap target is a missed conversion.
- **Kid-play framing excludes my core vibe** — inflatable crabs and beach balls are cute but I came here for home refresh, Stanley drops, and whatever Magnolia collab is live. The hero isn't speaking my love language.
- **No personalized or editorial entry point** — no "New in Home," no "What's trending this week," nothing that feels curated *for me* vs. broadcast at everyone.

## Recommendations

- **Swap or A/B the hero to include a home/lifestyle angle** — even a split-screen with a styled summer patio next to the pool toys would catch my eye and tell me "your stuff is here too."
- **Pin Target Circle balance + a "welcome back" bar** just below the nav — one line, personalized, easy to implement. "You have 847 Circle points. Shop to redeem." I would tap that immediately.
- **Add a visible shop button in the hero** — "Shop from $1" in Target's signature red pill button. The copy is there; give me somewhere to tap.
- **Surface a trending-editorial strip below the hero** — "What Tess is loving this week: patio pillows, outdoor rugs, s'mores kits" — even generic editorial that nods to home + summer crossover would lift engagement for my persona.
