# Homepage snapshot · www.target.com · 2026-05-14
**Score:** 7/10 · **Type:** Site journey · **2026-05-14**
## Executive summary

- Okay, Target, I see you — lush tropical vibes, a gorgeous model in a chunky cream knit wrap, and "The Vacation Edit is here" front and center. This is very much a brand moment, not a "here's the deal" moment. It reads as aspirational and seasonal, which I love, but I'm tapping in as a Circle member who wanted to know what's new *and* maybe grab a discount, and the page is giving me editorial magazine, not "hey loyal Tess, here's your Circle offer."

## What's working

- **The vacation edit headline** — "The Vacation Edit is here" is punchy and seasonal. It landed the moment the page loaded. I immediately understood the campaign.
- **The model and lifestyle photography** — Curly-haired woman in a cream knit wrap surrounded by tropical plants is *chef's kiss* Target aesthete. She looks like someone I'd follow on Instagram, which is exactly the energy this brand should chase.
- **The "Explore the collection" CTA button** — White pill button on the dark tropical background has good contrast. One tap and I'm in — no hunting required.
- **Clean mobile layout** — The nav (hamburger, bullseye, account, cart), search bar, and hero all stack neatly. No pinching, no horizontal scroll, nothing broken.
- **Seasonal relevance** — Mid-May is exactly when I'm thinking about beach trips and summer plans. This timing feels intentional and right.

## What's weak

- **Zero Circle/loyalty presence above the fold** — I am a Drive Up + Cartwheel devotee. I have a Circle balance right now. There is not one pixel of acknowledgment. This is a miss for the core Target shopper.
- **No offer or discount signal** — "Resort-ready staples" sounds cute, but what am I saving? Even a "Free shipping on orders $35+" bar would change the energy from browse to buy.
- **"Explore the collection" is soft CTA copy** — Love the button, but "Explore" is window-shopping language. "Shop the Vacation Edit" or "Get resort-ready" is more action-oriented.
- **No price anchor anywhere** — Not a single product thumbnail, price point, or even a "starting at $X" to let me calibrate if this is Target-affordable or Target-upscale. With home decor and throw pillows on my mind, I need a signal.
- **"Commerce" in the top bar is confusing** — That label reads like a developer mode label left in. It doesn't mean anything to a shopper and introduces a micro-moment of friction.

## Recommendations

- **Add a slim Circle/loyalty bar** directly below the nav: "Hi Tess — you have $X in rewards. Ready to use them?" This costs nothing and converts loyal shoppers immediately.
- **Swap "Explore the collection" for "Shop the Vacation Edit"** — same button placement, punchier verb, lower bounce rate.
- **Overlay a price anchor on the hero** — A small "Tops from $15 · Coverups from $22" tag in the corner keeps it aspirational while signaling Target-affordable rather than premium-mystery.
- **Add a shipping/returns strip** between the nav and the hero — "Free shipping on $35+ orders · Free returns every day" is two seconds of text that removes a conversion blocker before I even need to think about it.
