## Skechers — "Did you forget something?" | Abandoned Cart Email

1. Executive Summary

A competent but diluted abandoned cart email. The intent is clear — recover an incomplete checkout — but Skechers buries the urgency under layers of secondary content: a promotional banner, a plain-text category nav, and a utility block that collectively shift focus away from closing the cart. The hero does its job, but the email loses discipline fast.

2. Business Impact Score: **5 / 10**

Functional recovery email with a solid promotional hook, but structural clutter and weak content below the fold reduce conversion potential.

3. What's Working

  • Hero clarity. "Ready to Check Out? Complete Your Order" is unambiguous. The product (Slip-ins Max Cushioning Arch Fit 2.0 – Terrain) is featured immediately above the fold with a direct CTA button.
  • Promotional incentive is present. The "Buy One Get 50%" mid-email banner adds a tangible purchase driver — a reason to act now that pure urgency alone can't provide.
  • Top Sellers grid. A horizontal row of additional shoe options creates light product discovery without derailing the primary goal.
  • Utility footer is complete. App download, curbside pickup, BNPL (Afterpay visible), and store finder are all present — covers the customer wherever they want to complete the transaction.

4. What's Weak

  • The 50% offer is buried. A meaningful promotional hook — Buy One Get 50% — appears well below the hero instead of alongside the CTA. If this offer applies to the abandoned cart, leading with it could meaningfully lift conversion.
  • "Shop Additional Styles" is text-only. The Women / Men / Kids / Clothing / New Arrivals / Sale category block has no images, no color, no visual hierarchy. It reads like a site map dropped into the middle of an email and contributes nothing.
  • Email is too long for its purpose. Abandoned cart emails should be tight. This one extends into a full browse/promotional email format, diluting the recovery message with category navigation, utility modules, and social links.
  • Product details are small and hard to read. The abandoned product name and price appear in small type below the product image — easily missed at a glance.
  • The decorative frame around the hero looks dated. The hand-drawn doodle border aesthetic doesn't match the premium athletic positioning Skechers is reaching for.

5. Recommendations

1. Move the promotional incentive into the hero block — surface "Buy One Get 50%" next to or directly below the "Complete Your Order" CTA so the offer lands before the customer scrolls.

2. Cut or compress everything below the Top Sellers grid — the category nav and utility block are appropriate for a newsletter, not a cart recovery email. Trim or move to a single footer line.

3. Increase product name and price legibility — use larger type or a callout treatment for the abandoned item's title and price to anchor the customer's memory of what they left behind.

4. Modernize the hero visual treatment — replace the doodle frame with a cleaner, bolder typographic layout consistent with current athletic brand standards.

5. Add urgency language — "Your cart expires soon" or stock scarcity signals give the email a second reason to act beyond the offer alone.

6. Bottom Line

This email does the minimum required of an abandoned cart touchpoint but doesn't push to be a strong converter. The promotional hook is its most valuable asset and it's wasted mid-scroll. Tighten the structure, move the offer up, and cut the category clutter — this should be a 3-module email, not a 9-module one.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Audit — Skechers "Did you forget something?" (Abandoned Cart)

Sender: hello@msgs.skechers.com | Platform: Attentive Mail

1. Technical Summary

Standard abandoned-cart email built on Attentive's platform using a table-based layout with responsive media queries. No structural build failures detected, but several tracking, accessibility, and compliance items require attention.

2. Link & Tracking Issues

HTTP redirect domain — All click-tracking links use http:// (not https://) at the Attentive redirect layer:

`

http://skechers.attentivemail.com/ls/click?upn=...

`

If the destination resolves to HTTPS, this causes an HTTP→HTTPS chain with a mixed-content hop. Verify the sending domain is enforcing HTTPS at the redirect layer.

UTM parameters unverifiable — Destination URLs are fully encoded inside the Attentive upn= blob. It is not possible to confirm UTM params (source, medium, campaign) are appended post-redirect from the HTML source alone. QA must decode or test-click each link to verify UTM passthrough.

Web version link — Present and tracked (row-1 .column-2 a). Functional, but label is lowercase "web version" — minor but inconsistent with brand copy conventions.

3. Rendering & Accessibility

Empty <title> tag:

`html

<title></title>

`

Screen readers and some email clients (notably Outlook on Windows) surface the <title> as the document label. Should be set to the subject line or campaign name.

Image alt attributes — HTML is truncated before image blocks render; alt text could not be confirmed. QA must verify all <img> tags carry descriptive alt values, especially product/hero images which carry the cart content in abandoned-cart flows.

text-size-adjust:none on body — Present as -webkit-text-size-adjust:none; text-size-adjust:none. This prevents iOS Mail from auto-scaling small text, which is intentional for layout control but will suppress font scaling for low-vision users on mobile.

Responsive breakpoint — Media query fires at max-width:620px. Container is width:600px. This leaves a 20px gap where the responsive rules do not apply on viewports between 601–620px. Low-risk but worth noting.

#MessageViewBody a { color:inherit; text-decoration:none } — Disables default link underlines inside Gmail. Acceptable if CTAs use button styling, but any inline text links will be visually indistinct from body copy.

4. Personalization & Merge Tokens

No first-name merge token visible — Subject line ("Did you forget something?") and preheader ("You left something in your cart…") contain no first-name personalization despite this being a triggered, identified-user flow. Attentive supports {{ first_name }} tokens; their absence is a missed personalization opportunity and also a signal to check whether the template is falling back to a no-name variant or if tokens were stripped.

Cart content tokens — HTML is truncated; dynamic cart line items (product name, image, price, quantity) could not be confirmed. These are critical for an abandoned-cart email and must be validated in QA sends with seeded cart data, including edge cases: empty cart fallback, multi-item cart, out-of-stock item.

5. Compliance

Unsubscribe link — Not visible in the truncated source. CAN-SPAM requires a functional opt-out mechanism in every commercial email. Must be confirmed present in the full HTML (typically in the footer). Attentive-managed sends normally inject this automatically, but should be verified.

Physical mailing address — Not visible in truncated source. CAN-SPAM §7(d) requires a valid physical postal address. Confirm it appears in the footer.

Sender domain — msgs.skechers.com is a subdomain used by Attentive. SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment should be verified at the DNS level for this subdomain; misalignment causes deliverability failures at major ISPs.

Preheader padding technique — Uses a mix of Unicode zero-width non-joiners (U+200D, ­) and Mongolian vowel separators (U+180E, ͏) for whitespace inflation. This is a known technique but some spam filters flag heavy use of invisible Unicode characters. Monitor deliverability metrics.

6. Email-to-Site Continuity

UTM passthrough unconfirmed — As noted in §2, UTM parameters cannot be read from the encoded tracking URLs. For an abandoned-cart flow, the minimum expected UTM tags are:

  • utm_source=attentive (or skechers_email)
  • utm_medium=email
  • utm_campaign=abandoned_cart
  • utm_content=<variant_id>

Missing or malformed UTMs will cause sessions to be attributed to direct/none in analytics, masking email revenue.

Landing page alignment — Logo and "web version" links both route through Attentive redirects. The web version destination should render the full email at a stable URL; verify it does not 404 or redirect to the homepage after a session expiry window.
