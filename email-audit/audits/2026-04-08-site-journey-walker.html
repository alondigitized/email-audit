# Walker Miles — Skechers.com Mobile Journey Review

Date: April 8, 2026 | Yesterday's Score: 4/10

Executive Summary

Today's journey was a mixed bag — the good news is Skechers finally put Hands Free Slip-ins front and center where I can actually see it. The product page was clean, the deal was obvious, and the size buttons were easy to tap. But the cart page was completely blank — a white screen, nothing there. That's not a minor glitch. That's a broken checkout. And search still throws women's shoes at me first even though I'm logged in as a guy. Small steps forward, one giant step back with that cart.

Score: 5/10 — Up one point from yesterday, but only because the product journey improved. The blank cart prevents any actual purchase.

Business Impact Score: 5/10

The site got me excited about a shoe, showed me a deal, and then showed me a blank white screen when I tried to check out. That's a lost sale. Every day this cart bug exists is revenue walking out the door.

What's Working

  • Slip-ins are front and center. The homepage hero immediately shows "SKECHERS HANDS FREE Slip-ins" — exactly what I came for. That's an improvement. I didn't have to hunt.
  • BOGO 50% OFF deal is impossible to miss. Blue banner at the very top on every page. As a price-conscious guy, that hooked me immediately. The red "Buy 1, Get 1 50% OFF! Applied at Cart" callout on the product page sealed the deal.
  • Men's navigation drawer is clean and logical. "Hands Free Slip-ins" is listed as a quick-link shortcut right in the men's menu. That's smart. One tap and I'm there.
  • Product page is well laid out. Product name, price ($135.00), star rating, and deal are all visible without scrolling. The Hands Free Slip-ins badge confirms I'm in the right category.
  • ADD TO CART button is big and blue. Full-width, bottom of screen, hard to miss. Good for my fingers.
  • Size grid has large tap targets. Each size box is well-spaced. I could tap a size without squinting or missing. The "Please select a size" warning in red was clear when I forgot to pick one.
  • 262 results on the Slip-ins page. Plenty of options, and the width filters (Wide Fit, Extra Wide Fit) showed up prominently — useful for guys my age who need a comfortable fit.

What's Weak

  • Cart page is completely blank. Critical failure. Step 8 shows a pure white screen. Nothing. No items, no totals, no checkout button. I tapped "Add to Cart," presumably it worked, and then the cart page loaded as an empty white void. I can't buy anything. This is the most important page in the entire journey and it's broken.
  • Login shows zero confirmation or personalization. After logging in (Step 3), the page looks identical to before I logged in. Same "Spring Style Guide! Shop Now" banner. No "Welcome back, Walker." No loyalty point balance. Nothing. I have no idea if I'm actually logged in.
  • "Join Skechers Plus" banner shown to an existing member. Step 2 shows "Free Shipping with Skechers Plus — Join Now for free." I'm already a member. This is the opposite of personalization — it's a reminder that the site doesn't know who I am.
  • Search returns women's shoes first. I searched "slip in" and the first two visible results are both labeled "Women's." I'm logged in as a male customer. The site should know to show me men's first.
  • 1,023 search results with no pre-filtering. "Slip in" returned over a thousand results. That's overwhelming. No automatic filter for my gender, no "best match" narrowing. Just a wall of product.
  • Product name is unnecessarily complicated. "Skechers Slip-ins: Max Cushioning Arch Fit 2.0 - Azusah" — I don't know what "Azusah" means. At 62, I'm not going to Google a shoe name. Keep it plain.

Recommendations

1. Fix the cart immediately. This is a P0 bug. A blank cart page stops all transactions cold. Nothing else matters until this works.

2. Show a logged-in state. After login, display "Hi Walker" or a welcome message somewhere in the header. Show my Skechers Plus status and point balance. Make me feel like a member, not a stranger.

3. Fix the Skechers Plus banner for existing members. If I'm logged in and already a member, the "Join Now for free" message should be replaced with a member benefit message — "Free shipping on this order, Walker."

4. Default search to my gender. I'm a logged-in male. "Slip in" should show men's results first, or at minimum offer a prominent gender toggle at the top of results.

5. Reduce search result overwhelm. 1,023 results needs smart defaults — relevance sort, gender filter, or a "Best for you" curated section at the top.

6. Simplify product names. Drop the model code names like "Azusah." Use plain language: "Max Cushioning Arch Fit 2.0 — Gray/White."

Bottom Line

Skechers improved the top of the funnel — I can find Slip-ins faster now, and the deal messaging is strong. But the bottom of the funnel is broken. A blank cart page means nobody is buying anything today, regardless of how good the product page looks. Until that's fixed, this journey has a 0% conversion rate for me. Fix the cart, personalize the logged-in experience, and filter search by gender — then we're talking a real score improvement.

Today: 5/10 vs Yesterday: 4/10 — Marginal improvement, but cart regression is a business emergency.

## Technical Audit

## Technical Summary

Site runs on Salesforce Commerce Cloud (Demandware), confirmed by /on/demandware.static/Sites-USSkechers-Site/ asset paths. 13+ third-party tracking scripts load on every page. Bot protection (KPSDK) is rate-limiting automated sessions, causing cascading 429s. Cart page TTFB spikes 3-4x vs all other pages. LCP and CLS are unmeasured across all 8 steps, leaving core Web Vitals incomplete. 32 axe violations span 5 page types with 4 critical severity issues concentrated on the product detail page.

## Accessibility

Critical (4 violations)

  • aria-required-parent (Homepage): ARIA role used without required parent container — likely a listitem/option rendered outside its list/listbox.
  • aria-required-children (View Product): Parent ARIA role missing required child roles, e.g. a role="list" with no role="listitem" children.
  • aria-valid-attr-value (View Product): One or more ARIA attributes have invalid values (e.g. aria-controls="nonexistent-id").
  • button-name (View Product): Buttons with no text content, aria-label, or title — likely icon-only buttons (size selector, close, etc.).

Serious (5 violations)

  • link-name (Homepage): Anchor elements with no text and no aria-label — likely image-only links without alt text on the <img>.
  • aria-dialog-name (View Product): Modal/dialog elements missing aria-label or aria-labelledby.
  • aria-input-field-name (View Product): Input fields with role="textbox" or similar but no accessible label.
  • color-contrast (View Product): Foreground/background color pairs fail WCAG 2 AA 4.5:1 ratio.
  • link-in-text-block (Men's Shoes): Inline links differentiated only by color — fails non-color distinction requirement.

Moderate (10+ violations)

  • landmark-no-duplicate-main, landmark-main-is-top-level, landmark-unique, landmark-complementary-is-top-level (Homepage, Men's Shoes): Multiple <main> elements present; <aside> nested inside <main> rather than at document root. Likely caused by widget injection (Monetate, Stylitics) inserting full page fragments.
  • heading-order (Homepage): Heading hierarchy skips levels (e.g. <h1> followed by <h3>).
  • region (Homepage, Men's Shoes): Content exists outside any landmark region.
  • aria-allowed-role (Homepage, Men's Shoes): Invalid role value on element type (e.g. role="list" on a <div> that should use native <ul>).

## SEO

## Mobile Optimization

  • Viewport height JS shim: --vh: 6.640000000000001px is set inline on <html> — this is a well-known workaround for iOS Safari's dynamic viewport height (100vh bug). The floating-point value (6.640000000000001 vs 6.64) indicates the calculation is done via window.innerHeight / 100 without rounding. Functionally fine but parseFloat(...).toFixed(2) would be cleaner.
  • No <meta name="viewport"> visible in truncated HTML samples — cannot confirm it is present. If missing or misconfigured, mobile layout will be broken.
  • No server-side mobile detection apparent — appears to be a single responsive codebase, which is correct.

## Console & Network Errors

429 Rate Limiting (6 of 8 errors)

The endpoint https://www.skechers.com/149e9513-01fa-4fb0-aad4-566afd725d1b/2d206a39-8ed7-437e-a3be-862e0f06eea3/fp?x-kpsdk-v=j-1.2.308 is a KPSDK (Kaspersky/DataDome bot protection) fingerprinting endpoint. The path pattern (uuid/uuid/fp) and query param x-kpsdk-v are characteristic of this SDK. In an automated test session, the fingerprinting calls are being rate-limited because the browser environment doesn't pass the bot-detection challenge.

Impact: The KPSDK 429s are likely cascading — downstream requests that depend on a valid session token (including the cart page request) will also fail or be throttled, explaining the /cart/ 429.

This means automated performance/monitoring testing against this site will produce artificially degraded results — real user traffic with a valid fingerprint will not hit these 429s. However, if real users are also hitting cart 429s (e.g. during traffic spikes), the 153ms TTFB is an undercount of the actual degradation.

CORS block — Stylitics CSS

`

Access to CSS stylesheet at 'https://web-assets.stylitics.com/style.css?...'

blocked by CORS policy: No 'Access-Control-Allow-Origin' header

`

The Stylitics outfit widget CSS is being blocked. This is a misconfiguration on the Stylitics CDN — their CDN is not returning Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://www.skechers.com (or *) for stylesheet requests. The widget will render without styles. This should be reported to Stylitics as a CDN configuration issue.
